maketitle

# Overview and the two theorems we prove

A permutation can look perfectly uniform on almost every label and still be
far from uniform. The clearest example is parity. Let mu be uniform on the
alternating group A_n. The images of any n-2 labels are exactly uniform,
because fixing the images of n-2 labels leaves a transposition free, and a
transposition reverses parity. Yet mu is as far from the uniform law on
S_n as it can be: |mu-U_{S_n}|_{TV}=tfrac12.

So partial information, by itself, never forces full mixing. This note proves
the precise sense in which it almost does. There are two ingredients. The
first turns information about partial permutations into a bound on the
Fourier matrix of mu. The second uses the Fourier bound to force
mixing, provided the sign (parity) of mu is unbiased. The parity example
sits exactly at the boundary: it has perfect partial information but a
sign mean of pm1.

Throughout, G=S_n, U=U_{S_n} is uniform on G, and for a probability
mu we write
[
 |mu-U|_{TV}=tfrac12sum_{gin G}|mu(g)-U(g)|.
]
For an irreducible unitary representation rho of G we set
[
 widehatmu(rho)=sum_{gin G}mu(g),rho(g),
  D_rho=dimrho,
  |A|_{HS}^2=tr(A^*A),
]
using the unnormalized trace. For a partition lambda⊢ n we write
D_lambda for the dimension of the corresponding irreducible, and
[
 C_u=sup_{mge1}sum_{lambda⊢ m}D_lambda^{-u},
 
 Z_u(n)=sum_{lambda⊢ n, lambdanotin{(n),(1^n)}}D_lambda^{-u}.
]
The two partitions (n) and (1^n) are exactly the one-dimensional types
(trivial and sign); Lemma~lem:degrees shows C_u<∞ and
Z_u(n)to0 for every u>0.

[Coset caps control the whole Fourier matrix]
Partition {1,...,n} into b nonempty blocks M_1,...,M_b and let
H_i=Sym(M_i) act on its block and fix everything else. Let fge0 be a
measure of total mass at most 1 obeying the coset cap

 f(gH_i)le (B)/([G:H_i]) for all gin G, 1le ile b.

Then for every u>0 and every irreducible rho of degree D,

 |widehatwidehat f(f(rho)|_{HS}^2le b,B,C_u,D^{-1+(u+2)/b}.



The headline consequence needs the averaged information about partial
permutations, plus unbiased sign.

[Two convolutions suffice]
Let nto∞ through multiples of 8. Let mu_n be probabilities on
S_n with sign mean o(1), and suppose the images of one omitted block of
size n/8 are on average nearly uniform:
[
 E_{|M|=n/8},|mu_{n,H_M}-U_{G/H_M}|_{TV}=o(1),
]
where mu_{n,H_M} is the pushforward of mu_n to the cosets S_n/H_M.
Then
[
 |mu_n^{*2}-U_{S_n}|_{TV}→0.
]


The proof has three independent parts, and we give each in full. Section
sec:degrees proves the degree sums C_u<∞, Z_u(n)to0 together
with an exponential dimension lower bound. Section sec:trim passes from
the averaged hypothesis to a single subprobability with unit coset cap.
Section sec:fourier proves Theorem~thm:main and, with Plancherel,
deduces Corollary~cor:headline.

# Symmetric-group degrees

Write lambda' for the transpose of lambda and let
[
 L(lambda)=max(lambda_1,lambda'_1), k(lambda)=n-L(lambda)
]
be the length of a longest row or column and the number of boxes below it.
The hook-length formula says
D_lambda=n!/prod_{xinlambda}h(x), where h(x) counts x and the boxes
strictly to its right or below it.

[Tableau lower bound]
Let lambda⊢ n have first-row length a (so the lower diagram has
k=n-a boxes). For every integer b with 0le blemin(a,k),
[
 D_lambda ge binom{a}{b}.
]

Proof. 
A Young subdiagram is one closed under moving a box up or left. Starting from
the lower diagram (a genuine Young diagram of size k) and repeatedly
deleting a lower corner yields a Young subdiagram zeta of every size
0le ble k; fix one of size b. Its width is at most b, because the
first row of any partition of b has length at most b.

Count standard tableaux of the subdiagram made of the full first row (length
a) and zeta. Put the labels 1,...,b in the first b cells of the top
row. Choose any b of the labels {b+1,...,a+b} for the cells of zeta,
and fill zeta in one fixed standard relative order (this exists since
zeta is a Young diagram). Fill the remaining a-b top-row cells
increasingly with the leftover labels, of which there are exactly (a)-(b).
This is a valid standard tableau: the top row is increasing, and every cell of
zeta lies in a column 1,...,b whose top cell already carries a label
le b, smaller than any chosen label. Distinct choices of the b-subset
give distinct tableaux, so there are binom{a}{b} of them.

Every standard tableau of a Young subdiagram extends to a standard tableau of
lambda, by adjoining the missing boxes in an order that adds outer corners
with increasing labels. Hence D_lambdagebinom ab.


We also use the standard partition bound: with p(k) the number of partitions
of k and p(0)=1,

 p(k)le e^{3sqrt k}(kge1).

For a diagram with first row (after orienting a longest line) of length n-k
there are at most p(k) lower diagrams, hence at most 2p(k) shapes with
L(lambda)=n-k (the factor 2 records the two possible orientations).

[Degree sums and an exponential deficit bound]
For every u>0: C_u<∞ and Z_u(n)to0. Moreover there are absolute
constants c_1>0,c_2>0 such that for all sufficiently large n and every
lambda⊢ n,

 D_lambda ge c_1,e^{,c_2,k(lambda)}.

For each fixed positive k, D_lambdato∞ uniformly over shapes with
k(lambda)=k. One may take c_1=tfrac13 and c_2=(log 3)/(48).

Proof. 
Orient a longest line as the first row; let its length be L=n-k, and if it
was originally the first column transpose everything (dimensions unchanged).
If Lle n/16, then every hook has length at most 2Lle n/8, so
prod_x h(x)le(n/8)^n and the hook formula gives
D_lambdage (n/e)^n/(n/8)^n=(8/e)^nge2^n.

So assume L>n/16, hence k=n-L. Put b=min(k,floor( L/3)), so
1le ble L/3 and ble k, and Lemma~lem:tableau gives
[
 D_lambda ge binom{L}{b} ge Big((L)/(b)Big)^{b} ge 3^{b}.
]
If b=k then b=kge k/48, while if b=floor( L/3) then
floor( L/3)>n/48-1ge k/48-1 (using n=k+L and L>n/16). In both
cases bge k/48-1, whence
D_lambdage 3^{k/48-1}=tfrac13,3^{k/48}. This is (eq:deficit) with
c_1=tfrac13, c_2=(log3)/(48). When Lle n/16 the bound 2^n
certainly dominates tfrac13 e^{c_2k} for large n since kle n. Finally,
if b=k then D_lambdagebinom{n-k}{k}, which tends to infinity for each
fixed k as nto∞.

Here is the sum. Group shapes by deficit kge1: there are at most 2p(k) of
them, and each has D_lambda^{-u}le 3^{u}e^{-uc_2k} by (eq:deficit).
For fixed k every term tends to 0 as nto∞. The skeleton bound
2p(k),3^u e^{-uc_2k}le 2·3^u e^{3sqrt k-uc_2k} is summable in k and
independent of n. Dominated convergence gives Z_u(n)to0. Adding the two
linear types shows sum_{lambda⊢ n}D_lambda^{-u}to2, and the same
skeleton bound shows the full sum is uniformly bounded, so C_u<∞.


# One capped measure from averaged information

The averaged hypothesis mentions one omitted block at a time. We first select
a single favourable partition, then trim one measure that obeys every coset
cap at once.

[Selecting one partition]
Let b divide n and suppose a law mu on S_n satisfies
E_{|M|=n/b}|mu_{H_M}-U_{G/H_M}|_{TV}ledelta. Then some partition into
b equal blocks M_1,...,M_b has
sum_{i=1}^b|mu_{H_{M_i}}-U_{G/H_{M_i}}|_{TV}le bdelta.

Proof. 
Choose a uniformly random ordered equal partition. Each block is a uniform
n/b-subset, so the expected sum of the b errors is at most bdelta; some
partition attains at most the expectation.


[Trimming to unit cap]
Let H_1,...,H_ble G, let mu be a probability, and put
delta_i=|mu_{H_i}-U_{G/H_i}|_{TV}, delta=sum_idelta_i. There is a
subprobability 0le flemu with
[
 f(G)ge 1-delta, f(gH_i)le (1)/([G:H_i])(gin G, 1le ile b).
]

Proof. 
Trim the systems one at a time. For system i, in each coset Q whose current
mass exceeds the uniform level 1/[G:H_i], scale all current point masses in
Q down to that level; leave other cosets untouched. Trimming only lowers
masses, so it never breaks a cap established earlier, and it never raises a
coset above the level, so it establishes cap i. The mass removed while
trimming system i is the total positive excess of the current coset masses
over uniform for system i. Since the current measure is pointwise
lemu, that positive excess is pointwise no larger than the positive excess
of mu, so the removed mass is at most
sum_{Q:,mu(Q)>U(Q)}(mu(Q)-U(Q))=delta_i. Summing over i removes at
most delta in total.


For our application we choose b=8, take the partition from
Lemma~lem:select (so delta=o(1)), and let f be the subprobability
from Lemma~lem:trim. Then flemu_n, f(S_n)=1-o(1), and
(eq:cap) holds with B=1 for all eight blocks.

# Plancherel, amplification, and the Fourier bound

## Plancherel and the deficit inequality

[Plancherel with a mass deficit]
For every complex function a on G,

 |G|sum_{gin G}|a(g)|^2=sum_{rhoinIrr(G)}D_rho,|widehatwidehat a(a(rho)|_{HS}^2.

Consequently, if fge0 has mass zle1 and U is uniform, then

 Big(sum_{gin G}|f(g)-U(g)|Big)^2
 le 2,(1-z)^2
   +2!!sum_{rhoinIrr(G),,rhonetriv}D_rho,|widehatwidehat f(f(rho)|_{HS}^2 .


Proof. 
The identity (eq:plancherel) is the Fourier inversion identity for the
regular representation, obtained by expanding both sides, or from the
orthogonality of matrix coefficients. For the second part write
f-U=(f-zU)+(z-1)U, whose trivial Fourier coefficients are 0 and z-1.
By (eq:plancherel) applied to the complex function f-zU,
[
 |G|sum_g|f(g)-zU(g)|^2=sum_{rhonetriv}D_rho|widehatwidehat f(f(rho)|_{HS}^2 ,
]
since the trivial coefficient of f-zU vanishes. Cauchy--Schwarz and
sum_g U(g)=1 give
[
 sum_g|f(g)-U(g)|leBig(|G|sum_g|f(g)-zU(g)|^2Big)^{1/2}+|z-1|.
]
Squaring and using (x+y)^2le2x^2+2y^2 gives (eq:deficit-plancherel).


## From Fourier bounds to mixing

[Amplification]
Let mu_n be probabilities on G with |widehatmu_n(sgn)|=o(1). Let
f_n be either a subprobability with f_nlemu_n and f_n(G)to1, or a
probability with |f_n-mu_n|_{TV}to0. Suppose there are constants
A,gamma>0 with
[

  for every rhonetriv.
]
Then for every fixed integer r with rgamma>1,
[

]

for all rhonetriv and rbeta>1.

Proof.

## The coset-cap estimate

[Cosetwise isotypic estimate]
Let Hle G, let fge0 have mass zle1 and obey
f(gH)le B/[G:H] for all g, and let tauinIrr(H) have degree a.
Let P_{rho,H,tau} be the orthogonal projection of the G-module rho

 sum_{rhoinIrr(G)}D_rho,|widehatwidehat f(f(rho)P_{rho,H,tau}|_{HS}^2le Bz,a^2 .

Every multiplicity copy of tau is included.

Proof.

and e=0 off H; character orthogonality shows widehatwidehat e(e(rho)=P_{rho,H,tau}.
Since |chi_tau|le a we have
|e(h)|le a^2 U_H(h) where U_H(h)=1/|H| on H. Positivity of f gives,
for every g,
[
 |(f*e)(g)|lesum_h f(gh^{-1})|e(h)|le a^2(f*U_H)(g)
 =a^2,(f(gH))/(|H|)le(a^2B)/(|G|).

[

# Scope of this note

We have proved the paper's main theorem (coset caps bound every nontrivial
Fourier matrix, Theorem~thm:main) and its principal consequence (averaged
partial information on 7n/8 labels plus unbiased sign forces mixing in two
convolutions, Corollary~cor:headline). The remaining results of the
original document are alternative routes to these same conclusions or to
variants that impose different hypotheses (a random omitted set, entropy
comparisons valid under every tilt, signed palettes, kernel/equivariant forms,
or a conditional-product form). Each is an independent strengthening or
reformulation under different input; none is needed for the theorem proved
here, and all rest on the same three mechanisms displayed above: the degree
sums of Section~sec:degrees, the trimming lemma of Section~sec:trim,
and the central isotypic estimate combined with Plancherel.
