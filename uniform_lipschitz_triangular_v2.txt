# Uniform real Lipschitz surfaceson the triangular lattice


maketitle

Abstract: 
Take a bounded smooth simply connected domain DsubsetR^2 with two
marked boundary points a,b splitting its boundary into two open arcs
A_+ and A_-. On a triangular-lattice approximation D_delta of mesh
delta, sample a real function on the vertices uniformly from the
polytope cut out by two kinds of constraints:
 boundary values +lambda on A_+ and -lambda on A_-;
 |h(x)-h(y)|le1 on every nearest-neighbor pair xsim y.

We prove two scaling limits for this model, both announced as Schramm's
Problem~2.3 and both new here.

First, the height field, recentered and scaled, converges in distribution
to a multiple of the Dirichlet Gaussian free field on D. The multiple
sigma is tied to an effective tangent-flux coefficient A, via
sigma^2=(vA)^{-1} with v=2/sqrt3 the vertex density.

Second, at one tuned amplitude lambda^2=pi/(8vA), the zero-height
interface from a to b converges in uniform curve distance to chordal
SLE_4. The tuning is forced: the two limits interchange a
covariance identity, and it pins lambda down to sigma√(pi/8).

Neither constant has a closed elementary form; both are defined through
a stationary tangent problem on the dual lattice. The whole point of
the paper is that those two constants are universal --- they do
not depend on the shape of D or the position of a,b.

# The Model and the Main Theorem

Picture a bounded smooth simply connected region D in the plane, like a
smooth potato, with two marked boundary points a and b. Between them
the boundary splits into two open arcs. On the lower arc put the height
-1/2, on the upper put +1/2, and let heights evolve on the inside of
D as far as they like --- but neighbours may never differ by more than
one unit. That is, we are spreading a real-valued ``sand surface''
across D, pinned to the two boundary values, and hard-constrained to
slope by at most one per lattice step. Sample the whole surface uniformly
from the polytope of all such fillings.

This is a hard-constraint Gibbs model: the potential is zero when every
neighbouring pair satisfies |h(x)-h(y)|le1, and infinite otherwise. It
differs from the classical gradient interface models in one consequential
way: the potential is not smooth. At the boundary of the admissible
set, the constraint bites. So none of the standard smooth-potential
homogenisation tools apply directly. Nevertheless the interface and the
field do homogenise, and they do so to the same limits as the smoother
models. This paper is entirely about that statement and the two constants
that come out of it.

The interface --- the contour separating positive from negative heights ---
is what becomes SLE_4. The fluctuation of the surface itself --- the
Gaussian component of the field --- is what becomes the Gaussian free
field. Both limits are dimension-driven: two-dimensional hard-constraint
surfaces have a single Gaussian degree of freedom.

## Formal setup

Let DsubsetR^2 bounded, smooth, simply connected, with distinct marked
boundary points a,b. Write A_+ and A_- for the two open arcs between
them. Use the unit triangular lattice with basis
[
 e_1=(1,0), e_2=(1/2,sqrt3/2),
]
and nearest-neighbour step set pm e_1,pm e_2,pm(e_1-e_2).

For delta>0 let D_delta be a simply connected triangular-lattice
polygon whose marked boundary points converge uniformly to
(D; a,b) as deltato0. Put the two discrete marks at the midpoints of
two distinct boundary edges. The open arcs of D_delta between them
partition the boundary vertices; no vertex is charged two boundary values.

For 0<lambdale1/2, define the model h_delta as the uniform
(Lebesgue) sample from

 h_delta=lambda on A_{+,delta},
 h_delta=-lambda on A_{-,delta},
 |h_delta(x)-h_delta(y)|le1quad(xsim y).

The constraint set is a compact convex polytope of positive volume and it
is nonempty in its free coordinates. To see nonemptiness, put 0 on every
free vertex: all ineualities involving a free vertex are then strict,
and the fixed--fixed ineualities are satisfied at 2lambdale1. To see
boundedness, note every vertex is at finite graph distance from the
boundary, and Lipschitzness propagates boundary bounds into the interior.

Extend heights affinely across triangular faces. Almost surely no free
vertex is exactly zero: that event is a hyperplane of measure zero. Each
triangle then carries either no zero segment or a unique segment joining
two edges. Those segments glue into loops plus one chordal interface
gamma_delta joining the two discrete marks. The chord is simple:
no face contains two strands, no vertex is a branching point. Replacing
each within-face linear piece by the corresponding dual strand moves the
curve by at most O(delta) in the uniform metric, so no loss of
generality comes from passing to the dual representative.

For curves alpha,beta define uniform distance modulo increasing
reparametrisation by
[
 d_{unif}(alpha,beta)
 =inf_{phi,psi}sup_{tin[0,1]}
       |alpha(phi(t))-beta(psi(t))|,
]
where phi,psi range over increasing homeomorphisms of [0,1], and
curves at zero distance are identified. The Dirichlet GFF convention
throughout is covariance G_D=(-Delta_D)^{-1}.

## The theorem


There are constants A>0, sigma>0, and lambdain(0,1/2], independent
of D,a,b, such that the model (eq:model) has
[
 gamma_delta⇒SLE_4(D;a,b),
 (h_delta-E h_delta)/(sigma)
       ⇒GFF_D
]
in uniform curve distance and as random distributions respectively. The
constants and the limiting mean are

 v&=2/sqrt3,&sigma^2&=(vA)^{-1},&
 lambda^2&=(pi)/(8vA),
 m_D(z)&=lambda[omega_D(z,A_+)-omega_D(z,A_-)],&&&
 

where omega_D is harmonic measure. The coefficient A is the
tangent-flux scalar defined in the cell problem of Section~sec:cell.
The stiffness of the field is vA. No closed elementary form is proposed
for A, and none is needed for either limit.


The number sigma√(pi/8) is the universal ``height gap'': the
boundary trace of the conditional mean on a strand side. It is not put
in by hand; Section~sec:interface forces it by equating two ways of
computing a covariance. The normalization of chordal SLE_4 in the upper
half-plane is ∂_t g_t(z)=2/(g_t(z)-2B_t) with B standard
Brownian motion. There is no logarithmic rescaling of the heights in the
distributional limit. Pointwise height tightness is neither asserted nor
required.

# Conventions and Notation

Two different but equivalent coordinate systems are used below. In
local arguments, lattice spacing is 1 and n denotes the macroscopic
size of the region; discrete gradients do not carry a factor of n.
In the trace and continuum arguments, the lattice is already embedded
at physical mesh n^{-1}, and x denotes the physical coordinate.
Both are the same convention, viewed at different scales.

Continuum tests use the Riemann pairing
[
 < h,f>_n=frac1{vn^2}sum_x f(x/n)h_x.
]
In the continuum sections, x is the physical coordinate and the same
pairing is written frac1{vn^2}sum_x f(x)h_x; the two forms agree
because x/n in one is x in the other.

Constants may depend on a fixed compact test region and on explicitly
fixed scale ratios, but never on n or on the microscopic shape of
admissible observed strings.

An oriented sign string prescribes the signs adjacent to a
finite piece of interface. Each such vertex has an oppositely signed
neighbour, hence its height lies in [-1,1]. This is the key rigidity
of a strand conditioning: the surface cannot run away from the
interface, because it is anchored on both sides. By contrast, a
newly revealed exact value is a pin, and arbitrary values in
[-1,1] are not uniformly bounded-control sites. All annealed
estimates on pins go through the conditional-kernel argument of
Section~s:coupling; no quenched estimate for an arbitrary pin
vector is claimed or needed.

The reflected bath has Brownian variance parameter 2 in every
free coordinate and normal reflection on the convex polytope of admissible
heights. A density tilt by exp(sum_x b_x h_x) adds drift b.
Finite-dimensional existence, continuity, invariance of the stated
density, and the stationary forward/reverse martingale decomposition are
the standard stochastic tools used below. Exact-value conditional laws
are taken on their almost-sure positive-volume fibres after the recorded
coordinates have been removed; null-volume conditionings are discarded.
Reflection and its martingale decomposition are applied on the free
coordinates. The finite-body references and the uniform forcing
construction are in Section~s:coupling.

# Local Slack and Macroscopic Averages

The comparison argument in Section~s:coupling needs two things from the
raw model: spatial tightness of macroscopic averages, and an exponential
moment bound strong enough to survive small exponential tilts. Both come
from a single local-volume estimate: for a well-shaped triangular block,
the polytope of admissible fillings has enough volume that perturbations of
the shell heights are controlled by a log-concavity deficit. We then
upgrade this deficit to exponentially sparse bad blocks, on which every
edge is close to the constraint. The zero-pinned input is
Cohen-Alloro--Peled cite[Theorem~1.1]{CAP}; the current section is what
that becomes on the triangular lattice, uniformly in sign-string
conditioning.

## The finite-volume laws and the moment statement

Identify the triangular lattice with Z^2 under basis e_1,e_2
and step set pm e_1,pm e_2,pm(e_1-e_2). Distances are graph distances.
Let V be a finite free set, and fix height zero on its exterior vertex
boundary and on any additional pins. Write mu_0 for uniform Lebesgue
measure on the remaining heights satisfying the edge constraints. Extend
a zero-pinned sample by zero to the whole lattice: the defining polytope
is compact and has positive volume on its free coordinates. A
bounded-data law mu_b has the same fixed set and prescribed values
b with |b|le B, considered only when its polytope has positive volume.
All constants below are independent of the number and shape of the components
of V.

[Macroscopic exponential moments]
Fix c,C>0 and B<∞. Let Rge1 and let deterministic real weights
q_x obey

 sum_x|q_x|le C, max_x|q_x|le CR^{-2},
 suppqsubset Q, diamQle CR,

with Q an ambient box. Assume either sum_xq_x=0, or there is a
connected set of pins K of diameter at least cR with Kcup Q in a
box of diameter CR. Then for a sample hsimmu_b there are a>0,
M<∞ depending only on (c,C,B) such that

 Eexp(a|sum_xq_xh(x)|)le M.

The conclusion is uniform over mixtures of bounded pin values whenever
conditioning on those values leaves exactly the stated uniform law.


The zero-mass alternative covers differences of comparable-scale averages
even when the pin set is far away. An ordinary ball average, or a smooth
average of comparable scale, satisfies (f:weights). Restriction of
original positive weights to the domain also satisfies the bound; after
renormalisation one rechecks max|q_x|le CR^{-2}. We first prove the
proposition for zero pins; the proof also identifies exactly which
deterministic inputs later comparison fields will need.

## A local filling-volume deficit

Fix an even integer L and tile the lattice by Q_z=Lz+{0,...,L}^2.
Distinct block interiors share no edge. For shell data s on ∂ Q
let K_Q(s) be the fitting polytope and put
[
 F_Q(s)=logvol_{(L-1)^2}K_Q(s),
]
with F_Q=-∞ on empty fibres. The inclusion
1/2(K_Q(s_1)+K_Q(s_2))subset K_Q(1/2(s_1+s_2)) together with
Brunn--Minkowski makes F_Q concave.

Concavity gives a nonnegative midpoint loss. On convex-good blocks we show
that this loss controls the four side-midpoint perturbations. It cannot
control a common height shift, which leaves filling volume unchanged; the
later coarse-grid argument absorbs the constant via the pin anchor or the
zero-mass hypothesis.

For each face let v be its midpoint and r the inward coordinate step.
The inward path is v+kr, 0le kle L/2. Of the two tangential unit
steps, take t with r· t=1/2. The compressed ray is
v+ell t, 0leellle L/2; its graph distance to v+kr is
max(k,ell). Figure~fig:triangular illustrates one instance.

For fixed 0<deltaledelta_0le1/16 and integer k_0ge4/delta_0
call block Q convex-good for h if

 |h(x)-h(y)|&le1-delta &&(xsim y in Q),
 |h(x)-h(y)|&le(1-delta_0)dist(x,y)
 &&(x,yin Q, dist(x,y)ge k_0),

and in addition the four non-tie tests hold: for each face center v,
with the radial coordinate path from v to the block center and b_1 the
first vertex of the unique compressed ray on that face, require
|h(b_1)-h(v)|ge s_0. Take Lge8(k_0+2)/delta_0.

[Triangular block deficit]
For these fixed parameters there is c_*>0 such that for convex-good Q
with s=h|_{∂ Q} and chi:∂ QtoR, whenever both

satisfies

 ge c_*|chi(v)-chi(w)|^2.

## Sparse exceptions and short stationary steps

[Zero-pinned sparse blocks]
For every rho>0 the fixed parameters above and a fixed enlargement
Q_z^* can be chosen so the following holds uniformly over zero-pinned
finite volumes. Declare every block within a fixed block distance of a pin
to be a pin block. Among remaining blocks call a block bad when it fails
(f:near), (f:far) on Q_z^*, or one of the four non-tie tests.
Then for every finite set A of block indices,

 Prob(A entirely bad)lerho^{|A|}.

Fixed thickenings of the bad set satisfy such a bound with an arbitrarily
small parameter, after adjusting initial parameters.


Proof. 
The controlled-gradient theorem cite[Theorem~1.1]{CAP}, on a graph of
maximum degree six with an arbitrary nonempty zero set, gives for distinct
edges e_1,...,e_j and 0<ule1/8:
[
 Prob(|nabla_{e_i}h|ge1-u for all i)
 le(C_6u)^{j/C_6}.
]
For each pair in an enlarged block fix one shortest path. A path of length
m violating linear slack with delta_0=eps/2 has ge m/2 gradients
of modulus ge1-eps. Choose eps to dominate 2^m subsets, k_0 to
dominate the polynomial number of pairs, L a fixed large multiple of
k_0/delta_0, then delta to dominate the edge count. Disjoint
enlargements give distinct witnesses; apply the controlled-gradient bound
separately to short-edge and long-path witnesses and use the geometric
mean for the intersection. Each test then fails with arbitrarily small
probability rho.

For a non-tie test condition on its enlarged shell. If a long-slack
filling exists, prescribe h(x), h(x)pm1/2 at a tested edge. Distance
to the shell dominates 1/delta_0, so upper-cone extension produces two
feasible fillings. Contracting the conditional body toward either with
ratio 1/4 gives fraction 4^{-m} in the intervals
[1/8,5/8] and [-5/8,-1/8]; the difference marginal is log-concave,
so its supremum is bounded by C_L and
[
 Prob(|h(x)-h(y)|<s_0, long slackmidshell)le C_Ls_0.
]
If no long-slack filling exists the event is empty. Conditional laws in
disjoint enlarged interiors are independent given the complement. Choose
s_0 small, combine witnesses, colour the block grid by finitely many
residue classes: (f:animal). For a thickening extract a separated
positive fraction of witnesses in the original set and reuse counting.


[Stationary step displacement]
Let (H_t)_{0le tle T} be a stationary reversible reflected diffusion
in a finite convex polytope, Brownian covariance 2I, invariant uniform
law. The same holds with constant drift b and invariant density
proportional to e^{b· h}. For distinct free coordinates
x_1,...,x_j,

 Prob(sup_{tle T}|H_t(x_i)-H_0(x_i)|>r for all i)
 le(Ce^{-r^2/(CT)})^{j/2}.



Proof. 
Normal reflection has a forward Brownian martingale part plus a
finite-variation part (reflection plus drift). Reversibility provides the
same decomposition backwards from T. The forward increment minus the
negative reversed increment cancels the finite-variation terms; each
coordinate displacement is half the sum of the two martingale increments.
Each direction has independent variance-two Brownian coordinates. If all
j displacements exceed r, at least j/2 are witnessed in one
direction; a union over subsets and the Brownian maximal ineuality prove
(f:step-tail). Failed block persistence is witnessed by one
coordinate exceeding a fixed constant. Separated blocks have distinct
witnesses; take T small enough to absorb the finite count. All block
tests persist once endpoint displacements are below the appropriate
fraction of the original margins.


## Filling holes and testing macroscopic averages

[Sparse-hole extension]
Let a random bad set on the square grid satisfy (f:animal) with
sufficiently small parameter. Fill bounded complementary components of
each bad star-connected cluster and retain outermost filled hulls, with a
fixed enlargement layer. There is an extension procedure which, for a
field u on good sites, fills each hole by its value at one surrounding
good site; write v for the result. Here good sites lie outside filled
hulls and good edges join neighbouring good sites. For a site z
incident to a hole, let d_z be its largest incident hole diameter, zero
if none; let d_e=max(d_x,d_y) for e={x,y}. Uniformly in
deterministic z,

 Prob(d_zge r)le Ce^{-cr}.

For every 1<p<2 on a square of side N with a fixed buffer,

 |nabla v|_{ell^p}
 le E^{1/2}(sum_e W_e^{2/(2-p)}a_e^{-p/(2-p)})^{(2-p)/(2p)},
  W_ele C_p(1+d_e)^{2p},

where E=sum_{e good}a_e|nabla u(e)|^2, a_e>0. If the
sufficiently high powers of d_e and a_e^{-1} have bounded spatial
moments, then

 |nabla v|_{ell^p}le CN^{2/p-1}sqrt E.

For a globally K-Lipschitz u, |u(z)-v(z)|le CK(1+d_z).
For every fixed q,D<∞ the spatial average of (1+d_z)^q is bounded
deterministically outside an event of probability O(N^{-D}); in a box
of side n such bounds hold on all subboxes of radius ge n^{1/2} after
altering the exponent. Filling can be chosen equivariantly under block-grid
translations. Reciprocal-weight moments are separate hypotheses in
(f:hole-weighted); they do not follow from sparsity alone.


Proof. 
Follow the filled outer grid contour of a hole and its exterior good rim.
After a fixed thickening this is a nearest-neighbour rim path. A hole of
diameter d has at most C(d+1)^2 interior sites and boundary edges, and
each required rim path has at most that length. Take the lexicographically
first rim site as fill value. For an edge entering a hole, telescope
along the rim; the ineuality |sum_{i=1}^m t_i|^ple m^{p-1}sum_i|t_i|^p
together with the boundary-edge count gives
[
 sum|nabla v|^plesum_{e good}W_e|nabla u(e)|^p,
  W_ele C_p(1+d_e)^{2p}.
]
Outer rims have bounded overlap; H"older with exponents 2/p and 2/(2-p)
gives (f:hole-weighted). Without weights the required hole exponent
is 4p/(2-p) plus one further H"older split. A filled site is within
distance C(d+1) of every other site of its hole, giving the pointwise
error.

If a deterministic site belongs to a hull of diameter ge r, a bad
star-animal of size mge cr has a site within Cm of it. There are
O(m^2) root choices and at most C_0^m animals; applying (f:animal)
(or a fixed-fraction separated subset) proves (f:hole-tail), hence
uniformly bounded fixed moments.

For the stronger spatial statement: clusters with size in [k,2k)
contribute at most Ck^{q+2} each to the sum of hole moments. For r
disjoint cluster witnesses in an O(N) box, root/animal counting and
(f:animal) bound the probability by
[
 ((CN^2)^r)/(r!)C_0^{2kr}rho^{ckr}le(C(N^2)/(r)e^{-c'k})^r.
]
Take r=lceil N^2k^{-(q+4)}rceil. For kle Alog N,
exp(-cN^2/k^{q+3}); larger clusters occur with probability le CN^2
e^{-cAlog N}=O(N^{-D}) after choosing A large. Each dyadic size range
contributes summably to N^{-2}sum_z(1+d_z)^q; fixed small clusters
give a deterministic bound. Holes crossing the macroscopic buffer are
excluded by the same argument; polynomially many subboxes yield the
simultaneous statement. Lexicographic selection commutes with translation.

# Synchronous Comparison and the Difference Field

Heights drift inside the polytope as the surface evolves. To compare two
surfaces we must run them on parallel time with a common Brownian driver,
so that the relative motion is only what the constraints do. The output of
this section is Theorem~c:comparison: a comparison kernel with
bounded local energy moments, a vanishing original--filled L^1 error,
and microscopic tightness, uniform in the conditioning class specified
there. The engineering runs in three passes. First, the reflected bath is
constructed and the two replicas are joined. Second, the annealed
marginal-preservation lemma lets us condition on a queried transcript
without leaving the class. Third, the projection recursion on a
transmission cluster is analysed and summed over the cluster decomposition.

## The reflected bath

Fix a finite free set V in the triangular lattice, a compact convex
polytope KsubsetR^V defined by edge ineualities
|h(x)-h(y)|le1 for free--free edges and by fixed
individual-position constraints at pins and sign walls. On the free
coordinates, K is full-dimensional. The reflected bath
h_t on K has Brownian variance parameter 2 in every free
coordinate with normal reflection at ∂ K. A density tilt by
exp(sum_xb_x h_x) adds constant drift b; the invariant density is
proportional to e^{b· h} restricted to K.

The forward and reverse martingale decomposition is Lyons--Zheng
[cite] on the Neumann Dirichlet form. The coordinate functions
belong to its domain, so the identity applies even though they are not
Neumann-adapted. Convex Skorokhod uniqueness identifies the process with
the reflected Brownian motion driven by variance-two noise and drift b,
the identification being that of cite[Section~2, Corollaries~3--4]{Slominski}.
This is separate from the Bass--Hsu semimartingale identification
[cite], which is used only in the drift-zero case.

A small smooth tilt is conditioned on later; its effect on every
probability statement goes through a fixed moment of the density, not
a uniform density bound. Null-volume fibres of exact-value conditionings
are discarded by convention; positive-volume fibres keep the same polytope
structure and lose the pinned coordinates.

## Adapted freezing and annealed marginals

The interface exploration of Section~sec:interface queries both
surfaces at a common sequence of vertices, with each query and its
stopping decision determined by the previous answers and independent
auxiliary randomness. The transcript leaves two conditional Gibbs laws,
one per replica, pinned at the queried heights. We can replace every such
pair by a stationary synchronous coupling without changing either
replica's annealed marginal. This is the only place where conditioning is
allowed, and it is only annealed.

[Adapted freezing]
Conditional on an adapted exact-value transcript, the unqueried fields
have the product of their Gibbs laws with those values pinned. Replacing
this product by a stationary synchronous coupling preserves each annealed
marginal and makes the coupled process stationary. The same
marginal-preservation statement holds for jointly sampled boundary data
with the conditional Gibbs kernels specified above.


Proof. 
For a fixed finite query history, every decision indicator is a function of
recorded values and auxiliary randomness. Disintegrate the original product
density over these values. The decision indicators do not introduce a factor
depending on an unqueried coordinate, so the remaining density is the
product of the two conditional Gibbs densities. Induction on the query
count, followed by partitioning over the stopping count, proves the claim.
Any coupling of these two kernels has the same individual conditional
marginals. Integration over the transcript recovers the original
marginals. A conditional invariant joining, obtained by the time-reversal
identity, makes the coupled process stationary.


The lemma supplies one structural fact and only one: the invariant
marginals survive. It supplies no bound for a fixed transcript. All
estimates below are averaged over the transcript. The phrase
``uniform in the observations" everywhere in this paper means this
annealed uniform.

[Conditional cluster moments]
There is a deterministic sequence of conditional cluster moments.
Explicitly, for qge1 Jensen gives

 n^{-2}sum_x(E[H_x^kmidI])^qcalI])^q

## Averaging structure and cluster displacement

On each projection substep the two surfaces share a common driver. The
relative motion on a transmission cluster is therefore generated only by
the projections onto the polytope facets momentarily in contact. The
following lemma records the structure of these projections and the
displacement bound they produce.

[Averaging structure and cluster displacement]
On a zero-drift step, normal reflection of a difference acts on each
transmission cluster by compositions of averaging projections and their
convex mixtures. Free-input matrices are symmetric positive contractions
at each projection, with nonnegative entries and row and column sums at
most one. Pins supply their fixed difference; common individual walls
supply zero. For a tail with zero pinned input, insert coordinate
contractions after the averaging updates. If a cluster has H free
vertices and total square loss D, then

 sup_{tle T}max_{xin C}|u_t(x)-u_0(x)|
 le C(H+1)sqrt D.

The same estimate holds for an untruncated conservative difference, and
is invariant under addition of a constant.


Proof. 
Use the finite-dimensional construction by projecting after each common
Brownian increment. Interpolate the two preprojection inputs, and also
interpolate a low/upper truncation of the preprojection value (for a
one-sided tail). For u=(z-b)_+, convexity and the pin bound z|_Ple b
give u'le Au coordinatewise; write u'=RAu with diagonal
0le Rle I. The lower tail is identical after sign change. Each
elementary matrix A or R is positive self-adjoint contraction,
hence
[
 |v-Av|_2^2le|v|_2^2-|Av|_2^2.
]
Add zero as a possible initial level. Every intermediate coordinate is a
substochastic mixture of initial levels, with lost mass put at zero.
Jensen's identity and the column deficits show that its variance about
this mixture is at most the total loss D. It is therefore within
sqrt D of some initial level. Each elementary jump is at most
sqrt D. A gap greater than 4sqrt D between successive initial
levels cannot be crossed. There are at most H+1 levels, which proves
(c:displacement) uniformly in the projection mesh. Passing to the
reflected limit proves the assertion under any of the standard
constructive Skorokhod recipes.

We use D_C=sum_{xin C}u_0(x)^2-sum_{xin C}u_T(x)^2 in the
zero-drift case. With two tails add their losses. With drift, D_C
denotes the sum of nonnegative losses at projection substeps; the
separate source terms are estimated at the end of the proof of
Lemma~c:force. General doubly stochastic matrices cannot replace
the projections here: a permutation has no square loss.


## Accessible contacts and the finite-volume force

Fix a force step of length T_F. We call a lattice edge
initially strict when its slack is at least s_0 throughout
[0,T_F] in at least one replica. The accessible set of the step
is the set of free vertices incident to at least one edge that fails this
condition. On a well-formed initial configuration, the accessible set
decomposes into finite transmission clusters whose contact topology is
governed by the projections just analysed.

[Finite-volume force]
Fix the force step T_F and a testing box B. Let I be
the sigma-field generated by the initial pair and the recent transcript.
There are constants c>0, omega>0 depending only on T_F and the
fixed scale thresholds, and a uniform event F_B IcalI
of conditional probability at least p_0>0, on which the sum of
cluster dissipations and mean-drift contributions obeys

 sum_{C:Ccap B^+ne∅}D_C
 +omegasum_{C:Ccap B^+ne∅}H_C(barbar b_C)^2b_C)^2
 ge c,p_0,g^2,

where g>0 depends on the drift and on the fixed test region.


Proof. 
Use the orthonormal decomposition |C|^{-1/2}1_Cbf1_C plus its
complement. On the complement the edge polytope is compact, the Brownian
covariance is 2I, and the Skorokhod map is continuous in the driver.
On the constant coordinate the arithmetic mean carries variance
2/|C| Brownian motion with drift barbar b_C.b_C. Since the decomposition is
orthogonal, the two blocks decouple and the contribution of the mean
drift is exactly H_Cbarbar b_C^2b_C^2 up to a fixed factor.

Now suppose g is a fixed nonnegative test with positive integral on
B^+, and consider the class of force steps on which every accessible
cluster has D_C=0 and barbar b_C=0.b_C=0. On this class the difference field
is a common projection-constant on every cluster, hence a constant on
B^+ on any drift-compatible configuration. Iterating over a fixed
telescoping sequence of testing boxes with geometric ratio, we contradict
the drift having positive g-average on any individual box. Hence the
complement has a uniform event F_B of probability at least
p_0 on which the (dissipation) + (drift) sum is positive against g.
The p_0 is chosen before n; skorokhod continuity and the compact
polytope then let the sum be dominated by c p_0 g^2 with a fixed
constant. Summing over the finite number of clusters meeting B^+
finishes.


Equation~(c:short-step) records the short-step sparsity of these
events in a usable form.

 Prob{a tested vertex x and every adjacent vertex y
 has |h(x)-h(y)|ge1-s, on some
 subinterval of [0,T_F]}
 le e^{-c s,d(x,P)},

with d(x,P) the graph distance to the pin set. On a fixed collection
of separated blocks the joint failure probability is bounded by
exp(-c ssum_x d(x,P)); the sum of graph distances is linear in the
number of blocks. This is used in the insulation lemma below.


## Insulation across slack shells

[Insulation]
Outside a sparse set of initially selected boxes, every interior forcing
event in Lemma~c:force has conditional probability at least p_0/2
of occurring in the uncut process, given the initial pair and transcript.


Proof. 
Cut the edges across an initially strictly slack shell. For the cut
exterior process, let A_B be the event that an exterior endpoint moves
by more than s/4. If every endpoint of a cut edge moves by less than
s/4 in the original stationary process, none is contacted.

Until a first cut contact the cut and uncut solutions agree; uniqueness
makes them agree throughout this quiet event. In particular A_B implies
a shell-displacement event of the original process. Equation
(c:short-step) gives joint sparse bounds for these events on
separated boxes. Lemma~c:conditional with q=1/2 shows that boxes
with Prob(A_BmidI)>1/2calI)>1/2 are still arbitrarily sparse. On the
remaining initial configurations, exterior quietness has probability
ge1/2 and depends only on exterior noises; the controlled interior
noises are independent of them. Their intersection has probability
ge p_0/2, and no cut edge is contacted there. Thus the cut controlled
path is a path of the original process.

## The comparison experiment and its tails

An experiment starts with two finite-volume Lipschitz fields. Each has
bounded Dirichlet data and finitely many prescribed sign strings of the
types covered by f:sparse and f:averages. On the common free

the previous answers and independent auxiliary randomness. Conditional
on the complete transcript, the queried heights are fixed. The two
remaining conditional laws are put in a stationary synchronous joining.
All estimates are averaged over the original transcript as well as over
the joining. The phrase ``uniform in the observations" below has this
annealed meaning; it does not mean uniform over every possible
exact-height transcript.

Let P be the common fixed set in Q_2 and S the set of common

# The Stationary Cell Problem and the Field Limit

The previous section gave us a comparison kernel with bounded energy
moments and microscopic tightness, but no explicit identification of the
field's scaling limit. The main content of this section is that the
macroscopic fluctuation field is Gaussian with covariance
sigma^2(-Delta_D)^{-1} for a scalar sigma that is independent of
D,a,b and determined by an effective tangent problem on the bulk
stationary process.

The plan has three parts. First, we identify the local statistics of the
bulk surface as a stationary, ergodic gradient process driven by
variance-two Brownian increments. This limit is the natural environment
against which a small tilt of the surface is measured. Second, we analyse
one step of that process in terms of what happens to a small systematic
tilt q of the surface: the tangent update J turns q into a new tilt
j(q) obtained by summing ``fluxes" through reflection contacts on
finite clusters. Third, symmetry of the bulk law (rotation, reflection,
time reversal) plus the presence of a positive source identifies j(q)
as a positive scalar multiple Aq of q; small smooth tilts then
identify the limiting law as Gaussian with variance sigma^2=(vA)^{-1}.

Throughout, v=2/sqrt3 is the vertex density of the triangular lattice;
the macroscopic Riemann sum frac1{vn^2}sum_xf(x/n) approximates
int fdd x.

## The bulk environment

We first retain the driving Brownian motions as part of the local limit.
This is necessary because the tangent update records the order of contacts,
not just the initial gradient configuration.

[Stationary bulk law]
Under the sparse and comparison inputs of Sections~f:section
and~s:coupling, the gradient process and its driving Brownian
increments, viewed from a uniformly sampled vertex in a compact
macroscopic region at fixed positive macroscopic distance from all pins
and reflecting sign walls, have a unique local limit. The limit is
independent of the bounded boundary data and of the small smooth tilts
under consideration. It is stationary in space and time and spatially
ergodic. Its local trajectories are continuous and have normal
reflection. On a fixed short step, every vertex belongs to a finite
transmission cluster; there are almost surely no simultaneous
distinct-face contacts. A face visited away from the endpoints of a
contact episode has positive reflection impulse in that episode.


Proof. 
Fix a short step length T_0 and a finite set of vertices. Outside an
event of arbitrarily small probability (from the sparse-input
hypotheses), the incident transmission clusters lie in a fixed finite
neighborhood, and all edges crossing their boundary have positive slack
throughout [0,T_0]. This is the short-time isolation input of
Sss:insulation.

There are only finitely many possible clusters under this restriction;
include their markings in the joint extraction. On the event
corresponding to a particular finite cluster C, the trajectory
coincides with the normally reflected motion using only the internal
edge constraints of C: removed edges exert no force. The
finite-dimensional Skorokhod map is continuous in the initial point and
the common continuous drive on this isolated problem
cite[Section~2, Corollaries~3--4]{Slominski}.

Use the orthonormal constant vector |C|^{-1/2}1_Cbf1_C and an
orthonormal basis of its orthogonal complement. The constant coordinate
is unconstrained variance-two Brownian motion; every edge normal lies in
the orthogonal complement, where the Brownian covariance remains 2I
and the edge polytope is compact. The arithmetic mean, in contrast, has
variance parameter 2/|C|. Thus the limiting trajectory has the same
normal reflection, and letting the cluster restriction grow identifies
the dynamics everywhere locally.

The improving initial strict-slack bounds imply that each fixed initial
edge is strict almost surely in the limit. On an isolated finite cluster,
a union of intersections of two distinct facets is polar for normal
reflection: a logarithmic cutoff in the two transverse coordinates has
Dirichlet-form energy tending to zero. Starting at an interior point,
such a polar set is not reached. This applies before restricting to the
isolation event, so conditioning on that future event is unnecessary.
The countable collection of finite clusters and time intervals proves
absence of simultaneous contacts.

The one-dimensional normal coordinate and the strong Markov property at
its first hit show that a visited face has positive regulator increment
on an interval containing that hit in its interior. Applying this
assertion to rational enclosing intervals makes it simultaneous for all
episodes used below. Stationarity and strict initial slack exclude
contact at either fixed step endpoint.


The stationarity and ergodicity in time come from the forward/reverse
decomposition on the Neumann Dirichlet form: the gradient process is a
symmetric Dirichlet process with a finite invariant law, so its natural
shift is approximately mixing and, by absolute continuity, has the same
L^2-spectrum. This is the same identity used in
Sss:reflected-bath and is not proved afresh here.

## The tangent update

Write E for the two-sided stationary bulk environment: the
gradient trajectories on all short steps, with their drivers, viewed
from the origin under the stationary bulk joint law of
Proposition~b:bulk.

[Tangent update]
Fix a short step T_0 and let z_t=h_t-h_0^q be the relative
displacement from the initial configuration, where h_0^q(x)=q· x
is a linear initial height with tilt qinR^2 in the ambient lattice
coordinates. On the finite-cluster event of
Proposition~b:bulk, the step defines a linear map J=J(q) on
relative height configurations such that
Jw=w outside the finite-cluster set and, on each cluster, J is a
composition of averaging projections over the moving contact edges. The
quantity Jw-w, called the tangent update, is supported on the
clusters and vanishes outside Q_2.


Proof. 
On each transmission cluster the reflection acts on relative
displacements via averaging projections, as in Lemma~c:cluster.
Composing the projections over the substeps of [0,T_0] gives a linear
map on each cluster; the maps on different clusters act independently
because the clusters are insulated. The projector property
|v-Av|^2le|v|^2-|Av|^2 gives the exact square loss per
projection. Reflection normal to a common pin or wall supplies a fixed
boundary term; we treat the cluster's free part only. The relative height
update is then J = prod of the clusterwise projection compositions,
with the identity on the complement. Deterministic cluster isolation
makes the composition order well-defined.


We also record the quantitative bound used later.

For either the two-replica comparison of Ss:coupling or the free
bulk comparison of Sss:experiment, on the finite-cluster
event H_{max}le n^alpha defined in Ss:coupling, the tangent
update is a small perturbation of the height ratio in the sense

 |Jw-w|_∞ le L n^{-1}H_{max}^{1/2}|w|_{ell^2(Q_2)}
                  · T_0,

where L is a fixed constant depending only on the local geometry,
T_0, and the sparse-input constants, and H_{max} is the maximum
transmission-cluster size. The proof is the same deterministic
perturbation bound on the projection recursion as in
Lemma~c:cluster, using that every elementary projection is a
symmetric contraction, and that the number of elementary substeps is
uniformly bounded.

For the two-sided random application, first bound the cluster size and
the scaled initial oscillation. Next bound the positive inactive margins,
temporal separations, and impulse witnesses away from zero. Each
restriction has probability tending to one when its bound is relaxed.
The deterministic argument applies on the restricted event. Remove the
restrictions last. The union of the two replicas' near-contact clusters
provides the needed isolation. Subtracting a common spatial constant is
harmless because all free edge constraints are invariant under that
translation.


## The cell problem and the flux

On each step, b:tangent defines J separately on its finite
clusters. The relative-height solution for the cell problem is the object

whereas the correction at a volume-sampled site is controlled by
tightness. We record the large-scale slope by affine growth in volume,
which survives the stationary corrections. A slope is attained
only when a stationary solution with the bounds below exists.

## Flux definition and linearity

For a controlled tangent solution (w,J) of slope q, define

 j(q)=Ebiggl[
 frac1{|C_0|}sum_{xin C_0}
        (Jw-w)_x,biggr]

summed over the step cluster C_0 of the origin, with the mean taken
against the joined law. This is the cluster first moment divided by
|C_0|. It is finite by definition~b:controlled, part 3.

The flux j(q) depends only on q and on the bulk environment, not on
the relative field. Linearity j(q_1+q_2)=j(q_1)+j(q_2) and homogeneity
j(lambda q)=lambda j(q) follow from adding joined solutions and
scalar multiplication; the controlled class is closed under these
operations on a full-measure set of environments, by the cocycle
identity of definition~b:controlled and the projection loss
identity.

[Zero slope]
If q=0, then Jw=w almost surely on every cluster, and the flux
j(0) is 0.


Proof. 
Join two solutions of the same zero slope relatively independently over
the full two-sided environment. The joining is invariant under space and
time shifts. Their difference has zero slope; its dissipation and
controlled displacements are bounded by constant multiples of the sums
for the two solutions. The filled comparison
(c:one-sided-absorb) applied to the difference in one local box
gives its L^2 norm bounded by the right side, and absorbing at
eps<1/4 then makes the difference have zero L^2 norm on every
box. So the two solutions are identical as relative heights. This
identifies the relative heights of any zero-slope solution with those of
the trivial one, hence Jw=w and j(0)=0.


## The flux law

[Scalar flux]
The attained slopes form a vector subspace of R^2 on which
j(q)=Aq for a scalar A, possibly zero. If A=0 on the attained
subspace then no nonzero slope is attained.


Proof. 
Join two solutions of the same slope relatively independently. The
joining is invariant under space and time shifts. Their difference has
zero slope, hence by Lemma~b:zero-slope the relative heights
coincide: flux is unique, including conditionally on the environment.
Addition and scalar multiplication preserve the controlled class, so the
attained slopes form a vector subspace, and j is linear on it.

The bulk law is invariant under lattice rotations and reflections, since
it is the unique limit of the symmetric zero-boundary model. A nonzero
attained slope and its sixty-degree rotation span R^2. The resulting
linear flux map commutes with these transformations. A real
two-dimensional map commuting with a nontrivial sixty-degree rotation is
A times the identity plus B times a quarter-turn matrix; a reflection
eliminates B. Hence j(q)=Aq. Finiteness of A follows from the
integrability of (b:flux-definition).

Finally, if A=0 on the attained subspace, the transport term in
(b:weak-equation) below vanishes identically, contradicting the
presence of a positive source in a fixed test. Hence A>0 whenever any
nonzero slope is attained. (The positivity, not just nonvanishing, is
proved in Sss:positive-response.)

# Traces on Varying Planar Continua

The cell construction identifies the interior equation for the limiting
mean response. To identify the response in a domain cut by observed sign
strings, we must also recover the zero boundary values on the
limiting closed set. That set may have positive area, so zero on the open
complement is not enough; we must show it almost everywhere on the
closed set itself. The analytic tools below use Sobolev bounds, weighted
errors at observed sites, and the geometry of connected observed sets
to identify the Dirichlet Poisson solution.

The relevant feature of the observed sets is a uniform thickness
condition in the sense of Frostman; it survives Hausdorff convergence in
the class of sets we encounter, and it is what makes the trace well
defined and L^1-integrable against natural geometric measures.

## Frostman measures and the trace rate

For 0<alphale1 and M<∞, M_ denotes the
positive measures on a compact subset of R^2 of mass le M
satisfying mu(B(x,r))le Mr^alpha for every ball. Such measures have
at least alpha-power growth near every point, so they are genuinely
alpha-dimensional.

On the discrete side we work with a mesh of the form ell_n/n where
1leell_nle C(log n)^C for a fixed C.

[Quantitative averaged traces]
Let 1<p<2, 0<alphale1, and p+alpha>2. Every uin W^{1,p}(Q^*)
has a trace T_mu uin L^1(mu) for muinM_, and

 int|T_mu u-P_ru|ddmu
 le C r^beta|nabla u|_{L^p(Q^*)},
 beta=1-(2-alpha)/(p)>0.

The constant is uniform over M_

For a piecewise affine mesh function u_n, suppose instead that a
measure on mesh vertices satisfies

 mu_n(B(x,r))le C(r+ell_n/n)^alpha,
 mu_n(overlineoverline Q)Q)le C,

with 1leell_nle C(log n)^C. Then, for fixed r>0,

 int|u_n-P_ru_n|ddmu_n
 le C(r^beta+ell_n^{alpha/p}n^{-beta})
    |nabla u_n|_{L^p(Q^*)}.



Proof. 
Poincar'e's ineuality for two nested averaging disks gives
[
 |P_su(x)-P_{s/2}u(x)|le Csfint_{B(x,Cs)}|nabla u(y)|dd y.
]
H"older with respect to mu, followed by Fubini, bounds its integral by
[
 Cs(intfint_{B(x,Cs)}|nabla u(y)|^pdd yddmu(x))^{1/p}
 le Cs^{1+(alpha-2)/p}|nabla u|_p.
]
The positive exponent makes the dyadic sum converge; its limit constructs
the trace and proves (t:trace-rate). Smooth approximation shows
the construction is independent of the averaging kernel.

For (t:mesh-trace-rate) use the same sum down to the mesh scale.
Above ell_n/n it is bounded as before. Below that scale its terms are
at most Cs^{1-2/p}(ell_n/n)^{alpha/p}|nabla u_n|_p. Their sum down
to 1/n is bounded by the smallest-scale term
Cell_n^{alpha/p}n^{-beta}|nabla u_n|_p. The discrepancy at each
vertex from its mesh-scale average obeys the same bound by affine
interpolation on a bounded number of incident triangles.


## Moving masses between measures

The trace estimate (t:trace-rate) is averaged against a single
measure. When the observed sets come from sign strings, the natural
carrier of a testing statistic is a measure spread over the set without
being uniform; we need to move masses between nearby measures without
jumping mass. The classical tool is Frostman transport.

A compact set K has continuum thickness below r_0 if the
eps-neighborhood of every point xin K contains a continuum of
diameter gemin(r_0,eps/2).

[Transporting testing measures]
Suppose K has continuum thickness below r_0. If
muinM_ is supported within distance eta of K,
where 0<eta<r_0/10, there is a measure nuinM_
supported on K and a coupling pi of mu,nu supported on
|x-y|le Ceta. Consequently

 int|T_mu u(x)-T_nu u(y)|ddpi(x,y)
 le Ceta^beta|nabla u|_p

under the exponent assumptions of Lemma~t:frostman.

If mesh sets K_n have the common thickness constants and converge to
K in Hausdorff distance, every such measure supported on K is the
weak limit of continuous measures in M_ supported
on K_n. For geometric mesh graphs, these may additionally be projected
onto their vertices, giving the common bound C(r+n^{-1})^alpha.


Proof. 
Partition the plane into squares of side eta. For each square with
positive mass choose a nearby point of K and, by thickness, a continuum
of diameter geeta within distance Ceta of the square. Such a
continuum supports a probability measure nu_S with
[

]
To construct it, project onto a coordinate axis on which the diameter is
at least eta/sqrt2 and lift normalized Lebesgue measure on the
projection interval by choosing the point with smallest remaining
coordinate in each fiber. Compactness makes this selection Borel. The
projection of a disk of radius r has length at most 2r.

Move the mass of each square to this measure. If r<eta, at most
boundedly many chosen continua meet a specified r-ball, since each is
within Ceta of its assigned square. The resulting mass is at most
CMeta^alpha(r/eta)le CMr^alpha. For rgeeta use the original
mass bound in the Ceta enlargement. This proves the measure assertion
and the displacement bound. Compare each trace value with its eta
average using (t:trace-rate). For paired points the difference of
their averages is at most Cetafint_{B(x,Ceta)}|nabla u|.
Integrating as in the preceding proof gives (t:transport-trace).

For the final assertion take eta_n↓0 larger than both the
Hausdorff distance and a fixed multiple of n^{-1}. Perform the same
construction on K_n at scale eta_n and project its measures onto
vertices at distance O(n^{-1}). Before projection the measures are
genuinely Frostman at every scale. Projection changes the mass bound to
C(r+n^{-1})^alpha and does not change the weak limit.



## Weighted anchoring errors at observed sites

The reason observed values cannot be moved by an arbitrary spatial
displacement is that they are pinned: at each observed site we know the
exact value or at least have a uniform bound, and we can use that bound
directly. The next estimate turns those pin values into a decay rate
against a spread test measure.

[Weighted anchor errors]
Let q>1, and let S_n be retained observed vertices with positive
weights w_x. Suppose
[
 sum_{xin S_n}w_x|z_x|^2le E_n,
  n^{-2}sum_{xin S_n}w_x^{-q}le C_q.
]
For a probability measure mu_n on these vertices with
a_n=max_xmu_n({x}),

 sum_xmu_n({x})|z_x|
 le C_q E_n^{1/2}n^{1/q}a_n^{(1+1/q)/2}.

In particular, if a_nle C(ell_n/n)^alpha, the bound tends to zero
in expectation when sup_nE E_n<∞ and q>(2-alpha)/alpha. The
deterministic inverse-weight bound may hold outside exceptional events,
provided their contributions to the displayed quantities tend to zero.


Proof. 
Cauchy--Schwarz gives the upper bound
E_n^{1/2}(sum_xmu_n(x)^2/w_x)^{1/2}. H"older and
sum_xmu_n(x)=1 give
[

 le (sum_xw_x^{-q})^{1/q}
      (sum_xmu_n(x)^{2q/(q-1)})^{(q-1)/q}

The resulting power of n is -alpha/2+(2-alpha)/(2q)<0.
Polylogarithmic factors do not affect convergence, and
E E_n^{1/2}le(E E_n)^{1/2}.


## The trace proposition

[Varying observed sets and filled values]

Let K_n have the mesh thickness property with common constants and
converge in Hausdorff distance to K. Let their mesh vertices carry
random values z_n, and let U_n be random piecewise affine extensions
on Q^*. Fix 3/2<p<2. Assume the following bounds, uniformly in n:
 E|U_n|_{W^{1,p}}le C, and the means E U_n have a
      subsequence converging strongly in L^p to u;

      Lemma~t:anchors for some q>3, with E E_nle C;

retained vertices by the map in the hypotheses. The moved measures may
depend on the sample; they obey (t:coarse-frostman) with the same
deterministic constants. Lemma~t:anchors makes their integrals of

The trace proposition says a limit field vanishes on the closed observed

that determines the response as a Dirichlet Poisson solution on the slit

restriction of u to V belongs to H^1_0(V).

zero. The Poincar'e ineuality inherited from Q gives existence. Its
zero extension belongs to W^{1,p}(Q^*). Approximating in H^1 by
functions in C_c^∞(V) and using Lemma~t:frostman proves that
v has zero 1/2-Frostman trace on K.

Now consider w=u-v. Then w is harmonic in V and has zero

## Consistency of the tilt transfer

For completeness, we record why the exponential moment of the tilt
transfers the whole apparatus uniformly in s and in f. This is not a
new argument, but a checklist of the dependencies.

The key point is that any subset of a high-probability event retains the
good probability under the tilt up to the factor |e^{tX_{n,s}(f)}|_2.
By f:tilts, that factor is bounded for |t| small; the smallness
threshold depends only on the ambient average and cluster moments, both
of which are controlled uniformly in the signed exterior data and the
sign-wall shapes. Since the entire finite-volume proof behind
Theorem~c:comparison uses only these objects, every conclusion of
that theorem holds for the tilted measure with constants changed by a
fixed factor. In particular the moment bound, the energy bound, and the
dissipations in (c:energy-bound) survive without adjustment.

The sign-string conditioning plays a single role in all this: it fixes
the values of the ``wall" summands in (c:energy) on a linear set of
the polytope. No other part of the construction depends on the shape of
the strings. Hence the tilt transfer is uniform over the string family.
This is the only place where an exact-value observation is used in the
trace argument; every other exact-value use in this paper goes through
Lemma~c:freezing, whose annealed interpretation is made explicit
in its statement.


# Conditional Covariance on the Observed Set


The trace analysis identifies the mean response on the slit domain
Q∖ K. The same apparatus identifies the fluctuations
around that mean, i.e. the conditional covariance of the field given
the sign transcript observed on K. Since each sign observation
corresponds to a linear constraint on the polytope, and the trace
argument rules out the observed heights escaping to zero width, the
remaining conditional law is a uniform model of the same type as before,
just on the slit domain. The cell field limit then gives the conditional
covariance.

## Uniform covariance error

[Conditional covariance]
Fix a bounded smooth region Q, a compact family

## Continuity of the Green form

[Green continuity for this class]
If compact sets K_n containing ∂ Q satisfy the common
thickness condition and tend in Hausdorff distance to K, then
[
 < f_n,G_{Q∖ K_n}g_n>
 →< f,G_{Q∖ K}g>
]
whenever bounded smooth tests f_n,g_n converge uniformly to f,g.
The same assertion holds for mesh-thick approximants.


Proof. 
Put v_n=G_{Q∖ K_n}g_n and extend by zero. Their variational
identities and the Poincar'e ineuality on Q give a common H^1
bound. After taking a subsequence they converge strongly in L^2 and
weakly in H^1 to v. Every compact subset of Q∖ K is
eventually in Q∖ K_n, so -Delta v=g there. Since
v=0 on K_n by construction and K_n Hausdorff-converges to K,
v=0 on K by the trace theorem
(Theorem~t:trace applied to v with the bounded source g).
Hence v=G_{Q∖ K}g by uniqueness. The pairing converges
because v_nin H^1(Q) are uniformly bounded and f_n are uniformly
bounded smooth tests. For mesh-thick approximants, replace
K_n with a fixed Hausdorff-close subset of K with the common
thickness constants and use Lemma~t:transport.


## Transfer through a random kernel

A random observed signed string is not itself a deterministic slit
domain. What matters is that, conditional on the transcript, the field
law is a uniform model of the same type on a random slit domain, with
the conditional-kernel property (t:kernel) below. The next
statement is the transfer from the conditional covariance to the random
observed-set covariance.

[Transfer through an exact conditional kernel]

Let S_n take values in a space S_n of signed transcripts,
and suppose

 Law(h_nmid S_n=s)=P_{n,s}
 quadhbox{for almost every }s.

Then, for fixed tests or compact families as in Theorem~t:covariance,
the conditional covariance differs in L^1 from
sigma^2< f,G_{V_{n,S_n}}g> by o(1).

If K_{n,S_n} converges in distribution to K, these random Green
forms converge in distribution and in expectation to those on
Q∖ K. Convergence in L^1 to a limiting random form
additionally requires that the sets converge in probability on a common
probability space.


Proof. 
Condition on S_n and apply the deterministic uniform error
(t:uniform-covariance); its expectation is no larger than that
error. Lemma~t:green-continuity gives convergence of the random
Green forms. They are uniformly bounded for bounded tests, because the
Dirichlet Green quadratic form of every subdomain is bounded above by
that of Q. This also gives convergence of expectations and the stated
L^1 assertion under a common coupling.


For an exploration that queries signs and makes every next query or
stopping decision from its previous answers and independent
randomness, the full transcript has the conditional-kernel property
(t:kernel): its decision indicators introduce no factor depending
on an unqueried height. Disintegrating the finite-dimensional Lebesgue
density therefore leaves exactly the uniform density with the recorded
signs imposed. An exact-value exploration gives instead the
corresponding frozen-value kernel; it is covered only when the
finite-volume estimates have been proved for that class. An arbitrary
field-dependent selection of a string need not satisfy
(t:kernel). For example a label chosen by the sign of a standard
Gaussian variable changes its conditional variance from one to
1-2/pi, although either deterministic label could have been assigned
the original reference kernel. Uniform estimates for reference kernels
do not identify the law of such a selection.


## Local boundary variance

The barrier estimates of Section~sec:geometry need one further
consequence of Theorem~t:covariance: a bounded test supported in
a small neighborhood of a smooth piece of K has bounded conditional
variance, uniformly in the transcript, at least if the test region is a
fixed positive fraction of the window.

[Local boundary variance]
In the setting of Theorem~t:covariance, let Wsubset Q be a
bounded open window of diameter r>0 with dist(W,∂ Q)>0.
Let fin C_c^∞(W) be a real test supported in W. Let Bsubset
∂ Kcap W be a smooth Jordan arc of length ge cr with
positive clearance from ∂ W, and suppose f is supported at
positive distance from ∂ W∖ B. Then

 Var_{n,s}(X_n(f)midsigma_n(s))
 le C_{r_0}sigma^2 r,|f|_∞^2 + o(1),

uniformly in n, s, and the observed transcript, with the o(1)
term going to zero as nto∞ after the other parameters are fixed.


Proof. 
Apply Theorem~t:covariance formally with the test f paired
against itself: the conditional variance is the conditional covariance
of f with itself. Uniform (t:uniform-covariance) bounds the
difference between the conditional variance and
sigma^2< f,G_{Q∖ K}f> by a vanishing term.
Near the smooth arc piece B, the Dirichlet Green of Q∖ K
is comparable to that of the half-plane restricted to B (the
thickness condition of K rules out a thinner slit geometry), and the
half-plane Dirichlet Green kernel distinguishes two-points by a fixed
logarithmic potential. Integrating a bounded test supported in a
diameter-r window against itself gives a bound proportional to
r|f|_∞^2, with the constant depending only on r_0 and on the
fixed clearances.


# Feasible Shifts and Volume Distortion

The barrier estimates in the interface section need a perturbation
argument: to force the interface to avoid a prescribed barrier, we
raise the surface near that barrier, propagate the raise through
near-contact clusters, and check that the raise does not distort the
underlying measure beyond a controlled multiplicative factor. The
difficulty is that the raise has to be feasible for the current
polytope, not merely large.

All heights are in lattice units, and the reference measure
is normalized Lebesgue on a positive-volume conditional height
polytope. Conditioning specifies signs along oriented strands and may
include the bounded fixed data allowed in
Corollary~f:conditioned-sparse. Sites with specified signs are
left unchanged by the map. The sparse edge estimates of that corollary
apply also to zero-pinned laws by taking no sign strings.

## Near-contact cluster moments

Fix a small threshold s_*in(0,1/2). The near-contact graph
G_{s_*} joins neighboring free vertices whose heights differ by at
least 1-s_*. Its connected components are the near-contact clusters,
of size H_x at a free vertex x in the cluster.

The sparse box cover of f:sparse and f:conditioned-sparse
implies that the near-contact clusters have bounded moments.
Explicitly, use the fixed-box cover with central-edge tests: a good
box has slack on every edge incident to its central box, so a nontrivial
near-contact component can occupy only bad boxes, apart from a bounded
enlargement at its endpoints. A connected collection of m occupied
boxes contains a separated subcollection of at least m/C boxes, and
its probability is at most rho^{m/C} by (f:animal). Taking
rho smaller than the reciprocal of the lattice-animal counting
constant gives an exponential tail for the number of occupied boxes.
The conclusion is

 sup_xE H_x^k le C_k
 quadhbox{for all }k<∞,

uniformly over the conditioning systems covered by
Corollary~f:conditioned-sparse. In particular, choosing
k>2/alpha gives

 Prob[max_x H_x > n^alpha]le C_k n^{2-alpha k}=o(1)
 (nto∞).

Deleting edges or restricting the graph preserves the bound. No
convergence rate from homogenisation enters this calculation.

## The determinant identity

[Tangential vector fields]
Let PsubsetR^N be a compact full-dimensional polytope with
normalized Lebesgue measure. Suppose u:PtoR^N is Lipschitz and
tangent to each facet of P. Then

 Edivu=0,
 E(divu)^2
 =Etr((Du)^2)
 leE|Du|_{HS}^2.



Proof. 
Facet-wise integration by parts with the tangency u· n_F=0
gives int_Pdivu=sum_Fint_F u· n_F=0. This
is the mean-zero identity. For the square, integrate by parts twice:
with u extended by zero outside P (the extension is Lipschitz
and tangent to facets, so it vanishes normal to them),
[
 int_P(divu)^2
 =-int_Psum_{i,j}u_i∂_i∂_j u_j
 =int_Psum_{i,j}∂_ju_i,∂_iu_j
 =int_Ptr((Du)^2).
]
The trace of the square of the matrix Du is at most |Du|_{HS}^2
by Cauchy--Schwarz on singular values (equality for symmetric matrices).


## The shift derivative

The perturbation we construct depends piecewise on a threshold
parameter s>0. For each edge e of the lattice and each
configuration h, let t_e(h) be the first value of s at which the
two endpoints of e become connected in the threshold-s near-contact
graph on the configuration h.

[Shift derivative bounds]
Let f(h) be a shift profile satisfying |f(x)|le L and

 |nabla f(e)|le L/n
 quadhbox{for every edge e whose endpoints are in different
 s_1-near-contact clusters,}

with f exactly constant on each s_1-cluster. Order equal thresholds
by a fixed tie rule. Define the candidate shift

the component prescription as s crosses t_e. Then, almost

entries of magnitude at most one. On a component of size H, each
entry of Du has magnitude at most CLH/n. Consequently

 |Du|_{op}le CLH_{max}^2/n,

 E|Du|_{HS}^2le C_L,
 
 E(divu)^2le C_L.



Proof. 
When s passes t_e, write J_e=v_{t_e+}-v_{t_e-} for the change of

(eq:shift-cluster-moments) and Lemma~lem:shift-divergence
gives (eq:shift-mean-bounds). The expectation bounds are under
the original conditional law; no derivative bound after additional
exploration is assumed.

## Direction preservation

The construction preserves the direction of a reinforcing shift. If
f is nonnegative on an s_1-component, its corrected shift is
nonnegative there, including when it is grounded. If positive and
negative prescription regions have separation of order n, the
components on G_n cannot connect them. This observation permits
opposite prescribed signs in separated corridors. A crossing
application must still establish its own initial event E and verify
that an o(1) error in the prescribed shift leaves its required strict
ineualities intact.


# Geometry of Crossings and Barriers

This section assembles the geometric inputs to the interface-limit
argument. The interface estimates in the Schramm--Sheffield and
Sheffield--Sun style work with deterministic crosscuts of the domain;
those crosscuts are provided by the conditioning, not by the model
itself. The conditioning is on signs adjacent to observed interface
strands, which are much thinner than the full surface. Each observation
is a sign on a vertex that also has a neighbor of the opposite sign, so
its height lies in [-1,1]; this is the rigidity that lets us use sign
strands.

The geometric statements below use the following convention. A
signed barrier is an oriented compact Jordan arc in the domain,
labelled positive or negative, at fixed positive macroscopic distance
from the marked points and the initial and final pieces of the interface.
The object is to show that continuations of the observed strands
avoid such barriers with a uniform positive probability even when many
barriers are placed simultaneously. The arguments follow sections
2.3 and 3.3--3.7 of [cite] closely, with the field inputs
supplied by the present work.

## Bounded insertion and pure-arc signs

[Bounded local insertion]
For every fixed L<∞ there is p_L>0 with the following property.
Condition on any admissible exact oriented strands. In a patch of diameter
at most L, at distance at most L from one of their tips or from
bounded Dirichlet boundary, prescribe a compatible sign pattern on at most
L additional vertices. The probability of that pattern is at least
p_L. In particular every feasible prescribed extension of at most L
interface steps has a probability bounded below by a constant depending
only on L. The assertion remains valid when other positive string sites
have additional upper bounds in (0,d], provided no prescribed new
positive site is one of those upper-bounded sites.


Proof. 
String heights have absolute value at most one. Lipschitzness therefore
bounds heights in the patch by a deterministic M(L). Enclose it in a
larger fixed box and use Corollary~f:conditioned-sparse with a
long-distance deficit greater than 2M+20; the small linear-slack
version gives this by enlarging the fixed box. The good-shell event has
probability at least 1/2, uniformly in the original strand conditioning.

Fix such a shell and give the new vertices their requested values pm b
with 0<b<1/4. Clip any feasible filling between the lower and upper
distance cones of these prescriptions. At each other sign-constrained
vertex the clipping interval contains zero, since that vertex has graph
distance at least one from the prescribed vertices. Consequently clipping
preserves its sign. Since the clipping interval contains zero, it also
preserves every interval [0,d] without making b depend on d, and
preserves fixed exterior values of absolute value at most 1/2. The
shell remains unchanged because of the long-distance deficit.

Let g be the resulting filling. For every original filling h, the
map h|-> th+(1-t)g with 0<t<b/(2(M+b)) gives the required signs
on the patch. Convexity preserves all old constraints. If the number of
free box coordinates is N, its Jacobian is t^N; both N and t
depend only on the fixed patch and box. Integrating the conditional
volume bound over good shells proves the result. For an interface
extension prescribe signs on the two sides of the finitely many edges.


[Pure-arc sign]
In a local sector with only positive string boundary, the limiting
bounded harmonic conditional mean has Brownian boundary trace at least
c>0, away from endpoints and incompatible sides. On a negative sector
its trace is at most -c. On an exterior boundary arc alone the trace is
its prescribed value. These statements are sequentially uniform at
diverging lattice scales.


Proof. 
The conditional law is log-concave and monotone in its boundary data and
in its one-sided constraints. Lemma~g:insertion gives a uniform
positive chance that a tested positive string height exceeds a fixed
b>0, even after other positive sites have been restricted to (0,d].
A one-dimensional log-concave density on [0,1] with this tail bound
assigns at most Cd mass to [0,d]. One proof integrates the chord
lower bounds for its logarithm between a point in [0,d] and a point
carrying the prescribed tail mass. So for small fixed d>0, the tested
height exceeds d with conditional probability at least 1/2.
Monotonicity makes the conditioning that all earlier indicators vanish
the worst one. Successive uniform couplings show that the positive
string heights dominate d times independent fair Bernoulli variables.

The lower bound c transfers to the harmonic trace by the same
subsequential compactness used in Sss:trace-inputs. At a negative
access the sign flips and the same argument gives -c. On an exterior
boundary arc alone the trace is the prescribed datum, since no interface
sample is adjacent.


## The complete-crosscut upper bound

[The complete-crosscut upper bound]
For a complete single crosscut, the positive-side conditional mean is at
most 1/2, and the negative-side conditional mean is at least -1/2.
This assertion is not made at an open interface tip.


Proof. 
Condition on all heights on the negative side. At a positive string
vertex, a crossing edge gives an upper bound at most one. Delete the
crossing edges and replace these bounds by independent walls [0,1].
This raises the conditional field by monotonicity. Raising the exterior
positive data to 1/2 raises it again. The resulting positive-component
law is invariant under h|-> 1-h, so its mean equals 1/2. Average
the comparison over the conditioned negative heights. The negative
statement follows by reflection. Disconnection of the two components by
the full crosscut justifies deleting exactly these crossing constraints.


## Exact-strand comparison

[Exact-strand comparison]
Fix concentric inner and outer disks with radii in a fixed ratio strictly
greater than one. Consider two base conditioning systems whose domains
contain the closed outer disk and have the same unconditioned lattice
graph there. All vertices constrained by the original strand
observations, additional walls, or Dirichlet data must be outside the
outer disk in both systems. Thus the entire outer disk, not just its
annulus, is free in the two base laws. Each system has anchored observed
or Dirichlet pieces of comparable diameter and distance outside this
disk. Let I specify a bounded collection of oriented strands all of
whose adjacent sign-constrained vertices lie in the inner disk. Then
[
 C^{-1}Prob_2(I)leProb_1(I)le CProb_2(I),
]
with a constant depending only on the fixed ratios and number of strands.
The graphs need agree only inside the outer disk. The assertion does not
compare laws with different pre-existing inner observations.


Proof. 
Concatenate the coordinatewise couplings of Proposition~f:bounded
on the entire outer disk. The two conditional laws differ only in their
bounded exterior data and their pin or Dirichlet values outside the outer
disk. Since the free region is identical, the couplings on the outer
disk preserve the class of bounded-data conditional laws with the
indicated gradients. Both sides of the conditioning have bounded
Lipschitz constants, so the coordinate coupling is a bi-Lipschitz map
preserving the sign constraints on the inner disk up to a fixed
multiplicative constant on volume; monotone mixtures and Markov's
ineuality on the free coordinates give the comparison. The constant
depends only on the fixed ratios, on the number of strands and their
lengths.


## The uniform barrier principle

The interface argument frequently presents us with a finite family of
prescribed barriers, each a signed arc, and asks for a uniform positive
probability of avoiding all of them simultaneously. The next two
statements provide it.

[High crossing from one side]
Let S be a signed straight crosscut of a rectangle, and let
ext be a fixed positive-distance enlargement. Fix
Hin(0,∞). Suppose the barrier endpoints have clearance
theta R from the boundary of the corridor for a fixed
theta>0. Then, uniformly in the conditioning and in R, the
probability that the interface crosses S without going above
+H is at most p(H,theta)<1 with p(H,theta)↓0 as
Hto∞.


Proof. 
Apply the complete-crosscut upper bound of Lemma~g:upper-half
along the crosscut. A crossing without ever exceeding +H leaves an
accessible sign pattern on the negative side of the crosscut whose
conditional mean is at most 1/2, so any positive excursion above H
before crossing contributes a corresponding positive amount to the
positive-side trace. The upper bound p(H,theta) follows from
monotonicity and f:conditioned-sparse; the threshold theta
enters only through the fixed clearance, so the bound is uniform in R.


[Uniform barriers]
For every theta>0 and m<∞ there is p=p(theta,m)>0 such
that the following holds at every lattice scale. Condition on any
admissible collection K of exact oriented strands. For a compatible
collection of at most m (theta,R)-barriers, the probability that
all continuations of the observed strands avoid these barriers is at
least p. Endpoints on pure exterior arcs are allowed provided, for a
fixed lambda_*>0, their positive attachment arcs have prescribed
heights at least lambda_*, and their negative attachment arcs have
prescribed heights at most -lambda_*. For these barriers p may also
depend on lambda_*. Interior attachment constants do not depend on
lambda_*. For diverging-scale applications assume local thickness of
the observed set in the endpoint testing windows: after restriction to a
generous square of side comparable to theta R around such a window,
every retained observed component either meets the square's rim or has
diameter at least c(theta)R. The nearby attachment component has
diameter at least c(theta)R. Arbitrarily many isolated microscopic
observed islands in these windows are not included.


Proof. 
We first treat Rto∞. Away from endpoint pockets the barrier has
clearance theta R/5. A grid of mesh eta R/100 with etalltheta
meets its bounded thickening in at most Cmeta^{-2} cells. Circuits
and crossings in those cells form a fence around the barrier; fill
bounded holes in the thickening before taking the outer fence. Opposite
signs have disjoint enlarged supports. The grid crossings and circuits
can be chosen in overlapping rectangles so that, together with their
endpoint attachments, they form a connected fence. Their number depends
on eta,m, not on the length or microscopic oscillation of the barrier.
We first construct the attachments to the pure boundary, then force the
interior fence using Lemma~g:high-crossing and
Lemma~g:shifts.

Each interior crossing rectangle has a fixed enlargement contained in
the free part of the corridor, by the clearance theta R/5. In the
endpoint construction only rectangles in the free core are used, with
enlargements smaller than their distance from the removed boundary
neighborhood. Attachment across that neighborhood is a separate collar
problem treated next.

We verify the endpoint attachments, including irregular pockets. In the
annulus with radii 5theta R/8 and 7theta R/8 about an endpoint z,
delete the union Q of coarse cells meeting the 2eta R-neighborhood
of the conditioned boundary. Keep the component containing the barrier
as a core. On either side there is a collar between the core and the
pure boundary. Its central testing arc has diameter at least
theta R/32, distance O(eta R) to that boundary, and access
distance at least theta R/32 to other conditioned boundary or to
either lateral cutoff. These assertions follow from the radial-pocket
and clearance conditions: a path violating the last assertion would
either leave the annulus or cross the barrier where its clearance is at
least theta R/5. Corner contacts are resolved by a deterministic
perturbation of at most one coarse cell.

Explore, within the collar only, the positive cluster attached to its
pure positive boundary. Query a vertex only from an already discovered
positive neighbor. A failed query is negative, and its height is bounded
in absolute value by one. On the negative-crossing event, the
core-facing failed queries contain a negative frontier joining the two
lateral cutoffs and screening the testing cells. The queried positive
vertices lie behind that frontier. This is an adapted exploration, not a
crossing selected after the full field is seen.

The testing measure is within Ceta R of F: a testing cell has a
path of that diameter to its nearest pure boundary piece, and the
screening frontier must intersect this path. Transport its mass to F
and then past the polylogarithmic holes to retained zero vertices. The
frontier is connected between the lateral cutoffs and has diameter
comparable to theta R, so this transport has the common Frostman
bounds. The samplewise averaging and anchor estimates of
Lemmas~t:frostman--t:anchors, in units of theta R, give,
for some beta>0,
[
 limsup_{Rto∞}E[|<mu,U>|^2mid E]
 le C_{p_*}(eta/theta)^{2beta}.
]
All geometry and eta>0 are fixed before this lattice-scale limit. The
original--filled L^1 estimate transfers the tested first moment at that
fixed scale. Since hle h^0+u and the comparison kernel has zero mean
conditional on the transcript,
[
 limsup_{Rto∞}E[<mu,h>mid E]
 le C_{p_*}(eta/theta)^beta.
]
This is not a nonpositive bound at fixed eta; its right side vanishes
only afterwards as eta/theta↓0. No Poisson equation or
Poisson uniqueness theorem is applied to the comparison tail. For a
variable X with variance v,
|E[Xmid E]-E X|le√(v/Prob(E)). Thus the collar-crossing
probability tends to zero. The reflected argument treats a negative
pocket.

It remains to join these attachments by the interior fence. Choose eta
so that the collar failures together have probability less than 1/10
for large R. In each positive fence rectangle, planar crossing duality
gives a path with heights greater than -H unless there is a transverse
path with heights at most -H. Apply Lemma~g:high-crossing to the
reflected field to exclude the latter event. The reflected construction
supplies paths with heights less than H in negative rectangles. The
same crossing construction in the finitely many overlapping rectangles
supplies any required circuits. Choose fixed H sufficiently large that
all these threshold-crossing failures together have probability less
than 1/10. The successful threshold crossings and collar attachments
therefore occur together on an event E_{fence} of probability at
least 4/5.

The prescribed shift is deterministic given the conditioned geometry: set
it equal to H+1 on each positive crossing rectangle and to -(H+1) on
each negative crossing rectangle. Interpolate to zero within their fixed
free enlargements, keeping zero at all observed sites. The separated
supports give a bounded prescription with edge differences at most
C(H,theta,eta)/R, as required by Proposition~g:shifts. Keep
its sign reinforcing on the corresponding collar. The corrected shift
differs from this prescription by o(1), so it turns each threshold path
into a strict sign path. It also preserves the successful collar
attachments: the compatibility and clearance conditions give internal
diameter distance at least cR from a collar or prescription support of
one sign to a prescription support of the other. The near-contact
components used by the shift construction have diameter o(R) in the
free graph. A component meeting a positive collar therefore contains no
negative prescription, and its averaged shift is nonnegative; the
negative case is reflected. Grounded components have zero shift.

All of theta,eta,H are fixed before Rto∞. Apply
Proposition~g:shifts once to E_{fence}. It gives a uniformly
positive probability for the connected sign fence under the original
law. That fence prevents every continuation from entering the protected
side. This proves the lower bound above a fixed threshold
R_0(theta,m).

## Hookup probability

Fix a positive exterior datum for the two accessible signs, and
one-sided uniform bounds at every retained observed site, symmetric
around zero with amplitude lambda_*in(0,1/2]. Let (GB) denote the
combined assumptions:
(GB) all retained observations lie in [-1,1], all retained pins have
absolute value at most 1/2, and the exterior arcs of the interface are
labelled consistently. Under (GB) the comparison and trace inputs of
Ssec:conditional-covariance apply, and the conditional field
covariance is controlled.

[Hookup bounds]
Assume (GB). Fix R>0 and an inner landing side sigma. Suppose the
inner and outer records Θ_r,Phi_R each have quality at least
q>0, with r and R in a fixed compact ratio. Then, uniformly in the
records and in the lattice realization,

 Prob(Z^sigmamid Phi_R,Θ_r)le C q^{-alpha},

and

 Prob(Z^sigmamid Phi_R,Θ_r)ge c q^alpha/log R,

where C,c>0 and alpha>0 depend only on the fixed scale geometry,
upon lambda_*, and on the constants in (GB). The exponent alpha is
the same in both bounds.


Proof. 
The upper bound follows by the point-versus-continuum estimate from
the harmonic measure of a planar Brownian walk hitting a bounded arc.
Conditioning on Phi_R and Θ_r fixes the sampled interface and
its near neighborhood. The remaining free part of the surface is a
bounded-data submodel; the walk observes only its own interface
positions. Under (GB) the interface is bounded in [-1,1] on the
lamination of observed sites; the harmonic access structure sampled from
v is that of a bounded harmonic function. By Theorem~t:covariance
the field fluctuations are Gaussian with covariance sigma^2 times the
Dirichlet Green of the remaining domain, and the interface is a sample
of the corresponding SLE_{16/sigma^2} curve if the underlying
integrability holds. In either case the hit probability of a bounded arc
B_0 of length sim! R q is at most C q^{1} times the hit
probability of any arc of length R containing it, uniformly in the
field. This yields (g:hookup-upper) by rescaling.

The lower bound uses the same harmonic-access construction with the
inverse estimate. By Lemma~g:barriers, the sampled interface
makes contact with a bounded arc of length comparable to Rq on the
band of rays from v with a probability bounded below by c q^alpha.
The exponenent alpha comes from the geometry of the collar
attachments in Lemma~g:barriers; it is a fixed positive number
depending only on fixed scale ratios. Combined with the standard
two-dimensional Green function estimate for a point-vs-continuum
access, this yields (g:hookup-lower).

The dependence on lambda_* enters only through the two ends of the
bounds, through the well-definedness of Z^sigma (a sample with
lambda_*-bounded interfaces has Z^sigma positive probability).


## Separation under a harmonic landing

[Separation under a harmonic landing]
For every eps>0 there is c(eps)>0 such that, for all sufficiently
large R,

 Prob(
 Q(Phi_{3R})wedge Q(Θ_{2R})>c(eps)
 mid Phi_{4R},Θ_R,Z^sigma)>1-eps

uniformly in all admissible records for which the conditioning has
positive probability. The observer and original boundary are outside
the disk of radius 6R.


Proof. 
First suppose the inner quality is bounded below. Expose the external
arms inward, taking r_{j+1}=(1-10Q_j)r_j, where Q_j is their
quality or zero if the hookup has become impossible. Conditional on
the current records, but not on Z^sigma, there are constants
c_0,c_1>0 such that

 Prob(Q_{j+1}ge (2Q_j)wedge 1/100midPhi_{r_j},Θ_R)
       &ge c_0, 
 Prob(Q_{j+2}=0midPhi_{r_j},Θ_R,Q_{j+1}<2Q_j)
       &ge c_1. 

For (G3), steer the two interface tips and the walk into three separated
corridors of size r_jQ_j. Their annulus is disjoint from the fixed
inner observations. Proposition~g:barriers and the local Harnack
bound for the walk bridge give the required probability whenever r_jQ_j
exceeds a fixed threshold. Below that threshold the finite step
construction of Lemma~g:insertion applies. The steering sites lie
within a bounded distance of the existing tips.

For (G4), let F_j denote the sigma-field of the current
records at scale r_j. Put L={Q_{j+2}=0} and M_1 the event that
the walk bridge comes within (2Q_jr_{j+2})wedge|x-y| of the tips before
any escape. The field-free stopped-walk estimate in
cite[proof of (3.38), case i=1]{SSdiscrete} gives

 Prob(Lcap M_1midF_j) cProb(M_1midF_j).

The conditioning and geometric hypotheses for that estimate are:
after fixing ext_{r'}gamma, stop the walk at its first
visit within a=(2Q_jr')wedge|x-y| of either tip. Reveal only the walk
up to this first approach. The conditioned inner reversed record leaves
the first walk bridge as a simple walk transformed to hit its joining
vertex before zero. Harnack compares this transform with ordinary walk
by fixed factors in the approach neighborhood: the fixed inner record is
at distance comparable to R, whereas ale 2Q_jr'le r'/50. The
approached strand extends a distance comparable to a, the other tip is
at distance at least a, and the preceding annular width is at least
5a. These are exactly the hypotheses of the source's first-approach
walk argument. Its strong Markov and planar hitting estimate have a
numerical lower bound, independent of a/r_j, and force a strand hit
before the next quality-controlled annulus is passed. Integrating this
stopped-record estimate gives (g:short-stopped). In particular one
must not additionally fix the later walk segment after it has escaped
from this close approach; a uniform estimate for each such completed
record would be false.

Iterating (G3), followed by Lemma~g:hookup, shows that a quality
q>0 with a fixed annular buffer remaining has

 Prob(Z^sigmamidhbox{current records})
       ge c q^alpha/log R,

for a fixed alpha>0. The radial loss while doubling the quality is
bounded by a geometric sum. Let M_n count visits to
(2^{-n},2^{1-n}]. By (G4), survival for 2m such visits has
unconditioned probability at most (1-c_1)^m. Multiply by
(g:hookup-upper) and divide by (g:quality-lower) at the
first visit to the band:

 Prob(M_n>2mmidPhi_{4R},Θ_R,Z^sigma)
       le C c_0^{-n}(1-c_1)^m.

For a sufficiently large fixed A, the bounds in (g:quality-band)
with m=An have a geometric tail in n. Choose N so that their sum
over nge N is less than eps/3, and so that
[
 C A sum_{nge N} n 2^{-n} < 1/2.
]
Here C bounds the radial loss: one visit to the n-th band costs at
most 10·4R·2^{1-n}, since r_j-r_{j+1}=10Q_jr_j. Outside the
exceptional event in (g:quality-band), there are at most 2An
such visits. Thus, while the quality stays below 2^{-N}, the total
radial loss is less than R/2. With probability at least 1-eps/3, a
fixed positive quality is therefore attained before the radius falls
from 4R to 7R/2.

To rule out a subsequent collapse at 3R, use the two mechanisms in
cite[proof of Lemma~3.15, equations (3.40)--(3.42)]{SSdiscrete}.
Condition on the first record at which that positive quality is
attained, and reveal the external strands to radius 3R. If their tips
are within sR, their midpoint is now fixed. Expose the continuations
successively to barriers at radii 2sR,4sR,...,cR about that
midpoint. Hookup requires breaching each barrier.
Proposition~g:barriers bounds each conditional breach probability
by 1-p; the number of scales is comparable to log(1/s), giving
Cs^beta for a fixed beta>0. Fixing the completed field while
leaving the walk bridge unexposed supplies the additional upper factor
C/log R from (g:hookup-upper). For a walk--strand approach, fix
ext_{3R}gamma and reveal the walk only to its first
sR-approach to that strand. Beurling gives Cs^zeta, for a fixed
zeta>0, for reaching radius 5R/2 without a hit. At its first
entrance there, the remaining unexposed bridge again contributes
C/log R. Hence the joint probability of late collapse and hookup is
at most C(s^beta+s^zeta)/log R. Divide by (g:quality-lower)
at the already attained positive quality, then take s small.

For general inner quality expose both records: increase the inner radius
by factor 1+10Q_j while decreasing the outer radius by 1-10Q_j,
using the smaller of their qualities. Until the target radii 2R and
3R, their annuli are disjoint. The same finite collection of compatible
barriers proves (G3)--(G4) for this minimum; independence of the fields
in the two annuli is not needed. The same band calculation and
late-collapse bound establish (g:separation-bound). The
conditional records in these steps are exactly the two-sided exploration
records used in cite[Lemma~3.15]{SSdiscrete}; their field part consists
solely of specified oriented strands.



## Comparison of sampled configurations

The reference-weight comparison is the sampling analogue of the barrier
principle. Given the inner record probability, we ask how it depends on
the outer record, and the answer is that it does not, up to a fixed
multiplier.

[Comparison of sampled configurations]

There are C<∞ and R_0<∞ such that the following holds
for Rge R_0. Take two domains containing the disk B_{6R}, with
observers outside that disk, and any two external records
phi,phi' at radius 4R having positive hookup probability. For
every event H determined by Θ_R, with the same specified
incident-edge label and landing side in the two experiments,

 C^{-1}Prob'(HmidPhi'_{4R}=phi',Z'^sigma)
 le Prob(HmidPhi_{4R}=phi,Z^sigma)
 le CProb'(HmidPhi'_{4R}=phi',Z'^sigma).

The constant is independent of R and of additional unused annuli
outside radius 4R.


Proof. 
Before hookup conditioning, the field observations in each
Phi_{3R} are only the signs adjacent to its two external arms,
supported outside radius 3R-O(1). The event specifying an oriented
beta_{2R}^sigma tests vertices inside radius 2R+O(1). Apply
Lemma~g:strand-comparison with inner disk of radius 9R/4 and
outer disk of radius 5R/2. Both base laws have no observations in
this outer disk, their graphs agree there, and their external arms
connect scale 3R to the original boundary outside scale 6R,
providing the anchors. Thus the inner oriented-strand laws are
comparable by a fixed factor under these different external records. At
this step no Θ-record has already been imposed in either base
law: the inner record is precisely the additional event being
compared. The reversed-walk law is also comparable by a fixed factor: a
walk from either entrance point has probability bounded below to visit
the other before reaching zero. Apply the strong Markov property at
that visit. The field and walk factors are independent before hookup.
Thus there is a reference weight p_{2R}(theta) for inner records such
that their probabilities given a record at radius 3R are comparable
to this weight.

Choose the quality threshold in Lemma~g:separation with error
1/2, and let X be the inner records at 2R exceeding it. Let
Q be the event that both the 2R inner and 3R outer
records exceed it. Separation holds even conditional on a specified
Θ_R=θ. Hence conditioning further on Q changes
the probability of this specified inner record by at most a fixed
factor. On Q, Bayes' formula, the reference-weight
comparison, and Lemma~g:hookup give
[
 Prob(Θ_{2R}=thetamidQ,Z^Q,Z^sigma,Phi_{3R})
 asymp frac{p_{2R}(theta)}{sum_{xiin X}p_{2R}(xi)}
 (thetain X).
]
Indeed the numerator before normalization is comparable to
p_{2R}(theta)/log R, and summing determines its normalization.
Average over Phi_{3R}, then sum over all thetain X whose
restriction to radius R is θ. The resulting reference ratio
is independent of the external experiment, proving
(g:sample-comparison-bound) first for single records, then for
events. Conditioning on data farther out and averaging over the radius
4R records preserves the same constant.


## Non-return and additional crossings

[No additional sampled excursions]
Let J(r,R)J(r,R) be the event that more than two disjoint interface
arcs cross from the disk B_r to ∂ B_R, or that the walk
exits B_R after first entering B_r and before its landing at zero.
For every eps>0 there are A(eps)<∞ and r_0(eps)<∞
such that

 Prob(J(r,R)J(r,R)midPhi_R,Θ_r,Z^sigma)<eps

when rge r_0(eps), R>A(eps)r, the observer and boundary are
outside B_{4R}, and the conditioning has positive probability. The
same holds, with a harmless change of eps, conditioned only on
Phi_R and Z={S_tau=0}.


Proof. 
Suppose, for a contradiction along a sequence with the desired
coefficients, that a fixed event of probability p_0>0 produced a
third crossing. Expose only the first two crossings as Θ_r and
Phi_R. The third crossing, together with the first two, provides
three disjoint topologically separated arcs of the interface, each at
positive macroscopic distance from the observer and from the exterior
boundary.

Use the barrier fence of Lemma~g:barriers. Fix the outer record
Phi_R and the inner record Θ_r. Reveal only a bounded
enlargement of them, and choose fixed corridors of width comparable to
r in the annulus that any third crossing would have to occupy. The
prescribed fences in those corridors, together with the endpoint
attachments, form an event E_{fence} on which
Proposition~g:barriers asserts a uniform lower bound on the
probability of avoiding the fences. The complement of E_{fence} in
the present setting is a bounded enlargement of the two original
crossings, together with the collar attachments guaranteed by
Proposition~g:barriers, so its contribution to Prob(J...)
is at most the small probability from the barrier escape. This yields a
contradiction to the assumption that a third crossing occurs with
nonvanishing probability on the sampled configuration, when R/r is
larger than a fixed multiple of 1/eps.

The walk statement is identical: a walk exit from B_R after entering
B_r, before landing, would create a fourth crossing of the annulus
unless the walk's landing is on a third interface arc. The same fence
construction rules this out.



## Landing clearances from the original boundary and observer


The survival estimate of the next subsection requires the walk not to
land too close to the original boundary or observer. The two lemmas
below establish this.

[Clearance from the original boundary]

Let S be an independent full walk from v, stopped only on the
original boundary at tau_D, and let gamma be the completed
interface. Put d=dist(v,∂ D) and
[
 M={S_j: j<tau_D, S_j is adjacent to gamma}.
]
Under (GB), uniformly over domains, observers, and the
indicated exterior data,

 lim_{eta↓0}limsup_{dto∞}
 Prob(dist(M,∂ D)<eta d)=0.

The distance of the empty set is infinity. In particular the assertion
holds for a terminal harmonic landing, or simultaneously for every
old-prefix landing of this walk that is not on ∂ D.


Proof. 
Adapt the full-walk argument of cite[Lemma~3.23, especially
equations (3.53) and (3.55)]{SSdiscrete}. First establish pure-arc
repulsion. For yin∂_+, choose any deterministic lower bound
ble d_D(y,∂_-). At successive fixed-ratio scales between b
and r, take the component of B(y,rho)cap D accessible from y
and its circular crosscut separating y from ∂_-. The
clearance construction used in Lemma~g:extra supplies a barrier
attached to the two positive boundary pieces on either side of y.
Its endpoint pockets stay on ∂_+, since their diameter is
smaller than b. Explore the interface only up to the next outer
crosscut. Its exact sign transcript leaves the inner barrier compatible,
so Proposition~g:barriers gives a fixed conditional chance of
avoiding this crosscut and every smaller access to y. Iterating, then
reflecting for yin∂_-, gives constants C,zeta>0 such that

 Prob{d_D(y,gamma)<r}le C(r/b)^zeta+o_d(1)

when r,b are fixed positive multiples of d and r<b. The lattice
error is uniform for these fixed multiples. This is the deterministic
crosscut argument of cite[Theorem~3.22]{SSdiscrete}, with its field
input replaced by our exterior version of Proposition~g:barriers.

Fix r=eta d, and condition on gamma. At the first walk visit to
M at distance less than r from ∂ D, the strong Markov
property and the planar walk estimate give a fixed probability of
exiting D in the ball of radius 2r+O(1) about a closest boundary
point. One can force the walk to surround that point inside this ball,
which forces the exit. Its continuation and the adjacent interface edge
then give an internal path of diameter at most 4r+O(1) from the exit
Y=S_{tau_D} to gamma. Consequently

 Prob{dist(M,∂ D)<r}
 le CProb{d_D(Y,gamma)<4r+O(1)}.

Here Y, unlike the interface landing, is independent of gamma.
Conditional on Y, apply (GB2) whenever its internal distance to
the opposite arc exceeds s=sqrteta,d, using the deterministic
lower bound b=s in that estimate. This gives Ceta^{zeta/2}+o_d(1),
which tends to zero along eta↓0 and then dto∞.


[Clearance from the observer]
Let d=dist(v,∂ D). Under (GB), for
every eta>0 there is p_{obs}(eta)>0 such that

 liminf_{dto∞}Prob(d_D(Y,v)>eta d)ge p_{obs}(eta),

where Y=S_{tau_D} is the exit of the walk from D. The same holds
simultaneously for every old-prefix landing of this walk not equal to

Proof. 
Apply the nested shielding argument of cite[Lemma~3.24]{SSdiscrete},
using Proposition~g:barriers. Expose the first interface approach
to the circles centered at v of radii 2^{-j-1}d. Given its exact

## Survival of a stopped harmonic landing

[Survival of a stopped harmonic landing]
Let T be any stopping time for the filtration generated by oriented
initial interface pieces, K=gamma[0,T], and
d=dist(v,∂ D). Let X_T be the first landing
of an independent full walk S on ∂ D(K); let Y be its
landing for the completed interface. For every eps>0 there are
p>0, d_0<∞ such that

 Prob(Prob(Y=X_Tmid K,S)<p)<eps
 (d>d_0),

uniformly over domains, observers, and stopping times under (GB).


Proof. 
Fix eps>0 and consider the three failure modes of X_T.
 X_T within distance eta d of ∂ D. By
Lemma~g:boundary-landing (applied with r=eta d) this has
probability at most eps/4 for small eta.
 X_T within distance eta d of v. By
Lemma~g:observer-landing this has probability at most eps/4 for
small eta.
 X_T within distance eps R of the old tip gamma(T).
By the planar walk hitting estimate (point--versus--continuum, the same
argument as in (g:hookup-upper)) this has probability at most
eps/4 for large R/eps.

Let F denote the retained event where none of the three holds.
Prob(F)ge1-3eps/4.

Condition now on F and on K and S. Then X_T is at distance at
least eta d from ∂ D and v, and at distance at least eps R
from the old tip. Apply Lemma~g:extra with radii (r,R)=(eps R/2, R)
(taking eps smaller if necessary so R/(eps R/2)=2/eps is bigger
than the required A(eps)). The complement of the exceptional event
J is measurable from the conditioning (K,S); on it, the
completed interface and the stopped interface differ only through a
bounded number of extra crossings of the annulus B_R∖ B_{eps R/2}.
Those crossings cannot reach B(X_T,eps R/2), since any such reach would
place a third crossing at macroscopic boundary distance from gamma[0,T]
and hence produce a third crossing of B_{eps R/2} from the outside,
contradicting J^c.

Consequently, on FcapJ^c, the completed interface and the
stopped interface agree on B(X_T,eps R/2), so Y=X_T as well. Hence

 Prob(Y=X_Tmid K,S)ge 1-eps/4

on FcapJ^c, which has probability at least 1-eps for
small eta and large R/eps.

Setting p=1-eps/4 and using that Prob(F^ccupJ)
gives (G9). The uniformity in domains, observers, and stopping
times comes from the uniformity of Lemmas~g:boundary-landing,
g:observer-landing, g:extra, and of the walk hitting estimate
used in the third failure mode.


## Walk non-return

[Walk non-return]
Under (GB) fix 0<r<R<d/10 and set rho=√(rR). Let
S be an independent full walk from v; let gamma be the completed
interface. Then

 Prob(S introduces a return to B_r after exiting 
 B_{2rho}, before hitting gamma)
 le C,b(R/r)+o_d(1),

where b(R/r)to0 as R/rto∞ and o_d(1)to0 as dto∞.
The bound is uniform over stopping signatures under (GB).


Proof. 
Decompose S at its successive visits to the annulus B_{2rho}∖ B_rho.
Between two such visits, S either exits B_R or does not. If it does
not exit B_R, then S makes a bounded-level excursion; the probability
of any such excursion reaching B_r while remaining inside B_R is at
most C,((rho)/R)^beta for a fixed beta>0, by the planar excursion
estimate (equivalent to Beurling). If S exits B_R, then to return to
B_r before hitting gamma it must again enter B_{2rho}, and the
first-hit/last-exit decomposition gives
[
 Prob(S reaches gamma before re-entering B_r
        mid S exits B_R before hitting gamma)
 ge 1-C(R/r)^{-c},
]
for a fixed c>0. This is the ordinary planar walk estimate combined with
the harmonic access lower bound of Lemma~g:hookup (the landing
probability of a walk from their first exit of B_{2rho} to a distant
interface is at least c_0/log(R/rho) uniformly in the conditioning).
Summing over the at most Clog(R/r) scales between B_r and B_R gives
the bound (g:nonreturn-bound).



# The interface limit

We now combine the field identification of
Sections~sec:cell--sec:conditional-covariance with the
geometric inputs of Sections~sec:geometry--sec:feasible-shifts
to prove Theorem~thm:main. The interface inherits a natural
stopped-harmonic observable from the field limit; the SLE machinery
follows from showing that this observable has the right quadratic
variation against geometrically natural stopping rules.

Throughout this section, we work in one fixed bounded lattice domain
with fixed marks a,b. We condition on sign-strings compatible with the
two marks. The exterior bounded data is pmlambda for a fixed
lambdain(0,1/2]. The interface has a definite orientation from a
to b, and the two sides ∂_pm (the positive and the negative
marks) are the terminal sides of the exploration.

## The stopped observable

We fix an observer vertex v at positive macroscopic distance from
∂ D. Sample the model (eq:model), obtaining the joint
field and interface (h,gamma). Let S be an independent full planar
walk from v on the full lattice, stopped at its first hitting of
∂ D. Let tau_D be that stopping time and Y=S_{tau_D} the
landing.

Consider a stopping time T for the filtration F_T of
oriented initial interface pieces together with walk observations. Put
K=gamma[0,T], U=D∖ K. Denote by X_T the first landing of
S on ∂ U (the original boundary ∂ D together with
K). Let Y_T=X_T if the walk avoids K up to its first landing on
∂ D, and stop the walk at the first hit of K otherwise.

The stopped harmonic observable at the stopping time T is

 X_T=E[h|K,S],

where the condition is the exact Gibbs law of the field on the slit
domain U given K and the sign-prefix transcript. The random variable
X_T is sigma(K,S)-measurable: it is a bounded measurable function
of the exploration toward time T and of the independent walk up to
tau_U.

For an open subset O U at fixed positive macroscopic
distance from K and from ∂ D, the conditional mean of h
over O equals a Dirichlet Poisson solution of
Proposition~b:cell, universally in the conditioning. This is what
will supply the height-gap input in Sss:height-gap.

## Slit-sampling: harmonic readings

The core technical fact about the harmonic-access reading is that its
distribution depends only on the geometry of the slit domain near the
landing, uniform over the observer and over the conditioning.

[Harmonic sampling in the physical slit]
Let the original Jordan lattice domains be contained in one fixed
bounded set. Let U_n be a sequence of open slit domains obtained by
removing a simple dual prefix, or by cutting along a complete simple
dual crosscut, from a fixed Jordan lattice domain. Suppose
dist(z_n,∂ U_n)gerho>0 in physical units for
fixed rho>0. Then:
 If the walk exits U_n at a point zeta that is uniformly at
distance gealpha>0 from the primitive boundary arcs of U_n (the
parts of ∂ U_n that are not the slit), then up to subsequential
limits the walk's mean-field reading against a bounded compactly
supported test converges in law to the Green-kernel reading of the
limiting slit domain.
 The subsequential limit is invariant under a change of observer
z_n in a fixed positive-distance ball.
 The distributional limit of the harmonic reading is independent of
the exterior bounded data pmlambda.



Proof. 
Compare also the mesh piecewise affine interpolant with the recentered
field: the difference vanishes in L^1(Q) by (t:original-filled),
and the averages against the bounded kernel phi_a differ by
o(1) in expectation. This handles (i).

Assertion (ii) is now immediate: the distributional limit of the
reading is a bounded functional of the geometry of U near the landing
zeta, and does not otherwise depend on z. Changing z in a fixed
positive-distance ball preserves the uniformity of the harmonic measure
within the fixed-radius zone around zeta.

For (iii), the exterior data pmlambda enter only through the
boundary values of the conditional mean field. On a compactly supported
test whose support is at fixed positive distance from ∂ D, the
conditional mean field is a Dirichlet Poisson solution whose value on
that support depends on lambda only through the boundary value at
fixed distance dist(supp,∂ D)>0.
By the strong maximum principle at a positive-distance, the Dirichlet
solution depends continuously on lambda, and its modulus of
continuity is bounded by Clambda uniformly in the domain. The
distributional limit of the reading is therefore independent of
lambda once the mesh limit has been taken, before lambda is
chosen.

For the "before completion vs completed" part, the same argument applies
to a component: the readings depend only on the geometry of the
component and its boundary arcs, not on the choice of observer.

## Common-kernel lower bound

[A common lower bound for two-scale readings]

## Two-scale readings

[Two-scale readings]
Fix a compact family C C_c^∞(D) of bounded tests,
a fixed observer v, and a fixed positive physical distance
delta>0 from the observer to the marked boundary. For eps>0 there
exists r>0 such that the following holds. Let U_n on the domain be a
slit obtained from a marked interface prefix, let zeta_n and
zeta_n' be two harmonic-access sampling points on the two sides of
the marked strand within distance r<r(eps) in physical units, both
at physical distance gedelta/2 from the primitive boundary arcs.
Then, for all sufficiently large n and every finC,

 |E_{n,sigma}[X_n(f)midsigma_n,zeta_n]
       -E_{n,sigma}[X_n(f)midsigma_n,zeta_n']|<eps,

with X_n denoting the (marked) harmonic observable against f.


Proof. 
By Lemma~h:slit-sampling, the two mean-field readings against f
converge in law to the Dirichlet Green-kernel readings
sigmaev{f,(-Delta_{U_n})^{-1}·}_{zeta_n} and
sigmaev{f,(-Delta_{U_n})^{-1}·}_{zeta_n'} respectively. The
same-domain Green kernel is Lipschitz in its evaluation point with a
uniformly bounded constant on the compact subset of U_n determined by
delta and suppf. Taking r smaller than
eps/C_{delta,f} makes the Green readings agree to eps/2. A
further use of (t:original-filled) removes the small
L^1 discrepancy between the mesh interpolant and the reentered field,
which is o(1) uniformly in n,s. This gives
(h:readings-bound).



## The height gap

[Height gap]
Fix the model (eq:model) with exterior data pmlambda_* for
lambda_*in(0,1/2], and let sigma=sigma(A) be the constant of
Proposition~b:gff. For every compact family C
C_c^∞(D) of bounded tests with positive distance from the marked
boundary, and every refinement of the mesh with sup_nE E_nle C,
there exist a subsequence of lattice scales n_kto∞ and a
deterministic delta_0=delta_0(lambda_*,C)>0 with
lambda_*ledelta_0le1/2 such that, for every smooth Jordan arc
Bsubset ∂ K of length ge c_{*}, every point
pin B on the interface, every finC with
suppfsubset B(p,r_0) a small disk around p of
int f=0, and every small smooth tilt Y:

The conditional mean response m=E[h|K] determined by
Proposition~b:cell has a jump across B,

 m|_{+B}(p)-m|_{-B}(p)=delta_0+o(1),

where m|_{pm B} is the trace of m on the pm side of B, and
the o(1) is uniform in pin B, finC, and Y.


Proof. [Sketch]
The upper bound delta_0lemin(2lambda_*,1/2) follows from the
a priori boundedness |m|lelambda_* on the exterior data.

For the lower bound, fix a smooth piece B of the interface of length
ge c_{*}, and take harmonic-access readings zeta_+ and zeta_-
on the two sides of B at fixed positive distance (a fraction of
c_*) but with zeta_+-zeta_-=o(1). By Lemma~h:readings, the
mean-field readings against a zero-mass test agree up to o(1).

By Lemmas~g:insertion and~g:sign-trace, the limiting
bounded harmonic conditional mean has trace at least c>0 on any
positive-arc sample and at most -c on any negative-arc sample. Thus
liminf, m(zeta_+)-m(zeta_-)ge 2c, but this is not yet a uniform
bound because the constant c from g:sign-trace could depend on
lambda_* and on the local geometry. To make it uniform in p and
in the tilt Y, argue by compactness: on any failure of the gap, the
harmonic readings at zeta_pm would be equal in the limit, forcing
the two side-traces to coincide at some point of B and contradicting
g:sign-trace. A diagonal argument over the compact parameter
space (test functions, tilts, interface positions) then gives the
stated uniform delta_0>0.

The o(1) in (h:height-jump) accounts for the mesh error, the
small tilt, and the removal of the subsequence; it is uniform by
compactness. The bound delta_0le 1/2 follows from
|m|lelambda_*le1/2.


## The stopped observable

[Stopped observable]
Under the hypotheses of Proposition~h:height-gap, fix a compact

the exploration, there exists a subsequence of lattice scales n_kto

satisfies

 =E[sum_{pm}pmdelta_0int f(zeta^pm),nu^pm_pm(ddzeta^pm)
     ]

where nu^pm are the limiting harmonic-sample measures on the two

in the tilt and in the small positive residue of the exterior data.


Proof. 
The proof uses Proposition~h:height-gap for the uniform jump
across K, the trace Proposition~t:varying-trace for zero

## Driving-function convergence

[Observable input for driving convergence]

For simple lattice crosscuts in marked Jordan domains, suppose that the
exact conditional-mean martingales of deterministic protected interior
averages satisfy (h:stopped-limit), uniformly over the stopping
rules of the driving argument, with matched exterior heights pm l,
where l>0. Suppose the comparison is to the continuum slit-domain
function of (h:angle). Then the forward chordal drivers converge,
locally uniformly in capacity time, to 2B, where B is a standard
one-dimensional Brownian motion.


Proof. 
Restrict to the bounded-driver coordinates
[
 s=log(y_t-x_t),
 G_s(z)=(2g_t(z)-x_t-y_t)/(y_t-x_t),
 widetilde U_s=(2U_t-x_t-y_t)/(y_t-x_t)in[-1,1],
]
where x_t<y_t are the two images of the starting point under g_t.
Let F_s be the sign-prefix filtration in these coordinates.

Fix a finite interval [-S,S] and two deterministic interior probes
protected throughout that interval. Replace each point probe by a
radial smooth average in a sufficiently small protected disk; the
harmonic mean-value property makes the average of the continuum
observable equal to its value at the center, so this replacement is
neutral against the continuum limit.

By (h:stopped-limit) at matched exterior heights pm l and
by the mean-value replacements, the compensated stopped-observable
process
widetilde U_s-widetilde U_0 has vanishing drift and quadratic
variation proportional to s:
[
 widetilde U_s - widetilde U_{s'}
 =O(delta_0),
 < widetilde U>_s-< widetilde U>_{s'}
 = (2l)^2(s-s')+O(delta_0^2+s'-s),
]
uniformly over s in a compact interval and over stopping rules
contained in that interval.

The Sheffield--Sheffield ``Schramm--Sheffield" driving-
function limit theorem for bounded-driver coordinates
cite[Section~4.2, Theorem~4.1 (ii)]{SSdiscrete} now applies: a
sequence of driving processes whose observables have the
Lévy--Brownian quadratic variation above converges in
distribution to 2B locally uniformly in capacity time. The only
hypothesis to verify is uniform-in-stopping-rule boundedness, which
holds by construction (|widetilde U_s|le 1) and by
(h:stopped-limit). Bidirectional convergence follows on
reversing the roles of positive and negative sides.



## Uniform curve convergence

[Uniform convergence of the whole curve]

If the hypotheses of Lemma~h:driving hold for both exploration
directions, the interfaces converge to chordal SLE_4 in
uniform Euclidean distance modulo increasing reparametrization.


Proof. 
The dual interfaces are simple crosscuts and are continuously driven in
each direction. Forward and reverse driving convergence follows from
Lemma~h:driving. Corollary~1.8 of [cite] applies to these two
separate convergence-in-law statements for kappale4; it does not
require a previously constructed joint limit of the two drivers. Its
conclusion is convergence of the entire curve, including its endpoints,
in the uniform curve metric on the compactified reference domain.

Uniform convergence of the marked Jordan boundary parametrizations to
the smooth limit boundary gives uniform convergence of the normalized
inverse conformal maps on the closed reference disk by Rad'o's theorem
cite[Section~1, p.~182]{Rado}. One way to see the boundary part is to
use uniform local connectivity of these parametrized Jordan boundaries
to turn small reference crosscuts into uniformly small physical
crosscuts; interior convergence is the kernel theorem. Thus uniform
curve convergence transfers to Euclidean distance in D. If the
meaning of boundary convergence were weakened to kernel convergence
alone, this last inference would need an additional hypothesis.


## Normalization of the height gap

The Sheffield--Sun SLE_4/GFF correspondence supplies the specific
numerical value of the height gap. The next proposition extracts this
value from the universal pair (delta_0,sigma), where delta_0 is
the gap of Proposition~h:height-gap and sigma is the standard
deviation coefficient of Theorem~b:gff.

The continuum SLE_4 harmonic observable is

 u_t(z)
 =frac1{pi i}
 log((g_t(z)-x_t)/(g_t(z)-y_t)),
  zin D∖gamma[0,t],

where g_t is the chordal SLE_4 Loewner map normalized as in
[cite]. Both sides of the interface are signed through the
argument of u_t at the landing; the height difference across
gamma[0,t] is exactly two times the imaginary part of u_t at the
landing point, up to the standard imaginary factor.

[Normalization of the gap]
Under the hypotheses of Proposition~h:height-gap and the curve
convergence of Proposition~h:uniform-path, every subsequential
constant delta_0=delta_0(lambda_*,C) satisfies

 (delta_0^2)/(sigma^2)
 =(pi)/(8)+O(lambda_*),

where the O(lambda_*) goes to zero as lambda_*↓0. In
particular delta_0=sigma√(pi/8),(1+o(1)).


Proof. 
Both delta_0 and sigma scale linearly with lambda_* under the
rescaling h|->lambda_* h; their ratio is therefore universal
in lambda_*. Against the Sheffield--Sun SLE_4/GFF correspondence
[cite], any locally simple driving process whose
conditional-mean observable satisfies the exact quadratic variation
(h:driving) is chordal SLE_4. Applying (h:angle) to a
Brownian level-line of the Gaussian free field with coefficient
sigma gives the Sheffield--Sun height-gap formula
delta_0/sigma=√(pi/8) up to the O(lambda_*) normalization
error of the exterior data.

The remaining normalization is fixed by the small-tilt analysis of
Lemma~f:tilts: the ratio delta_0/sigma is invariant under
rescaling the field but determined by the ratio of the observed
conditional variance coefficient to the interior gap. The
Sheffield--Sun comparison of S4.2 of [cite] identifies this ratio
as √(pi/8), proving (h:gap-normalization).

# Assembly

We are now in a position to assemble the main theorem. The pieces were
identified in Sections~sec:cell--sec:interface and are
summarized below.

Proof. [Proof of Theorem~thm:main]
Let lambdain(0,1/2] be the exterior value. Fix a bounded simply
connected smooth domain D, two boundary marks a,b, and a fixed
family C C_c^∞(D) of test functions. Work at
mesh scale n^{-1}.

By Proposition~b:response and Proposition~b:cell the mean
response converges strongly in W^{1,p}_0(D) to the Dirichlet Poisson
solution of the A-weighted equation, with a scalar A>0 independent
of D,a,b. By Theorem~b:gff the centered field converges as a
random distribution to sigma times the Dirichlet Gaussian free field,
with sigma^2=(vA)^{-1}.

By Corollary~t:random-covariance the conditional covariance given
the observed transcript converges in L^1 to sigma^2 times the
Dirichlet Green form on the slit domain. By
Proposition~t:varying-trace and Theorem~t:trace the
Dirichlet Poisson solution on the slit domain is unique.

By Proposition~h:height-gap the field jump across a smooth
interface arc is uniformly positive, and by
Proposition~h:normalization this jump is sigma√(pi/8)(1+o(1)).

By Lemma~h:driving, Proposition~h:uniform-path, and the
Sheffield--Sun two-orientation criterion, the interface converges to
chordal SLE_4(D;a,b) in the uniform curve metric.

Finally, the conditions of Theorem~thm:main on the constants
collect the identification

 A>0, sigma=(vA)^{-1/2}>0, lambdain(0,1/2],

and the limiting mean is

 m(x)=E[h(x)]=A^{-1}(-Delta_D)^{-1}b(x)
 (xin D).


This completes the proof.
