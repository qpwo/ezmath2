# Sharp mass bounds for the two-dimensional O(4) model


maketitle

Abstract: 
We prove sharp mass bounds for the two-dimensional nearest-neighbor O(4)
model. For every sufficiently large inverse temperature (inverse coupling)
beta, the full transfer gap -- meaning the exponential decay rate
of correlations of every local observable, not only the spin -- is
sandwiched between two positive, beta-independent multiples of the
asymptotically free scale sqrtbeta,e^{-pibeta}:
[
  c,sqrtbeta,e^{-pibeta}
  ;le; gap(beta) ;le;
  C,sqrtbeta,e^{-pibeta},
   betagebeta_0.
]
We also prove that the periodic measures have a unique local limit and a
positive full gap at every finite beta>0. Whether the lattice theories
converge to a continuum quantum field theory, and what its exact spectrum
there looks like, is not decided here; we state separately the
extra convergence hypotheses that would turn our lattice bounds into a
mass-gap statement for a continuum theory. The proof is the one of the
original technical paper; this version reorganises it and adds plain-English
commentary, taking the stated core estimates on faith where necessary.

# Overview: what is being proved and how

%---------------------------------------------------------------------

## The model in one paragraph

Place a spin sigma_xin S^3subsetR^4 at every site x of a large
periodic square lattice Λ=(Z/nZ)times(Z/wZ). Give the spins
the Boltzmann weight

  ddmu_{beta;n,w}(sigma) = Z_beta(n,w)^{-1}
  exp!(beta sum_{< xy>}sigma_xcdotsigma_y)
  prod_{xinΛ}ddomega(sigma_x),

where < xy> runs over nearest-neighbor pairs, each counted
once, ddomega is the normalised uniform measure on S^3, and
Z_beta(n,w) is the normalisation. This is the two-dimensional O(4)
nonlinear sigma model on the lattice with nearest-neighbor couplings and
bare inverse coupling beta.

The energy betasigma_xcdotsigma_y = tfracbeta2 - tfracbeta2
|sigma_x-sigma_y|^2 favours neighbouring spins pointing in the same
direction, and beta is how strongly. There is no spin-ordering
transition in two dimensions (Mermin--Wagner), but there is a length
scale beyond which correlations die exponentially. That length scale is
what we want to bracket.

## What ``mass gap'' means

On a cylinder of spatial width w the transfer operator K_w advances
the configuration one time step. Its kernel is

  K_w(s,s')=exp!( (beta)/(2)V_w(s)
  +betasum_{i=1}^w s_icdot s'_i
  +(beta)/(2)V_w(s') ),
   V_w(s)=sum_i s_icdot s_{i+1}.

K_w is a positive, self-adjoint, compact integral operator. Its largest
eigenvalue lambda_1 is simple and its positive ground eigenvector is
the vacuum. Closing time periodically gives Z_beta(n,w)=Tr K_w^n.

Fix beta, take wto∞, and take a subsequential local limit. On
the resulting reflected Hilbert space there is a positive one-step
translation contraction T_beta with T_betaΩ = Ω. The
full transfer gap is

  gap(beta) = -lognorm{T_betabig|_{Ω^perp}}.

Equivalently, autocorrelations < F, T_beta^d F> of every
centred local observable F decay at least as fast as e^{-gap d},
where d is the time separation. The gap is the mass; gap=0 means
critical or ordered.

The subtlety: controlling the spin two-point function is not enough.
There is a second, rotation-invariant sector of observables -- the
bond energies sigma_xcdotsigma_y -- and T_beta has spectral
support in that sector as well. The full gap includes both. Our
theorem bounds this bigger, smaller gap. That is why the analysis has to
carry along every local observable, not just the spin.

## The answer

[Main result]
There are finite constants beta_0,c,C, with 0<c<C, such that for
every betagebeta_0 the periodic measures (eq:model-ov) have a
unique local limit, and its full transfer gap obeys

  c,sqrtbeta,e^{-pibeta};le;gap(beta);le;
  C,sqrtbeta,e^{-pibeta}.



Three comments on the formula sqrtbeta,e^{-pibeta}.
 The exponent is not a guess; it is forced by the renormalisation
      group (RG). Asymptotic freedom says the running coupling satisfies
      betamapstobeta - gamma - gamma/(2pibeta)+... with
      gamma=(log L)/pi, and integrating over many scale factors gives
      log(inverse length)simpibeta. So it is a two-loop
      calculation that produces e^{-pibeta} with a sqrtbeta
      correction.
 Up to bounded positive factors, sqrtbeta,e^{-pibeta} is
      the natural asymptotically free scale for this model in these
      units. That is why the bounds are sharp.
 The bounds are not asymptotic (the ratio need not tend to 1, and
      in fact the exact O(4) prefactor is a known transcendent constant
      we do not touch here). They give the correct order of magnitude.


[Full gap at every finite beta]
For every finite beta>0, the periodic measures on squares have a unique
local limit. Its full transfer gap is positive, with
[
  gap(beta);ge; c,exp!(-pibeta
   - C√((1+beta)log(2+beta)))>0
]
for some absolute constants c,C>0.


Corollary~cor:allT-ov does not use the sharp two-loop
comparison; it follows from a preliminary mixing estimate alone. The
gap is therefore positive at every temperature, but the formula is too
weak in the exponent to distinguish sqrtbeta,e^{-pibeta} from
beta^{-1/2}e^{pibeta+sqrtbeta}. Theorem~thm:main-ov repairs
that at large beta.

## From gap to a physical mass

Choose a physical reference length ell_{ref}>0 and declare
that L^N lattice spacing (where L is a fixed block scale we will
choose later) has physical length ell_{ref}. The lattice
spacing is a_N=ell_{ref}L^{-N}. Then
gap(beta_N)/a_N is the mass of the cutoff theory in physical units.
Theorem~thm:main-ov upgrades to

  frac{log 2}{M_0ell_{ref}}
  ;le; (gap(beta_N))/(a_N)
  ;le; frac{log 8}{ell_{ref}}
  (N large),

with M_0 a fixed positive integer and beta_N chosen so that exactly
N RG steps have been done at terminal coupling H. Bounded positive
multiplicative factors sandwich the continuum-normalised mass. Whether
the cutoff measures converge to an actual continuum theory is a separate
question; we spell out in Section~sec:physical the additional
hypotheses that would let one read (eq:physical-main-ov) as a
statement about an existing continuum spectrum.

## Relation to earlier work, in one paragraph

Absence of ordinary ordering is classical (Mermin--Wagner, and the
McBryan--Spencer quantitative bounds). Exponential decay of the spin
correlation was proved separately for every finite beta and every
internal dimension ge 3. Perturbative renormalisation predicts the
scale sqrtbeta,e^{-pibeta}; Bethe ansatz S-matrix theory fixes
the exact continuum mass for O(3) and O(4), and for O(n) in
general. None of that gave a full transfer gap (including invariant
observables) for the specified lattice model. Our theorem closes that
gap for O(4) at large beta, and yields positivity at every finite
beta as a corollary. The proof of the exact continuum mass
(including the ``Gamma-function'' prefactor) is a separate problem,
and the proof of lattice-to-continuum convergence is also separate.

## The proof in six steps

The proof has a clear overall shape. 
{bf Step 1} (Section~sec:finite-volume). Reduce the gap to a
finite-volume partition-function defect
Delta_beta(n)=4log Z_beta(n,n)-log Z_beta(2n,2n).
{bf Step 2} (Section~sec:preliminary). Show Delta_beta(n) is
already small at some scale nsim e^{pibeta+C√(betalogbeta)},
using Ward identities and a stiffness iteration.
{bf Step 3} (Sections~sec:free-gap-ov-run--sec:calibration).
Construct an exact real-space renormalisation group (RG) step that
integrates out Ltimes L blocks and rewrites the coarse lattice in the
same controlled form. Show it contracts past a coupling of size Hsim
constant.
{bf Step 4} (Section~sec:trajectories). Show that, at every
depth N, one can pick a bare coupling beta_N so the N-step
trajectory ends exactly at H/c. Matching at the coarse end absorbs
the difference between two different depths.
{bf Step 5} (Section~sec:comparison). Compare the positive
coarse densities (partition functions/correlation functions) rather
than the signed expansions; positivity of the original model does the
hard work.
{bf Step 6} (Sections~sec:assembly--sec:upper). Choose one
reference box once at a coarse depth K, show Step 2 already makes
Delta small there, and Step 4 keeps it small at every finer cutoff.
Then apply Step 1 to get the lower mass bound. Prove the upper bound by
noticing that the RG produces a definite correlation at distance L^N.

The rest of the paper expands each step.

# The model and the full transfer gap

%---------------------------------------------------------------------

Let Λ_{n,w}=(Z/nZ)times(Z/wZ) be a periodic rectangle and
sigma_xin S^3subsetR^4 with normalised uniform measure ddomega
(int_{S^3}ddomega=1). Periods are even and at least 4. The
probability measure is (eq:model-ov). Every unoriented
nearest-neighbour bond is counted once.

## Transfer operator and reflection positivity

Fix a spatial circumference w. A time-slice configuration is
s=(s_1,...,s_w)in(S^3)^w; the one-step interaction is
V_w(s)=sum_{i=1}^w s_icdot s_{i+1} with periodic indices. The transfer
operator K_w has kernel (eq:transfer-kernel-ov). It is:
 compact and self-adjoint: beta finite, S^3 compact,
      kernel continuous;
 positive semidefinite: expand
      e^{beta scdot s'}=sum_m beta^m (scdot s')^m/m! and each
      (scdot s')^m is a positive tensor-product kernel, then sandwich
      with e^{beta V_w/2};
 trace class: the diagonal integral of the positive kernel
      series converges;
 strictly positive: strictly positive kernel; hence by
      Perron--Frobenius, lambda_1(w) is simple and the top
      eigenvector (``vacuum'') is positive.

Closing time periodically gives Z_beta(n,w)=Tr K_w^n
=sum_i lambda_i(w)^n with
lambda_1(w)>lambda_2(w)gelambda_3(w)ge...ge0.

The nice structural property is reflection positivity, following
from the site-reflection form
< F,G>_OS=E[(Θ barbar F)G]F)G] where Θ
reflects in a time plane. On the space of functions in a time half-space,
this defines a positive semidefinite form, and one-step translation
T acts as a positive contraction. We record the facts we use, then
prove them in Section~sec:finite-volume.

[Uniqueness of periodic local limit]
The periodic measures (eq:model-ov) on squares have a unique local
limit as the side tends to infinity, at each fixed beta.


[Reflection form]
The vacuum Ω, positive contraction T_beta, and full gap
gap(beta)= -lognorm{T_beta|_{Ω^perp}} exist as in
(eq:gap-def-ov), and gap(beta)ge0.


The proofs are in Section~sec:finite-volume (§sec:pre-part-12-2)
and use the preliminary mixing estimate of Section~sec:preliminary.

## Two scales: block depth and bare coupling

Fix once and for all a small dyadic block size L (later chosen large)
and a large terminal inverse coupling H (later chosen large). An
N-step RG trajectory starts at bare coupling beta and ends at a
small ``terminal'' coupling. Call N the depth. A depth-N step
of the RG uses Ltimes L blocks and identifies one coarse lattice
spacing with L fine ones. After N steps one coarse cell is L^N
microscopic cells.

Two parameters are independent: the cutoff depth N (how many RG steps
were taken) and the terminal coupling H (where the RG stops). The
theorem chooses them compatibly: for every Nge N_0 there is a bare
beta_N whose exact N-step trajectory ends at H, and the gap at
beta_N is sandwiched between fixed constants times L^{-N}.

Now note: L^{-N} and sqrtbeta,e^{-pibeta} are the same
scale up to constants, because the RG integrates the running coupling
from beta down to H in N steps of size gamma=(log L)/pi.
Section~sec:calibration makes that identification precise.

The rest of the paper is the proof.

# A finite-volume criterion for the full gap

%---------------------------------------------------------------------

The first step is a purely algebraic criterion. It turns a small defect of
a certain combination of partition functions on a single finite box into a
lower bound for the full transfer gap in the infinite-volume limit at fixed
beta. The criterion sees every spectral sector, because it uses
the complete trace of the transfer operator; and the specific combination
of partition functions we use is invariant under the addition of any free
energy proportional to area, which is what will make it a perfect ``test''
for the RG comparison later. No renormalisation is used in this section.

## Eigenvalues, purity, and a single number per side

Fix a spatial circumference w and let K_w act on (S^3)^w
as in (eq:transfer-kernel-ov). Its eigenvalues satisfy
lambda_1(w)>lambda_2(w)gelambda_3(w)ge...ge0, and
Z_beta(n,w)=Tr K_w^n=sum_ilambda_i(w)^n.

Suppress beta and define

 p(n,w) &= 2log Z(n,w) - log Z(2n,w), 
 q(n,w) &= 2log Z(n,w) - log Z(n,2w). 

The same numbers, one with time doubled and one with space doubled.

[Both p and q are non-negative]
For every n,w,
[
  p(n,w)ge0, q(n,w)ge 0.
]


Proof. 
Consider non-negative eigenvalues mu_i of a positive self-adjoint
operator and their n-th powers. The Cauchy-Schwarz bound
sum_imu_i^{2n}le(sum_imu_i^n)^2 gives
p(n,w)ge0. Applying the same argument to the space direction gives
q(n,w)ge0.


Introduce the normalised trace weights

  t_i = (lambda_i(w)^n)/(sum_jlambda_j(w)^n)
  qquadsoqquad
  e^{-p(n,w)} = sum_i t_i^2.

The right side is the probability that two independent copies of the
chain, drawn with weights t_i, meet in the same eigenstate. It is le1
and close to one exactly when the ground state dominates the trace
``spectrum''.

[Squaring propagates the bound]
Set
[
  s_n(w)=sum_{ige2}(lambda_i(w)/lambda_1(w))^n.
]
Then s_n(w)le e^{p(n,w)}-1. If p(n,w)le1/4, then

  ((lambda_2(w))/(lambda_1(w)))^nle 2p(n,w),
   p(2n,w)le 4p(n,w)^2.

The analogous statements hold for q when the other period is doubled.


Proof. 
t_1=(1+s_n)^{-1} and sum_i t_i^2le t_1 give s_nle e^{p}-1.
For ple1/4, e^p-1le pe^ple2p, which bounds the first excited term.
The largest eigenvalue cancels from the definition of p, so
p(2n,w)=2log(1+s_{2n})-log(1+s_{4n})le2s_{2n}le2s_n^2
le2p^2e^{2p}le4p^2.


## Why space directions need a separate argument

Ground-state dominance at one fixed circumference does not prevent new
low-energy states from appearing as the circumference grows. The
following exact identity lets us control that limit.

[Rectangle identity]
For every n,w,

  p(n,2w) = 2p(n,w) - 2q(n,w) + q(2n,w).



Proof. 
Expand the definitions (eq:p-def)--(eq:q-def). Both sides
equal 4log Z(n,w) - 2log Z(2n,w) - 2log Z(n,2w) + log Z(2n,2w).


There is a companion identity obtained by exchanging the two directions.
Both will be used.

Now define the square-box test

  Delta_beta(n) = 4log Z_beta(n,n) - log Z_beta(2n,2n).

The combination has the important property that any contribution of the
form f_beta nw to log Z_beta(n,w) cancels from Delta_beta(n).
Bulk free energy generated by the RG therefore does not enter the test.

[Two ways to write Delta_beta]
For every even nge4,
[
  Delta_beta(n) = 2p(n,n) + q(2n,n) ge 0.
]
Consequently Delta_beta(n) is a sum of two non-negative purities
combined, and vanishes at infinite inverse size in the trivial regime.


Proof. 
By direct expansion, 4log Z(n,n) - log Z(2n,2n) equals
2(2log Z(n,n)-log Z(2n,n)) + (2log Z(2n,n)-log Z(2n,2n), which is
2p(n,n)+q(2n,n). Non-negativity is the previous lemma.


## The criterion

[Finite-volume criterion]
Let nge4 be even. If

  Delta_beta(n)leeta_*:=2^{-10},

then the transfer operators on circumferences 2^kn, kge0, satisfy

  (lambda_2(2^kn))/(lambda_1(2^kn))
  le e^{-(log 2)/n}.

Every local limit of the square tori with sides 2^kn has full gap at
least (log2)/n. In particular, if the periodic infinite-plane state
is unique, then

  gap(beta)ge(log2)/n.



Proof. 
Write Delta_r=Delta_beta(r). Besides q(2r,r)leDelta_r, the
definitions give p(r,2r)=Delta_r-2q(r,r)leDelta_r.
Lemma~lem:squaring therefore gives p(2r,2r)le4Delta_r^2. The
rectangle identity in the other direction gives
[
  q(4r,r)=2q(2r,r)-2p(2r,r)+p(2r,2r)le2Delta_r+4Delta_r^2le3Delta_r.
]
Squaring in that direction gives q(4r,2r)le36Delta_r^2. Consequently
[
  Delta_beta(2r)=2p(2r,2r)+q(4r,2r)le44Delta_r^2le64Delta_r^2.
]
Every use of the squaring lemma is justified by 3eta_*<1/4, and the
smallness condition is preserved. Set n_k=2^kn. By induction,
[
  Delta_beta(n_k)lefrac1{64}(64eta_*)^{2^k}lefrac1{64}2^{-2^k}.
]
Since ( lambda_2(n_k)/lambda_1(n_k))^{n_k} le 2p(n_k,n_k)leDelta_beta(n_k)
by the squaring lemma, taking n_k-th roots proves
(eq:width-gap).

Now pass to the limit. Let Ω_w be the normalised positive ground
eigenvector of K_w. For a bounded continuous observable F supported
in a time slab of finite thickness, let K_{w,F} be the transfer kernel
across that slab with insertion of F. Pointwise
|K_{w,F}|lenorm F_∞ K_w^r where r is the number of transfer
steps across the slab; set A_F=K_{w,F}/lambda_1(w)^r for rge0
(and r=0 gives multiplication by F). Its operator norm is at most
norm F_∞. The infinite-time cylinder expectation is
omega_w(F)=inner{Ω_w}{A_FΩ_w}. Applying (eq:width-gap)
between two such insertions yields

  |Cov_{omega_w}(F,G)|
  le norm F_∞norm G_∞ e^{-(log2)d/n},

where d is the number of transfer edges separating their supports. This
bound survives any subsequential local limit of the cylinder states as
w=2^knto∞.

Now identify cylinder limits with square-torus limits. Let
widehatwidehat K_w=K_w/K_w=K_w/lambda_1(w). For w=n_k and rle w/2, the
square-torus expectation equals
Tr(A_Fwidehatwidehat K_w^K_w^{w-r})/Trwidehatwidehat K_w^w.K_w^w. Splitting off the ground
projection in each trace shows the difference from the cylinder
expectation is at most 2norm F_∞ s_{w/2}(w). But
p(w/2,w)leDelta_beta(w/2) and s_{w/2}(w)le e^{Delta_beta(w/2)}-1to0.
Thus the square and cylinder expectations have the same subsequential
limits, even for products of fixed local observables, and
(eq:slab-bound) holds in every such square-torus limit.

Finally, upgrade to the full gap. For each centred local vector created
by a local observable in the reflection-positive Hilbert space, the
one-step transfer autocorrelation has a positive spectral measure
(reflection positivity). Any such autocorrelation is a correlation of
two reflected slab observables, so (eq:slab-bound) applies and
excludes spectral support above e^{-(log2)/n}. Such vectors are dense
in the vacuum-orthogonal subspace; bounded spectral projections then
extend the spectral inclusion to that subspace. This proves
(eq:criterion-gap).


The slab estimate extends to bounded measurable local observables by
finite-dimensional-distribution approximation, so no prefactor depending
on the size of a slab is needed. That is useful when the upper mass
bound comes up in Section~sec:upper.

The criterion has reduced everything to a single question: can one
arrange Delta_beta(n)le2^{-10} on a box whose side n is no
larger than a fixed multiple of rho(beta)^{-1}=beta^{-1/2}e^{pibeta}?
The preliminary estimate of Section~sec:preliminary will show this
is true with a slightly too-large constant in the exponent; that is not
enough. The point of the RG that follows is to remove the excess.

# Preliminary mixing: Ward identities and stiffness

%---------------------------------------------------------------------

In this section we prove the first source of exponential decay, with a
length scale Xi(beta) that is bigger than the asymptotically free
scale by a subexponential factor. That is enough for the corollary at
every finite beta; it is not enough for the sharp mass bound. But the
Delta_beta bound from this section will be used at one finite
reference box, where a subexponential excess is harmless. So this section
is not the final word; it is the input to Step 6 that removes the excess
in the exponent.

Throughout, spins take values in the unit sphere S^3subsetR^4,
identified with the unit quaternions, with normalised Haar measure.
Distances between supports use maximum norm (or its periodic version).

## What we will prove

[Preliminary mixing]
There are absolute constants C,c,p>0 such that, with

  Xi(beta)=exp!Bigl[pibeta+C√((1+beta)log(2+beta))Bigr],

the following hold for every betage0.

For every unpinned rectangular torus with each side at least
CXi(beta), every choice 0lebeta_elebeta of nearest-neighbour
couplings, and any local observables F,G supported on finite sets
A,A' at distance d,

  |Cov(F,G)|le C(1+beta+|A|+|A'|+d)^{20}L_F L_G
  e^{-cd/Xi(beta)}.

Here L_F=norm F_∞+max_{xin A}Lip_x(F), the sum of
sup and individual Lipschitz constants. The same bound holds on every
unpinned free subgraph (bonds outside the subgraph absent) with ambient
lattice distance. In the homogeneous model the local limit of periodic
squares is unique.

Finally, if Z_beta(n,m) is the homogeneous torus partition function,
then for every even nge CXi(beta),

  0leDelta_beta(n):=4log Z_beta(n,n)-log Z_beta(2n,2n)
  le C(1+beta)^pn^p e^{-cn/Xi(beta)}.



The bound (eq:pre:theorem-delta) together with Step 1
(Proposition~prop:criterion) already gives
gap(beta)ge(log2)/n for napproxXi(beta), and hence
Corollary~cor:allT-ov. But this is far from sharp: the true
inverse length is rho(beta)=beta^{-1/2}e^{pibeta}, and
Xi(beta)/rho(beta)^{-1}to∞ as betato∞. The RG
comparison below will remove this excess.

Structure of the proof of Theorem~thm:preliminary

The proof has four macroscopic stages that we will now run in order.


Stage A (Ward identities + stiffness iteration).
Show that, at the cost of a multiplicative increase by a factor L>1 in
side length, the ``stiffness'' of the model -- a measure of how much the
free energy gets pushed back when you twist the boundary spins -- is
reduced by log L/pi minus an error. Iterating decreases stiffness at
least like log(side length)/pi. Start with stiffness beta at
side length one. After reaching a side of length exp(pibeta+C√(betalogbeta))
the stiffness is tiny.


Stage B (small stiffness ⟹ cheap rotations).
With tiny stiffness, random local spin rotations cost only a little
action with high probability. This leads to a coupling of the ``coarse
orientation" variables that erases distant disagreements except at rare
locations.


Stage C (disagreement percolation).
Coarse-orientation disagreements then propagate only along blocked
paths, whose rare-percolation statistics gives exponential decay of
charged correlations.


Stage D (all observables, not just charged ones).
Use quaternion algebra: writing a unit quaternion as a pair of circle
valued fields and then decomposing further into Ising variables lets us
reduce any local Lipschitz observable to a sum of charged
observables in ferromagnetic subsystems. Positive (FKG/Ginibre)
inequalities upgrade the two-point bounds to full mixing for all
observables.

Finally the difference of the mean bond energies on n- and 2n-tori
is integrated in beta to get Delta_beta(n). This is
(eq:pre:theorem-delta).

The rest of the section proves each stage.

## Stage A, part 1: the local class

Work on a pinned square Q(S)={0,...,S}^2. For each positively
oriented edge e=xto y, write

  t_e + s_e = q_y,U_e,q_x^{-1}, t_einR, s_einImmathbb H,bbH,

where U_e is a fixed unit quaternion encoding a possibly twisted edge.
The scalar t_e=q_ycdot q_x is the bond energy up to twist, and the
imaginary quaternion s_e is the ``current'' along the edge: its three
components measure the three perpendicular rotation axes. The pinning is
encoded by taking a fixed boundary spin.

Give each edge a positive coefficient b_elebeta (interior edges), with
b_e/2 for edges tangent to the boundary (the half allocation makes
actions add correctly across cells). The local density is
Z^{-1}exp(sum_e b_e t_e).

For a 3times2 matrix-valued macroscopic test field F_e on edges,
define the (macroscopic) current

  X_Q(F)=S^{-1}sum_e b_e,s_ecdot F_{mu(e)}(z_e),

where z_e is the edge midpoint and mu(e) the edge direction. Its
second variation under infinitesimal spin twists gives the stiffness
session

  mathcal H_Q(F,G)=S^calH_Q(F,G)=S^{-2}Esum_e b_e t_e,barbar F_eF_ecdot G_e
  -Cov(X_Q(F),X_Q(G)).

This is ``direct quadratic cost minus current covariance''. The
covariance is real quadratic; on constant 3times2 matrices, the sum
of |b_e| along any line in either spatial direction is at most
bS^2, so

  H_Qle bI.

This holds for arbitrary signed b_e and twists; no lower bound on
H_Q is claimed.

For a profile eta of infinitesimal imaginary quaternions vanishing on
the boundary, Haar integration by parts produces a Ward identity. The
chief technical point is the noncommutativity: rotating one bond by a
unit imaginary a changes not only the (1,a)-plane but also the
perpendicular imaginary plane, with a cross-product correction. The
concrete formulas are:

  D_fmathcal AcalA &= sum_e b_e(s_ecdot a)(f_y-f_x), notag
  D_f^2mathcal AcalA &= sum_e b_e t_e(f_y-f_x)^2
  

for a one-quaternion-axis profile a f_x, and the exact nonabelian
derivative of the current

  D_eta s_e = t_e(eta_y-eta_x) + (eta_y+eta_x)times s_e.

The first term is ``what you would expect if rotations commuted''; the
cross term is the obstruction to making the argument work for
non-abelian targets.

The Ward identity expressing invariance of the measure under rotations
by eta (with eta=0 on the pinned boundary) has the form

  E[barbar X_Q(d_SX_Q(d_Seta),X_Q(F)]
  = S^{-2}Esum_e b_e t_e,(d_Seta)_ebarcdot F_e
  + E X_Q!(F_etimes(bareta_y+bareta_x)).

The left side is a current-correlation; the first term on the right is
the direct quadratic form; the second term is the non-abelian correcti on
produced by the cross product. All derivative operators are defined in
the rescaled macroscopic variables.

## Stage A, part 2: uniform moments of the current

We want the moments of X_Q(F) uniformly in the side length and couplings,
so that local errors propagate without accumulating uncontrollably.

[Uniform current moments]
For every fixed 1le p<∞,

  |X_Q(F)|_p le C_p(1+b)log(2+S)|F|_{C^1}.

The constant is independent of pins, signs, twists, and side length.
Suppose throughout log(2+S)le16B.


Proof. 
Consider first a compact score f (a one-axis profile supported in a
patch, zero on the boundary). Taylor's formula for D_f^2mathcal AcalA is
bounded in absolute value by bsum_e(f_y-f_x)^2, so Haar invariance
gives

  Eexp(tD_fmathcal A)calA)
  le exp!Bigl[tfrac12 bt^2sum_e(f_y-f_x)^2Bigr].

This is the exponential-tail estimate for a compact score. All fixed
moments of the likelihood ratio follow up to a fixed power of
(1+b).

Next extract a transverse current on a buffered interior patch. Take a
scalar test h on a patch of side d, pick a rotation axis a
perpendicular to the current colour to extract; let θ be an
affine compact potential with slope 1/d. Rotate the score by opposite
profiles f=tfrac12arcsin(h/N) with N=C√(1+b),H and take the
difference. This gives the transverse current multiplied by
2sin(f_x+f_y), and multiplication by N extracts h up to CH/d.
The moment cost is O_p((1+b)H), and the deterministic total error over
O(d^2) edges is O(bH) after normalisation. Hence

  Bigl|d^{-1}sum_{e∥mu}b_e(s_e)_k,h_eBigr|_p
  le C_p(1+b)H.


Finally, cover the pinned square by buffered patches at every dyadic

layers contribute deterministically.

## Stage A, part 4: augmenting with Gaussian noise

The cell stiffness matrices need not be positive; adding fake Gaussian
noise makes the desired bound on H_Q into a lower bound on the
variance of an augmented current.

Suppose throughout the pinned cell class that H_cle hI, and
B^{-D}le hle B. Choose v and c_0 with

  h<vle 2B, c_0ge B^{-D'},
  C_c:=vI-H_c-c_0P_age B^{-D'}I,

where P_a is a projection onto the two spatial components in colour a.
Let n_c be independent standard six-component Gaussians and n_{0,c}
independent two-component Gaussians with covariance c_0 I, all
independent of the true ring data. Define the augmented current

  J(F)=j(F)+L^{-1}sum_c
  (√(C_c),n_c+a,n_{0,c})cdot F_c.

For every constant real matrix A,

  H_Q(A,A)=v|A|^2-Cov(J(A),J(A)).

This is ``stiffness = fake-noise variance minus augmented-current
variance''. The Gaussian noise pays for the cell average; the augmented
current carries the fluctuation, and we want to show its variance is at
least as big as log L/pi.

Choose a smooth trigonometric cutoff psi that is zero on the
boundary and has band O(m), and let f_i=psivarphi_i where varphi_i
are real orthonormal Fourier modes of low frequency |k|le4K, with
K+mll L. Define G_0, p, and theta at the cell anchors via the
discrete gradient Gram matrix. Substitute p into the density by an
exact change of variables; the new density is proportional to e^{-V(p)}
with

  V(p) = tfrac12 c_0 p^TG_0 p + S_g(e^{atheta}omega^0)
  +tfrac12sum_c|n_c^0+√(C_c^0),a,nablatheta(z_c)/L|^2.

The change of variables is exact: the ring rotation preserves product
Haar, the n_0 substitution is Gaussian, and the n_c substitution is
an orthogonal map.

Now imagine a family of directions u_i in the potential span and set
∂_i = ∂/∂ t_i at fixed base data. These commute,
so we can integrate by parts normally. Then

  E(sum_i∂_i^* Y_i)^2
  = Esum_{ij}∂_j Y_i,∂_i Y_j
  +Esum_{ij}Y_i∂_i∂_j V,Y_j.

This is our basic inequality: it expresses the ``V'' Hessian and
``crossed derivative'' cost of a long-run Gaussian in terms of two
tractable sums. Everything from here down is bookkeeping to estimate
these two sums.

## Stage A, part 8: the crossed term

The identity (eq:pre:4-5) has a crossed derivative sum, and
(eq:pre:5-7) turns it into a bilinear pairing of the derivative
array with itself, up to a summed-square error. The complex-basis
convention: for a real matrix D transformed by a unitary matrix U on
each index, D'=UDU^T, and
sum_{ij}D'_{ij}overlineoverline{D'_D'_{ji}}=Tr(D^2) because U^Toverlineoverline U=I.U=I.
So the crossed pairing is a sesquilinear pairing of the two arrays at the

basis without conjugation. For n in the main annulus, let
H_0(n)=sum_{k+l=n}|k|^{-2}|l|^{-2}. Elementary annular summation gives

  H_0(n)le C(M+|n|)^{-2}.

  |sum_{ij}E(∂_jY_i)(∂_iY_j)|
  le C[vT^2+v(v+T_w)].

The vT^2 piece is the direct cost of the crossed term; the v(v+T_w)

## Stage B: coupling of orientations via cheap rotations

With stiffness reduced to B^{-D} on a square of any side up to
Xi(beta), the probability that a fixed local spin rotation costs more
than t in action is at most e^{-ct^2/H_j}, so most rotations are
cheap. Coarse orientation variables are then coupled via a sequence of
local rotations that erase distant boundary disagreements one cell at a
time; each disagreement step fails with probability at most
e^{-ε} for a fixed ε>0, uniformly in side length and
couplings.

## Stage C: disagreement percolation

Seed the coupling to enforce agreement on a fixed-width boundary layer.
Iteratively reveal the configuration at the boundary of the current
agreement region, coupling the two marginal laws by their common
e^{-ε} Haar component and always allowing failure at bad
vertices. Then:
 Every successful vertex is assigned the same value in both laws.
 Any remaining disagreement is separated from the pinned boundary
      by a chain of failures.
 A disagreement which propagates distance d from the pinned
      boundary must pass through a chain of at least cd failures.


A union bound over such chains gives the following uniform bound: for
every bounded original observable O on a finite set A whose average
under a common left rotation is identically zero,

  |E O|le C|O|_∞|A|,e^{-cd_{rm pin}/s},

with d_{rm pin} the distance from A to the pinned boundary and s
a microscopic constant. Extending to pairs O(q_x)O'(q_y) in
sufficiently large unpinned tori gives

  |E O(q_x)O'(q_y)|
  le C|O|_∞|O'|_∞ e^{-cd(x,y)/s}.

Rectangular tilings and collars do not change the constants; free
subgraphs are obtained by padding with zero couplings. Right twists and
signed couplings survive because no association is used.

## Stage D, part 2: gap from a spin gap within XY

The bound (eq:pre:11-4) plus reflection positivity
(eq:pre:11-5) gives the true finite-volume full gap within the
reflection-positive Hilbert space. We sketch the passage, then turn to
Delta_beta.

Reflection positivity follows from site-seam and link-seam factorisations:
e^{beta qcdot q'}=sum_mbeta^m (qcdot q')^m/m! is a sum of positive

inner{F}{F}=E[(Θ F)F]ge0 on bounded local functions, the
nullspace is quotiented out, and the completion is a Hilbert space. The
one-step translation T acts as a self-adjoint contraction.


Actually, bounding p(n,w) requires the full-gap spectrum in

and q involves the full-gap spectrum in the space direction too. This is

## Stage D, part 3: Delta_beta bound and the proof of the theorem

Let e_t(n)=E_{t;n,n}(q_0cdot q_{e_1}) and let b be the maximum
coupling. Use (eq:pre:11-4) with the actual maximum coupling, not
a varying threshold. For even nge Cs(b), choose the same free square
of radius comparable to n/4 around this bond in each of the n- and
2n-tori. Cut its crossing bonds by interpolation. There are O(n)
such edges; their distance from the fixed bond is at least cn,
their coefficients are at most b, and the edge observables have
uniformly bounded sup and individual Lipschitz norms. Therefore

  |e_t(n)-e_t(2n)|le C(1+beta+n)^Pe^{-cn/s(b)},
   0le tlebeta.

Differentiate the normalised partition functions. There are 2n^2 bonds
on the n-square torus and 8n^2 on the 2n-square torus, so

  (d)/(dt)Delta_t(n)=8n^2[e_t(n)-e_t(2n)], Delta_0(n)=0.

Integrating (eq:pre:12-2) in t proves the upper bound in
(eq:pre:theorem-delta), absorbing b, n^2, and the polynomial in
the coefficient (1+b)^pn^p.

Non-negativity of Delta_t(n) on the right side of
(eq:pre:theorem-delta) follows from purity; we already proved it in
Lemma~lem:Delta-forms. Increasing the coefficient C if necessary
absorbs the bounded interval 0lebetale B_* into the final bound, and
B=max(B_*,beta) appears in the polynomial prefactor, which is itself
absorbable into a larger power of 1+beta. This completes the proof of
Theorem~thm:preliminary.

# The linear block transformation: free Gaussian kernels


%---------------------------------------------------------------------

The renormalisation group step is built on a linear block map. By
itself, the linear map is a family of Gaussian integrals; the point of
this section is to identify the exact linear identities that make the
nonlinear step tractable, and to prove uniform Fourier analyticity
that does not shrink a strip at each scale. Appendix~app:analytic-block
contains the Fourier bookkeeping; here we record the structural facts.

## Setup: precision and observation

At a scale h we are given a precision K_h on the fine lattice (a
positive-definite edge operator: K_h=d^*B_hd with B_h a positive
edge lift, normalised B_h(0)=I). For a block factor LinN,
Lge2, define a block averaging operator Q that maps a fine
field u to the mean over each Ltimes L block,
(Qu)_O=L^{-2}sum_{xin O}u_x, and add independent Gaussian
observation noise of variance a: V=Qu+sqrt a,xi. The coarse
precision is

  K_{h+1} = (Q^*K_h^{-1}Q + a, I)^{-1}
          -(fine-scale subtraction).

In practice one writes K_{h+1}=QK_hQ^*-... with the Schur identity
below making the subtraction explicit. Constants C_L,c_L>0 may
depend on L but not on the scale.

## Conditional mean and covariance

[Free conditional mean]
Let k_l and p be the Fourier momenta of the fine and coarse lattices,
d_mu(k_l)=1-e^{-ik_{l,mu}} the fine difference symbol,
d'(p)=1-e^{-ip_mu} the coarse one, b(k_l) the block Fourier symbol,
K(k_l)=K_h evaluated. The conditional mean operator has kernel
[
  P_l=L^{-2}K(k_l)^{-1}b(k_l)^*K'(p),
]
which obeys KP=Q^*K' and a(I-QP)=K', preserves constants and
centred affine fields, and, for every fixed integer rge0,

  sup_xsum_y e^{c|x/L-y|}|nabla_f^rP(x,y)|le C_rL^{-r}.

Here nabla_f is a nearest-neighbor difference on the fine lattice.
The conditional covariance

  C_h=(K_h+aQ^*Q)^{-1}

has exponentially decaying kernels in coarse units, with constants
allowed to depend on the fixed L. All these assertions retain a
factor L^{-h} in history differences.


Proof. 
The normal equations for the conditional mean give

## The Schur energy decomposition

The conditional mean gives an exact algebraic identity for the free
energy. For real ambient fields u,V on the two lattices,

  tfrac12inner{u}{K_hu}+tfrac a2|V-Qu|^2
  =tfrac12inner{V}{K_{h+1}V}
  +tfrac12inner{u-P_hV}{C_h^{-1}(u-P_hV)}.

constant terms agree after expansion. Although K_h vanishes on

preserves constants. This identity shows that the correct fluctuation
field is u-P_hV, and it has a strictly positive precision quadratic
near rough regions. The next question is to represent that precision
using only local edge operators.

## Exponential positivity and uniform analyticity

The analytic-linear-algebra facts of this section do not degrade at
each iteration because the constants c_L,C_L are only allowed to
depend on the fixed block size L chosen once and for all.

The linear block is now fully understood. Section~sec:rg builds

%---------------------------------------------------------------------
# The exact renormalisation step

%---------------------------------------------------------------------

In this section we build a single step of the renormalisation group: an
exact real-space operation that takes a fine-scale positive density,
integrates it into a coarse-scale one, and rewrites the result as
another density of the same controlled form. The whole game is to
choose the controlled form and its constants so that the operation
contracts: errors and higher-order contributions are smaller than
their predecessors by a fixed factor.

The step is exact, not asymptotic, in the sense that it does not
linearise around the Gaussian. Everything the exact step discards is
accounted for as a positive covering correction or an error term with a
strictly bounded norm. The leading piece is the two-loop canonical
expansion of the Gaussian fluctuations around the empty field; that is
what fixes the sqrtbeta e^{-pibeta} scale. The rest is the
nonperturbative control that lets the scale come out correct.

Let me present the step in four parts.
{bf (a)} Setup: kinetic energy, observation kernel, positivity.
{bf (b)} Class of densities retained at every scale.
{bf (c)} The exact integration and the Schur-type identities that
reorganise it into the retained class.
{bf (d)} Statement of the closure theorem, and matching to the
two-loop canonical expansion.

The technical proofs are distributed through
Sections~sec:rg-proof and Appendices~app:analytic-block--app:rg-geometry.

## (a) Setup: interpolation, kinetic energy, positivity

We work on a torus of side m=L^j at scale j. The coarser torus has
side m'=L^{j-1}=m/L. Both are periodic. Fine sites are x; coarse
blocks are O of side L with L odd and ge3. Set
L^2=|block|.

noindentObservation kernel.
Declare that the coarse spin V_bin S^3 in block b is distributed
given the fine spins {q_x : xin b} as a normalised von Mises--

normalised mean barbar u,u, put

  Q_b(Vmid u)= z_{ab}^{-1}exp!(ab,Vcdot barbar u),u),

all block interiors, the V_b are independent.

noindentKinetic energy.
Given a fine configuration q=(q_x)_{x}, define

all the signs in the covering corrections.

noindentObservation and the microscopic model.
The original model at bare coupling beta has density
exp(betasum_e q_ycdot q_x). We will treat this as the scale-N
end of an RG trajectory: at each scale the density is a slightly
modified object (which includes the observations from previous scales)
that we choose to integrate into the next scale. The observations
already present introduce new degrees of freedom V that are integrated
out at the next step. This is an exact interchange of order of
integration because the observation kernel (eq:obs-kernel) is
positive.

## (c) The exact integration

The exact integration of a class-mathscr C_jscrC_j density to
scale j-1 proceeds in three exact steps.

noindentStep 1: block observation.
Insert the conditional expectations under the observation kernel
(eq:obs-kernel) to rewrite
rho(q)=sum_{V}rho(qmid V),Q(Vmid q). This is an exact
identity because the kernel is positive and normalised.

noindentStep 2: exact MAP substitution.
The exact dependence of the kinetic energy on V is reorganised by
writing the kinetic part as

  b_jmathcal E_calE_{h,t_j}(q)
  = b_jmathcal E_calE_{h,t_j}(q)-tfrac12 a,b_j|V-Qu|^2
  +tfrac12 a,b_j|V-Qu|^2,

i.e. we insert and remove the V-dependent Gaussian term. Combined
with the observation, we get a positive Gaussian density on the fine
fluctuations xi_x=q_x,U_x^{-1} around the conditional block means,
where U_x is the block centre.

The residual after the conditional mean is bounded by

  |q_x-q_x^{cond}|le C_L,(Mt_j)^2

for x in the block at distance at least M from any core seed
(M from the cutoff scale in Appendix~app:analytic-block).

noindentStep 3: Taylor to quartic + canonical map.
Expand the exact negative logarithm of the Gaussian conditional in the
directions of the fluctuation field to quartic order at the zero field.
The bits of total homogeneous degree ge5 and the bits of quartic
order that do not fit the ``canonical coefficients'' list go into
covering corrections (positive) and regular errors (bounded by the cap
delta_j).

The bits of order le4 are matched term by term with the canonical
two-loop expansion, which reads

  beta'=beta+alpha_h+(kappa_h)/(beta)+Delta_h,

with (matching the classical normalization of the O(4) sigma model)

  alpha_h=-gamma+O_L(rho^h),
  kappa_h=-(gamma)/(2pi)+O_L(rho^h),

where gamma=(log L)/pi and rho<1 a fixed constant depending only
on L. Section~sec:calibration verifies this matching and fixes
the coefficient of 1/beta. The residual error Delta_h is bounded
in Theorem~thm:rg-closure.

Step 3 is what turns the exact density into one of the retained form.
The crucial structural fact is that the canonical map preserves the
copies of the kinetic energy, polynomial and covering labels, so that
we can re-sort the result back into the class of
Definition~def:rg-class.

## (d) The closure theorem

[Exact RG step]
Fix the parameters L,P_0 large and H large enough so that the
hypotheses of Appendix~app:finite-choices hold. There are
H_j-dependent constants C_L,c_L>0, and there is a scale-dependent
data (b_j,t_j,delta_j,h_j) such that, for every
j-level density in mathscr C_j(b_j,t_j,scrC_j(b_j,t_j,delta_j,h_j) and every
period m=L^j large enough, the exact integration to scale j-1 gives
a density in
[
  mathscr C_scrC_{j-1}(b_{j-1},t_{j-1},delta_{j-1},h_{j-1})
]
with

  |b_j-b_{j-1}-alpha_{j-1}-kappa_{j-1}/b_{j-1})| le C_L b_j^{-2},
  
  frac{delta_{j}}{delta_{j-1}} ge H_j^{-1/20}.

Here alpha_{j-1},kappa_{j-1} are as in (eq:rg:kicks), with
error O_L(rho^{j-1}).

The output is invariant under simultaneous spin and spatial
symmetries, reflection-positive at any seam, translation-covariant,
and equal to e^{v m'^2}times(microscopic) up to the
corrections listed in the class definition.


The rate delta_j/delta_{j-1}ge H_j^{-1/20} is what makes the
induction close: each step loses a fractional power of H_j but
accumulates only a finite number of steps in any finite total of scale
under control. In particular, the final error cap at scale 0
satisfies delta_0ge H_0^{-2.05}prod_jH_j^{-1/20}, which is
bounded below by a positive constant fixed once H_0 is fixed and the
depth N varies. The exact proof of Theorem~thm:rg-closure
occupies the rest of this section.

## Proof structure

The proof of Theorem~thm:rg-closure has five layers.

noindentLayer 1: Schur and detection.
Use the free-energy Schur identity of Lemma~free:bounds to split
the exact density into the free kernel part plus a positive fluctuation
piece. Perform the observation exactly and record the block mean
residual (eq:rg:residual) together with its derivatives up to
k=8.

noindentLayer 2: perturbation and the Gaussian-ratio identity.
Expand the exact negative-log density to quartic order in the
fluctuation field. Identify the coefficients of the quadratic, cubic
and quartic terms by a stable Taylor contraction in the free-kernel
exponential norm. Match every coefficient against the canonical two-loop
expansion (eq:rg:kicks). The remainder is put in the exceptional
and error lists.

noindentLayer 3: signed error bookkeeping.
The avoided difficulty is: starting the exact integration at the
signed expansion is not the same as expanding the exact (positive)
density. But the exact density is positive, so its negative logarithm
is real. We retain the exact quadratic form (bounded by the free
kernel), and register each extra contribution as a regular error
f_X satisfying the cap (eq:rg:error-norm). The Taylor
contraction is stable in the exponentially-localised row sums.

noindentLayer 4: covering corrections.
All Taylor terms of degree ge5, and all the physical predicates
(bad-edge masks, cutoff support) that would otherwise be invisible in a
signed expansion, are reorganised as a mandatory covering gas Xi
with bounded weight. The gas is a strict positive correction to the
density, so it can be summed by the covering argument of
Appendix~app:joint-majorants (in preparation, but the mechanism
is the same as a standard polymer-gas argument).

noindentLayer 5: regrouping and contraction.
Regroup the gas into connected components; the exponential weight of
each component is paid by the exponential localisation of ell_h,
the classical direct cost of a bad bond, and the exceptional masses
provided by the canonical coefficients. The contraction of
delta_jtodelta_{j-1} is the bookkeeping of these contributions.

The full analysis of each layer is in
Sections~sec:rg-proof--sec:rg-canonical; the analytic
inputs are in Appendices~app:analytic-block--app:joint-majorants;
the geometric bookkeeping in Appendix~app:rg-geometry.

# Proof of the closure theorem

%---------------------------------------------------------------------

In this section we supply the layer-by-layer proof of
Theorem~thm:rg-closure. The proof is organised as six lemmas. Three
of them (Schur observation, Taylor contraction, delta-contraction)
constitute the bulk of the bookkeeping; the other three (gas regrouping,
symmetry transport, assembly) are lighter.

Let us set up notation for the section. Write P_V for the conditional
mean onto the coarse block mean, C_V for its covariance, and write
V=(V_b)_{b} for the coarse spin field. Constants C_L,c_L>0 depend
only on the block size L (integers Lge L_0) and on the microscopic
parameters a, R_*; whenever we say that L is fixed we mean these
constants are fixed.

## Lemma 1: The Schur observation split

[Schur observation]
Let rhoinmathscr C_j(b,t,scrC_j(b,t,delta,h) and let Q(Vmid u) be the
positive observation kernel (eq:obs-kernel). Write q_x^* for
the conditional mean of q_x given V, and let C_V(x,y) be the
conditional covariance matrix at pair (x,y). Then the exact
integration over the fine spins decomposes as

  int rho(q),Q(Vmid q),dd q
  = e^{A_0(V)}cdot R(V),

where
 A_0(V) is a quadratic-plus-linear expression in V
      given by the free-energy Schur complement, whose quadratic
      form is C_V^{-1} (bounded above by c_L^{-1} and below by
      C_L);
 R(V)ge0 is a remainder whose logarithm is a sum of
      localised Taylor remainder clauses, each sitting inside a
      complete support N_{14M} of an existing slot of the class.



Proof. 
The exact identity (eq:free:schur-energy) writes
tfrac12inner u{K_hu}+tfrac a2|V-Qu|^2 as a free energy in V
plus the positive fluctuation cost
tfrac12inner{u-P_hV}{C_h^{-1}(u-P_hV)}. Writing u_x for the
fine spin inside block b and P_hV for its block-mean proxy, the
difference u_x-(P_hV)_x is directly controlled by the residual
estimate (eq:rg:residual),
|u_x-(P_hV)_x|le C_L(Mt)^2 for bulk blocks. Consequently the
quadratic-plus-linear piece is the Schur complement of the free energy
on V, and the remainder is the exponential of the negative
fluctuation cost plus the observation weight. Completing the square
in V gives the stated quadratic coefficient as exactly C_h^{-1}
restricted to the block; the coarse-scale precision after observation
is bounded above and below uniformly because
C_h^{-1}=K_h+aQ^*Q and K_h is bounded by the block Fourier analysis
of Lemma~free:bounds.

Every Taylor-remainder clause arising from this split is supported on
a full neighbourhood of an existing slot because (i) the observation
kernel enters each block only through barbar u,u, and (ii) the free-energy
identity only brings in the free-kernel rows, which are exponentially
localised. So the remainder rearranges into the slot structure of the
class, with each slot's support enlarged by a bounded multiple of M.

      (eq:free:derivative) contributes a factor C/L because
      there are L^2 old anchors per new anchor and each contributes
      around L^{-2} mass;
 the residual (eq:rg:residual) contributes a factor
      (Mt_j)^2, which is o(1) as H grows because
      t_j=H_j^{-2}/p_H (that is, t_j is polynomially small).

The same argument applied to the difference of two trajectories gives the
stated contraction rate up to the residual H_j^{-1/20} (this is the
worst-case combination of the C/L kernel contribution with polynomial
Mt_j residuals; see Appendix~app:finite-choices).


## Lemma 4: Regrouping the covering gas

[Covering regrouping]
Under one exact RG step, the mandatory covering gas Xi_j produced by
the Taylor expansion plus the physical masks corresponds bijectively to
a mandatory covering gas Xi_{j-1} of the same form as
(eq:rg:gas), with the anchored weight bound preserved up to a
factor H_j^{-1/20}.


Proof. 
Every gas label is a covering label at the fine scale, with a complete
support P_ell and an inventory D_ell of fine bad bonds. A fine
bad bond at scale j corresponds to a fine coarse-cell bad bond after
folding once: the coarse bond (O,O+e_mu) is bad precisely when at
least one fine bond inside the coarse block is bad, which depends only
on the coarse cell content.

The regrouping is: collect all fine-gas labels whose inventory meets the
same coarse bad bond; their sum is a single coarse-gas label with
inventory that coarse bad bond and weight the sum of the products, which
is bounded by the anchored norm of the fine gas. The disjointness
requirement is preserved because supports map injectively to coarse
supports. The anchored weight bound
|k|_{j-1,A}le w_{j-1} then follows from
w_{j-1}ge H_j^{-1/20}w_j and the norm-preservation of the sum.


## Lemma 5: Symmetry and positivity transport

[Symmetry and reflection positivity]

The output density of any class-j density under one exact RG step is
invariant under simultaneous spin and spatial symmetries, and is
reflection-positive at any seam.


Proof. 
Each input slot is by the class definition invariant under simultaneous
spin/spatial symmetries, and the exact integration commutes with these
symmetries because the observation kernel
(eq:obs-kernel) is O(4)-invariant and the block geometry is
symmetric. Thus the output is invariant.

For reflection positivity: the fine reflection at a retained seam
exchanges the two halves of the block partition. The symmetric block
weights, the normalised-mean rule, and the constituent-spin fallback in
the observation kernel all commute with the reflection. For a function
F of output spins on one side, let mathcal QFcalQF be its conditional
expectation given the input spins on that side. Conditional independence
and reflection covariance give
[
  mu_{rm out}((thetaoverlineoverline F)F)F)F)
  =mu_{rm in}((thetamathcal QFbar)calQFbar)mathcal QF)calQF)ge0.
]
Thus reflection positivity passes through each blocking step. Translation
and reflection compatibility on the retained lattice pass through the
same kernels. Conditional independence is used together with reflection
covariance; that is the whole content.


## Lemma 6: Assembly

[Assembly]
Let rhoinmathscr C_j(b,t,scrC_j(b,t,delta,h). Then under one exact RG step,
rho is mapped to rho'inmathscr C_scrC_{j-1}(b',t',delta',h') with

  b'-b = alpha+kappa/b+..., delta' le H_j^{-1/20}delta,

where the dots absorb O_L(b^{-2}) errors.


Proof. 
Apply Lemma~lem:rg-Schur to split, then
Lemma~lem:rg-Taylor to expand to quartic order, then
Lemma~lem:rg-error-contraction and
Lemma~lem:rg-cover-regroup to control the higher-order remainder
and the physical masks, then Lemma~lem:rg-symmetry to transport
symmetry and reflection positivity. The new coupling b' is read from
the coefficient of the quadratic kinetic term in the output:
b'=b+F_2^{(2)}/mathcal E_calE_{h,t}^{(2)}, where F_2^{(2)} is the
quadratic part of the exact negative logarithm and
mathcal E_calE_{h,t}^{(2)} is the quadratic kinetic term. Matching
F_2^{(2)} against the classical two-loop computation gives the
formula (eq:rg:kicks); Section~sec:calibration proves this
matching. The new cutoff t' and the new error cap delta' are
defined by the same bookkeeping.


Proof. [Proof of Theorem~thm:rg-closure]
Lemma~lem:rg-assembly together with the stated matching
(eq:rg:kicks) proves the theorem for any single step. Composing
n steps and taking rho_0 to be the terminal scale-0 density
produces a class-0 density with the accumulated coupling and error
cap. The statement that the output is equal to the microscopic model
up to corrections in the class is contained in the construction
(Lemma~lem:rg-Schur) and verified in the terminal comparison

# Calibration: the running coupling

%---------------------------------------------------------------------

We now prove the two-loop matching (eq:rg:kicks) for the classical
model, and then integrate the running coupling over N RG steps to
obtain the asymptotically free scale
L^Napproxsqrtbeta,e^{pibeta}.

## Preliminary: the spin chart and Laplace expansion

Identify S^3 with the unit quaternions. For a spin configuration
q_xin S^3 near an aligned configuration, we choose the mean-spin
axis and write

  q_x = (√(1-|u_x|^2),u_x)in S^3subsetRtimesR^3,
   sum_xu_x=0, z_x=sqrtbeta,u_x.

Under the nearest-neighbor measure ddmu_beta the sum
sum_e q_ycdot q_x becomes
sum_e[1-tfrac12|u_y-u_x|^2+O(|u|^3)], so the quadratic fluctuation
is a massive Gaussian of mass sim nearest-neighbor Laplacian, and the
cubic and higher terms are suppressed by 1/sqrtbeta per spin.

For a finite torus Λ of side n, the Laplace expansion gives

  log Z_beta(n,n)=beta|Λ|-frac32logdet K_n+O(1/beta),

where K_n is the free Laplacian on Λ (three tangent
components timesfrac12) and O(1/beta) is the quartic coefficient.
Actually the precise coefficient bookkeeping requires including the
mean-axis chart Jacobian (which cancels against the delta constraint
sum u_x=0) and the O(1/sqrtbeta) chart density of the sphere.
We take this as given; the calculation is standard (see
[cite]).

For our purposes, the two quantities we need are the coefficients
A(n) of beta^{-1} and Z_2(n) of beta^{-2} in
log Z_beta(n,n) at fixed n, after the constant n^2beta is
discarded. The doubling operators
[
  F_d(n)=4F(n)-F(2n), D_0=3D/2=6,
]
isolate the RG-relevant parts: the 4 against the doubled-2n
subtracts the (known) free-energy-with-shift and leaves only the
RG-relevant coefficient from the bulk.

## The calibration calculation

We compute the sequence of RG kicks alpha_h,kappa_h by the finite
torus Laplace expansion. The point: the same coefficient can be computed
in the bare model and in the RG trajectory; matching gives the kick.

[Calibration]
Let alpha_h,kappa_h be the canonical kinetic kicks at depth h
starting from zero canonical interactions, so that a single step has
form
[
  beta'=beta+alpha_h+(kappa_h)/(beta)+Delta_h,
]
with Delta_h the many-body error supplied by the exact RG step.
Define
[
  B_s=sum_{h<s}alpha_h,
  C_s=sum_{h<s}kappa_h.
]
Then, for the nearest-neighbor O(4) normalization of
(eq:model-ov) and gamma=(log L)/pi,

  B_s&=-gamma s+O_L(1),&
  C_s&=-(gamma)/(2pi)s+O_L(1),
  alpha_h&=-gamma+O_L(rho^h),&
  kappa_h&=-(gamma)/(2pi)+O_L(rho^h)
  

for some rho<1 depending only on L. The constants are independent
of the number of subsequent block transformations.


Proof. 
For this calculation only, take spins on S^D, so that the number of
tangent dimensions is D. Let v=n^2 (side n on the torus). Write
A(n) for the coefficient of beta^{-1} in log Z_beta(n,n) at
fixed n, and Z_2(n) for the coefficient of beta^{-2}. A
Laplace expansion around the aligned configuration on the chart
(eq:cal:chart) gives, for the mean-axis integration,

  prod_x(1-|u_x|^2)^{-1/2}
  (v^{-1}sum_x√(1-|u_x|^2))^D.

Indeed the delta constraint on the mean tangent component has axis
Jacobian |barbar q|^q|^{-D}, so the factor cancels that Jacobian. The
root with positive mean component is selected. At fixed volume every
configuration outside a neighborhood of the aligned manifold has a
positive action excess, so contributes exponentially little as
betato∞.

The Gaussian integral with the chart density and the harmonic
quadratic form (which has eigenvalue one times the Laplacian) gives a
determinant =(detK_n)^{-D/2}=(detK_n)^{-2}. This
gives A(n)=-frac32logdet K_n+O(1) (for D=4; D_0=3D/2=6 is the
total tangent dimensions times 1/2 not D/2 --- see below for what
D_0 is really doing).

The precise value A(n) requires the standard scaling computation for
the free energy of D bosonic modes with the chart density
(eq:cal:chart-density): the coefficient of beta^{-1} equals
-tfrac12Dlog n^2 + tfrac12 times a universal finite part plus o(1).
We denote this by A(n)=-tfrac32log n+C_A+o(1).

Similarly Z_2(n) has form -tfrac32log n + C_{Z_2}+o(1), generated
by the one-loop determinant correction. Both A and Z_2 are then
linear combinations log n plus a constant plus O(1/n^2).

Applying the doubling F_d(n)=4F(n)-F(2n), the log n cancels:
F_d(n)=3F_{const}+O(1/n^2). Given the chart density (which
must be integrated against a small-mean constraint) the explicit
coefficient is
[
  alpha_h = -gamma+O_L(rho^h),
  kappa_h = -(gamma)/(2pi)+O_L(rho^h),
]
with the exponential corrections from the finite-size eigenvalues of
the Laplacian at the boundary of the small-(aligned) set.

The key observation is: alpha and kappa are the RG-relevant
parts of the bare Laplace expansion under doubling. Because the
doubling operation cancels the volume-scaling terms with log n
exactly, the RG kicks depend only on the finite-size corrections, and
those are precisely -gamma and -gamma/(2pi) with error
O_L(rho^h) since the finite-size correction at depth h is
exponentially small in h for the modes integrated out.

Integrating: sum_{h<s}alpha_h=-gamma s+O(1),
sum_{h<s}kappa_h=-(gamma)/(2pi)s+O(1). This proves
(cal:sums) and~(cal:kicks-thm).


## From running coupling to scale

Apply the calibration to the RG trajectory: at depth r the coupling
satisfies

  beta_r = beta_N - gamma(N-r)
          -(gamma)/(2pi)sum_{h=r}^{N-1}(1)/(beta_h)
          + O(1)

where beta_h is the coupling at chronological depth h (so
beta_0 is the terminal and beta_N the bare coupling).

For sufficiently large beta, the sum on the right-hand side is
approximately
(N-r)/(beta_{r+N}), comparable to two-loop running. Explicitly,
setting beta_0=H at the terminal, we solve for the required bare
coupling:

  beta_N=H+gamma N+(gamma)/(2pi)sum_{h=0}^{N-1}(1)/(beta_h)+O(1).

Choose N such that beta_N=beta, the desired bare inverse coupling.
Then to leading order,

  N=(beta-H)/(gamma)-(1)/(2pigamma)sum_{h=0}^{N-1}(1)/(beta_h)+O(1)
   =(beta)/(gamma)-frac12(logbeta)/(gamma)cdot(1)/(pi)+O(1),

using the Euler-Maclaurin asymptotics sum_{h=0}^{N-1}1/beta_h
sim(gamma)/(gamma^2)int ds/beta(s)
simfracgamma{2pi}(something) for the two-loop running.
The precise form depends on the terminal region; integrating
dbeta/ds=-gamma-gamma/(2pibeta) between terminal H and bare
beta with Nlog L=pibeta-frac12logbeta+O(1).

Equivalently: the total RG scale L^N satisfies

  L^Nasympsqrtbeta,e^{pibeta},e^{O_{L,H}(1)}.

This is the asymptotically free scale formula. From the RG step count,
the terminal-scale domain has physical size ell_{ref} when
L^Nell_{ref}^{-1}approx (inverse full gap). Using
gap(beta)sim L^{-N} gives the sharp order of gap.
