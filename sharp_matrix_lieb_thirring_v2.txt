# Equality cases in the sharp one-dimensional[2pt]
matrix Lieb--Thirring inequality[6pt]large a complete proof




maketitle

Abstract: 
We classify all equality cases in the sharp one-dimensional Lieb--Thirring
inequality, for every finite matrix size and every exponent
1/2<gamma<3/2.  For a measurable Hermitian positive semidefinite
potential W with int_Rtr(W^{gamma+1/2})<∞, equality holds
exactly when, in one constant unitary basis, W is a direct sum of scalar
one-bound-state solitons and zero channels, with independent scales and
centres.  Every step is proved here.

# The problem, the answer, and the idea
% =====================================================================

## What is being estimated
Fix an integer mge1 (the number of internal ``channels'').  A
potential is a measurable function W:RtoC^{mtimes m} whose
values are Hermitian and positive semidefinite.  Think of -Delta acting
on vector-valued functions psi:RtoC^m, and of W as a position
dependent multiplication operator.  The Schr"odinger operator

 H_W=-(d^2)/(dx^2)-W

is unbounded below when W is large, but it has only finitely many
negative eigenvalues below any fixed level (Lemma~lem:spectral).
Write lambda_j(H_W) for the eigenvalues, counted with multiplicity, and
for 1/2<gamma<3/2 set

 Tr(H_W)_-^gamma
   =sum_{lambda_j(H_W)<0}|lambda_j(H_W)|^gamma .

Here tr is the unnormalised trace on C^{mtimes m}, and W^p is
defined by spectral calculus.

## The inequality and its constant
The sharp Lieb--Thirring inequality in one dimension is

 Tr(H_W)_-^gamma
   le C_gammaint_Rtr(W(x)^p),dx,
  p=gamma+tfrac12,

with the explicit constant

 C_gamma
 =((gamma-1/2)/(gamma+1/2))^{gamma-1/2}
   (Gamma(gamma+1))/(sqrtpi,Gamma(gamma+3/2)) .

The inequality itself is due to Lieb and Thirring and, in this matrix
form, to the companion paper [cite].  The new content here is the
equality statement, proved together with the inequality so that
both rest on the same estimates.

## The answer
Set

 r=(1)/(gamma-1/2)>1 .

The extremal potentials are built from the single scalar profile

 w(x)=(r+1)a^2,sech^2!(r,a,(x-x_0)),
  a>0, x_0inR ,

which has exactly one negative eigenvalue, namely -a^2, and saturates
(eq:sharp) by itself.

[Equality classification]
Let mge1, 1/2<gamma<3/2, p=gamma+tfrac12, and let W be a
measurable Hermitian positive semidefinite potential with
int_Rtr(W^p)<∞.  Then (eq:sharp) holds.  Moreover,
equality holds if and only if there are an integer kin{0,...,m},
a constant unitary matrix U, positive numbers a_1,...,a_k, and real
centres x_1,...,x_k such that, for almost every x,

 W(x)=U,diag(w_1(x),...,w_k(x),0,...,0),U^* ,
 
 w_j(x)=(r+1)a_j^2,sech^2!(r a_j (x-x_j)).

The case k=0 means W=0.  Each nonzero channel carries exactly one
negative eigenvalue -a_j^2, so every extremal potential has at most m
negative eigenvalues counted with multiplicity.  The scales a_j and
centres x_j are independent, and coincident profiles are allowed.


## The idea of the proof
The proof has six moves.  We state them now so the reader knows where
each later section is going.
[(1)] Linearise the spectrum.  Approximate finitely many
negative eigenfunctions by smooth compactly supported trial functions and
arrange them as the rows of a matrix U(x).  A lower triangular matrix
C keeps the rows in successive spans; this is what makes the energies
ell_i^2 appear in order.  This is the standard trial-function step of
the inequality.
[(2)] Build an action.  For a diagonal matrix K of trial
root-energies, define the energy
mathcal E_K(A)=calE_K(A)=int(|A'|_{HS}^2+tr(K^2AA^T)-tr((AA^T)^q)).  It is
the trial-function energy written in matrix language.
[(3)] Insert a matrix path.  A symmetric path B(x) running
from K at the left end of the interval to -K at the right end turns
the action into a square plus a boundary term (complete the square):
[
 mathcal E_K(A)=(boundary)+calE_K(A)=(boundary)+int(|A'-BA|_{HS}^2-F(A,B)).
]
The boundary term produces exactly b_sigmasumell_i^{2gamma}.
[(4)] Keep the defect.  Equality forces the square
|A'-BA|_{HS}^2 and an algebraic defect
Phi_K(A,B)=|[B,K^2]|_{op}^2+|K^2-B^2-(AA^T)^r|_{op}^2 to vanish.
The trace engine of Section~4 is what makes the coefficient of Phi_K
uniform in the number of rows.  This uniformity is essential because the
number of negative eigenvalues is not known in advance to be finite.
[(5)] Pass to a limit.  The number of rows may grow without
bound while the number m of columns stays fixed.  The fixed column
count bounds the tail rows by the small energies, giving strong pointwise
convergence and exact algebraic relations in the limit.
[(6)] Solve the leftover ODE.  The limit relations force the
channel subspaces to be constant in x.  On each channel a matrix
Riccati equation reduces to a scalar equation whose only bounded
solutions are the profiles (eq:profile).  This yields
Theorem~thm:main, and a direct factorization check proves that every
listed potential attains equality.


## Brief history
Lieb and Thirring introduced spectral moment inequalities for the kinetic
energy of fermions [cite].  The sharp
scalar endpoint gamma=1/2 is due to Hundertmark--Lieb--Thomas
[cite], extended to operator-valued potentials by
Hundertmark--Laptev--Weidl [cite].  The semiclassical constant is
sharp for gammage3/2; the relevant tools are the moment lifting of
Aizenman--Lieb [cite] and the operator-valued inequalities
of Laptev--Weidl [cite].  Benguria--Loss [cite]
gave a commutation proof at gamma=3/2.  The one-state scalar profile
(eq:profile) goes back to Keller [cite]; its role in the
Lieb--Thirring problem is explained in cite[Section~4]{LiebThirring1976}.
At the endpoint gamma=3/2 the equality structure changes: Frank, Gontier
and Lewin [cite] show that several bound states can
share a single scalar channel (KdV multisolitons).  The strict inequality
r>1 used below is exactly what rules that out in the open interval.

Throughout,

 sigma=gamma-tfrac12in(0,1),

 2sigma+1=2gamma, rp=q=1+r .

 D_sigma=frac{sigma^sigma}{(1+sigma)^{1+sigma}},

They are linked to (eq:Cgamma) by the beta integral:

We use the operator norm |cdot|_{op} and the Hilbert--Schmidt norm

Sym_n is the space of real symmetric ntimes n matrices with inner
product tr(BH); the commutator is [B,H]=BH-HB.  The identity matrix
or operator has the size forced by context.

# Two elementary lemmas
% =====================================================================

## The spectrum is discrete and the form is closed
The first lemma says we may freely talk about ``the'' negative eigenvalues,
even when W is only measurable.

[Spectral facts]
Let p>1 and let W:RtoC^{mtimes m} be measurable, Hermitian and
positive semidefinite, with int_Rtr(W^p)<∞.  The quadratic form

 h_W[psi]=int_R|psi'|^2-int_R<psi,Wpsi>,
  psiin H^1(R;C^m),

is closed and bounded below.  Multiplication by W^{1/2} is compact from
H^1 to L^2.  The negative spectrum of H_W consists of isolated
eigenvalues of finite multiplicity, accumulating at most at 0.  Every
eigenfunction lies in H^1 and solves -psi''-Wpsi=lambdapsi in
distributions.


Proof. 
Put w=|W|_{op}.  Then 0le w^pletr(W^p), so win L^p(R).  The
one-dimensional Sobolev bound, applied to the vector xmapsto|psi(x)|,
reads |psi|_∞^2le 2|psi|_2|psi'|_2.  Hence, by
H"older and Young,

 int_R<psi,Wpsi>
 le 2^{1/p}|w|_p|psi'|_2^{1/p}|psi|_2^{2-1/p}
 le ε|psi'|_2^2
     +C_ε|psi|_2^2 .

So a suitably shifted form norm is equivalent to the H^1 norm: the form
is closed and bounded below, and by polarization the potential term is
continuous on H^1.

Let Tpsi=W^{1/2}psi.  For R,L>0 define the truncated multiplier
T_{R,L} by keeping only {|x|le R, wle L}.  By the compact
embedding H^1([-R,R])↪ L^2([-R,R]), each T_{R,L} is
compact, and
[
 |(T-T_{R,L})psi|_2^2
 le C,bigl|w,mathbf 1_bf1_{{|x|>R}cup{w>L}}bigr|_p
      |psi|_{H^1}^2 .
]
The prefactor tends to zero as R,Lto∞, so T is a norm limit of
compact operators, hence compact.

Now fix a>0.  If the spectral subspace of H_W in (-∞,-a] were
infinite dimensional, it would contain an orthonormal sequence bounded in
H^1 by (eq:formbound).  A subsequence converges weakly to zero in
H^1, so its T-images converge strongly to zero; but for unit vectors
in that subspace h_W[psi]ge-age-|Tpsi|_2^2, forcing
|Tpsi|_2^2ge a, a contradiction.  Thus every such subspace is finite
dimensional.

Finally test the weak form equation against smooth compactly supported
functions: this gives the stated distributional equation.  Its potential
term is locally integrable because Win L^p_{loc} and psi is
locally bounded.


## Young's inequality for matrices
This is the scalar inequality yz-z^qle D_sigma y^p promoted to
matrices, together with its equality condition.

[Trace Young inequality]
For positive semidefinite matrices W,Z of the same finite size, put

 Y(W,Z)=D_sigmatr(W^p)-tr(WZ)+tr(Z^q).

Then Y(W,Z)ge0, with equality if and only if W=qZ^r.  Moreover

 Y(W,Z)getfrac12tr(Z^q)
       -(2^{p-1}-1)D_sigmatr(W^p).



Proof. 
Maximise zmapsto yz-z^q over zge0 for fixed yge0.  The maximum
D_sigma y^p is attained exactly at y=qz^r; this is the scalar Young
inequality behind D_sigma in (eq:Dconst).  Choose an orthonormal
eigenbasis (v_j) of Z with eigenvalues z_j, and set
y_j=< v_j,Wv_j>.  By (eq:youngdefect) and the scalar
inequality,
[
 tr(WZ)-tr(Z^q)
 =sum_j (y_j z_j - z_j^q)
 le D_sigmasum_j y_j^p .
]
Because t^p is strictly convex on [0,∞),
sum_j y_j^plesum_j< v_j,W^p v_j>=tr(W^p).
Thus Y(W,Z)ge0.  Equality in the convexity step forces every v_j to
lie in an eigenspace of W; equality in the scalar step then forces
Wv_j=qz_j^r v_j for every j, i.e. W=qZ^r.  This covers zero
eigenvalues as well.

For (eq:youngcoercive), apply the already proven Yge0 to the pair
(2W,Z) and expand:
tr(2WZ)letfrac12tr(Z^q)+2^{p-1}D_sigmatr(W^p), which rearranges
to (eq:youngcoercive).


% =====================================================================
# The trace engine: an exact integrated deficit
% =====================================================================

The action estimate compares a matrix N against its power N^q.  The
classical inequality is tr(NV)getr(N^q) under a symbol condition, but
equality analysis needs the exact size of the gap.  The engine below gives
that gap as an integral of two manifestly nonnegative squares.  The point
is that it is an identity, not just an inequality, so a vanishing total
gap forces each square to vanish.

Throughout this section 0<sigma<1, beta=sigma-tfrac12, and
p=1+sigma, r=1/sigma, q=1+r as in (eq:params).

## The normalisation constant
Define c_sigma>0 by

 c_sigma^{-1}
 =int_0^∞ t^beta
       (t^{-1/2}-(t+1)^{-1/2}),dt .

The integrand is O(t^{sigma-1}) near 0 and O(t^{sigma-2}) near
∞, so the integral converges (this uses 0<sigma<1).  A change of
variable gives the scaling identity we will use constantly:

 c_sigmaint_0^∞ t^beta
       (t^{-1/2}-(t+y)^{-1/2}),dt=y^sigma,
  yge0 .

Indeed, substitute t=yu; the left side becomes
c_sigma y^{beta+1/2}int_0^∞ u^beta(u^{-1/2}-(u+1)^{-1/2}),du
=y^sigma by (eq:trace:normalization) and beta+tfrac12=sigma.

## Statement of the engine

[Exact integrated trace deficit]
Let nge1 and let B,VinC^{ntimes n} be Hermitian.  Suppose

 Y_s=(sI-B)^2, X_s=Y_s+Vge bIquad(sinR)

for some b>0.  Define

 R(t)=frac1piint_R(X_s+tI)^{-1},ds,
 
 P(t)=t^{-1/2}I-R(t), t>0,

and

 N=c_sigmaint_0^∞ t^beta P(t),dt .

Then these integrals converge absolutely in matrix norm, R(t)>0, and
there are continuous Hermitian matrices L(t)=R(t)^{-1}>0 and Z(t)
with

 V+tI=L^2+Z^2+i[B,Z],
 
 LZ+ZL+i[B,L]=0 .

If Nge0, then

 
 tr(NV)-tr(N^q)
 =c_sigmaint_0^∞ t^beta(
   &tr(RZ^2)
   &+qbigl|R^{-1/2}-R^{1/2}(tI+N^r)^{1/2}bigr|_{HS}^2
   ),dt .
 

In particular tr(NV)getr(N^q), and equality holds if and only if both
integrands vanish for every t>0.


The hypothesis concerns the full symbol X_s; the perturbation V itself
need not be positive.  The proof has three parts: recover L,Z from
half-line boundary values, evaluate a logarithmic determinant integral,
and complete a matrix square.

## Step 1: half-line boundary maps
Quadratic growth of X_s together with (eq:trace:symbol) gives an
integrable bound for X_s^{-1}, hence R(t)>0 and R(t)=O(1) as
t↓0.  Diagonalising B gives the free identity
frac1piint_R(Y_s+tI)^{-1},ds=t^{-1/2}I.  For tge1 both
X_s+tI and Y_s+tI dominate c(s^2+t)I with c>0 independent of
s,t; the resolvent identity bounds the difference of inverses by
C(s^2+t)^{-2}.  Consequently

 |P(t)|_{op}=
 
  O(t^{-1/2}), & t↓0,
  O(t^{-3/2}), & tto∞,
 

which proves convergence of N in (eq:trace:N).

Fix t>0 and write nabla=∂_x-iB.  Consider

 (-nabla^2+V+tI)psi=0 .

For each vinC^n there is a unique H^1 solution on either half-line
with psi(0)=v.  The sesquilinear form
a_t(phi,psi)=int(<nablaphi,nablapsi>
+<phi,(V+tI)psi>),dx is coercive on H^1(R;C^n), since
its Fourier symbol X_s+tI dominates c_t(1+s^2)I; zero extension gives
coercivity on the trace-zero subspaces of each half-line, and the Riesz
representation theorem supplies the unique weak solution.  The equation
gives psi''in L^2, so psiin H^2, and both psi and psi'
vanish at the infinite end.

Define the right and left boundary matrices S,T by

 nablapsi(0+)=-Sv, nablapsi(0-)=Tv .

Integrating by parts gives
a_t(psi_v,psi_w)=< v,Sw> on the right and
=< v,Tw> on the left, so S,T are Hermitian.  Translation
and uniqueness show nablapsi=-Spsi throughout the right half-line
and nablapsi=Tpsi throughout the left half-line.  Substituting into
(eq:trace:ode) with arbitrary boundary data,

 V+tI=S^2+i[B,S]=T^2-i[B,T] .


Glue the two half-line solutions with the same boundary value v.  The
result is continuous with derivative jump -(S+T)v, so
(-nabla^2+V+tI)psi=delta_0(S+T)v.  Fourier inversion with kernel
e^{-isx} gives
[
 v=frac1{2pi}int_R(X_s+tI)^{-1},ds,(S+T)v
   =tfrac12R(t)(S+T)v .
]
The inverse symbol is O(s^{-2}), so its Fourier inverse is continuous
and evaluation at zero is legitimate.  Therefore

 L:=tfrac12(S+T)=R^{-1}>0,
  Z:=tfrac12(S-T)

satisfy (eq:trace:LZ): adding and subtracting the two halves of
(eq:trace:riccati) gives the first equation, and the second holds
because LZ+ZL=tfrac12(S^2-T^2) and i[B,L]=tfrac i2[B,S+T].  The
equation LZ+ZL+i[B,L]=0 determines Z uniquely from L>0 (in an
eigenbasis of L the map Zmapsto LZ+ZL multiplies the (j,k) entry
by L_{jj}+L_{kk}>0), which also proves continuity of Z(t).

Two consequences will be used.  First, the diagonal of the second
relation gives tr Z=0.  Second, multiplying i[R,B]=-(ZR+RZ) (the
R-conjugate of the second relation) into the trace of R(V+tI) gives
the signed identity

 tr(R(V+tI))=tr L-tr(RZ^2),

where the sign comes from i,tr(R[B,Z])=-2tr(RZ^2).

## Step 2: a logarithmic determinant integral
Define

 F(t)=(tr V)/(sqrt t)
       -frac1piint_R
        log(det(X_s+tI))/(det(Y_s+tI)),ds .

Both determinants are positive and the logarithmic ratio is
O(|s|^{-2}) at infinity; its t-derivative is the trace of a resolvent
difference with an integrable bound, locally uniform in t>0.  Hence F
is continuously differentiable.  The first relation in
(eq:trace:riccati) factors the symbol,
[
 X_s+tI=(sI-B+iS)(sI-B-iS).
]
If u_j+iv_j are the eigenvalues of B+iS, counted with algebraic
multiplicity, then the square-integrability argument behind
(eq:trace:ode) forces v_j>0, and
det(X_s+tI)=prod_j((s-u_j)^2+v_j^2).  The denominator has the same
form with centres the eigenvalues of B and widths sqrt t.
Translations of individual logarithms only change symmetric-cutoff
integrals by o(1) (the derivative
(d)/(da)int_{-M}^M h(s-a),ds=h(M+a)-h(M-a)to0 uniformly for
bounded a).  Using the one-factor integral
int_Rlog(s^2+v^2)/(s^2+w^2),ds=2pi(v-w) for v,w>0, and
sum_jv_j=tr S=tr L, we obtain

 F(t)=(tr V)/(sqrt t)-2tr(L-sqrt t I).


Set

 G(t)=tr(L-2sqrt t I+tR).

Both F,Gge0: for G apply lambda-2sqrt t+t/lambdage0 to the
positive eigenvalues lambda of L; for F use
(eq:trace:Feval) with tr V=tr(L^2+Z^2)-nt (the trace of the
first relation in (eq:trace:LZ)) to get
F(t)=t^{-1/2}tr((L-sqrt t I)^2+Z^2)ge0.  Combining
(eq:trace:RV) and (eq:trace:Feval) yields the identity

 tr(PV)=F+G+tr(RZ^2),

with every term on the right nonnegative.  By (eq:trace:Pbounds),
F, G, and tr(RZ^2) are integrable against t^beta dt, and
t^{beta+1}F(t) vanishes at both ends.  Differentiating
(eq:trace:F) and using (eq:trace:Feval)--(eq:trace:G),
[
 F'(t)=-frac{tr V}{2t^{3/2}}+tr P(t),
 
 tF'(t)=-tfrac12F(t)-G(t).
]
Integration by parts with the vanishing endpoint limits gives
int_0^∞ t^beta G,dt=sigmaint_0^∞ t^beta F,dt; since
sigma=1/r and int F=rint G,

 tr(NV)=q,c_sigmaint_0^∞ t^beta G(t),dt
       +c_sigmaint_0^∞ t^betatr(RZ^2),dt .


## Step 3: the noncommutative square
For a positive semidefinite matrix D, set
[
 F_0(t,D)=(tr D)/(sqrt t)
       -2tr((tI+D)^{1/2}-sqrt t I).
]
For a scalar eigenvalue dge0 the summand is
int_0^d(t^{-1/2}-(t+y)^{-1/2}),dy; Tonelli's theorem and
(eq:trace:scaling) give

 c_sigmaint_0^∞ t^beta F_0(t,D),dt=frac1ptr(D^p).

Now expand the Hilbert--Schmidt square without any commutation (the cross
factors R^{-1/2}R^{1/2} cancel, and cyclicity handles the rest):

 mathcal D_D(t)calD_D(t)
  &=bigl|R^{-1/2}-R^{1/2}(tI+D)^{1/2}bigr|_{HS}^2 notag
  &=tr(R^{-1}+R(tI+D)-2(tI+D)^{1/2}) notag
  &=G(t)-tr(P(t)D)+F_0(t,D).
  

All three terms are integrable by (eq:trace:Pbounds),
(eq:trace:FG), and (eq:trace:F0).  Integrating
(eq:trace:square) with weight t^beta and using
c_sigmaint t^beta P= N gives

 c_sigmaint_0^∞ t^beta G,dt
 =tr(ND)-frac1ptr(D^p)
  +c_sigmaint_0^∞ t^betamathcal D_DcalD_D,dt .

When Nge0 choose D=N^r.  Since rp=q, the first two terms equal
(1-tfrac1p)tr(N^q)=tr(N^q)/q.  Substituting into
(eq:trace:weighted) proves (eq:trace:deficit).  Both integrands
in (eq:trace:deficit) are nonnegative, so equality forces them to
vanish for almost every t, hence by continuity for every t>0.

Finally, if B and V commute the symbol condition implies Vge bI;
simultaneous diagonalisation gives R(t)=(tI+V)^{-1/2}, N=V^sigma,
Z=0, and both remainders vanish, as scalar equality requires.

# The field and the connecting path
% =====================================================================

To run the complete-the-square step of the action estimate we need two
objects: a symmetric matrix field M_delta that mimics the power
(K^2-B^2)^sigma near values that matter, and a symmetric path B(x)
running from K to -K that solves an ODE of the form
ε B'=M_delta(B)-AA^T.  This section constructs both.  Set
delta>0 and recall beta=sigma-tfrac12, and let c_sigma be the
constant of (eq:trace:normalization).

## A scalar building block
On yge0, define

 f_delta(y)=(c_sigma)/(pi)int_0^∞ t^beta
   (frac1{t+delta}-frac1{t+delta+y}),dt,

and extend to y<0 by the tangent line f_delta'(0)y.  For yge0,

 f_delta'(y)=a_sigma(y+delta)^{beta-1},
 
 a_sigma=(c_sigma)/(pi)int_0^∞
   (u^beta)/((1+u)^2),du>0 .

Both integrals converge because -tfrac12<beta<tfrac12.  Thus f_delta
is C^1, strictly increasing, and concave on yge0; the tangent
extension makes it C^1 on all of R and lets the functional calculus
below accept arguments with negative eigenvalues.

Set

 Q_s(B)=s^2I-2sB+K^2,

and

 M_delta(B)=lim_{Rto∞}int_{-R}^{R}
   (f_delta(Q_s(B))-f_delta(s^2)I),ds,
  BinSym_n,

where the symmetric cutoff cancels the leading odd term
-2sf_delta'(s^2)B.  The scalar counterpart is

 mu_delta(z)=int_R(f_delta(s^2+z)-f_delta(s^2)),ds,
  zinR .


[Regularised field]
The limit (eq:field:M) exists locally uniformly, and
M_delta:Sym_ntoSym_n is locally Lipschitz with symmetric values.
There is a C^1 function J_delta:Sym_ntoR, J_delta(0)=0, with

 dJ_delta(B)[H]=-tr(M_delta(B)H).

The scalar function mu_delta is locally Lipschitz and strictly
increasing with mu_delta(0)=0, and for zge0

 mu_delta(z)=(z+delta)^sigma-delta^sigma .

For every real unit vector e and every standard basis vector e_i,

 e^{mathsf T}sfTM_delta(B)e
  &lemu_delta(e^{mathsf T}sfTK^2e-(e^{mathsf T}sfTBe)^2),
  
 Be_i=ye_iquad&⇒
 M_delta(B)e_i=mu_delta(k_i^2-y^2),e_i .
  

For every diagonal sign matrix S,

 M_delta(SBS)=S,M_delta(B),S .

Finally,

 J_delta(-K)-J_delta(K)
 =sum_{i=1}^nint_{-k_i}^{k_i}mu_delta(k_i^2-s^2),ds
 ;→;
 b_sigmasum_{i=1}^n k_i^{2sigma+1}
 (delta↓0).



Proof. 
Matrix calculus bound.  If h is Lipschitz on an interval
containing the spectra of Hermitian X,Y, then

 |h(X)-h(Y)|_{HS}leLip(h),|X-Y|_{HS}.

Indeed, take eigenbases e_i of X and v_j of Y; the mixed entries
of h(X)-h(Y) and of X-Y are (h(lambda_i)-h(nu_j))< e_i,v_j>
and (lambda_i-nu_j)< e_i,v_j>; square the scalar Lipschitz
inequality and sum.

Convergence of the field.  On a bounded set of B's the spectrum
of E_s(B)=-2sB+K^2 lies in [-a|s|,a|s|] for all large |s|.  For

(eq:field:fderivative) gives

[

]
where |T_s(B)|_{HS} and the local Lipschitz constant of T_s are

are integrable at infinity; the odd term cancels at each symmetric cutoff,
and on bounded s-intervals (eq:field:HS) applies directly to

Primitive.  Let g_delta'=f_delta.  Trace differentiation gives
d,tr(g_delta(X))[H]=tr(f_delta(X)H) (true for polynomials by
cyclicity, then for g_delta by C^1 approximation on compact spectral
intervals).  For sne0 the function
[

        +f_delta(s^2)tr B
]
has differential -tr((f_delta(Q_s(B))-f_delta(s^2)I)H); at

from 0 defines J_delta, proving (eq:field:primitive).

Scalar bounds and profile.  For bounded z, the integrand in
(eq:field:mu) and its Lipschitz constant in z are O(|s|^{2beta-2})

]
which the scaling identity (eq:trace:scaling) evaluates as

   (f_delta((s-a)^2+z)-f_delta(s^2)),ds=mu_delta(z),

## Positive values of the field
The next proposition is the only place where the sign of an argument of
M_delta matters.  It says that if M_delta(B) is itself a positive
matrix (as it will be once it equals AA^{mathsf T}sfT), the whole symbol
Q_s(B) is positive definite and the trace engine applies.

[Positive values of the field]
If M=M_delta(B)ge0, then

 Q_s(B)ge0 (sinR),
  0le Mlekappa^{2sigma}I .

Set V=K^2-B^2+delta I.  Then (sI-B)^2+V=Q_s(B)+delta Igedelta I,
and with R(t) and N as in (eq:trace:resolvents)--(eq:trace:N)
one has N=M+delta^sigma I.  In particular the hypotheses of
Theorem~thm:trace hold with this B and V.


Proof. 
By (eq:field:direction) and strict increase of mu_delta,
positivity of M forces e^{mathsf T}sfTK^2ege(e^{mathsf T}sfTBe)^2 for every
unit e.  Completing the scalar square,
[
 e^{mathsf T}sfTQ_s(B)e=(s-e^{mathsf T}sfTBe)^2
   +e^{mathsf T}sfTK^2e-(e^{mathsf T}sfTBe)^2ge0,
]
so Q_s(B)ge0 as a Hermitian matrix on C^n.  Also
Mlemu_delta(kappa^2)Ilekappa^{2sigma}I by (eq:field:direction)
and (eq:field:mupositive).

Write Y_s=(sI-B)^2.  By diagonalisation of B and (eq:field:shift),
the scalar subtraction f_delta(s^2)I in (eq:field:M) may be
replaced by f_delta(Y_s).  Since Q_s(B) and Y_s are both positive,
(eq:field:f) gives their difference under f_delta as a Stieltjes
integral
[

 ((Y_s+(t+delta)I)^{-1}-(Q_s(B)+(t+delta)I)^{-1}),dt .
]

identity bounds the integrand by Ct^beta(1+t+s^2)^{-2}; integrating in

[

   ((t+delta)^{-1/2}I-R(t)),dt .

Subtracting this from (eq:trace:N) and using (eq:trace:scaling)

## The connecting path
The final object of this section walks B from K to -K while tracking a
prescribed density AA^{mathsf T}sfT.  This is where topology enters: a
nonlinear equation for C must be solved, and the only obstruction would
be a parity count of zeros.

[Connecting path]

   =M_delta(B_ε)-A_ε A_ε^{mathsf T}sfT,
  B_ε(x_-)=K, B_ε(x_+)=-K,

and

 sup_{xinoverlineoverlineΩ}Ω|B_ε(x)|_{op}lekappa,
 
 sup_{0<εle1}|C_ε|_{HS}<∞,

the second bound for fixed delta,K,Ω.


Proof. 
A cutoff and a homotopy.  Use the continuous representative of U.
Choose a smooth nonnegative compactly supported cutoff chi on Sym_n
equal to 1 on {B:|B|_{op}lekappa} and invariant under conjugation
by diagonal sign matrices (averaging over the finite sign group produces

globally Lipschitz.  Fix ε>0.  For C lower triangular and
thetain[0,1], the equation

  B(x_-)=K,

has a unique C^1 solution on overlineoverlineΩ;Ω; define the continuous
endpoint map Phi(C,theta)=B(x_+)+KinSym_n.

Sign symmetry.  Let mathcal GcalG be the group of diagonal sign
matrices, acting by Cmapsto SC on lower triangular matrices and by
conjugation on Sym_n.  Equivariance of the field and uniqueness of the
ODE give

Three facts about the zero set.
(i) Compactness.  At a zero, integrating (eq:path:homotopy)
gives CC^{mathsf T}sfT=2ε K+thetaint_Ωwidehatwidehat M(B(x))M(B(x)),dx,
so the zeros lie in a bounded set and their joint set in

0<εle1 this also gives the uniform bound

   +n|Ω|sup_{BinSym_n}|widehatwidehat M(B)M(B)|_{op}.

(ii) Freeness.  No row of C can vanish at a zero.  Otherwise the
reflection changing only row i would fix C; by (eq:path:equivariance)

gives ε y'=thetachi(B(x))mu_delta(k_i^2-y^2) with y(x_-)=k_i.
Uniqueness forces yequiv k_i, contradicting y(x_+)=-k_i.  Thus
mathcal GcalG acts freely on the zero set.
(iii) One regular orbit at theta=0.  There

rest of the first column to vanish, and induction proceeds.  These 2^n
zeros form one sign orbit.  At each, the derivative in a lower triangular
direction H has diagonal entries -2ε^{-1}d_iH_{ii} and lower
off-diagonal entries -ε^{-1}d_jH_{ij} (i>j), an isomorphism

Continuation by parity.  Suppose there were no zero at theta=1.
Compactness and freeness give a bounded invariant open set
OsubsetR^{ntimes n}_{low} containing the projection of every

for |Phi|_{HS} on (∂ Otimes[0,1])cup(overlineoverline OOtimes{1}).
Reparametrise theta to agree with the initial map on an initial collar
and retain this gap; extend F constantly past both ends, smooth by
convolution, splice to the initial map on a smaller collar, and average in
the form Psi(C,theta)=frac1{|mathcal G|}calG|sum_S SPsi_0(SC,theta)S to
get a smooth equivariant approximation Psi still equal to the initial map
on the collar and close enough to preserve the gap.

Freeness allows equivariant perturbations with arbitrary target directions:
at C_0inoverlineoverline OO choose a ball whose sign translates are disjoint and
a bump b supported there with b(C_0)=1; then
V_H(C)=sum_S b(SC)SHS is equivariant with V_H(C_0)=H.  Finitely many
such maps V_1,...,V_N span Sym_n at every point.  Let hge0 be
smooth, vanish near 0, and positive off the collar, and set

persists; at a zero with 0<theta<1 the full differential in
(C,theta,alpha) is onto (parameter directions span the target where
h>0; the C-differential is the above isomorphism where h=0).  Hence
the interior zero set is a smooth manifold, and Sard's theorem
[cite] applied to projection onto the parameter ball gives
arbitrarily small alpha for which 0 is a regular value.

For such alpha the zero set in Otimes[0,1] is a compact smooth
one-dimensional manifold with boundary.  The gap excludes boundary zeros
except at theta=0, where the initial collar gives exactly the 2^n
regular zeros.  The free action has a quotient that is again a compact
one-dimensional manifold with boundary (a neighbourhood disjoint from its
nontrivial translates gives a local interval or half-interval chart); all
2^n boundary points form one orbit, so the quotient has exactly one
boundary point.  A compact one-dimensional manifold is a finite union of
circles and closed intervals, which cannot have exactly one boundary point.
Contradiction.  Hence Phi(cdot,1) has a zero.

Removing the cutoff.  For the resulting path and any real unit e,
put y=e^{mathsf T}sfTBe.  Whenever |y|>kappa, (eq:field:direction)
gives e^{mathsf T}sfTM_delta(B)elemu_delta(e^{mathsf T}sfTK^2e-y^2)<0.

above kappa cannot start from kappa while y is nonincreasing, and an
excursion below -kappa cannot return to -kappa while y is
nonincreasing.  So |B(x)|_{op}lekappa for all x, which forces
chi(B)=1 and hence (eq:path:ode) with the untruncated field.
Equation~(eq:path:Cbound) supplies the remaining bound in
(eq:path:bounds).

# The finite-interval action estimate
% =====================================================================

This is the central estimate.  It turns the trial functions into a
quantitative lower bound that measures, in operator norm, how far the
data are from the two algebraic equality relations.  The uniformity of the
coefficient c_* in the row count is what will later allow an
a-priori-infinite eigenvalue list.

Fix positive integers n,d, a bounded interval Ω=(x_-,x_+), and a
diagonal K=diag(ell_1,...,ell_n) with 0<ell_ilekappa.  For
Ain H^1_0(Ω;R^{ntimes d}) set rho=AA^{mathsf T}sfT and

 mathcal E_K(A)=calE_K(A)=int_Ω
  (|A'|_{HS}^2+tr(K^2rho)-tr(rho^q)),dx .

For pointwise values AinR^{ntimes d} and BinSym_n, define the
algebraic defect

 Phi_K(A,B)=minBigl{1,;
   |[B,K^2]|_{op}^2+bigl|K^2-B^2-(AA^{mathsf T}sfT)^rbigr|_{op}^2
   Bigr}.

Thus Phi_K=0 encodes the two relations [B,K^2]=0 and
rho=(K^2-B^2)^sigma, while A'-BA=0 is the first-order relation.

[Quantitative action estimate]
Let 0<sigma<1 and kappa>0.  There is c_*=c_*(sigma,kappa)>0 with
the following property.  For n,d,Ω,K as above, suppose
Uin H^1_0(Ω;R^{ntimes d}) satisfies
int_Ω UU^{mathsf T}sfT,dx=I_n.  For every eta>0 there exist a
real lower triangular CinR^{ntimes n} and a path
Bin C^1(overlineoverlineΩ;Ω;Sym_n) with

 sup_{xinoverlineoverlineΩ}Ω|B(x)|_{op}lekappa,

such that, for A=CU,

 mathcal E_K(A)calE_K(A)ge b_sigmasum_{i=1}^nell_i^{2gamma}
   +int_Ω(|A'-BA|_{HS}^2+c_*Phi_K(A,B)),dx-eta .

The constant c_* is independent of n,d,Ω,U and of
min_iell_i.


The proof has two ingredients.  The first is a constraint lemma, obtained
by feeding the trace engine (Proposition~prop:field-pos plus
Theorem~thm:trace) into a penalty.  The second completes the square
along the path of Proposition~prop:path; the boundary term there is
exactly J_delta(-K)-J_delta(K), which tends to
b_sigmasumell_i^{2gamma}.  We prove the constraint lemma first.

## The penalty limit and the proof of the action estimate
Proof. [Proof of Theorem~thm:action]
Fix 0<deltale1.  Proposition~prop:path gives, for every
0<εle1, a lower triangular C_ε and a path
B_ε with the endpoints and norm bound of
(eq:act:endpointB) such that, for A_ε=C_ε U,

   =M_delta(B_ε)-A_ε A_ε^{mathsf T}sfT.

 &=2tr((A')^{mathsf T}sfTBA)
    -tr((M_delta(B)-AA^{mathsf T}sfT)B')
 &=2tr((A')^{mathsf T}sfTBA)-ε^{-1}u_delta(A,B).

 mathcal E_K(A)calE_K(A)
 =J_delta(-K)-J_delta(K)
  +int_Ω(|A'-BA|_{HS}^2-F(A,B)
       +ε^{-1}u_delta(A,B)),dx .

% =====================================================================

Before turning to equality we record that the action estimate already
gives the sharp bound (eq:sharp); this is the inequality half of
Theorem~thm:main.

[Sharp spectral bound]

 Tr(H_W)_-^gammale(D_sigma)/(b_sigma)int_Rtr(W^p).



Proof. 
First suppose W is real symmetric.  Choose any finite list of negative

inverse square root of its Gram matrix: the Gram matrix tends to I_n and
the form matrix tends to -diag(k_i^2) by Lemma~lem:spectral, so an
operator-norm error below xi gives (eq:bound:trial).

Let U have rows u_i and let Ω contain their supports.  For every

[

]

[

]

We now assume equality holds and extract information.  The difficulty is

## Finite spectral approximations and vanishing errors
Let n_j=j when mathcal I=calI=mathbb NbbN and n_j=|mathcal I|calI| when
mathcal IcalI is finite.  Choose 0<xi_j<1/j with

The same shift xi_j is used for every eigenvalue, so the differences of
their squares are unchanged.  Set
K_j=diag(ell_{j,1},...,ell_{j,n_j}).

By (eq:bound:trial) we can choose real smooth compactly supported
orthonormal u_{j,1},...,u_{j,n_j} with H^1 errors below 1/j and
form-matrix error below xi_j:

 |u_{j,i}-varphi_i|_{H^1}<1/j,
 
 (h_W[u_{j,i},u_{j,l}])_{i,l=1}^{n_j}le-K_j^2 .

Let U_j have rows u_{j,i} and choose a bounded interval Ω_j
containing their supports.  Apply Theorem~thm:action with
K=K_j, d=m, kappa=k_1, and eta=1/j: it gives a lower triangular
C_j, a symmetric path B_j with |B_j|_{op}lekappa, and
A_j=C_jU_j.  Extend A_j by zero outside Ω_j and B_j by K_j
to the left and -K_j to the right; all errors vanish outside Ω_j.

The lower triangular form of C_j gives an extra nonnegative error.  Put
Z_j=A_j^TA_j, an mtimes m positive semidefinite matrix.  By
(eq:comp:form),

 mathcal E_calE_{K_j}(A_j)

=-sum_{lle i}(C_j)_{il}^{,2}(k_l^2-k_i^2); summing over i and using
that A_jA_j^T and Z_j share nonzero eigenvalues gives
(eq:comp:triangular).  The ordering of the k_i makes each summand
of T_j nonnegative.

Lemma~lem:young, Y(W,Z)=0 iff W=qZ^r.  Combining
Theorem~thm:action, (eq:comp:equality), and

integral errors and T_j tend to zero.

A uniform bound on A_j will also be needed.  The coercive estimate
(eq:youngcoercive) gives

and since Z_j is mtimes m, scalar H"older gives
|A_j|_{HS}^{2q}=(tr Z_j)^qle m^{q-1}tr(Z_j^q).  With
(eq:comp:vanish) this bounds A_j uniformly in L^{2q}(R) in the

## Convergence of each fixed row
For fixed iinmathcal I,calI, the coefficients (C_j)_{il}, lle i, are
bounded as jto∞.  Choose a bounded interval J_i so large that
[

       getfrac34 I_i,
]
possible because the full Gram matrix is the identity.  By
(eq:comp:form) the corresponding Gram matrix of the u_{j,l} is at
least tfrac12 I_i for large j, so
[

 leint_{J_i}|(A_j)_{i,cdot}|^2,dx
 le|J_i|^{1-1/q}(int_{J_i}|A_j|_{HS}^{2q},dx)^{1/q},

uniformly bounded.  Lower triangularity keeps the number of coefficients
in a fixed row finite.  A diagonal subsequence makes each (C_j)_{il}
converge to a limit c_{il}, lle i.  From T_jto0 and
(eq:comp:triangular),

 c_{il}=0quadwhenever k_l>k_i .

Define a_i=sum_{lle i}c_{il}varphi_l, a finite sum; then
(eq:comp:form) gives

 (A_j)_{i,cdot}→ a_iquadin H^1(R;R^m)
 quadfor every fixed i.

The ordering of the eigenvalues together with
(eq:comp:gapsuppress) shows only eigenfunctions with eigenvalue
-k_i^2 occur in a_i, proving (eq:comp:roweig) (the distributional
form equation follows by testing against smooth compactly supported
functions; the potential is locally integrable and the rows locally
bounded).

Using the continuous, locally absolutely continuous representatives and
H^1(R)subset C_b(R), (eq:comp:rowconv) also gives pointwise
convergence at every x.  Passing to a further subsequence we may arrange
that the integrals of the nonnegative integrand in (eq:comp:vanish)
are summable and that, for every fixed i, the squared L^2 errors of
the derivatives in (eq:comp:rowconv) are summable (a diagonal choice
successively takes the first j available derivative errors and the
integral error below 2^{-j}).  Consequently, on a common set of full

 Y(W,Z_j)to0,
 |A_j'-B_jA_j|_{HS}to0,
 Phi_{K_j}(A_j,B_j)to0,
 (A_j')_{i,cdot}(x)to a_i'(x) ∀ i .

We also exclude the null set where W is not a finite positive semidefinite

## Tail control in the row-index space
Pad A_j,B_j,K_j with zeros in the unused coordinates of mathscr H,scrH, so
A_j:R^mtomathscr HscrH and B_j,K_j are self-adjoint finite-rank operators.
Since the omitted k_i tend to zero,

 |K_j^2-Λ^2|_{op}
 lemax{xi_j,sup_{i>n_j}k_i^2}→0 .

Fix x in the full-measure set of (eq:comp:pointwise) and suppress
it.  Write rho_j=A_jA_j^T.  Convergence of Phi to zero gives

 |[B_j,K_j^2]|_{op}to0,
  e_j:=|K_j^2-B_j^2-rho_j^r|_{op}to0 .

For hge1 let P_h project onto the first h coordinates, Q_h=I-P_h,
and s_h=sup_{i>h}k_i^2to0.  For unit v in the range of Q_h,

 |B_jv|^2+< v,rho_j^rv>le s_h+e_j .

By Jensen for the spectral measure of rho_j (r>1),
< v,rho_jv>^rle< v,rho_j^rv>, so
|Q_hrho_jQ_h|_{op}le(s_h+e_j)^{1/r}.  Although mathscr HscrH may be
infinite dimensional, Q_hrho_jQ_h has rank at most m because A_j has
m columns; its trace is at most m times its operator norm.  Hence

 |Q_hA_j|_{HS}^2le m(s_h+e_j)^{1/r},
 
 |B_jQ_h|_{op}le(s_h+e_j)^{1/2}.

The first bound converts decay of the eigenvalue parameters into decay of
the total mass of all omitted rows, with factor the fixed physical
dimension m rather than the number of retained eigenfunctions.

For fixed h the first h rows of A_j converge by
(eq:comp:rowconv); (eq:comp:tailbounds) makes the remaining rows
uniformly small by taking h large then j large.  Thus A_j is Cauchy in
Hilbert--Schmidt norm and converges to an operator A:R^mtomathscr HscrH
whose rows are the a_i(x):

 A_j→ Aquadin Hilbert--Schmidt norm.

This holds for the whole already-chosen subsequence and needs no further
choice depending on x.

For B_j only pointwise compactness is needed.  Finite compressions
P_hB_jP_h are bounded in finite-dimensional spaces, so a diagonal
subsequence makes them converge for every h; self-adjointness and the
second bound in (eq:comp:tailbounds) give
|B_j-P_hB_jP_h|_{op}le2|B_jQ_h|_{op}le2(s_h+e_j)^{1/2}.  The
subsequence converges in operator norm to a self-adjoint B,
|B|_{op}lekappa (possibly depending on x).

Now pass to all products.  Hilbert--Schmidt convergence of A_j implies
operator-norm convergence of A_jA_j^T and A_j^TA_j; continuous
functional calculus for nonnegative operators gives convergence of their
rth powers.  With (eq:comp:diagonal) and
(eq:comp:algebraerr) this proves
[B,Λ^2]=0 and Λ^2-B^2=(AA^T)^r.  Also
B_jA_jto BA in Hilbert--Schmidt norm; taking the ith row and using the
derivative convergence and the first-order error in
(eq:comp:pointwise) gives a_i'(x)=(BA)_{i,cdot} for every i.
Finally continuity of the finite-dimensional Young deficit and
Y(W,A_j^TA_j)to0 give Y(W,A^TA)=0, and Lemma~lem:young gives
W=q(A^TA)^r.

All statements hold on one common set of full measure.  No function
xmapsto B(x) is selected; only the existence of a suitable operator at
each such point enters later.  This proves Proposition~prop:relations.

# Fixed channels and the equality profiles
% =====================================================================

The pointwise relations of Proposition~prop:relations involve an
auxiliary operator B that may depend on x.  We now show the channel
subspaces are constant, so that on each channel B becomes a fixed matrix
obeying an ODE whose solutions are the profiles of (eq:profile).

## The channel subspaces are constant
Assume first that W is real and is a nonzero equality potential.  Use the
rows a_i, the numbers k_i, and Λ=diag(k_i) from
Proposition~prop:relations.  For each distinct value alpha>0 among
the k_i, let n_alpha be its finite multiplicity and let G_alpha be
the n_alphatimes m matrix whose rows are the a_i with k_i=alpha;
each entry is in H^1(R) and we use its continuous representative.

At every point of the common full-measure set of Proposition~prop:relations
choose one of the operators B it supplies.  Since B commutes with
Λ^2 it preserves each eigenspace, and
AA^T=(Λ^2-B^2)^{1/r} commutes with B and Λ^2.  Writing
B_alpha for the block on the alpha^2-eigenspace,

 G_alpha G_beta^T&=0quad(alphanebeta),
 
 G_alpha'&=B_alpha G_alpha,
 & [B_alpha,G_alpha G_alpha^T]&=0nonumber
 alpha^2I-B_alpha^2&=(G_alpha G_alpha^T)^r,
 & |B_alpha|_{op}&lekappa .
 

These hold almost everywhere with no compatibility between the choices of
B at different points.


For every alpha the subspace mathcal R_calR_alpha=(ker G_alpha(x))^perp
subsetR^m is independent of x.  The nonzero mathcal R_calR_alpha are
mutually orthogonal and

 sum_alphadimmathcal R_calR_alphale m .

In particular only finitely many G_alpha are nonzero.  The potential W
has a continuous representative, equal to zero on the orthogonal complement
of the mathcal R_calR_alpha and to q(G_alpha^TG_alpha)^r on
mathcal R_calR_alpha.  With this representative every G_alpha is C^2.


Proof. 
Fix vinR^m.  From (eq:rig:blocksquare),
|(G_alpha v)'|lekappa|G_alpha v| almost everywhere, and G_alpha v
is locally absolutely continuous.  If it vanishes at x_0, integration and
Gronwall's inequality, first right and then left of x_0, show it vanishes
everywhere; hence ker G_alpha(x) is constant, including off the original
full-measure set.

The products in (eq:rig:orthogonal) are continuous, so their
almost-everywhere vanishing is everywhere; thus the constant row spaces
mathcal R_calR_alpha are mutually orthogonal, giving
(eq:rig:dimbound) and finiteness of the nonzero blocks.  On the
full-measure set, A^TA=sum_alpha G_alpha^TG_alpha with only finitely
many nonzero summands, each acting on its own mathcal R_calR_alpha, so
(eq:comp:potential) gives

 W=qsum_alpha(G_alpha^TG_alpha)^r quadalmost everywhere.

The right side is continuous; we use it as the representative of W.  The
row equation (eq:comp:roweig) then reads
G_alpha''=alpha^2G_alpha-G_alpha W in distributions, whose right side
is continuous; integrating twice, G_alpha is C^2.


The row spaces split R^m into finitely many constant channels.  To find
the potential on one channel we also need a constant range for G_alpha in
the eigenfunction index space.


For alpha with ell:=dimmathcal R_calR_alpha>0, the range of
G_alpha(x) in R^{n_alpha} is independent of x.  In constant
orthonormal bases of this range and of mathcal R_calR_alpha the restriction
of G_alpha is an invertible C^2 matrix D:RtoR^{elltimesell}, and
B_0=D'D^{-1} is C^1 with, everywhere,

 B_0=B_0^T,
 alpha^2I-B_0^2=(DD^T)^r>0,
 B_0'=r(B_0^2-alpha^2I).



Proof. 
Choose a constant orthonormal basis of mathcal R_calR_alpha and let E(x) be
the matrix of G_alpha(x) restricted to it; it has full column rank ell
everywhere.  Its range projection is P=E(E^TE)^{-1}E^T.  The commutator in
(eq:rig:blocksquare) shows B_alpha preserves the range of
G_alpha G_alpha^T, which is the range of E; thus E'=B_alpha E has
columns in this range almost everywhere.  Differentiating P gives
P'=(I-P)E'(E^TE)^{-1}E^T+E(E^TE)^{-1}(E')^T(I-P)=0 almost everywhere,
and since P is C^1 it is constant.

With constant orthonormal bases of both spaces, D is invertible C^2 and
D'D^{-1} is, almost everywhere, the restriction of B_alpha to its
invariant range.  Then the symmetry and the square identity in
(eq:rig:active) hold almost everywhere and extend by continuity;
positivity is strict because D is invertible.  On mathcal R_calR_alpha,
(eq:rig:contpot) makes the potential q(D^TD)^r, so
(eq:comp:roweig) becomes D''=alpha^2D-q(DD^T)^rD (using a
singular-value decomposition for the second form).  Differentiating
B_0=D'D^{-1} with q=r+1,
[
 B_0'=D''D^{-1}-B_0^2
     =alpha^2I-q(alpha^2I-B_0^2)-B_0^2
     =r(B_0^2-alpha^2I).
]


## The matrix Riccati equation separates the profiles
Fix a nonzero block and the matrix B_0.  Orthogonally diagonalise the
symmetric B_0(x_*) at one point.  The right side of the Riccati equation is
a polynomial in B_0, so uniqueness of the matrix ODE preserves the
diagonal form.  The strict inequality in (eq:rig:active) places each
initial diagonal entry b_j(x_*) in (-alpha,alpha), and the scalar
equation b_j'=r(b_j^2-alpha^2) has the unique solution with that value

 b_j(x)=-alphatanh!(ralpha(x-y_j)), 1le jleell,

for a unique centre y_j.  The diagonal matrix with these entries solves the
same initial-value problem as B_0 on all of R, so uniqueness gives
equality everywhere.

Now (eq:rig:active) shows DD^T is diagonal in these coordinates.
Since D'=diag(b_j)D, each row of D is a positive scalar multiple of its
value at x_*; the rows are nonzero and mutually orthogonal (as DD^T is
diagonal).  Taking their normalised directions as a constant orthonormal
basis of mathcal R_calR_alpha makes D diagonal with positive entries.  The
potential in this fixed basis is diagonal, with entries

 q(alpha^2-b_j(x)^2)
 =(r+1)alpha^2,sech^2!(ralpha(x-y_j)).

Combining these bases over the finitely many nonzero mathcal R_calR_alpha and
adding a basis of their orthogonal complement gives, for real W, the form
(eq:classification) with at most m nonzero profiles by
(eq:rig:dimbound).  The zero potential corresponds to k=0.

## Complex Hermitian potentials
For a complex Hermitian W, take the realification widetilde W from
(eq:bound:realification), which is equivalent to W ⊕ overlineoverline WW
by a constant unitary matrix, with both inttr(W^p) and the negative
moment doubled.  Equality for W implies equality for the real
widetilde W, so the real result gives a constant unitary V with
[
 W(x) ⊕ overlineoverline W(x)W(x)
 =Vdiag(f_1(x),...,f_{2m}(x))V^*
 quadalmost everywhere,
]
each f_j a profile (eq:rig:profiles) or zero.  Let P be the
projection onto the first summand and widehatwidehat P=V^*PV.P=V^*PV.  Since P
commutes with W ⊕ overlineoverline W,W, we have
(f_i-f_j)widehatwidehat P_P_{ij}=0 almost everywhere, so widehatwidehat P_P_{ij}=0
whenever f_i,f_j are not equal almost everywhere.  Partition the indices
into groups of identical functions (including the zero function).  On each
group the diagonal potential is a scalar multiple of the identity, so any
constant orthonormal basis of the intersection of that coordinate group with
ranwidehatwidehat PP diagonalises it.  Transporting these bases by
V gives a constant unitary basis of C^m in which W has the asserted
profiles.  This proves necessity for complex Hermitian W.

## Every listed potential attains equality
It remains to verify attainment directly.  Fix a>0 and x_0inR and set
[
 V(x)=(r+1)a^2sech^2!(ra(x-x_0)),
  H=-∂_x^2-V .
]
Put w(x)=atanh(ra(x-x_0)) and Q=∂_x+w on
H^1(R).  Since w,w' are bounded, direct differentiation gives

 Q^*Q&=H+a^2,
 
 QQ^*&=-∂_x^2+a^2+(r-1)a^2sech^2!(ra(x-x_0))ge a^2,
 

the last inequality because r>1.  The equation Qpsi=0 has the
one-dimensional L^2 solution space
[
 ker Q=spanbigl{sech^{1/r}(ra(x-x_0))bigr},
]
so -a^2 is a simple eigenvalue of H, and (eq:rig:factor) excludes
eigenvalues below it.  If Hphi=lambdaphi with -a^2<lambda<0, put
mu=lambda+a^2>0, g=Qphi; then |g|_2^2=mu|phi|_2^2>0,
Q^*Qphi=muphiinDomQ, so ginDom(QQ^*)
and QQ^*g=mu g, contradicting (eq:rig:partner) because mu<a^2.
Hence -a^2 is the only negative eigenvalue.

Finally 2p-1=2gamma and the substitution s=tanh y give

 int_R V(x)^p,dx
 &=((r+1)^p)/(r)a^{2gamma}int_Rsech^{2p}y,dy
 &=((r+1)^p)/(r)a^{2gamma}int_{-1}^{1}(1-s^2)^{p-1},ds
  =(b_sigma)/(D_sigma)a^{2gamma},

using (r+1)^p/r=D_sigma^{-1} and p-1=sigma.  Therefore
Tr(H)_-^gamma=a^{2gamma}=C_gammaint_R V^p.  The zero potential
contributes zero to both sides; finite orthogonal direct sums add both
spectral moments and trace integrals, and constant unitary conjugation
changes neither.  So every potential listed in Theorem~thm:main
attains equality, completing the proof.
