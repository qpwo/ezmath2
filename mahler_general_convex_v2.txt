# The Mahler Conjecture for General Convex Bodies




maketitle

Abstract: 
We prove the Mahler conjecture for general convex bodies, in every
dimension and with no symmetry or regularity hypothesis. For every
convex body $KsubsetR^n$ and its Santal'o point $s(K)$,
[
 |K|,|(K-s(K))^circ| ge frac{(n+1)^{n+1}}{(n!)^2},
]
with equality if and only if $K$ is a simplex. Two consequences are
given: a sharp functional (log-concave) Mahler inequality, and an
entropy--transport inequality.

# Introduction and overview

A convex body in $R^n$ is a compact convex set with nonempty
interior.  Write $|A|$ for $n$-dimensional Lebesgue volume and
$<cdot,cdot>$ for the Euclidean inner product.  A simplex
is the convex hull of $n+1$ affinely independent points.

Polarity and the Santal'o point.: 
For $zinintK$ let
[
 (K-z)^circ={yinR^n: < y,x-z>le 1 for all xin K}
]
be the polar body of $K$ re-centered at $z$.  The function
$zmapsto |(K-z)^circ|$ is strictly convex on $intK$ and
tends to $+∞$ at the boundary; hence it has a unique interior
minimizer, the Santal'o point $s(K)$.  (We prove this in
Section~sec:cones.)  The volume product is
[
 P(K)=|K|,|(K-s(K))^circ|
     =inf_{zinintK}|K|,|(K-z)^circ|.
]
It is invariant under invertible affine maps.

[Mahler conjecture]
For every $nge1$ and every convex body $KsubsetR^n$,
[
 P(K) ge frac{(n+1)^{n+1}}{(n!)^2}.
]
Equality holds if and only if $K$ is a simplex.


Two remarks.  First, because $P(K)$ is the minimum over translation
points, Theorem~thm:mahler also gives the displayed lower bound
for $|K|,|(K-z)^circ|$ at every interior point $z$.
Second, the symmetric Mahler conjecture asks for the stronger
constant $4^n/n!$ on the restricted class of centrally symmetric bodies.
That sharper statement is different and is proved separately in the
companion paper~[cite]; the argument here does
not use symmetry.

Context.: 
The problem originates with Mahler, who settled the planar case and its
triangle equality among polygons [cite], and who formulated
the higher-dimensional symmetric version [cite].  Meyer gave
a new proof of the planar general inequality with a complete equality
characterization (triangles are the only minimizers)
[cite].  The affine center used here belongs to Santal'o's
theory [cite].  Asymptotic lower bounds (inverse
Santal'o / Bourgain--Milman type) go back to
[cite]; these give exponential constants but not
the sharp simplex value.  Sharp results for special classes include the
polytope case with at most $n+3$ vertices via shadow systems
[cite], strict local minimality at every simplex
[cite], the three-dimensional symmetric case
[cite], and the general three-dimensional case
[cite].  The present argument works for arbitrary bodies
in all dimensions and also pins down the global equality case.

The proof at a glance.: 
The proof has a single spine:
[label=arabic*.]
item Cone reduction (Section~sec:cones):  translate $K$
  and lift it to a cone $CsubsetR^{n+1}$.  The volume product equals,
  up to an explicit constant, a product of two Laplace integrals on the
  cone and its positive dual $D$.  It suffices to show that product is
  $ge1$.
item Gaussian projections (Section~sec:proj):  build two
  maps of a Gaussian into the cones using orthogonal projections, with
  carefully chosen biases and covariance.  Their Jacobians turn the
  Laplace product into an entropy-type lower bound.
item Entropy inequality (Section~sec:entropy):  change of
  variables plus Jensen reduces the cone inequality to $mathcal EcalEle0$
  for an explicit error functional $mathcal E$.calE$.
item Matrix comparison (Sections~sec:quad--sec:layers):
  compare the whole field of projection derivatives to spectral
  thresholds of a linear Gaussian matrix $L=sum_i G_iM_i$.  Hermite
  calculus separates the linear part; the leftover ``layer'' terms are
  bounded below by zero.
item Linear field and equality
  (Section~sec:linear):  express the remaining terms as averages
  over eigenvalue pairs of $L$, absorb them with a strict one-variable
  inequality, and show equality forces the projection to be
  coordinatewise, hence $K$ a simplex.

The dimension-independent scalar inequalities used throughout are
collected once (Proposition~prop:scalar-input) and proved in the
two appendices, Sections~sec:scalar and~sec:segments.

Functional and transport consequences.: 
The geometric theorem implies a sharp functional (log-concave) Mahler
inequality for arbitrary convex potentials with no centering
assumption:
[Functional Mahler]
For every $nge1$ and every proper lower-semicontinuous convex
$varphi:R^ntoRcup{+∞}$ with
$0<int e^{-varphi}<∞$,
[
 Big(int_{R^n}e^{-varphi(x)},dxBig)
 Big(int_{R^n}e^{-varphi^*(y)},dyBig) ge e^n,
]
where $varphi^*(y)=sup_x{< x,y>-varphi(x)}$.
The second integral may be infinite, in which case the inequality is
trivial.

This is sharp ($e^n$ is attained).  Via Gozlan's equivalence it yields
an entropy--transport inequality (Corollary~cor:entropy-transport).
Both are proved in Section~sec:consequences and are not
inputs to the geometric argument.

# Polarity, the cone reduction, and the scalar input

## The minimizing center
The support function is $h_K(u)=sup_{xin K}< u,x>$.  For
$zinintK$ and $nge2$, polar coordinates give
[
 |(K-z)^circ|=frac1nint_{S^{n-1}}big(h_K(u)-< z,u>big)^{-n},dsigma(u),
]
where $sigma$ is surface measure on the unit sphere.  As a function of
$z$ this is strictly convex: $tmapsto t^{-n}$ is strictly convex for
$t>0$, and two distinct centers have inner products differing on a set
of positive surface measure.  It blows up at $∂ K$: at a limiting
boundary point with supporting normal $u_0$, the quantity
$ε=h_K(u_0)-< z,u_0>to0$; the integrands share a
Lipschitz constant in $zin K$, are $O(ε)$ on a spherical cap
of radius $ε$, and that cap has measure $gtrsim
ε^{n-1}$, so its contribution is $gtrsimε^{-1}$.
Hence a unique interior minimizer exists.  In dimension one, with
$K=[a,b]$, the same conclusion follows from
$(z-a)^{-1}+(b-z)^{-1}$.

For an invertible affine map $xmapsto Bx+a$ we have
$(BK+a-(Bz+a))^circ=B^{-mathsf T}sfT(K-z)^circ$, the two volume factors
change by reciprocal determinants, and $s(BK+a)=Bs(K)+a$.  Thus $P$ is
affine-invariant and the Santal'o point is affine-equivariant.

## The cone reduction
Put $m=n+1$.  Fix $z_0inintK$ and define the cone
[
 C={(y,t): tge0, yin t(K-z_0)},qquad
 D=C^*={w: < w,x>ge0 for all xin C}.
]
Here the star is positive duality.  Both cones are closed, convex,
solid (nonempty interior) and pointed (contain no line).  For interior
vectors $Vin D$ and $Uin C$ write the Laplace integrals
[
 chi_C(V)=int_C e^{-< V,x>},dx,qquad
 chi_D(U)=int_D e^{-< U,x>},dx
]
in $m$-dimensional Lebesgue measure.

[Cone reduction]
For $U=(0,m)$ and $V=(0,1)$,
[
 chi_C(V),chi_D(U)=((n!)^2)/(m^m),|K|,|(K-z_0)^circ|.
]
For any closed convex solid pointed cone $C$ with dual $D=C^*$
and interior $Uin C$, $Vin D$, both Laplace integrals are finite, and
their product and the inner product $< U,V>$ are preserved
under $(C,D,U,V)mapsto(BC,B^{-mathsf T}sfTD,BU,B^{-mathsf T}sfTV)$ for
every invertible linear map $B$.


Proof. 
A point $(w,t)$ lies in $D$ iff $tgesup_{xin K-z_0}< -w,x>$.
Since $0$ is interior to $K-z_0$ this forces $tge0$, and the slice at
height $t$ is $-t(K-z_0)^circ$.  Integrating slices,
[
 chi_C((0,1))=n!,|K|,qquad
 chi_D((0,m))=n!,m^{-m},|(K-z_0)^circ|,
]
giving the product formula.  Interiority of $V$ gives
$< V,x>ge c|x|$ on $C$, and interiority of $U$ the analogue
on $D$, so both integrals are finite.  Under the linear rescaling the
two change-of-variables factors are $|det B|$ and $|det B|^{-1}$,
while inner products in the exponentials are unchanged.


By Lemma~lem:cone-reduction it suffices to prove

 chi_C(V),chi_D(U) ge 1

whenever $CsubsetR^m$ is closed, convex, solid, pointed, $D=C^*$,
$UinintC$, $VinintD$, and
$< U,V>=m$.  These are the standing cone
assumptions; the constant $m$ in the inner product is what makes the
Gaussian normalization below come out exactly.  We will additionally
see that equality forces $C$ to be a linear image of an orthant, which
by Section~sec:linear is equivalent to $K$ being a simplex.

# Biased Gaussian projections and simultaneous normalization


We now attach to the cone data a family of Gaussian projections whose
means move along a fixed curve in $C$.  Their derivative field supplies
both the Jacobians in the entropy estimate and the matrix fields in the
later quadratic analysis.  Throughout, fix cone data satisfying the
standing assumptions and a symmetric $T$ with
[
 t_-Ile Tle t_+I,qquad Σ=I+T,qquad Z=Σ^{1/2}G,
]
where $G$ is standard Gaussian in $R^m$.  Thus every eigenvalue of
$Σ$ lies in $[.978,1.064]$.  Expectation is in $G$ unless stated.

Matrix trace conventions.: 
For matrix fields, set
[
 tau B=tfrac1mEtrB,quad
 tau_Λ B=tau(Λ B),quad
 < B,E>_Λ=tau_Λ(Bcirc E),quad
 Bcirc E=tfrac12(BE+EB),
]
for symmetric $B,E$ and deterministic symmetric weight $Λ$;
subscript $I$ is omitted.  Transposition and cyclicity give
$tr(Λ BE)=tr(Λ EB)$ without
assuming commutation, so $<cdot,cdot>_Σ$ is a
positive inner product (as $Σ>0$).  We write
$|B|_2^2=tau(B^{mathsf T}sfTB)$ and $[B,E]=BE-EB$.
