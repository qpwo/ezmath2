# A sharp entropy bound and the simplex inequality for isotropic constants




maketitle

Abstract: 
We prove the strong isotropic constant conjecture: in every dimension
n, among all convex bodies in R^n a simplex has the largest
isotropic constant, and it is the only maximizer. We also prove the
sharper, more general sharp entropy bound
h(f)ge m+tfrac12logdetCov(f) for every log-concave density f
on R^m, with equality if and only if f is an invertible affine
image of a product of m independent one-sided exponentials. The body
inequality then follows by a geometric reduction to one dimension higher.


# Introduction

## The question

A set KsubsetR^n is a convex body if it is closed, bounded,
convex, and has nonempty interior. The simplest convex bodies are the
simplices: the convex hull of n+1 points not lying in a
hyperplane (a segment when n=1, a triangle when n=2, a tetrahedron
when n=3). Write |K| for the n-dimensional volume of K and
b_K=|K|^{-1}int_Kx,dd x for its centroid. The covariance matrix of
the uniform probability measure on K is
[
 Σ_K=frac1{|K|}int_K(x-b_K)(x-b_K)^{mathsf T}sfT,dd x,
]
and the isotropic constant of K is

 L_K=((detΣ_K)/(|K|^2))^{1/(2n)}.

The exponent 1/(2n) is chosen so that L_K is unchanged if K is
replaced by an invertible affine image A(K): the determinant of
Σ_{A(K)} grows by (det A)^2, the squared volume |A(K)|^2
grows by (det A)^2, and the ratio is invariant. So L_K measures
the shape of K, not its size or orientation.

[The simplex constant]
Let Delta_n be a regular simplex in R^n. A direct computation gives
[
 L_{Delta_n}=frac{(n!)^{1/n}}{(n+1)^{(n+1)/(2n)}√(n+2)}.
]
By affine invariance every simplex has this same value; call it L_n^star.


Bourgain, studying maximal functions associated with convex bodies,
asked whether L_K is bounded independently of the dimension
[cite]. This is the slicing problem. Klartag proved
L_Kle Cn^{1/4} [cite]; after a long line using Eldan's
stochastic localization [cite],
Klartag and Lehec proved a dimension-independent bound using Guan's
covariance estimate [cite]. That settles the
qualitative question but not the sharp value.

The strong isotropic constant conjecture asks for the exact
extremal value and the extremizer: the simplex should maximize L_K
and be the unique maximizer. Our first theorem proves it.

[The simplex inequality]
For every integer nge1 and every convex body KsubsetR^n,

 L_Klefrac{(n!)^{1/n}}{(n+1)^{(n+1)/(2n)}√(n+2)}=L_n^star,

with equality if and only if K is a simplex.


In the plane the bound was known, with uniqueness of the triangle
[cite]; partial restrictions had
been obtained for special bodies [cite].
Theorem~intro:simplex settles all dimensions at once.

For a convex body K with 0 in its interior, the polar is
K^circ={yinR^n:< x,y>le1 for all xin K}.
Klartag showed that the sharp simplex bound implies the nonsymmetric
Mahler inequality cite[Corollary~1.2]{Klartag2018}

 |K|,|K^circ|gefrac{(n+1)^{n+1}}{(n!)^2}.

So Theorem~intro:simplex also proves Mahler's inequality in every
dimension.

## The bridge: entropy of log-concave densities

Optimizing L_K directly over all convex bodies is hopeless. The key
idea (Fradelizi and Mar'in Sola [cite]) is to
pass to a larger, more flexible world, that of probability densities,
and to prove a sharp inequality there.

Let f:R^mto[0,∞) be a probability density. Its
differential entropy and covariance are
[
 h(f)=-int_{R^m}f(x)log f(x),dd x,
 Cov(f)=int_{R^m}(x-barbar x)(x-x)(x-barbar x)^x)^{mathsf T}sfTf(x),dd x,
]
with barbar x=x=int_{R^m}xf(x),dd x. All logarithms are natural and
0log0=0. A nonnegative function is log-concave if it has the
form f=e^{-V} with V convex; up to a null set every such density has
a proper lower semicontinuous convex potential V:R^mto(-∞,+∞],
where V=+∞ encodes f=0.

[The sharp entropy bound]
Let mge1 and let f be a log-concave probability density on R^m.
Then

 h(f)ge m+frac12logdetCov(f).

Equality holds if and only if, almost everywhere, f is the density of
M(E_1,...,E_m)^{mathsf T}sfT+b, where M is an invertible real
mtimes m matrix, binR^m, and E_1,...,E_m are independent
random variables with the one-sided exponential density
e^{-u}mathbf1_bf1_{{uge0}} (mean 1, variance 1).


The bound is sharp. The product of m independent mean-one
exponentials has entropy m, covariance I_m, and logdet I_m=0, so
equality holds. Under an invertible affine map xmapsto Ax+b, both
h(f) and tfrac12logdetCov(f) increase by log|det A|, so the
gap
Dgap(f)=h(f)-m-tfrac12logdetCov(f) is affine invariant. Thus the
theorem says: among log-concave densities with covariance I_m the
entropy is minimized by the independent-exponentials product, and only
by it.

The reduction to bodies is one clean construction
(Section~sec:geometry): place K at height one over the origin,
form the cone it generates in R^{n+1}, and put the exponential
density on the cone. Applied in dimension n+1, the entropy bound
becomes exactly the simplex bound, and entropy equality becomes the
statement that the cone is an affine image of an orthant, which holds
if and only if K is a simplex. This is the equivalence of Fradelizi
and Mar'in Sola cite[Proposition~5.2]{FradeliziMarinSola2026}, and it
is where the constant L_n^star comes from. In dimension one the
entropy bound is due to Melbourne, Nayar, and Roberto
[cite]. Our Theorem~intro:entropy
proves the inequality and classifies equality in every dimension.

## How the proof works, in words

The route has five big steps. The single most important idea is that a
strict remainder is kept the whole way, and it vanishes exactly
on the extremizers.

Step 1: transport from a Gaussian.:  Push a standard Gaussian
forward to f by the optimal (Brenier) map and let J be its
Jacobian, a positive definite symmetric matrix at each point. Because
J has positive eigenvalues, any continuous scalar function acts on
J by spectral calculus. Define A=q^{-1}(J), where
[
 q(a)=(varphi(a))/(Phi(-a))
]
is the derivative of t(a)=-logPhi(-a), with varphi,Phi the
standard Gaussian density and distribution function. The map q is a
smooth increasing bijection Rto(0,∞). An affine change of the
target makes E A=0, the key normalization (not the usual covariance
normalization).

Step 2: first chaos versus the rest.:  Gaussian measure has an
orthogonal decomposition into homogeneous polynomials of fixed degree
(chaoses). Since E A=0, the degree-zero part of A is gone, and
[
 A=sum_{r=1}^m x_rX_r+Y, X_r=E[x_rA], B=sum_{r=1}^mX_r^2,
]
where Y has only chaoses of degree ge2. In the extremal
(independent-exponentials) case B=I. The proof makes both
Bneq I and Yneq0 cost a positive amount.

Step 3: express the gap.:  A covariance identity involving
the Ornstein--Uhlenbeck operator rewrites Dgap in terms of A and
its derivatives. Convexity of the target potential gives an energy
bound that keeps a nonnegative term mathcal CcalC penalizing unequal
eigenvalues of A. Matrix functions of A do not commute, so
mathcal CcalC cannot be discarded.

Step 4: absorb, with slack.:  Two scalar one-variable
inequalities (one for absorption, one for pairs of eigenvalues) feed two
matrix lemmas (a commutator square and a block Cauchy--Schwarz) to
reduce Dgap to a quadratic form nonpositive on every chaos degree
ge2. The end is Theorem~mat:entropy-smooth: a smooth uniformly
convex density satisfies
[
 2h(mu)-2m-logdetCov(mu)ge
 frac1{500}Etr(Y^2)+frac1{1000}sum_{i=1}^momega(lambda_i(B)),
]
where lambda_i(B) are the eigenvalues of B and omegage0 is an
explicit weight vanishing only at 1. This strict remainder is stronger
than the inequality and is what classifies equality.

Step 5: remove smoothness, then classify equality.:  An
arbitrary log-concave density is approximated by smooth uniformly convex
ones preserving entropy, covariance, and first moments
(Section~geo:section, via the Moreau envelope), giving the
inequality for all f. For equality, approximate a sharp f by smooth
ones and let the strict remainder shrink. It forces Yto0 in Gaussian
L^2, Bto I, and the transport Jacobians to converge to q(A) for
a linear field A=sum x_rX_r with sum X_r^2=I. The mixed partial
derivatives of that limit commute, which
(Section~sec:rigidity) forces the X_r to commute, which
diagonalizes A and yields independent one-dimensional exponential
transports. That is the equality case.

The geometry.:  Section~sec:geometry computes the entropy
of the exponential density on the cone over K in terms of |K| and
Σ_K. Plugging into Theorem~intro:entropy in dimension n+1
gives the simplex bound; equality recycles the equality classification
to show the cone is an orthant, hence K a simplex.

## Roadmap
Section~tr:section sets up the Gaussian transport, the affine
normalization, and the energy estimate with the surplus mathcal C.calC.
Section~mat:section decomposes the entropy gap and proves the
smooth bound (Theorem~mat:entropy-smooth) using two scalar
propositions. Section~sc:section proves those two propositions,
and the Appendix~cert:verification certifies every numerical

# Gaussian transport and a matrix surplus

This section builds the machinery. We start from a smooth, uniformly
convex density, push the standard Gaussian onto it by the optimal map,
reparametrize the Jacobian, choose affine coordinates so that the
reparametrized field has mean zero, and prove an energy estimate that
keeps track of eigenvalue separation.

## Standing smoothness assumptions

For most of the proof we work with a probability density e^{-V} on
R^m satisfying

## The one-dimensional scalar model

Let varphi and Phi be the standard Gaussian density and
distribution function. For ainR define

 t(a)=-logPhi(-a), q(a)=t'(a)=(varphi(a))/(Phi(-a)),
 d(a)=q(a)-a,

and then

 k(a)=(t(a))/(q(a)), b(a)=k'(a), l(a)=b'(a).

There is a geometric meaning to t: if G is standard Gaussian then
Phi(-G) is uniform on (0,1), so t is exactly the monotone
transport from a standard Gaussian to the exponential law of mean one
(the ``entropy benchmark'' with entropy 1 and variance 1). The
function q=t' is therefore the derivative of that transport.

The identities

 q'=qd, b=1-kd, l=k-q+ab

follow by differentiating the definitions. In particular q-k=ab-l.
These recur constantly, so it is worth checking them once: q'=t'',
and differentiating q=varphi/Phi(-cdot) gives
q'=q(q-a)=qd. From t=qk we get t'=q'k+qk'=q, i.e.
qdk+qb=q, i.e. b=1-kd. Then l=b'=-k'd-kd'=-(1-kd)d-k(qd-1),
which after expanding equals k-q+ab. That last equality uses
q'=qd.

[Basic properties of q]
The function q is a smooth increasing bijection Rto(0,∞),
and
[

]

Proof.

[

]

increasing. The positivity of the variance gives q'<1.

[

Because the hazard rate q(a+u) is increasing,

[

## Spectral calculus and divided differences

For a real symmetric matrix we interpret scalar functions by spectral
calculus: if f is defined on the spectrum, f(A) means
Udiag(f(lambda_i))U^{mathsf T}sfT for any orthonormal
eigenbasis A=Udiag(lambda_i)U^{mathsf T}sfT. For
differentiable f the divided difference is

 f[u,v]=(f(u)-f(v))/(u-v),&uneq v,[2mm]
 f'(u),&u=v.

The Daleckii--Krein formula [cite] (see also
cite[Section~2.4]{Noferini2017}) says: in an eigenbasis of a symmetric
A, the derivative of f(A) in a symmetric direction Z has entries
f[a_i,a_j]Z_{ij}. This is the tool that lets us handle matrix
functions whose derivatives do not commute.

## Transport, Jacobian, and the reparametrization

Brenier's theorem [cite] gives a convex potential
phi with (nablaphi)_#gamma_m=mu, unique up to null sets. The
full-space regularity theorem of Cordero-Erausquin and Figalli
cite[Theorem~1.1]{CorderoErausquinFigalli2019}, which extends
Caffarelli's interior theory [cite] to unbounded domains,
makes phi smooth; for m=1 this follows directly from the two
distribution functions. Caffarelli's contraction theorem
[cite] and its inverse bound then give the Jacobian

 J=D^2phi.


 c_+^{-1/2}Ile Jle c_-^{-1/2}I.

The lower bound comes from applying contraction to the inverse
transport; quantitatively, source Hessian le aI and target Hessian
ge bI give a transport Lipschitz constant le√(a/b) (see also
cite[Theorem~1]{ChewiPooladian2023}).

Because J is symmetric positive definite, the symmetric matrix field

 A=q^{-1}(J)

is well defined. The spectral bounds on J confine the spectrum of A
to a compact interval, and q is smooth there with uniformly bounded
derivatives, so A and its functions behave well.

The usual isotropic coordinates would normalize Cov(mu)=I. We
instead impose the weaker but better-suited normalization E A=0.

## Covariance and the first Gaussian chaos

Let N=-Delta+xcdotnabla be the nonnegative Ornstein--Uhlenbeck
operator and R=(Id+N)^{-1}. The degree-d Gaussian chaos is the
span of coordinatewise products of one-dimensional Hermite polynomials
with degrees summing to d; these spaces give an orthogonal
decomposition of L^2(gamma_m), on which N and R act by d and
(1+d)^{-1}. Write ∂=(∂_1,...,∂_m) and
∂^*v=sum_r(x_rv_r-∂_rv_r), so N=∂^*∂.

with dual norm
[

Helffer--Sj"ostrand covariance representation
cite[Proposition~4.1(ii)]{DuerinckxOtto2020}, with a direct chaos proof
below. The term -2tau(t) in (mat:deficit-exact) determines its
completion: since qk=t, subtracting (Id+N)k from q in the
covariance quadratic form produces exactly the cross term -2t.

# Approximation of log-concave densities

Theorem~mat:entropy-smooth assumes a potential with positive,
bounded Hessian. We now remove those restrictions while preserving
entropy, covariance, and first moments. The same approximation later
studies equality, by applying the strict remainder to smooth densities
whose entropy gaps tend to zero.

## Convex approximation with entropy convergence

We use extended-valued convex potentials: V:R^mto(-∞,+∞]
is proper if it is finite somewhere and never -∞. The
convention e^{-∞}=0 allows bounded supports. The next lemma
gives the common integrable bound used for the approximation, including
its entropy integrand.


Let V:R^mto(-∞,+∞] be proper, lower semicontinuous, and
convex, with 0<int_{R^m}e^{-V(x)}dd x<∞. Then there are a>0
and bge0 with

 V(x)ge a|x|-bqquad(xinR^m).

In particular e^{-V} has moments of every order and
int|V|e^{-V}<∞, with |V|e^{-V} read as zero where V=+∞.

Proof. 
The convex set {V<∞} has positive measure and hence nonempty
interior (otherwise its affine hull would be a proper affine subspace).
Choose a simplex with all vertices in this set and with nonempty
interior; convexity bounds V above by the largest vertex value
throughout the simplex, so some ball B(x_0,r) lies in a sublevel set
{Vle M}, Mge V(x_0).

The closed convex set A={Vle M+1} has finite volume, since
|A|le e^{M+1}int e^{-V}. It is bounded: the convex hull of
B(x_0,r) and a point zin A contains a cone with base an
(m-1)-disk of radius r through x_0, perpendicular to z-x_0, and
height |z-x_0|, whose volume tends to infinity with |z-x_0| (in
dimension one, use the length of the interval from x_0 to z).

Choose R>r so Asubset B(x_0,R). If d=|x-x_0|ge R and
V(x)<∞, put y=x_0+(R/d)(x-x_0); then ynotin A and convexity
gives
[
 M+1<V(y)le(1-frac Rd)V(x_0)+frac RdV(x),
]
so V(x)>V(x_0)+d/R. The inequality is automatic if V(x)=+∞. On
the compact ball overlineoverline B(x_0,R),B(x_0,R), lower semicontinuity and
properness give a finite lower bound (a sequence with values tending to
-∞ would have a convergent subsequence and violate lower
semicontinuity). Combining the two bounds, after enlarging b, gives
(geo:linear-coercivity).

For moments, e^{-V(x)}le e^be^{-a|x|}. For entropy, the elementary
bound

 |u|e^{-u}le C_be^{-u/2}(uge-b)

holds because |u|e^{-u/2} is bounded on [-b,∞); substitute
u=V(x) and use (geo:linear-coercivity).

# From vanishing slack to exponential products

Write Dgap(f)=h(f)-m-tfrac12logdetCov(f) for the nonnegative
entropy gap. Theorem~mat:entropy-smooth controls the nonlinear part
of the normalized matrix field and the sum of squares of its linear
coefficients. We now show that this control determines every equality
case. The structural step is local: the first two nonconstant terms of
q(A(x)), for a linear symmetric field A, force its coefficients to
commute whenever q(A) is a Jacobian.

# The simplex inequality and its equality cases

We transfer Theorem~intro:entropy to convex bodies through the
exponential density on a cone. The construction appears in
cite[Proposition~2(b)]{FradeliziMeyer2008}, its entropy reduction in
cite[Lemma~5.2 and Proposition~5.2]{FradeliziMarinSola2026}, and the
moment formulas also in cite[Lemma~2.5]{Klartag2018}. The computation
identifies equality in the body inequality with entropy equality in one
higher dimension; the support of the resulting exponential product then
determines the body.

Proof. [Proof of Theorem~intro:simplex]
Let KsubsetR^n be a convex body. Translation preserves L_K and
preserves being a simplex, so assume K has centroid zero. Write
Σ_K=Cov(X) for X uniform on K, and define

 mathcal C_K=calC_K={(tx,t):xin K, tge0}subsetR^{n+1}.

Convexity of K makes mathcal C_KcalC_K convex. It is closed because K
is compact: a convergent sequence (t_jx_j,t_j) with t_jto0 has
t_jx_jto0, and when the limiting height is positive one divides by
that height. The cone has nonempty interior and its only point at height
zero is the origin.

Define the log-concave density

 f_K(y,t)=frac{e^{-t}}{n!,|K|},mathbf1_bf1_{mathcal C_K}calC_K(y,t).

The change of variables y=tx (Jacobian t^n for t>0) gives
[
 int_{mathcal C_K}calC_Ke^{-t}dd ydd t
 =|K|int_0^∞ t^ne^{-t}dd t=n!,|K|,
]
so f_K is a probability density. The same change of variables shows it
is the density of (TX,T), where T is independent of X with density
t^ne^{-t}/n! on (0,∞). Integration by parts gives
[
 E T=n+1,qquadE T^2=(n+1)(n+2),qquadVar(T)=n+1.
]
Since E X=0, independence makes the cross-covariance of TX and T
vanish. Hence

 h(f_K)&=n+1+log(n!,|K|),
 Cov(f_K)&=
 (n+1)(n+2)Σ_K&00&n+1,
 
 detCov(f_K)&=(n+1)^{n+1}(n+2)^ndetΣ_K.
 

Applying Theorem~intro:entropy in dimension n+1 yields

 (n!)^2|K|^2ge(n+1)^{n+1}(n+2)^ndetΣ_K,

which is precisely
[
 L_Klefrac{(n!)^{1/n}}{(n+1)^{(n+1)/(2n)}√(n+2)}.
]
Every step from the entropy inequality to (eq:body-cone-bound) is
reversible, so equality in the body bound holds if and only if f_K
has equality in Theorem~intro:entropy.

Suppose equality holds. By that theorem the law with density f_K is an
invertible affine image of a product of n+1 mean-one exponentials. The
closed support of f_K is exactly mathcal C_K:calC_K: it has positive
density on the interior of the cone, and this interior is dense in the
cone. Taking closed supports gives

 mathcal C_K=b+M[0,∞)^calC_K=b+M[0,∞)^{n+1}

for an invertible matrix M and a vector b.

The origin is the unique extreme point of mathcal C_K:calC_K: every nonzero
zinmathcal C_KcalC_K is the midpoint of 0 and 2z, while
0=(u+v)/2 with u,vinmathcal C_KcalC_K forces the nonnegative heights of
u and v to vanish, hence u=v=0. An orthant also has the origin as
its unique extreme point, and invertible affine maps preserve extreme
points, so (eq:body-cone-affine-orthant) forces b=0.

Choose positive multiples w_0,...,w_n of the respective columns of
M, and let a_i be the last coordinate of w_i. Each w_i is a
nonzero point of mathcal C_K,calC_K, so a_i>0. Put p_i=w_i/a_i. The
section at height one is

 Ktimes{1}=Bigl{sum_{i=0}^nc_iw_i:c_ige0,
 sum_{i=0}^nc_ia_i=1Bigr}=conv{p_0,...,p_n}.

The p_i are linearly independent (the w_i are), hence affinely
independent in the height-one hyperplane, and K is a simplex. (To
picture it: slice the cone by the hyperplane t=1; dividing each
generating ray w_i by its positive height a_i lands the endpoint on
that slice, and the slice is the convex hull of the p_i.)

Conversely, let K=conv{v_0,...,v_n} be a simplex,
and let M have columns (v_i,1). These are linearly independent and
M[0,∞)^{n+1}=mathcal C_K,calC_K, |det M|=n!,|K| (subtract the first
column from the others and expand along the last row). If E_0,...,E_n
are independent mean-one exponentials, the density of
M(E_0,...,E_n)^{mathsf T}sfT at (y,t)inmathcal C_KcalC_K is
[
 frac1{|det M|}exp(-sum_{i=0}^n(M^{-1}(y,t))_i)
 =frac{e^{-t}}{n!,|K|},
]
because the last row of M consists of ones. This is exactly f_K up
to immaterial boundary values, so Theorem~intro:entropy gives
equality in (eq:body-cone-bound).


For n=1 every convex body is an interval, hence a simplex, and the
constant is 1/√(12): an interval of length ell has variance
ell^2/12, matching the formula.
