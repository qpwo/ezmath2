============================================================
EQUIDISTRIBUTION OF PRIME-DEGREE TORUS PACKETS
WITH ARBITRARY LOCAL TYPE
A Guided Tour for Young Mathematicians
============================================================

We present, in plain language with full proofs, a theorem about
how certain closed orbits in a matrix-group quotient distribute
as the arithmetic parameter grows. It is a higher-dimensional
Duke-type equidistribution theorem, in prime degree at least
five, in which every possible local homothety type is allowed,
including lattices over nonmaximal orders of arbitrary index.

Every technical term is introduced when it first appears.
Every result below is proved. A glossary at the end restates
each term in one sentence. A reading guide at the end maps
each Part to the corresponding section of the original paper.

THE PLOT, IN ONE PARAGRAPH.

A unimodular lattice in R^n is a discrete subgroup of R^n of
covolume one. The set X_n of all unimodular lattices (up to a
choice of Z-basis) carries a natural probability measure m_n,
the Haar measure. Fix a totally real number field K of prime
degree n >= 5, fix a full lattice M in K, and fix an ordering
sigma of the real embeddings of K. Arithmetically, this data
produces a finite collection of closed "diagonal" orbits in
X_n, one per homothety class of M and one per sign pattern in
the group W_n of the n x n diagonal matrices with +-1 diagonal
and product +1. The "packet" is that finite collection,
weighted by the volumes of the orbits; the packet probability
is the corresponding probability measure on X_n. As the
multiplier-order discriminant D(M) grows, the packet measures
converge weakly to m_n. The whole proof has three parts:
(1) an exact identity expressing the packet mean of any
lattice-vector count as a weighted sum over integral ideals
of K; (2) arithmetic estimates on the weights, uniform in
the local type, that make the ideal sum behave like a short
interval of a smooth function; (3) ergodic theory: the resulting
mass bound of size r^n on small balls forces positive entropy,
which excludes every possible non-Haar ergodic component by
a theorem of Einsiedler, Katok, and Lindenstrauss.

============================================================
PART 0. THE SPACE, THE GROUP, THE QUESTION
============================================================

0.1. Unimodular lattices.

A lattice in R^n is the set of integer linear combinations of
n linearly independent vectors v_1, ..., v_n:

    L = Z v_1 + Z v_2 + ... + Z v_n.

The lattice L is unimodular if |det(v_1 | v_2 | ... | v_n)| = 1;
one says its fundamental parallelepiped has covolume one.
Two unimodular lattices that differ only by a Z-basis change
are considered the same point of X_n. Formally,

    X_n = SL_n(Z) \ SL_n(R),

with the right action of SL_n(R) given by

    (Z^n g) h = Z^n (g h).

This is a noncompact, finite-volume manifold of dimension
n^2 - 1. It carries a unique left-SL_n(R)-invariant probability
measure, called the Haar probability and denoted m_n. It is
the uniform measure: to test that a family of packet
probabilities converges to m_n we just need every continuous
compactly supported function f on X_n to satisfy
int f d mu_i -> int f d m_n.

The connection to lattices: for g in SL_n(R), the right coset
SL_n(Z) g corresponds to the unimodular lattice with rows given
by the rows of g. Equivalently, X_n is the moduli space of
covolume-one lattices in R^n, up to Z-basis change.

0.2. The diagonal group A_n.

Let

    A_n = { diag(e^{u_1}, ..., e^{u_n}) :
            u_1, ..., u_n in R, u_1 + ... + u_n = 0 }.

This is a connected abelian Lie group isomorphic to R^{n-1}.
It acts on X_n by right multiplication: (Z^n g) a = Z^n (g a).
For Lambda in X_n and a in A_n, write Lambda a for the image
of Lambda under this action.

The orbits Lambda A_n are compact when Lambda is an arithmetic
lattice attached to a number field (which is the only case we
care about). The classical Duke theorem, in this language, says
that for n = 2 certain infinite families of these closed A_n-
orbits become distributed like m_n. The higher-rank Duke
problem asks for the same phenomenon for n >= 3.

0.3. Fixed data.

  - n >= 5 is a fixed prime.
  - K is a totally real number field of degree n. "Totally
    real" means every embedding K -> C has image in R; it
    has exactly n such embeddings, which we order as
    sigma = (sigma_1, ..., sigma_n).
  - M is a full Z-lattice in K: a free Z-submodule of K of
    rank n, whose Q-span is all of K.
  - The multiplier order of M is

       O(M) = { alpha in K : alpha M is contained in M }.

    This is an order of K; it always contains the maximal
    order R = O_K.
  - The multiplier-order discriminant is

       D(M) = |Disc(K)| * [O_K : O(M)]^2,

    where [O_K : O(M)] is the index. When M is an R-ideal this
    equals |Disc(K)|.

0.4. What "local homothety" means.

For each rational prime l, localize at l: K_l = K (x)_Q Q_l,
M_l = M (x)_Z Z_l, etc. Two full lattices M' and M'' in K
are locally homothetic if, for every l,

    M'_l = c_l M''_l in K_l, with c_l in K_l^*.

That is, the two completions agree at every prime after a
scalar multiplication. This is strictly finer than "same
multiplier order": two lattices can have the same O(M) and D(M)
while lying in different local types.

0.5. Packets and their probability.

Fix the local homothety type of M (the family of all full
lattices locally homothetic to M). For each K^*-homothety
class M' in this local type, embed M' in R^n via sigma and
rescale to covolume one:

    Lambda_{M', sigma}
        = covol(sigma(M'))^{-1/n} sigma(M').

Let W_n be the finite group

    W_n = { diag(eps_1, ..., eps_n) : eps_j in {+1, -1},
            eps_1 * eps_2 * ... * eps_n = 1 }.

W_n has order 2^{n-1}; it is the group of sign matrices that
preserve lattice shapes up to sign reversal. Since n is odd,
the sign "flip all coordinates" is in W_n; this is the source
of the "distinct orbits" bookkeeping below.

The packet of (K, M, sigma) is the finite set of orbits

    P_{K, M, sigma} = { Lambda_{M', sigma} w A_n :
        M' runs over K^*-homothety classes in the local type,
        w runs over W_n,
        with duplicate orbits identified }.

(The next part proves both compactness and finiteness; the
"packet model" lemma reconstructs the packet as a disjoint
union, orbit by orbit, from the ideal-class and local-unit
data.)

For an orbit O in the packet, let vol(O) be the volume with
respect to Haar measure on A_n, normalized by product Haar on
A_n, and let nu_O be the A_n-invariant probability measure on O.
Then the packet probability is

    mu_{K, M, sigma} =
        ( sum_{O in P_{K, M, sigma}} vol(O) nu_O )
        / ( sum_{O in P} vol(O) ).

By construction mu_{K, M, sigma} is a probability on X_n. It
is a weighted average of the Haar probabilities of the packet
orbits, weighted by orbit length.

0.6. The main theorem.

THEOREM (paper Theorem thm:main).
Fix a prime n >= 5, and a sequence (K_i, M_i, sigma_i)
satisfying D(M_i) -> infinity. Then in the weak-* topology of
probability measures on X_n,

    mu_{K_i, M_i, sigma_i}  -->  m_n.

Equivalently: for every continuous compactly supported f on X_n,

    int f d mu_{K_i, M_i, sigma_i}  ->  int f d m_n.

Additionally the family is tight: no amount of mass escapes
into the cusp of X_n. Weak convergence follows from tightness
plus identification of every subsequential limit.

The theorem is unconditional, exact for every prescribed local
type, for every totally real field in the allowed degrees and
every full lattice M in that field. In particular, D(M) may
grow while |Disc(K)| stays bounded, if the index [O_K : O(M)]
grows.

0.7. Prior work, briefly.

  - LINNIK (1968) initiated the ergodic approach to arithmetic
    equidistribution.

  - DUKE (1988) proved equidistribution of length-weighted
    closed geodesics attached to fundamental real-quadratic
    discriminants on the modular surface.

  - EINSIEDLER, LINDENSTRAUSS, MICHEL, VENKATESH ("ELMV";
    2009, 2011) built the general relation "arithmetic
    separation plus subconvexity" controlling periodic torus
    orbits, and proved cubic equidistribution unconditionally.
    Their packet construction already distinguishes local
    homothety from the coarser condition "same multiplier
    order."

  - KHAYUTIN (2019) obtained the entropy side using arithmetic
    separation and Galois invariants, under maximal-order and
    two-transitivity hypotheses.

  - LEMKE OLIVER, THORNER, ZAMAN (2024) proved
    equidistribution for sequences of orders in every fixed
    prime degree at least five, at the cost of a small
    exceptional set of fields.

  - The theorem above: unconditional, no exceptional set, for
    every local type, including sequences of orders with
    increasing index in a fixed field.

The arithmetic ingredient is not subconvexity. It is an
effective residue estimate for the Dedekind zeta function
zeta_K together with the uniform form of Shiu's theorem for
multiplicative functions. Odd prime degree rules out the
quadratic subfields that appear in the exceptional alternative
of Stark's continuation of the zero-free region.

0.8. Roadmap of the proof.

Put R = O_K, D = |Disc(K)|, q = [R M : M], Q = q * sqrt(D).
This is the natural counting scale; D(M) <= Q^{2n}. The rest
of the tour proves:

  Step A (Part II).  The packet measure of any "lattice-vector
  count" E_f (sum of f over nonzero lattice vectors) equals a
  specific weighted sum over integral ideals:

      int E_f d mu = (1 / (sqrt(D) kappa_K))
                     * sum_a P(a) V_f (N(a) / Q).

  Here P(a) is a containment probability for the prescribed
  local type, V_f is a "product-fiber" integral (f integrated
  along a fixed-product slice of the real diagonal torus), and
  kappa_K = Res_{s=1} zeta_K(s).

  Step B (Parts III-IV).  The weights P(a) have exact total
  mass Z_S / q and power-saving tails. The Dedekind zeta
  residue estimate (via Stark) plus Shiu's theorem give a
  weighted count of ideals in short intervals:

      sum_{x - y < N(a) <= x} P(a)  <<_n  kappa_K y / q,

  for Q^{3/4} <= x <= A_0 Q and eta x <= y <= x.

  Step C (Parts V-VI).  The weighted count gives two
  geometric consequences:

    (i) (Cusp.) The packet mass of the set of lattices with a
        nonzero vector of sup-norm <= theta is at most
        C_n theta^n. So the family is tight.

    (ii) (Balls.) The weak limit mu on X_n satisfies an
         ordinary-ball bound mu(x B(r)) <= C_Omega r^n for
         x in any fixed compact Omega. This is one power of
         r smaller than dim A_n = n-1 could give.

  Step D (Part VI).  The ball bound forces positive entropy
  in the flow direction a(1) in A_n. Ergodicity plus the
  prime-degree measure classification theorem of EKL then
  identifies every ergodic component of the limit as the
  Haar measure m_n. Tightness and uniqueness of the limit
  finish the proof.

============================================================
PART I. STRUCTURAL FACTS
============================================================

I.1. The embedding map on full lattices.

Fix a Z-basis alpha_1, ..., alpha_n of M. The matrix

    B_sigma = ( sigma_j(alpha_i) )_{i,j}

has (i, j) entry sigma_j(alpha_i). For alpha = sum_i alpha_i c_i
in M with integer coefficients c_i, the row vector of
embeddings is

    sigma(alpha) = (sigma_1(alpha), ..., sigma_n(alpha))
                 = (c_1, ..., c_n) B_sigma.

Thus sigma(M) = Z^n B_sigma, a row lattice of rank n in R^n.
Its covolume is |det B_sigma| = sqrt(Disc(M)) = sqrt(D) [R:O(M)],
where Disc(M) is the discriminant of M. For a nonzero fractional
R-ideal I, covol(sigma(I)) = sqrt(D) N(I), with N(I) the
absolute norm of I.

I.2. Half-logarithmic coordinates on the real torus.

Define

    C = { t in (R^*)^n : prod_{j=1}^n |t_j| = 1 }.

Every t in C can be written uniquely as
    t = (eps_1, ..., eps_n) * (e^{u_1}, ..., e^{u_{n-1}},
                               e^{-u_1 - ... - u_{n-1}}),
where eps_j in {+1, -1} and u_1, ..., u_{n-1} in R. We put
on C the measure that is counting measure on the 2^n sign
choices, times Lebesgue measure on the u-coordinates. Under
the embedding c -> diag(c) of C into SL_n(R), this is the
Haar measure on the diagonal maximal split torus.

I.3. Diagonal one-parameter flows.

Pick real numbers lambda_1, ..., lambda_n with
lambda_1 + ... + lambda_n = 0 and lambda_i distinct. Define

    a(t) = diag(e^{lambda_1 t}, ..., e^{lambda_n t}),  t in R.

Let

    lambda = min_{i != j} |lambda_i - lambda_j|  > 0.

This "gap" lambda measures how quickly the diagonal flow
separates coordinates. Entropy of the flow a(1) will be
bounded below by a fixed multiple of this gap.

============================================================
PART II. THE PACKET AS A WEIGHTED IDEAL SUM
============================================================

This Part proves Step A of the roadmap. Every symbol appearing
in later Parts is introduced here.

II.1. The scale Q and the local nonmaximal l-adic data.

Put R = O_K, E = R^* = O_K^*, D = |Disc(K)|, and
kappa_K = Res_{s=1} zeta_K(s). Define the maximal-order span
of M and the index

    J_0 = R M       (an integral R-ideal containing M),
    q   = [J_0 : M] (the index),
    Q   = q * sqrt(D).

Claim II.1.1. q R is contained in O(M), and O(M) is contained
in R. In particular, D(M) = D [R : O(M)]^2 <= D q^{2n} <= Q^{2n}.

Proof. Because J_0 = R M and M has rank n, one has
q J_0 subset M (the quotient J_0/M has order q and is
annihilated by q; use the standard finite-abelian-group
structure). Multiply by R: q R J_0 = q J_0 subset M. But
q R J_0 = (q R) J_0, and every element of q R is an
algebraic integer in K, so q R is contained in the multiplier
order O(J_0) of J_0. Since J_0/M is finite and q annihilates
it, O(J_0) is also the multiplier order of M modulo q, and
combining with the definition yields q R subset O(M).

The reverse inclusion: any alpha in O(M) has an integral
action on M, so alpha is an algebraic integer in K, i.e., in
R. Thus O(M) subset R. Composing gives q R subset O(M) subset R,
hence [R : O(M)] <= [R : qR] = q^n, so D(M) <= D q^{2n}.

Finally, D(M) -> infinity implies Q -> infinity: from
D(M) <= Q^{2n}, D(M) -> infinity gives Q -> infinity. QED

II.2. Localized lattices.

For a rational prime l, set R_l = R (x)_Z Z_l, M_l = M (x)_Z
Z_l. The integral closure of the ideal J_0 at l is
(J_0)_l = j_l R_l for some j_l in K_l^*. Define

    L_l = j_l^{-1} M_l,  an additive subgroup of K_l.

Claim II.2.1. L_l is an open subgroup of R_l of finite index.
Moreover, R_l L_l = R_l, [R_l : L_l] = q_l, and q = prod_l q_l.

Proof. First, [R_l : j_l^{-1} M_l] = [j_l R_l : M_l] = (q)_l,
since J_0 / M has order q. That is finite, so L_l is open of
finite index. For "R_l L_l = R_l": R_l L_l = R_l j_l^{-1} M_l
= j_l^{-1} R_l M_l = j_l^{-1} (J_0)_l = j_l^{-1} j_l R_l = R_l.
The index computation [R_l : L_l] = q_l is immediate from the
finite-index formula. The product formula q = prod_l q_l is
the multiplicativity of the index over the S-supported primes.
QED

Set S = {l : l | q}. For l not in S, q_l = 1, so
L_l = R_l.

II.3. Local homothety classes.

For each ordinary ideal class [I] of R, choose one fractional
R-ideal I in that class. Let N(I) denote the finite set of
full lattices N with

    (a) N is locally homothetic to M;
    (b) R N = I.

Claim II.3.1. The set N(I) is finite. Every N in N(I)
satisfies [I : N] = q and lies between q I and I. The
cardinality T = |N(I)| is independent of [I]. Local unit Haar
probability on each finite set of local choices induces uniform
probability on N(I), and the choices at different primes are
independent.

Proof. Write I_l = i_l R_l for some i_l in K_l^*. By II.2, the
possible completions of a member of N(I) are exactly
N_l = i_l z_l L_l, with z_l in R_l^*. Taking maximal-order
spans in the local homothety forces the remaining multiplier to
be a unit: if N'_l = c_l M'_l, then (R N')_l = c_l (R M')_l
= c_l J_0_l = (c_l R_l) J_0_l, so the extra multiplier c_l
must be a unit modulo the local maximal-order normalizer.
Reversing, any (z_l) in prod_l R_l^* produces a candidate
lattice N_l = i_l z_l L_l, and these are contained in I_l (since
L_l subset R_l) and contain q I_l (since q_l R_l subset L_l;
similarly L_l subset R_l applied to q). Finiteness: N(I) is a
subset of the finite set of lattices between q I and I.

The patching over S is unique by the primary decomposition of
I/qI. The cardinality T = |N(I)| is independent of [I]:
multiplication by i_l identifies the finite set of local unit
orbits at l for two choices of I, so the count is independent
of the ideal. Local unit Haar probability gives each choice
probability 1/|R_l^* : stab_{R_l^*}(L_l)|; the product over l
in S of these probabilities is uniform on N(I), independent of
the choice of I. QED

One caution: the label count T need not equal q. T counts
unit translates, q is the base saturation index; the two
coincide only when q_l = |R_l^* : stab_{R_l^*}(L_l)| at every
l in S, which is not the general case.

II.4. The real torus, the unit group, and class-number formula.

Recall C = {t in (R^*)^n : prod |t_j| = 1} with half-log-Haar
measure from I.2. The embedded unit group sigma(E) is a
discrete cocompact subgroup of C. Let F be a measurable
fundamental domain. By the totally real case of the analytic
class-number formula,

    vol(F) = 2^{n-1} R_K,  and  h_K vol(F) = sqrt(D) kappa_K.

Here h_K is the ordinary class number, R_K the regulator
expressed in deleted logarithmic coordinates. The 2^{n-1}
accounts for the 2^n sign choices in C modulo the 2 sign
kernel of log|.|.

II.5. The packet-model lemma.

LEMMA II.5.1 (packet model).
The map

    Phi(N, t) = (Q N(I))^{-1/n} sigma(N) t,

defined on pairs (N, t) in N(I) x C for each ideal class [I],
induces a bijection

    disjoint union over [I] of  ( N(I) x C ) / E

onto the union of the packet orbits. Here E acts by
eps : (N, t) -> (eps N, sigma(eps)^{-1} t).
Counting measure on N(I)-labels times half-log-Haar on C
descends to the sum of the orbit Haar measures. In particular,
the packet probability is uniform over the ideal classes [I]
and over N in N(I), followed by integration dt / vol(F) over F.

Proof. SURJECTIVITY. Every packet orbit is, by definition,
Lambda_{M', sigma} times some t in C. The maximal-order span
of M' is a fractional ideal in some class [I]; local
homothety plus the choice of representative N in N(I) gives
the label data, and the real scaling gives t. So Phi hits
every orbit.

INJECTIVITY. Suppose two pairs (N, t) and (N', t') map to
the same point of X_n:

    (Q N(I))^{-1/n} sigma(N) t
        = (Q N(I'))^{-1/n} sigma(N') t'.

Absorb the ratio of the two normalizing scalars into a single
diagonal real map D: then D sigma(N) = sigma(N').

CLAIM: N' = bN for some b in K^*. Proof of claim: first pick
alpha in N with all n coordinates of sigma(alpha) nonzero.
Such alpha exists because N is a full lattice: for any Z-basis
x_1, ..., x_n of N, the integer linear combination

    alpha = x_1 + N x_2 + N^2 x_3 + ... + N^{n-1} x_n

has coordinates that are polynomials in N, and each coordinate
is not the zero polynomial because the x_i are K-linearly
independent; so only finitely many integers N give a zero
coordinate, and all other N give coordinates all nonzero.)

Now: D sigma(alpha) = sigma(beta) for some beta in N' (because
D sigma(N) = sigma(N')). Since sigma(alpha) has no zero
coordinate and D is diagonal, D = diag(sigma(beta / alpha)).
In particular D = sigma(b) for b = beta / alpha in K^*. So
sigma(N') = sigma(b) sigma(N) = sigma(b N), hence N' = b N.
(Without the "all coordinates nonzero" choice of alpha, the
diagonal D would not be uniquely determined; this is exactly
the place where the choice matters.)

By construction R N = R N' = I. So b I = I, hence
b in E = R^*. The pairs (N, t) and (N', t') differ by the
E-action, establishing injectivity of the quotient map.

STABILIZER AND VOLUME. The stabilizer of a fixed label (N, +1)
in E is the totally positive subgroup of
{eps in E : eps N = N}, of finite index in E. Its image in C
acts by log translations. The all-signs-negative unit -1 acts
trivially on C. So the E-action is faithful on log coordinates,
and the quotient of each label orbit by its stabilizer is a
single compact A_n-orbit. By injectivity, distinct label
orbits produce distinct packet orbits (no double counting).
Each orbit inherits precisely its Haar probability, and the
total measure of the "fundamental domains" N(I) x F is
h_K * T * vol(F). Dividing by this quantity gives the packet
probability: uniform on the classes [I], uniform on N(I)
within each class, integrate dt / vol(F) over F. QED

II.6. Containment probabilities.

For a rational prime l and a tuple k_l = (k_p)_{p | l} of
nonnegative integers, let

    P_l(k_l) = Pr_{z in R_l^*} { x in z L_l }.

Here x in K_l is a fixed element with valuations v_p(x) = k_p
for every p | l, and the probability is with respect to
normalized unit Haar measure on R_l^*. The probability does not
depend on the choice of x because the unit group acts
transitively on every fixed valuation shell.

For an integral nonzero R-ideal a, set

    P(a) = prod_{l in S} P_l( (v_p(a))_{p | l} ).

Claim II.6.1. For alpha in I \ {0}, the probability that a
uniform N in N(I) contains alpha equals P( (alpha) I^{-1} ).

Proof. By Claim II.3.1, the N in N(I) correspond to
choices of z_l in R_l^* modulo unit stabilizer, with
N_l = i_l z_l L_l. The vector alpha is in N if and only if
alpha is in i_l z_l L_l for every l, equivalently (alpha / i_l)
is in z_l L_l; the probability at l is exactly P_l at the
valuation profile of alpha I^{-1}, and the product across l
gives P((alpha) I^{-1}). QED

II.7. Product-fiber integral and the unfolding proposition.

For a nonnegative Borel function f on R^n and a unimodular
lattice Lambda in X_n, define

    E_f(Lambda) = sum_{0 != v in Lambda} f(v).

For z > 0, define the product-fiber integral

    V_f(z) = sum_{omega in {+-1}^n}
             int_{R^{n-1}} f( omega_1 e^{u_1}, ...,
                          omega_{n-1} e^{u_{n-1}},
                          omega_n z e^{-u_1 - ... - u_{n-1}} )
             du_1 ... du_{n-1}.

This is f integrated over the C-orbit with absolute coordinate
product z, in the half-log coordinates.

PROPOSITION II.7.1 (unfolding).
Let mu_* = mu_{K, M, sigma}. Then, for every nonnegative
extended-valued f,

    int_{X_n} E_f d mu_* =
        (1 / (sqrt(D) kappa_K)) *
        sum_{a integral, a != 0} P(a) V_f( N(a) / Q ).

The identity holds in the sense of nonnegative extended
integrals; possible infinities agree.

Proof. By Lemma II.5.1,

    int_{X_n} E_f d mu_*
      = (1 / (h_K vol(F))) sum_{[I]} int_F
         sum_{alpha in I \ {0}} P((alpha) I^{-1})
         * f( (Q N(I))^{-1/n} sigma(alpha) t ) dt.

The map alpha -> (alpha) I^{-1} runs over the integral ideals
in the inverse class of I, each fiber being one free E-orbit.
Summing over E and using the fundamental domain F unfolds to
integration over C, by Tonelli. The absolute coordinate product
of the normalized vector (Q N(I))^{-1/n} sigma(alpha) is

    N((alpha) I^{-1}) / Q = N( (alpha) I^{-1} ) / Q.

Translating the first n-1 logarithms and relabeling the 2^n
signs identifies this C-integral with V_f at height
N(a) / Q where a = (alpha) I^{-1}. Jacobian one (the
translation of log coordinates is measure-preserving and
relabeling is a bijection on sign patterns). Every integral
ideal a occurs in exactly one inverse class. The coefficient
is 1 / (sqrt(D) kappa_K), using h_K vol(F) = sqrt(D) kappa_K
from II.4. This proves the identity. QED

When M is an R-ideal, q = 1 and P(a) = 1, so the proposition
reduces to the classical Hecke unfolding formula for the
maximal order.

PART III. LOCAL WEIGHT ESTIMATES
============================================================

This Part proves Step B, the "easy half": exact total mass and a
power-saving tail for the S-supported containment weights defined
in Part II.

III.1. Notation for valuation tuples.

Let k = (k_l)_{l in S} range over tuples with k_l = (k_p)_{p | l},
each k_p a nonnegative integer. Set

    d_l(k_l) = l^{ sum_{p | l} f_p k_p },
    d(k)     = prod_{l in S} d_l(k_l),
    P(k)     = prod_{l in S} P_l(k_l),
    Z_l      = prod_{p | l} (1 - l^{-f_p})^{-1},
    Z_S      = prod_{l in S} Z_l.

Here e_p and f_p are the ramification index and residue degree of
p over l in K / Q, so sum_{p | l} e_p f_p = n. Empty products are
one. Thus d(k) equals the norm of the S-supported part of an
integral ideal with profile k; P(k) is the containment
probability of such a profile.

LEMMA III.1 (shell-sum; paper eq. shell-sum).
sum_k P(k) / d(k) = Z_S / q.

Proof. Normalize additive Haar measure on R_l to have mass one.
The valuation shell { x in R_l : v_p(x) = k_p for each p | l }
has additive mass Z_l^{-1} / d_l(k_l). (The local zeta factor
gives the normalization, and prod_p l^{-f_p k_p} = d_l(k_l)^{-1}
gives the shell volume.)

The conditional law on the shell is unit-Haar invariant because
the unit group is transitive on any fixed nonnegative valuation
shell. So P_l(k_l) = fraction of the k_l shell lying inside L_l.

Since L_l is an additive subgroup of R_l of index q_l, its
additive mass is 1 / q_l. Summing over shells:

    sum_{k_l} P_l(k_l) / d_l(k_l) = Z_l / q_l.

Multiplying over l in S:

    sum_k P(k)/d(k) = (prod Z_l) / (prod q_l) = Z_S / q.

Points with a zero component have additive measure zero in R_l
and contribute nothing. QED.

LEMMA III.2 (weight-moment; paper eq. weight-moment).
Let s = 1/(2n). Then for every eps > 0,

    sum_k P(k) d(k)^{s-1}  <<_{n, eps}  q^{eps} / q.

Proof. Choose x from additive-Haar on L_l; write its component
valuations as v_p = v_p(x). The key geometric fact is that
R_l L_l = R_l. This implies that the projection of L_l onto
each component K_p (the p-adic completion of K) contains a unit
of the local ring R_p. Modulo the kth power of the maximal ideal
of R_p, that unit produces at least l^{ceil(k/e_p)} distinct
residues. Since uniform additive measure on L_l descends to
uniform measure on this finite additive image, we get

    Pr_{x in L_l} ( v_p(x) >= k )  <=  l^{-ceil(k/e_p)}   for k >= 1.

Note: this argument does not need the projections to be
independent, or even to contain full integer rings; a unit per
component suffices.

Now put j = max_{p | l} ceil(v_p / e_p). Then
d_l(v_l) = l^{sum_p f_p v_p} <= l^{n j}. If j >= a >= 1, some
v_p is at least e_p (a - 1) + 1. By union bound and the
Pr inequality,

    Pr(j >= a) <= n l^{-a}.

Therefore

    E_{L_l} d_l(v_l)^s
        <= 1 + n sum_{a >= 1} l^{(n s - 1) a}
        <= 1 + C_n l^{-1/2},

because n s - 1 = 1/2 - 1 = -1/2 < 0.

Now the shell-mass computation again: on the kth shell, the
conditional law is uniform measure on L_l intersected with the
shell. So

    sum_k P(k) d(k)^{s-1}
        = (1/q) prod_{l in S} ( Z_l * E_{L_l} d_l(v_l)^s ).

Bound each factor:
    Z_l * E d_l^s  <=  (1 - l^{-1})^{-n} (1 + C_n l^{-1/2}).

At every sufficiently large prime l (larger than a constant
depending only on n, eps), this factor is at most l^{eps}. The
finitely many remaining primes contribute a constant depending
only on n, eps. Since prod_{l in S} l <= q,

    prod_{l in S} [ factor at l ] <<_{n, eps} prod_{l in S} l^{eps}
                                    <= q^{eps}.

So sum_k P(k) d(k)^{s-1} <<_{n,eps} q^{eps}/q. QED.

LEMMA III.3 (weight-tail; paper eq. weight-tail).
With s = 1/(2n),

    sum_{d(k) > Q^{1/8}} P(k)/d(k)  <<_n  (1/q) Q^{-s/16}.

Proof. Apply Lemma III.2 with eps = s/16. For d(k) > Q^{1/8},

    d(k)^{-1} = d(k)^{-s} * d(k)^{s-1} <= Q^{-s/8} d(k)^{s-1}.

So

    sum_{d(k) > Q^{1/8}} P(k)/d(k)
        <= Q^{-s/8} sum_k P(k) d(k)^{s-1}
        <<_{n} (q^{-1} q^{s/16}) Q^{-s/8}
        <= q^{-1} Q^{-s/8 + s/16}    (using q <= Q)
        = q^{-1} Q^{-s/16}.

QED.

============================================================
PART IV. RESIDUE-RELATIVE WEIGHTED IDEAL COUNT
============================================================

This Part proves Step B, the "hard half": a weighted count of
integral ideals in short intervals, with the Dedekind zeta residue
kept explicit throughout.

IV.1. Ideals and the Dedekind zeta function.

Let a_K(m) = #{ integral R-ideals a : N(a) = m }. The residue at
s = 1 is

    kappa_K = Res_{s=1} zeta_K(s)
            = 2^{r_1}(2 pi)^{r_2} h_K R_K / (w_K sqrt(D)).

For K totally real, r_1 = n and r_2 = 0. Roots of unity: since
K is totally real, the only roots of unity are {+1, -1}, so
w_K = 2. Therefore

    kappa_K = 2^{n-1} h_K R_K / sqrt(D).

Since vol(F) = 2^{n-1} R_K (Part II), this yields
h_K vol(F) = sqrt(D) kappa_K as claimed.

Throughout, uniforms: every constant subscripted by n is allowed
to depend only on n. We are not allowed to depend on K, M, or
the local type. This is where the difficulty lies; estimates
must be uniform across all sequences of (K_i, M_i, sigma_i).

IV.2. Zero-free residue and residue lower bound (Stark 1974).

LEMMA IV.1 (residue; paper lemma residue).
For D sufficiently large in terms of n,

    h zeta_K(1 + h) = (1 + o_{D}(1)) kappa_K,
    uniformly for 0 < h <= 4 / log D,
    and kappa_K^{-1} <<_n log D.

For the remaining finitely many fields (those with D below the
cutoff), the first comparison holds uniformly for sufficiently
small positive h, and kappa_K^{-1} <<_n 1.

Proof. Set L = log D.

STEP 1 (a clean zero-free region up to width 1/L).

Stark's Lemma 3 (Stark 1974): zeta_K has at most one zero in the
box Re(s) >= 1 - (4L)^{-1}, |Im(s)| <= (4L)^{-1}; such a zero, if
present, is real and simple. Stark's Lemma 8 (also applies to
non-normal K): a real zero beta >= 1 - (4 n! L)^{-1} forces K to
contain a quadratic subfield.

Since n is odd, K has no quadratic subfield (a quadratic subfield
would have degree 2 dividing n). So the hypothetical real zero
does not exist, and for a constant c_n > 0 every nontrivial zero
rho of zeta_K satisfies

    |1 - rho| >= c_n / L.

STEP 2 (Hadamard identity and a bound at sigma_0 = 1 + 1/L).

Define S_K(sigma) = sum_rho Re(1/(sigma - rho)) for sigma > 1.
The classical Hadamard identity (Stark, eq. (9)) gives

    S_K(sigma) =
        1/sigma + 1/(sigma - 1) + L/2
        + zeta_K'/zeta_K(sigma)
        + G_n(sigma),

where G_n(sigma) = (n/2)(Gamma'(sigma/2)/Gamma(sigma/2) - log pi).

The zero-sum terms are nonnegative. G_n is bounded on [1, 2]. The
Euler product gives zeta_K' / zeta_K <= 0 on (1, infinity). At
sigma_0 = 1 + L^{-1},

    S_K(sigma_0) <= 1/sigma_0 + L + 0 + 0 + O_n(1) <<_n L.

STEP 3 (persistence of the bound).

For sigma in (1, 1 + 4/L], rho = beta + i gamma, define

    X = L (1 - beta),   Y = L gamma,   U = L (sigma - 1).

Then X >= 0, 0 < U <= 4, and |X + iY| >= c_n by STEP 1.

Compute:

    Re( (sigma - rho)^{-1} ) / Re( (sigma_0 - rho)^{-1} )
        = [(X + U)/(X + 1)] * [((X+1)^2 + Y^2) / ((X+U)^2 + Y^2)]
        <= 4 * (1 + 3/c_n)^2.

The first factor is <= 4 because X >= 0 and U <= 4. The second
factor is bounded by a constant times (1 + 1/c_n)^2 via
|X + 1 + iY| <= |X + iY| + 1 <= (1 + 1/c_n) |X + iY| and
|X + U + iY| >= |X + iY|, giving the n-bounded factor shown.

Summing gives 0 <= S_K(sigma) <<_n S_K(sigma_0) <<_n L throughout
(1, 1 + 4/L].

STEP 4 (log derivative near the pole).

Define F_K(sigma) = (sigma - 1) zeta_K(sigma). Then

    F_K' / F_K = 1/(sigma - 1) + zeta_K'/zeta_K(sigma)
               = S_K(sigma) - 1/sigma - L/2 - G_n(sigma)
               = O_n(L),

using the Hadamard identity re-solved for zeta_K'/zeta_K.

STEP 5 (F_K extends to sigma = 1).

Because zeta_K has a simple pole at s = 1 with residue kappa_K,
F_K extends analytically to sigma = 1 and F_K(1) = kappa_K > 0.
For 0 < h <= 4 / L:

    |log F_K(1 + h) - log F_K(1)|
        <= h * sup_{1 < sigma < 1+h} |F_K'/F_K|
        <<_n h L <= 4 C_n.

So F_K(1 + h) / kappa_K is bounded above and below by constants
depending only on n. That is,

    h zeta_K(1 + h)  asymp_n  kappa_K  for 0 < h <= 4 / log D.

STEP 6 (residue lower bound).

At sigma_0 = 1 + L^{-1}, zeta_K(sigma_0) >= 1 (Euler product of
factors > 1 at sigma_0 > 1). So

    F_K(sigma_0) = L^{-1} zeta_K(sigma_0) >= L^{-1}.

But F_K(sigma_0) = kappa_K (1 + O_n(1)) from STEP 5. So
kappa_K >>_n 1/L, hence kappa_K^{-1} <<_n log D.

STEP 7 (finiteness of the discarded fields).

Only finitely many fields of fixed degree n have discriminant
below a cutoff D_0. Direct proof: nonzero vectors of the
normalized lattice D^{-1/(2n)} sigma(R) have absolute coordinate
product at least D^{-1/2}; by Mahler's compactness criterion
this places the family {D^{-1/(2n)} sigma(R)} in a compact set
of the space of lattices. Local basis lifts and a finite cover
give bases of R with uniformly bounded conjugates. A basis
element outside Q generates K (n prime), and its monic integral
minimal polynomial therefore has bounded coefficients. There are
only finitely many such polynomials. Each corresponding zeta
function has a simple pole at 1; the remaining claims follow
from continuity and the fundamental identity at those finitely
many fields. QED.

IV.3. Shiu's theorem in Pollack's uniform form.

LEMMA IV.2 (Shiu uniform).
Let b be a nonnegative multiplicative function with b(m) <=
d_n(m) for all m. Then for Q sufficiently large in terms of n,

    sum_{u - v < m <= u} b(m)
        <<_n  (v / log u) * exp( sum_{l <= u} b(l) / l ),
    for all u^{1/2} <= v <= u.

Proof. This is Pollack's Theorem 1.1 with all excluded residue
sets empty. To check uniformity:

    d_n(l^j) = binom(j + n - 1, n - 1) <= n^j (j >= 1);

    d_n(m) <<_{n, eps} m^{eps}.

Both are growth conditions required by Pollack. Pollack's strict
interval condition u^beta < v <= u is satisfied by taking
beta = 1/4; thus the theorem covers the stated range including
both endpoints. QED.

IV.4. The weighted count (paper prop. weighted-count).

PROPOSITION IV.3 (weighted count).
Fix A_0 >= 1 and 0 < eta <= 1. For Q sufficiently large in
terms of n, A_0, eta,

    sum_{x - y < N(a) <= x} P(a)  <<_n  kappa_K y / q,

for all x, y with Q^{3/4} <= x <= A_0 Q and eta x <= y <= x.
The implied constant is independent of A_0 and eta.

Proof.

PART 1 (building the multiplicative comparison function).

Set b(m) = a_K(m) * 1_{(m, q) = 1}. This function is
nonnegative and multiplicative. It is bounded by d_n: at every
rational prime l (including ramified primes), the local
generating series for a_K is

    prod_{p | l} (1 - T^{f_p})^{-1},

which is coefficientwise bounded by (1 - T)^{-n} because
sum_{p | l} f_p e_p = n and each e_p >= 1. So a_K(l^j) <= d_n(l^j)
for every j >= 0. Multiplicativity gives the global bound.

PART 2 (the split).

Split an integral ideal a as a = a_S * a_cop, where
a_S = prod_{l in S} a_{(l)} has valuation-tuple k (= the
S-supported part), and a_cop has norm coprime to q. Because
P_l = 1 at every l not in S, the weight factorizes:
P(a) = P(k) * 1. So

    sum_{x - y < N(a) <= x} P(a)
        = sum_k P(k) sum_{(x-y)/d(k) < m <= x/d(k)} b(m).
   (IV.4)

PART 3 (Case 1: d(k) <= Q^{1/8}).

Put d = d(k) and u = x/d. Then u >= Q^{5/8}, so log u >=
(5/8) log Q and u >= D^{1/4}. Put h = 1/log u. Note h <=
4 / log D: since log u >= (5/8) log Q >= (5/8) log D and 1/log u
<= (8/5) / log D <= 4 / log D for log D >= 2. So Lemma IV.1
applies.

By Lemma IV.1: h zeta_K(1 + h) <<_n kappa_K, so
zeta_K(1 + h) <<_n kappa_K log u. Note: no upper restriction on
u. Increasing u only decreases h, so the lemma applies even when
D stays bounded and q grows.

Positivity of Euler logarithm:

    sum_{l <= u} a_K(l) l^{-1-h} <= log zeta_K(1 + h).

So

    exp( sum_{l <= u} a_K(l) l^{-1-h} )  <<_n  kappa_K log u.

Since a_K(l) <= n on primes (n prime -> each rational prime
splits into at most n primes in K, each contributing at most 1
to a_K(l), and a_K(l) = 1 for unramified un-split primes, so
a_K(l) <= n for all l), and since sum_{l <= u} (log l) / l
<< log u, we have

    sum_{l <= u} a_K(l) (l^{-1-h} - l^{-1})  <<_n  h log u <= 1.

Consequently

    exp( sum_{l <= u} a_K(l) / l )  <<_n  kappa_K log u.

PART 4 (remove S-primes).

We have log Z_S = sum_{l in S} log Z_l and, from the Euler
product,

    log Z_l = sum_{l in S} a_K(l)/l + O_n(1).

So log Z_S = sum_{l in S, l <= u} a_K(l)/l + O_n(1) plus the tail
sum_{l in S, l > u} a_K(l)/l. Bound the tail: for those l,

    a_K(l)/l <= n / l <= n / u.

The number of such l is at most (log q) / log u. So the tail is

    <<_n log q / (u log u) * 1 <<_n log(2q) / Q^{5/8} <<_n 1.

Combining:

    exp( sum_{l <= u} b(l) / l )  <<_n  kappa_K log u / Z_S.
   (IV.5)

PART 5 (Shiu in the short interval).

The interval length v = y/d satisfies v >= eta u. Since u >= Q^{5/8},
for Q sufficiently large at fixed eta we have eta u >= u^{1/2}.
Apply Shiu's theorem (Lemma IV.2) to the inner sum in (IV.4):

    sum_{u - v < m <= u} b(m)
        <<_n  (v / log u) * exp( sum_{l <= u} b(l) / l )
        <<_n  (v / log u) * (kappa_K log u / Z_S)
        = v kappa_K / Z_S.

Summing over k with d(k) <= Q^{1/8}, weighting by P(k):

    contribution (Case 1)
        <=_n  (kappa_K / Z_S) sum_k P(k) (y / d(k))
        =     (kappa_K y / Z_S) sum_k P(k)/d(k)
        <=_n  (kappa_K y / Z_S) * (Z_S / q)     [Lemma III.1]
        = kappa_K y / q.

PART 6 (Case 2: d(k) > Q^{1/8}).

Terms with d(k) > x vanish: the inner sum in (IV.4) is empty
because x/d < 1. For remaining terms x/d >= 1.

Elementary divisor-sum bound (valid for z >= 1):

    sum_{m <= z} d_n(m) <= z (1 + log z)^{n-1}.

Reason: multiply out the n-fold divisor series and bound by
integrating the first n-1 variables.

Using the weight-tail (Lemma III.3) with s = 1/(2n):

    contribution (Case 2)
        <= C_n (x / q) * (1 + log x)^{n-1}
           * sum_{d(k) > Q^{1/8}} P(k)/d(k)
        <= C_n (x / q) * (1 + log x)^{n-1} * (q^{-1} Q^{-s/16})
        = C_n (x / q) Q^{-s/16} (1 + log x)^{n-1}.

Bound: by Lemma IV.1 with q | log D, kappa_K^{-1} <<_n log(3D),
and D <= Q^2. Up to n-bounded constants, the ratio

    (contribution Case 2) / (kappa_K x / q)  -->  0

as Q -> infinity.

PART 7 (closing).

At fixed A_0 and eta, increase the threshold for Q until the
Case 2 contribution is at most kappa_K eta x / q <= kappa_K y / q.
The Case 1 contribution is bounded by C_n kappa_K y / q with C_n
independent of A_0 and eta. So

    sum_{x - y < N(a) <= x} P(a)  <<_n  kappa_K y / q.

The uniform-in-(A_0, eta) constants come from Lemma IV.1 (whose
implied constant only depends on n), the multiplicative divisor
bound, and Lemma III.1/III.2/III.3 (each of which has an
n-bounded implied constant). QED.


============================================================
PART V. NONESCAPE AND TIGHTNESS
============================================================

This Part proves Step C(i): the packet measures stay tight, i.e.,
they do not let mass escape to the "cusp" of X_n. The key is an
instantiation of the weighted count from Part IV with f equal to
the indicator of a cube about the origin.

V.1. Cusp sets and Mahler's criterion.

For 0 < theta < 1, define the open subset

    Cup(theta) = { Lambda in X_n : exists 0 != v in Lambda,
                                    ||v||_infty < theta }.

A lattice in Cup(theta) contains a "short vector" relative to the
standard cube [-theta, theta]^n. Mahler's compactness criterion
(Mahler 1946) says: { Lambda : no nonzero vector with ||v|| < theta }
is compact in X_n. So the complement of Cup(theta) is compact, and
Cup(theta) exhausting X_n as theta -> 0 measures "escape to the
cusp."

PROPOSITION V.2 (tightness; paper prop. tightness).
Every sequence of the packet probabilities with Q -> infinity is
tight. In particular, for 0 < theta < 1,

    limsup_{Q -> infinity} mu_*
        { Lambda : exists 0 != v in Lambda, ||v||_infty < theta }
        <=  C_n theta^n.

Every subsequential probability limit is A_n-invariant.

Proof. Take f = 1_C where C = [-theta, theta]^n. Then E_f(Lambda)
counts nonzero v in Lambda cap C; on Cup(theta) we have E_f >= 1
because the short vector is in the open cube (still contained in
C, up to the boundary of measure zero, which does not matter for
integrals). So

    mu_* ( Cup(theta) ) <= int_{X_n} E_f d mu_*.

Compute V_f(z) for this f.

For fixed z > 0, the C-orbit intersection with the cube is
parameterized by (u_1, ..., u_{n-1}) in R^{n-1} and one global
sign; the indicator of C is 1 iff

    |omega_j e^{u_j}| < theta for j < n,   and
    |omega_n z e^{-u_1 - ... - u_{n-1}}| < theta.

The signs omega_j only affect the signs, not absolute values, so
the indicator depends only on the u_j. Set w_j = log theta - u_j
for j < n. Then

    |omega_j e^{u_j}| < theta iff w_j > 0 (for j < n),

and the last condition becomes sum_{j<n} u_j > log(z/theta),
i.e., sum_{j<n} w_j < n log theta - log z = log(theta^n / z).

So the volume (in u-coordinates) of the set where the indicator
equals 1 is (for S = log(theta^n / z) > 0)

    volume = S^{n-1} / (n-1)!.

Therefore

    V_f(z) = 2^n / (n-1)! * max(0, log(theta^n / z))^{n-1}.

Set X = theta^n Q and U = Q^{3/4}. Applying Proposition II.7.1
(unfolding):

    int E_f d mu_* =
        (2^n / (n-1)!) * (1 / (sqrt(D) kappa_K))
        * sum_{N(a) <= X} P(a) *
            ( max(0, log(X/N(a))) )^{n-1}.

So we must bound

    S := sum_{N(a) <= X} P(a) (log(X/N(a)))^{n-1}.

Note that V_f vanishes for z > theta^n, i.e., N(a) > X = theta^n Q.
If X < 1, the sum is empty and the integral is zero.

BOUND A (small N(a) <= min(U, X)). We have

    (log(X/N(a)))^{n-1} <= (log X)^{n-1}

because N(a) <= X means X/N(a) >= 1. Since X <= Q * 1 (theta < 1),
log X <= log Q. So

    sum_{N(a) <= min(U, X)} P(a) (log(X/N(a)))^{n-1}
        <= (log Q)^{n-1} sum_{N(a) <= U} P(a).

Apply Proposition IV.3 with x = y = U = Q^{3/4}:
sum_{N(a) <= U} P(a) << kappa_K U / q = kappa_K Q^{3/4}/q.

So the "small" contribution is
    <<_n (log Q)^{n-1} kappa_K Q^{3/4}/q.

Divide by sqrt(D) kappa_K (in the unfolding formula):
    <<_n (log Q)^{n-1} Q^{3/4} / (q sqrt(D))
    = (log Q)^{n-1} Q^{-1/4}.
Since q sqrt(D) = Q, this is O_n(Q^{-1/4} (log Q)^{n-1}).

BOUND B (medium U < N(a) <= X). Define truncated cumulative
sum

    A_U(t) = sum_{U < N(a) <= t} P(a),   U <= t <= X.

Integration by parts:

    sum_{U < N(a) <= X} P(a) (log(X/N(a)))^{n-1}
        = (n-1) int_U^X A_U(t) (log(X/t))^{n-2} dt/t.

The boundary terms vanish because A_U(U) = 0 (empty sum over
interval of length zero) and (log(X/X))^{n-1} = 0.

By Proposition IV.3 at x = y = t (with t in [U, X], t >= Q^{3/4},
t <= X <= Q <= A_0 Q for A_0 = 1): A_U(t) << kappa_K t/q. So

    S_medium <<_n (kappa_K/q) int_U^X (log(X/t))^{n-2} dt
             = (kappa_K/q) X int_0^1 (log(1/v))^{n-2} dv
             = (kappa_K/q) X * Gamma(n-1)
             <<_n kappa_K X / q.

Divide by sqrt(D) kappa_K:
    <<_n X/(q sqrt(D)) = theta^n Q / Q = theta^n.

Combine:

    int E_f d mu_*  <<_n  theta^n + Q^{-1/4} (log Q)^{n-1}.

Letting Q -> infinity gives limsup mu_* (Cup(theta)) <= C_n theta^n.

TIGHTNESS. Fix a mass error eps > 0; choose theta small enough
that C_n theta^n < eps; then by the limsup bound there is Q_0 for
which every packet with Q >= Q_0 has mass <= eps on Cup(theta). The
complement of Cup(theta) is compact (Mahler). Finitely many packet
supports with Q < Q_0 are compact by the packet-model lemma. So the
whole sequence is tight.

INVARIANCE. Each packet measure is A_n-invariant by construction
(it is a weighted average of A_n-invariant orbit probabilities).
Any weak subsequential limit is therefore A_n-invariant, by
testing against continuous functions and using that A_n acts by
pushforward on the space of probabilities (pushforward of invariant
is invariant) and compactness of tight probability families. QED.

============================================================
PART VI. ORDINARY BALL BOUNDS
============================================================

This Part proves Step C(ii): the ball bound. The exponent n is
one more than dim A_n = n-1, which is what will force positive
entropy in Part VII.

VI.1. The ordinary ball B(r).

For r > 0, define

    B(r) = { h in SL_n(R) : max_{i, j} | h_{ij} - delta_{ij} | < r }.

This is the radius-r ball around the identity in the sup-norm on
matrices, intersected with SL_n(R). It is an open neighborhood of
the identity.

PROPOSITION VI.1 (ball bound; paper prop. ball-bound).
Let mu be a weak limit of packet probabilities along Q -> infinity.
For every compact Omega subset X_n, there are C_Omega > 0 and
r_Omega > 0 such that

    mu( x B(r) )  <=  C_Omega r^n  for all x in Omega, 0 < r < r_Omega.

Proof. The critical step is a "small-box" lower bound for V_f, then
we feed it into Proposition IV.3 and conclude the upper bound on
int E_f d mu.

STEP 1 (small boxes in a fixed compact of n-tuples).

Fix a compact V subset (R^*)^n and b_0 >= 1. For w in V and small
r, take

    f = 1_{ w + [-b_0 r, b_0 r]^n }.

Each coordinate of w has fixed sign and is uniformly separated from
zero on V, and the box has logarithmic width O_V( r ) in every
coordinate. So the absolute coordinate product of every point of
the box lies within Cr of z_w = prod_j |w_j|, for a constant C
depending only on V, b_0, n. As a result,

    V_f(z) <= C r^{n-1} * 1_[z_w - Cr, z_w + Cr](z).

Choose r_0 > 0 so small that z_w > 4C r_0 on V.

STEP 2 (apply weighted count).

Use Proposition IV.3 with

    x = (z_w + 2Cr) Q,  y = 4Cr Q.

Then x - y = (z_w - 2Cr) Q, and the slice height z_w avoids the
boundary. The V_f-support [z_w - Cr, z_w + Cr] Q is contained in
the interval (x - y, x].

Uniformity in w and Q: for small enough r <= r_0 and large enough
Q, the interval satisfies

    Q^{3/4} <= x <= A_0 Q   with A_0 = z_w + 2C r_0,
    eta x <= y <= x        with eta = 4Cr / A_0 <= 1.

(The constant A_0 grows with z_w, but z_w is bounded above on V.)

Apply Proposition IV.3:
    sum_{x - y < N(a) <= x} P(a)  <<_n  kappa_K y / q.

Combine with V_f bound:

    int E_f d mu_*  <=  (1 / (sqrt(D) kappa_K))
        * C r^{n-1} * sum_{x-y < N(a) <= x} P(a)
        <<_{V, b_0, n}
           (1 / (sqrt(D) kappa_K)) * C r^{n-1} * (kappa_K y/q)
        =   (r^{n-1} / (sqrt(D))) * (C y / q)
        =   (C r^{n-1} / Q) * (y / 1)  (using sqrt(D) q = Q)
        =   r^{n-1} * (C * 4CrQ) / Q
        =   4C^2 r^n.

(Note: The last identity absorbs all constants into a single n-
bounded "C^2 r^n" because Q cancels.) The important structural
term is r^n, independent of Q.

STEP 3 (choosing w_x at each x in Omega).

We need to choose a vector w_x in each lattice x (for x in Omega)
with all coordinates nonzero, uniformly over x in Omega.

Claim: every full lattice has a vector with all nonzero
coordinates. Proof: take any Z-basis x_1, ..., x_n of the lattice;
the integer combination

    x_1 + N x_2 + N^2 x_3 + ... + N^{n-1} x_n

has coordinates that are polynomials in N; each coordinate is not
identically zero, since the basis vectors are linearly independent.
Only finitely many integer N give a zero coordinate. Any other N
works.

Uniformize over Omega: near each center x in Omega, choose a
continuous local lift to basis matrices, and continue the chosen
vector using its fixed integer basis coefficients (the integer N
and the basis-change identification are locally constant on Omega,
so we can choose them uniformly). On a compact neighborhood inside
the chart, the coordinates of w_x stay bounded away from zero and
bounded above. A finite cover of Omega by such neighborhoods,
combined with a maximum over finitely many choices, gives choices

    w_x in a fixed compact V' subset (R^*)^n.

This pointwise selection need not be continuous or measurable; it
is only used to control one test function per x, and we do not
integrate over x.

STEP 4 (reduce ball-mass to E_f at w_x).

Fix x in Omega. For h in B(r), each coordinate of w_x * h - w_x
satisfies

    |(w_x h - w_x)_j| <= r * sum_{i} |(w_x)_i|  <=  b_0 r

for one fixed b_0 depending only on V', by Holder's inequality
applied entry-by-entry. So every lattice in x B(r) contains a
nonzero vector in w_x + [-b_0 r, b_0 r]^n, hence satisfies

    E_f( x h ) >= 1  for h in B(r).

Consequently, for each fixed x in Omega and small fixed r,

    mu_i( x B(r) ) <= int E_f d mu_i  <<_{V', b_0, n}  r^n.

The bound's implied constant is independent of r; the threshold
in Q_i may depend on r, which is exactly what we need.

STEP 5 (pass to the limit mu).

Fix x and r. For all i large enough (threshold depending on r),
mu_i( x B(r) ) <= C_Omega r^n. Since x B(r) is open, Portmanteau
gives

    mu( x B(r) ) <= liminf_i mu_i( x B(r) ) <= C_Omega r^n.

The same constant C_Omega works for every fixed x in Omega with
the same r-allowance. This proves the bound for all 0 < r < r_Omega
(the value r_Omega depending on the uniform r_0 in STEP 1). QED.

============================================================
PART VII. ENTROPY AND IDENTIFICATION OF THE LIMIT
============================================================

This Part proves Steps D of the roadmap: positive entropy from
the ball bound, ergodic decomposition, and EKL classification.
Finally we give the full proof of the main theorem.

VII.1. Diagonal one-parameter flow.

Choose real numbers lambda_1, ..., lambda_n, summing to zero and
distinct. Set

    a(t) = diag(e^{lambda_1 t}, ..., e^{lambda_n t}),  t in R,
    lambda = min_{i != j} |lambda_i - lambda_j| > 0.

We bound the entropy of the flow map x -> x a(1) from below.

For any A_n-invariant probability nu, write h_nu(a(1)) for the
measure-theoretic entropy of the transformation x -> x a(1)
with respect to nu.

PROPOSITION VII.1 (entropy from balls; paper prop.
entropy-from-balls).
Let n >= 3 and let mu be an A_n-invariant probability on X_n.
Suppose for every compact Omega subset X_n there exist C_Omega,
r_Omega > 0 such that

    mu( x B(r) ) <= C_Omega r^n  for x in Omega, 0 < r < r_Omega.

Then every A_n-invariant probability xi satisfying xi <= c mu for
some finite c has h_xi( a(1) ) >= lambda / 3.

Proof. Fix 0 < b < 1/4 and consider the two-sided neighborhoods

    T_t = a(t) B(b) a(-t)  cap  a(-t) B(b) a(t),  t >= 0.

STEP 1 (structure of T_t).

If h in T_t, then

    |h_ii - 1| < b,    and    |h_ij| < b e^{-|lambda_i - lambda_j| t}
                              for i != j.

(Standard computation: conjugation by a(t) multiplies the (i, j)
entry by e^{(lambda_i - lambda_j) t}, and the two-sided
intersection bounds both t and -t, so entries pick up the smaller
of the two decay factors, giving the absolute exponent.)

Set r = e^{-lambda t}. Every term in the determinant expansion of
det(h) other than the product of diagonal entries contains at least
two off-diagonal entries; each off-diagonal entry is bounded by
b r. There are n! - 1 such terms; their total is O_{n,b}(r^2).
Since det(h) = 1 (h in SL_n(R)), we get

    prod_i h_ii = 1 + O_{n, b}(r^2).

Consequently the diagonal matrix

    d(h) = diag( h_11, ..., h_{n-1, n-1}, (prod_{i<n} h_ii)^{-1} )

lies in a fixed compact subset D subset A_n: its log entries are
bounded uniformly in t (and r -> 0), because all h_ii are needed
to be within b of 1 and the product correction is bounded.

Also, d(h)^{-1} h in B(C r) for a constant C = C(n, b), because
off-diagonal entries are O(r) and the diagonal correction is O(r^2)
plus the entrywise-close-to-1 diagonal.

STEP 2 (tube covering).

In log coordinates, D is a bounded subset of R^{n-1}. Cover D by a
mesh of size ~r (in log coords); there are at most N_t <= C r^{-(n-1)}
centers d_j. Enlarging C to absorb the matrix-multiplication error:

    T_t  subset  union_{j=1}^{N_t} d_j B(C r).    (VII.1)

STEP 3 (tube mass for xi).

Fix a compact Omega subset X_n. For x in Omega, all centers
x d_j lie in the fixed compact set Omega D. So domination xi <= c mu
and the ball bound give, for all sufficiently large t (i.e., small
r = e^{-lambda t}), for every x in Omega:

    xi( x T_t )
        <= sum_{j=1}^{N_t} xi( x d_j B(C r) )       [by (VII.1)]
        <= N_t * c * C_Omega (Cr)^n                 [domination + ball bound]
        <= C_{Omega, b, c} r^{-(n-1)} r^n
        = C_{Omega, b, c} r
        = C_{Omega, b, c} e^{-lambda t}.             (VII.2)

Since xi is a probability, the constant can be enlarged (uniformly
in Omega) to cover the finitely many integer times t before the
estimate holds.

STEP 4 (apply ELMV's two-sided tube criterion).

We use (Einsiedler-Lindenstrauss-Michel-Venkatesh, Duke 2009,
Corollary 3.3). In the notation

    B^{(-s, t)} = a(-s) B a(s)  cap  a(-t) B a(t),

the criterion says the following. If flow-invariant probabilities
xi_i converge weakly to a probability xi, and t_i -> infinity, then
h_xi(a(1)) >= eta, provided that for every compact set Omega there
is an identity neighborhood B and a constant C_Omega such that

    xi_i( x B^{(-t_i, t_i)} ) <= C_Omega e^{-2 eta t_i}
    for all x in Omega.

The criterion applies on the noncompact lattice space; the
requirement "xi is a probability" is essential.

Apply with: constant sequence xi_i = xi, t_i = i, identity
neighborhood B = B(b), eta = lambda/3. The tube
B(b)^{(-t, t)} = T_t. From (VII.2),

    xi( x T_t ) <= C e^{-lambda t}
                <= C e^{-2 lambda t / 3}     (since 1 >= 2/3)
                = C e^{-2 eta t}.

So the criterion's hypothesis is met, and h_xi(a(1)) >= eta =
lambda/3. QED.

LEMMA VII.2 (entropy in ergodic components;
paper lemma entropy-components).
Under the hypotheses of Proposition VII.1, almost every A_n-ergodic
component of mu has positive entropy for a(1).

Proof. The group A_n = R^{n-1} is locally compact and second
countable, and its action on X_n (a standard Borel space) is
continuous. The ergodic decomposition theorem (Greschonig-Schmidt
2000, Theorem 5.2, specialized to invariant probabilities)
supplies a measurable decomposition

    mu = int_Z nu_z d tau(z),

where each nu_z is an A_n-invariant and A_n-ergodic probability,
and Z is the quotient with respect to a factor fixed by A_n.

MEASURABILITY. Set T(x) = x a(1). The function z -> h_{nu_z}(T) is
measurable. Proof: choose increasing finite Borel partitions P_k
generating the Borel sigma-algebra of X_n. For every T-invariant
probability nu,

    h_nu(T) = sup_k inf_{N >= 1} (1/N) H_nu( V_{j=0}^{N-1} T^{-j} P_k ).

Here H_nu denotes the Shannon entropy of a finite partition. The
right side is a countable (sup over k, inf over N, sum over j) of
Borel measurable functions of nu, so it is measurable in z.

ROKHLIN ENTROPY INTEGRAL. The component partition is fixed by T,
since a(1) in A_n. Rokhlin's entropy integral formula (Rokhlin
1967, Theorem 9.8) then gives

    h_mu(T) = int_Z h_{nu_z}(T) d tau(z).        (VII.3)

The same formula holds after restricting to any measurable
collection of components. It does not require the nu_z to be
T-ergodic: the induced transformation on the component space is
the identity and contributes zero entropy.

CONTRADICTION. Suppose Z_0 = { z : h_{nu_z}(T) = 0 } had weight
w = tau(Z_0) > 0. Then

    xi = (1/w) int_{Z_0} nu_z d tau(z)

is an A_n-invariant probability satisfying xi <= w^{-1} mu.
Equation (VII.3), applied to the restricted decomposition,
gives h_xi(T) = 0, contradicting Proposition VII.1 (which
requires h_xi >= lambda/3 > 0). Thus almost every ergodic
component has positive entropy. QED.

VII.3. Einsiedler-Katok-Lindenstrauss measure classification.

CITE (EKL 2006, Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 1.4): for prime n >= 3,
any A_n-invariant A_n-ergodic probability on
    SL_n(R) / SL_n(Z)
is the Haar measure m_n whenever some diagonal one-parameter
subgroup has positive entropy.

Their action is on the LEFT. Our X_n = SL_n(Z) \ SL_n(R) with
right action by a(t) matches EKL after the map
Gamma g -> g^{-1} Gamma.

Precisely: from a point Gamma g, our right multiplication by a(t)
gives Gamma g a(t). Under inversion, that becomes
a(t)^{-1} g^{-1} Gamma = a(-t) g^{-1} Gamma, which is a LEFT
multiplication by a(-t). Entropy is unchanged by inversion of an
invertible transformation (and by replacing a one-parameter flow
by its inverse: entropy of a(t) equals entropy of a(-t)). So EKL
applies to our convention with no additional hypothesis.

VII.4. Proof of the main theorem.

PROOF OF THEOREM thm:main.
Let mu_i be the packet probabilities along a sequence in the
theorem's hypothesis, so D(M_i) -> infinity.

  (a) Q_i -> infinity. Follows from Claim II.1.1: D(M_i) <= Q_i^{2n}
      and D(M_i) -> infinity forces Q_i -> infinity.

  (b) Tightness. Proposition V.2 gives limsup mu_i(Cup(theta)) <=
      C_n theta^n, so for small theta all the packet measures are
      tight on the complement of the cusp.

  (c) Invariance. Proposition V.2 also shows every subsequential
      limit mu is A_n-invariant.

  (d) Ball bound. Proposition VI.1 gives mu( x B(r) ) <= C_Omega r^n
      for x in any compact Omega, uniformly on the limit mu.

  (e) Positive entropy. Proposition VII.1 applies (hypotheses met
      from (d)), giving h_xi(a(1)) >= lambda/3 for every
      A_n-invariant xi <= c mu.

  (f) Positive entropy in each ergodic component. Lemma VII.2
      applies, so almost every A_n-ergodic component of mu has
      positive entropy for a(1).

  (g) Classification. Since n is prime and >= 3, the EKL
      classification makes every A_n-invariant, A_n-ergodic,
      positive-entropy probability on X_n equal to m_n.

  (h) Reconstruction. Combine (f) and (g): every ergodic
      component of mu equals m_n. Integrating the components
      (ergodic decomposition) gives mu = m_n.

  (i) Uniqueness and no mass loss. Every subsequential limit is
      m_n, by (h). Together with tightness from (b), this proves
      mu_i -> m_n as i -> infinity. And by (h) plus tightness,
      no mass escapes into the cusp.

The estimates (b)-(e) were made for each prescribed local type
separately; no averaging over different local module types is used.

This closes the proof. QED.


============================================================
PART VIII. ADELIC VERSION (APPENDIX)
============================================================

This appendix identifies the packet measure with an adelic
pushforward of torus Haar probability. It is not needed for the
main proof; the purpose is to show that the lattice
parametrization derived in Parts II-VII agrees with the standard
adelic formulation from the literature.

VIII.1. Adelic data.

Let A_f denote the finite adeles of Q, and set

    Z_hat = prod_l Z_l,
    A_Q = R x A_f,
    A_K = K (x)_Q A_Q,
    R_hat = R (x)_Z Z_hat.

Choose once and for all a Q-basis b_1, ..., b_n of K and let
rho(alpha) denote multiplication by alpha on row coefficient
vectors in this basis. This embeds the algebraic torus

    T_K = (Res_{K/Q} G_m) / G_m

into G = PGL_{n/Q}. The base matrix

    B_sigma = ( sigma_j(b_i) )_{i, j}

has the useful property

    rho(alpha) B_sigma = B_sigma diag(sigma_1(alpha), ..., sigma_n(alpha)).

We call this identity the regular diagonalization of rho(alpha).
It says that the rational multiplication matrix becomes a
diagonal matrix in the sigma-embedding, with the n conjugate
values along the diagonal.

VIII.2. The completed lattice attached to g_f.

Fix g_f in G(A_f). Choose any matrix lift to GL_n(A_f) (the
choice is unique modulo the center of GL_n, i.e., modulo
A_Q^*-values). Set

    M_hat(g_f) = Z_hat^n g_f^{-1},
    M(g_f)    = K cap M_hat(g_f).

LEMMA VIII.1. For every g_f in G(A_f), the subset M(g_f) is a
full Z-lattice of K, and its pro-finite completion equals
M_hat(g_f).

Proof. Choose an integer d >= 1 with

    d Z_hat^n subset M_hat(g_f) subset d^{-1} Z_hat^n.

(Existence: g_f is a product over primes of local matrices; only
finitely many are not integral, so d can bound their
denominators.) Patch the finite quotient d^{-1} Z^n / d Z^n: this
gives a full lattice M = K cap M_hat whose completion is
M_hat(g_f). Full rank: the Q-span of M is K because
M_hat(g_f) has full Q_hat-rank and K has dimension n.

Change of projective lift: if g_f is replaced by c g_f for a scalar
finite idele c in A_Q^*, then M_hat(g_f) is multiplied by c^{-1}.
Every such c is a rational scalar times an element of Z_hat^*; the
latter fixes Z_hat^n and hence every completed full lattice.
So the change of lift rescales M(g_f) by a rational number,
which corresponds to the same K^*-homothety class. QED.

VIII.3. The adelic packet.

Fix g_inf in G(R) with

    g_inf^{-1} T_K(R)^circ g_inf = A_n.

Here T_K(R)^circ is the identity component of the real points of
T_K. Existence: the real points of T_K are a maximal R-split
torus in G(R) = PGL_n(R); its identity component is a conjugate
of A_n inside PGL_n(R). The odd-degree identification
G(R) = SL_n(R) (via [h] -> (det h)^{-1/n} h) transports this to
A_n subset SL_n(R).

Define the adelic packet as the projection of

    T_K(Q) \ T_K(A_Q) g

to G(Q) \ G(A_Q) / G(Z_hat), equipped with the pushforward of
torus Haar probability. Here g = (g_inf, g_f) in G(A_Q), and
G(Z_hat) is the standard compact-open subgroup.

PROPOSITION VIII.2 (adelic-packet).
Assume the data above. Then:

  (i) The adelic torus quotient T_K(Q) \ T_K(A_Q) is compact.
  (ii) The pushforward of torus Haar is a probability on
       G(Q) \ G(A_Q) / G(Z_hat); after the standard identification
       with X_n, it agrees with mu_{K, M(g_f), sigma'} for some
       ordering sigma' of the real embeddings of K.
  (iii) Every prescribed local homothety type occurs this way as
        g_f varies over G(A_f).
  (iv) Changing the rational basis (b_i) or the projective matrix
       lift of g has no effect on the packet.
  (v) Changing the allowed real diagonalizer (g_inf) only reorders
      the real embeddings sigma' but does not change the packet.
  (vi) Therefore Theorem thm:main includes every adelic
       presentation of any local homothety type.

Proof. We work through the identifications.

STEP 1 (ambient quotient ~ X_n). Given a matrix lift g_f in
GL_n(A_f), choose a basis matrix c in GL_n(Q) for the Q-span of
Q^n cap Z_hat^n g_f^{-1}. Then c g_f in GL_n(Z_hat), so the
number-field side produces the real lattice Z^n (c g_inf),
which after normalization to covolume one lies in X_n. Changing
the basis c or the lift g_f does not change that lattice.

For odd n, the signed real nth root [h] -> (det h)^{-1/n} h
identifies G(R) = PGL_n(R) with SL_n(R), and G(Z) = PGL_n(Z)
with SL_n(Z). So we work with X_n = SL_n(Z) \ SL_n(R).

STEP 2 (torus quotient).

Multiplication matrices modulo scalars give

    T_K(Q) \ T_K(A_Q)  ~  K^* \ A_K^* / A_Q^*.

To see the inclusion direction: a matrix whose projective class
lies in T_K becomes, over any ground field F_0 containing Q, a
scalar multiple of a multiplication operator over the algebraic
closure of F_0. Multiplication operators commute with the regular
representation of K, so the matrix belongs to (K (x) F_0)^*.
The centralizer of the regular representation is precisely the
algebra of multiplication operators, so the projective class is
K^* x F_0^*. This works already over F_0 = Q. Finite lifts may be
chosen integral at almost all primes, giving the compact open
finite part.

STEP 3 (finite idele action).

A finite idele t_f in A_f^* acts on the completed lattice by

    M_hat  |-->  t_f^{-1} M_hat.

Thus the finite-idele translates of a fixed completed lattice are
exactly the local homothety type prescribed in Part II (the
choice t_f ranges over the finite ideles, whose action is
precisely by local multipliers c_l in K_l^*).

STEP 4 (stabilizer).

The stabilizer of M_hat(g_f) in A_K^* is

    { z in A_K^* : z M_hat = M_hat }  =  (O(M) (x) Z_hat)^*.

Equality holds precisely when both z and z^{-1} preserve M_hat.
Modulo the central A_Q^* (which corresponds to the PGL reduction),
the projective stabilizer is the image of (O(M) (x) Z_hat)^* in
T_K(A). This unit group is compact open in the finite ideles and
has finite index in R_hat^*; at each prime l not dividing q, it
equals R_l^*. Its projective image is compact open as well. (No
invertibility of M over O(M) is used: the stabilizer condition is
additive and only depends on M_hat as a lattice.)

STEP 5 (decomposition, measures, matching to Part II).

Divide T_K(A) by the image of R_hat^*. Fractional-ideal
factorization together with

    A_Q^* = Q^* x R^+ x Z_hat^*

gives, for each ordinary ideal class [I], one real fiber
E \ C (E = R^*, C as in Part II). These fibers are compact by
the unit theorem. The omitted finite unit group is compact, so
the whole torus quotient is compact. This proves (i).

At the stabilizer of M_hat, the finite fibers retain the
translates N(I) from Part II. The same decomposition becomes

    disjoint union over [I] of ( N(I) x C ) / E,

with exactly the action from the packet-model lemma (Lemma
II.5.1). Torus Haar measure pushes to:

  - uniform counting measure on each finite unit orbit N(I) (this
    is the "label" part);
  - the half-log-Haar measure on C, i.e., counting on 2^n sign
    components times Lebesgue on the u-coordinates;
  - after normalization, the packet probability mu_{K, M(g_f),
    sigma'} from Part II.

For g_inf = [B_sigma], the regular diagonalization identifies
the resulting real lattices with the displayed map (Q N(I))^{-1/n}
sigma(N) t of Lemma II.5.1. This proves (ii).

STEP 6 (occurrence of every local type, and independence).

Every full lattice M in K can be obtained by choosing a basis
matrix for its completed lattice and taking its inverse as g_f.
So (iii) holds: every local type is realized by some g_f.

For (iv): if a rational basis change matrix is c in GL_n(Q), then
B'_sigma = c B_sigma and rho' = c rho c^{-1}, while the packet data
have representatives g'_infty = c g_infty and g'_f = c g_f. The
torus is conjugated and the translated packet is left-multiplied
by c, which disappears in the ambient rational quotient
G(Q) \ G(A_Q) / G(Z_hat). So the packet is unchanged. Also, any
other allowed real diagonalizer must be of the form [B_sigma d P]
with d real diagonal and P a permutation matrix, because it must
permute the joint eigenspaces. The diagonal factor translates the
real torus and permutes its sign components, preserving its Haar
probability; the permutation reorders the embeddings. So the
packet is unchanged up to re-indexing sigma. This proves (v).

Combining (ii)-(v), Theorem thm:main includes every adelic
presentation of any local homothety type. QED.

PART IX. GLOSSARY
============================================================

Each term is restated in one sentence.

A_n (diagonal group). The subgroup of SL_n(R) of trace-zero
diagonal matrices, isomorphic to R^{n-1}.

absolute norm N(a). For a nonzero R-ideal a, the cardinality of
R/a; extended multiplicatively to fractional ideals by N(I J^{-1})
= N(I)/N(J).

class number h_K. The number of ordinary ideal classes of R.

covolume. The determinant of any basis matrix of a lattice;
equal to the volume of its fundamental parallelepiped.

cusp (of X_n). The noncompact end of X_n, detected by existence
of short lattice vectors; the complement of a compact core.

Dedekind zeta zeta_K(s). The Dirichlet series sum_a N(a)^{-s}
over nonzero R-ideals; has a simple pole at s = 1 with residue
kappa_K.

discriminant D = |Disc(K)|. Absolute value of the field
discriminant of K.

D(M) (multiplier-order discriminant). D(M) = D * [R : O(M)]^2.

E (unit group). R^* = O_K^*, the group of units of K.

E_f (lattice-vector sum). E_f(Lambda) = sum_{0 != v in Lambda} f(v)
for a nonnegative Borel function f on R^n.

entropy h_nu(a(1)). The measure-theoretic entropy of the flow
map x -> x a(1) with respect to an A_n-invariant probability nu.

ergodic component. A maximal A_n-invariant piece of a measure on
which the A_n-action is ergodic.

Haar measure (m_n). The left-SL_n(R)-invariant probability on X_n.

homothety (K^*-). Two lattices M', M'' are K^*-homothetic if
M'' = alpha M' for some alpha in K^*.

ideal class. Equivalence class of fractional R-ideals modulo
principal ones.

kappa_K. Residue of zeta_K at s = 1; equals
2^{r_1}(2 pi)^{r_2} h_K R_K / (w_K sqrt(D)).

lattice / full lattice / unimodular. Lattice: discrete subgroup of
R^n spanned by n linearly independent vectors. Full: free rank-n
Z-submodule of K. Unimodular: covolume one.

L_l (localized lattice). j_l^{-1} M_l where (R M)_l = j_l R_l.

local homothety. M'_l = c_l M''_l for every l for some
c_l in K_l^*.

local type. The equivalence class of M under local homothety.

Mahler's compactness criterion. A family of unimodular lattices is
relatively compact iff the shortest nonzero vector length is
bounded away from zero.

maximal order R. O_K, the ring of integers of K.

multiplier order O(M). { alpha in K : alpha M subset M }.

m_n. The Haar probability measure on X_n.

N(I), N(a). Absolute norms of fractional and integral ideals.

O_K, R. The ring of integers of K; R = O_K throughout.

orbit. Set Lambda a for a fixed Lambda in X_n and a in A_n.

packet. The finite set P_{K, M, sigma} of A_n-orbits
Lambda_{M', sigma} w A_n, w in W_n, M' a homothety-class
representative.

P(a), P_l(k_l). Containment probabilities: probability that a
random local unit translate of the module contains a fixed element
of the given valuation profile.

prime degree. n prime (here n >= 5).

regulator R_K. The covolume in R^{r_1 + r_2 - 1} of the embedded
unit group, in real logarithmic coordinates.

Shiu's theorem. A short-interval Brun-Titchmarsh type inequality
for nonnegative multiplicative functions bounded by the n-fold
divisor function.

Stark's lemmas 3 and 8. (a) At most one zero of zeta_K in a strip
of width c / log D near s = 1; (b) any such real zero would force
a quadratic subfield.

tightness. A family of probability measures on a locally compact
space whose mass concentrates on compact subsets, uniformly in
the family.

tube criterion (ELMV). If the two-sided neighborhoods
a(t) B a(-t) cap a(-t) B a(t) have mass decaying like
C e^{-2 eta t} uniformly on compacts, then h_xi(a(1)) >= eta.

W_n. The 2^{n-1} element group of diagonal +-1 matrices in
SL_n(R) (product of entries equal to 1).

X_n. The moduli space SL_n(Z) \ SL_n(R) of unimodular lattices.

The argument above is the full proof of Theorem thm:main. It has
six substantive steps:

  Part II     identifies the packet mean of any lattice-vector
              count E_f with a weighted sum over integral ideals
              of K (Prop II.7.1).

  Part III    gives exact total mass (Lemma III.1) and a
              power-saving tail (Lemma III.3) for the weights.

  Part IV     gives a weighted count of ideals in short intervals
              (Prop IV.3) via the Dedekind zeta residue (Stark)
              and Shiu's theorem.

  Part V      translates the weighted count into tightness
              (Prop V.2): no escape into the cusp.

  Part VI     translates the weighted count into an ordinary-ball
              bound mu( x B(r) ) <= C r^n (Prop VI.1).

  Part VII    feeds the ball bound into the ELMV two-sided tube
              criterion (Prop VII.1), extracts positive entropy in
              every A_n-ergodic component (Lemma VII.2), and
              classifies every such component as m_n by EKL
              (n prime -> the classification applies).

  Part VIII   is an appendix connecting the lattice description to
              the adelic quotient picture.

A patient reader can compare any specific estimate here with the
corresponding section of the original paper using the reading
guide above. Every step in the original proof is reproduced here
with full proofs; the only stylistic changes are plain-language
introduction of each term, explicit identification of the generic
"all coordinates nonzero" element in the packet-model lemma, and
the split of the original adelic appendix into statement +
step-by-step proof.
