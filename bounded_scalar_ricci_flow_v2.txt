parmedskipnoindentStrategy of the proof.

The proof follows one strategy. Fix a hypothetical concentration point
where curvature blows up at T. Successive rescalings produce a finite
tree of Ricci-flat limits; the outermost nonflat limit defines a scale
a(t) that cannot change by any fixed factor on short intervals. On the
corresponding buffered intervals, parabolic rescaling places the Ricci
energy K of the outermost bubble in a limiting spacetime where the
renormalized Einstein--Hilbert functional and the companion tree inequality
force Kto 0; that pins a(t) and rules out the concentration point.
The mechanics are in four blocks. Block~I (``The spatial curvature
radius'' to ``Buffered intervals'') estimates curvature at all scales,
describes the slice limits, and selects the buffered intervals. Block~II
(``Concentration estimates'' to ``The maximal function'') bounds the Ricci
heat equation and the root energy. Block~III (``Collar extension'')
builds and estimates the analytic exterior from a fixed collar. Block~IV
(``Functional change and the contradiction'' to ``Continuation'') supplies
the lower and upper bounds on the functional variation and closes the
argument. A reader focused on the main idea should read Blocks~I and
IV first, treating Block~III as a black box for the error analysis.

parmedskipnoindentIntroduction.

A Ricci flow is a one-parameter family of Riemannian metrics solving the
heat-type equation

 ∂_t g=-2Ric(g).

On a closed manifold it exists and is smooth for a short time; the central
question is what makes the maximal smooth existence time T<∞ finite.
The full curvature tensor decides this: if |Rm| stays bounded on [0,T),
the flow continues past T (Hamilton, 1982). The scalar curvature
R=tr_gRic is only a trace, so a bound on R is far weaker; it controls
volume by ∂_t,dmu_g=-R,dmu_g but leaves the full geometry free.
The scalar-curvature extension problem asks whether a uniform bound on R
alone already forbids a finite-time singularity. The answer is affirmative
in real dimension four.
src{pdf: https://doi.org/10.4310/jdg/1214436922}

[Smooth extension]
Let M be a closed connected smooth four-manifold, 0<T<∞, and let
g(t), 0le t<T, be a smooth Ricci flow from smooth initial metric with
sup_{Mtimes[0,T)}|R(g(t))|<∞. Then there are ε>0 and a
smooth Ricci flow widetilde g on M for 0le t<T+ε with
widetilde g(t)=g(t) for all t<T.



Let g(t), 0le t<T<∞, be a smooth Ricci flow on a closed four-manifold
with finite maximal smooth existence time T. Then
limsup_{t↑ T}max_MR(g(t))=+∞.


Proof. 
(∂_t-Delta)R=2|Ric|^2ge0 and the maximum principle bound R below
on any finite interval. A finite upper limit would bound |R| near T; since
R is smooth on the compact initial part, Theorem~thm:extension applied
componentwise would contradict maximality of T.


The proof rules out contraction of a specific outermost bubble scale. A
concentration point produces a finite rooted tree of Ricci-flat limits, one for
each scale at which curvature blows up; the outermost nonflat limit has a
length a(t) that is positive before T but tends to zero at the singular
time. On short intervals a cannot change by a fixed factor: rescaling would
freeze the pointed geometry, so any fixed-factor change would contradict the
identity of a fixed limiting tree. Parabolic rescaling on the buffered
intervals therefore produces a limiting spacetime in which the Ricci energy K
of the outermost bubble is comparable from start to end. The renormalized
Einstein--Hilbert functional computed on that scaling, together with a sharp
inequality from the companion paper, forces Kto0. But Kto0 means
distances are preserved on the concentration scale, which pins a(t) and
contradicts its prescribed finite change. Hence no concentration point exists
and the flow extends smoothly.

parmedskipnoindentThe geometric obstruction.

Hamilton proved short-time existence; v{S}ev{s}um showed a uniform bound on
Ric forces continuation. A scalar
bound is weaker because after rescaling a would-be singularity the scalar
curvature can tend to zero while full curvature stays bounded away from zero.
The limit is then Ricci-flat without being flat, so a scalar bound permits a
whole tree of collapsing Ricci-flat bubbles.
src{pdf: ./pdf/src-sesum2005.pdf (Curvature tensor under the Ricci flow)}

Bamler--Zhang proved the heat-kernel and curvature estimates under scalar
bounds, including the four-dimensional energy and compactness theory used
below, and obtained smooth extension when H_2(M;F)=0 for all
F, Corollary~1.10 of the first part. Simon continued through the
limiting space by an orbifold flow; Bamler's convergence theory gives smooth
convergence off a codimension-four set; Liu--Simon treat four-dimensional
convergence under scalar-integrability hypotheses. All allow
concentrated curvature and orbifold points; the continuation below instead
keeps the given flow on its original manifold. Blow-up is known for
K"ahler--Ricci flows (Zhang), for global Type~I singularities (Enders,
M"uller and Topping), and for local Type~I points (Buzano and Di Matteo);
no rate is imposed.

parmedskipnoindentThe outermost scale and the static functional.

Successive rescalings at a concentration point produce a finite rooted tree of
Ricci-flat pieces; the outermost nonflat vertex has a length scale a(t)
described below. The proof rules out contraction of a(t) while
allowing arbitrarily small descendants.

Retain the true metric through an annular collar of radius b=Na (N large,
fixed) and attach an ALE end in Euclidean quotient coordinates. The
renormalized Einstein--Hilbert functional is
[
 E(g)=lim_{Dto∞}Big[int_{{r<D}}R,dmu_g-int_{{r=D}}
 (∂_jg_{ij}-∂_ig_{jj})n_i,dS_deltaBig],
]
the mass term of Deruelle--Ozuch, Definition~4.2 and Proposition~4.4 of their
paper. It scales by the square of the length unit and its first
variation pairs the velocity with tfrac12Rg-Ric. A companion study of path
selection on degenerating Ricci-flat trees proves the sequential inequality
E=o(M) on each fixed
limiting tree (M the weighted, dimensionless Ricci error). The static input is
recorded below; the argument must verify the hypotheses for flow metrics and compare
the functional variation with spacetime Ricci energy.
src{pdf: ./pdf/src-deruelleozuch2025.pdf (Deruelle and Ozuch, A Lojasiewicz inequality for ALE metrics)}

Two estimates drive the argument. On a nearly flat annulus the contracted
Bianchi identity removes the critical radial harmonic mode of Ric, improving
the annular decay beyond the dimensional r^{-2} rate so the loss outside the
collar is small against the energy inside. The exterior beyond the collar is
built as an analytic function of the metric on the one fixed material collar,
with the longer exterior entering only an error estimate; hence the extension
differentiates in time on patches without a smoothly varying tree. This rests
on the Saint-Venant relation of the Calabi complex (Khavkine) and on Korn
control modulo rigid motions (Ciarlet and Ciarlet), both used in the collar
construction below.
src{pdf: ./pdf/src-khavkine2017.pdf (Khavkine, The Calabi complex and Killing sheaf cohomology)}

parmedskipnoindentProof structure and conventions.

The static input is fixed first. The scale estimates, genuine orbifold limits,
finite trees, and the continuous scale a(t) follow, together with buffered
intervals on which a changes by a fixed factor with root length tending to
zero after parabolic normalization. The Ricci-equation estimates come next:
the exterior decay beyond the outermost scale, and the maximal and weighted
interior estimates controlling the root energy. The collar construction then
builds the exterior extension and its error. A final comparison of functional
variation with Ricci energy: an explicit lower bound cK^2 and upper bound
zeta_i(K+1)^2 force Kto0; then distances are preserved so the
curvature-radius maximum defining a is preserved, contradicting its
prescribed change, while absence of a concentration point gives a smooth
terminal metric and continuation on M.

Throughout, Delta=trnabla^2 and |A|_{k,r}=sum_{j=0}^k r^j|nabla^jA| (the
pointwise magnitude unchanged, derivatives scaled). Quotient-chart components
are Cartesian components on the Euclidean cover; constructions are equivariant
and descend. Constants may depend on a fixed finite differentiation order and,
after extraction, on a fixed finite tree; uniformity in a sequence index, length
or collar factor N is stated where needed, and a sequential little-oh is taken
after fixing the parameters in its statement.

parmedskipnoindentThe static input and its normalization.

Theorem~2.2 of the companion study on path selection is recorded here. Constants and small

parmedskipnoindentGeometric data and the functional.

Let T be a finite rooted tree of four-dimensional Ricci-flat spaces
(V_v,h_v). A vertex has one exterior end and one punctured end per child.
Every end carries Cartesian coordinates on a Euclidean annulus modulo a finite
subgroup of O(4) acting freely on S^3; the group on a joined end is
nontrivial, and the two groups on an edge are identified with a fixed orthogonal
identification included in the charts. Put tau=log r on an exterior end and
tau=-log r at a puncture. In vertex units,

 h_v=delta+O(e^{-mutau})

on joined ends, with scaled derivatives, for some mu>0; the unjoined
exterior end of the root requires h_v-delta=O(r^{-q}) with q>1. Bounds
through order 20 suffice.

For an edge from a parent p to a child c truncate both end coordinates at
tau=L_e and identify the collars by

 x_p=e^{-2L_e}x_c, a_c=a_pe^{-2L_e}, a_{rm root}=1.

The physical radius rho=a_vr agrees in both charts and
tau_p+tau_c=2L_e; the metric in vertex units is multiplied by a_v^2 in
physical units. Gluing the boundary faces with their compatible collars
produces the manifold.

Assume the sequence of metrics has all L_eto∞ and converges to h_v on
compacts of every open vertex, with uniform weighted Ricci control

 |rho^2Ric|_jle CM
 e^{-mutau},&joined half-ends r^{-q},&root exterior 1,&cores,
  j=10,

with Mto0 (tensor magnitude physical; covariant derivatives scaled by powers
of rho). Decreasing mu or q is allowed.

Define, in physical root units,

 E(g)=lim_{Dto∞}Big{int_{r<D}R,dmu_g
 -int_{r=D}(∂_jg_{ij}-∂_ig_{jj})n_i,dS_deltaBig}.

All chart integrals are quotient integrals.

[Renormalization and first variation]
The functional (eq:E-def) is defined on the preceding metrics. For a
variation with the same root decay,

 DE_g(dot g)=intbig<tfrac12Rg-Ric,dot gbig>_g,dmu_g.

It is invariant under diffeomorphisms isotopic to the identity whose root
displacement, with scaled derivatives along the isotopy, is O(r^{1-q'}),
q'>1. A global scaling by s, with a coordinate dilation restoring
delta, multiplies E by s^2. Under (eq:tree-ricci-error),
|E|le CM.


Proof. 
On the root end write g=delta+p. The density obeys
R√(det g)=∂_i(∂_jp_{ij}-∂_ip_{jj})+O(|p||∂^2p|+|∂ p|^2),
the error O(r^{-2q-2}), radially integrable; this proves convergence. The
linear boundary term in the variation cancels the variation of the subtracted
integral, and remaining boundary products are O(D^{2-2q})to0; integration by
parts gives (eq:first-variation). For a Lie derivative the contracted
Bianchi identity kills the interior divergence and the boundary term vanishes by
the displacement decay, so the functional is invariant along the isotopy.
Differentiating the scaling law at s=1 with a compensating dilation (identity
in a fixed outer region, metric varied by 2g inside and O(r^{-q}) on the
tail, paired with an integrable Ric) gives 2|E|le CM, hence E=0 for
Ric=0.


[Static tree inequality]
This is Theorem~2.2 of the companion study. For the fixed tree and sequences above,

 E(g)=o(M),

and for M=0 it means E(g)=0.

Throughout, (M^4,g(t)), 0le t<T, is the closed flow with |R|le C_R.

parmedskipnoindentThe spatial curvature radius.


For a small cap l_0>0 and a material point x,
l(x,t)=sup{0<rle l_0:sup_{B_t(x,r)}|Rm|le r^{-2}}.


The definition uses the whole spatial ball. Then l is positive, continuous
before T, and 1-Lipschitz: |l(x,t)-l(y,t)|le d_t(x,y) (a ball of radius
r-d_t(x,y) at y lies inside the radius-r ball at x with a larger allowed

[Scale estimates and degeneration]
For a small cap and all sufficiently late times:

 cr^4leVol_t B_t(x,r)&le Cr^4, int_M|Rm|^2,dmu_tle C,
 
 |nabla^kRm|(x,t)&le C_kl^{-2-k},

The set {l<r} is covered by a bounded number of radius-Cr balls.
Distances converge uniformly to a compact metric quotient Z; off finitely many
concentration points the metric converges smoothly. Small-scale limits are
complete noncollapsed Ricci-flat orbifolds, smooth off finitely many genuine
orbifold points with nontrivial finite group acting freely on S^3; a nonflat
limit has one Euclidean quotient end with nontrivial group, a flat one is a
global Euclidean quotient with at most one exceptional point. Within each
cluster the maximal inequivalent nonflat limits form a finite rooted tree with a
unique outermost entry, and the joining regions carry quantitative neck charts.

Fix the terminal time in the smooth part and use h-dependent conjugate heat

parmedskipnoindentSlice limits, cones, and quantitative necks.

Rescaled slices h_i^{-2}g(t_i) based at x_i with h_ito0, t_i↑ T
are precompact as length spaces (two-sided volume bounds bound the number of
disjoint balls); the bad-set cover has bounded cardinality so a diagonal limit
identifies a finite exceptional set S. Restricted maps are locally
bi-Lipschitz, extend by continuity, and preserve the injectivity radius bound, so
the limit is complete; smooth convergence off S gives a smooth Ricci-flat
metric there, called regular. Ball volumes converge and are regular
Riemannian volume. The polar defect vanishes and Bishop becomes exact on the
regular part of the limit.

The exceptional set cannot separate positive-volume regular components: a
supposed separation by S forces approximating minimizers through thin
neighborhoods; the polar inequality and Fubini over the passage give
[
 crhoVol(U)le CBig(Vol(N_{Crho}(S))+int mBig),
]
first take the sequence limit and then rho↓0 to contradict. Hence
the limit is a complete orbifold, singular only where point-picking gives
nontrivial concentrated curvature.

Write d for distance from a puncture (or a fixed point at infinity). Vanishing
annular energy rules out r^2|Rm|geε in a middle annulus (energy
cε^6 on a ball), and interior stationary estimates give the same for
every fixed scaled derivative. Then (4-Delta)(d^2/2)=0 distributionally with
Delta(d^2/2)=4 and |nabla^2(d^2/2)|=4, so nabla^2(d^2/2)=g and the end is
a cone dr^2+r^2g_L, L=S^3/Gamma for finite Gamma acting freely and
nontrivially (nontrivial group; two-sided volumes bound |Gamma|). Fixed-ratio
annuli are arbitrarily close to round quotient annuli, and overlapping annuli
identify the groups.

[Quantitative neck]
If a nonflat complete Ricci-flat limit with |Rm|le r^{-2} near a marked
point has 0<mu<sigma<1 and r_-<r_+, then for small r_-/r_+ and large
enclosing annulus, after decreasing mu,sigma,

 |Rm|(x)le r^{-2}big((r/r_+)^sigma+(r_-/r)^sigmabig),
 r_-<r<r_+,

through all fixed scaled derivatives.


Proof. 
Trim the boundaries by a fixed factor (otherwise a sequence of violating annuli
with escaping ratios contradicts smooth convergence). If the estimate fails,
choose approximate maximizers of r^2|Rm|/w_mu(r), w_mu(r)=(r/r_+)^mu+(r_-/r)^mu,
with ratios Q_ito∞ at s_i; both boundaries escape after dilation by
s_i^{-1}. Dividing the rescaled curvature by A_i=s_i^2|Rm|(s_i)to0 gives a
bounded divided tensor. The second Bianchi identity and
DeltaRm=Rm*Rm+nabla^2Ric* make the divided quadratic term vanish and the
forcing vanish on fixed annuli; interior elliptic estimates yield a nonzero
limiting Cartesian tensor H, harmonic and satisfying the uncontracted Bianchi
identity on the flat punctured cover. Spherical harmonics (radial powers j,
-j-2) and mu<1 leave only H=r^{-2}C for constant C; Bianchi then reads
x_iC_{jkab}+x_jC_{kiab}+x_kC_{ijab}=0 for all x, forcing C_{·· ab}=0,
contradiction. Derivatives follow by interior elliptic estimates; slowly varying
boundary coefficients are absorbed by decreasing mu.


parmedskipnoindentRemoval of punctures.

Work near a punctured orbifold point. A constant rescaling makes g(0)=delta;
(eq:neck-decay) gives |g-delta|=O(r^mu), |Dg|=O(r^{mu-1}),
|D^2g|=O(r^{mu-2}) on the lifted ball. Harmonic coordinates solve
∂_i(A^{ij}∂_jy^a)=0 with A^{ij}=√(det g),g^{ij} and
y^a=x^a on ∂ B_R; coercivity gives a variational solution, and after
rescaling a weak gradient estimate with C^{0,alpha} forcing O(R^mu) gives
|Dy-I|_∞=O(R^mu), so y is a diffeomorphism for small R. Uniqueness
makes it equivariant; recentering and a constant linear normalization give
D^ky=O(r^{1+alpha-k}) for kge2 and the same for the inverse. The
harmonic-coordinate metric obeys G-G(0)=O(r^alpha), DG=O(r^{alpha-1}),
D^2G=O(r^{alpha-2}).

Thus Gin W^{2,p} for all p<4/(2-alpha); take p_0=2+alpha/2>2. No
distributional point defect occurs (integration by parts across ∂ B_rho
has first-derivative O(rho^3) and second-derivative O(rho^{2+alpha})
terms). The harmonic-coordinate Einstein equation holds a.e.,

 G^{ab}∂_a∂_bG_{ij}=Q_{ij}(G^{-1},DG,DG),

with Q quadratic in DG, Lemma~4.1 of DeTurck and Kazdan. For 2<p<4,
DGin L^{4p/(4-p)} and elliptic regularity improves the exponent,
p_{k+1}=2p_k/(4-p_k) with 1/p_k=tfrac12-2^k(tfrac12-1/p_0), reaching p>4
in finitely many steps; then Gin C^{1,beta}, and Schauder theory with
(eq:weak-einstein) gives smoothness. This fills the orbifold point without
integrability assumptions on Einstein deformations.

A trivial group is impossible: its smooth filling has near-Euclidean volume
ratios, which propagate (via (eq:almost-bishop), error O(h^{1/2})) to a
smaller complete nonflat limit at a point-picked center near the filled center;
letting the start ratio go to Euclidean forces at least Euclidean ratio on fixed
balls of that limit, while Bishop gives the opposite at regular centers and the
radial-Laplacian/Hessian argument forces a smooth flat cone, contradiction. The
end group of a nonflat limit is likewise nontrivial. A complete flat limit
develops to a global Euclidean quotient with at most one tip (finiteness of the
deck group from sheet counting and noncollapse; the only possible singular point
is the tip).

parmedskipnoindentTerminal convergence, empty set, finite trees.

For a compact set K off the finite exceptional set, nested compactness and a
fixed radius r control the whole remaining interval [t_i,T) by spatial
persistence and a finite cover, giving fixed charts, bounded derivatives, and
smooth terminal convergence (local injectivity gives one sheet). If the
exceptional set is empty, finitely many such charts bound curvature globally and
the DeTurck equation continues the flow past T (principal part
g^{ij}∂_i∂_jg after adding L_Wg); so only a genuine
concentration point needs work.

Call two nonflat small-scale limits equivalent if their scales have bounded
ratio and centers are a bounded distance apart in either scale. Each nonflat
limit contains a regular energy ball (orbifold filling, end decay, and the
stationary curvature bound give a ball of energy at least e_1>0). Such balls
for inequivalent entries are disjoint in the slices; the total energy bounds the
number by C/e_1. Dilating to curvature maximum and ordering give an ancestor
relation with a unique maximal entry; recursively this is the finite rooted tree
of Theorem~thm:degeneration. Intermediate limits between consecutive
entries are flat, so a child is a cone tip; on the flat annuli lge cr and
(eq:ricci-improvement) gives r^2|Ric|_{k,r}le Cr, and
Lemma~geom:neck supplies the necks with positive decay and the matching
quotient groups (end variables tau_p=log(A/r), tau_c=log(r/(Ae^{-2L})),
tau_p+tau_c=2L realize exactly the static half-neck weights).

parmedskipnoindentThe outermost scale.

[A continuous outermost scale]
Fix a small c_*>0 and a bounded continuous psige0 with psi=0 on
[0,c_*], psi>0 on [2c_*,1], and set

 a(t)=sup_{yinU},t),psibig(l(y,t)^2|Rm|(y,t)big).

After shrinking U the supremum is attained in the interior at every
late time; a is positive, continuous, and a(t)to0. For every sequence of
late times, a(t) and any maximizer represent the outermost nonflat entry, and
every other nonflat entry has scale at most C a.


Proof. 
Normalized regular energy balls detect curvature: at max|Rm|=1 the derivative
bound keeps |Rm|ge1/2 on a fixed ball, and removing tip neighborhoods leaves a
point at positive distance from every tip with curvature radius bounded above
and below, so l^2|Rm|ge2c_* and l is comparable to the vertex scale. Near
the punctures l^2|Rm|<c_*/2 eventually (definite-energy balls would accumulate
against finite energy). Hence the integrand in (eq:root-def) is continuous
on a fixed compact interior set with an interior maximum, positive and continuous

parmedskipnoindentContinuation.

Absence of a concentration point gives a smooth limiting metric on M at T
(finitely many curvature-bounded charts, the DeTurck principal part, and the
metric/connection evolution), and the DeTurck equation started there continues the
flow on M past T with matching spatial derivatives. Retaining the given flow
for t<T and this solution for tge T produces a smooth Ricci flow
widetilde g on Mtimes[0,T+ε) with widetilde g=g for t<T,
proving Theorem~thm:extension. The argument uses arbitrary smooth initial
data in real dimension four and continues on the original manifold, not an
orbifold.
