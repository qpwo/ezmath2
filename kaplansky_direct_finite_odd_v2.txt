# A Counterexample to Kaplansky's Direct-Finiteness Conjecture in Odd Characteristic


maketitle

Abstract: 
Kaplansky conjectured in 1972 that the group algebra of any group
over any field would be ``directly finite'': if ab=1 then ba=1.
The conjecture is true when the field has characteristic zero, but it
fails in positive characteristic once the group is allowed to be
infinite. This paper constructs a counterexample in odd characteristic.
We exhibit one explicit odd prime~p, a field~K of order~p^4,
a finitely generated group~G containing torsion, and two finite
sums a,bin K[G] with ab=1 and bane 1. The same two sums then
produce an injective cellular automaton on~K^G that is not surjective,
so the group~G is not surjunctive.


# Introduction


## The puzzle: one-sided versus two-sided inversion

Let K be a field and G a group. The group algebra K[G]
consists of all formal finite sums
[
 c_1 g_1 + c_2 g_2 + ... + c_r g_r,
  c_iin K, g_iin G,
]
added coordinate by coordinate and multiplied by writing out both sides,
using the group law g_ig_j and extending linearly. Two sums are equal
only when their coefficients agree on every~g. So K[G] is like an
infinite-dimensional vector space over K, with G itself as a basis,
plus a multiplication rule coming from the group.

Concrete example. When G=Z and K is any field, the
group algebra K[Z]bbZ] is (isomorphic to) the ring of Laurent
polynomials K[x,x^{-1}] in one variable: send the integer~n to~x^n.
That ring is commutative, so left and right behave the same there.
Not every group algebra is commutative; for example, if G is
nonabelian, then K[G] is noncommutative.

A ring R is directly finite when the equation ab=1, for
a,bin R, always implies~ba=1. Fields are directly finite (the
only nonzero element with a multiplicative inverse is the obvious one).
The ntimes n matrix ring Mat_n(K) over a field is directly finite
because linear maps on an n-dimensional vector space have finite
dimension as input and output. In general, any finite-dimensional
algebra over a field is directly finite: any one-sided inverse between
linear maps on an n-dimensional space has rank~n, so it is
automatically a two-sided inverse. A ring in which every matrix ring
Mat_n(R), nge1, is directly finite is called stably finite.

Since a finite group G produces a finite-dimensional algebra K[G],
it is directly finite (and stably finite; the matrix rings Mat_n(K[G])
are again finite-dimensional). So the interesting question is about
infinite groups.

## Kaplansky's question

In his 1972 book on fields and rings, Kaplansky proved that when the
field has characteristic~0, the group algebra K[G] is directly finite
for every group~G; his proof used finite von Neumann algebras. He then
asked whether the same conclusion holds in characteristic~p>0, where
his von Neumann-algebra method does not apply directly.

A large body of later work found positive partial answers. Ara, O'Meara,
and Perera (2002) proved stable finiteness of K[G] for G free-by-amenable
over any division ring. Elek and Szab'o (2004) proved stable finiteness
for every sofic group: informally, a group that can be approximated by
finite permutation models. Since many familiar infinite groups are
sofic -- all residually finite groups, all amenable groups, all free
groups, and many more -- this covered a wide range of cases.
These theorems constrain any counterexample: whatever group we build
must be far from sofic.

The purpose of this paper is to give an actual counterexample, in a
single specific odd characteristic.

## The main theorem

Put m=binom{1200}{600} and let p be the least integer >1 that
divides (m!)^2+1. This p is an odd prime; see Lemma~lem:field.
We construct:
 a field K of order p^4 (an explicit quadratic tower over the
      prime field F_p);
 a finitely generated group G containing nontrivial elements of
      finite order;
 two finite sums a,bin K[G] with ab=1 and bane 1.


The construction is entirely finite: every field element, every group
generator, every coefficient is produced by a finite list of operations
from fixed finite sets. No infinite search and no word-problem oracle
is needed to define a,b; the point of the construction is to build them
from finite data rather than by deciding equalities in~G.


Let m=binom{1200}{600} and let p be the least integer h>1
dividing (m!)^2+1. Then p is an odd prime. There is a specified
field K of order p^4, a specified finitely generated group G
containing torsion, and specified sums a,bin K[G] satisfying
ab=1 but bane 1. In particular Kaplansky's direct-finiteness
conjecture fails in odd characteristic.


The word ``specified'' means: written as an explicit composition of
finite operations on finite sets, in an order that is fixed in advance.
Different coefficients may happen to coincide at the end; that is
harmless. What matters is that a and b are objective consequences
of a finite recipe, not of an arbitrary choice among infinitely many.

# The field, the graph, and the local group


The finite-data stage has three independent ingredients: a prime~p and
a specific field K of order p^4; a simple triangle-free graph~Gamma
on m vertices together with an extremely compact factorization of its
adjacency matrix; and a finite group H with a natural action that will
later host the crucial idempotent. Each ingredient is built once and for
all here, in the order needed later.

## The prime and the field


Recall m=binom{1200}{600}, a specific (very large) positive integer,
and let p be the least integer h>1 that divides (m!)^2+1.
The definition of~p is purely arithmetic. We need four properties
of~p and one field-extension construction.


With m=binom{1200}{600} and p the least h>1 dividing
(m!)^2+1, we have:
 p is an odd prime, and p>m.
 p divides (m!)^2+1, so -1 has a square root in F_p.
 If etainF_p^* is any nonsquare, then the polynomial
      X^2-eta is irreducible over F_p and its root field
      K_0=F_p[xi]/(xi^2-eta) is a quadratic extension of F_p.
 The root xi of X^2-eta is not a square in K_0,
      so X^2-xi is irreducible over K_0 and
      K=K_0[nu]/(nu^2-xi) is a further quadratic extension.
      Consequently |K|=p^4.


Proof. 
(1) The least divisor >1 of any integer n>1 is prime: if it were
composite, one of its proper divisors would also be >1 and would be
a smaller divisor of~n. So p is prime. Now, if ple m, then
p would divide m! (since mge p), so also p would divide
(m!)^2; combined with pmid (m!)^2+1 this gives pmid 1,
impossible. Hence p>m>600. Since mge600ge1 and p>mge600,
m! is even (as mge2), so (m!)^2+1 is odd, and pne2.
Therefore p is odd.

(2) The congruence (m!)^2equiv -1pmod p says that -1 is the
square of m! modulo~p, that is, -1 is a quadratic residue in
F_p.

(3) A polynomial X^2-eta over a field is irreducible exactly when
eta is not a square in that field. Nonsquares exist in F_p^*
for every odd prime~p: the map x|-> x^2 on F_p^* is
two-to-one on its image, so only half of the p-1 elements are
squares, and p-1ge2 gives at least one nonsquare. For such~eta
the polynomial X^2-eta has no roots in F_p, hence is irreducible
and F_p[X]/(X^2-eta) is a field of p^2 elements.

(4) In K_0=F_p[X]/(X^2-eta), the two roots of X^2-eta are
xi and -xi. Therefore xi·(-xi)=-xi^2=-eta is the
field norm of xi down to F_p. By (2), -1 is a square in F_p.
Since eta is chosen to be a nonsquare, the product -eta is a
product of a square and a nonsquare, hence a nonsquare in F_p.

Now suppose, toward a contradiction, that xi=y^2 for some yin K_0.
Apply the field norm N_{K_0/F_p}(u)=u· u' (where u' is the
conjugate, negating the xi-coordinate). Because N is multiplicative,
N(xi)=N(y^2)=N(y)^2, which would exhibit the nonsquare~-eta
as a square in F_p. That is impossible. So xi is not a square
in~K_0, and hence X^2-xi is irreducible over~K_0. The quotient
K=K_0[nu]/(nu^2-xi) is therefore a field of |K_0|^2=p^4 elements.


From now on, K is the specific field K=F_{p^4} supplied by
Lemma~lem:field. We descend to F_p only when we need to check
things like ``-1 is a square.'' All our formulas over K are
already fixed.

Several additional choices are needed below. Because we want the
construction to be ``specified'' in the sense of the introduction,
we spell out an explicit finite recipe for each choice.
 Representatives and order on F_p.
      Identify F_p with {0,1,...,p-1} using the remainder
      in 0,...,p-1, so that F_p has a natural total order.
      Choose eta as the least nonsquare in F_p^* under this order.
 Representatives and order on K.
      Write every element of K uniquely as
      c_0+c_1xi+c_2nu+c_3xinu with c_jinF_p. Order K by
      the lexicographic order on (c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3). This gives a
      total order on K.
 A generator zeta of K^*. Put M=p^4-1.
      Choose the least element zeta of K^* whose multiplicative
      order equals M. Such an element exists; the reasoning is
      standard but we include it for completeness below.


Why does an element of order M exist? Let b be the least common
multiple of all element orders in K^*. Conventionally ble M because
every element of K^* has order at most M. On the other hand, every
element of K^* is a root of X^b-1 (because each element's order
divides~b), so X^b-1 has at least M distinct roots. A nonzero
polynomial over a field has at most as many roots as its degree, so
Mle b. Hence b=M. Now, by the definition of least common multiple,
we can choose elements of K^* whose orders are the prime powers
occurring in the factorization of M; raising appropriates elements to
the right powers gives an element of order exactly each prime power
factor. Their product, being the product of commuting elements of
pairwise coprime orders, has order exactly M. So an element zeta
of order M exists.

In particular M=p^4-1 and all of its powers are invertible in K
(equivalently: the multiplicative group of K is cyclic of order
M=p^4-1). We will divide by various integers below; every such
integer is a factor of~M and hence nonzero in~K.

## The graph and the low-rank fit


The second finite ingredient is a simple triangle-free graph~Gamma
on many vertices whose adjacency matrix admits a factorization into
a number of rank-one pieces that is very small compared to the number
of vertices. That is what allows Section~op:compression-section
to build a ``rank paradox'' with which the odd-characteristic
counterexample becomes possible.

Set [1200]={1,2,...,1200}. Enumerate all six-hundred-element
subsets I⊆[1200] in increasing lexicographic order (comparing
subsets as increasing lists of integers) and denote them
I_1,...,I_m with m=binom{1200}{600}=binom{1200}{600}. Remember
this same~m is the one appearing in the definition of~p.

Define a simple graph Gamma on vertex set {1,...,m} by
declaring the distinct vertices ine j to be adjacent exactly when
the intersection I_icap I_j has fewer than 200 elements:
[
 isim j quadiffquad |I_icap I_j| < 200.
]
By convention a vertex is never adjacent to itself, so the graph is
simple.


The graph Gamma is simple and triangle-free. Let
[
 T={T⊆[1200]:|T|le 400},
 t=|T|=calT|=sum_{j=0}^{400}binom{1200}{j}.
]
There exist coefficients alpha_i(T) and beta_j(T) in F_p,
indexed by TinT, such that the mtimes m matrix
F given by
[
 F_{ij}=sum_{TinT},beta_j(T)
]
satisfies F_{ii}=1 for every i and F_{ij}=0 for every pair of
distinct nonadjacent vertices~i,j. Moreover t/m<1/100.

Proof. 
Triangle-free. Suppose i,j,k are pairwise adjacent. Then each
of the three pairwise intersections has at most 199 elements (since
``fewer than 200'' and integer valued). So
[
 |I_icup I_jcup I_k| ge |I_i|+|I_j|+|I_k| - |I_icap I_j| - |I_jcap I_k| - |I_kcap I_i|
 ge 3· 600 - 3· 199 = 1203,
]
by inclusion-exclusion. But the union is contained in [1200], which
has only 1200 elements. Contradiction. So Gamma has no triangles.

The fit. Pick, for each i, the indicator function
[
 alpha_i(T)=1,&T⊆ I_i,0,&otherwise.
]
To build beta_j, define the following polynomial in the formal
variables X_1,...,X_{1200} over F_p:
[
 Q_j(X)=prod_{k=200}^{599}frac{sum_{xin I_j}X_x-k}{600-k}.
]
Every denominator lies in {1,2,...,400}, hence is invertible
in F_p because~p>600>400. Expand Q_j as sum_alpha c_alpha X^alpha
with c_alphainF_p and multi-indices alphainN^{[1200]}
(so X^alpha=prod_x X_x^{alpha_x}). Define the square-free
reduction of Q_j to be
[
 Q_j^{sf}(X)=sum_alpha c_alphaprod_{x:alpha_x>0} X_x,
]
in which every positive exponent has been replaced by 1. Set
beta_j(T) to be the coefficient of prod_{xin T}X_x in
Q_j^{sf} for each T⊆[1200]. Because Q_j has
total degree 400 (a product of 400 linear forms), only the
TinT can appear. The total degree of Q_j bounds the
support of beta_j exactly to subsets of size at most 400.

Evaluation on 0-1 vectors. The square-free reduction does
not change the value of the polynomial at any 0-1 point (because
0^n=0 and 1^n=1 for every nge1). In particular, evaluate at the
indicator vector 1_{I_i}in{0,1}^{[1200]} (entry 1 at positions
xin I_i, entry 0 elsewhere). On the one hand,
[
 Q_j(1_{I_i})
 =prod_{k=200}^{599}(|I_icap I_j|-k)/(600-k).
]
On the other hand, expanding Q_j^{sf} and evaluating at
1_{I_i}, each monomial prod_{xin T}X_x contributes 1 if
T⊆ I_i and 0 otherwise. So
[
 Q_j(1_{I_i})
 =sum_{TinT},beta_j(T)=F_{ij}.
]
Equating the two evaluations gives

 F_{ij}=prod_{k=200}^{599}(|I_icap I_j|-k)/(600-k).


Fit conditions. If i=j then |I_icap I_j|=600, so every
numerator 600-k cancels the corresponding denominator, giving
F_{ii}=1. If ine j are nonadjacent then |I_icap I_j|ge200 and
le 599, so the factor with k=|I_icap I_j| is exactly zero,
forcing F_{ij}=0. For adjacent distinct pairs the product is
generally nonzero, but we do not need any particular value; only the
diagonal and the nonedge vanishing matter. This is the threshold-Kneser
polynomial specialization of cite[Proposition~13]{Haviv2018Theta}
and cite[Theorem~3.2]{GolovnevHaviv2022}.

The ratio bound. Combinatorial estimates. The binomial
coefficients binom{1200}{j} increase with~j as long as jle599
(because binom{n}{j+1}/binom{n}{j}=(n-j)/(j+1)>1 when j<(n-1)/2
and n=1200). So on the sum defining t, the largest term is at
j=400, and
[
 t le 401binom{1200}{400}.
]
For m=binom{1200}{600} we can compare factorials directly:
[
 frac{binom{1200}{400}}{binom{1200}{600}}
 =prod_{u=401}^{600}(u)/(u+200)
 le (tfrac 57)^{100}.
]
Here the inequality is a crude but adequate estimate: for
ule 500, the factor u/(u+200) is at most 500/700=5/7; for
uge501, the factor is at most 600/800=3/4<1, so it contributes
less than one. Multiplying all one hundred factors gives the displayed
bound. Then
[
 frac tmle 401(tfrac 57)^{100}.
]
The cube of 5/7 is 125/343<1/2, so (5/7)^{99}<(1/2)^{33}. Thus
 (5/7)^{100}<(1/2)^{33}  as well. Multiplying by 401<40100<2^{33}
gives
[
 frac tm<2^{33}· frac1{2^{33}}=1,
]
which is weaker than needed; more precisely,
[
 100 · frac tm < 40100(tfrac 57)^{100}
 <frac{40100}{2^{33}}<1,
]
so t/m<1/100.


Now we have our two finite combinatorial ratios. The first is
t/m<1/100: the low-rank fit uses far fewer summands than there are
vertices. The second, coming from the group H below, is
d/w>1/96: the idempotent each vertex carries has a regular rank
that is a fixed nonzero fraction of~|H|. Section~op:compression-section
will combine the two ratios into a matrix with strict inequality
wt<md between its shapes.

## The finite group H


The third finite ingredient is the local group. It is where the large
idempotent lives, and it is where the crucial ``one-sided annihilation
in each direction'' happens.

Let P=(F_p)^3 be the additive group of three-dimensional column
vectors over F_p. It has p^3 elements. Let M=p^4-1, as in the
field construction, and let
[
 A_0=(Z/MbbZ/MZ)^P
]
be the group of functions a:PtoZ/MbbZ/MZ, with
addition defined pointwise: (a+a')(x)=a(x)+a'(x) for each xin P.
This is a finite abelian group of order M^{p^3}.

The group P acts on A_0 by translation: for sin P and
ain A_0, define s· ain A_0 by
[
 (s· a)(x)=a(x-s),  xin P.
]
This is a group action: 0· a=a and (s+t)· a=s·(t· a).
Form the semidirect product
[
 H=A_0rtimes P, 
 (a,s)(a',s')=(a+s· a', s+s').
]
The identity of H is (0,0). The inverse of (a,s) is
(-s^{-1}· a, -s), i.e., (-(-s)· a,-s) since in characteristic
p subtraction is the same as~+(-s). Concretely: (a,s)(-(-s)· a, -s)=(a-s·((-s)· a), 0)=(a-s·(-s· a), 0)=(a-0,0)=(0,0).
The order of H is
[
 w:=|H|=|A_0|·|P|=M^{p^3}· p^3.
]
So H is finite. The group algebra K[H] is thus a
finite-dimensional algebra over K, of dimension w as a K-vector
space (and w^2 as a K-algebra of linear maps on K^w).

The two subgroups of H that matter now are U and V, defined
below. Both are isomorphic to P, but they sit inside H in different
ways.

Translation subgroup. Define
[
 u(s)=(0,s), U={u(s):sin P}⊆ H.
]
Because (0,s)(0,s')=(0,s+s'), the map s|-> u(s) is an
isomorphism Pto U.

Characteristic element. Let cin A_0 be the function
[
 c(0)=1, c(x)=0 for xne0.
]
The group law gives (c,0) the exact order M in H (as an element
of the abelian normal subgroup A_0).

Conjugate subgroup. Define
[
 v(s)=c,u(s),c^{-1}, V={v(s):sin P}⊆ H,
]
where c in the formula denotes (c,0)in H and c^{-1}=(-c,0)
uses the additive inverse in A_0. A short computation with the
group law in H gives the explicit form

 v(s)=(c-s· c, s), sin P.

Indeed (c,0)(0,s)=(c,s) and (c,s)(-c,0)=(c+s·(-c),,s)=(c-s· c,s).


The subgroups U and V satisfy Ucap V={1}, and Ucong Vcong P.

Proof. 
Suppose (0,0)=u(s)=v(s') for some s,s'in P. Comparing second
coordinates gives s=s'. Comparing first coordinates gives
0=c-s· c, i.e., c=s· c. The left side evaluates at 0 to
c(0)=1; the right side evaluates at 0 to (s· c)(0)=c(-s).
But -sne 0 when sne0, and c vanishes away from the origin, so
c(-s)=0. Thus s=0, and the only common element is the identity.
The isomorphism statements follow from the displayed definitions:
s|-> u(s) is a bijection Pto U preserving the group law, and
s|-> v(s) is a conjugate of~s|-> u(s) inside the group~H,
hence also isomorphic to P.


Why does this matter? In Section~sec:modular we shall build an
idempotent~e with
[
 (u(s)-1)^ell e = 0, e,(v(s)-1)^ell=0
 quadfor every sin P,
]
where ell=(p-1)/2. The first annihilation uses u(s)in U on the
left; the second uses v(s)in V on the right. Because Ucap V={1},
the two sides do not have to agree, and no symmetry forces either one
onto the other. This asymmetry is what makes the later triangular
compression work.

That completes the finite data. In later sections, we always write
m=binom{1200}{600}, t=|T|,calT|, w=|H|, and
d=dim_KeK[H] for the regular rank of the idempotent e we
are about to build in Section~sec:modular.

# The large idempotent with ordered annihilations


This section is the technical heart of the paper. In the previous
section we built three pieces of finite data: a prime p, a field
K of order~p^4, and a finite group H=A_0rtimes P with two
different subgroups U,Vcong P meeting trivially. Now we use all that
data to build one particular idempotent ein K[H] with two properties:
 Large regular rank. The dimension d=dim_K(eK[H])
      of the image of left multiplication by e satisfies
      d/w>1/96, where w=|H|.
 Opposite annihilations. Writing ell=(p-1)/2, we have
      (u(s)-1)^ell e=0 and e,(v(s)-1)^ell=0 for every sin P.


The construction proceeds in three stages.
 Ssubsec:B-def: identify a subspace B⊆ K^P on
      which both u(s)-shifts and v(s)-shifts have the same
      annihilation behavior. This requires a piece of translation
      calculus (the operators X_j) and care with degrees.
 Ssubsec:admissibility: use the polynomial identity test of
      Schwartz and Zippel to produce many ``admissible'' characters
      lambdainA_0hat, i.e., characters lambda for which
      a certain Gram matrix~N_lambda is nonsingular.
 Ssubsec:idempotent: assemble these ingredients into an
      idempotent e spread over many matrix-block summands of K[H],
      compute its regular rank exactly, and verify the annihilations.


A last subsection (Ssubsec:edge-zero) turns the above
annihilations into the ``edge products vanish'' identity used later.

Throughout, we do all linear algebra over~K first, then lift to
K[H] using the Fourier idempotents of the abelian normal subgroup
A_0⊆ H.

## The annihilated subspace B⊆ K^P


Let ell=(p-1)/2 and D=3(p-1). Work in the K-vector space K^P
of functions Pto K, with standard basis mathbf e_xbfe_x (a 1 at
x and 0 elsewhere). We want a linear subspace on which left shifts
and right shifts both annihilate ell-fold.

Translation calculus. For sin P let L_s:K^Pto K^P be the
shift operator L_smathbf e_x=bfe_x=mathbf e_bfe_{x+s}. Define three operators
[
 X_j=L_{ε_j}-1_{K^P},  j=1,2,3,
]
where ε_j are the standard generators of P=F_p^3.

The L_s commute because the group P is abelian. So the X_j commute
as well. A stronger fact: because we are in characteristic~p, the
binomial coefficients binom pk are divisible by p for 1le kle p-1;
so (1+X_j)^p=1+X_j^p and
[
 X_j^p=(L_{ε_j}-1)^p = L_{pε_j}-1 = L_0-1 = 0.
]
Thus each X_j is nilpotent of index at most p. These are the
``differential operators of order 1'' associated to the three
generators of P.

[Monomial basis]
The p^3 vectors
[
 X_1^{n_1}X_2^{n_2}X_3^{n_3}mathbf e_0,bfe_0,  0le n_1,n_2,n_3< p,
]
form a K-basis of K^P.

Proof. 
The standard basis {mathbf e_x:xbfe_x:xin P} already has cardinality
p^3=dim_KK^P. So it suffices to show every mathbf e_sbfe_s is in the
K-span of the displayed vectors.

Fix s=(s_1,s_2,s_3)in P. Since 1+X_j=L_{ε_j}, we have
(1+X_j)^{s_j}=L_{s_jε_j} and
[
 mathbf e_s=L_sbfe_s=L_smathbf e_0bfe_0
 =prod_{j=1}^3 L_{s_jε_j}mathbf e_0bfe_0
 =prod_{j=1}^3 (1+X_j)^{s_j}mathbf e_0bfe_0

## Choosing a compatible projection


Our ultimate idempotent e will be a block-diagonal sum, one block per
character of~A_0. Each block is a projection matrix onto the image
of~B. The two opposite annihilations we want follow automatically
from Lemma~lem:B-annihilation once e is a projection onto
im B. But e must live inside K[H], and there the two shifts
u(s) and v(s) act through matrix representations of different
shapes. This subsection shows that for most characters the
corresponding block projection exists and is nonsingular.

Characters of A_0.: 
A_0=(Z/MbbZ/MZ)^P is finite abelian of order
|A_0|=M^{p^3}. A character is a homomorphism
lambda:A_0to K^*. It is uniquely determined on the coordinate
generators c_x (the function with value~1 at xin P and 0
elsewhere): lambda(c_x)=zeta^{a_x} for some
a_xin{0,...,M-1}. Because zeta has order M and is an
element of~K, every character has values in~K.

The shift group P⊆ H acts on characters by
(slambda)(a)=lambda((-s)· a). Conjugating the Fourier
idempotent p_chi^{A_0} of a character by u(s) gives
[
 u(s),p_chi^{A_0},u(s)^{-1}=p_{schi}^{A_0}.
]

The diagonal matrix and the Gram form.: 
For lambdainA_0hat abbreviate chi_x=xlambda (the
translate of lambda by x) and define the diagonal
p^3times p^3 matrix

 D_lambda=diag!(chi_x(c):xin P).

Every entry lies in K^* because chi_x is a character and c is
a group element. So D_lambda^{-1} exists and is diagonal.

The matrix

 N_lambda=B^{T},D_lambda^{-1},B

is a symmetric htimes h matrix over~K.

Call lambda admissible if
 its P-stabilizer is trivial, i.e., the orbit Plambda has
      size exactly p^3; and
 N_lambda is invertible.

Both conditions are checkable in finite time, and the check is
deterministic: enumerate the p^3 translates and compute one
htimes h determinant.

Most characters are admissible.: 
The two modes of failure each account for less than half of the
characters. Their union is then smaller than half, so more than half
succeed. This count is the key estimate of the whole construction.


More than half of the |A_0| characters are admissible.

Proof. 
Stabilizers. Fix a nonzero sin P. Translation by s
partitions P (of size p^3) into p^2 orbits of size~p. A
character lambda is fixed by s if and only if lambda(c_x) is
constant on every such orbit, so exactly M^{p^2} characters are
fixed. The union over the p^3-1 nonzero s therefore has size at
most (p^3-1)M^{p^2}, i.e. a fraction at most
[
 (p^3-1),M^{-p^2(p-1)}
 le (p^3-1)/M < 1/4 .
]
The last inequality uses M=p^4-1>4p^3, which holds for pge5;
we have p>600.

Singular N_lambda. The entries of D_lambda^{-1} range
independently over K^* as lambda ranges over all characters of
A_0, because chi_x(c) for distinct x are independent characters.
Introduce independent variables tau_x and Cauchy--Binet gives

 Q(tau):=det!(B^{T}{xin P}B)
 =sum_{substack{J⊆ P|J|=h}}det(B_J)^2prod_{xin J}tau_x ,

where B_J is the htimes h submatrix of B consisting of the
rows indexed by~J, taken in the fixed increasing order.

The right side is a sum of distinct monomials (different J give
different exponent vectors), so it is a nonzero polynomial. Its total
degree is exactly~h. Because B has full column rank by
Lemma~lem:monomial-basis, some maximal minor B_J is nonzero.
The polynomial Q therefore has a nonzero leading monomial and does
not cancel.

Now apply Schwartz's grid bound~[cite] (see also
Zippel~[cite]): a nonzero polynomial of total degree at
most~a vanishes on at most an a/M fraction of a product grid whose
coordinates each range over M values. Here Q has degree h and
the grid is (K^*)^{p^3} of side M. Thus Q vanishes on at most
the fraction h/M of characters. Since hle p^3 (it counts a
subset of K^P) and M=p^4-1, this is at most
[
 (h)/(M)le(p^3)/(p^4-1)<frac14 ,
]
because p^4-1>4p^3 for pge5.

Combining: fewer than 1/4+1/4=1/2 of characters fail either
condition. So more than half admit a trivial stabilizer and an
invertible Gram form simultaneously.


## Realizing the projection in K[H]


Each admissible character lambda gives a projection T_lambda on
the p^3-dimensional block attached to~lambda. The task is to
write that projection as an element of K[H] whose regular rank we
can compute. The Fourier idempotents do this job: for each character
lambda, the products p_{xlambda}u(s) (for x,sin P) form a
K-basis of one matrix block of K[H]. Then we sum over lambda.

One character, one block.:

Mat_{p^3}(K), via u(s)|-> L_s and c|-> D_lambda.

Summary so far.: 
We have a minimal exceptional spherical map: connected, on the sphere,
with exactly one face of nonidentity value, and minimal among all such.
We now perform local reductions. Each reduction either produces a
strictly smaller exceptional map (contradiction to minimality) or
forces a local restriction on the configuration. After the reductions,
the restrictions combine with Euler's formula to give a final
contradiction.

Reduction 1: every edge is nonneutral.: 
Suppose first that a nonloop edge is neutral. Contract it: edges with
value 1 and their darts disappear from the boundary-word description,
and the graph stays connected. Face evaluations are unchanged, since
the two neutral darts contributed nothing. Minimality fails.

If a neutral edge is a loop, keep the side containing the exceptional
face; the retained side is connected, all its faces retain their
evaluations, and the discarded loop disappears. Minimality fails.

Hence every edge carries a nontrivial label.

Reduction 2: every edge is a nonbridge.: 
Suppose an edge is a bridge with value s. Deleting it splits the map
into two connected components, and the unique boundary walk of each
adjacent face splits into two walks, one per component. After suitable
choice of starting point and orientation, the old walk is
W_1,s,W_2,s^{-1}, where W_1,W_2 are the two split walks.

If some W_a has nonidentity value, keep the component containing it,
with the same face type as before. This component is connected and has
exactly one exceptional face (that of W_a). Minimality fails.

If both W_a are trivial, the original face was either ordinary or
exceptional. If it was ordinary, the exceptional face is on one of the
two components; keep that one. If the original face was the unique
exceptional one, both W_a are trivial (this was assumed), so sWs^{-1}
was the exceptional word; keep the component whose walk is the whole
sWs^{-1} from the other side. In every case, a smaller exceptional map
results.

So every edge is a nonbridge.

Reduction 3: the two faces across an edge have distinct types.: 
Suppose an edge separates two faces Phi,Phi' of the same type iin
V(Delta). Delete the edge and merge the two faces into one of type
i. If the boundary walk of Phi near the edge reads sD and that
of Phi' reads s^{-1}E (with the shared edge contributing s on
one side and s^{-1} on the other, and D,E the remaining letters),
the merged walk reads DE. Direct computation gives

 DE=s^{-1}(sD)s,(s^{-1}E).

If sD evaluates to 1, then s^{-1}(sD)s also evaluates to 1; if
sD evaluates to gne1, then its conjugate s^{-1}gs also has value
ne1. Conjugation preserves identity-versus-nonidentity. Hence:
 ordinary + ordinary to ordinary;
 ordinary + exceptional (in either order) to exceptional.


We are assuming the map has exactly one exceptional face, so
never both-exceptional. In every case, the merge produces a smaller
map with at most one exceptional face, and thus a smaller exceptional
map. Minimality fails.

Therefore the two face types across an edge labelled gamma are
exactly the two endpoints of~gamma.

Reduction 4: at most one loop, bounding the exceptional monogon.: 
Suppose there is a loop. Its two sides are faces (possibly with other
stuff in between, but the loop has just its two immediate face-sides).
Consider the side not containing the exceptional face. Retain
the loop and everything on that side; erase everything on the side
that contains the exceptional face. Retained faces are all ordinary
(they were ordinary before). The erased side becomes a single monogon
whose walk is the loop alone, and its value is the loop's value,
nonidentity by Reduction 1. So this monogon is the unique exceptional
face of the new, smaller map.

Minimality fails, unless the erased side consisted only of that
isolated monogon originally. Hence every loop bounds the exceptional
monogon in the original map. Since there is only one exceptional face
(it bounds only one walk), there is at most one loop, and if it
exists it bounds exactly the exceptional monogon.

Reduced configuration.: 
Combining the four reductions, a minimal exceptional map satisfies:
 every edge is nonneutral;
 every edge is a nonbridge;
 the two faces across an edge labelled gamma have the two
      distinct endpoint types of gamma;
 at most one loop exists, and it bounds the exceptional monogon.


We continue in the next reduction. The one remaining configuration to
rule out is that a face has two consecutive nonloop darts with the
same label. Doing so will give the final contradiction with Euler's
formula.

## Euler deficit and the final contradiction


Recall the reduced configuration: no neutral edges, no bridges,
distinct endpoint types across every edge, at most one loop (bounding
the exceptional monogon), and no face has two consecutive nonloop
darts with the same label.

Vertices away from the loop.: 
Let u be a vertex that is not the base of any loop. Read the face
types around u cyclically; call the resulting sequence
i_1,i_2,...,i_r, where i_a is the type of the a-th face,
and r=deg u. Successive i_a differ: the edge between them has
endpoints i_{a} and i_{a+1}, by Reduction 3. No immediate
reversal by Reduction 5 (which says the labels, hence the ordered
endpoints, are distinct).

So we get a closed walk i_1,i_2,...,i_r,i_1 in Delta with
successive edges distinct and length rge1. Since Delta is
simple and triangle-free, no such closed walk has r<4: length 1
or 2 would be a loop or a doubling of an edge in a simple graph;
length 3 would be a triangle. Thus deg uge4.

Faces away from the loop.: 
Let F be an ordinary face not incident to the loop. Its boundary is
nonempty: any connected graph with at least one edge has nonempty
dart cycles. Successive labels differ by Reduction 5, so the closed
walk in Delta has length at least 4. Thus |∂ F|ge4.

Euler deficit.: 
For a connected spherical drawing,

 sum_{u}(4-deg u)+sum_{F}(4-|∂ F|)=8,

which follows from Euler's formula and sum_udeg u=sum_F|∂
F|=2e (every edge contributes two to each side).

No loop present. Every vertex satisfies deg uge4, so
contributes le0. Every ordinary face has |∂ F|ge4, so
contributes le0. Only the exceptional face can contribute
positively: its walk is nonempty, so |∂ F|ge1 and the
contribution is at most 3. Total le3<8, contradicting
(eq:gluing-deficit).

Loop present. The base vertex u_0 of the loop has deg u_0
ge2 (the loop contributes two), so contributes at most 2. The
exceptional monogon has walk of length 1, so contributes 3. The
ordinary face on the other side of the loop sees the loop's dart with
value ne1 (by Reduction 1), so its walk is nonempty and cannot be
a monogon (a monogon would have just the loop's dart, giving value
ne1, contradicting ordinari-ness); hence its length is at least
2, contributing at most 2. All other vertices and faces
contribute at most zero. Total le 2+3+2=7<8, contradicting
(eq:gluing-deficit).

Hence no minimal exceptional spherical map exists. So Lto D_i^0
is injective, i.e., D_i^0to Q is injective, for every vertex~i.

## Extending to the full vertex group


We now extend D_i^0to Q to D_ito Q. The argument is a
permutation-action construction.

Fix i. Let Ω=Ltimesℕ, with L acting by left
multiplication on the first coordinate. The action of D_i^0 on
Ω, transported through D_i^0↪ L, is free on
countably infinitely many orbits. The same is true of the left
D_i^0-action on D_itimesℕ.

Pair the D_i^0-orbits bijectively between the two sets and choose
one point in each pair. Extending D_i^0-equivariantly gives a
D_i^0-equivariant bijection phi_i:D_itimesℕtoΩ.
Transport the D_i left-multiplication along phi_i: this gives a
D_i-action on Ω whose restriction to D_i^0 is exactly the
original L-action.

Do this independently for each i. On a shared edge gamma={i,j},
both copies of the generator x_gammain L act on Ω by the
same element, so the resulting actions glue to a Q-action on
Ω. The action restricted to each D_i is faithful, since
the original left action on D_itimesℕ is faithful and
phi_i is a bijection.

Therefore D_ito Q is injective for every vertex i. This
completes the proof of Lemma~lem:triangle-free-gluing.

## Applying the criterion to H_i


We now apply the lemma to our concrete setting. Recall H=A_0rtimes
P with A_0=(Z/MbbZ/MZ)^P, P=F_p^3, U=u(P),
V=cUc^{-1}, and Ucap V={1} from Proposition~prop:UV-triv.
For each edge i<j of Gamma we identify the cyclic subgroup
generated by v(1,barbar j,j,barbar j^2)j^2) in H_i with the one generated
by u(1,barbar i,i,barbar i^2)i^2) in H_j. Here the bars denote residues in
F_p, and both elements have exact order p.


Every natural homomorphism H_ito G_0 is injective. Consequently
the induced maps K[H_i]to K[G_0] on group algebras are injective.


Proof. 
Apply Lemma~lem:triangle-free-gluing with Delta=Gamma and
D_i=H. The vertex groups H_i are finite, the cyclic edge groups
have order p, and the edge embeddings are given by the displayed
vectors. We must verify the short-word condition.

Fix a vertex i. The incident edges split into two kinds:
 j<i: the syllable from edge {i,j} in H_i lies in~U and
      is u(lambda·(1,barbar i,i,barbar i^2))i^2)) for some scalar
      lambdainF_p^*; the vector (1,barbar i,i,barbar i^2)i^2) is attached
      via the H_j side.
 j>i: the syllable from edge {i,j} in H_i lies in~V and
      is v(lambda·(1,barbar j,j,barbar j^2))j^2)) for some
      lambdainF_p^*.


Within each of U or V, distinct neighbors j correspond to the
vectors (1,barbar j,j,barbar j^2)j^2)inF_p^3. Three of these vectors with
three distinct residues barbar j_1,j_1,barbar j_2,j_2,barbar j_3j_3 are linearly
independent over F_p: the determinant
[
det
 1 & barbar j_1j_1 & barbar j_1^2j_1^2
 1 & barbar j_2j_2 & barbar j_2^2j_2^2
 1 & barbar j_3j_3 & barbar j_3^2j_3^2

= (barbar j_2-j_2-barbar j_1)(j_1)(barbar j_3-j_3-barbar j_1)(j_1)(barbar j_3-j_3-barbar j_2)j_2)
ne 0,
]
because p>m>3, so the three residues are distinct in F_p. The
same argument handles two vectors (a 2times 2 minor of the above)
and one vector (nonzero).

So a word of at most 3 syllables, all in U or all in V, with
distinct labels, evaluates nontrivially in F_p^3 and hence in H_i.

For a mixed word of length 2 or 3: exactly one of U or V
supplies a single syllable, and the other supplies one or two. After
a cyclic permutation, a putative relation would equate a nonidentity
element of U to a product of elements of~V. But Ucap V={1}
and the U-element is nonidentity, contradiction. For lengths 2
and 3, cyclically distinct labels are just distinct labels, so
the check is exhaustive.

All hypotheses of Lemma~lem:triangle-free-gluing hold, giving
the injection H_ito G_0. The induced map K[H_i]to K[G_0] is
injective because distinct elements of an embedded subgroup remain
distinct basis elements of the group algebra.


Consequently G_0 is a single group, finitely generated (by the
finite sets of generators of the finitely many H_i), containing
each H_i as a subgroup, and the e_iin K[G_0] satisfy
Corollary~op:edge-zero.
