A finite nonempty set A of points in some Euclidean space is Ramsey if, for every integer rge 2, there is an integer Dge 1 such that every map c:R^Dto{1,...,r} is constant on a congruent copy of A. Congruence preserves every pairwise distance at the original scale, and no measurability or other regularity of c is assumed.

Erdos, Graham, Montgomery, Rothschild, Spencer and Straus introduced Euclidean Ramsey theory and proved that every finite Ramsey set is spherical: it lies on a sphere of finite radius inside its affine hull. Graham conjectured the converse. Frankl and Rodl settled nondegenerate triangles and then all nondegenerate simplices. Kriz covered finite sets with a soluble transitive isometry group and all cyclic trapezoids. Cantwell treated vertex sets of regular polytopes.

Leader, Russell and Walters proposed that a finite set is Ramsey exactly when it embeds in a finite transitive Euclidean set; an isometric subset of a transitive set is subtransitive. The group-theoretic and Hales-Jewett formulations in the same paper led to the Block Sets Conjecture, and Ivan, Leader and Walters analysed possible block sizes.

Karamanlis embedded every simplex in a finite product of regular polygons, and Behague constructed soluble transitive extensions for many known Ramsey classes and further permutation configurations. Ivan, Leader and Walters developed generalized prism constructions under soluble symmetry. Moore and, independently, Mirabi proved that adjoining one point outside the affine span of a Ramsey set preserves the Ramsey property.

Palvolgyi built a seven-point circle configuration that is not Ramsey, disproving the spherical conjecture. The appendix to that version states that generic seven-point circle configurations are not Ramsey, and an author's note announces a proof that every finite transitive set is Ramsey. The algebraic classification is independent of those announcements.

Theorem. The set A is Ramsey if and only if there is a matrix PinMat_{d+1}(B) satisfying

and

The entries of p_i ⊗  1 and 1 ⊗  p_i are formed componentwise, and no separate condition is imposed on the multiplied constant row or column of P. Only its spatial block is prescribed.

The criterion is congruence-invariant in affine dimension and uses exact relations in the coordinate field; it is not a method for recovering those relations from numerical data. Applying m_F entrywise to the evaluation equations produces an ordinary sphere equation

 |a_i|^2 + ellcdot a_i + c = 0  (1le ile s) 

for some ellin F^d and cin F. The matrix condition asks that the same identities hold in B before multiplication. Every subtransitive set satisfies it, and so does every spherical set whose points give independent functionals on the quadratic polynomials. The latter covers every set of at most five points on a circle, hence every cyclic quadrilateral, and in particular the cyclic kites of Leader, Russell and Walters, so the necessity direction of the subtransitive characterization fails.

Eberhard proved that almost all sets of d+2 points on the (d-1)-sphere are not subtransitive, so subtransitivity is rare among spherical sets even though it is sufficient.

Nine points obtained from algebraically independent rational parametrizations of the circle, and a twelve-point configuration of three rotated squares containing Liouville's constant, fail the matrix condition and are therefore not Ramsey.

For sufficiency the coordinate-field matrix is converted into a finite rational combination of symmetric tensors of affine functions on R^d. Its evaluations at points of A vanish as rational tensors, while its gradient Gram matrix is I_d. For necessity the tensor is assumed to be missing; algebraic separation then yields a fixed finite coloring that avoids A in every ambient dimension.

The tensor cancellations must produce a congruent copy at scale one, with no continuity available from an arbitrary coloring. Such a tensor first determines signed rational multiplicities of finitely many affine functions whose positive and negative parts cancel separately at each evaluation value. A Gaussian smoothing, discretized and then rationalized inside a fixed convolution image, makes the negative gradient second moment small while keeping the cancellations exact.

The positive and negative lists, evaluated at the points of A, give vectors f_i,g_i of one fixed length with

 |f_i-f_j|^2 = |a_i-a_j|^2,  |g_i-g_j|^2 = q|a_i-a_j|^2, 

where q>0 is arbitrarily small and each g_i is a coordinate permutation of f_i. Distances and coordinate multisets match exactly.

A free group on letters xi_z, one per real sequence of finite support, then supplies paths: ordered products of s-tuples, each of whose factors records either an ordered scaled copy of A or a common element in every position. Equal path endpoints with arbitrarily small weight ratios force the Ramsey property. Because the number t of variable positions in a Hales-Jewett line is unknown in advance, one endpoint is prepared at weights 1,1/2,...,1/n after fixing the word length n, before the words are colored; an ultrafilter construction realizes the products by finite strings that agree in every common factor, and the t variable positions contribute squared-distance factor t(1/t)=1. Compactness then supplies a finite ambient dimension for each number of colors.

The paths supplied by the tensor step must finally be made to share an endpoint; permutation averaging places their discrepancies in a subgroup corrected by appending paths of unit-scale copies, and further averaging makes the correction weight small against the larger original weight.

Nine algebraically independent points on a rational parametrisation of the circle give a spherical set that is not Ramsey. Palvolgyi's generic seven-point claim already implies this, by passing to a seven-point subset. The criterion reaches it directly by a determinant calculation.

Proposition (nine circle points). Let t_1,...,t_9 be algebraically independent real numbers over Q and put

 a_i = ((1-t_i^2)/(1+t_i^2), (2t_i)/(1+t_i^2))  (1le ile 9). 

The nine points a_i form a spherical set that is not Ramsey.

Proof. The parametrisation lies on the unit circle and is injective, because t_i=(a_i)_2/(1+(a_i)_1); the points are distinct, affinely span R^2, and have coordinate field F=Q(t_1,...,t_9). Algebraic independence identifies F with a rational function field, so B=F ⊗ _Q F is the localization of Q[T_1,...,T_9,U_1,...,U_9] inverting all nonzero polynomials in the T variables alone and all nonzero polynomials in the U variables alone. In particular B is a domain and embeds in the fraction field Q(T_1,...,T_9,U_1,...,U_9).

Put p(t)=(1,(1-t^2)/(1+t^2),(2t)/(1+t^2))^{mathsf T}sfT. Over the fraction field the nine tensor evaluation equations have coefficient matrix D with rows (p_alpha(T_i)p_beta(U_i))_{0lealpha,betale 2}. The determinant is nonzero: specialize the nine pairs (T_i,U_i) to the grid {0,1,-1}^2, ordered as 0,1,-1 in both coordinates. All denominators remain nonzero, and the resulting matrix is the ordinary Kronecker product C ⊗  C with

 C=1&1&01&0&11&0&-1,  det C=2, 

so its determinant is 2^6=64. This evaluates the specific determinant expression and does not assert a specialization of the whole localized ring B.

The only solution of Dvec(P)=0 over the fraction field is therefore P=0, and injectivity of the embedding of B gives the same conclusion over B. Such a matrix has no multiplied spatial block I_2, so the criterion excludes the Ramsey property.

A second negative example uses one transcendental parameter and a derivation inside the coordinate field. Its weighted cancellation parallels the derivation obstruction in Palvolgyi's smaller circular example.

Proposition (three concentric squares). Set

 t=sum_{m=1}^∞ 10^{-m!},  u=(1-t^2)/(1+t^2),  v=(2t)/(1+t^2), 

and Q_0={(pm 1,0),(0,pm 1)}. Define the rotations

 R_+=u&-vv&u,  R_-=u&v-v&u,  X = R_+ Q_0 cup R_- Q_0 cup Q_0. 

The twelve points of X lie on the unit circle and are not Ramsey.

Proof. First 0.11<t<0.12 and t is transcendental. The rational partial sums t_m=p_m/q_m with q_m=10^{m!} satisfy 0<t-t_m<2q_m^{-(m+1)} for mge 2. If t had an irreducible polynomial PinZ[T] of degree d, then P(t_m)ne 0 and |P(t_m)|ge q_m^{-d}, while a bound Cge 1 for |P'| on [0,1] would give |P(t_m)|le C|t-t_m|<2C q_m^{-(m+1)}, a contradiction for large m.

The rotations R_+ and R_- act through angles theta=2arctan t and -theta with 0<theta<pi/4. Their pairwise differences and their differences from zero are nonzero and below pi/2 in absolute value, so the three squares are disjoint. All twelve points lie on the unit circle, and Q_0 already affinely spans the plane.

The coordinate field of X is F=Q(t), because its coordinates include u and v and t=v/(1+u). The derivation ∂=(1+t^2)/(2)(d)/(dt) on F has ∂ u=-v and ∂ v=u. Write R_0=I_2, label the points x_{ε,q}=R_ε q with εin{+,-,0} and qin Q_0, and assign weights beta_{+,q}=beta_{-,q}=1 and beta_{0,q}=-2. Put p_i=(1,x_i)^{mathsf T}sfT for the twelve labels. Then

 sum_i beta_i p_i p_i^{mathsf T}sfT=0,  sum_i beta_i (∂ p_i) p_i^{mathsf T}sfT=0,  sum_i beta_i (∂ p_i)(∂ p_i)^{mathsf T}sfT=0&00&4 I_2. 

The first identity uses sum_q beta_{. q}=0, sum_q q=0 and sum_q q q^{mathsf T}sfT=2I_2. Writing J=0&-11&0, the derivatives of the rotations are ∂ R_+=R_+ J, ∂ R_-=-R_- J and ∂ R_0=0. The spatial squared moment is 2(1+1-2)I_2=0; the mixed moment is 2J-2J=0; and the derivative moment is 2I_2+2I_2=4I_2. The constant and vector entries vanish by the symmetric weights on Q_0.

Define the Q-linear map Phi:F ⊗ _Q Fto F by Phi(a ⊗  b)=(∂ a)(∂ b). Expanding each entry of PinMat_3(B) into pure tensors a ⊗  b, the product rule expresses Phi((p_i ⊗  1)^{mathsf T}sfTP(1 ⊗  p_i)) as a sum of four types of term.

The types carrying ∂ a or ∂ b alone vanish under sum_i beta_i by the first two moment identities and the transpose of the second. The remaining term is a b,(∂ p_{ialpha})(∂ p_{ibeta}), whose beta_i-summed value is 0&00&4 I_2_{alphabeta}ab. Hence

 sum_i beta_i Phi((p_i ⊗  1)^{mathsf T}sfT P(1 ⊗  p_i)) = 4sum_{alpha=1}^2 m_F(P_{alphaalpha}). 

A matrix satisfying the criterion would make the left side zero and the right side 8, a contradiction. The argument uses a derivation only on F and adds no regularity condition to the colorings in the definition of the Ramsey property.
