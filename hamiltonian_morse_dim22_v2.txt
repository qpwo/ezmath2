It remains to supply the handle-cancellation proof behind Smale's theorem. Throughout, W is a simply connected closed smooth D-manifold with Dge9, given with an ordered handle decomposition. A handle of index i is a copy of D^itimes D^{D-i} attached along S^{i-1}times D^{D-i}, with belt sphere {0}times S^{D-i-1} in the new boundary; a slide changes the integral basis of the handles of one index by an elementary addition; consecutive handles of indices i and i+1 cancel when the attaching sphere of the latter meets the belt sphere of the former transversely in one point. Attaching maps of later handles are carried along by these operations, so the represented manifold is unchanged. These are the standard facts of Morse theory and of the h-cobordism theorem (Milnor, Morse Theory, 1963; Milnor, Lectures on the h-Cobordism Theorem, 1965).

First remove handles of index one and coindex one. Connectedness cancels all but one 0-handle along a tree of 1-handles, and the dual operation leaves one D-handle. To remove the remaining 1-handles, insert cancelling 2/3 pairs as follows. Let P be the union of the 0- and 1-handles; it retracts onto a graph whose free generators correspond to the 1-handles, and pi_1(∂ P)topi_1(P) is an isomorphism because the handles of P relative to its boundary have indices D-1 and D. Since pi_1(W)=0 and handles of index at least three do not change the fundamental group, the attaching words of the 2-handles normally generate this free group. Fix a generator and insert a cancelling 2/3 pair whose 2-handle attaches along a trivial circle in a small ball of ∂ P. Express the chosen generator as a product of conjugates of the old attaching words and the inverse words; sliding the new 2-handle over the old ones realises this product, a slide being a band sum whose band path gives the conjugation and whose orientation gives the choice of word or inverse. General position lets the bands avoid the other attaching circles. The new attaching circle is now homotopic to a standard circle running once over the chosen 1-handle and avoiding the others, and a generic homotopy of circles in the (D-1)-manifold is an isotopy avoiding the other attaching circles, since the expected dimensions of self-intersections, or of intersections with another attaching circle in a one-parameter family, are at most 3-(D-1)<0. Transporting the framing along this isotopy, the new 2-handle cancels the chosen 1-handle. Repetition removes all 1-handles; the reversed decomposition removes the (D-1)-handles, its inserted pairs having original indices D-3,D-2 that introduce no 1-handles. The remainder has handles only in indices 0, D, and 2,...,D-2.

That homotopy may be taken to be an isotopy of the attaching sphere. Smooth it relative to the initial sphere and its fixed part, and apply parametric general position: for a one-parameter family of maps of a 2-sphere into the (D-1)-manifold L, the expected dimension of self-intersections, or of intersections with another attaching 2-sphere, is at most 2+2+1-(D-1)<0. Since D-1ge8, the relative version leaves room to spare and each map in the family can be made an immersion. At the endpoint the altered disk and collar form a compact subset of the open set V, so a small approximation keeps the altered disk and collar off the belt spheres, while keeping small neighbourhoods of the other transverse intersections fixed preserves those intersections. Isotopy extension transports the framing. Repetition leaves exactly one intersection with the chosen belt sphere.

Finally the chosen i-handle is ordered last among the i-handles and the chosen (i+1)-handle first among the (i+1)-handles; the two are consecutive and cancel, and intersections with other handles do not obstruct the operation because the attaching maps are carried along by the cancellation. No handles of index 1 or D-1 are introduced. Each cancellation lowers the total count by two, and while the count exceeds lambda(W) the excess identity supplies another cancellable pair. The process ends with exactly lambda(W) handles; equality in the excess identity forces rank B_i=t_i(W) for every i, and the chain-rank identity then gives rank C_i=r_i(W)+t_i(W)+t_{i-1}(W). The Morse function of this decomposition has exactly these index counts, which is the existence statement of Smale's theorem and completes the proof of SM(W)=lambda(W).
