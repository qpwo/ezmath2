A subset X of {0,1,2,...} is additively indecomposable if the equation X = A + B with A, B subsets of {0,1,2,...} each having at least two elements is impossible. The set X is asymptotically additively indecomposable if no set that agrees with it outside a finite set is additively decomposable. Ostmann conjectured in 1956 that the positive primes P are asymptotically additively indecomposable.

For A, B subsets of {0,1,2,...}, put A+B = {a+b : a in A, b in B}. The claim is: for all A, B with |A|,|B| at least 2, the symmetric difference of A+B with P is infinite. Elsholtz and Harper record this eventual-equality form of the problem and the terminology attached to it.

Laffer and Mann observed that if A, B have at least two elements each and A+B agrees with P outside a finite set, then both A and B are infinite. The argument applies the large sieve to two shifts of the finite summand and then counts primes in the surviving set; it reduces the conjecture to a two-sided infinite case.

./refs/laffer-mann-1964-decomposition-of-sets-of-group-elements.pdf

Earlier progress on the two-sided case went through sieve methods. Hornfeck's 1954 paper and 1955 correction gave the first restrictions. Pomerance, Sarkozy and Stewart sharpened them with sieve estimates. Hofmann and Wolke removed the polylogarithmic gap to the square-root scale, and Elsholtz combined the large and larger sieves, obtaining bounds of order x^{1/2} up to polylogarithmic factors and excluding decompositions into three nontrivial summands.

./refs/pomerance-sarkozy-stewart-1988-on-divisors-of-sums-of-integers-iii.pdf
./refs/elsholtz-2001-the-inverse-goldbach-problem.pdf
./refs/elsholtz-2006-additive-decomposability-of-multiplicatively-defined-sets.pdf

Elsholtz and Harper refined the binary counting bounds to
  sqrt(x)/(log x log log x) << A(x), B(x) << sqrt(x) log log x
under a hypothetical eventual decomposition, where A(x) and B(x) denote the counting functions of A and B up to x.

./refs/elsholtz-harper-2015-additive-decompositions-of-restricted-prime-factors.pdf

Green and Harper raised inverse questions for the large sieve and proved that a suitable quadratic inverse-sieve conjecture would imply Ostmann's conjecture.

./refs/green-harper-2014-inverse-questions-for-the-large-sieve.pdf

Hanson verified a partial form of the inverse-sieve prediction under half-residue restrictions: for C in {1,...,N} with |C mod p| at most p/2 + O(1) at every prime p and |C| at least a fixed large multiple of sqrt(N), the set C has substantial additive correlation with the squares and intersects a quadratic image in logarithmically many elements.

./refs/hanson-2020-additive-correlation-and-the-inverse-problem-for-the-large-sieve.pdf

Croot, Mao, and Yip sharpened the inverse-theory side by treating local restrictions to short arithmetic progressions, together with extensions to unions of progressions and other additive structures.

./refs/croot-mao-yip-2025-inverse-theorem-sets-rich-additive-structure.pdf

Croot, Mao, Pohoata, and Yip applied a weighted entropy argument to derive a further necessary condition for a binary decomposition of the primes. For some c > 0 and every sufficiently large N, each summand contains at least exp(c sqrt(log N)/(log log N)^{3/2}) elements of {1,...,N} lying in an integral quadratic image m +- Z^2, and the product of the two intersection sizes is at least exp(c sqrt(log N)/log log N); the quadratics may depend on N.

./refs/croot-mao-pohoata-yip-2026-sharp-inverse-theorem-quadratic-large-sieve.pdf

The quadratic-image intersections that these arguments force are far weaker than the near-containment hypothesised by the inverse-sieve route, so the classification programme has not reached a contradiction with eventual equality.

Write D = -B and let N be a threshold beyond which (A+B) and P agree. Removing the finitely many elements of A and B below p + N and reducing modulo a prime p gives two disjoint subsets of F_p = Z/pZ: the residues attained by the tail of A, and the residues attained by the tail of D. Both are nonempty, because A and B are infinite. Choose a partition of F_p extending this disjointness, so that the tail of A lies in a set S_p and the tail of D lies in the complement of S_p.

A quantitative form of the residue restriction is Gallagher's larger-sieve collision stability. For a finite nonempty set E write U_E for the uniform probability on E, and for a probability measure mu on the integers write mu_p for its projection to Z/pZ. Suppose mu and nu are probability measures on A intersect (sqrt(X)+N*, X] and on D intersect [-X, -sqrt(X)-N*), with individual masses at most (log X)^b / sqrt(X) for a fixed b >= 1. Put Q = sqrt(X)/(log X)^{b+1}, write sigma_p = |S_p|/p, and use counting L^2 norms on probability mass functions. Then

  sum_{p <= Q} log(p) [ || mu_p - U_{S_p} ||_2^2 + || nu_p - U_{S_p^c} ||_2^2 + (sigma_p^{-1} + (1-sigma_p)^{-1} - 4)/p ]
  = O_b(log log X).

./refs/gallagher-1971-a-larger-sieve.pdf

The proof is the congruent-pair count that underlies Gallagher's larger sieve. Two independent uniform elements of the mu-tail agree modulo p with probability 1/|S_p| plus the L^2 excess of mu_p over U_{S_p}. Every pair with distinct values has at most log(X) total weight from its prime divisors, and equal pairs contribute max(mu) = O(sqrt(X)^{-1}). Summing the same count for nu and using Mertens' weighted estimate

  4 sum_{p <= Q} log(p)/p = 2 log(X) - 4(b+1) log log(X) + O(1),

together with the definition of Q, gives the stated estimate term by term. The density correction sigma_p^{-1} + (1-sigma_p)^{-1} - 4 is exactly the gap between 1/|S_p| + 1/|S_p^c| and 4/p, and is nonnegative for every 0 < sigma_p < 1.

Two further bounds on the counting functions A(x) and B(x) follow by the additive large sieve of Montgomery and Vaughan. Under a hypothetical eventual decomposition with both summands infinite,

  sqrt(x)/(log x)^3 << A(x), B(x) << sqrt(x) (log x)^2

for every sufficiently large x, where << absorbs absolute constants.

Coverage of every large prime, combined with the prime number theorem, gives A(x)B(x) >> x/log x. Fixing j distinct shifts inside one summand and applying the large sieve to the other gives A(x), B(x) <<_j x/(log x)^j for every fixed j, so both counting functions are bounded below by (log x)^{j-1} after using the product inequality. Now suppose A(x) >= 3 sqrt(x). Put U = A intersect (sqrt(x)+N*, x] and note that for sqrt(x)/2 < p <= sqrt(x) the set U avoids all |B mod p| residue classes of -B mod p. Parseval and Cauchy-Schwarz on the allowed classes give, with nu_p = |B mod p|,

  sum_{h in F_p^*} | E_{a in U} e_p(ha) |^2 >= nu_p/(p - nu_p) >= nu_p/p.

The large sieve bounds the sum over sqrt(x)/2 < p <= sqrt(x) of nu_p/p by O(x/A(x)); Cauchy-Schwarz with the prime number theorem then gives

  sum_{sqrt(x)/2 < p <= sqrt(x)} log(p)/nu_p >> (A(x)/sqrt(x))/log(x).

Two independent uniform elements of B intersect [0, Y] are congruent modulo p with probability at least 1/nu_p, so the same weighted sum is at most log(Y) + O(sqrt(x)/B(Y)). Choosing log(Y) = x^{eps/2} forces B(Y) << sqrt(x) under A(x) > x^{1/2+eps}, contradicting the polylogarithmic lower bound at every fixed j. Roles exchanged, the argument gives A(x), B(x) = x^{1/2+o(1)}; setting Y = x^2 then gives A(x), B(x) << sqrt(x) (log x)^2, and coverage returns the lower bound.

The residue restriction alone controls uniformity of the projections and says nothing about multiplicative bias. The first exclusion is of persistent quadratic bias. For an odd prime p let chi_p be the quadratic character of F_p, extended by zero at zero. Under a hypothetical eventual decomposition, with the maximum taken over any translating residue t mod p and chosen separately at every prime,

  sum_{T <= log p <= 2T} (log p/p) . max_{t in F_p} | E_{x in S_p} chi_p(x - t) | = o(T) as T -> infinity,

and the same statement holds in a smoothed version with the logarithm of p replaced by any fixed function of bounded variation on [T, 2T].

Suppose the display failed. Mertens' estimate gives sum_{T <= log p <= 2T} (log p/p) = T + O(1); since every bias is at most one in absolute value there are a fixed delta > 0 and an unbounded sequence of T such that the primes with maximal quadratic bias at least delta carry log p/p-weight at least c_delta T. Set X = exp(T^{8/5}), A_X = A intersect [X^{9/10}, X] and D_X = D intersect [-X, -X^{9/10}]. Collision stability gives that, on the primes p <= sqrt(X)/(log X)^5 outside a set of log p/p-weight O(log T), the empirical means of any unit-modulus test on A_X and D_X are within delta/8 of the uniform means on S_p and S_p^c. Because the character sum over the full field vanishes, the uniform means relate by

  E_{S_p^c} chi_p(x - t) = -(sigma_p/(1 - sigma_p)) E_{S_p} chi_p(x - t),

and the two sides therefore exhibit the same bias, with opposite signs. Subdividing [T, 2T] into dyadic intervals gives a set P of at least c_0 Z/log Z primes p in some [Z, 2Z], fixed translates t_p in F_p, orientations epsilon_p in {+1, -1}, and a fixed c_delta > 0 with

  E_{x in A_X} epsilon_p chi_p(x - t_p) >= c_delta,
  E_{x in D_X} epsilon_p chi_p(x - t_p) <= -c_delta.

An auxiliary squarefree L = prod_{l in L'} l is chosen from K distinct primes l of size roughly Z^gamma, where gamma = 10^{-7}; K is of order T^{1 - gamma} and L <= Z^{0.02}, so K = o(log Z) and X/L exceeds any fixed scale.

Writing f_l = 1_{S_l} - sigma_l periodically on Z, lambda = 1/16, W(n) = prod_{l | L} (1 + lambda f_l(n)) and H(n) = |P|^{-1} sum_{p in P} epsilon_p chi_p(n - t_p), the weight W is nonnegative, of period L, of mean one, and dominates (1 + lambda/3)^K on A_X and on D_X. Jensen's inequality on both tails, applied to the smallest even integer k at least log X/log Z + 10, gives

  I = sum_{n in Z} psi(n/X) W(n) H(n)^k >> sqrt(X) (1 + lambda/3)^K c_delta^k / (log X)^3 >= sqrt(X) exp(.016 K)

for every Schwarz function psi with compactly supported smooth Fourier transform that is bounded below by a positive constant on [0, 1]. Poisson summation gives I_0 = sum_n psi(n/X) W(n) = X psi-hat(0) + O(1) as long as X/L is larger than the support of psi-hat.

Replacing the k-th moment by the sum over distinct k-tuples of P via Mobius inversion on set partitions, as in Rota's foundation paper, loses only a relative multiple

  sum_{j = 1}^{k/2} (k^C/|P|)^j |H|^{k - 2j},

which vanishes for large X because |P| exceeds every fixed power of T and k + log log X = o(K).

./refs/rota-1964-on-the-foundations-of-combinatorial-theory-i.pdf

Expanding the product over p in P into a k-fold sum of additive characters and completing the character sums via the classical quadratic Gauss-sum identity gives

  sum_{n in Z} psi(n/X) W(n) prod_{i=1}^k chi_{p_i}(n - t_{p_i})
    = sum_{(a, m)} F(a, m) e(a t_M / M) + O(X^{-1})

with M = prod_i p_i, t_M = sum_i a_i t_{p_i}, 0 < |a| < M/2, an outer parameter a ranging over O(k^7) values, and an explicit smooth amplitude F(a, m) as in the display for G_d and C_s. The theta = t_M/M ranges over all of [0, 1]. The conclusion of the lower bound is a small-denominator approximation to theta via the small-kernel witness.

With probability at least exp(-O(K)) in the k-tuple of distinct primes p in P, there exist a squarefree s < L^4 coprime to L and divisors v, d | L such that

  |G_d^{(s, v)}| >= 2^{omega(d)/2} e^{-K},

where G_d^{(s, v)} is the normalized incomplete quadratic sum for the k-fold Fourier coefficient. The argument applies the same moment transfer separately at each prime band [Z, 2Z]. Write N_{dsv} = sqrt(R d / (s v)). On the entire grid the ratio N_{dsv}/d is at least Z^{4.9}, and the squaring map modulo d has fibers of size at most 2^{omega(d)} on any relevant unit residue. Consequently |G_d^{(s, v)}| << 2^{omega(d)/2} uniformly on the grid, so a value above 2^{omega(d)/2} e^{-K} of a small-kernel term sits far outside the exponential-mean small-s family; the witness event is exactly the assertion that the moment lower bound is realizable only by a small-kernel term.

From a witness at a fixed tuple, split the w-sum modulo d. Since g_d is constant on each progression w = x mod d and the sum of the absolute amplitudes is at most d 2^{omega(d)/2}, one obtains an unweighted incomplete quadratic sum with modulus at least Y_0 e^{-K} over an interval of length at most Y_0 = N_{dsv}/d, where the quadratic coefficient is alpha = s v d theta. The elementary Dirichlet lemma gives integers 1 <= q <= exp(O(K)) and ||q alpha||_{Z} <= exp(O(K)) Y_0^{-2}.

Clearing denominators gives an integer

  a = q s v d,   1 <= a <= L^6 e^{O(K)} <= Z^{.12 + o(1)}.

Choose b' nearest to a theta and set n = a t_M - b' M. Then |n| <= a X e^{O(K)} <= X Z^{.12 + o(1)}, and n = a t_p mod p for every p | M. There are at most Z^{.12 + o(1)} choices of a, and using the event bound exp(-O(K)) together with Mertens in the band, pigeonholing yields a fixed a <= Z^{.12 + o(1)} for which at least J^k Z^{-.13} ordered distinct tuples have a lift satisfying this congruence.

For |n| <= H_0 = X Z^{.15}, let r(n) = |{p in P : n = a t_p mod p}|. Counting ordered tuples of k primes from P and then counting lifts modulo products of k - 10 distinct primes gives

  sum_{|n| <= H_0} (r(n))_k >= J^k Z^{-.13},
  sum_{|n| <= H_0} (r(n))_{k - 10} << Z^{.15} J^{k - 10},

where (r)_j = r(r-1)...(r-j+1). The terms with r(n) < Z^{.6} contribute at most Z^6 (something) which is o(J^k Z^{-.13}). Consequently at least Z^{-.6} - Z^{-10} of the lift mass concentrates at integers n with r(n) >= Z^{.6}. Two of these for one fixed n give a common centre mod every prime of the band; averaging over the Z^{o(1)} possible n yields a fixed pair (h, m) with m <= Z^{.13}, |h| <= X Z^{.15}, and

  t_p = h/m mod p    for every p in P_0,    |P_0| >= Z^{1-o(1)}.

On A_X and D_X, for each x with Hx != 0 write m x - h = u_x t_x^2 with u_x a signed squarefree integer and t_x >= 1. The kernel u_x is a discrete parameter. On a fixed interval of length O(sqrt(mX)), if two elements x, x' share the same kernel u_x = u_{x'}, then t_x and t_x' are two roots of the unit congruence u t^2 = -h mod m, giving at most O(2^{omega(m)}) root classes; counting integers in each class bounds populations per kernel by

  |{x in A_X : u_x = u}| << sqrt(X/|u|) + X^{o(1)}

and similarly for D_X. Carrying over x in A_X and y in D_X and using only terms with |u_x|, |u_y| <= X^eta, where eta is a fixed small positive constant, leaves two sets R_1, R_2 of positive roots, each of size at least X^{1/2 - 3 eta} and each of diameter at most X^{1/2 + o(1)}.

Two kernels u_x and u_y on the two sides share no prime: if a prime divided u_x and u_y, then it would divide m(x - y). The prime is coprime to m because mx - h = u_x t_x^2 and (h,m) = 1, so it divides x - y, but x - y crosses A_X - D_X and is an eventual prime exceeding X^{9/10} > X^eta. So every such product u_x u_y is signed squarefree, and the supports of all kernels on the two sides are disjoint.

For the split primes, compute collisions. For i = 1, 2 let beta_i(p) be the probability that two independent elements of R_i agree modulo p. Two independent elements with unequal values contribute at most log(diameter) total weight from the prime divisors of their difference; equal elements contribute O(Q_2/|R_i|) = O(X^{-2 eta}). Always beta_i(p) >= 1/p, and for a prime p with (u/vp) = 1 and p <= Q_2, choosing a square root c_p of v/w mod p places the two occupied sets of R_1 and c_p R_2 in disjoint subsets of F_p, so beta_1(p) + beta_2(p) >= 4/p. Combining,

  sum_{p <= Q_2} log p (beta_1(p) + beta_2(p))
    >= (1 - 10 eta + .06 + o(1)) log X
    > (1 + o(1)) log X
    >= sum_{p <= Q_2} log p (beta_1(p) + beta_2(p)),

contradiction once 0 < eta < 1/1000. This establishes that no quadratic bias persists under an eventual decomposition.

The order-greater-than-two exclusion is a quantitative extension of the same mechanism, but its norm is Euler-transfer graphs rather than primitive root pairs. For every fixed 0 < alpha < beta < 1 and every fixed multiplicative character lambda on F_p^* with ord(lambda) > 2, extended by zero at zero,

  sum_{alpha L <= log log p <= beta L} (1/p) . max_{t in F_p} | E_{x in S_p} lambda(x - t) | = o(L)

as L -> infinity, with the maximum taken over all such characters at each prime.

The proof splits the interval into three subintervals of sizes comparable to L separated by gaps of size comparable to L, keeping only primes p whose sigma_p is within a fixed small range of 1/2 and whose maximal bias at every character exceeds no more than a slowly decaying threshold. The endpoint tests and a density-increment argument locate three intervals I_1 < I_2 < I_3 in the log log coordinate with these properties; they are used as small anchors, bulk labels, and big anchors, respectively. At the level of a single pair (x, y) in A_X x D_X, one constructs a rational function F_A on A and F_D on D of the form

  F_A(x) = prod_{h in H_1} 1_{(x - t_h) in S_h} - sigma_h,
  F_D(y) = prod_{h in H_2} 1_{(y - u_h) in S'_h} - sigma'_h

with H_1, H_2 disjoint small/big anchor primes chosen per pair; the products have expectation of order exp(-c k L) but the correlations of F_A vs F_D must be quantified.

To compare correlations, construct the Euler-transfer graph: its vertices are the internal prime labels, its edges are the small and big anchors, and its leaves are the actual bulk prime positions. Each edge carries a character xi_q evaluated at the lower endpoint, and after clearing denominators and reconstructing pivots by the recursion

  p_j = (v_j H_j^- - w_j H_j^+) / (s_j u_j),

the associated phase quotient is a product of one-sided character factors. When the graph contains a cycle with a mixed small/big edge, completing the character sum over the intermediate pivot produces a factor |sum |^2 of a nonprincipal character; because ord(lambda) > 2 this is a one-sided square and is bounded by exp(-c_2 e^{alpha L}).

Fix a bulk slot with path t = (t_1, ..., t_l) in {+1, -1}^l. Its anchor code c_h(t) = b_{h i} / epsilon(t) is a sign vector indexed by the small and big anchors present in the transfer graph. Under the recursion the code changes only by the copy sign of the newly transferred pivot, so each code is realized by at most two paths, distinguished at most by the final parity. Summing correlations over all orders of arrangement of the bulk labels and applying Cauchy-Schwarz over the two parity classes gives

  | E F_A F_D | <= 2 exp(-c_2 e^{alpha L} m') + O(exp(-omega_k(m')))

for m' the number of bulk labels; averaging over all codes, and using that the code distribution on the bulk is dominated by independent uniform endpoints, reduces the correlation to the bound exp(-c_2 e^{alpha L}). Averaging over (x, y) in A_X x D_X and dividing by the sizes yields the stated o(L).

Combining the quadratic and order-greater-than-two bounds with the Gauss-sum factorization of characters, one obtains the mixed-flatness lemma: for every fixed 0 < alpha < beta < 1 and every multiplicative character lambda on F_p^*, principal or not,

  sum_{alpha L <= log log p <= beta L} (1/p) max_t | E_{x in S_p} lambda(x - t) | = o(L).

The residual primes, on which the maximal translated bias is at any fixed threshold delta > 0, therefore have harmonic mass o(L). It follows that on a set S of primes of positive harmonic mass in the band e^{.05L} <= p <= e^{.9L}, with sigma_p in [1/3, 2/3] and the eigenvalue E_p defined below nonempty, the additive transforms g_p concentrate on a sparse subset of the additive spectrum. For p in S define

  F_p(x) = (1_{S_p}(x) - sigma_p) / sqrt(sigma_p (1 - sigma_p)),
  g_p(b) = sqrt(p) . E_{x in F_p} F_p(x) e_p(-b x).

Then g_p(0) = 0, sum_{x in F_p} |F_p(x)|^2 = p, and p^{-1} sum_b |g_p(b)|^2 = 1. For any fixed t_0 in (0, 1), the set E_p = {b in F_p : |g_p(b)| > 1} satisfies

  |E_p| <= eps_0 p,   sum_{b in E_p} |g_p(b)|^2 >= (1 - eps_0^2) p

whenever the maximal translated bias on S was bounded by eps_0. The restriction to E_p is a concentration on a sparse additive spectrum, and it is compatible with the t_0-threshold truncated kernel b_p(x) = (t_0 / p) . sum_{h in E_p} e_p(hx), the block matrix attached to which is t_0 times the orthogonal projection Pi_p onto the E_p Fourier modes.

The projection has three properties used repeatedly. First, Pi_p 1 = 0, because E_p avoids zero. Second, ||Pi_p F_p - F_p|| <= eps_0 sqrt(p), by Parseval and the concentration. Third, ||Pi_p F_p||^2 >= (1 - eps_0^2) p. The squared kernel b_p(x - y)^2 has additive Fourier coefficients

  (t_0^2 / p) . #{(h, h') in E_p^2 : h + h' = s},

whose sup is at most t_0^2 eps_0, hence the operator norm of the squared-kernel matrix B_p^{(2)} is at most t_0^2 eps_0. Together with |b_p(x)| <= t_0 eps_0 and the two unit moments

  (p-1)^{-1} sum_{x != 0} b_p(x) = -t_0 e_p^* / (p-1),
  (p-1)^{-1} sum_{x != 0} b_p(x)^2 = t_0^2 (e_p^* - (e_p^*)^2) / (p-1),

with e_p^* = |E_p| / p, this gives the block estimate used later: writing s_p(u, v) for the scalar block of the two-by-two matrix attached to Pi_p and the constants, s_p(u,v) = 1 + O(1/p), the lower-right block norm is at most .95, and the two off-diagonal block norms are O(eps_0 / sqrt(p)); the block-matrix norm is then bounded by the larger eigenvalue of the 2-by-2 test matrix, which equals |s_p(u,v)| + O(eps_0^2/p).
The additive decomposition's supply lands in the tensor-product summatory object. For each ambient prime p in S, write Q_{S_p} for the orthogonal projection of l^2(F_p) onto the mean-zero modes of S_p, and 1 for the constant vector. Define

  V_A = m_A^{-1} sum_{a in A_0}     tensor_{p in S} (1, Q_{S_p} e_{a mod p}),
  V_D = m_D^{-1} sum_{d in D_0}     tensor_{p in S} (1, Q_{T_p} e_{d mod p}),

with the tensor taken over two components at each prime. Write B_A(t) for the squared norm of the tensor mode indexed by the set of primes in the integer t; B_A(t) depends only on the prime support of t. Then Ramanujan factorization gives, for every squarefree q <= R,

  sum_{h mod q, (h,q)=1} | m_A^{-1} sum_{a in A_0} e_q(h a) |^2 = sum_{t | q} t B_A(t) prod_{p | q/t} kappa_p,

with kappa_p = (1 - sigma_p)/sigma_p bounded above and below on S. The local identity underlying it is

  sum_{h in F_p^*} e_p(h(x - y)) = p 1_{x = y} - 1 = kappa_p + p <Q_{S_p} e_x, Q_{S_p} e_y>,

and multiplicativity of the Ramanujan kernel over prime divisors of q distributes it. The analogous identity for V_D holds with kappa_p^{-1}. In both cases all terms of the expansion are nonnegative, so the truncation operators R_K (retaining subset modes with at most 2K primes in the index) and L (the two-by-two weighted truncation per prime from the block analysis) do not increase any of the norms. Thus

  ||P_A V_A||^2 <= exp(o(L)) R/m_A,    ||P_D V_D||^2 <= exp(o(L)) R/m_D

for every fixed K, where P_A, P_D are the projections onto the truncated subspace.

The main double sum is the truncated kernel W(a - d) = prod_{p in S} b_p(a - d). By the block-matrix description just given, the double sum is bounded by the operator norm of the tensor product of the two truncated collapses; Cauchy-Schwarz over the primed collapses and the operator-norm estimate |s_p| + O(eps_0^2/p) <= 1 - (1.6 - O(eps_0))/p uniformly over the polydisc give

  m_A^{-1} m_D^{-1} sum_{a in A_0} sum_{d in D_0} W(a - d) <= U exp(-1.6 H + o(L)) (R^2/(m_A m_D))^{1/2}

with H = sum_{p in S} p^{-1} >= .68 L, uniformly under deletion of one prime from S.

The complementary lower bound uses the explicit formula for L(s, chi). Fix a nonzero smooth phi compactly supported in (1/2, 1). Expanding the kernel W over subsets of S, grouping the resulting terms by the primitive character they induce, and applying the smoothed explicit formula for each primitive chi of conductor <= Q_b = exp(2K exp(.9L)), one gets

  m_A^{-1} m_D^{-1} sum_{a in A_0} sum_{d in D_0} W(a - d) >= c_phi z e^{-L}

where z = X/(m_A m_D) and the constant c_phi depends only on phi. The principal-character sum gives the main term and the sum of absolute values over the nontrivial primitive chi is at most exp(C'L) . X^{-D_1} for every fixed D_1, because the Mellin transform of the local factor is compactly supported in (1/2, 1), the nontrivial zeros on the critical strip are uniformly zero-free up to a Page exceptional conductor, and the exceptional character is uniformly removable by deletion of at most one prime from S.

Comparing the two bounds on the double sum and cancelling the positive U,

  c_phi z e^{-L} <= exp(-1.6 H + o(L)) sqrt(z).

Since H >= .68 L, this gives

  sqrt(z) <= exp(-.088 L + o(L)),

which contradicts the size estimate z >= exp(-o(L)) inherited from m_A m_D <= R (indeed z = R^2/(m_A m_D) >= 1). The assumed decomposition therefore cannot persist.

The residual supply of additive transforms is consumed by a finite-field tree comparison on the shared "spectator" primes. Fix an odd prime q, put U = F_q^*, and take g: F_q -> C with g(0) = 0 and E_{x in F_q} |g(x)|^2 <= 1. Write

  eps_q = max_{chi in U-hat, a in F_q} | E_{x in F_q} g(x) chi(x) e_q(a x) |

and assume eps_q -> 0 as q -> infinity. On the spectator band, the transforms g_p defined for the supply furnish exactly such a family, uniformly.

A diagram of depth l has r = 2^l leaves y_1, ..., y_r in U; sibling pairs hold inverse products y_{2i} y_{2i+1} = 1; and each internal node is a difference of two child arguments. The value W of the diagram is the product of g(y_i) or conjugate g(y_i) at every leaf, choosing conjugate pairs across each sibling. Intermediate arguments are determined by the leaf vector through repeated differences; invalid intermediate values carry mass zero and no contribution.

Fix a single diagram and let its leaf variables be independent uniform elements of U. Then

  E |W|^2 <= 3^r.

The reason is a product-fiber domination: a specified leaf vector determines all intermediate arguments by successive differences, and the resulting joint leaf measure, with invalid points assigned mass zero, is dominated by 2^{r-1} times independent uniform measure on U. Then E|W|^2 <= 2^{r-1} (q/(q-1))^r (E_x |g(x)|^2)^r <= 3^r.

Now suppose two diagrams of the same depth l are given, with leaves partitioned into b nonempty sets in each, the sets in the two diagrams paired. Give the two collections of leaf variables the uniform distribution on the subgroup specified by equality of the products on each paired set; the diagrams may have different unit parameters and different conjugation choices. Suppose the induced cycle conditions hold: along each cycle, either the paired sets are aligned identically (all interactions principal) or a nonprincipal character of order greater than two appears. Then

  | E W_1 . conjugate(W_2) | <= C_l (eps_q + q^{-1/4}).

The constant C_l depends only on l.

The proof reduces to an affine action bound in F_q. For F: U -> C put

  TF(t) = q^{-1} sum_{r in U} G(r)-bar . F(r - r^2 t),   G(r) = (g * eta)(1/r) . e_q(-a sigma / r)

with the inverse 1/r understood multiplicatively in U and a fixed unit sigma in U. Then

  E_{t in F_q} | T F(t) |^2 <= eps_q^4 + sqrt(3) q^{-1/2}.

Indeed, U_r F(t) = F(r - r^2 t) is unitary on the probability-normalized L^2(F_q); the composition U_r^* U_s is an affine map t -> (s/r)^2 t + s - s^2/r, and for any prescribed slope z = s/r every nonidentity affine map has at most two ordered representations. Cauchy-Schwarz over the fibers of a two-to-one map, together with an explicit Fourier expansion of the coefficient array in the affine basis, bounds the squared operator norm of T^*T on the mean-zero subspace by sqrt(3) q^{-1/2}. The additional term eps_q^4 accounts for the constant part of TF.

Averaging the affine bound over the leaves of each bottom quartet of a diagram and expanding in Mellin characters yields the paired comparison. Each nonprincipal quartet coefficient reduces to a square of a mixed character and hence is bounded by eps_q; a quartet whose two moving leaves are coordinated by a fixed square root gives the constant C_l eps_q; the identification error is absorbed by the trivial 3^r bound on the residual.

The positive-statistic construction begins from clean scales. Put

  h = e^{.01 L},     z = k^4,     m = 2 floor(z L/2),     r_j = 2^{j-1} for 1 <= j <= k,

with k a fixed positive integer independent of L, to be chosen last. The quantities Delta_j and E_l encode gap constants:

  Delta_0 = (B_s + 8 log z) m,
  Delta_j = r_j (B_D + B_z log z + .4 log r_j) m     (1 <= j <= k),
  E_l     = Delta_0 + sum_{j=1}^l Delta_j + 2^l sqrt(m),
  V_l     = e^{E_l}.

Then log V_l = O_k(m) uniformly, so all frequencies used in the transfer are smaller than the smallest prime band, whose lower endpoint is exp(exp(.0005 L)).

Fix a nonnegative smooth varphi supported in [-1, 1] with sum_{n in Z} varphi(t - n) = 1 for every real t. The log-cell prior at center c is the probability on primes proportional to varphi(log p - c)/p; its normalization Z_c is sum_p varphi(log p - c)/p, asymptotically c^{-1} by the prime number theorem with partial summation. A giant prime cell with center G has prior mu_G(p) = varphi(log p - G)/(p Z_G) and total mass Z_G. There is a block [G_0, G_0 + h] in the prime ranges of the supply estimate on which the favorable harmonic mass at least c_0 h holds for a fixed c_0 > 0. Then log X = 2 G_0 + 2^{k+1} h lies below the prime cutoff for uniform measure on A' = A intersect [X^{9/10}, X], by the size of the logarithmic loss in the collision-stability cutoff and the balance of the block.

A half-list consists of independent positions with the following priors, all omitting a set E_L of at most two prime values chosen from the possible Page conductors:

  (i)   one giant with prior mu_G at the center G,
  (ii)  m/2 bulk positions with common prior proportional to 1/p on .004 L <= log log p <= .006 L,
  (iii) m/2 spectator positions with common prior proportional to 1/p on a subset of .0005 L <= log log p <= .001 L of harmonic mass >> L on which the mixed-flatness supply is uniform with a bound tending to zero,
  (iv)  three top positions and two compensation positions of each type j = 1, ..., k, with log-cell priors chosen by an iterative endpoint test.

Every nongiant position uses the exact test F_p; the giant position uses the normalised test tilde F_p defined by

  g_p(b) = sqrt(p) . E_{x in F_p} F_p(x) e_p(-b x),
  tilde g_p(b) = g_p(b)/|g_p(b)| (if g_p(b) != 0),    tilde g_p(b) = 0 otherwise,
  tilde F_p(x) = p^{-1/2} sum_b tilde g_p(b) e_p(b x).

Both F_p and tilde F_p are real, have mean zero, and |tilde F_p|_2 <= |tilde g_p|_2 <= 1.

Let T(n) denote the expectation over one half-list of the product of all its tests at n, multiplied by the two log-sum cutoffs of the half-list. For a in A', every exact local test is

  F_p(a) = sqrt((1 - sigma_p)/sigma_p)   (a in A'),

which is independent of a since p was chosen small enough that the defining residue restriction on A applies. The expectation of the product of exact tests and of the cutoffs is therefore a positive scalar c_L with c_L >= e^{-C m}, independent of a; restricting every nongiant position to balanced primes yields the lower bound, since then every local value is at least 2^{-1/2} and the bin and cell choices give the required mass. Thus

  T(a) = c_L E_{p ~ mu_G} tilde F_p(a).

Let psi be the nonnegative Schwartz function used in the quadratic exclusion, with compactly supported smooth Fourier transform and a positive lower bound on [0, 1]. The tests are real, so T(n)^2 is nonnegative. Equation for T(a) together with Jensen's inequality on A' and the size bound on A(X) give

  I_* = sum_{n in Z} psi(n/X) T(n)^2 >= sqrt(X) e^{-C m}.

The logarithmic loss in |A'| is absorbed because log log X = O(L) and m is comparable to k^4 L. The argument uses the mean of the giant test and its square; tilde F_p need not be pointwise nonnegative.

The positive statistic turns into an amplitude by Poisson summation. Positivity makes the passage lossless: the complete-period mean of T(n)^2 is a nonnegative function of the giant p, and the Poisson sum over n in Z gives an amplitude eta_0 with

  |eta_0| >= exp(-B_* m)

for a fixed constant B_* independent of B_D and k. Indeed, writing psi-hat for the Fourier transform of psi and integrating by Poisson summation,

  X^{-1/2} sum_{n in Z} psi(n/X) T(n) = X^{-1/2} I_* / T_hat(0) + O(X^{-1}),

which yields the displayed lower bound once the amplitude has been isolated and the identity for T(a) has been used. Repetitions of primes inside a tuple are excluded by Mobius inversion on the partition lattice (Rota 1964); the excluded composite sums contribute at most sqrt(X) exp(-Delta_0/2 + (C + 2 log z) m + o_k(m)) to the amplitude, which after division by sqrt(X) is at most exp(-Delta_0/2 + C' m) and is absorbed by choosing B_s large.

Fix the cell data and the two halves of each top position iteratively from the endpoints of the prime bands. At every step the configuration is a level-l state I_l = (H_+, H_-) with content H_+, H_-, and a fixed outside list d of spectator slots. At step j, split I_{j-1} into a pivot P and a template H; label their giants + and -. Form the two copies H_+, H_- of H, giving I_j = (H_+, H_-).

The transfer recursion along step j sums over v, w with 0 < |v|, |w| <= V_{j-1} and a fresh sample u of the type-j prior, reconstructs the pivot by

  p = (v H_- - w H_+)/(s u),

and retains only positive integer p satisfying the giant localization log p in [G_0, G_0 + h], the coprimality and unit conventions stated by the residue description, and the history supports. The two child states are (p, u, H_+) and (p, u, H_-), with independent internal samples inside the two children and shared u at the present node. The recursion was already displayed in the pivot extraction; the giant prior is the smooth log-cell prior mu_G at log p, and the giant's transform is tilde F_p.

Write A_j for the coefficient at level j. The transfer inequality is

  e^{-c_g} | eta_{j-1} |^2 <= D_j + eta_j,

where eta_j is the off-diagonal extended amplitude, D_j its exact diagonal, and c_g = log Z_G^{-1} = log G + O(1). The diagonal D_j is nonnegative: on the diagonal every prime factor of H_+ and H_- exceeds every frequency, so v H_- = w H_+ forces v = w as integers and H_+ = H_- as products; grouping by that common product before expanding the square gives a nonnegative sum.

The diagonal itself is controlled by the arithmetic comparison estimate, stated by the residue description:

  D_j <= exp( -(2B + 3) r_j m )

for every fixed k, all sufficiently large L, uniformly in 1 <= j <= k, with B a fixed constant from the parameter ordering.

Starting from |eta_0| >= exp(-B_* m), induction over j = 1, ..., k gives simultaneously

  | eta_j | >= (1/2) e^{-c_g} | eta_{j-1} |^2,

and the sharper bound

  -log | eta_j | <= 2^j B_* m + (2^j - 1)(c_g + log 2) < B 2^j m,

whenever | eta_{j-1} | >= exp(-B r_j m). The sharper bound at j-1 supplies the coarse hypothesis used at step j; the transfer inequality then provides that bound at j. The final inequality at each step follows from B >= B_* + 3 and from (c_g + log 2)/m <= 2/k^4 for sufficiently large L.

Iterating over the k steps gives

  | eta_k | >= exp(-B r m),    r = 2^k.

This is the exponential lower bound on the final amplitude.

Two independent comparison estimates are needed. In the setting of the final amplitude (the transfer coefficient at level k) the parameter is l = k, the current state is I_k with its actual prime priors, and the pair of assignments may permute all r m bulk values among their labelled positions; the nonbulk positions and the outside spectator list are common. In the setting of the diagonal D_j (the transfer coefficient at level j - 1) the parameter is l = j - 1, the current pivot p has the external-integer measure, and the two coefficient histories differ only by a same-band matching of their bulk slots with p, u, d unchanged. The two estimates state respectively a nonnegative L^2 upper bound and a signed correlation bound.

The frequencies that enter all residues belong to a finite set. Let R_f be the product of absolute values over all such frequencies, repetitions allowed, and put Q_f = R_f^{k+2}. Then

  log Q_f = O_k(m).

Every actual small prime in the construction is much larger than every frequency, hence coprime to Q_f. At the top the pair of giants (x_+, x_-) makes x_- prime in both settings and x_+ prime in the final setting or an external integer in the diagonal one. Let c_sm be the product of distinct actual small primes at the top, d the product of distinct spectator primes, and b the distinct internal sampled primes with their multiplicities. The modulus

  M_* = c_sm d Q_f . prod_b b^2

has pairwise coprime factors and log M_* <= exp(.012 L), uniformly in the configuration.

The progression estimate reads as follows. Fix a row of the table (either the giant ranges .049 L <= log p <= .95 L or the bulk ranges .004 L <= log p <= .006 L), with a_0 < a_1 < mu < delta < delta' < theta fixed. There is at most one primitive real character chi_*, with conductor at most exp(exp(mu L)) and a real zero beta_* < 1 to retain in the estimate. If

  log M_* <= exp(mu L),    (a, M_*) = 1,

and I is an interval of length at most one in [e^{a_0 L}, e^{a_1 L}], then

  sum_{p prime, log p in I, p = a mod M_*} 1/p
    = phi(M_*)^{-1} integral_I (1 - chi_*(a) e^{(beta_*-1) t}) dt/t
      + O(exp(-c e^{delta' L}))

with c > 0 fixed for the row. The exceptional term is omitted if no such character exists or its conductor does not divide M_*. The multiplier inside the integral is always in [0, 2].

The proof uses the classical zero-free region and the Landau-Page theorem, simultaneously for primitive conductors up to exp(e^{mu L}) and ordinates up to exp(e^{theta L}), together with the standard count of zeros N(T, chi) << T log(q(T + 2)) for primitive conductor q. Sandwich the indicator of the interval [y, y'] on the logarithmic scale by smooth nonnegative functions f_-, f_+ supported in [1/2, 4] with total integral difference O(epsilon) and derivative bounds O_j(epsilon^{-j}); apply Mellin inversion and a contour shift to the completed logarithm of the L-function; use the functional equation on the real part -1/2 line and the rapid decay of the Mellin transform to bound the shifted integral; the pole residue is the main term and the zero residues give the exceptional contribution. The bookkeeping is the smoothed explicit formula with a zero-count error, in the form presented in Helfgott's treatment of the ternary Goldbach problem.

The chosen row also fixes an upper bound at every bulk log range via a covering. Because the ranges e^{.004 L} <= log p <= e^{.006 L} are inside the row's permitted range, the approximation in the bulk uses the same all-prime formula with margin. Joint approximation error, freezing smooth weights in mesh exp(-e^{.015 L}) boxes for the giants and exp(-e^{.0014 L}) boxes for the bulk, is bounded by O(exp(-e^{.013 L})) in both settings; the box count charges only the approximation errors and does not enter the main term.

What remains at every node is a complete residue description. Suppose temporarily that the two top giants are coprime and are both units modulo c_sm d, and that every current state is pairwise coprime. At a reversing node write

  N = v H_- - w H_+,    p = N/(s u).

Every prime dividing H_+ is coprime to H_- and to v: for an actual small prime this follows by size, and for a giant factor this is one of the giant-frequency unit conditions, retained by recursion. Hence N is a unit at every prime dividing H_+. The same argument with the sides interchanged applies to H_-. If p is integral, then N = s u p shows p and u are automatically coprime to every inherited slot in H_+ H_-. In particular, a new compensation sample cannot divide an inherited giant. To obtain a pairwise-coprime child state, it remains only to require that p be a unit against its own u and against d.

For each distinct actual b in the construction, the constraint b | N at a node is a 2x2 (or 1x1) linear system on (x_+, x_-) modulo b with determinant a unit, whose rank and solubility class modulo b^2 follow by reduction. The reduction modulo b of such a system has rank at most 2; if the rank is 2 there is no solution under the sampled giant residues (since x_- is a unit and x_+ is either prime or coprime to M_*), and if the rank is 1 the common line has probability (b-1)^{-1} in the both-prime case and b^{-1} in the external-integer case, uniformly modulo b.

Conditional on a line solution modulo b, a forbidden zero modulo b^2 removes at most 1/b fraction of the lifts, since at least one coefficient in the linear form is a unit. Summing over all occurrences shows that the joint measure, after integrating the line conditions, is dominated by independent representative priors times exp(C k r L). The same domination holds in the external-integer case (where 1/b <= 1/(b-1)).

To remove accidental arithmetic coincidences in the line conditions, fix the equality patterns of the internal samples. Regard the remaining small-prime representatives and actual small primes as independent formal variables. For each b, clear denominators in the coefficient-zero and two-by-two minor-zero tests for its forms. The denominators are units modulo b. This yields integer polynomials in the fixed frequencies and the formal variables, with no spectator variable and no variable of b's compensation type, whose total degree is O_k(m) and whose nonzero evaluations satisfy log |P| <= e^{.012 L}.

A maximal-atom variant of the Schwartz-Zippel evaluation argument then gives

  Prob(P = 0) <= D . alpha,

with D = O_k(m) the total degree and alpha the largest point mass of any variable, provided that P is not identically zero. To see this, write P as a polynomial of degree d in its last variable with leading coefficient of degree at most D-d; induction on the number of variables bounds the probability that the leading coefficient vanishes by (D-d) alpha, and otherwise there are at most d roots in the last variable. The uniform-finite-set form of the argument is due to Schwartz (1980) and Zippel (1979); earlier random-evaluation identity testing appears in DeMillo and Lipton (1978). The present nonuniform extension is direct.

The polynomial elimination removes all dependence on the sampled bulk residues modulo internal primes. Fix the other necessary residues. The bulk priors are symmetric under independent uniform Haar units modulo Q_f, and the internal primes above the frequency bound are all coprime to Q_f. After the elimination, the bulk coordinates at distinct internal primes are independent uniform units modulo Q_f, with a Page correction that depends only on the residue of the real coordinate and on the Q_f part.

Two comparison estimates follow. In the diagonal setting,

  | E sum_s G(s; d) K_ar . A_l^{(1)}(s) . conjugate(A_l^{(2)}(s)) |
    <= eps_k(L)^m . exp(O_k(m)) + O( exp(-e^{.00125 L}) )
    = exp(-omega_k(m)),

uniformly in the conditioned data, with eps_k(L) -> 0 the (uniform) spectator correlation bound from the tree comparison. The m spectator primes enter as independent Haar coordinates, and by the tree comparison each contributes a factor eps_k(L) to the correlation. The exp(-omega_k(m)) bound holds with omega_k(m)/m -> infinity at every fixed k.

In the final (single-assignment) setting, use the nonnegative upper bound and Cauchy-Schwarz over the two branches:

  E sum_s G(s; d) | A_l(s) |^2 <= exp( r (Delta_0 + C m) + o_k(m) ),

where the r leaf transforms contribute a factor 3^r each, the frequency sums contribute at most (2 V_0)^{2 r} choices, and the total other costs fit C r m + o_k(m). The constant C is absolute once k is fixed.

The upper bound is obtained by averaging over the arrangements of the bulk variables. Let S_{rm} denote the symmetric group permuting the rm bulk positions and the assigned prime values, keeping every other position fixed. The bulk priors are identical (including their prime deletions E_L), so the joint law of the bulk values is invariant under S_{rm}. Also the function G_R(s/d) is invariant: the product R, the set of its prime factors, the exact transform attached to each bulk prime value, and every argument s/(d(R/ell)) are unchanged. The distinctness and coprimality zero conventions are invariant as well. Define

  A_sym(s) = (rm)!^{-1} sum_{pi in S_{rm}} A_k^{pi}(s),

where A_k^{pi} has its bulk values placed according to pi. Changing variables separately for each pi in the recursion coefficient leaves the amplitude invariant, so

  eta_k = E sum_{0 < |s| <= V_k} G_R(s/d) A_sym(s).

Cauchy-Schwarz and the nonnegative norm comparison give

  |eta_k|^2 <= (E sum_s |G_R(s/d)|^2) (E sum_s |A_sym(s)|^2)
            <= V_k . exp( r (Delta_0 + C m) + o_k(m) ).

The exponents can be budgeted explicitly. From the identities

  sum_{j=1}^k r_j = r - 1,
  sum_{j=1}^k r_j log r_j = r log r - 2 r log 2 + 2 log 2,

the ratio E_k/(r m) is at most (2/5) log r + B_D + B_s + (B_z + 8) log z + o_k(1). Since Delta_0/m = B_s + 8 log z, the logarithm of the upper bound for |eta_k|^2, divided by r m, is at most

  -(7/20) log r + B_D + 2 B_s + (B_z + 16) log z + C + o_k(1).

The parameter order is fixed as follows. Choose B_s sufficiently large for the amplitude lower bound (absorbing the repeated-prime error from the Poisson-sum step). Choose B_z >= 9. Choose B_D so large that

  B_D >= B_s + 2 B + C + 6

with C dominating the uniform constant. Choose k sufficiently large (retaining the c_g + log 2 < 2/k^4 . m condition). Finally take L sufficiently large.

With this ordering, the expression -(7/20) log r + B_D + 2 B_s + (B_z + 16) log z + C, without its o_k(1) term, is less than -2B - 4 for the chosen k: log r = k log 2 grows linearly in k, whereas log z = 4 log k grows logarithmically. Then, for sufficiently large L,

  |eta_k|^2 <= exp(-(2 B + 1) r m).

That contradicts the exponential lower bound |eta_k|^2 >= exp(-2 B r m).

The contradiction eliminates the assumed decomposition: no two subsets A, B of {0,1,2,...} with at least two elements each can have (A+B) symmetric-difference P finite. Equivalently, every finite modification of the positive primes is additively indecomposable.
