All scalars are real. A rooted tree has a distinguished root, every node has
finitely many predecessors, and the set of children ch(s) may be countably
infinite. A vector is a real function of the nodes, supported on finitely many
of them. Below its support the two fields are zero. From the leaves upward,
at a node s,

$$A_s(v)=\Big(\sum_{c\in ch(s)}P_c(v)^2\Big)^{1/2},\qquad B_s(v)=\Big(\sum_{c\in ch(s)}Q_c(v)^2\Big)^{1/2},$$

$$P_s(v)=\max\{A_s(v),\,v_s+B_s(v)\},\qquad Q_s(v)=\max\{B_s(v),\,-v_s+A_s(v)\}.$$

Empty sums are zero, so the instruction depends only on the finite predecessor
closure of the support. At every node

$$P_s(v)-Q_s(v)=v_s,\qquad P_s(v)\ge A_s(v),\qquad Q_s(v)\ge B_s(v),$$

and one of the last two inequalities is an equality. Indeed, subtracting $v_s$
from the formula for $P_s$ gives the formula for $Q_s$; and the maximum at
$P_s$ is attained either at its first entry, which makes the first inequality
tight, or at its second, which makes the second one tight. Upward induction
shows in addition that the pair $(P,Q)$ is the coordinatewise least
nonnegative pair with difference $v_s$ whose two entries dominate the
Euclidean norms of their children.

Three trees carry the constructions. On the tree $T$ of all finite sequences of
positive integers, with root the empty sequence, put

$$N_\Sigma(v)=P_o(v)+Q_o(v).$$

Let $T_n$ be the disjoint union of the sets of sequences of length at most $n$,
with root $r_n$, and let $J$ be the tree with root $\rho$ whose children are the
roots $r_n$. Vectors on $J$ carry one extra coordinate, at $\rho$. Fixing that
coordinate at zero gives

$$N_0(v)=P_\rho(v)=Q_\rho(v),$$

and leaving it free gives

$$N_J(v)=P_\rho(v)+Q_\rho(v).$$

On the subspace where $v_\rho=0$ one has $N_J=2N_0$. The completions of the
three normed spaces are written $X_\Sigma$, $X_0$, $X_J$. A finite set of nodes
is initial when it contains the root and all predecessors of its elements;
$\Pi_H$ is the restriction of coordinates to $H$ and $F_H$ is its kernel.

For an infinite-dimensional real Banach space with norm $N$, let $cof$ be the
set of closed finite-codimensional linear subspaces and set

$$\hat\delta_N(t)=\inf_{N(x)=1}\ \sup_{F\in cof}\ \inf_{N(y)\ge1,\ y\in F}
\Big(\frac{N(x+ty)+N(x-ty)}{2}-1\Big),\qquad t>0.$$

The maximum version $m_N$ replaces the average by the maximum of the two
endpoint norms and the constraint $N(y)\ge1$ by $N(y)=1$. The norm is
asymptotically midpoint uniformly convex (AMUC) when $m_N(t)>0$ for every
$t>0$; the averaged condition was introduced under this name by Dilworth,
Kutzarova, Randrianarivony, Revalski and Zhivkov, whose Definition 2.2 is the
maximum version used to define AMUC.

./pdf/dilworth-kutzarova-randrianarivony-revalski-zhivkov-2016.pdf

The one-sided modulus is

$$\bar\delta_N(t)=\inf_{N(x)=1}\ \sup_{F\in cof}\ \inf_{N(y)=1,\ y\in F}
\big(N(x+ty)-1\big),$$

and the norm is asymptotically uniformly convex (AUC) when this quantity is
positive for every positive $t$. Two elementary comparisons hold:

$$\tfrac12 m_N(t)\le\hat\delta_N(t)\le m_N(t).$$

The upper bound is immediate. For the lower bound fix a unit center and a
witnessing subspace, and intersect that subspace with the kernel of a
functional that norms the center. Every direction in the intersection then has
both endpoint norms at least one, so the average excess is at least half the
maximum excess. Taking the two infima and suprema gives the claim. For a fixed
unit center and direction the mean of the two endpoint norms is an even convex
function of the radius, hence nondecreasing on the positive half-line; so the
inner infimum of $\hat\delta_N$ may be taken over unit directions, and
$\hat\delta_N$ is nondecreasing. Positivity for $0<t<1$ therefore implies
positivity at every radius.

An AUC norm is AMUC with the same modulus, since a maximum dominates an
average. The converse fails. Theorem 2.4 of Dilworth, Kutzarova,
Randrianarivony, Revalski and Zhivkov exhibits a renorming of Hilbert space
that is AMUC, with maximum modulus at least $\sqrt{1+t^2/2}-1$ for
$0<t\le1$, while the one-sided modulus vanishes on that range. The two
properties are therefore distinct, and the renorming question for AMUC norms
is the one settled negatively by the Kadets-Werner example.

A negative answer uses the Kadets-Werner refinement of the Bourgain-Rosenthal
construction, whose unit ball is relatively compact in measure, so that the
result of Baudier applies to it. That space has the Schur and the Daugavet
properties, contains no infinite-dimensional reflexive subspace, and is
therefore nonreflexive.

./pdf/kadets-werner-2004.pdf

./pdf/baudier-2026.pdf

Tree norms have a separate history. The norm of James on the binary tree takes
a supremum, over finite families of pairwise disjoint segments, of the sum of
the squared sums along the segments; page 739 of his announcement carries the
construction. Girardi proved that the canonical predual of the James tree space
has an asymptotic convexity modulus of power type three, and that the full dual
is asymptotically uniformly convex.

./pdf/james-1974-separable-banach-nonseparable-dual-bams.pdf

./pdf/girardi-2001-dual-james-tree-auc.pdf

The closest relative of the two-field recursion is the positive tree cone
recalled by Banakh after Ghoussoub, Maurey and Schachermayer: a nonnegative
field at a node dominates the Euclidean norm of its children, and the norm is
taken after a symmetric convex hull and an interpolation step. The present
constructions differ in two ways: a vector is the difference of two least
nonnegative fields, and the norm is an explicit cost at the root, with no
interpolation.

./pdf/banakh-2000-weak-unit-balls.pdf

The statements proved in sequence: the three recursion norms are complete
Banach norms, and the projections onto initial sets are contractive; the
joined spaces $X_0$ and $X_J$ are reflexive; both root-sum norms have averaged
midpoint modulus at least $t^3/128$ for $0<t<1$; the fixed-root norm satisfies
a homogeneous cubic estimate; all three spaces admit no equivalent AUC norm;
the infinite tree admits a second proof of the $t^3$ bound by a stopped product
of field ratios; on the joined tree a logarithmic transform of two bounded
supermartingales produces a gap for infinite separated families; and the
constructions that replace the Euclidean aggregation inside a component of
height $h$ by $\ell_p$ aggregation with $p=1+1/h$ satisfy a sixth-power
midpoint estimate, uniform in $h$, together with a positive averaged modulus
and a uniform separation of the unit sphere from weakly null midpoint
perturbations.

The three formulas define norms. The fields are positively homogeneous, and an
upward induction gives subadditivity: the Euclidean norm of the child fields is
monotone in nonnegative arrays and obeys the triangle inequality, so each of
the two entries of each maximum is subadditive, and a maximum of two subadditive
functions is subadditive. Negation exchanges $P$ and $Q$. Symmetry and the
triangle inequality for $P_o+Q_o$, and for the common field at $\rho$, follow.
Moreover

$$|v_s|\le\max\{P_s(v),Q_s(v)\}\le N(v)$$

by following the child inequalities to the root, which gives definiteness and
continuity of every coordinate functional. The coordinate vectors are linearly
independent, so all three spaces are infinite dimensional. Positive homogeneity
and subadditivity also give

$$|P_s(v)-P_s(w)|\le N(v-w),\qquad |Q_s(v)-Q_s(w)|\le N(v-w).$$

Completing the normed spaces preserves the root formulas, so the fields extend
to continuous functions on the completions.

For a finite initial set $H$ the fields of $\Pi_H v$ vanish outside $H$, the
coordinates inside $H$ are unchanged, and each maximum is nondecreasing in the
child fields, so induction gives $P_s(\Pi_Hv)\le P_s(v)$ and the same for $Q$.
Thus $\Pi_H$ is contractive. For $N_0$ the fixed coordinate $\rho$ is omitted
from $H$. The projection has finite-dimensional range and extends to the
completion.

Take an increasing sequence of finite initial sets $H_k$ that covers the tree.
If a finite vector $v$ is supported in $H_k$, then

$$N(x-\Pi_{H_k}x)\le N(x-v)+N(\Pi_{H_k}(v-x))\le 2N(x-v).$$

Density of finite vectors therefore forces $\Pi_{H_k}x\to x$ for every vector of
the completion. In particular the coordinates separate points, and a vector
whose coordinates vanish outside a finite initial set $H$ coincides with
$\Pi_Hx$, so support inside $H$ keeps its coordinate meaning. This is the
density statement for $F_H$ that the energy argument needs.

Reflexivity of the joined spaces is read from their finite-height components.
On $T_n$ put $M_n(v)=\max\{P_{r_n}(v),Q_{r_n}(v)\}$ and let $E_n$ be the
completion in this norm. With $m_s=\max\{P_s,Q_s\}$,

$$m_s\le |v_s|+\Big(\sum_{c\in ch(s)}m_c^2\Big)^{1/2}.$$

Writing $v_j$ and $m_j$ for the Euclidean norms of the coordinates and of the
numbers $m_s$ on level $j$, this gives $m_j\le v_j+m_{j+1}$, hence
$M_n(v)\le\sum_{j=0}^n v_j\le\sqrt{n+1}\,\|v\|_2$. In the other direction the
child inequalities give $\big(\sum_{|s|=j}P_s(v)^2\big)^{1/2}\le P_o(v)$ and
the same for $Q$, level by level, so

$$\frac{\|v\|_2}{2\sqrt{n+1}}\le M_n(v)\le\sqrt{n+1}\,\|v\|_2.$$

Thus $E_n$ is the Hilbert space $\ell_2(T_n)$ under an equivalent norm, and is
reflexive.

For a vector with zero joining coordinate put
$L(v)=\big(\sum_n M_n(v|_{T_n})^2\big)^{1/2}$. At the joining root the two
child arrays are $A=\big(\sum_nP_{r_n}(w)^2\big)^{1/2}$ and
$B=\big(\sum_nQ_{r_n}(w)^2\big)^{1/2}$, and $N_0(v)=\max\{A,B\}$ while
$L^2=\sum_n\max\{P_{r_n}^2,Q_{r_n}^2\}$. Since

$$A^2+B^2=\sum_n\big(P_{r_n}^2+Q_{r_n}^2\big),$$

the two quantities satisfy $N_0(v)\le L(v)$ and $L(v)^2\le A^2+B^2\le
2N_0(v)^2$, so

$$N_0(v)\le L(v)\le\sqrt2\,N_0(v).$$

For a free joining coordinate $a=v_\rho$, with the remaining coordinates
written $w$,

$$N_J(v)=\max\{2A-a,\,2B+a\},\qquad
\max\{|a|,L(w)\}\le N_J(v)\le |a|+2L(w).$$

The lower bound uses $P_\rho-Q_\rho=a$, hence $|a|\le N_J$, and
$N_J\ge A+B\ge L(w)$; the upper bound uses $A,B\le L(w)$.

An $\ell_2$ sum of Banach spaces is complete, since a Cauchy sequence converges
in every component and its Cauchy bounds pass to finite component sums and then
to the supremum. The dual of the $\ell_2$ sum is the $\ell_2$ sum of the duals:
Cauchy-Schwarz gives one inequality, and testing a functional on finitely many
almost norming component vectors with scalar coefficients proportional to their
dual norms gives the other. Finite component vectors are dense, and applying
the identification twice shows that the $\ell_2$ sum of reflexive spaces is
reflexive. By the comparisons above, $X_0$ and $X_J$ are $\ell_2$ sums of the
spaces $E_n$ under equivalent norms, with one extra coordinate in the second
case, so both are reflexive Banach spaces with separable duals, and both
projections $\Pi_H$ are contractive of finite codimension.

The quantitative core is a single local inequality, propagated to a boundary.

Lemma. Let a finite rooted tree $T$ share the root $o$ of the ambient tree,
contain the predecessors of its nodes, and have the property that every node of
$T$ is a leaf of $T$ or has all of its children in $T$. Let $P,Q,P',Q'$ be
nonnegative fields on $T$ with

$$P'-P=Q'-Q=d\ge0,$$

let each of the four satisfy $a_s^2\ge\sum_{c\in ch(s)}a_c^2$ at every internal
node of $T$, and let at least one of the two inequalities for $P,Q$ be an
equality at every internal node. Then

$$\sum_{b\ \text{a leaf of}\ T}d_b^2\le
\big[d_o(2P_o+d_o)(2Q_o+d_o)\big]^{2/3}.$$

Proof. Put $E_s=[d_s(2P_s+d_s)(2Q_s+d_s)]^{2/3}$. At an internal node the claim
$E_s\ge\sum_cE_c$ is proved as follows. Suppose first that
$P_s^2=\sum_cP_c^2$, and set

$$U_s=(P'_s)^2-P_s^2=d_s(2P_s+d_s),\qquad V_s=(Q'_s+Q_s)^2=(2Q_s+d_s)^2,$$

with the same definitions at the children. The upper inequality for $P'$ and
the equality for $P$ give $U_s\ge\sum_cU_c$. The triangle inequality in
$\ell_2$ applied to the child fields $Q'$ and $Q$ gives $V_s\ge\sum_cV_c$.
Holder's inequality with exponents $3/2$ and $3$ then gives

$$\sum_cE_c=\sum_cU_c^{2/3}V_c^{1/3}\le
\Big(\sum_cU_c\Big)^{2/3}\Big(\sum_cV_c\Big)^{1/3}\le
U_s^{2/3}V_s^{1/3}=E_s.$$

If the tight field at $s$ is $Q$, exchange the roles of $P$ and $Q$. At a leaf
$b$ the three factors $d_b$, $2P_b+d_b$, $2Q_b+d_b$ are each at most
$d_b+(2P_b+d_b)+(2Q_b+d_b)$, and each is at least $d_b$, so $E_b\ge d_b^2$.
Iterating $E_s\ge\sum_cE_c$ through the finite tree and discarding the
nonnegative energies of internal levels leaves the sum over the leaves. This
proves the lemma.

The boundary of an initial set now produces the defect at the root. Let $x$ be
supported in a finite initial set $H$ and let $z$ vanish on $H$. Put $P_s=P_s(x)$, $Q_s=Q_s(x)$ and

$$P'_s=\tfrac12\big(P_s(x+z)+P_s(x-z)\big),\qquad
Q'_s=\tfrac12\big(Q_s(x+z)+Q_s(x-z)\big).$$

Writing $D_s^\sigma=P_s(x+\sigma z)-P_s(x)=Q_s(x+\sigma z)-Q_s(x)\ge0$, the two
differences agree, and

$$d_s=P'_s-P_s=Q'_s-Q_s=\tfrac12\big(D_s^++D_s^-\big)\ge0.$$

Convexity and the triangle inequality in $\ell_2$ give the upper inequalities
for $P'$ and $Q'$: averaging the two child inequalities and applying the
triangle inequality in $\ell_2$ to the averaged child fields gives
$P'_s\ge\|(P'_c)_c\|_2$, and the same for $Q'$. At every internal node of $H$
one of the base inequalities is tight, by the recursion. Apply the lemma to the
tree consisting of $H$ together with the first nodes outside $H$, keeping at
each such boundary node $b$ the full field values of all four arrays. The
fields of $x$ vanish at a boundary node, so

$$P'_b=Q'_b=d_b=\tfrac12\big(P_b(z)+Q_b(z)\big),$$

and the lemma gives

$$\sum_{b\in B}d_b^2\le\big[d_o(2P_o+d_o)(2Q_o+d_o)\big]^{2/3}.$$

The sum is finite because only finitely many nodes carry a nonzero coordinate
of the tail.

Write $r_s=\max\{P_s(z),Q_s(z)\}$ for $s$ in $H$. On $H$ the two fields of $z$
agree, since $z_s=0$ there, so $r_s$ is their common value and

$$r_s^2=\max\Big\{\sum_{c\in I_s}r_c^2+\sum_{b\in J_s}P_b(z)^2,\
\sum_{c\in I_s}r_c^2+\sum_{b\in J_s}Q_b(z)^2\Big\},$$

where $I_s$ holds the children of $s$ inside $H$ and $J_s$ those outside.
Hence $r_s^2\le\sum_{c\in I_s}r_c^2+\sum_{b\in J_s}(P_b(z)^2+Q_b(z)^2)$ and,
summing over the levels of $H$,

$$r_o^2\le\sum_{b\in B}\big(P_b(z)^2+Q_b(z)^2\big)\le
\sum_{b\in B}\big(P_b(z)+Q_b(z)\big)^2=4\sum_{b\in B}d_b^2,$$

so $r_o\le2\big(\sum_{b\in B}d_b^2\big)^{1/2}$ with
$d_b=\tfrac12(P_b(z)+Q_b(z))$.

For the root-sum spaces $N=N_\Sigma$ and $N=N_J$ one has $N(z)=2r_o$. Suppose
$N(x)=1$ and put $d=d_o$. Since $P_o+Q_o=1$,

$$(2P_o+d)(2Q_o+d)=4P_oQ_o+2d+d^2\le(1+d)^2,$$

and the two displays give

$$N(z)^3\le64\sum_{b\in B}d_b^2{}^{3/2}\le64\,d\,(1+d)^2.$$

This is the cubic estimate for both root-sum norms. If $N(z)\ge t$ with
$0<t<1$, then $d\ge t^3/256$: for $d\le1$ the inequality
$N(z)^3\le64d(1+d)^2\le256d$ applies, and for $d>1$ the bound holds because
$t^3/256<1$. The average gain is $2d$, so it is at least $t^3/128$.

Both estimates extend to the completions. Fix a unit center $x_0$ and
$\varepsilon>0$, choose a finite unit vector $x$ with $N(x-x_0)<\varepsilon$,
and take $H$ to be the predecessor closure of its support. For every $y\in F_H$
with $N(y)\ge1$ the estimate at $x$, applied to $z=ty$, gives an average excess
of at least $t^3/128$; moving the center from $x$ to $x_0$ changes each endpoint
norm by less than $\varepsilon$, so the average excess at $x_0$ is at least
$t^3/128-\varepsilon$. The supremum over finite-codimensional subspaces
contains $F_H$, and $\varepsilon$ is arbitrary, so

$$\hat\delta_N(t)\ge\frac{t^3}{128}\qquad(0<t<1)$$

for $N=N_\Sigma$ on $X_\Sigma$ and for $N=N_J$ on $X_J$.

The fixed-root norm behaves differently at the root. Let $N=N_0$, so that
$N(v)=P_\rho(v)=Q_\rho(v)$, and keep the same notation with $o=\rho$. The
averaged defect is

$$d=\frac{N(x+z)+N(x-z)}{2}-N(x),$$

and the lemma now gives

$$N(z)^3\le8\sum_{b\in B}d_b^2{}^{3/2}\le8\,d\,(2N(x)+d)^2,$$

because both fields of $x$ at $\rho$ equal $N(x)$. The triangle inequality gives
$d\le N(z)$, and $2N(x)+d\le2N(x)+N(z)$, so for $z\ne0$

$$\frac{N(x+z)+N(x-z)}{2}\ge N(x)+\frac{N(z)^3}{8\,(2N(x)+N(z))^2}.$$

Tail density and continuity of the fields extend this inequality from finite
tails to every $z\in F_H$ with $z\ne0$, with the finite center fixed. The
estimate is invariant under scaling of the pair $(x,z)$, so no normalization of
the center is involved, and the factor eight, rather than sixty-four, records
that the common root field carries the tail norm without the doubling that the
sum of the two root fields introduces.

A reciprocal form of the same inequality is useful. Let $\mathcal B$ be the
first exit from $H$, keep the fields $P,Q$ and the defects $d$ of the averaged
arrays, and for a node $s$ of the finite tree put

$$B_s=\Big(\sum_{b\in\mathcal B,\ s\preceq b}d_b^2\Big)^{1/2},\qquad
w_s^P=2P_s+d_s,\qquad w_s^Q=2Q_s+d_s,$$

where $s\preceq b$ means that $s$ lies on the path from the root to $b$,
including $s=b$. Both $w$ fields dominate the Euclidean norms of their children
and equal $d_b$ at a boundary node, so $w_s^P\ge B_s$ and $w_s^Q\ge B_s$.
Applying the lemma to the descendant subtree rooted at $s$ gives
$B_s^3\le d_sw_s^Pw_s^Q$, hence

$$d_s\ge\frac{B_s^3}{w_s^Pw_s^Q}$$

whenever $B_s>0$, with equality at every boundary node. The defect at each node
is therefore at least the accumulated boundary mass cubed, divided by the two
root fields.
