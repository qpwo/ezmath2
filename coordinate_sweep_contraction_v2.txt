\begin{abstract}
The Thorp shuffle on $N=2^d$ labeled cards mixes the full permutation to
uniform in $\Theta(\log N)$ physical shuffles. The upper bound uses an
absolute number of \emph{coordinate sweeps} (each sweep = $d$ physical
shuffles). This document gives the complete proof, following the
weighted-contraction route. Everything the proof uses from representation
theory is collected in Part~\ref{part:toolkit} as a precise, quotable list.
\end{abstract}

\part{What is the problem}\label{part:intro}
%=========================================================================

\section{The shuffle, in plain words}\label{sec:shuffle}

Take a deck of $N=2^d$ cards. Label the $d$ positions of a card by bits
$x=(x_1,\dots,x_d)\in\mathbb F_2^d$. \emph{One physical Thorp shuffle}
does the following.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Deal the deck into the $N/2$ unordered pairs of positions that differ
      only in the first bit, and in each pair independently swap the two
      cards with probability $1/2$. (This is a "riffle" along the first
      coordinate: it is the classical Thorp move.)
\item Rotate the $d$ coordinates cyclically, so that the next shuffle
      riffles along what was the second coordinate.
\end{enumerate}

In coordinates a physical shuffle sends $(x_1,u)\mapsto(u,x_1+\xi_u)$,
where $u=(x_2,\dots,x_d)$ and the bits $\xi_u$ are independent and fair.
After $d$ physical shuffles the
rotation has come back to the identity, so $d$ physical shuffles are the
same as \emph{the same matching applied independently along each of the
$d$ coordinate directions, once.} We call that object a
\emph{coordinate sweep}.

A single card is already uniform after one sweep (it has been swapped
according to $d$ independent fair coins). The subtlety is that the
\emph{joint} law of all the cards is not uniform: the cards have used the
same switches, so they are correlated. The theorem says that an
\emph{absolute constant} number of sweeps (i.e.\ $O(d)$ physical shuffles)
destroys every trace of this correlation, measured in total variation
distance from the uniform permutation.

\section{Statement of the theorem}\label{sec:statement}

Let $S_N$ be the symmetric group. A permutation $g\in S_N$ acts on
positions, and the product $gh$ means "apply $h$ first, then $g$".
Let $q_d$ be the law of one physical shuffle; it is a probability measure
on $S_N$. Then $K_d=q_d^{*d}$, the law of one coordinate sweep, is the
$d$-fold convolution. We write $K_d(\lambda)$ for the expectation of the
representing matrix $\rho_\lambda(g)$ of the irreducible unitary
representation labelled by the partition $\lambda\vdash N$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fourier}
K_d(\lambda)=\sum_{g\in S_N} K_d(g)\,\rho_\lambda(g).
\end{equation}
Write $D_\lambda=\dim\rho_\lambda$. Because one matching layer averages
the (self-adjoint unitary) action of a product of disjoint transpositions,
its Fourier matrix is an orthogonal projection; therefore every
$K_d(\lambda)$ is a contraction.

\begin{theorem}[Main; weighted sweep moments]\label{thm:main}
There exist positive weights $W_\lambda$ (one per partition
$\lambda\vdash 2^d$) and an absolute constant $p_*<\infty$ such that, for
every $d\ge1$ and every $\lambda\vdash 2^d$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-bound}
D_\lambda^{3/4}\ \le\ W_\lambda\ \le\ D_\lambda,
\qquad
W_\lambda\,\|K_d(\lambda)\|_{p_d}^{p_d}\le 1
\quad\text{for some }2\le p_d\le p_*.
\end{equation}
Consequently $\|K_d(\lambda)\|_{\op}\le D_\lambda^{-3/(4p_*)}$, and there
is an absolute $q=4p_*/3<\infty$ with the unweighted bound
$D_\lambda\|K_d(\lambda)\|_q^q\le1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Optimal order of mixing]\label{cor:mix}
Let $t_{\rm mix}(d)$ be the least number of physical shuffles after which
the distance from uniform is at most $1/4$, uniformly over the starting
deck, and put $N=2^d$. There is an absolute constant $C$ with
\[
 \Big\lceil\frac{2}{N}\log_2\frac{3N!}{4}\Big\rceil
 \ \le\ t_{\rm mix}(d)\ \le\ C\,d .
\]
In particular $t_{\rm mix}(d)=\Theta(\log N)$.
\end{corollary}

The upper bound is the hard direction and occupies Parts
\ref{part:dim}--\ref{part:moment}, \ref{part:mixing}. The lower bound is
Part~\ref{part:lower}.

\section{The plan, in plain words}\label{sec:plan}

Split the $d$ coordinate directions into two blocks of $\approx d/2$ each.
The positions become a rectangular board of $m$ rows by $n$ columns
($mn=N$). The first block of directions shuffles \emph{within rows}; the
second shuffles \emph{within columns}. Each block is itself a collection of
smaller, independent coordinate sweeps (one independent copy inside each
row, and one inside each column).

\emph{Induction on $d$.} Suppose we already understand the two smaller
sweeps on the row group and the column group. The full sweep is the
product of the row part and the column part. In any irreducible
representation $V_\lambda$, the operator norm of a product is not simply
the product of the norms; the overlap between the row decomposition and
the column decomposition gets in the way. Part~\ref{part:overlap} is
exactly the estimate of this overlap: how much of a vector that lives in
a single row-type can survive projection onto a single column-type inside
$V_\lambda$.

The key engineering idea is the \emph{weight} $W_\lambda$. It is a number
between $D_\lambda^{3/4}$ and $D_\lambda$, built so that the ratio
$W_\lambda/(W_\mu W_\nu)$ exactly pays for the overlap cost of going from
a row-type $\mu$ and a column-type $\nu$ up to $\lambda$. This is what
makes the induction close with a \emph{bounded} Schatten exponent
$p_d\le p_*$, independent of $d$.

Three independent ways to prove the overlap estimate are known. This
document gives one of them completely: the \emph{weighted contraction}
route, whose overlap estimate (Theorem~\ref{thm:occupied}) is an inequality
about a board with deleted cells. It is the most self-contained. The other
two routes ("deletion frames" and "coherent states") prove the same kind
of bound by other techniques; we mention them in
Remarks~\ref{rem:frames}--\ref{rem:coherent} but do not develop them,
in order to keep this account short enough to actually read.

\part{Notation and the standard tools we use}\label{part:toolkit}

\section{Conventions}\label{sec:conventions}

All vector spaces are over $\mathbb C$ and all representations are unitary.
For a partition $\lambda$ of $N$ (a Young diagram with $N$ boxes) let
$V_\lambda$ be the irreducible $S_N$-representation and
$D_\lambda=\dim V_\lambda$. On a \emph{product} of groups, $\mu,\nu,\dots$
denote tuples of partitions and $V_\mu,D_\mu$ are the tensor products and
products of dimensions. Traces on a single matrix space are
\emph{unnormalised}; Schatten norms are
$\|A\|_p^p=\Tr(|A|^p)$ for $2\le p<\infty$ and $\|A\|_\infty$ is the
operator norm. All logarithms in estimates are natural; we write
$f\lesssim g$ or $f=O(g)$ when $f\le Cg$ for an absolute constant $C$,
and constants are relabelled freely.

We use the symmetric group $S_L$ of degree $L$ and allow $L=0$ (the
trivial group) and the empty partition (dimension $1$). Box coordinates
are $(i,j)$ with $i$ the row and $j$ the column; the \emph{width} of
$\lambda$ is $\lambda_1$ and $k:=N-\lambda_1$ counts the boxes below the
first row.

\section{The toolkit}\label{sec:factlist}

These are the classical facts used in the proof. Each is precise and ready
to quote.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(T1)] \textbf{Regard irreducibles on subgroups.} Restricting $V_\lambda$
  to a Young subgroup $S_{L_1}\times\cdots\times S_{L_r}$ decomposes into
  tensor products $V_{\lambda^{(1)}}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{\lambda^{(r)}}$,
  with the $\lambda^{(i)}$ running over all tuples with
  $|\lambda^{(1)}|+\cdots+|\lambda^{(r)}|=N$ and a nonnegative integer
  multiplicity (the Littlewood--Richardson coefficient). In particular
  \emph{restriction to $S_{N-1}$ is "delete one box"}: $V_\lambda$
  restricted to $S_{N-1}$ is the direct sum of $V_{\lambda^-}$ over all
  ways to remove a corner box. \emph{(Branching; see Sagan 2001, Thm 2.8.3,
  or James 1978.)}

\item[(T2)] \textbf{Two subgroups at once.} If $V_\sigma$ is an irreducible of
  $S_{m}$ and $V_\tau$ of $S_{n}$, then
  $\Ind_{S_m\times S_n}^{S_{m+n}}(V_\sigma\otimes V_\tau)$ contains
  $V_\chi$ with multiplicity given by the Littlewood--Richardson
  coefficient for the skew shape $\chi/\sigma$ filled by the content of
  $\tau$. \emph{(Littlewood--Richardson; James 1978, Thm 16.4.)}

\item[(T3)] \textbf{Pieri's rule.} Induction of a single row (or single
  column) adds boxes to the first (or first column) subject to the diagram
  staying a partition. \emph{(Stanley EC2, Thm 7.15.7.)}

\item[(T4)] \textbf{Hook-length formula, with the correction we keep.} For
  $\lambda\vdash L$,
  $D_\lambda=L!/\prod_{\text{boxes}}\text{hook}(b)$. If $\lambda$ is the
  diagram $\alpha$ decorated by a single extra long top row of length
  $L-|\alpha|$, the ratio of the top-row factorial to the hooks it creates
  is at least $\binom{L}{|\alpha|}\exp(-|\alpha|/(L-|\alpha|+1))$, obtained
  by comparing the top-row part with $\binom{L}{|\alpha|}$ and bounding the
  hook perturbation in the first row. We use this repeatedly. \emph{(Sagan
  2001, Thm 3.10.2.)}

\item[(T5)] \textbf{Ordinary Schur--Weyl duality.} On the tensor power
  $(\mathbb C^h)^{\otimes L}$ the groups $U(h)$ and $S_L$ act and commute,
  and
  $(\mathbb C^h)^{\otimes L}\cong\bigoplus_{\gamma\vdash L,\ \ell(\gamma)\le h}
  W_\gamma\otimes V_\gamma$ with $W_\gamma$ the polynomial
  $U(h)$-representation of highest weight $\gamma$. A positive matrix $P$
  acting identically on all $L$ factors has, on the $U(h)$-type $\gamma$,
  largest eigenvalue given by the highest-weight monomial
  $\prod_i x_i^{\gamma_i}$ in the eigenvalues of $P$ sorted decreasingly;
  this is also a lower bound for $\Tr(P^\gamma)$. \emph{(Etingof et al.,
  Thm 4.57 and Cor 4.59; Schur characters in Stanley EC2 Thm 7.10.3.)}

\item[(T6)] \textbf{Signed tensor space} $(\mathbb C^h\oplus\mathbb C^h)^{\otimes s}$
  (the "even" block and the "odd" block). The group $S_s$ acts by
  permuting factors and inserting a sign equal to the sign of the
  reordering of the odd factors. This $S_s$-action commutes with a diagonal
  $U(h)\times U(h)$ action, one $U(h)$ per block. Fixing the number $s_e$ of
  even slots and $s_o$ of odd, a $U(h)\times U(h)$-sector with top weights
  $(\gamma,\xi)$ has multiplicity space, as an $S_s$-representation,
  $\Ind_{S_{s_e}\times S_{s_o}}^{S_s}(V_\gamma\otimes V_{\xi^{\rm tr}})$,
  where $\xi^{\rm tr}$ means the transpose because the odd block contributes
  a sign. This is the standard "sign twist and induction" combination.
  \emph{(Etingof et al., with James 1978 Thm 6.7 for the sign and Thm 16.4
  for the induction.)}

\item[(T7)] \textbf{Group-algebra matrix actions.} Given a matrix $M$ on an
  irreducible $V_\mu$ of a subgroup $R\subseteq S_N$, form the element of
  $\mathbb CR$ with matrices $M$ on the $V_\mu$-block and $0$ elsewhere.
  Viewed in any larger representation, its restriction to $R$ acts as
  "the chosen matrix on one copy of the carrier, and identity on every
  multiplicity copy". Its coefficient at $g\in R$ is
  $\frac{D_\mu}{|R|}\Tr(M\rho_\mu(g^{-1}))$. \emph{(Orthogonality and
  Plancherel; Etingof et al.)} We call this a \emph{matrix action
  supported on $\mu$}. When $M$ is the rank-one projector onto a unit
  vector $e$, it is an orthogonal projection on the $R$-part and is written
  $P_{\mu,e}$; it uses one direction of the carrier and is the identity on
  multiplicity copies.

\item[(T8)] \textbf{Cauchy--Schwarz for sums of projections.} When a positive
  operator is a sum of orthogonal projections onto orthogonal subspaces,
  comparison between two such decompositions is controlled by
  Cauchy--Schwarz, and nonnegativity of each irreducible's contribution
  lets one take traces representation by representation. We use this
  without further comment.

\item[(T9)] \textbf{Entropy of an occupation law.} For a probability vector
  $(\omega_{ij})$ supported on the cells of a board and marginals
  $(p_i),(q_j)$, the relative entropy of $\omega$ from the product
  $(p_iq_j)$ is at most $\log(mn/s)$ when $\omega$ is uniform on $s$
  occupied cells of an $m\times n$ board. \emph{(Standard; the argument is
  the entropy/subadditivity duality of Carlen--Cordero-Erausquin 2009,
  Thm 2.1.)}

\item[(T10)] \textbf{Interpolation of a bilinear Schatten map.} If a bilinear
  map has norm $\le e^{E/2}$ at the $2$-Schatten endpoint and $\le1$ at the
  operator-norm endpoint, then it has norm $\le e^{E/p}$ at the
  $p$-Schatten norm for $2\le p<\infty$, with $E$ independent of $p$.
  \emph{(Complex interpolation of Schatten classes / three-lines; see the
  computation in Part~\ref{part:interp}. We spell it out.)}
\end{enumerate}

\part{A dimension lower bound}\label{part:dim}
%=========================================================================

\section{Every nontrivial type that survives is large}\label{sec:dimbound}

One matching layer kills every partition of length $>N/2$: averaging a
swap along one coordinate projects onto the invariants of a Young subgroup
$S_2^{N/2}$, and only partitions of length $\le N/2$ survive. So we only
need to bound types $\lambda$ with at most $N/2$ rows. Below, $k=N-\lambda_1$
is the number of boxes below the first row.

\begin{lemma}[Dimension away from the first row]\label{lem:dim}
There is an absolute $c>0$ such that, for every $N\ge2$ and every
nontrivial partition $\lambda\vdash N$ with at most $N/2$ rows,
\[
 \log D_\lambda \ \ge\ c\,k,\qquad k=N-\lambda_1.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If both width and height are $<N/10$, every hook length is $\le N/5$, so
the hook formula gives $\log D_\lambda\ge cN\ge ck$.

Otherwise pass to $\lambda$ or its transpose so the first row has length
$u\ge N/10$; let $v=N-u$ be the remaining boxes. Keep that whole first
row and a subdiagram of size $\ell=\min(v,\lfloor u/2\rfloor)$ below it.
By branching (T1) $D_\lambda$ is at least the dimension of that
subdiagram. For a diagram with first row $u$ and any fixed lower part of
size $\ell$, the hook formula plus (T4) bounds
\[
 D_\lambda\ \ge\ \binom{u+\ell}{\ell}\,\exp\!\Big(-\frac{\ell}{u-\ell+1}\Big).
\]
The lower part has width $\le\ell$, so it adds at most $\ell$ to each top-row
hook; the correction is the displayed exponential. In the original case
$\ell\ge c_1 k$ (large $N$); in the transpose case the height hypothesis
gives $v\ge N/2\ge k$, so again $\ell\ge c_1 k$. With $\ell\le u/2$ the
logarithm is $\ge(\log3-1)\ell\ge c_2 k$. Finite small $N$ are covered by
decreasing $c$; the only one-dimensional irreducibles are trivial and
sign, both excluded here.
\end{proof}

In particular, if $k>N^{1-\eps}$ for a small fixed $\eps>0$ then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dim-large}
 \log D_\lambda\ \ge\ c_0\,N^{1-\eps}.
\end{equation}

\part{The overlap theorem}\label{part:overlap}
%=========================================================================

\section{A board with deleted cells}\label{sec:board}

This is the technical heart. Picture an $m\times n$ board, $mn=N$. Colour
some cells \emph{occupied} (there are $s$ of them) and the rest
\emph{deleted} (there are $t=N-s$). Put the symmetric group $G_s$ on the
occupied cells. Restricting a representation $V_a$ of $G_s$ to the
within-row group $S_{n}^m$, and also to the within-column group $S_m^n$,
gives two decompositions that do not commute. The theorem bounds how a
row-type direction and a column-type direction can overlap.

\begin{theorem}[Overlap on an arbitrary occupied board]\label{thm:occupied}
Let $a\vdash s$ be a partition lying in the $(h,h)$-hook, i.e.\ no box of
$a$ has both row index and column index greater than $h$; here $1\le h\le N$.
Decompose $V_a$ under the row group and, separately, under the column
group. For row-types $\sigma$ (a tuple of partitions) and column-types
$\theta$, let $I_{\sigma,u}\colon V_\sigma\to V_a$ and
$I_{\theta,v}\colon V_\theta\to V_a$ be isometries onto orthogonal copies,
over all copies $u$ and $v$, chosen so that their images span the
corresponding isotypic parts. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:occupied}
 \sum_{u,v}\|I_{\sigma,u}^* I_{\theta,v}\|_{\op}^2
 \ \le\ \exp\!\big\{C[(m+n+1)h^2\log(N+2)+t]\big\}\,
      \min\Big\{1,\ \frac{D_\sigma D_\theta}{D_a}\Big\}.
\end{equation}
Here $C$ is absolute. When $s=0$ the empty representation has dimension $1$.
\end{theorem}

\emph{In plain words.} The overlaps between the two decompositions are
tiny: the total squared overlap is bounded by an explicit factor (the
exponential) times the "quantum" ratio $D_\sigma D_\theta/D_a$, never
more than $1$. The exponential depends on the hook width $h$ and on the
number $t$ of deleted cells, which is exactly the cost of having a
non-rectangular occupied region.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:occupied}]
For $s=0$ there is nothing to prove. Throughout we use the signed tensor
space $\mathcal L=(\mathbb C^h\oplus\mathbb C^h)^{\otimes s}$ and the
signed $S_s$-action (T6).

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Realisation.}
By (T5)--(T7) the standard signed realization carries $V_a$ inside
$\mathcal L$; concretely, at fixed parity counts $s_e,s_o$ and sector top
weights $(\gamma,\xi)$ (both of length $\le h$, degrees $s_e,s_o$), the
sector's multiplicity space is $\Ind_{S_{s_e}\times S_{s_o}}^{S_s}(V_\gamma\otimes
 V_{\xi^{\rm tr}})$. There is a sector containing $V_a$: take $\gamma$ as the
first $h$ rows of $a$ and $\xi^{\rm tr}$ as the rest (a diagram of width
$\le h$), so that the relevant Littlewood--Richardson coefficient is
positive. This sector is the arena for the rest of the proof.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Size and entropy of sectors.}
The dimensions of the $U(h)\times U(h)$ carriers, and the number of
sectors, are at most $\exp(O(h^2\log(N+2)))$ (count symbol occupations row
by row in a semistandard tableau). The multiplicity of any $S_s$-type
$\chi$ in the signed action is also $\le\exp(O(h^2\log(N+2)))$: given
$(\gamma,\xi)$ contributing to $\chi$, the skew shape $\chi/\xi^{\rm tr}$
has content $\gamma$ and its part in the first $h$ columns has at most
$h^2$ boxes, beyond which columns are determined. Hence (with
$u=|\gamma|+|\xi|$)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sector-entropy}
 D_\chi\ \le\ \exp(H(\gamma,\xi))\ \le\ D_\chi\exp(O(h^2\log(N+2))),
 \qquad
 H(\gamma,\xi)=\sum_i\gamma_i\log(u/\gamma_i)+\sum_j\xi_j\log(u/\xi_j).
\end{equation}
The first inequality is $D_\chi\le\binom{u}{|\gamma|}D_\gamma D_\xi$ (from
T2 and transposition-invariance of dimension) with multinomial bounds; the
second is (T4) applied to the hooks in the first $h$ rows and columns
together with $|\gamma\cap\xi^{\rm tr}|\le h^2$.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Turning overlap into a trace.}
Let $P_\sigma,P_\theta$ be the row- and column-isotypic projections inside
$V_a$, and expand the embeddings: the sum of squared Hilbert--Schmidt norms
$\sum_{u,v}\|I_{\sigma,u}^*I_{\theta,v}\|_2^2=\Tr_{V_a}(P_\sigma P_\theta)$,
which dominates the required sum of squared operator norms. Lift to a
sector projector $P$ of $\mathcal L$ and subgroup isotypic projectors
$Q_\sigma,Q_\theta$. Since $P$ commutes with the signed permutations and
each irreducible contributes nonnegatively,
\[
 \Tr_{V_a}(P_\sigma P_\theta)\ \le\ \Tr_{\mathcal L}(Q_\sigma P Q_\theta P).
\]

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Majorising by product states.}
We bound $Q_\sigma$ in the positive semidefinite order by a probability
mixture of tensor products $S=\bigotimes_{\text{cells}} S_i$, with the same
even state $S_i$ in every cell of row $i$. The prefactor is
$D_\sigma\exp(Cm\,h^2\log(N+2))$. On one row of length $l$, bound the
projection for a partition $\chi$ by the sum over compatible sector top
weights $(\gamma_0,\xi_0)$; in each sector, average by
$U(h)\times U(h)$ the identical-cell product of the diagonal state with
entries $(\gamma_{0,i}/l,\xi_{0,j}/l)$. After averaging this product acts
as a scalar $\ge\exp(-H(\gamma_0,\xi_0))/\dim(\text{carrier})$ on that
sector (highest-weight eigenvalue via T5), and the dimensions are folded
into the prefactor. Do the same for $Q_\theta$ with column states $T_j$ and
prefactor $D_\theta\exp(Cn\,h^2\log(N+2))$. Positivity then reduces us to
bounding $\Tr_{\mathcal L}(S P T P)$.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{The quarter-root filter.}
Two normalisations are needed at once: the global sector must pay
$\exp(-H(\gamma,\xi))$ in the squared norm, and the per-cell overlaps must
average to $\le1$ under the row- and column-marginals. Let $\bar S,\bar T$
be the average cell-states over the $s$ occupied cells, put
$X=\bar S^{1/2}$, $Y=\bar T^{1/2}$, and set
$S_i'=X^{-1/2}S_iX^{-1/2}$, $T_j'=Y^{-1/2}T_jY^{-1/2}$ (inverses on
supports). With $L=(X^{1/2})^{\otimes s}P(Y^{1/2})^{\otimes s}$ we have
$\Tr_{\mathcal L}(SPTP)=\|(S')^{1/2}L(T')^{1/2}\|_2^2$. The matrix $L$ acts
on the sector with operator norm $\le\exp(-H(\gamma,\xi)/2)$: indeed
$(X^{1/2})^{\otimes s}=(\bar S^{1/4})^{\otimes s}$ has norm
$\le\exp(-H(\gamma,\xi)/4)$ on that sector by the largest-weight monomial
in the eigenvalues of $\bar S$ (whose sum is $1$); the same for $\bar T$.
By matrix-coefficient orthogonality for the block-unitary group, $L$ is an
integral of $U^{\otimes s}$ over block unitaries with total coefficient
$\le \exp(-H(\gamma,\xi)/2)\cdot(\text{squared carrier dimension})$.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{The cell product and an entropy bound.}
For a block unitary $U$ let $z_{ij}=\Tr(S_i'UT_j'U^*)\ge0$. The weighted
squared Hilbert--Schmidt norm of $U^{\otimes s}$ on the occupied cells is
$\prod_{\text{cells}}z_{ij}$. Under the product of row-marginals $p_i$ and
column-marginals $q_j$ (uniform occupation of the $s$ cells), the mean is
$\Tr(\bar S^{1/2}U\bar T^{1/2}U^*)\le1$ by Cauchy--Schwarz. So the
$\omega$-average of $\log z_{ij}$ is at most the relative entropy of the
occupation law $\omega$ from $p_iq_j$, which is $\le\log(mn/s)\le\log(N/s)$
(T9). Hence $\prod z_{ij}\le\exp(s\log(N/s))\le\exp(t)$. Inserting the
integral for $L$ and the triangle inequality,
\[
 \Tr_{\mathcal L}(SPTP)\ \le\ \exp\{C h^2\log(N+2)+t\}\,\exp(-H(\gamma,\xi)),
\]
and $D_a\le\exp(H(\gamma,\xi))$ by \eqref{eq:sector-entropy}. Multiplying by
the mixture prefactors $D_\sigma D_\theta$ and dividing by $D_a$ gives
\eqref{eq:occupied}, with $\min\{1,D_\sigma D_\theta/D_a\}$ recording that
the overlap can never exceed $1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:frames}
A second, independent proof of an overlap bound uses \emph{deletion frames}:
resolve a row-type vector by remembering only $t$ marked positions per row,
control the resulting marked kernels with a Schatten-$8$ estimate, and use
a signed-tensor Schatten-$2$ estimate for the complementary shapes. It gives
a uniform Schatten exponent without weights.
\end{remark}
\begin{remark}\label{rem:coherent}
A third proof, on \emph{coherent states}, keeps the deleted positions as an
ordered list and compares row and column projections by averaging
highest-weight coherent vectors and matching their one-cell densities by a
minimum-norm (Kempf--Ness type) argument. It yields an explicit restoration
factor $e^{3t}\binom{N}{t}$. These routes are not needed below.
\end{remark}

%=========================================================================
\part{From overlap to the row-column Fourier estimate}\label{part:fourier}
%=========================================================================

\section{The weighted overlap}\label{sec:weighted-overlap}

Now fix the split of the sweep. Write $N=2^d$ as $mn$ with $m,n$ both of
order $\sqrt N$ (they are the sizes of a column and a row for a balanced
split of the $d$ bits). The row part acts within rows, the column part
within columns; both are products of smaller independent sweeps.

We assume for the estimate that the current $\lambda$ is not sparse, in the
sense
\begin{equation}\label{eq:not-sparse}
 k=N-\lambda_1>N^{1-\eps},\qquad \lambda^{\rm tr}_1\le N/2,
\end{equation}
with the small $\eps$ of \eqref{eq:dim-large}; the sparse range is handled
in Part~\ref{part:sparse}. The weights $W_\lambda$ are built in
\eqref{eq:Jdef} below; for the moment only their two properties matter,
$D_\lambda^{3/4}\le W_\lambda\le D_\lambda$, and the exact minimisation
formula \eqref{eq:Jdef}.

\begin{proposition}[Weighted overlap]\label{prop:weighted-overlap}
Under \eqref{eq:not-sparse}, for irreducibles $\mu$ of the row group and
$\nu$ of the column group and unit carrier vectors $e,f$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-overlap}
 W_\lambda\,\Tr_{V_\lambda}(P_{\mu,e}P_{\nu,f})
 \ \le\ W_\mu W_\nu\,\exp\!\big\{O((\log D_\lambda)/d)\big\},
\end{equation}
with an absolute implied constant independent of the types and of $d$.
\end{proposition}

The proof needs the weight formula, so we state it now.

\section{The weights}\label{sec:weights}

The weight is a small ``tax'' on each diagram designed so that the tax of
a parent equals the tax of its children \emph{plus} exactly enough to pay
for restoring deleted cells. Fix absolute constants
\[
 b_0=\tfrac14,\quad \delta=\tfrac1{16},\quad b_1=\tfrac{b_0\delta}{4},\quad
 A=10,\quad \eta=\frac{b_1}{2A},\quad \eps=\frac{\eta}{8},
\]
and the penalty function
\[
 F_L(r)=r\min\{\log(L/r),\ \eta\log L\}\qquad(1\le r\le L),\qquad F_L(0)=0.
\]
For a diagram $\lambda\vdash L$ and a subdiagram $a\subseteq\lambda$
(itself a Young diagram) let $r=L-|a|$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Jdef}
 J(\lambda)=\min_{a\subseteq\lambda}\big\{b_0\log D_a+b_1 r\log L-AF_L(r)\big\},
 \qquad W_\lambda=D_\lambda\,e^{-J(\lambda)}.
\end{equation}
Empty diagrams have $J=0$, $W=1$. Since $AF_L(r)\le\frac{b_1}{2}r\log L$,
the minimised expression is nonnegative, and $a=\lambda$ shows
$0\le J(\lambda)\le\tfrac14\log D_\lambda$; hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weight-range}
 D_\lambda^{3/4}\ \le\ W_\lambda\ \le\ D_\lambda.
\end{equation}
On tuples (product groups) $J$ is summed and $W$ multiplied.

The point of $J$ is this \emph{trimming lemma}: the minimising $a$ can be
taken inside the $(h,h)$-hook, $h=\lfloor N^\delta\rfloor$, at additive
cost $O(h^2)$ in the minimum of \eqref{eq:Jdef}. Indeed, trimming the boxes
with both indices $>h$ from any minimiser $a_1$ to get $a$ changes
$\log D_{a_1}-\log D_a$ by at least $x(\log h-C)-Ch^2$ where $x$ is the
number trimmed, while the penalty changes by at most $(b_1\log N+A)x$; as
$b_1<b_0\delta$ the trim is favorable. Consequently, taking $a$ in the
hook with $t=N-|a|$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trim}
 b_0\log D_a+b_1t\log N-AF_N(t)=J(\lambda)+O(h^2),
 \qquad t\log N\le C(\log D_\lambda+h^2).
\end{equation}

\section{Proof of the weighted overlap}\label{sec:fourier-proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:weighted-overlap}]
Take the hook subdiagram $a$ from \eqref{eq:trim}, and choose a diagram
$b\vdash t$ so that $V_\lambda$ occurs in
$\mathcal I=\Ind_{G_{N-t}\times G_t}^{G_N}(V_a\otimes V_b)$ (possible by
branching and decomposing the restriction). The trace on $\mathcal I$ of
$XY$, for $X=P_{\mu,e}$ and $Y=P_{\nu,f}$, bounds the wanted trace on
$V_\lambda$, since each $G_N$-type contributes nonnegatively.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Fibers at deleted sets.}
$\mathcal I$ is a direct sum over the $t$-sets $Z$ of the board; at $Z$, the
stabiliser acts as $V_a$ on the complement of $Z$ and $V_b$ on $Z$. Let
$v_i$ count $Z$ in row $i$ and $u_j$ in column $j$, and set
\[
 L_R=\prod_i\binom{n}{v_i},\quad
 L_C=\prod_j\binom{m}{u_j},\quad
 L_0=\binom{N}{t},\quad
 E_0=H_R+H_C-H_0,
\]
$H_R=\sum_i v_i\log(n/v_i)$, $H_C=\sum_j u_j\log(m/u_j)$,
$H_0=t\log(N/t)$ (zero terms are $0$). For a nonempty profile $E_0\ge0$ (it
is $t$ times a relative entropy). The layout count is controlled by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:layout}
 \log\frac{L_{RC}^2L_0}{L_RL_C}\ \le\ E_0+O(t),
\end{equation}
where $L_{RC}$ is the number of $t$-sets with the given row- and
column-profiles: $\log L_R\ge H_R$, $\log L_C\ge H_C$,
$\log L_0\le H_0+t$ by binomial bounds, and subadditivity of entropy gives
$\log L_{RC}\le E_0+2t$ (take a uniformly random occupied cell with
independent row index $I$ and column index $J$, and use
$H(V\mid I,J)\le H(V\mid I)+H(V\mid J)-H(V)$).

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Diagonal blocks.}
The block trace of $XY$ pairs $Z,Z'$ with equal profiles; off-diagonal
blocks are bounded by the geometric mean of diagonal overlaps (Cauchy--
Schwarz for $X^{1/2}UX'^{1/2}$). For a diagonal block at $Z$, the row
stabiliser $R_Z$ acts separately off and on $Z$; if $\alpha=(\sigma,\tau)$
denotes the two parts, then (T7, orthogonality, $|R|=L_R|R_Z|$)
\[
 X_{ZZ}=\frac{D_\mu}{L_R}\sum_\alpha\frac{1}{D_\alpha}\mathcal P_{\alpha,M_\alpha},
\]
the $M_\alpha\succeq0$ with $\sum_\alpha\Tr M_\alpha=1$ obtained by tracing
out the multiplicity space. Similarly $Y_{ZZ}$ with $D_\nu/L_C$ and column
stabiliser types $\zeta=(\theta,\pi)$. The only subtlety is that $M_\alpha$
need not be a product of a surviving-cell state and a deleted-cell state;
the next lemma (which needs no independence) removes this worry.

\begin{lemma}[Arbitrary carrier vectors]\label{lem:entangled}
Let $A_l\colon E'\to E$ and $B_r\colon F'\to F$ be finite families with
$\sum_r|\langle x,B_r y\rangle|^2\le C\|x\|^2\|y\|^2$. Then for unit
$w\in E\otimes F$ and $w'\in E'\otimes F'$,
$\sum_{l,r}|\langle w,(A_l\otimes B_r)w'\rangle|^2\le C\sum_l\|A_l\|_\op^2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $l$, $B\mapsto\langle w,(A_l\otimes B)w'\rangle$ has operator
norm $\le\|A_l\|_\op$; by trace-norm duality it is
$\sum_s c_s\langle x_s,By_s\rangle$ with unit $x_s,y_s$, $c_s\ge0$,
$\sum_s c_s\le\|A_l\|_\op$. The triangle inequality in $\ell^2(r)$, then
squaring and summing in $l$, finishes.
\end{proof}

The fiber overlap for arbitrary pure carrier vectors $w,w'$ therefore has
the form $\sum_{l,r}|\langle w,(A_l\otimes B_r)w'\rangle|^2$ with $A_l$ the
row-decomposition overlaps and $B_r$ the column ones. On $Z$ itself the
row and column subgroups intersect trivially, so the pairwise products of
their group elements use unique factorisations; Plancherel in $G_t$ gives
for unit $x,y$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:on-Z}
 \sum_r|\langle x,B_ry\rangle|^2\ \le\ \frac{D_\tau D_\pi}{D_b}
 \frac{t!}{\prod_i v_i!\prod_j u_j!}\ \le\ \frac{D_\tau D_\pi}{D_b}e^{E_0+O(t)}
\end{equation}
(using $E_0=t\log t-\sum v_i\log v_i-\sum u_j\log u_j$ and factorial
bounds). Lemma~\ref{lem:entangled} upgrades this to arbitrary carrier
vectors, and Theorem~\ref{thm:occupied} bounds $\sum_l\|A_l\|_\op^2$.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Putting the pieces together.}
Write $S=\max\{0,\log(D_\sigma D_\theta/D_a)\}$. Combining
\eqref{eq:occupied}, the fiber bound via Lemma~\ref{lem:entangled}, and
\eqref{eq:on-Z}, and dividing by $D_\alpha D_\zeta$ from the diagonal-block
formulas, the pure-state bound becomes
\[
 \frac{1}{D_aD_b}\exp\{E_0-S+O(t+(m+n+1)h^2\log(N+2))\}.
\]
The positive mixtures have total weight $1$, so no extra count appears.
Multiply by $D_\mu D_\nu L_{RC}^2/(L_RL_C)$ and use
$D_\lambda\le L_0D_aD_b$ and \eqref{eq:layout}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pref-final}
 \frac{D_\mu D_\nu}{D_\lambda}
 \exp\{2E_0-S+O(t+(m+n+1)h^2\log(N+2))\}.
\end{equation}

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{The weight budget pays $2E_0-S$.}
Restrict $\mu_i$ to its complement row of size $n-v_i$: the surviving type
$\sigma_i$ is a subpartition of $\mu_i$ of that size; likewise $\theta_j$
is a subpartition of $\nu_j$ of size $m-u_j$. So
\[
 J(\mu)+J(\nu)\le b_0\log(D_\sigma D_\theta)+b_1t\log N
 -A\Big(\sum_i F_n(v_i)+\sum_j F_m(u_j)\Big).
\]
Together with \eqref{eq:trim}, this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budget}
 J(\lambda)-J(\mu)-J(\nu)\ \ge\ -b_0 S+AE_0
 -O\big(h^2+(mn^{1-\eta}+nm^{1-\eta})\log(N+2)\big).
\end{equation}
Therefore, with $\mathcal E$ the error in \eqref{eq:budget},
\[
 2E_0-S-[J(\lambda)-J(\mu)-J(\nu)]
 \le (2-A)E_0-(1-b_0)S+\mathcal E\le\mathcal E,
\]
since $A\ge2$, $b_0\le1$, $E_0,S\ge0$. This is exactly the statement that
replacing $D_\mu D_\nu/D_\lambda$ by $W_\mu W_\nu/W_\lambda$ (i.e.\
dividing \eqref{eq:pref-final} by $D_\lambda e^{-J}$ and multiplying by the
child weights) leaves only the error $\mathcal E$. All error terms and the
count of profiles are $O((\log D_\lambda)/d)$ by \eqref{eq:dim-large}, and
$h\le N^{1/16}$, $m,n\asymp\sqrt N$, $\eps<\eta/2$. This is
\eqref{eq:weighted-overlap}.
\end{proof}

\part{Schatten interpolation}\label{part:interp}
%=========================================================================

\section{A $p$-moment bilinear estimate}\label{sec:interp}

The row part and the column part of the sweep multiply (in the scan order)
to give $K_d$. Each part, inside a row or a column, is an independent
smaller sweep. Let $\mathcal B(M,M')$ on $V_\lambda$ be the product of the
two subgroup matrix actions, using arbitrary matrices $M_\mu$, $M'_\nu$ on
their irreducibles.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:interp}
Under \eqref{eq:not-sparse}, for $2\le p<\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interp}
 W_\lambda\|\mathcal B(M,M')\|_p^p
 \le e^{O((\log D_\lambda)/d)}
 \Big(\sum_\mu W_\mu\|M_\mu\|_p^p\Big)\Big(\sum_\nu W_\nu\|M'_\nu\|_p^p\Big),
\end{equation}
with the constant in the exponent independent of $p$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a single pair of types, the squared $2$-norm is a trace of a product of
positive matrix actions; diagonalising and using \eqref{eq:weighted-overlap}
gives
$W_\lambda\|\mathcal B(M,M')\|_2^2\le e^{E}(\sum_\mu W_\mu\|M_\mu\|_2^2)
(\sum_\nu W_\nu\|M'_\nu\|_2^2)$ with $E=O((\log D_\lambda)/d)$. Summing over
type tuples with triangle and Cauchy--Schwarz absorbs the log counts
(which are $O(N^{3/4}\log N)$, covered by \eqref{eq:dim-large}) into $E$.
Also $\|\mathcal B(M,M')\|_\op\le\max_\mu\|M_\mu\|_\op\max_\nu\|M'_\nu\|_\op$
by unitary restriction.

Now interpolate. Let the bilinear map $\Phi$ carry $(M,M')$ to
$\mathcal B(M,M')$ with output weighted trace-norm. It has norm $\le e^{E/2}$
at the $2$-Schatten endpoint and $\le1$ at the operator-norm endpoint.
Normalise inputs to $p$-norm $1$; test the output against a dual-norm-one
matrix. On the strip with value at $z=2/p$, extend the inputs blockwise by
polar factors with exponent $pz/2$ on absolute values (zero singular values
kept zero) and the test matrix with exponent $p'(1-z/2)$,
$p'=p/(p-1)$. Boundary norms are $\le1$; the three-lines inequality gives
factor $e^{(E/2)\theta}=e^{E/p}$ at $\theta=2/p$. Raising to the $p$-th
power gives \eqref{eq:interp}, and the exponent coefficient does not grow
with $p$.
\end{proof}

%=========================================================================
\part{The sparse shapes}\label{part:sparse}
%=========================================================================

\section{Representations whose first row is almost the whole diagram}
\label{sec:sparse-proof}

When $k=N-\lambda_1$ is small, the general machinery is overkill; a direct
combinatorial estimate is better. This is the range that seeds the
induction.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:sparse}
For $k=N-\lambda_1\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparse}
 \|K_d(\lambda)\|_\op\ \le\ (Cd)^k\,(k/N)^{k/8}
\end{equation}
for an absolute $C$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Work on ordered injective $k$-tuples of positions $x\mapsto y$. Let $K$ be
the sweep transition kernel. On functions of $k$-tuples that sum to zero
over any one coordinate (all others fixed), $K$ acts; this subspace contains
$V_\lambda$ because $V_\lambda$ occurs on $k$-tuples (invariants upon
restriction to $G_{N-k}$, branching T1) but not on $(k-1)$-tuples.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{The pair-factor formula.}
A sweep sends each card along a path; the path of a card is fixed by its
start and end, since the successive end coordinates replace the start ones.
For two cards, let $\ell$ be the last coordinate where they differ at the
start and $r$ the first coordinate where they differ at the end. Give the
pair weight $w=0$ if $r>\ell$, $w=2$ if $r=\ell$, $w=1$ otherwise. Then
$K(x,y)=N^{-k}\prod_{\text{pairs}}w$. (Paths do not collide iff
$r\le\ell$ for all pairs; a switch used by two paths rather than one
contributes the factor $2$ exactly when a pair has $r=\ell$.) This works for
any subset of cards.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Cancelled kernel.}
Because the zero-sum subspace forces all proper-coordinate marginals to
vanish, we may replace $K$ by the fully cancelled
\[
 D(x,y)=N^{-k}\sum_{B\subseteq[k]}(-1)^{k-|B|}\prod_{\text{pairs in }B}w .
\]
This vanishes unless the contact graph (pairs with $w\ne1$) has no isolated
vertex, and its absolute column sums are $\le2^k$ (each subset term in
absolute value is the nonnegative doubly stochastic sweep kernel).

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Inclusion of occupied sets.}
In $K^*K$ we sample $x$ from the row $z$ of $K^*$ (a reverse scan). The
occupied set $S$ of $x$ satisfies, for every fixed $F$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inclusion}
 \Pr(S\supseteq F)\le(k/N)^{|F|}.
\end{equation}
This is the upper product-of-marginals property: true at the reverse scan
start, and preserved by a random switch (test one switched site, average
the two bounds; test both, and the product of marginals can only grow under
averaging). The final marginals are $k/N$ (one position is uniform after
all coordinates).

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{A spanning forest.}
A no-isolated-vertex graph on $S$ contains a spanning forest with $c\le k/2$
rooted trees of size $\ge2$. There are $\le C_0^k$ forest shapes, choose
roots in $\binom Nc$ ways and path types in $2^k$ ways. Conditioned on the
common routing, expose children after parents: a common-routing child has
at most two possible start positions per meeting switch, and an
independent-path child summed over fresh positions meets a given parent at
stage $i$ with expectation $\le2^i2^{-(i-1)}$. Each embedding uses $k$ sites,
so multiply by $(k/N)^k$ via \eqref{eq:inclusion}. Summing gives, for the
signed kernel $K^*D$, absolute row sum $\le(C_1d)^k(k/N)^{k/2}$ using
$\binom Nc\le(C_2N/k)^{k/2}$ for $c\le k/2$. The $\ell^2$-operator (Schur)
bound on this subspace bounds $K^*K$ there, proving \eqref{eq:sparse}.
\end{proof}

%=========================================================================
\part{The induction that proves the main moment bound}\label{part:moment}
%=========================================================================

\section{Closing the induction}\label{sec:induction}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
All $K_d(\lambda)$ are contractions. Partitions of length $>N/2$ are killed
by one matching layer; the trivial one satisfies \eqref{eq:main-bound} at any
exponent.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Sparse range.} For $1\le k\le N^{1-\eps}$,
\eqref{eq:sparse} with $W_\lambda\le D_\lambda\le N^k$ gives, for all large
$d$ and any sufficiently large absolute exponent $p$,
\[
 W_\lambda\|K_d(\lambda)\|_p^p
 \le N^k\cdot N^{k}\,N^{-\eps k p/8}\cdot(\log\text{-factor})^k\le1 .
\]

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{The recursive step.} Suppose \eqref{eq:main-bound}
holds at the two smaller bit sizes $d_1,d_2\le\lceil d/2\rceil$ with their
exponents. Apply \eqref{eq:interp} at $p=\max(p_{d_1},p_{d_2})\ge2$, feeding
in the two subgroup sweep parts. Each input on a tuple is a tensor product of
smaller sweeps, so each weighted $p$-th power term of the inputs is $\le1$
by the inductive hypothesis, and the sums over type tuples use only the
count bounds. Thus
\[
 W_\lambda\|K_d(\lambda)\|_p^p\le\exp\{C(\log D_\lambda)/d\}
\]
with $C$ absolute. Since $W_\lambda\ge D_\lambda^{3/4}$,
\[
 \|K_d(\lambda)\|_p^p\ \le\ D_\lambda^{-3/4}e^{C(\log D_\lambda)/d}
 \ \le\ D_\lambda^{-1/2}
\]
for large $d$ (so $C/d\le1/4$), which gives $\|K\|_\op^p\le\|K\|_p^p\le D_\lambda^{-1/2}$.

Push the exponent by a small factor $1+\theta$. Interpolation of Schatten norms
gives $\|K\|_{p(1+\theta)}^{p(1+\theta)}\le\|K\|_\op^{\theta p}\|K\|_p^p$ for
$\theta\ge0$. Write $E=C(\log D_\lambda)/d$, so that $W_\lambda\|K\|_p^p\le e^{E}$
and $\|K\|_\op^{\theta p}\le(D_\lambda^{-3/4}e^{E})^{\theta}$ (using
$W_\lambda\ge D_\lambda^{3/4}$). Then
\[
 W_\lambda\|K\|_{p(1+\theta)}^{p(1+\theta)}
 \le D_\lambda^{-3\theta/4}\,e^{E(1+\theta)}\le1
\]
once $\theta\ge C'/d$ with an absolute $C'$ (take $C'=2C$). Take
$p_d=p(1+C'/d)$; then \eqref{eq:main-bound} holds at $p_d$.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Base case.} For each fixed small $d$, a nontrivial
sweep matrix is a product of orthogonal projections that together generate
$S_N$, so its operator norm is strictly below $1$ on every nontrivial
$V_\lambda$ (equality would need a common invariant vector for all matching
switches, whose generated group is all of $S_N$). Hence a finite absolute
base exponent $p_0$ exists covering the finitely many $d\le d_0$, chosen also
large enough for the sparse range.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Bounded exponent.} The recurrence multiplies the
two child exponents by $1+C'/d$ and then takes a maximum. Along repeated
halvings $d\mapsto\lceil d/2\rceil$ the sum of $1/d$ is a convergent
geometric series, so $p_d\le p_*<\infty$ with $p_*$ absolute. This proves
\eqref{eq:main-bound}, hence Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{proof}

%=========================================================================
\part{From the moment bound to mixing}\label{part:mixing}
%=========================================================================

\section{Plancherel and Cauchy--Schwarz}\label{sec:mixing}

Let $\pi$ be the uniform permutation distribution, and let $\Pr_j$ be the law
of $j$ independent forward sweeps. Finite-group Plancherel and
Cauchy--Schwarz (Diaconis--Shahshahani 1981, Sections 2--3) give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TV}
 4\,\|\Pr_j-\pi\|_{\TV}^2\ \le\ \sum_{\lambda\ne(N)} D_\lambda\|K_d(\lambda)^j\|_{\HS}^2 .
\end{equation}
We bound $\|K_d(\lambda)^j\|_{\HS}^2\le D_\lambda\|K_d(\lambda)\|_\op^{2j}$.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and $W_\lambda\ge D_\lambda^{3/4}$,
$\|K_d(\lambda)\|_\op\le D_\lambda^{-3/(4p_*)}$. Hence
\[
 D_\lambda\|K_d(\lambda)^j\|_{\HS}^2\ \le\ D_\lambda^2\,D_\lambda^{-3j/(2p_*)}.
\]
Taking $j=8p_*/3$ (an absolute number of sweeps) gives
$D_\lambda^2D_\lambda^{-4}=D_\lambda^{-2}$. So
\[
 4\|\Pr_j-\pi\|_{\TV}^2\ \le\ \sum_{\substack{\lambda\ne(N)\\ \lambda^{\rm tr}_1\le N/2}} D_\lambda^{-2}.
\]
This sum tends to $0$ as $d\to\infty$: for $k>N^{1-\eps}$ it is bounded by
\eqref{eq:dim-large} and the partition count; for $1\le k\le N^{1-\eps}$,
$D_\lambda\ge e^{-1}(N/k)^k$ (top-row hook estimate as in Lemma~\ref{lem:dim})
and there are at most $2^k$ such diagrams. Since the product law determines
the deck from any fixed starting deck, this is a worst-case bound. Thus an
absolute number $j$ of sweeps gives TV distance $\le1/4$ uniformly in $d$
(large $d$; the finitely many small $d$ converge by the strict Fourier gap
and only increase the absolute sweep count, and convolution cannot increase
TV). One sweep is $d$ physical shuffles, so $t_{\rm mix}(d)\le Cd$. This is
the upper bound of Corollary~\ref{cor:mix}.

%=========================================================================
\part{The matching lower bound}\label{part:lower}
%=========================================================================

\section{Counting reachable permutations}\label{sec:lower}

\begin{proposition}[Support obstruction]\label{prop:support}
Let $q_d$ be one physical shuffle. For every integer $t\ge0$,
\[
 \|q_d^{*t}-U_{S_N}\|_{\TV}\ \ge\ 1-\frac{2^{tN/2}}{N!}.
\]
Thus TV distance $\le1/4$ needs at least
$\lceil(2/N)\log_2(3N!/4)\rceil=2d-O(1)$ physical shuffles.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
One physical shuffle uses $N/2$ independent fair bits (one per pair), so
$t$ shuffles use $tN/2$ fair bits and can produce at most $2^{tN/2}$
permutations. Testing against the uniform measure (which weighs every
permutation and every non-reachable point in the complement),
$\|q_d^{*t}-U_{S_N}\|_{\TV}\ge1-2^{tN/2}/N!$. The threshold
$2^{tN/2}/N!\le3/4$ rearranges to the stated lower bound; Stirling gives the
$2d-O(1)$ form.
\end{proof}

This is the lower bound of Corollary~\ref{cor:mix}. Combined with the upper
bound, $t_{\rm mix}(d)=\Theta(d)=\Theta(\log N)$.
