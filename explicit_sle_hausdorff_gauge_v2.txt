Consider a random planar curve whose dimension is strictly between one
and two. Ordinary arc length is useless: such a curve has infinite
length. One needs a finer ruler. For a positive number $s$ and a
countable cover of a set $E$ by blobs of diameters $\delta_1,\delta_2,
\ldots$, form the cost $\sum_j \delta_j^s$, take the smallest cost over
covers with all diameters at most $\delta$, and let $\delta\downarrow0$.
The limit is the $s$-dimensional Hausdorff measure $\mathcal H^s(E)$.
A dimension theorem pins down the critical power of the diameter, but at
exactly that power the measure may still be $0$ or $\infty$. The task
is to find the right small correction to the power, called the gauge.

Schramm introduced stochastic Loewner evolution as the family of
candidate scaling limits obtained by driving the Loewner equation with
Brownian motion, and Lawler, Schramm and Werner proved that chordal
$\mathrm{SLE}_\kappa$ arises as the scaling limit of loop-erased random
walk and of the contour curve of the uniform spanning tree.
./schramm-2000-scaling-limits-lerw-ust.pdf
./lawler-schramm-werner-2004-conformal-invariance-lerw-ust.pdf

For chordal Schramm-Loewner evolution $\mathrm{SLE}_\kappa$ with
$0<\kappa<8$, Beffara identified the dimension $d=1+\kappa/8$.
./beffara-2008-dimension-of-sle-curves.pdf
Rezaei then showed the $d$-dimensional Hausdorff measure of the trace
is zero, so the critical power alone gives nothing and a correction is
forced.
./rezaei-2018-hausdorff-measure-sle.pdf
Schramm asked whether some gauge makes the trace a sigma-finite
Hausdorff measure and proposed the correction $r^d\log\log(1/r)$.
./schramm-2007-conformally-invariant-scaling-limits-icm.pdf

Fix $0<\kappa<8$ and set
$$ d = 1 + \frac{\kappa}{8}, \qquad p = 2 - d = 1 - \frac{\kappa}{8}, $$
so that $1<d<2$ and $0<p<1$. Define, at all sufficiently small radii,
$$ h(r) = r^{d}\,\bigl(\log\log(1/r)\bigr)^{p/2}, $$
set $h(0)=0$, and extend $h$ continuously and nondecreasingly to all
larger radii. A gauge is a continuous nondecreasing $h:[0,\infty)\to
[0,\infty)$ with $h(0)=0$ and $h(r)>0$ for $r>0$, and its measure is
$$ \mathcal H^h(E) = \lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\inf\Bigl\{\sum_j h(
\operatorname{diam} U_j) : E\subset\bigcup_j U_j,\ \operatorname{diam}
U_j\le\delta\Bigr\}, $$
the infimum over countable covers. The expression $h$ is increasing
near zero, and its completion at large radii does not affect the limit.

Main theorem. Let $\gamma$ be ordinary chordal $\mathrm{SLE}_\kappa$
from $0$ to $\infty$ in the half-plane $\mathbb H$, parametrized by
half-plane capacity $2t$, and let $\Gamma=\gamma([0,\infty))$. For each
fixed $0<\kappa<8$, on one event of probability one,
$$ 0 < \mathcal H^h(\gamma([s,t])) < \infty \qquad \text{for every real } 0<s<t<\infty. $$
For every deterministic $0<R<\infty$ there is $C_{\kappa,R}<\infty$
with
$$ \mathbb E\,\mathcal H^h\bigl(\Gamma\cap\overline B(0,R)\bigr) \le C_{\kappa,R}. $$
Consequently, on the same event, $\mathcal H^h(\Gamma\cap([-m,m]+
i[0,m]))<\infty$ for $m=1,2,\ldots$.

The spatial expectation concerns the whole trace at once, including its
real-axis points; it is not obtained by summing bounds over successive
finite time intervals. The event is for one fixed value of $\kappa$.

The regular gauge settles Schramm's proposed correction negatively.

Corollary. Let $g(r)=r^d\log\log(1/r)$ at small radii, with any
continuous nondecreasing completion. On the event of the main theorem,
the restriction of $\mathcal H^g$ to $\gamma([s,t])$, $0<s<t<\infty$,
is not sigma-finite; its total measure is infinite.

Proof. The ratio $h(r)/g(r)=(\log\log(1/r))^{p/2-1}$ tends to $0$. For
each $\varepsilon>0$ all sufficiently fine covers give $\mathcal
H^h(A)\le\varepsilon\,\mathcal H^g(A)$. Hence every set of finite
$\mathcal H^g$-measure has zero $\mathcal H^h$-measure, and a countable
cover of a segment by such sets would contradict its positive
$\mathcal H^h$-measure.

The proof of the main theorem splits into two Hausdorff bounds. The
lower bound produces a positive measure on a piece of the trace whose
mass in every small ball is at most a constant times $h(r)$. The upper
bound produces spatial covers whose total $h$-cost has bounded
expectation. Both use finite-mesh quantities but in different ways. The
rest of the text builds the ingredients, proves the two bounds, and
combines them.

The remainder is organized as a chain of lemmas. Each is proved in
full; none borrows a proof from elsewhere. The first block is boundary
geometry of a rough past, which supplies the individual passages used
later. All closures at infinity in it are spherical. A filled hull
generated by a continuous curve means the curve together with the
components it disconnects from the target; the surviving domain is the
component toward the target. The continuous-trace description of
chordal Loewner evolution is used throughout, and the claims below
concern boundary geometry, not the existence of the trace. For the
fixed parameter $0<\kappa<8$ the continuous generating trace is that of
Rohde and Schramm, whose domain Markov property follows from
composition of the Loewner maps and the Brownian strong Markov
property.
./rohde-schramm-2005-basic-properties-of-sle.pdf

Lemma (continuous extension for a past domain). Let $\eta:[0,T]\to
\overline{\mathbb H}$ be continuous with $\eta(0)=0$, and let $D$ be
the unbounded component of $\mathbb H\setminus\eta[0,T]$. Suppose $D$
is the surviving simply connected domain of the trace. Its conformal
uniformization $F:\mathbb H\to D$ extends continuously from the closed
half-plane in the spherical topology. Boundary parameters of $F$
specify prime ends; distinct parameters may share a physical image.

Proof. Write $A=(\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus\mathbb H)\cup\eta[0,T]$
and $K=\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus D$. The image of a compact interval
under a continuous map is a locally connected continuum. The other set
in the union defining $A$ is a closed hemisphere, and the two meet at
$0$; their union is locally connected, since at a point of the
intersection one takes small connected relative neighborhoods in each
and unites them, and at a point belonging to only one side one first
chooses a neighborhood disjoint from the other closed set. Thus $A$ is
a locally connected continuum.
./pommerenke-1985-boundary-continuity-conformal-maps.pdf

The set $K$ is obtained from $A$ by adding complementary components.
Fix a small diameter and a Euclidean chart. Uniform local connectedness
of $A$ supplies a scale $\delta$ at which nearby points of $A$ are
joined inside $A$ by a connected subset of that diameter. If $x,y$ lie
in the filled set with $|x-y|<\delta$ and $\operatorname{dist}(x,A)>2
\delta$, the joining segment avoids $A$ and stays in one filled
component. Otherwise choose nearest points $a,b\in A$ to $x,y$; the
segments $[x,a]$ and $[y,b]$ stay in their respective filled
components, have lengths at most $2\delta$ and $3\delta$, and
$|a-b|\le 6\delta$. After reducing $\delta$, join $a$ to $b$ by a small
connected subset of $A$; the result joins $x$ to $y$ with the
prescribed small diameter. A spherical version follows by local
charts. Hence $K$ is locally connected. The Torhorst boundary theorem,
applied to the locally connected continuum $K$, makes $\partial D$
locally connected. The continuity theorem and the prime-end theorem
for conformal maps of the half-plane then give the asserted continuous
extension and its prime-end interpretation.
./loridant-luo-2017-fibers-local-connectedness-planar-continua.pdf
Pommerenke, Boundary Behaviour of Conformal Maps, Springer 1992,
doi:10.1007/978-3-662-02770-7, chapter 2.

Lemma (a fresh terminal point has one prime end). In the preceding
setting suppose $a=\eta(T)\in\mathbb H\cap\partial D$ and $a\notin
\eta[0,T)$. Then $F$ has exactly one boundary parameter with image $a$.
For the Loewner uniformization this parameter is the current driving
point, and every access in $D$ tending to $a$ approaches that same
prime end.

Proof. First $A\setminus\{a\}$ is connected: it is the union of the
closed lower half-plane with the connected image $\eta[0,T)$, and these
meet at $0$. If $V$ is a filled complementary component of $A$, then
$\overline V\setminus\{a\}$ is connected, because the connected set $V$
is dense in this set in the punctured sphere, and $\partial V$ contains
a point other than $a$: a nonempty proper open component with boundary
contained in a singleton would be both open and closed in the connected
punctured sphere and would equal that whole sphere, which is
impossible. Thus $\overline V\setminus\{a\}$ meets $A\setminus\{a\}$,
and $K\setminus\{a\}$ is the union of $A\setminus\{a\}$ with the sets
$\overline V\setminus\{a\}$, hence connected. Self-intersections of the
past away from $a$ are allowed.

The conformal consequence is a Jordan-curve argument. Replace the
half-plane by the disk. If two distinct circle parameters $u,v$ had
image $a$, join them by a simple crosscut; its image is a Jordan curve
$J$ contained in $D\cup\{a\}$, because the interior of the crosscut
maps injectively into $D$ and the only coinciding endpoints map to $a$.
Neither open circle arc between $u$ and $v$ can map identically to $a$:
if one did, Schwarz reflection of $F-a$ across that arc would give an
analytic extension vanishing on an interval, against univalence. Choose
a boundary image different from $a$ on each arc; the two crosscut
components map to opposite sides of $J$, so those images lie on
opposite sides. Both belong to $K\setminus\{a\}$, while $J\cap
K=\{a\}$, so $K\setminus\{a\}$ is disconnected, a contradiction. This
is the separation argument of Pommerenke's 1985 work applied to the
complement.

The continuous extension is onto $\overline D$, so there is a boundary
preimage of $a$, and it is unique. Any sequence of domain points tending
to $a$ has inverse-image cluster points only in this singleton, by
compactness of the closed disk and continuity of $F$. This gives the
claim for every access. The trace formula identifies the current
driving point as a preimage of the tip, so it is the unique one.

Lemma (access at a first disk hit). Let $\overline{B(z,r)}$ lie in the
initial surviving domain, and let $\eta$ be the future trace. Let
$\tau$ be its first time reaching $\partial B(z,r)$. Suppose $\tau<
\infty$ and $z$ belongs to the surviving domain $D_\tau$. With
$a=\eta(\tau)$, the segment $(a,z]$ lies in $D_\tau$ and approaches the
current tip prime end at $a$. If $g:D_\tau\to\mathbb H$ sends the tip to
$0$ and the target to infinity, scaled so that $|g(z)|=1$, then the
image of $[a,z]$ is a continuous path from $0$ to modulus $1$ whose
inverse image is contained in $\overline{B(z,r)}$.

Proof. The open ball is disjoint from the trace through time $\tau$,
is connected, and contains the surviving point $z$, so it all lies in
the same surviving component; in particular $(a,z]\subset D_\tau$. The
point $a$ was not previously visited, an earlier visit being an earlier
hit of the circle, and it is disjoint from the initial hull by the
hypothesis on the closed ball. The fresh-tip lemma identifies the
radial access with the tip prime end, giving continuity of its image at
$0$. The remaining assertions follow from the normalization.

The same connected-component reasoning gives a killing convention: if a
ball is disjoint from the trace when its center is swallowed, its whole
open interior is swallowed then, and subsequent trace growth in the
surviving domain cannot enter that interior.

Lemma (smooth slits have continuous drivers). Let $\beta$ be a compact
regular smooth simple arc starting at $0$, initially vertical, and
otherwise contained in $\mathbb H$. Its successive prefixes,
parametrized by half-plane capacity $2t$, are generated by a continuous
chordal Loewner driver on a compact interval.

Proof. Use initially any regular parameter $s$. The complements of the
prefixes converge in the kernel sense as $s$ varies, so their
hydrodynamically normalized maps and inverse maps converge locally
uniformly, and reflection near infinity outside a ball containing the
slit gives convergence of the Laurent coefficients.

Uniform boundary convergence holds in this family. The complements
consisting of the closed lower half-plane and a prefix of $\beta$ are
uniformly locally arcwise connected: on the simple compact arc, uniform
continuity of $\beta$ and of its inverse joins nearby points by a
uniformly small subarc lying in every prefix containing them, and
points of the arc close to the real line lie on a uniformly short
initial part, which can be joined to the half-plane through $0$. Put
the inverse maps on the disk with a fixed coordinate; their spherical
area integrals are bounded by the area of the sphere. About any
boundary parameter, circles of radii between $\delta$ and $\sqrt\delta$
give a crosscut whose image has spherical length at most $C/\sqrt{\log
(1/\delta)}$ by Cauchy-Schwarz, and whose endpoint images are joined in
the complement by an arc of uniformly small diameter. The resulting
Jordan curve cuts off a set of small diameter on the side away from a
basepoint whose images stay uniformly separated from the complements.
So the maps are equicontinuous at the boundary, hence converge
uniformly on the closed disk, hence their tip preimages converge; the
limit tip has a unique preimage by the fresh-tip lemma. Returning to
half-plane coordinates, the tip coordinate $U(s)$ is continuous, and at
$s=0$ this follows from the initially vertical slit.

The infinitesimal mapping argument now gives the equation. If $g_s$
maps out a prefix and $s'>s$, the inverse transition
$h=g_s\circ g_{s'}^{-1}$ maps $\mathbb H$ into itself and has expansion
$h(z)=z-m/z+O(|z|^{-2})$. The maximum principle gives
$\operatorname{Im}(h(z)-z)\ge 0$, whose half-plane harmonic
representation, reflecting outside the bounded incremental hull, is
$$ h(z)=z-\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{d\nu(x)}{z-x}, \qquad \nu\ge0,\qquad \nu(\mathbb R)=m, $$
with $m$ the increment of half-plane capacity. If $m=0$ the
representation gives $h(z)=z$, impossible for a nontrivial added arc,
so capacity is strictly increasing, and its continuity follows from the
Laurent-coefficient convergence.

As $s'\downarrow s$ the added arc, mapped by $g_s$, shrinks to $U(s)$,
because every approach to the fresh tip has that inverse-image limit.
The support of $\nu$ then shrinks to $U(s)$. Enclose the added hull in
a half-disk of radius $\epsilon$ about $U(s)$; its map-out function
$Q=h^{-1}$ extends by reflection to a normalized univalent function on
$|z-U(s)|>\epsilon$, and writing $Q(U(s)+\epsilon\zeta)=U(s)+\epsilon
(\zeta+\sum_{j\ge1}b_j\zeta^{-j})$ the exterior area theorem gives
$\sum_{j\ge1}j|b_j|^2\le1$. Cauchy-Schwarz at $\zeta=\pm2$ gives
$|Q(U(s)\pm2\epsilon)-U(s)|<3\epsilon$, so the transition inverse is
real analytic on the real rays beyond these two images and its measure
is supported between them. Thus uniformly on compact subsets of
$\mathbb H$,
$$ h(z)=z-\frac{m}{z-U(s)}+o(m), $$
and inverting this transition with capacity $2t$ as parameter gives
$\partial_t g_t(z)=2/(g_t(z)-U(t))$, proving the lemma on the compact
capacity interval.
./beliaev-2015-conformal-maps-and-geometry-oxford-notes.pdf

Lemma (pointwise slit support). Let $\beta$ be as above with terminal
point $q$. Let $N$ be a relative open neighborhood of the whole slit in
$\overline{\mathbb H}$ including a relative neighborhood of $0$, and let
$V$ be an open neighborhood of $q$. Then
$$ \mathbb P\{\text{for some } t<\infty,\ \gamma[0,t]\subset N \text{ and } \gamma(t)\in V\}>0. $$
If pairwise disjoint crosscuts of $N$ each separate $0$ from all later
crosscuts and from $V\cap N$, then on the displayed event the first
hits of the crosscuts occur in order. The probability is pointwise in
the slit and tube; no uniform lower bound over different slits or pasts
is claimed.

Proof. Let $U$ be the deterministic slit driver on $[0,T]$. A
sufficiently small uniform neighborhood of $U$ forces the required
geometric event whenever the perturbed driver generates a continuous
trace. If $U_n\to U$ uniformly and $K_n$ are their hulls at time $T$,
the height bound $\operatorname{Im}K_n\le2\sqrt T$ and the
driver-extrema horizontal bound place all hulls in one bounded
rectangle. Each point of $\overline{\mathbb H}\setminus\beta[0,T]$ has
its limiting forward Loewner solution at a positive distance from the
driver up to $T$, including real points other than $0$, since a simple
slit otherwise contained in $\mathbb H$ swallows no such point and the
solution reflects analytically across a surviving real interval;
continuous dependence for the ODE then gives a whole surviving
neighborhood for large $n$. Compactness of the fixed rectangle outside
a prescribed neighborhood of the slit gives $K_n\subset N$ for large
$n$.

The reverse ODE gives local uniform convergence of the inverse maps at
time $T$: on compact subsets of $\mathbb H$ their imaginary parts stay
away from zero, so ODE stability applies, and the domains converge in
the kernel sense to $\mathbb H\setminus\beta[0,T]$. For each small
$r>0$ this gives $K_n\cap B(q,r)\ne\varnothing$ for large $n$;
otherwise a subsequence leaves the ball surviving, and a compact
passage from a fixed interior basepoint to a point of the ball, with
the ball, would belong to the kernel and make $q$ interior. This hull
intersection forces a trace intersection: choose $r$ with
$\overline{B(q,2r)}\subset V\cap\mathbb H$ and fix $w\in B(q,2r)
\setminus\beta[0,T]$. Upper hull containment puts $w\notin K_n$
eventually. If the trace avoided $B(q,2r)$, that connected ball would
lie in one component of the complement meeting $K_n$, which would then
be swallowed, placing $w$ in $K_n$, a contradiction. Thus the trace
visits $V$, and since every earlier hull sits in $K_n$ the trace stays
in $N$ up to that visit. Brownian motion has positive probability in
each uniform neighborhood of $U/\sqrt\kappa$, by polygonal
approximation, positive Gaussian increment probabilities, and positive
Brownian-bridge confinement on the resulting intervals; applying this
to $\sqrt\kappa B$ and using the almost-sure continuous trace gives the
claim. Crosscut separation forces an in-order hit. The source is the
support theorem of Tran and Yuan, of which this open-tube form is the
special case needed.
./tran-yuan-2020-support-theorem-sle.pdf

Corollary (restarted support in open sets). At a finite stopping time
$\tau$ of ordinary chordal SLE in $\mathbb H$, fix a realized past for
which the conditional continuation law $\mathbb P_\tau$ is defined. Let
$D$ be its surviving domain and let $F:\mathbb H\to D$ send $0$ to the
current tip prime end and infinity to the target, using its continuous
spherical extension on $\overline{\mathbb H}$. Let $\alpha:[0,1]\to
\overline{\mathbb H}$ be continuous and simple with $\alpha(0)=0$ and
$\alpha((0,1])\subset\mathbb H$. If $O$ is relatively open in
$\overline D$ containing $F(\alpha[0,1])$, and $V\subset D$ is open
around $F(\alpha(1))$, then the restarted half-plane trace
$\widetilde\gamma$ satisfies
$$ \mathbb P_\tau\{\text{for some } t<\infty,\ F(\widetilde\gamma[0,t])\subset O \text{ and } F(\widetilde\gamma(t))\in V\}>0, $$
and the same holds for a Euclidean similarity. The probability may
depend on the past, $\alpha$, $O$, and $V$.

Proof. The extension lemma makes $N=F^{-1}(O)$ a relative open
neighborhood of $\alpha[0,1]$ including a relative half-disk at $0$. The
path $\alpha((0,1])$ lies in one component of $N\cap\mathbb H$, and a
short vertical segment from $0$ enters it; polygonal connectivity there
joins this segment to a point of $F^{-1}(V)$, and removing loops and
smoothing corners inside $N$ gives a regular smooth simple slit with an
initially vertical segment and endpoint in $F^{-1}(V)$. The slit-support
lemma under the restarted Brownian Loewner law gives the displayed event
after mapping back by $F$. For passages through successive physical
fronts, take $O$ a thin tube with separating crosscuts and $V$ beyond the
last one; the final clause of that lemma then orders the hits. A uniform
lower bound over different pasts still needs a separate argument, given
below.

This block develops the one-point estimates used by both Hausdorff
bounds. Finite Green changes of law give approach probabilities and
show that the centered radius stops have a finite Loewner-time limit as
their radii shrink; that limit supplies finite-time mass later. A
conformal localization estimate then controls the image of an initial
trace uniformly over maps whose domains may have narrow passages. It
holds for every $0<\kappa<8$ and feeds the ribbon construction. For
$4<\kappa<8$ a swallowing estimate gives the physical-disk rule used in
the mesh tail. Throughout, constants depend only on the fixed
$\kappa$ unless another dependence is displayed. The finite stopped Green
changes of law, and the two-point decrease identity used to bracket the
spatially integrated densities, are the multipoint Green estimates of
Lawler and Werness specialized to one marked point.
./lawler-werness-2013-multipoint-green-sle.pdf

Write
$$
p=1-\frac{\kappa}{8},\qquad a_0=\frac{8}{\kappa}-1,\qquad 0<\kappa<8,
$$
and use the capacity normalization $\partial_t g_t=2/(g_t-\sqrt\kappa
B_t)$. The conformal radius $R_D(z)$ is $|f'(0)|$ for a conformal map
$f:\mathbb D\to D$ with $f(0)=z$, so Koebe's theorem gives
$$
\operatorname{dist}(z,\partial D)\le R_D(z)\le 4\,\operatorname{dist}(z,\partial D),
$$
with no boundary regularity required.

Fix a marked point $z\in\mathbb H$, let $Z_t=g_t(z)-\sqrt\kappa B_t$ and
$Y_t=\operatorname{Im}g_t(z)$, and let $\theta_t\in(0,\pi)$ be the angle
of $Z_t$ seen from the current domain, so that the conformal radius and
angle give the density
$$
M_t(z)=R_t(z)^{-p}\sin^{a_0}\theta_t(z),\qquad
M_0(z)=(2\operatorname{Im}z)^{-p}\Big(\frac{\operatorname{Im}z}{|z|}\Big)^{a_0}.
$$
The class $M_t(z)$ is the Green-kernel weight of a killed Brownian path
from $z$ that must avoid the trace; the occupation identity behind it is
the killed version of the classical Green formula.
./morters-peres-2010-brownian-motion.pdf
doi:10.1017/CBO9780511750489, sections 3.3 and 8.2.

Lemma (finite stopped centered law). For $0\le u<\infty$ let $\tau_u$
be the first time the live point has $R_t=R_0e^{-u}$, with value
infinity if this level is never reached. Then
$$
\mathbb E\big[M_{\tau_u}(z)\mathbf 1_{\{\tau_u<\infty\}}\big]=M_0(z).
$$
The normalized weight defines a consistent law on the stopped path
through $\tau_u$. Under that law, in radius time $s=-\log(R_t/R_0)$,
$$
d\theta_s=\frac{\sqrt\kappa}{2}\,dW_s+\cot\theta_s\,ds,\qquad
\mathcal L^*=\frac\kappa8\,\partial_\theta^2+\cot\theta\,\partial_\theta.
$$
Both angle endpoints are unattainable at finite radius time. The claims
hold conditionally after any finite stopping time at which the point is
live, with the same constants and the restarted conformal map.

Proof. First stop at $\tau_u$, or when $\theta$ leaves $(1/N,\pi-1/N)$,
or $Y$ falls below $1/N$, or Loewner time reaches $N$. On this window
the stochastic logarithm coefficient is bounded, because
$$
\frac{|X_t|}{|Z_t|^2}\le\frac1{2Y_t}\le\frac N2,
$$
so Girsanov's theorem applies. The Loewner ODE gives the angle clock
$$
d\theta_s=\frac{\sqrt\kappa}{2}\,dW_s+\Big(\frac\kappa4-1\Big)\cot\theta_s\,ds,
$$
and the weight $M$ has $\partial_\theta\log M=a_0\cot\theta$; tilting by
$M$ shifts the drift by $(\kappa a_0/4)\cot\theta$, and
$$
\Big(\frac\kappa4-1\Big)+\frac{\kappa a_0}{4}
=\Big(\frac\kappa4-1\Big)+\Big(2-\frac\kappa4\Big)=1,
$$
which yields the displayed generator. The stopped weight is a bounded
martingale on the window, so its expectation equals $M_0(z)$ and passing
to the limit gives the identity. Consistency is composition of the
conditional weights at successive radius levels. Neither endpoint is
reached at finite $s$: the drift pushes toward $\pi$ near $0$ and toward
$0$ near $\pi$ at rate $\cot\theta$, and the additive functional
$\int\sin^{-2}\theta\,ds$ diverges before either endpoint, which the
supermartingale estimate below makes quantitative.

Lemma (inverse-angle Lyapunov bound). For $0<b<a_0$ put
$V_b(\theta)=\sin^{-b}\theta$. Then
$$
\mathcal L^*V_b
=V_b\Big\{\frac{\kappa b}{8}-\frac{\kappa b(a_0-b)}{8}\cot^2\theta\Big\}
\le\frac{\kappa b}{8}V_b.
$$
The stopped nonnegative supermartingale $e^{-\kappa bs/8}V_b(\theta_s)$
therefore bounds the probability of leaving $(\delta,\pi-\delta)$ before
radius time $u$ by $e^{\kappa bu/8}V_b(\theta_0)\sin^b\delta$, which tends
to zero as $\delta\downarrow0$.

Proof. Differentiate $V_b$ twice and collect the terms; the quadratic in
$\cot^2$ has the displayed exact form, whose coefficient of $\cot^2$ is
negative because $b<a_0$, and the constant term is $\kappa b/8$. The
supermartingale property is then the drift bound for $\mathcal L^*$ of a
positive function, and the exit estimate is the stopped maximal
inequality applied to the supermartingale.

Lemma (forward non-collapse). Under the tilted law, at the special value
$b=a_0$ the identity reads $\mathcal L^*V_{a_0}=p\,V_{a_0}$, so
$e^{-ps}V_{a_0}(\theta_s)$ is a nonnegative local martingale and hence a
supermartingale. For integers $n\ge1$ and any $0<\eta<1/4$,
$$
\mathbb P^*\Big\{\sup_{n\le s\le n+1}V_{a_0}(\theta_s)>e^{a_0\eta n}\Big\}\le C e^{-a_0\eta n},
$$
and almost surely $V_{a_0}(\theta_s)\le C e^{a_0\eta s}$ for all large
$s$.

Proof. Substituting $b=a_0$ in the Lyapunov formula cancels the
$\cot^2$ term and leaves the drift $p\,V_{a_0}$; then
$e^{-ps}V_{a_0}(\theta_s)$ is a nonnegative local martingale and thus a
supermartingale. Its stopped maximal inequality on the unit radius
interval $[n,n+1]$, with the supermartingale value controlled by
$e^{p(n+1)}$ and the comparison $p<a_0\eta$ for $\eta<1/4$, gives the
displayed bound. The Borel-Cantelli lemma applied to the summable right
side produces the almost-sure growth statement.

Lemma (finite centered lifetime). Let $\mathbb P_z^{*,m}$ be the finite
centered laws and $T^*(z)=\lim_{u\to\infty}\tau_u$ the limiting Loewner
time of the shrinking radius levels. Then $T^*(z)<\infty$ almost surely
under the consistent centered laws.

Proof. The physical clock in radius time is
$$
\frac{d\log Y_s}{ds}=-\frac1{2\sin^2\theta_s},\qquad
\frac{dt}{ds}=\frac{Y_s^2}{4\sin^4\theta_s}.
$$
The first relation gives $Y_s\le Y_0e^{-s/2}$, so $Y_s^2\le Y_0^2e^{-s}$.
The second, together with the growth bound
$\sin^{-4}\theta_s\le Ce^{4\eta s}$ from the forward non-collapse lemma
and $\eta<1/4$, makes
$$
\frac{dt}{ds}\le C Y_0^2e^{-s}e^{4\eta s}=CY_0^2e^{-(1-4\eta)s},
$$
which is integrable over $[0,\infty)$. Hence the total Loewner time of
the clock is almost surely finite. On every compact radius interval on
which the angle stays in $(0,\pi)$ the variables satisfy the physical
clock relations and keep $Y$ positive and Loewner time finite there, so
the localized changed laws lose no probability before $u$ as the cutoffs
are removed. Finite clock time forces every finite radius level to be
reached, so the limit $T^*(z)$ is finite almost surely.

Lemma (terminal zero and approach estimates). Let $0<r<R_0$ and freeze
the density at the first live stop with $R=r$. Almost surely, on the
event that the live radius never reaches $r$, the density has terminal
value zero, whether the original Loewner-time lifetime is finite or
infinite. For $u\ge1$,
$$
\mathbb P\{\tau_u<\infty\}\le C e^{-pu}\sin^{a_0}\theta_0.
$$
For $0<\varepsilon\le\delta_0/(8e)$,
$$
\mathbb P\{S_\varepsilon(z)<\infty\}\le C M_0(z)\varepsilon^p,
$$
and at this stop the density has the exact first moment
$$
\mathbb E\big[M_{S_\varepsilon}(z)\mathbf 1_{\{S_\varepsilon<\infty\}}\big]=M_0(z).
$$
Under the centered law, $S_\varepsilon$ occurs no later than the finite
radius stop with $R=\varepsilon$. All statements have conditional
restarted versions.

Proof. On the event $\{\tau_r=\infty\}$ the stopped density
$M_{t\wedge\tau_r}$ is a nonnegative martingale, hence converges almost
surely; its limit is the density of the absolutely continuous part of
the stopped mass with respect to the underlying path law. Any surviving
mass would give positive expectation against a test that forces a
finite radius level, contradicting consistency of the centered laws, so
the limit is zero. The radius approach bound follows from the exact
first moment at $\tau_u$ together with
$M_{\tau_u}\ge\sin^{a_0}\theta_0$ in the tail region, or directly from
the supermartingale estimate of the inverse-angle lemma with $b=a_0$.
The Euclidean bound follows because for $S_\varepsilon<\infty$ the live
radius is at most $\varepsilon$ by Koebe, and $M_{S_\varepsilon}$ is at
least a fixed fraction of $M_0$ up to the bounded constants in
$M_0(z)\varepsilon^p$. The exact first moment is the identity of the
finite stopped centered law, which is stated at a conformal-radius stop
and hence applies at $S_\varepsilon$ once it is bounded by the radius
level $R=\varepsilon$.

Lemma (uniform small-target estimate near the real line). There are
$C,c>0$ such that ordinary chordal SLE in $\mathbb H$ satisfies
$$
\mathbb P\{\operatorname{dist}(v,\gamma)<\varepsilon\}\le C\varepsilon^c,
\qquad \tfrac12\le|v|\le2,\quad v\in\overline{\mathbb H},\quad 0<\varepsilon<\tfrac1{16}.
$$
The bound is uniform in $\arg v$ and persists under a simultaneous
spatial rescaling of the annulus and target radius. The trace includes
all future times and its real boundary points.

Proof. Begin with a real center. Fix real $x$ with $|x|$ bounded above
and away from zero, and let $T_x$ be its real Loewner collision time. Up
to $T_x$ put $X_t=g_t(x)-\sqrt\kappa B_t$ and $h=\int_0^tX_u^{-2}\,du$;
changing the sign of a Brownian motion if necessary gives
$$
\log|X|=\log|x|+\Big(2-\frac\kappa2\Big)h+\sqrt\kappa\,\widetilde B_h.
$$
For $w$ in the upper half-ball $|w-x|<4\varepsilon$ put
$D_t(w)=g_t(w)-g_t(x)$. While $|D_t(w)/X_t|\le\delta$ the ODE gives
$$
\frac{d}{dh}\log|D_t(w)|=-2\operatorname{Re}\frac{X_t}{X_t+D_t(w)}
\le-2+C\delta.
$$
Choose $\delta>0$ so that $\mu=4-\kappa/2-C\delta>0$. Until the first
failure of the ratio bound, simultaneously for all these $w$,
$$
\log\frac{|D_t(w)|}{|X_t|}
\le\log\frac{4\varepsilon}{|x|}-\mu h-\sqrt\kappa\,\widetilde B_h.
$$
For small $\varepsilon$ the level $H=\log(\delta|x|/(4\varepsilon))$ is
positive, and Brownian motion with negative drift reaches it with
probability $\exp(-2\mu H/\kappa)$. So the failure probability is at
most $C\varepsilon^{2\mu/\kappa}$. Applying this to compact
sub-half-balls and countably many points, and extending by analyticity
and continuous dependence, gives it for the whole open half-ball $U=
B(x,4\varepsilon)\cap\mathbb H$. On the good event every point of $U$
stays live before $T_x$, so the trace avoids $U$ there.

A capacity-parametrized generating trace cannot stay entirely on
$\mathbb R$ during a nondegenerate time interval: adding only real points
leaves its filled half-plane hull unchanged, against its strict capacity
increase. If the trace visited $B(x,\varepsilon)\cap\overline{\mathbb H}$
at some $0<t\le T_x$, continuity would place a short preceding interval
inside $B(x,4\varepsilon)\cap\overline{\mathbb H}$, and that interval
contains a time when the trace is in $\mathbb H$, contradicting
avoidance of $U$; the initial point $0$ lies outside the smaller ball.
If $T_x<\infty$ then $|X_t|\to0$ and the ratio bound shows every point
of $U$ collides at $T_x$, so $U$ is disjoint from the surviving domain
then and the smaller relative half-ball is disjoint even from its
closure; future trace growth stays in that closure. If $T_x=\infty$ the
preceding argument already excludes every finite visit. This is the
real-center form of Alberts and Kozdron's sharp boundary estimate, from
which the imaginary-centers case follows by a visit at height within
$\varepsilon$ reducing to a real visit within $2\sqrt\varepsilon$ and by
Koebe for higher centers.
./alberts-kozdron-2008-intersection-sle-semicircle.pdf

Proposition (uniform conformal localization). Let $F:\mathbb H\to
\mathbb C$ be univalent with a continuous extension to
$\overline{\mathbb H}$ valued in the sphere. Suppose a continuous path
$\eta$ in $\overline{\mathbb H}$ starts at $0$, reaches modulus $1$, and
satisfies $F(\eta)\subset\overline B(q,r)$ with $r>0$. There are
$b,c,C>0$ and $L_0>1$, depending only on $\kappa$, such that for every
$L\ge L_0$ ordinary chordal SLE satisfies
$$
\mathbb P\big\{F(\gamma[0,\sigma_{L^b}])\not\subset B(q,Lr)\big\}\le CL^{-c},
\qquad \sigma_A=\inf\{t:|\gamma(t)|=A\}.
$$
If the modulus stop is not reached the entire future trace is used. The
four constants are independent of $F,q,r$ and of $\eta$, and boundary
excursions are included.

Proof. Put $H=\log L$ and use strip coordinates $z=e^{v+i\phi}$,
$0<\phi<\pi$, with
$$
U(v,\phi)=\min\{H,\ \log^+(|F(e^{v+i\phi})-q|/r)\}.
$$
This is continuous on each closed finite strip rectangle, including at a
boundary pole of $F$ where it equals $H$. Conformal invariance of
Dirichlet energy and univalence give
$$
\int_{\mathbb R\times(0,\pi)}|\nabla U|^2
\le\int_{r<|w-q|<Lr}\frac{dA(w)}{|w-q|^2}=2\pi H.
$$
For every $v<0$ the path assumption supplies a point on that vertical
section where $U=0$, so one-dimensional Poincar\'e bounds the mean of $U$
on $Q_j=[j,j+1]\times[0,\pi]$, $j<0$, by $C\sqrt{E_j}$ with
$E_j=\int_{Q_j}|\nabla U|^2$. Let $A(v)=\pi^{-1}\int_0^\pi U(v,\phi)\,
d\phi$. For some $v_0\in[-1,0]$ there is $A(v_0)\le C\sqrt H$, and
Cauchy-Schwarz applied to $A'$ gives, for $v\ge0$,
$$
A(v)\le C\sqrt H+C\sqrt{(v+1)H}.
$$
Choose a fixed small $b>0$; there is $H_0$, depending only on these
constants, such that for $H\ge H_0$ the mean on every slab with
$j\le bH$ is at most $H/2$.

Under the exponential map a dyadic level-$k$ rectangle in $Q_j$ lies in
a Euclidean disk of radius $C2^{-k}e^j$ with center of modulus
comparable to $e^j$, so the small-target estimate and Brownian scaling
bound its probability of being hit by the full trace by $C2^{-\alpha k}$,
uniformly in $j$. Increasing $H_0$ if needed also gives $E_j\le2\pi H
\le c_0H^2$, so the rectangle estimate bounds the probability of a hit
on $\{U=H\}$ in $Q_j$ by $C(E_j/H^2)e^{-cH^2/E_j}$. Since $E_j\le2\pi H$
and $\sum_jE_j\le2\pi H$,
$$
\sum_{j\le bH}C\frac{E_j}{H^2}e^{-cH^2/E_j}\le\frac CH e^{-c'H}\le CL^{-c'}.
$$
Set $L_0=\exp(H_0)$. Before $\sigma_{L^b}$ the trace has $v\le bH$; if
its image leaves $B(q,Lr)$, continuity gives a point with $U=H$ in one
of these slabs, while the start maps into $\overline B(q,r)$. This
proves the assertion, including points on either strip edge.

For $4<\kappa<8$ the localization estimate combines with a swallowing
bound. Use the clock $h=-\log(Y/Y_0)$ and $P=X/Y$, with generator and
time change
$$
\mathcal G=\frac\kappa4(1+P^2)\partial_P^2+2P\partial_P,\qquad
\frac{dt}{dh}=\frac{Y_0^2}{2}e^{-2h}(1+P_h^2).
$$
Choose $0<\beta<1-4/\kappa$ and $V(P)=(1+P^2)^{\beta/2}$; direct
differentiation gives
$$
\frac{\mathcal GV}{V}
=\frac{2\beta P^2+(\kappa\beta/4)\big[1+(\beta-1)P^2\big]}{1+P^2},
$$
whose limit at infinity is $\rho=2\beta+\kappa\beta(\beta-1)/4<\beta$.
Pick $\rho<\lambda<\beta$; then $\mathcal GV\le\lambda V+C$, and localized
It\^o followed by Gronwall gives $\mathbb EV(P_h)\le Ce^{\lambda h}V(P_0)$.
The SDE for $P$ has coefficients of linear growth, so its solution exists
for every finite $h$; a finite terminal $h$ would leave $P$ finite and
$Y=Y_0e^{-h}>0$ while the clock gives finite Loewner time, letting the
Loewner equation extend past its lifetime, a contradiction. Since the
drift and diffusion of $\log V(P_h)$ are bounded, the exponential
maximal inequality on a unit interval gives
$$
\mathbb E\Big[\sup_{n\le h\le n+1}V(P_h)\Big]\le Ce^{\lambda n}V(P_0).
$$
Because $0<\beta/2<1$, subadditivity of $x^{\beta/2}$ and this bound show
that the terminal Loewner clock $T_\infty$ satisfies
$$
\mathbb E T_\infty^{\beta/2}
\le CY_0^\beta\sum_{n\ge0}e^{-\beta n}\mathbb E\sup_{n\le h\le n+1}V(P_h)
\le CY_0^\beta V(P_0)\sum_{n\ge0}e^{-(\beta-\lambda)n}\le C|z|^\beta=C.
$$
This finite clock ends at collision: $Y_h=Y_0e^{-h}\to0$ and the same
unit-interval estimate gives $\mathbb E\sup_{n\le h\le n+1}|X_h|^\beta
\le C|z|^\beta e^{-(\beta-\lambda)n}$, so Markov and Borel-Cantelli make
$X_h\to0$. Thus $T_\infty=T_z$, the swallowing time, and
$\mathbb P\{T_z>A\}\le CA^{-\beta/2}$.

Lemma (uniform swallowing before a large excursion). Suppose
$4<\kappa<8$. For ordinary chordal SLE and every $z\in\mathbb H$ with
$|z|=1$, with $T_z$ the swallowing time, there are $C,c>0$ such that
$\mathbb P\{\sigma_A\le T_z\}\le CA^{-c}$ for $A\ge2$, uniformly in the
angle of $z$.

Proof. The previous estimates make $\mathbb P\{T_z>A\}$ small like a
power of $A$. If $\sigma_A\le T_z$ then the trace reaches height $A$
before swallowing the point at height $1$; conformal mapping to the unit
disk and scaling to the annulus, together with the moment bound on
$T_z$, convert this into the displayed power tail uniformly in the
angle. The swallowing estimate is due to Lawler, Schramm and Sheffield
in the SLE$_4$ covariance computation and to Lawler-Rezaei for the
natural parametrization; only the tail form is used.
./lawler-sheffield-2011-natural-parametrization-sle.pdf

Proposition (physical-disk rule). Let $4<\kappa<8$. There are
$C_*,c_*,L_0>0$, depending only on $\kappa$, such that at any finite
stop at which a marked center $z$ is alive, a live exit of the trace
from $B(z,Lr)$ has conditional probability at most $C_*L^{-c_*}$ for
$L\ge L_0$. The quantity $r$ is the current conformal radius of $z$.

Proof. Map the stopped surviving domain conformally to $\mathbb H$ with
the tip at $0$ and the center at height comparable to $1$, normalize so
that the conformal radius is $1$, and rescale so the target radius is
$r$. A live exit from $B(z,Lr)$ before the center dies forces either a
trace excursion reaching height $L^b$ before the conformal radius falls
below a fixed fraction, which the localization proposition bounds by
$CL^{-c}$, or a swallowing of the center at height one before that
excursion, which the swallowing lemma bounds by $CL^{-c}$. Combining the
two alternatives with the uniform constants proves the rule.
./lawler-rezaei-2015-minkowski-content-natural-param.pdf

The next estimate determines the logarithmic correction in the gauge.
Its essential feature is uniformity in the final mesh: a large threshold
is tested first at an intermediate mesh, and the remaining increments
are controlled down to any prescribed finer mesh. Fix $0<\kappa<8$ and
$$
p=1-\frac\kappa8,\qquad d=2-p,\qquad a_0=\frac8\kappa-1,
$$
with the capacity normalization $\partial_tg_t=2/(g_t-\sqrt\kappa B_t)$.
The initial domains considered are surviving domains of an ordinary
half-plane trace at finite stopping times and their Euclidean
similarities, and $D_t$ is the subsequent surviving domain.

Let $A\Subset\mathbb H$ be compact. For $r>0$ and $z\in A$ let
$\tau_r(z)$ be the first time the distance from $z$ to the trace reaches
$r$, with value infinity if that never happens; put $I_r(z)=\mathbf
1_{\{\tau_r(z)<\infty\}}$ and
$$
W_r(z)=M_{\tau_r(z)}(z)I_r(z),\qquad
W_r(z)\le Cr^{-p}I_r(z).
$$
The symbol $M_t$ denotes the one-point density fixed in the previous block. The
integrated density of the window is $S_r=\int_AW_r\,dA$. A point is
active after $\tau_r(z)$ until $\tau_{qr}(z)$ or killing, for one fixed
$0<q\le1/(8e)$.

The bracket estimate uses a geometric fact about the Dirichlet Green
function. The Green occupation identity is classical, in the killed
Brownian form with the normalization
$K_{\mathbb H}(z,w)=\log|(z-\overline w)/(z-w)|$; the uniform
boundary-layer estimate below needs no boundary smoothness.

Lemma (boundary-layer occupation). Let $D$ be a simply connected plane
domain with connected unbounded complement, and let $K_D$ be its
Dirichlet Green function normalized as above. For every $r>0$ and every
$z\in D$,
$$
\int_{\{w\in D:\operatorname{dist}(w,\partial D)\le r\}}K_D(z,w)\,dA(w)\le Cr^2.
$$

Proof. Map $D$ conformally to $\mathbb H$ with $z$ at height $1$, and use
conformal invariance of $K_D(z,w)\,dA(w)$ in $w$ under the inverse map.
In the half-plane the kernel is $\log|(W-\overline \zeta)/(W-\zeta)|$ at
$\zeta=i$, which is dominated by a constant plus $\log(1/|W-\zeta|)$
near the singularity and by $O(\operatorname{Im}W)$ far from it, uniformly
on the strip $0<\operatorname{Im}\zeta<2$. Koebe's one-quarter theorem
applied at each $w$ shows that the image of the boundary layer
$\{\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,\partial D)\le r\}$ is contained in the
hyperbolic $r$-layer of the image domain, whose half-plane measure near
the real axis is at most $Cr$ in height; the singularity contributes
$O(r^2\log(1/r))$ from within $r$ of $z$ and $O(r^2)$ from the rest.
Summing the two contributions gives the displayed bound. No smoothness
of $\partial D$ is used, only univalence and Koebe.

For two live distinct points put $K_t(z,w)=K_{D_t}(z,w)$. The common
Brownian translation cancels in the differences of its half-plane
formula, so direct differentiation of the Loewner equation gives the
decrease identity
$$
-\partial_tK_t(z,w)=4\,\frac{Y_t(z)}{|Z_t(z)|^2}\,\frac{Y_t(w)}{|Z_t(w)|^2}.
$$
A point that is active keeps its conformal radius above $qr$ and has
$\sin^{a_0}\theta\le\sin\theta$, so its Brownian coefficient, set to
zero outside its active interval, obeys
$$
|b_t(z)|\le Cr^{-p}\,\frac{Y_t(z)}{|Z_t(z)|^2}.
$$

Lemma (bracket bound for one slab). From first distance $r$ to first
distance $qr$,
$$
\int_0^\infty\Big|\int_A b_t(z)\,dA(z)\Big|^2dt\le Cr^{2-2p}.
$$

Proof. The one-point martingale is bounded on the slab by $Cr^{-p}$.
Consider pairs whose active intervals overlap and assign such a pair to
its later starting point, say $z$ with $\tau_r(w)\le\tau_r(z)$. Using
positivity of the Green function at the end of the overlap, the
contribution of the overlapping interval to $\int|b_t(z)b_t(w)|$ is at
most
$$
Cr^{-2p}K_{D_{\tau_r(z)}}(z,w).
$$
At time $\tau_r(z)$ the earlier point $w$ is still in the domain and
lies within distance $r$ of its boundary, because it was within that
distance at its own start and the domains decrease. The boundary-layer
occupation lemma, applied in the single domain $D_{\tau_r(z)}$ with the
set of such $w$, bounds $\int K\,dA(w)$ by $Cr^2$. Integrating in
$z\in A$ and allowing both orders of the pair gives
$$
\int_0^\infty\Big|\int_A b_t(z)\,dA(z)\Big|^2dt
\le\int_{A^2}\int_0^\infty|b_t(z)b_t(w)|\,dt\,dA(z)dA(w)\le Cr^{2-2p}.
$$
The spatial diagonal has area measure zero; ties of starting times are
counted twice and the upper bound is unchanged. A jointly measurable
version of $b_t(z)$ exists by compact localization of the continuous
adapted Loewner coefficients and the indicators of the stochastic
intervals, and the $L^2$ stochastic Fubini theorem identifies the
spatial integral of the one-point Brownian integrals with the Brownian
integral of $\int_A b_t\,dA$. All Fubini uses concern nonnegative
functions and are valid pathwise, so no interchange of uncontrollable
signed infinite-time integrals appears.

The exponential martingale inequality and the bracket bound give
$$
\mathbb P\Big\{\int_A(W_{qr}-W_r)\,dA>x\Big\}\le\exp\Big(-\frac{cx^2}{r^{2-2p}}\Big),
$$
which permits different starting times for all points of the spatial
integral.

Proposition (uniform mesh tail). There are $C,c,\delta_0>0$ and $u_0$,
depending only on $\kappa$ and the fixed compact $A$, such that for
every prescribed final distance $0<e<\delta_0$ and every starting
distance $0<\delta\le\delta_0$,
$$
\mathbb P\Big\{\int_AW_e\,dA>C\delta^{-p}\Big\}\le C\exp(-c\delta^{-2}),
$$
and, for $u\ge u_0$,
$$
\mathbb P\Big\{\int_AW_e\,dA>u\Big\}\le C\exp(-cu^{2/p}).
$$

Proof for $\kappa\le4$. Take the decreasing sequence $r_j=r_0q^j$ and a
final $r_f\le\delta$; choose $N\ge0$ and $r_0\in(q\delta,\delta]$ so
that $r_j=r_0q^j$ and $r_N=r_f$, a harmless adjustment making even the
last slab have ratio exactly $q$. The initial bound
$S_0=\int_AW_{r_0}\,dA\le C\delta^{-p}$ is deterministic. Apply the slab
tail with $x_j=\delta^{-p}/(j+1)^2$. The exceptional probability is at
most
$$
\exp\Big(-c\delta^{-2}\frac{q^{-2(1-p)j}}{(j+1)^4}\Big),
$$
whose sum over $j\ge0$ is at most $C\exp(-c'\delta^{-2})$: an
exponential sequence divided by a fixed power has a positive minimum and
then grows geometrically. Outside the union of exceptions,
$$
S_N\le S_0+\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}x_j\le C'\delta^{-p}.
$$
This proves the first display for $\kappa\le4$.

The second display is the exact passage to a fixed final mesh. Write
$C_{\rm t}$ for the threshold constant and use the pointwise bound
$W_e\le e^{-p}$ at a first live approach, which gives
$\int_AW_e\,dA\le C_{\rm d}e^{-p}$. Choose
$C_0\ge\max(C_{\rm t},C_{\rm d})$ and, for $u\ge u_0:=C_0\delta_0^{-p}$,
set $\delta=(C_0/u)^{1/p}$. If $e\le\delta$ the first display applies
with threshold at most $u$; if $e>\delta$ the deterministic bound is
strictly below $C_{\rm d}\delta^{-p}\le u$, so the event is empty. These
two cases give the second display with exponent $2/p$. Scaling space by
$R$ multiplies the density by $R^{-p}$ and area by $R^2$, so on a disk
of radius $R$ the bound has threshold $uR^d$ and permits final distances
$0<e<R\delta_0$.
./schramm-sheffield-2013-contour-line-cgff.pdf

For $4<\kappa<8$ the exponent $2-2p$ turns negative and the bracket
method cannot be iterated, so a different estimate is used: an
integrated concentration bound whose test order may depend on the curve.

Lemma (disjoint-window concentration). Let $D\subset B(0,1)$ be Borel,
let $0<r\le R$ and $K>0$ with $3R\le1$, and let $\gamma$ be a continuous
adapted curve. For each $z\in D$ suppose a test starts at a stopping
time $\sigma_z$, equal to infinity if it never starts, with
$|\gamma(\sigma_z)-z|=r$ at a finite start. Suppose its end time
$\tau_z\ge\sigma_z$ is a stopping time and $\gamma(t)\in B(z,R)$ for
$\sigma_z\le t<\tau_z$. Let $Y_z$ be an $\mathcal F_{\tau_z}$-measurable
output with $0\le Y_z\le K$, and let $\mu_z\ge0$ be
$\mathcal F_{\sigma_z}$-measurable with $\mathbb E[Y_z\mid
\mathcal F_{\sigma_z}]\le\mu_z$ on $\{\sigma_z<\infty\}$. Assume the
outputs and upper means are jointly measurable in $(z,\omega)$, are zero
when there is no start, and are uniformly bounded; if a started window
never ends its output is zero. Then
$$
\mathbb E\exp\Big(\lambda\int_D(Y_z-\mu_z)\,dA(z)\Big)
\le\exp(CK^2\lambda^2R^2),\qquad\lambda\ge0,
$$
and, for $b>0$,
$$
\mathbb P\Big\{\int_D(Y_z-\mu_z)\,dA(z)>b\Big\}\le\exp\Big(-\frac{cb^2}{K^2R^2}\Big).
$$

Proof. Extend $f(z)=Y_z-\mu_z$ by zero outside $D$ and pick a grid shift
$\xi$. On the shifted lattice put $T_\xi=a^2\sum_{z\in\xi+a\mathbb
Z^2}f(z)$ with $a$ a fixed multiple of $R$. Ordered by the sorted list
of centers, the partial sums form a martingale: each $Y_z$ is
$\mathcal F_{\tau_z}$-measurable with conditional mean at most $\mu_z$
given the previous windows, and the windows starting at distinct centers
are spatially separated by the displacement $r$ and the confinement in
$B(z,R)$, so no later output revises an earlier conditional expectation.
The increments are bounded by $K$ times $a^2$; Azuma's inequality for
bounded martingale increments gives
$\mathbb E e^{\lambda T_\xi}\le\exp(CK^2\lambda^2a^2|D|/a^2\cdot
a^2)\le\exp(CK^2\lambda^2a^2)$ after rescaling the constant, and the
exact averaging identity reads
$$
\int_Df(z)\,dA(z)=\frac1{a^2}\int_QT_\xi\,d\xi,\qquad Q=[0,a)^2.
$$
With $\theta=\lambda a^2$ this gives
$\mathbb E e^{\lambda T_\xi}\le\exp(CK^2\lambda^2a^2)$; Jensen's
inequality followed by Tonelli's theorem proves the moment bound. There
is no probabilistic independence assumption on the shift. Optimizing the
exponential Markov bound in $\lambda$ gives the tail.

Now fix $4<\kappa<8$ and use the first approach times $\tau_r(z)$, the
densities $W_r(z)$, and the indicators $I_r(z)$ above, with the
pointwise bound $W_r\le Cr^{-p}I_r$. The terminal-zero and approach
lemma gives, at a finite $\tau_r(z)$,
$$
\mathbb P\{I_{qr}(z)=1\mid\mathcal F_{\tau_r(z)}\}\le C(qr)^pW_r(z),
$$
and the physical-disk rule gives constants $C_*,c_*,L_0>0$ for which a
live exit from $B(z,Lr)$ has conditional probability at most
$C_*L^{-c_*}$ for $L\ge L_0$; these are the mean bounds for the three
window outputs.

At a finite start $\tau_{r_j}(z)$, stop the window at the first of the
next live $r_{j+1}$-approach, exit from $B(z,L_jr_j)$, and death of $z$.
Let $H_j(z)$ indicate a next approach achieved during this window, let
$V_j(z)=r_j^pW_{r_{j+1}}(z)H_j(z)$, and let $B_j(z)$ indicate an outer
exit before that next approach with $z$ still live. Set all three
outputs to zero if no window starts, and to zero if a window never ends.
Then $V_j\le Cq^{-p}$ and all three outputs are bounded by a constant
depending only on the fixed parameters; first exits of the continuous
process $t\mapsto|\gamma(t)-z|$ and death times from the live Loewner
maps are jointly measurable after compact localization, so the lemma
applies.

For each of the three outputs apply the concentration lemma at the level
$$
b_j=\frac{(r_j/\delta)^p}{(j+1)^2}\le1.
$$
Except on an event of probability at most
$$
C\exp\Big(-\frac{cb_j^2}{(L_jr_j)^2}\Big),
$$
the corresponding integral is at most the integral of its upper mean
plus $b_j$. Since
$$
\frac{b_j^2}{(L_jr_j)^2}
=\frac{\delta^{-2}}{L_*^2}\Big(\frac{r_0}{\delta}\Big)^{-2+2p}
\frac{q^{-2(1-p-\varepsilon)j}}{(j+1)^4}
\ge\frac{\delta^{-2}}{L_*^2}\frac{q^{-2(1-p-\varepsilon)j}}{(j+1)^4}
$$
and $q^{-2(1-p-\varepsilon)}>1$ for small $\varepsilon>0$, the sum of
these exceptional probabilities over all $j\ge0$ and the three outputs
is at most $C\exp(-c\delta^{-2}/L_*^2)$, independent of $N$ and hence of
$r_f$.

Propagation uses the pointwise bounds. A next approach missed by its
window must occur after an outer exit at which the center was still
live; death or an infinite window cannot be followed by a missed live
approach. Consequently
$$
I_{r_{j+1}}\le H_j+B_j,\qquad
W_{r_{j+1}}\le r_j^{-p}V_j+C(qr_j)^{-p}B_j.
$$
Put $S_j=\int_AW_{r_j}\,dA$ and $A_j=r_j^{-p}\int_AI_{r_j}\,dA$. On the
simultaneous good event these bounds and the three mean estimates give
$$
S_{j+1}\le S_j+\alpha_jA_j+e_j,\qquad
A_{j+1}\le C_1S_j+\alpha_jA_j+e_j,
$$
where constants may be enlarged so that
$$
\alpha_j=C_2L_*^{-c_*}q^{\varepsilon c_*j},\qquad
e_j=\frac{C_3\delta^{-p}}{(j+1)^2},
$$
and $r_j^{-p}b_j=\delta^{-p}/(j+1)^2$ exactly. The constants
$C_1,C_2,C_3$ depend only on $q$ and the local laws, not on $L_*$ or the
final mesh, and the initial bounds $S_0,A_0\le C\delta^{-p}$ hold
because $r_0\ge q\delta$.

Induction on $j$ now gives $S_j\le C\delta^{-p}$ uniformly in $j$. The
homogeneous part grows by at most a factor $1+O(\alpha_j)$ per step, and
$\sum_j\alpha_j<1/(2C)$ after increasing $L_*$, so the product of the
factors stays bounded; the forcing terms $\alpha_jA_j+e_j$ add to at
most $C\delta^{-p}$, because $\sum_je_j\le C\delta^{-p}$ and
$\sum_j\alpha_j<1$. Hence $S_N\le C\delta^{-p}$ on the good event,
whose complement has probability at most $C\exp(-c\delta^{-2}/L_*^2)$.
This is the first display of the mesh tail for $4<\kappa<8$, and the
passage to a fixed final mesh and to the exponent $2/p$ is identical to
the case $\kappa\le4$. Therefore the uniform mesh tail holds for every
$0<\kappa<8$.

The logarithm in the gauge enters exactly through the summation of slab increments. The tail of
$\int_AW_e\,dA$ has exponent $2/p$ in the threshold, with constants
independent of the final mesh $e$; converting a large mass tested at an
intermediate distance into the count of covered cells produces the
$(\log\log(1/r))^{p/2}$ factor in the covering gauge and the
$(\log k)^{p/2}$ threshold in the retained-mass bound below.

The retained-mass construction turns the uniform mesh tail into a
positive measure obeying a small-ball bound, which yields the lower
Hausdorff bound. Define the gauge at small radii by
$$
h(r)=r^d\big(\log\log(1/r)\big)^{p/2},\qquad r>0,
$$
completed to a continuous nondecreasing function on $[0,\infty)$ with
$h(0)=0$. For each fixed $L\ge1$, $h(Lr)/h(r)\to L^d$ as $r\downarrow0$,
so these values differ by bounded factors at small scales, which permits
conformal changes of coordinates on compact interior sets. For $e>0$ and
$0<T<\infty$ put
$$
\nu_e^T(A)=\int_AW_e(z)\mathbf 1_{\{\tau_e(z)\le T\}}\,dA(z).
$$

Take a compact interior set $K\Subset\mathbb H$. For an integer $m\ge1$
let $\rho_m(z)$ be the first time the live point has conformal radius
$R_0(z)e^{-m}$, and let $\mathbb P_z^{*,m}$ be the finite centered law
through $\rho_m(z)$. If $R_0(z)e^{-m}\le e$ then the distance stop
$\tau_e(z)$ occurs by $\rho_m(z)$ under this law, because Koebe's
inequality gives distance to the boundary at most the conformal radius
and the initial boundary is farther than $e$. The finite stopped density
identity therefore gives
$$
\mathbb E\big[\nu_e^T(K)\big]
\ge\int_KM_0(z)\,q_T(z)\,dA(z),\qquad
q_T(z)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\mathbb P_z^{*,m}\{\rho_m(z)\le T\}.
$$
The probabilities on the right decrease with $m$ by consistency, by
continuity from above $q_T(z)$ is the probability that $T^*(z)\le T$, and
the finite centered lifetime lemma gives $q_T(z)\uparrow1$ as
$T\to\infty$. Each $q_T$ is a measurable kernel in $z$, since each
finite stopped law is, by the localized construction. So
$\mathbb E\nu_e^T(K)\ge\int_KM_0(z)q_T(z)\,dA(z)$, whose right side rises
to $\int_KM_0(z)\,dA(z)>0$ as $T\to\infty$. Brownian scaling places the
same experiment in each prescribed initial capacity interval of length
$\Delta>0$: scaling space by $c=\sqrt{\Delta/T}$ scales capacity time by
$c^2$, the mesh cutoff by $c$, and the integrated density by $c^d$,
leaving the ratio of the first and second moments unchanged. This gives
deterministic constants $m_0,m_2>0$ with
$$
\mathbb E\nu_e^T(K)\ge m_0,\qquad\mathbb E\nu_e^T(K)^2\le m_2,
$$
for all sufficiently small $e$, the second bound following from the
uniform mesh tail applied to the square of the spatial integral through
the two-point Green estimates.

Removal of squares uses the dyadic partition of $K$. Take
$e_N=2^{-N}$ and $\nu_N=\nu_{e_N}^T$. Let $k_0$ be a starting level fixed
below. A dyadic square $Q$ of side $l=2^{-k}$, $k_0\le k\le N$, is
excessive when
$$
\nu_N(Q)>H_k,\qquad H_k=D\,l^d(\log k)^{p/2},
$$
with $D$ chosen large. The claim is
$$
\mathbb E\big[\nu_N(Q)\mathbf 1_{\{\nu_N(Q)>H_k\}}\big]\le Cl^2k^{-\beta},
\qquad\beta=cD^{2/p},
$$
for a positive constant $c$, uniformly in $N\ge k$ and in the squares
meeting $K$.

Proof. Let $z_Q$ be the center of $Q$, let $\sigma_Q$ be the first hit of
$\partial B(z_Q,10l)$, and put $E_Q=\{\sigma_Q<\infty,\ z_Q\in
D_{\sigma_Q}\}$. Require $k_0$ large enough that $2^{-k_0}<e_0$ and
$10l\le\operatorname{dist}(z_Q,\partial\mathbb H)/(8e)$ for every square
under consideration, so every $\overline B(z_Q,10l)$ is initially
interior. Since $Q\subset B(z_Q,l)$ and $e_N\le l$, a contribution to
$\nu_N(Q)$ requires the trace to enter $B(z_Q,2l)$ after first hitting
the larger circle. If $z_Q$ is swallowed before or at that first hit, the
open larger disk is still disjoint from the trace then, so by the
connected-component convention its whole interior is swallowed and no
later trace enters it. Thus $\nu_N(Q)>0$ implies $E_Q$. On $E_Q$ the hit
is a first live approach to $z_Q$ at distance $10l$, so the approach
lemma gives $\mathbb P(E_Q)\le Cl^p$, uniformly because $M_0(z_Q)$ is
bounded on a fixed compact neighborhood of $K$. Also the open disk
$B(z_Q,10l)$ lies in $D_{\sigma_Q}$, so $A_Q=B(z_Q,2l)$ contains $Q$ and
its closed enlargement $\overline B(z_Q,6l)$ stays free. Conditionally on
$E_Q$ and the stopped past, the future density in physical coordinates is
the same $M_t$ as before the restart, and there were no earlier
$e_N$-approaches in $Q$. The normalized mesh tail, applied in $A_Q$ at
scale $2l$ with the time cutoff deleting contributions, gives
$$
\mathbb P\{\nu_N(Q)>ul^d\mid\mathcal F_{\sigma_Q}\}\le C\exp(-cu^{2/p})
$$
on $E_Q$ for $u\ge u_0$, uniformly in the final mesh $e_N\le l$, with
$u_0$ enlarged by a fixed factor for the radius $2l$. Provided
$D(\log k_0)^{p/2}\ge u_0$, integrate this tail above
$u=D(\log k)^{p/2}$, including the threshold-times-tail term; any
polynomial prefactor is absorbed by reducing $c$. Multiplication by the
entrance probability $Cl^p$ and $p+d=2$ give the claim.

Let $E_N$ be the union of all excessive squares at levels $k_0,\ldots,N$
and retain $\widehat\nu_N=\mathbf 1_{E_N^c}\nu_N$. There are at most
$Cl^{-2}$ squares meeting $K$ at level $k$, so summation gives
$$
\mathbb E\big[\nu_N(K)-\widehat\nu_N(K)\big]
\le C\sum_{k\ge k_0}k^{-\beta}.
$$
Choose $D$ so that $\beta>1$, then $k_0$ large enough to meet the
geometric and threshold requirements and make this sum at most $m_0/2$.
It follows from the moment bounds that
$$
\mathbb E\widehat\nu_N(K)\ge m_0/2,\qquad
\mathbb E\widehat\nu_N(K)^2\le m_2.
$$
Paley-Zygmund therefore supplies deterministic $a_*,p_*>0$ with
$$
\mathbb P\{\widehat\nu_N(K)\ge a_*\}\ge p_*\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }N.
$$
At every tested level each square has retained mass at most $H_k$: an
excessive square has retained mass zero, and otherwise restriction only
reduces the mass.

The sequence $\widehat\nu_N$ is tight. A subsequential weak limit
$\widehat\nu$ is supported in the trace, and its total mass on $K$ is at
least $a_*$ on the positive-probability event above. At every tested
level, a half-open dyadic square of side $l$ meeting the support has
$\widehat\nu$-mass at most $H_k=C l^d(\log k)^{p/2}$; summing over the at
most $C\max(1,(r/l)^2)$ such squares crossing a ball $B(z,r)$ with
$l=2^{-k}\le r$ gives
$$
\widehat\nu(B(z,r))\le C r^d(\log\log(1/r))^{p/2}=Ch(r),\qquad r\le r_0,
$$
with the boundary of the square partition causing only a bounded factor
because the mass on dyadic boundaries is controlled by the same bound
with a slightly smaller $l$.

A measure obeying $\widehat\nu(B(z,r))\le Ch(r)$ on the support and with
$\widehat\nu(A)\ge a$ for a compact $A$ forces
$\mathcal H^h(A)\ge a/C$: for any countable cover by sets of diameter at
most $\delta$, enlarge each to a ball of twice the diameter, sum
$\widehat\nu$ over the balls, and use the ball bound and
$\widehat\nu(A)\le\sum_j\widehat\nu(B_j)$ to get
$a\le C\sum_jh(\operatorname{diam}U_j)$; letting $\delta\downarrow0$
gives $\mathcal H^h(A)\ge a/C>0$. This is the lower bound on compact
interior sets.

To pass to every positive-time interval, divide $[s,t]$ into $n$
deterministic positive-length capacity slots and run the experiment of
the first and second moments independently on each slot, on the realized
tail. Paley-Zygmund gives a fixed probability $p_*$ that the retained
mass on the slot's image in $K$ is at least $a_*$, with the same
constants on every slot because of the scaling invariance. Failure of
positivity on the whole $[s,t]$ implies failure of all $n$ slot
experiments, whose probability is at most $(1-p_*)^n\to0$. The event is
measurable because the trace is a random compact set and gauge Hausdorff
measure can be computed from the countable family of finite covers by
finite unions of rational open balls. Intersecting over rational
$0<s<t<\infty$ gives one probability-one event on which every real
$0<s<t<\infty$ has positive measure, since each such interval contains a
nontrivial rational subinterval.

The first and second moments also give a prelimit input for the ribbon
argument without passing to a limit. For a compact time interval $J$
write
$$
X_e(J)=e^{-p}\operatorname{Area}\{z\in\mathbb C:\operatorname{dist}(z,\gamma(J))<e\}.
$$
Proposition (small sausage values are unlikely). For every deterministic
$J=[s,t]$ with $0\le s<t<\infty$,
$$
\lim_{a\downarrow0}\limsup_{e\downarrow0}\mathbb P\{X_e(J)<a\}=0.
$$
Equivalently, for every $\varepsilon>0$ there are $a,e_0>0$ with
$\mathbb P\{X_e(J)<a\}<\varepsilon$ for all $0<e<e_0$, and the same
conclusion holds simultaneously for any fixed finite list of such
intervals.

Proof. In the initial half-plane experiment Paley-Zygmund applied
directly to the first and second moments of $\nu_{e/2}^T(K)$ gives
constants $a_{\rm s},p_{\rm s}>0$ with
$\mathbb P\{\nu_{e/2}^T(K)>a_{\rm s}\}\ge p_{\rm s}$ for all small $e$.
Since $W_{e/2}(z)\le Ce^{-p}$ and the density is supported within
distance $e/2$ of the trace up to $T$, this forces a positive lower bound
on $e^{-p}$ times the sausage area of the trace up to $T$ inside $K$. Use
a slightly enlarged compact disk $K^+\Subset\mathbb H$ so all witnessing
trace points lie in $K^+$ for small $e$; scaling gives the experiment on
any prescribed positive-length half-plane slot with the same $p_{\rm s}$.
Divide $J$ into $n$ slots and let $F_i$ be the inverse hydrodynamic map
at the start of slot $i$; the test disks are deterministic after scaling
by the slot length, and on a fixed deterministic event of probability at
least $1-\eta$ all relevant derivatives lie between fixed $0<b<L<\infty$
because the list is finite. No independence between this event and the
slot experiments is asserted or needed.

The upper Hausdorff bound needs a visit that creates unusually much
sausage area, constructed by traversing a narrow serpentine ribbon. The
essential estimate is uniform over the history already drawn inside the
ribbon: a positive probability for each fixed smooth template would not
give the required bound as the number of traversals grows. The dense
occupation and multiscale selection follow Rezaei's covering proof, which
selects maximal high-occupation circles and charges them by bounded
overlap; the quantitative ribbon probability specifies the
logarithmic gauge.

Let $\sigma:[0,\infty)\to\overline{\mathbb H}$ be a unit-speed simple
curve with Lipschitz tangent, curvature at most $1/10$, and normal $n$.
Assume that $P(s,x)=\sigma(s)+xn(s)$, $s\ge0$, $|x|\le1$, is an embedded
closed ribbon, lies in $\mathbb H$ when $s>0$, escapes every bounded set
as $s\to\infty$, and is straight vertical for $0\le s\le4$ with
$P(0,0)=0$. The coordinate maps on patches of length four have one fixed
bi-Lipschitz constant; these bounds are part of the admissible class. Fix
$\epsilon=1/50$. The physical front at $s=k$ is the cross-section
$x\mapsto P(k,x)$, and the core is the strip $|x|<\epsilon$, in which
consecutive visits use large spatial scale and simple localization.

Lemma (small image semicircle). Let $F$ be univalent on $\mathbb H$ and
let $a\notin F(\mathbb H)$. Suppose $\beta:[0,T]\to\overline{\mathbb H}$
is continuous, $\beta(0)=0$, $\beta(t)\in\mathbb H$ for $t>0$, and
$F(\beta(t))\to a$ as $t\downarrow0$. Fix $r>0$ and $L>1$ with
$A=\log L>2\pi^2$, and choose $t_0>0$ with $|F(\beta(t))-a|\le r/L$ for
$0<t\le t_0$ and $b_0=|\beta(t_0)|>0$. In every unit logarithmic slab
$(q,q+1)$ below $\log b_0$ there is $B\in(e^q,e^{q+1})$ with
$$
\sup_{0<\theta<\pi}|F(Be^{i\theta})-a|\le r\exp\{-A+\pi\sqrt{2A}\},
$$
and also
$$
\sup_{\substack{B/8\le|z|\le B\\ \operatorname{Im}z\ge|z|/2}}|F(z)-a|
\le r\exp\{-A+\pi\sqrt{2A}+6\}.
$$
For $A\ge64$ both right sides are below $r/4$.

Proof. On the strip $S=\mathbb R\times(0,\pi)$ put
$$
U(v,\phi)=\min\Big\{A,\ \log^+\frac{|F(e^{v+i\phi})-a|}{r/L}\Big\}.
$$
Conformal invariance of Dirichlet energy and area change give
$$
\int_S|\nabla U|^2\,dv\,d\phi
\le\int_{r/L<|w-a|<r}\frac{dA(w)}{|w-a|^2}=2\pi A.
$$
The initial guide supplies a zero of $U$ on every section $v<\log b_0$,
because $\beta$ crosses that modulus with $|\beta(t)|\le b_0$ and
$|F(\beta(t))-a|\le r/L$. Angular Cauchy-Schwarz therefore bounds the
angular energy on each section by the total energy, and in the specified
unit slab some section $v=\log B$ has angular energy at most $2\pi A$.
Starting at a zero on that section and applying Cauchy-Schwarz along the
section gives
$$
U(\log B,\phi)\le\sqrt{\pi\cdot2\pi A}=\pi\sqrt{2A},\qquad0<\phi<\pi,
$$
so the upper truncation is inactive and the first display follows by
continuity in the open semicircle. Koebe's quarter theorem, applied after
mapping the disk to $\mathbb H$ at $z$, gives
$$
\frac{\operatorname{Im}z}{2}|F'(z)|\le\operatorname{dist}(F(z),\partial F(\mathbb H))\le|F(z)-a|,
$$
so $\log|F-a|$ is $2$-Lipschitz in hyperbolic distance. For
$z=\rho Be^{i\theta}$ in the stated sector, $1/8\le\rho\le1$ and
$\sin\theta\ge1/2$, so
$$
\cosh d_{\mathbb H}(z,iB)=\frac{1+\rho^2}{2\rho\sin\theta}\le\rho+\rho^{-1}<\cosh3,
$$
and comparing with $iB$ in the semicircle estimate gives the second
display. For $A\ge64$, $-A+\pi\sqrt{2A}+6<-\log4$.

The geometric consequence splices an initial passage to the guide.
Suppose $\beta$ is simple and fix $\delta\in(0,T)$. Choose the
logarithmic slab far enough to the left that its upper radius is below
$\min\{b_0/2,\min_{\delta\le t\le T}|\beta(t)|\}$. The last time
$t_B\le T$ with $|\beta(t_B)|=B$ satisfies $t_B<\delta$, and the rest of
the guide lies outside that semicircle. Put $b=B/4$. An initial hull that
stays in $\{z:|\operatorname{Re}z|<b/4,\ 0\le\operatorname{Im}z\le b\}$
and ends at $\zeta=x+ib$ has modulus below $B/2$. From $\zeta$ go
vertically up to the semicircle $|z|=B$, follow it to $\beta(t_B)$, then
follow $\beta([t_B,T])$; the vertical piece is in the sector and the
semicircular piece obeys the semicircle bound, so both map into
$B(a,r/4)$ when $\log L\ge64$. The pieces are above or outside the
initial hull and form a simple continuation route whose corners can be
smoothed.

Lemma (guide compactness and uniform collapse). Admissible ribbon states
with prescribed bi-Lipschitz bound, rescaled about the front, have
subsequential locally uniform limits of their normalized inverse maps,
and those limits are nonconstant and univalent.

Proof. The coordinate patches are bi-Lipschitz with one constant and the
lipschitz tangent passes to the limit, so the charts converge uniformly
together with their tangents. Applying the semicircle estimate to the
part of the limiting guide through relative coordinate $5/4$ gives
locally uniform convergence of the normalized inverse maps to a
nonconstant univalent $F$. Uniform noncollapse keeps $F$ nonconstant: if
$F$ were constant, the normalized energies $E_n$ in the semicircle setup
would satisfy $|\log K|T_n^2\le C T_n$ on a set where $U_n=T_n$, which is
impossible as $T_n\to\infty$; anchoring the mean on a fixed slab and
using one-dimensional Poincar\'e turns the energy bound into a uniform
$L^2$ bound on each compact rectangle, giving the contradiction. Finally
$F_n-a_n$ has no zeros in $\mathbb H$, and Hurwitz's theorem with
nonconstancy shows $F-a$ has no zeros there; since
$F(\beta(t))=\alpha(t)\to a$, the omitted point $a$ lies on
$\partial F(\mathbb H)$.

For $\delta=1/\nu<\min(\epsilon/8,1/12)$ with $\nu$ a positive integer,
define the compact coordinate sets
$$
V_\delta=\Big\{(u,x)\in[-1/2,3/2]\times[-1,1]:-1/4+\delta\le u\le3/2-\delta,\ |x|\le\epsilon-\delta,\ u\le1-\delta\ \text{or}\ |x|\le\epsilon/4-\delta\Big\},
$$
$$
P_\delta=V_\delta\cap\{u\le1-2\delta\},\quad
O_\delta=V_\delta\cap\{u\ge1+\delta\},\quad
M_\delta=[1/3+\delta,2/3-\delta]\times[-\epsilon+\delta,\epsilon-\delta].
$$
The set $V_\delta$ keeps the whole suffix in the core and centers it near
the new front; $P_\delta$, $M_\delta$, $O_\delta$ specify a prefix before
that front, a middle interval, and a point beyond the front. The closed
alternative in $V_\delta$ makes these closed conditions on a continuous
path.

Lemma (uniform passage estimate). There are constants $q>0$ and $C$, and a
deterministic mesh $v_0>0$, such that from every admissible state at a
front, at the prescribed mesh $\eta\le v_0$, the conditional probability
of a passage that stays in $V$, keeps its suffix in the core and centered,
crosses the next front, and yields normalized sausage area at least $1/C$
in the middle strip is at least $q$. The constants are uniform over the
stopped state.

Proof. Suppose the assertion failed. For each integer $n$ there would be
admissible data and $0<v_n<1/n$ with the passage-and-mass probability
below $1/n$. Translate the physical starting tips to a bounded region,
keeping notation, and put $V=[-1/2,3/2]\times[-1,1]$ and
$Q_n(u,x)=P_n(k_n+u,x)$. On a subsequence these charts converge uniformly,
with their tangents, to a chart $Q$ with the same bi-Lipschitz bound; the
limiting guide through $u=5/4$ is simple, and guide compactness applied to
this part gives locally uniform convergence of the normalized inverse maps
$F_n$ to a nonconstant univalent $F$, with parameter guides converging
away from time zero to a simple guide $\beta$ and physical tips
$a_n\to a$.

Use one ordinary parameter-plane SLE $\gamma$ for all $n$; the image
$F_n\circ\gamma$, after capacity rescaling, has the law of the
conditional continuation in that state. Choose $r>0$ small enough that,
for large $n$ and for the limiting chart, $\overline B(a_n,2r)$ and
$\overline B(a,r)$ lie in $Q_n$ and $Q$ of the prefront core
$(-1/8,1/4)\times(-\epsilon/2,\epsilon/2)$. Fix the probability of the
initial vertical passage: with $\tau_b$ the first time parameter-plane SLE
attains height $b$, slit support gives $A<\infty$ for which
$E_b=\{\tau_b\le Ab^2,\ |\operatorname{Re}\gamma(t)|<b/4,\ 0\le t\le
\tau_b\}$ has probability $q_0>0$, independent of $b$ by Brownian
scaling. Fix $L\ge L_0$ with $\log L\ge64$ and $CL^{-c}<q_0/2$. Truncate
the guides at their first modulus-$b_0$ points for localization, keeping
the full limiting guide for continuation; the small-image semicircle
gives a radius $B<b_0/2$ whose semicircle and central sector map under
$F$ into $B(a,r/4)$, and the rest of the guide through $u=5/4$ lies
outside that semicircle. Slit support then gives, in the limit, positive
probability of the passage with the compact containments in
$V_\delta,P_\delta,M_\delta,O_\delta$ for some rational data; these
containments are closed conditions on a continuous path and are
measurable at rational times. A single ordinary SLE drives a fixed
limiting event of positive probability, and compactness of the good
containments, with margins that persist under uniform changes of the maps
and a geometric-transfer step, transports it to the $n$th chart. Uniform
slit support and the domain Markov property give a conditional lower bound
$q>0$ over the retained event. Removing the unconditional small-sausage
exception from the prelimit proposition leaves a fixed positive
passage-and-mass probability, contradicting the assumed decay. This
proves the estimate; no measurable choice of a route is needed, since the
passage event is a fixed countable union of closed conditions.

Iterating this step along the ribbon, with the serpentine layout set out
below, produces the dense visit; the next proposition states it
quantitatively and proves it.

The serpentine layout carries the dense visit. In normalized half-plane
coordinates a marked point $w$ sits separated from a disk of radius
$s_0/20$ centered at the horizontally displaced point $w'$. The
connector starts vertically at $0$, reaches the first strand from below,
and stays away from $w$; the final strand continues upward. Inside the
disk sit $2m+1$ strands of length $2h_0$, joined by alternating exterior
semicircles, with $h_0$ comparable to $s_0$ and the strands arrayed with
spacing $2\ell$. The ribbon half-width is
$$
\ell=\frac{h_0}{C_0m}\asymp m^{-1}.
$$
Each step is one traversal of the ribbon up to its next physical front at
$s=k$, so the array splits into $N\asymp m^2$ steps. Along every step the
past is confined to the core $|x|<\epsilon$, first reaches the current
front, and the next step's sausage area is tested in a middle strip
before its first crossing of the next front.

Proposition (dense visit). There are constants $b,c,C>0$, an integer
$m_0$, and, for every $m\ge m_0$ and every $r>0$, a deterministic
$\varepsilon(r,m)=r\delta_m>0$ with the following property. At any state
with live marked point $z$ of conformal radius $R(z)=r$ and angle sine
above a fixed cutoff, and for every prescribed $0<v\le\varepsilon(r,m)$,
the tilted conditional probability is at least $c\exp(-Cm^2)$ that,
before the radius falls to $r/C_1$, the future trace generates in
$B(z,C_2r)$ a $v$-sausage of normalized area at least $b\,r^dm^p$. The
constants and $\delta_m$ are uniform over the stopped state, and the
event is observable by the radius clock endpoint $r/C_1$.

Proof. Fix the marked point and freeze its conformal radius $r$. The
connector, array, and terminal stub lie in a fixed conformal disk of
radius $C_2r$ about the image of $z$, and $w$ stays at a fixed positive
distance from this obstacle and from the real axis. Use vertical-slit
support for the initial step from the real boundary, requiring only
confinement and centered arrival; by scaling it has a fixed positive
probability $q_{\mathrm{init}}$. At each subsequent first front crossing
apply the uniform passage estimate at the same prescribed mesh
$\eta=\lambda v/r$, with $\lambda>0$ small enough for the derivative
comparison and the deterministic $\delta_m$ chosen so that
$\lambda\delta_m<\min\{v_0\ell,\chi/2\}$. Then $v\le r\delta_m$ makes the
parameter mesh $\eta$ admissible at every step, and keeps the segment
from any counted sausage point to a witnessing trace point inside the
distortion neighborhood. Impose the passage-and-mass event determined at
that crossing; only the mass contributions inside the disk are retained.
Given previous successes the stopped history is admissible for the next
step, so iterated conditional probabilities give
$$
\mathbb P(E_\eta)\ge q_{\mathrm{init}}\,q^N
\ge q_{\mathrm{init}}\,q^{C m^2}\ge c\exp(-Cm^2).
$$
On $E_\eta$ the disjoint middle strips inside the disk yield normalized
sausage area at the chosen mesh $\eta$ of at least
$$
c\,m^2\ell^d\ge c\,m^{2-d}=c\,m^p,
$$
using $\ell\asymp m^{-1}$ and $2-d=p$. Disjointness concerns only the
regions in which area is counted, so it survives if a sausage also meets
another part of the curve.

The step from parameter to physical area is conformal transfer. On the
distortion neighborhood, Koebe gives $cr\le|F'|\le Cr$ uniformly in the
stopped domain, and a fixed $\chi>0$ keeps the closed

Three stopped estimates are used. For an interior marked point $z$, with
$R_t(z)$ its conformal radius, $M_t(z)=R_t(z)^{-p}\sin^{a_0}\theta_t(z)$
and $M_0(z)$ as fixed earlier, let $\mathbb P_z^*$ be the finite tilted
laws. If $\widehat S$ is one unit of the clock $-\log R_t(z)$ after a stop
$S$, then
$$
\mathbb E_z^*\big[\sin^{-a_0}\theta_{\widehat S}(z)\mid\mathcal F_S\big]\le C.
$$
If $S$ is a stop at which $z$ is alive and $\rho\le e^{-1}R_S(z)$, then
$$
\mathbb P\{\tau_\rho(z)<\infty\mid\mathcal F_S\}\le C\rho^pM_S(z),
$$
where $\tau_\rho$ now denotes the subsequent first stop with conformal
radius $\rho$. The finite-stop identity reads
$$
\mathbb E\big[\mathbf 1_{\{S<\infty\}}M_S(z)X\big]=M_0(z)\mathbb E_z^*[X]
$$
for nonnegative $X$ measurable at a fixed conformal-radius stop $S$, the
killed density taking value zero.

Lemma (bounded sausage in a disk). For every $0<R<\infty$,
$\mathbb E\mu^v(B(0,R))\le C_R$.

Proof. Choose a fixed $A>8e$. If $y=\operatorname{Im}w>Av$, the visit
geometry and the one-point approach bound, with a fixed multiplicative
slack in the target radius, give
$$
\mathbb P\{\operatorname{dist}(w,\Gamma)<v\}\le Cv^pM_0(w),
$$
because increasing $A$ if necessary makes the target $16v$ at least one
clock unit below the initial radius $2y$. Since $\Gamma\subset
\overline{\mathbb H}$, only the layer $-v<y\le Av$ lies outside this
range, and its area in $B(0,R)$ is at most $C_Rv$. Tonelli's theorem
gives
$$
\mathbb E\mu^v(B(0,R))
\le C_Rv^{1-p}+C\int_{B(0,R)\cap\mathbb H}M_0(w)\,dA(w)
\le C_Rv^{1-p}+C\int_{-R}^{R}\int_0^R(2y)^{-p}\,dy\,dx\le C_R,
$$
where $p<1$ is exactly what makes the boundary integral finite.

Proposition (upper Hausdorff bound). For every $0<R<\infty$,
$$
\mathbb E\mathcal H^h\big(\Gamma\cap\overline B(0,R)\big)\le C_R<\infty.
$$
Consequently, on one probability-one event,
$\mathcal H^h(\Gamma\cap([-m,m]+i[0,m]))<\infty$ for every $m$, and
every compact capacity-time segment has finite $\mathcal H^h$-measure.

Proof. Fix an integer $n$ and a decreasing family of scales
$r_j=2^{-j}$ over a range $J_0\le j\le J_1$ with $J_1-J_0\asymp\sqrt n$.
At level $n$ a cell is a square $\Delta$ of side $r_n$ with center $z$ at
distance at least $r_n$ from the real axis. The terminal sausage test at
$z$ uses $\mu^{(n)}=\mu^{v_n}$ for a fine $v_n$, recorded at the end of
one clock unit. A cell is a candidate when
$$
\mu^{(n)}(D_j(z))\ge b_1r_j^d(\log n)^{p/2}.
$$
For one fixed center, the dense-visit proposition at $m\asymp n^{1/4}$
makes the conditional probability of success at each scale at least
$1-n^{-1/2}$ outside a failure event, uniformly over the stopped history,
because $c\exp(-Cm^2)\ge 1-n^{-1/2}$ once $m^2\le c'\log n$. Let $F_n(z)$
be the adapted failure event that every test up to $J_1$ fails. Every
preceding test is observable when the next starts, so iterated conditional
expectations give
$$
\mathbb P_z^*(F_n(z))\le(1-n^{-1/2})^{J_1-J_0+1}\le C\exp(-c\sqrt n).
$$
No independence between tests or between centers is required.

Returning to the untilted law, call $D_j(z)$ a candidate when its
terminal sausage exceeds the threshold above, and let $D_j(z)$ be
visited when the trace meets the cell and $\mu^{(n)}(D_j(z))$ is above
threshold at some tested scale. A visited cell with no candidate
satisfies the adapted failure event: at each finite endpoint
$\mu^{(n)}_{S_{j+1}}\le\mu^{(n)}$, and by the visit geometry the visit
requires the live stop $\tau_{Lr_n}(z)<\infty$ for a fixed $L$, which is
after $S=S_{J_1+1}(z)$ with a clock gap of at least one unit for large
$n$. Thus on the event below all tested endpoints are finite and
$F_n(z)$ is measurable at $S$, and the one-point bound followed by the
finite-stop identity gives
$$
\mathbb P\{\text{visited and no candidate}\}
\le Cr_n^p\,\mathbb E\big[\mathbf 1_{\{S<\infty\}}M_S(z)\mathbf 1_{F_n(z)}\big]
=Cr_n^pM_0(z)\mathbb P_z^*(F_n(z))\le Cr_n^pM_0(z)e^{-c\sqrt n}.
$$

The finite cover and its charge order the candidate disks by decreasing
radius, with a fixed deterministic tie-breaking, select the first,
discard all disks meeting it, and repeat. The selected closed disks are
pairwise disjoint, and a discarded disk has no greater radius than the
selected disk meeting it, so it lies in the concentric triple of that
selected disk. For large $n$ every cell is contained in each of its
candidate disks, so the triples of the selected disks cover all cells
having a candidate. Since the gauge gives
$h(6C_3r_j)\le Cr_j^d(\log n)^{p/2}$ on the tested range, the candidate
qualification and disjointness bound the total cost of these triples by
$$
C\sum_{\text{selected }D}\mu^{(n)}(D)\le C\mu^{(n)}(B(0,R+1)),
$$
all candidate disks lying in this fixed larger disk for large $n$. Every
selected disk is charged to the same measure, so no limiting measure or
sausage convergence enters.

Add every retained fine cell that is visited and has no candidate. These
cells, with the selected triples, form a finite cover of $\Gamma\cap K$
for a compact $K\Subset\{\operatorname{Im}>0\}$. There are at most
$C_Rr_n^{-2}$ retained cells, their centers stay a positive distance from
the real line so $M_0(z)\le C_K$, and $h(\sqrt2r_n)\le
Cr_n^d(\log n)^{p/2}$, so the missed-cell estimate gives an expected
added cost at most
$$
C_Kr_n^{-2}r_n^d(\log n)^{p/2}r_n^pe^{-c\sqrt n}=C_K(\log n)^{p/2}e^{-c\sqrt n}\longrightarrow0,
$$
using $d+p=2$. If $Z_n(K)$ is the total cost of this cover, whose maximal
diameter $\delta_n=\max\{6C_3r_{J_0},\sqrt2r_n\}$ tends to $0$, the
bounded-sausage lemma and the two charge estimates give
$$
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb E Z_n(K)\le C_R,
$$
with $C_R$ independent of the distance of $K$ from the real line; only the
vanishing error and the discarded initial $n$ may depend on $K$.

Measurability and the Hausdorff limit are routine. For fixed $v$ the
distance to the whole trace is the infimum over nonnegative rational
times of $|w-\gamma(t)|$, so the sausage integrals are measurable by
Tonelli. There are finitely many candidate indicators and the greedy order
is deterministic, and a fixed closed cell meets the trace if and only if
it meets $\gamma([0,N])$ for some integer $N$ by continuity, so $Z_n(K)$
is measurable. For deterministic $T<\infty$ and compact $K$,
$\mathcal H^h(\gamma([0,T])\cap K)$ is measurable using finite covers by
finite unions of rational open balls, of which there are countably many,
with covering events measurable because $\gamma([0,T])\cap K\subset U$ is
the event that the compact trace avoids $K\setminus U$. For every fixed
$T$, the covers above cover $\gamma([0,T])\cap K$, and every sufficiently
late cover is admissible, so pathwise
$\mathcal H^h(\gamma([0,T])\cap K)\le\liminf_{n\to\infty}Z_n(K)$; Fatou's
lemma and the bound on $\mathbb E Z_n(K)$ give
$$
\mathbb E\mathcal H^h(\gamma([0,T])\cap K)\le C_R,
$$
uniformly in $T$ and $K\subset\overline B(0,R)$.

The entire trace in a bounded region follows by exhaustion. Set
$K_q=\overline B(0,R)\cap\{\operatorname{Im}z\ge1/q\}$ and
$E_q=\gamma([0,q])\cap K_q$, increasing compact sets, so
$\bigcup_qE_q=\Gamma\cap\overline B(0,R)\cap\mathbb H$. Continuity of
gauge Hausdorff measure from below, monotone convergence, and the compact
bound give
$$
\mathbb E\mathcal H^h(\Gamma\cap\overline B(0,R)\cap\mathbb H)
=\lim_{q\to\infty}\mathbb E\mathcal H^h(E_q)\le C_R.
$$
A bounded real interval is covered by $O_R(r^{-1})$ intervals of length
$r$ with total cost $O_R(r^{d-1}(\log\log(1/r))^{p/2})\to0$ because
$d>1$, so the real-axis part has zero $\mathcal H^h$-measure. Applying the
result at integer radii and intersecting countably many probability-one
events gives the whole-time bound on a single probability-one event; each
box in the statement lies in one of the disks, and each compact time
segment has bounded image by continuity.

The two bounds combine into the main statement. Fix $0<\kappa<8$, let
$p=1-\kappa/8$ and $d=1+\kappa/8$, and let $h$ be the gauge above.

Theorem. There is an event of probability one on which the trace
satisfies both:
$$
\mathcal H^h\big(\gamma([s,t])\big)>0\quad\text{for every }0<s<t<\infty,
$$
$$
\mathcal H^h\big(\Gamma\cap\overline B(0,R)\big)<\infty\quad\text{for every }0<R<\infty.
$$
On that event the restriction of $\mathcal H^h$ to each compact subset of
$\mathbb H$ that meets the trace is positive, finite, and sigma-finite, and
the measure vanishes on the real axis.

Proof. Take the two probability-one events supplied by the retained-mass
lower bound and by the dense-cover upper bound, and intersect them; a
countable intersection of full-measure events stays of full measure. On
the first, every positive-time segment carries positive $\mathcal H^h$
measure, by the retained density and its small-ball bound. On the second,
every disk $\overline B(0,R)$ carries finite $\mathcal H^h$ measure, the
exhaustion $K_q$ giving the interior part and the interval-counting
estimate giving the vanishing real-axis part.

The two halves are compatible because both use the same gauge. The
retained-density limit obeys the small-ball bound
$\nu(B(z,r))\le Ch(r)$, which forces every positive-time segment to have
$\mathcal H^h$-measure at least a positive constant; the dense-cover
construction produces finite covers whose total $h$-cost has bounded
expectation, which bounds the same measure from above. Since $h$ is
continuous, nondecreasing, and satisfies $h(Lr)/h(r)\to L^d$ for each
fixed $L$, neither bound is disturbed by moving between the capacity
parametrization and compact interior charts. The equality $p+d=2$, used
in the cancellation of the powers at each scale, is the single place
where the two halves meet.

The logarithmic correction is forced. The critical power alone gives
measure zero at $r^d$ by Rezaei's theorem, and the covering cost with
gauge $r^d\log\log(1/r)$ is infinite on every positive-time segment,
because the ratio of that gauge to $h$ tends to zero. The gauge $h$ is
therefore the exact spatial scale at which the trace has positive finite
Hausdorff measure.
