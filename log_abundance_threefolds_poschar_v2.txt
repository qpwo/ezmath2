# Log abundance in numerical dimension onefor threefolds in positive characteristic



maketitle

Abstract: 
Let k be an algebraically closed field of characteristic p>3, and let
(X,B) be a projective log canonical threefold pair over k with
effective rational boundary B. If the log canonical divisor K_X+B is
nef and has numerical dimension one, then it is semiample: some positive
Cartier multiple is generated by its global sections. Neither
terminality nor Q-factoriality is assumed for the original X. The technical engine is a structural
result of the companion paper: a certain kind of nef cycle cannot exist
on a smooth threefold. The contribution here is to build that cycle from
an arbitrary log canonical pair, controlling the boundary throughout.

# Overview
addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Overview}

This paper proves a case of the abundance conjecture in algebraic
geometry, in positive characteristic. The result is advanced, but the
shape of the argument can be described in ordinary language.

The goal.: 
A threefold is a space of dimension three; over the complex
numbers it looks locally like C^3. To such a space and an effective
boundary divisor B one associates the log canonical
divisor K_X+B, a bookkeeping object recording how differential forms
twist. A divisor is nef if it never meets a curve negatively;
think of it as a direction in which the space does not bend downward.
We assume K_X+B is nef and has numerical dimension one. This
means K_X+B is positive in exactly one direction and numerically zero
in the other two.

Abundance predicts that some positive multiple of K_X+B has enough
global sections to define a map to a curve. We prove exactly that: some
positive Cartier multiple of K_X+B is globally generated, so K_X+B
is semiample and the associated map lands on a curve.

Why this is hard.: 
The hypothesis only says the divisor is not negative and is positive in
one direction. It does not by itself produce a single section upstairs.
The proof must rule out a hidden bad case: maximal nef dimension.
If that bad case occurred, the positive direction would be locally
invisible, so that no curve of degree zero would pass through a very
general point. The body of the paper derives a contradiction from this
assumption.

The contradiction in one sentence.: 
In the bad case one constructs a connected nef cycle D on a smooth
projective threefold V, living near a log canonical component, with a
torsion normal bundle and a local relation between a power of the
canonical bundle and D. The companion paper proves that no such cycle
can exist: its torsion normal line and canonical relation make the
Picard group of the formal neighborhood divisible by every power of
p, while intersection with an ample divisor supplies a nonzero
integer-valued degree, and a p-divisible group admits no nonzero map
to Z. Contradiction.

# Introduction


## The theorem

Throughout, a variety is integral and separated of finite type
over a field. A pair (X,B) has X normal and B an effective
Q-divisor with K_X+B Q-Cartier. We use the standard discrepancy
definitions of terminal, klt, dlt, and lc singularities; see
[cite]. For a nef Q-Cartier divisor L on a threefold, we say its
numerical dimension is one when, for an ample Cartier divisor
H,

L H^2>0
 and 
L^2 H=0.



Let k be an algebraically closed field of characteristic p>3. Let
(X,B) be a projective log canonical threefold pair over k, where
X is normal, B is an effective Q-divisor, and K_X+B is
Q-Cartier. If K_X+B is nef and has numerical dimension one, then
K_X+B is semiample.


The boundary coefficients are arbitrary rationals allowed by log
canonicity. Neither terminality nor Q-factoriality is assumed for the
original variety. The theorem proves the numerical-dimension-one case of
threefold log abundance in this characteristic range.

Geometrically, semiampleness gives a morphism f:Xto C with connected
fibers to a normal projective curve together with an ample divisor A
on C such that m(K_X+B)sim f^*A for some m>0. The target is a
curve because the numerical dimension is one; Stein factorization
produces f from any globally generated pluricanonical multiple.

## Background and context

Over the complex numbers, Miyaoka proved abundance for minimal
threefolds of numerical dimension one [cite], and Kawamata gave
another proof and completed abundance for minimal threefolds
[cite]. Keel, Matsuki, and McKernan established log abundance
for log canonical threefold pairs [cite].

In positive characteristic, Keel's semiampleness criterion [cite]
and the basepoint-freeness methods of Cascini, Tanaka, and Xu [cite]
provided the foundations. The threefold minimal model program was
developed by Hacon--Xu [cite], Birkar [cite], and
Birkar--Waldron [cite] in characteristic greater than five, with log
canonical and relative extensions by Waldron [cite] and
Hashizume--Nakamura--Tanaka [cite], and the characteristic-five
case by Hacon--Witaszek [cite].

For abundance itself, Xu--Zhang proved nonvanishing for terminal minimal
threefolds in characteristic greater than five [cite], and Witaszek
obtained klt nonvanishing and abundance in nef dimension at most two in
that range [cite]. Zheng Xu proved nonvanishing for
pseudoeffective log canonical divisors on projective lc threefold pairs
in characteristic greater than three, together with abundance in nef
dimension at most two cite[Theorems~1.4 and~1.8]{XuNonvanishing}, and
later proved abundance in numerical dimension two [cite].
The case left open, and treated here, has numerical dimension one and
maximal nef dimension three.

The companion paper [cite] treats terminal threefolds whose
nef canonical divisor has numerical dimension one. Its proof isolates a
stronger structural fact: a certain connected nef cycle cannot exist on
a smooth threefold. We state this cycle obstruction in
Theorem~thm:obstruction, with all its hypotheses. The present paper
constructs such a cycle from a log canonical pair with arbitrary rational
boundary; the obstruction is an essential input, not reproved here.

## The proof strategy

After an uncountable field extension and a crepant birational change, we
may suppose the underlying variety is terminal and Q-factorial.
Nonvanishing then gives a nonzero effective Cartier divisor
N_0sim m(K_X+B). We assume for contradiction that the nef dimension
of N_0 is three. The new difficulty, compared with the companion, is to
obtain a relation for a power of the canonical bundle near a component of
this log canonical divisor, controlling both the boundary and the
exceptional divisors of a resolution.

Proposition~prop:separation handles the boundary. It proves
B N_0 H=0 and shows that every boundary component is either contained
in Supp N_0 or disjoint from it. If the intersection were positive,
complete-intersection curves would have arbitrarily small ratios of
N_0-degree to anticanonical degree, and quantitative bend-and-break
cite[Theorem~1.1]{JLR} would then produce N_0-trivial rational curves
through very general points.

Next, adapting cite[Section~2.3]{Companion}, we raise the boundary to
its log canonical threshold along N_0, obtaining a coefficient-one
component on a dlt model. A partial MMP, trivial for the resulting full
log canonical class, makes that component disjoint from the rest of the
divisor. Reduced-boundary adjunction and surface abundance
[cite] then yield torsion on a Cartier multiple of the
component, and the companion's extension argument passes from the reduced
surface to the full nonreduced Cartier thickening.

Finally, terminality makes the singular locus finite, so the exceptional
divisors of a common resolution obey the same support separation as the
boundary. A finite separable cover removes the prime-to-p part of the
pluricanonical exponent. A connected component of the pulled-back Cartier
divisor, divided by the gcd of its multiplicities, has exactly the
numerical, torsion, and canonical properties forbidden by
Theorem~thm:obstruction. This contradiction completes the proof.

# The cycle obstruction and the initial reductions


We first state precisely the input taken from the companion paper
[cite]. We then reduce Theorem~thm:main to the task of
constructing the forbidden cycle. Boundaries are effective
Q-divisors, and canonical divisors on birational models are chosen
compatibly. Restrictions and intersections of Q-Cartier divisors are
computed using Cartier multiples.

## The obstruction supplied by the companion

For a nef divisor L over an uncountable algebraically closed field,
its nef dimension n(L) is the dimension of the target of its
nef reduction. In dimension three we use only the following
characterization: n(L)=3 if and only if no curve of L-degree zero
passes through a very general point, where very general means
outside a countable union of proper closed subsets. These properties and
the existence of the nef reduction are recalled in
cite[Definition~2.1 and Theorem~2.2]{XuNuTwo}. Numerical dimension and
nef dimension need not agree; both appear below.

[Cycle obstruction of [cite]]
Let k be an uncountable algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>3. There do not exist a smooth projective threefold V, a very
ample Cartier divisor H_V, and a nonzero effective Cartier divisor
D=sum_alpha m_alpha D_alpha satisfying all of the following:
[label=(roman*)]
 the D_alpha are distinct prime divisors, m_alphainZ_{>0},
the support of D is connected with simple normal crossings, and
gcd_alpha m_alpha=1;
 D is nef, its restriction to every D_alpha is numerically
trivial, and
[
D H_V^2>0, D^2 H_V=0, n(D)=3;
]
 the line bundle O_D(D) on the possibly nonreduced Cartier
scheme D is torsion;
 for some integer bge0 and integers b_alpha, there is an
isomorphism on a neighborhood of Supp D,

omega_V^{ ⊗  p^b}~=
O_V(sum_alpha b_alpha D_alpha).




Theorem~thm:obstruction is the structural statement proved in
cite[Sections~3--6]{Companion} for the data of its Proposition~2.1. The
end of Section~2 of that paper retains only V, D, H_V, the torsion
order, and the local canonical relation. Under these hypotheses its
Sections~3--5 prove Corollary~5.4, and its Proposition~6.8 gives the
contradiction. Hence the obstruction applies to any cycle with the
displayed properties; in particular, the construction below may start
from a log canonical divisor with boundary.

For orientation, the companion uses the torsion normal line and the
local canonical relation to control differential forms on the completion
along D, with poles allowed along D. Cartier descent then makes the
Picard group of the punctured formal neighborhood divisible by every
power of p. The numerical conditions give a nonzero integer-valued
degree homomorphism on that same group, and a p-divisible group admits
no nonzero homomorphism to Z. We take this as a theorem and supply
all its input data for a log canonical pair.

The canonical relation also implies an intersection identity used later
in the companion. A rational section realizing
(eq:canonical-cycle) has global divisor
sum b_alpha D_alpha+E with Supp E disjoint from Supp D. Since
D|_{D_alpha}equiv0, for every Cartier divisor A on V we get

K_V D A=0.


## Elementary numerical facts

The next observation converts zero intersection numbers into restrictions
that are numerically trivial.


Let T be an integral projective surface, H an ample Cartier divisor,
and P a nef Q-Cartier divisor on T. If P H=0, then
Pequiv0. Moreover, a nonzero effective Cartier divisor on T has
positive intersection with H.

Proof. 
For any integral curve Csubset T, a sufficiently large multiple of
H has an effective Cartier member E containing C; the effective
one-cycle of E has positive coefficient along C. Every component of
that cycle has nonnegative P-degree, while PE=0, so PC=0. This
proves the first assertion. The second is positivity of the ample degree
of each component of the effective one-cycle of a nonzero Cartier
divisor.


In particular, if N is a nonzero effective nef Cartier divisor on a
projective threefold and N^2H=0 for an ample Cartier divisor H, then

N|_Tequiv0 for every prime component T of N.

Indeed, writing N=sum a_T T with a_T>0, the nonnegative numbers
NHT have positive-coefficient sum N^2H=0, so each NHT=0, and
Lemma~lem:surface-nef gives N|_Tequiv0.

## Reduction to a terminal underlying variety

We use three results of Xu for projective lc threefolds in characteristic
greater than three: nonvanishing for a pseudoeffective log canonical
divisor, termination of flips in the pseudoeffective case, and abundance
when the divisor is nef of nef dimension at most two
cite[Theorems~1.4, 1.5, and~1.8]{XuNonvanishing}. All permit rational
boundaries. Relative MMPs and crepant Q-factorial dlt modifications
are available in this range, including characteristic five
cite[Theorem~1.2]{HW}, cite[Theorems~2.20 and~2.27]{XuNuTwo}. We also
use projective resolution of threefolds preserving the regular locus,
and resolution of divisor supports preserving their complement on a
regular variety; the required forms are recalled from
[cite] in cite[Section~2.1]{Companion}.


To prove Theorem~thm:main it suffices to rule out the following
data: an uncountable algebraically closed field k of characteristic
p>3, a terminal Q-factorial projective threefold X, an lc pair
(X,B), and a nonzero effective Cartier divisor

N_0sim m(K_X+B), minZ_{>0},

such that mK_X and mB are Cartier, and N_0 is nef with
nu(N_0)=1 and n(N_0)=3.

Proof. 
First extend the field to an uncountable algebraically closed one.
Integrality and normality persist, and a log resolution with its
discrepancy computation shows the base-changed pair is still lc. The
canonical divisor is defined by a rational top form on the smooth locus,
so the log canonical class also base changes. Nefness persists by ample
approximation, and the prescribed intersection numbers are unchanged.

Semiampleness descends along this extension. After making a generated
multiple divisible by the original Cartier index, it is the base change
of a line bundle on the original variety. Global sections commute with
field extension, and faithful flatness detects surjectivity of the
evaluation map. We may thus work over the enlarged field.

Write L=K_X+B. If n(L)le2, abundance in nef dimension at most two
applies. Suppose n(L)=3. Take a crepant projective Q-factorial dlt
modification (X_d,B_d) and resolve X_d projectively. Run a relative
canonical MMP of this resolution over X_d; the MMP with scaling applies
by cite[Theorem~1.2]{HW}. Its output is not of fiber type over the
three-dimensional base, and it gives a projective birational morphism
g:X_tto X_d with X_t terminal and Q-factorial and K_{X_t} nef
over X_d. Terminality is preserved by a canonical MMP: discrepancies do
not decrease, and a divisor contracted in a negative divisorial step has
strictly positive ordinary discrepancy over the output. Relative
pseudoeffectivity causes no obstruction for this birational morphism,
since adding a large pullback of an ample divisor makes the canonical
class big.

The exceptional divisor F=K_{X_t}-g^*K_{X_d} is g-nef, so the
negativity lemma gives Fle0. Since X_d is Q-factorial, the
divisor B_t=g^*B_d-F is effective and satisfies
K_{X_t}+B_t=g^*(K_{X_d}+B_d). Crepancy makes (X_t,B_t) lc.

The new log canonical class is nef of numerical dimension one and nef
dimension three. For the square, note that L^2T=0 for every prime
surface T on the original variety: place T in an effective member of
a multiple of the ample divisor in the hypothesis and use nonnegativity
of L^2 on its components. The projection formula gives the vanishing
upstairs. Numerical nontriviality persists because a curve downstairs is
dominated by a curve upstairs, and the no-zero-degree-curve property
persists over the birational isomorphism locus.

Relabel this pair as (X,B). By nonvanishing
cite[Theorem~1.4]{XuNonvanishing}, a positive multiple of K_X+B has an
effective representative N_0; it is nonzero because K_X+B is not
numerically trivial. Increasing the multiple makes N_0, mK_X, and
mB Cartier, as required.


From now until the proof of Theorem~thm:main we work with the data
of Proposition~prop:reduction. Fix a very ample divisor H on X.
Then N_0H^2>0, N_0^2H=0, and (eq:restriction-zero) holds. The
next task is to establish the same numerical triviality on the boundary
components.

# Separating the boundary from the log canonical cycle


The numerical relation N_0sim m(K_X+B) controls the log
canonical divisor, whereas Theorem~thm:obstruction needs a relation
for a power of the canonical bundle. The gap is the boundary B.
We now show that the boundary contributes only components of the cycle or
divisors entirely disjoint from it. The argument uses maximal nef
dimension and quantitative bend-and-break, and does not involve the MMP.

[Boundary separation]
Let X be a normal Q-factorial projective threefold over an
uncountable algebraically closed field, and let B be an effective
Q-divisor. Suppose N is a nonzero effective nef Cartier divisor with
nu(N)=1, n(N)=3, and Nsim_{Q}a(K_X+B) for some rational a>0.
Then for every ample Cartier divisor H one has

B N H=0.

Consequently N is numerically trivial on each prime component of B,
and every such component is either contained in Supp N or disjoint
from Supp N.

Proof. 
Replace H by a very ample multiple. The number BNH is nonnegative.
If it were positive, then B has a component on which N is not
numerically trivial, and the identity
(K_X+B)NH=N^2H/a=0 would give K_X N H<0.

For each sufficiently large integer l, choose positive integers
u_l,v_l such that u_lH and v_l(H+lN) are very ample. The general
complete intersection of members of these two linear systems is an
integral curve C_l contained in the smooth locus of X. Indeed,
normality makes the singular locus of X at most one-dimensional; a
general member of the first system meets that locus in finitely many
points, and a general member of the second avoids them. Bertini on the
smooth open then gives the integral curve. Its intersection numbers are
[
NC_l=u_l v_l,NH^2,
-K_XC_l=u_l v_l(-K_XH^2-lK_XNH),
]
so

(NC_l)/(-K_XC_l)
=(NH^2)/(-K_XH^2-lK_XNH)→0,

and -K_XC_l>0 for large l.

The quantitative bend-and-break theorem of Jovinelly, Lehmann, and Riedl
cite[Theorem~1.1]{JLR} applies to an integral curve in the smooth locus
of a projective variety over an algebraically closed field, with negative
canonical degree, and any nef Cartier test divisor. In dimension three it
supplies, through every closed point of C_l, a rational curve Gamma
with
[
0le NGammale4,(NC_l)/(-K_XC_l).
]
Fix l so large that the right-hand side is less than one. Since N is
Cartier, it follows that NGamma=0.

These complete intersections pass through points to which the
very-general-point condition applies. For the two fixed very ample
systems, the incidence of a point of X and a pair of members through it
is a product of projective bundles over X, hence irreducible and
dominating X. Restricting to the nonempty open set of pairs giving the
curves just considered leaves a dense open incidence and a dominant
evaluation map. Its image is constructible and contains a nonempty open
subset of X. Over the uncountable field this open contains a closed
point outside the countable union excluded by n(N)=3. The curve
Gamma through that point is a curve of N-degree zero through a very
general point, contradicting n(N)=3. This proves
(eq:boundary-zero).

Write B=sum b_T T with b_T>0. All numbers NHT are nonnegative, so
(eq:boundary-zero) gives NHT=0 for each T, and
Lemma~lem:surface-nef gives N|_Tequiv0. If T is not a
component of N, the canonical section of O_X(N) restricts to a
nonzero section on the integral surface T. Were TcapSupp N
nonempty, its zero scheme would be a nonzero effective Cartier divisor on
T, with positive ample degree, contradicting N|_Tequiv0. Hence the
two supports are disjoint.


Apply Proposition~prop:separation to N_0 and set

P_{out}=
∪_{substack{T a prime component of BTnotsubsetSupp N_0}}T.

This is a closed subset disjoint from Supp N_0. Thus
Supp BsubsetSupp N_0cup P_{out} is a union of two disjoint
closed sets. We keep this separation while isolating one component of
N_0 in the next section.

# Isolating a log canonical component


The effective divisor N_0 may have several intersecting components. We
now make one of them disjoint from the others while keeping the pullback
of the nef divisor. The construction adapts cite[Section~2.3]{Companion}:
raise the boundary to a log canonical threshold, lower the coefficient of
one component, and run an MMP that is trivial for the restored log
canonical divisor. The resulting nefness forces the desired disjointness.


Let k be an algebraically closed field of characteristic p>3. Let X
be a terminal Q-factorial projective threefold, let (X,B) be log
canonical with rational boundary, and let 0ne N_0sim m(K_X+B) be an
effective nef Cartier divisor, with m a positive integer. Assume N_0
is numerically trivial on each component of its support, and every
component of B is contained in or disjoint from Supp N_0. Let
P_{out} be the union of the latter components and put
[
U_X=X_{reg}∖(Supp N_0cup P_{out}).
]
Then there exist a projective Q-factorial klt threefold Y birational
to X, a rational boundary G_Y, an effective nef Q-Cartier divisor
N_Y, and a prime divisor S_Y with the following properties.
 The pair (Y,G_Y) is log canonical, S_Y has coefficient one in
G_Y, and s:=mult_{S_Y}N_Y is positive. For every
rational delta>0,

K_Y+G_Ysim_{Q}tau N_Y
 and 
K_Y+G_Y-delta S_Y is nef,

for tau>0 specified at the end.
 The divisor N_Y is numerically trivial on every component of its
support, and every component of G_Y is contained in or disjoint from
Supp N_Y.
 The divisor S_Y is disjoint from every other component of N_Y
and from Supp(G_Y-S_Y). In particular,

N_Y=sS_Y, G_Y=S_Y,
K_Y+S_Ysim_{Q}tau s,S_Y

on a neighborhood of S_Y, and S_Y|_{S_Y}equiv0.
 The birational correspondence identifies U_X with an open subset
of Y. On a smooth projective common resolution q:V_0to X,
r:V_0to Y that is an isomorphism over this open,

q^*N_0=r^*N_Y,

and the same equality holds after passing to a further common resolution.

One may take tau=m^{-1}+lambda, where
lambda=lct_{(X,B)}(N_0)ge0.


Proof. 
A coefficient-one component over Supp N_0.
On a log resolution of (X,B+N_0) the log canonical threshold is the
minimum of the finitely many ratios
[
(a(E,X,B))/(ord_E(N_0))
(ord_E(N_0)>0),
]
where a(E,X,B) is the log discrepancy and the divisors considered
include the strict transforms of the components of N_0. Thus lambda
is finite and rational, possibly zero, and (X,B+lambda N_0) is log
canonical. There is a log canonical place E of this pair with
ord_E(N_0)>0.

Take a projective crepant Q-factorial dlt modification
mu:(W,G)to(X,B+lambda N_0) with every extracted divisor of
coefficient one cite[Theorem~2.27]{XuNuTwo}. Set
[
N=mu^*N_0, tau=m^{-1}+lambda.
]
The relation

K_W+Gsim_{Q}tau N

holds since mu is crepant. The divisor N is nef and numerically
trivial on every component of its support: a curve in that support maps
to a point or to a curve in Supp N_0, and the projection formula
finishes.

Support separation on W.
Every component of G is contained in or disjoint from Supp N. For
strict transforms of components of B this is the hypothesis, while
components coming from lambda N_0 are contained in the support. An
extracted divisor has center in Supp N_0cup P_{out}, since
X is klt and the boundary vanishes outside this union; its irreducible
center lies in one of these two disjoint closed sets. In the first case
its order on N_0 is positive, so it is contained in Suppmu^*N_0; in
the second it is disjoint from that support.

The center on W of the place E lies in Supp N and, by crepancy and
the dlt property, in floor( G). Choose a component S of
floor( G) containing this center. The preceding separation gives
SsubsetSupp N. This covers lambda=0 as well; it does not require
all components of N to occur in G.

The modification is an isomorphism off the support.
The map mu has no exceptional divisors outside
Supp N_0cup P_{out}, and a projective small birational
morphism to a Q-factorial variety is an isomorphism. To see the latter,
push down a relatively ample Cartier divisor. A Cartier multiple of its
pushdown pulls back to the same multiple upstairs, since there are no
exceptional divisors; it has degree zero on every contracted curve,
contradicting relative ampleness if such a curve existed.

The partial MMP.
Choose a rational ε>0 small enough that

G-ε Sge0, tau N-ε Sge0.

Apply the partial MMP with lowered boundary G-ε S and restoration
divisor ε S, in the form of
cite[Proposition~2.21, Definition~2.23, and Lemma~2.24]{XuNuTwo}. Its
steps are (K_W+G-ε S)-negative and (K_W+G)-trivial. The lowered
pair is dlt and its log canonical divisor is pseudoeffective by
(eq:isolation-restored) and (eq:isolation-effective), so the
required termination is supplied by cite[Theorem~1.5]{XuNonvanishing}.
The threefold MMP package used here, including in characteristic five, is
recalled in cite[Section~2.3]{Companion}.

The partial-MMP conclusion is either a Mori fiber contraction for the
lowered divisor, trivial for the restored divisor, or nefness of the
restored divisor minus delta times the transform of S for every small
rational delta>0. We track S, the pullbacks of N, and support
separation through the steps, and then exclude the fiber alternative.

Tracking.
Write W_idashrightarrow W_{i+1} for a step, with transforms
G_i,N_i,S_i. The equality K_{W_i}+G_isim_{Q}tau N_i persists by
pushforward, so the contracted ray R_i satisfies

N_i· R_i=0, S_i· R_i>0.

Thus S_i cannot be the exceptional divisor of a divisorial step: the
negativity lemma would then give a contracted curve of negative
S_i-degree. Hence S survives throughout the MMP.

Let a:Tto W_i and b:Tto W_{i+1} be a common resolution of a step.
The divisor F=a^*N_i-b^*N_{i+1} is b-exceptional. If a curve on T is
contracted by b, its image on W_i is a point or is a curve contracted
to the common target, so F has degree zero on every such curve by
(eq:isolation-ray). Applying the negativity lemma to F and -F
gives

a^*N_i=b^*N_{i+1}.

This covers divisorial contractions and flips. The same argument for the
full log divisors gives
a^*(K_{W_i}+G_i)=b^*(K_{W_{i+1}}+G_{i+1}), so the restored pair stays
log canonical.

Equation~(eq:isolation-step-pullback) preserves nefness and
numerical triviality on the support. For the latter, lift a curve in
Supp N_{i+1} to a curve on T dominating it. Its image on W_i lies
in Supp N_i or is a point, because the inverse image of the support of
an effective Q-Cartier divisor is exactly the support of its pullback.
The projection formula gives degree zero. A boundary component contained
in Supp N_i stays contained in the transformed support. A component
disjoint from Supp N_i has strict transform on T disjoint from
a^*N_i, hence from b^*N_{i+1}, and since it maps onto its image on
W_{i+1}, the transformed component is disjoint from Supp N_{i+1}.

The open complement is preserved.
Every contracted curve has negative degree against the effective divisor
tau N_i-ε S_i, so lies in Supp N_i. A connected
positive-dimensional projective fiber contains a curve through each of its
closed points, so a fiber meeting the complement of Supp N_i consists
of a single point and does not meet the support. Properness and normality
show the contraction is an isomorphism from this complement onto an open
subset of its target. For a flip, this open in the target is
Q-factorial, being isomorphic to the corresponding open of W_i, so
the new small morphism is also an isomorphism over it.

No Mori fiber output.
The effective representative tau N_i-ε S_i persists on each
model, as does tau N_i-tS_i for every rational 0<tleε. A
curve in a fiber through a general point outside this effective support has
nonnegative degree, whereas the driving divisor of a Mori fiber
contraction is negative on every contracted curve. This rules out the
fiber alternative for the lowered class and for every smaller
perturbation of the restored class.

The output model.
Let Y be the resulting model. The partial-MMP conclusion gives
(eq:isolation-nef) for some rational 0<delta<ε. Negative
MMP steps preserve the Q-factorial dlt pair with lowered boundary, so
(Y,G_Y-ε S_Y) is dlt. Its Q-factorial underlying variety Y
is then klt: every place of zero log discrepancy for a dlt pair has center
in the reduced boundary, and removing that effective Q-Cartier
boundary strictly increases the discrepancy. Dlt preservation here is
used for the lowered boundary; for the restored boundary we use the
crepant comparison just proved.

Nefness isolates S_Y.
Let Tne S_Y be a component of N_Y. Numerical triviality of N_Y|_T
and (eq:isolation-nef) make -S_Y|_T nef. On the other hand
S_Y|_T is an effective Q-Cartier divisor; if it were nonzero, its
intersection with an ample Cartier divisor on the integral projective
surface T would be positive, contradicting anti-nefness. Hence
S_Ycap T=∅. Each other component of G_Y is either such a
component of N_Y or is disjoint from its support. This proves the stated
separation and the local identities (eq:isolation-local). The
equality N_Y|_{S_Y}equiv0 gives S_Y|_{S_Y}equiv0.

The common open.
All models share the open U_X. Resolve the graph of the correspondences
among them, keeping this smooth open unchanged, to obtain V_0.
Composing (eq:isolation-step-pullback) with N=mu^*N_0 proves
(eq:isolation-pullbacks), and pullback preserves the equality on any
further common resolution.


For adjunction we replace the isolated log canonical pair by a dlt pair.
The klt property of Y ensures that every divisor extracted below belongs
to the total transform of S_Y.


With the notation of Proposition~prop:isolation, there is a
projective crepant Q-factorial dlt modification
f:(Y_d,Σ)to(Y,S_Y) such that Σ is reduced,

SuppΣ=Supp f^*S_Y,

and f is an isomorphism off S_Y. On a neighborhood of Σ,

K_{Y_d}+Σsim_{Q}tau s,f^*S_Y.

Moreover f^*S_Y is numerically trivial on each component of Σ,
and Y_d shares the open U_X with X and Y.

Proof. 
The pair (Y,S_Y) is log canonical, since G_Yge S_Y and (Y,G_Y) is
log canonical. Take a crepant dlt modification extracting only log
canonical places. Its boundary Σ consists of the strict transform
of S_Y and its exceptional divisors, all of coefficient one. Every
extracted divisor has center in S_Y: outside S_Y the pair is the klt
variety Y. Such a divisor has positive order on S_Y, which proves
(eq:dlt-support). There are no exceptional divisors off S_Y, and
the small-morphism argument of the preceding proof shows f is an
isomorphism there.

Crepancy and (eq:isolation-local) give (eq:dlt-local). Every
curve in Σ maps to a point or to a curve in S_Y, where S_Y is
numerically trivial, so the projection formula gives the numerical
assertion. Under the identification of Proposition~prop:isolation,
U_X is disjoint from S_Y and hence is unchanged by f.


# Torsion on the full boundary cycle


The reduced dlt boundary Σ of Corollary~lem:dlt-boundary is a
surface pair to which adjunction and surface abundance apply. The
prepared-cycle obstruction, however, needs torsion on the effective
Cartier divisor including its multiplicities. We first give the
extension argument that passes from the reduced surface to the possibly
nonreduced Cartier thickening.

[{cite[Lemma~2.4]{Companion}}]
Let T be a normal projective threefold over an algebraically closed
field of characteristic p>0, and let E be a nonzero effective Cartier
divisor on T. Suppose E is numerically trivial on every integral
component of its support. If
[
O_T(E)|_{E_{red}∖ Z}
~=O_{E_{red}∖ Z}
]
for a finite set Z of closed points, then O_E(E) is torsion.

Proof. 
We reproduce the proof because both the finite exceptional set and the
nonreduced divisor occur in our application. Replace Z by
ZcapSupp E. At zin Z write R=O_{T,z}, with maximal ideal
m,m, and let t_z be a local equation of E. Normality gives
[
H^1_{m}m(R)=0,

ell_R(H^2_{m}m(R))<∞.
]
The second assertion uses the dimension-three hypothesis. Present R=A/I
with A regular local of dimension n, essentially of finite type over
the field. Each nonmaximal localization of R is normal of dimension at
most two, hence Cohen--Macaulay. Catenarity gives
dim A_{q}q-dim R_{p}p=n-3 for a prime
q above p,p, so the Auslander--Buchsbaum formula
gives projective dimension n-3 over A_{q}q. Thus
Ext^{n-2}_A(R,A) is supported only at the closed point.
It is a finite module, hence of finite length, and local duality gives the
assertion for H^2_{m}m(R).

Choose jge1 such that t_z^{j-1} annihilates H^2_{m}m(R)
at every zin Z; a single j works because Z is finite. For every
integer u, restriction sequences give a commutative diagram
[
[column sep=small]
0arrow[r]&O_T((u-j)E)arrow[r]arrow[d]
 &O_T(uE)arrow[r]arrow[d,equal]
 &O_{jE}(uE)arrow[r]arrow[d]&0
0arrow[r]&O_T((u-1)E)arrow[r]
 &O_T(uE)arrow[r]
 &O_E(uE)arrow[r]&0.

]
In local trivializations the left vertical map is multiplication by
t_z^{j-1}. Local cohomology with support in the finite set Z is the
direct sum of the corresponding local groups. The vanishing of the ambient
H^1_Z makes the connecting maps from the H^1_Z of the rightmost terms
to the ambient H^2_Z injective. Naturality and the choice of j give

H^1_Z(T,O_{jE}(uE))
→ H^1_Z(T,O_E(uE))
quadis zero for every uinZ.

The same j works for every twist: locally the invertible sheaves in the
diagram are free of rank one, and the annihilating map is still
multiplication by t_z^{j-1}.

Fix this thickening jE. The ideal of E_{red}∖ Z in
jE∖ Z is nilpotent. Lift a chosen generator of
O_T(E)|_{E_{red}∖ Z} to local generating sections on
a finite open cover of jE∖ Z. On overlaps the lifts differ by
sections in that nilpotent ideal times the line bundle. For u=p^e
sufficiently large, their uth tensor powers agree: in local frames this
is the identity (a+b)^{p^e}=a^{p^e}+b^{p^e} with b^{p^e}=0. They
therefore glue to a generator
[
s_jin H^0(jE∖ Z,O_{jE}(uE)).
]
Let s be its restriction to E∖ Z. The obstruction to extending
s over E is the image of the obstruction to extending s_j over
jE. Equation~(eq:torsion-obstruction-zero), applied to the exact
sequences for sections with support in Z, kills that image, so s
extends to a section of O_E(uE).

This extended section has no zeros. On each integral projective surface
component F of E_{red} it is nonzero because it is a generator
off Z. If its restriction to F vanished anywhere, its zero scheme would
be a nonempty effective Cartier divisor on F, with positive intersection
with an ample divisor, contradicting the numerical triviality of
O_F(uE). The argument uses only integrality of F, not normality.
The section is thus a generator on the reduction, and then on E, since
an element invertible modulo a nilpotent ideal is invertible. Hence
O_E(uE)~=O_E.


We apply this lemma to the reduced dlt model obtained by isolation. Recall
that s is the multiplicity of S_Y in N_Y and tau>0 satisfies
K_Y+G_Ysim_{Q}tau N_Y.


For the projective crepant dlt modification
f:(Y_d,Σ)to(Y,S_Y) of Corollary~lem:dlt-boundary, there is a
positive Cartier multiple M of f^*S_Y such that
[
Supp M=Σ,
 M|_{Σ_i}equiv0 for every component Σ_i,
 O_M(M) is torsion.
]

Proof. 
The relations furnished by isolation are

K_{Y_d}+Σsim_{Q}tau s,f^*S_Y
quadon a neighborhood of Σ,
 f^*S_Y|_{Σ_i}equiv0.

Let sigma:Σ'toΣ be the finite S_2-ification; it is
unchanged away from a finite set ZsubsetΣ
cite[Proposition~2.14]{XuNuTwo}. Reduced-boundary adjunction gives an
effective rational divisor Delta' such that (Σ',Delta') is a
projective semi-log-canonical surface pair and

K_{Σ'}+Delta'
sim_{Q}sigma^*((K_{Y_d}+Σ)|_Σ)
sim_{Q}tau s,sigma^*(f^*S_Y|_Σ).

We use adjunction in the form of cite[Lemma~2.19]{XuNuTwo}; see also
cite[Section~2.5]{Companion}. The S_2-ification is needed because the
reduced boundary need not satisfy S_2. Its codimension-one
singularities are nodes, and on its normalization the adjunction boundary
includes the conductor with coefficient one, so the normalized pair is
log canonical. These are the semi-log-canonical conditions needed for
surface abundance.

The surface Σ' is projective because it is finite over Σ.
The last class in (eq:slc-adjunction) is numerically trivial, by
(eq:adjunction-input) and the projection formula for curves.
Posva's abundance theorem for projective semi-log-canonical surfaces in
positive characteristic makes K_{Σ'}+Delta' semiample
cite[Theorem~3.1]{Posva}. A semiample numerically trivial rational line
bundle is Q-linearly trivial: a globally generated Cartier multiple
defines a morphism contracting every curve, hence constant on each
connected component, whose line bundle is trivial there.

Since tau s is positive and rational, clearing denominators in
(eq:slc-adjunction) yields a positive Cartier multiple M of
f^*S_Y with sigma^*O_Σ(M) trivial. In particular
[
O_{Y_d}(M)|_{Σ∖ Z}~=O_{Σ∖ Z}.
]
The divisor M is effective, its reduction is Σ, and it is
numerically trivial on every component of its support. Applying
Lemma~lem:torsion on the normal projective threefold Y_d proves
torsion on the full Cartier scheme M.


# Construction of the prepared cycle


We combine the isolated boundary with the original log canonical section.
The boundary supplies a divisor with torsion normal line; the section
supplies a canonical relation near its support. A finite cover removes the
prime-to-p part of the canonical exponent. This is the preparation of
cite[Sections~2.7--2.8]{Companion}, keeping the support of the original
boundary throughout.


Let (X,B,N_0) be the data of Proposition~prop:reduction in the
maximal-nef-dimension case. Then there exist a smooth projective threefold
V, a very ample divisor H_V, and a nonzero effective Cartier divisor
[
D=sum_alpha m_alpha D_alpha
]
having all the properties of Theorem~thm:obstruction. In particular
its support is connected with simple normal crossings,
gcd_alpha(m_alpha)=1, and O_D(D) is torsion.

Proof. 
Equation~(eq:restriction-zero) and Proposition~prop:separation
give the hypotheses of Proposition~prop:isolation. Choose its
isolated model (Y,G_Y,N_Y,S_Y) and the reduced dlt modification
f:(Y_d,Σ)to(Y,S_Y) of Corollary~lem:dlt-boundary. Let M be
the positive Cartier multiple of f^*S_Y supplied by
Proposition~prop:boundary-torsion.

The canonical section and its support.
Let V_0 be a smooth projective common resolution of X and Y_d,
dominating the intervening models. Write
[
q:V_0to X, h_0:V_0to Y_d, r=fcirc h_0:V_0to Y.
]
These models share the open
[
U_X=X_{reg}∖(Supp N_0cup P_{out}),
]
with P_{out} the union of boundary components disjoint from
Supp N_0. Choose the common resolution to be an isomorphism over this
open; projective resolution and resolution of divisor supports in
dimension three permit this [cite]. The pullback equalities
through the partial MMP give

q^*N_0=r^*N_Y.


The integer m satisfies N_0sim m(K_X+B), with mK_X and mB
Cartier. It gives a rational section theta of
omega_{V_0}^{ ⊗  m} with

div(theta)
=q^*N_0+m(K_{V_0}-q^*K_X)-m,q^*B.

The right side is an integral divisor linearly equivalent to mK_{V_0}.
Put E_0=Supp q^*N_0. Every prime component in
(eq:log-canonical-section) is contained in E_0 or disjoint from
it. For q^*B this is boundary separation on X. For a q-exceptional
divisor, its irreducible center lies in
[
X∖ U_X
=Supp N_0cup P_{out}cupSingX.
]
A terminal threefold is regular in codimension two, so
SingX is finite cite[Lemma~2.3]{Companion}. The center
is thus contained in Supp N_0 or disjoint from it, and taking inverse
images gives the assertion.

Let F_0 be the union of the components of
div(theta) outside E_0. Then

Z_0:=E_0cupSuppdiv(theta)=E_0sqcup F_0.

The disjointness is the point: components of q^*N_0 can cancel in
(eq:log-canonical-section), so we retain all of E_0 when choosing
the open set preserved by the resolution below.

Removing the prime-to-p exponent.
Write m=p^b m' with bge0 and (m',p)=1. Choose a nonzero rational
generator eta of omega_{V_0}^{ ⊗  p^b} and write
theta=ueta^{m'} with uin k(V_0)^times. Adjoin one root v of
v^{m'}=u and normalize V_0 in the resulting field. This is a finite
separable cover, possibly of degree less than m'. Over
V_0∖Suppdiv(theta) it is an integral component
of the torsor of m'th roots of the invertible section theta, a
description independent of eta. Since m' is invertible in k, that
torsor is finite 'etale.

Resolve the normalization and the total inverse image of Z_0,
preserving the regular 'etale open over V_0∖ Z_0. We obtain a
smooth projective threefold and a generically finite separable morphism
pi:Vto V_0 for which the total pullback support of N_0 has simple
normal crossings. The morphisms comparing divisors are
[
[column sep=large,row sep=large]
Varrow[r,"pi"] &V_0arrow[r,"q"]arrow[d,"h_0"']&X
&Y_darrow[r,"f"']&Y.

]
Here q,h_0,f are birational and pi is generically finite and
separable. Set rho=qcircpi and h=h_0circpi. By
(eq:cycle-common-pullbacks),
rho^*N_0=(fcirc h)^*N_Y, while the Cartier divisor of
Proposition~prop:boundary-torsion pulls back as h^*M.

The rational section veta is a section of
pi^*omega_{V_0}^{ ⊗  p^b}. The determinant of the differential is
a nonzero section of the rational line
omega_V ⊗ pi^*omega_{V_0}^{-1}, denoted J_pi; it is nonzero at
the generic point by separability. Consequently

psi=(veta),J_pi^{p^b}
quadis a rational section of 
omega_V^{ ⊗  p^b}.

Both factors are invertible on pi^{-1}(V_0∖ Z_0): the first is
a root of an invertible section, the second the Jacobian of an 'etale
morphism. Hence div(psi) is supported on
pi^{-1}(Z_0). By (eq:cover-closed-set) each of its prime
components lies in Supprho^*N_0 or is disjoint from that support.

A connected primitive Cartier divisor.
Put M_V=h^*M and choose a connected component C of Supp M_V. Since
M is a positive multiple of f^*S_Y and S_Y is disjoint from the
other components of N_Y, the set C is also a connected component of
Supprho^*N_0. Write the part of M_V supported on C as

aD, D=sum_alpha m_alpha D_alpha,
 a=gcd_{D_alphasubset C}mult_{D_alpha}(M_V).

The divisor D is Cartier because V is smooth, its multiplicities are
positive with gcd one, and its support is connected with simple normal
crossings.

Every curve in Supp M_V maps under h to a point or to a curve in
Σ. The projection formula and Proposition~prop:boundary-torsion
therefore make M_V numerically trivial on every component of its
support. Near C we have M_V=aD, so D|_{D_alpha}equiv0 for every
alpha. A curve not contained in Supp D has nonnegative intersection
with D by effectivity, while a curve in the support has intersection
zero. Hence D is nef, and every very ample H_V satisfies

D H_V^2>0, D^2 H_V=0.


Torsion survives on the entire Cartier scheme. Choose t>0 with
O_M(tM)~=O_M. The pullback divisor M_V is scheme-theoretically
Vtimes_{Y_d}M: its local equation is the pullback of a local equation
of M, which is nonzero since V is integral and h is dominant.
Pulling back the trivialization gives
O_{M_V}(tM_V)~=O_{M_V}, with no flatness assumption on h.
Restricting along Dsubset M_V and using M_V=aD near C gives

O_D(t,a,D)~=O_D.

Hence O_D(D) is torsion.

The section psi of (eq:canonical-root-section) has, on a
neighborhood W of C, divisor supported on D: its other components are
disjoint from C and can be removed from that neighborhood. Thus there are
integers b_alpha with

omega_V^{ ⊗  p^b}|_W
~=O_W(sum_alpha b_alpha D_alpha).


Numerical proportionality and very general points.
It remains to show no D-trivial curve passes through a very general
point of V. Put N_V=rho^*N_0. It is a nonzero nef divisor,
numerically trivial on every component of its support by the projection
formula. Since Supp D is contained in that support,
[
N_V H_V^2>0, N_V^2H_V=N_V D H_V=0.
]
Together with (eq:primitive-intersections), the Hodge index theorem
gives

Dequiv cN_V,
 c=(D H_V^2)/(N_V H_V^2)>0.

We verify this is numerical equivalence on every curve, not only on a
fixed hyperplane section, following cite[Section~2.8]{Companion}.

Let Gammasubset V be any integral curve. For lgg0 there is an
integral surface Tin|lH_V| containing Gamma. To see this, twist the
ideal of Gamma sufficiently far and multiply its generators by very
ample sections; the resulting linear system has no fixed divisorial
component and gives a birational map onto its image off Gamma. Bertini
irreducibility gives an irreducible general member, generically reduced on
that birational open. As a Cartier divisor on smooth V it has no
embedded components, hence is integral. On a smooth projective resolution
widetilde Tto T, let d,n,e be the pullbacks of D|_T,N_V|_T,H_V|_T.
Their intersections are
[
d^2=n^2=dn=0, e^2>0,
de=l,D H_V^2, ne=l,N_V H_V^2.
]
Hence d-cn has square zero and is orthogonal to e. The Hodge index
form is negative definite on the orthogonal complement of the
positive-square class e, so d-cn is numerically zero. The projective
surjection widetilde Tto T supplies an integral curve dominating
Gamma: take the closure of a closed point of the fiber over the generic
point of Gamma. The projection formula then gives
(D-cN_V)·Gamma=0. This proves (eq:cycle-proportionality).

Finally let Z_isubset X be the countably many proper closed subsets
excluded by the nef reduction of N_0. Remove from V the
non-quasi-finite locus of rho and all sets rho^{-1}(Z_i). These are
proper closed subsets, since rho is dominant and generically finite,
and their complement contains very general points because k is
uncountable. Every curve through a point of this complement maps to a
curve on X. If it were D-trivial, then (eq:cycle-proportionality)
and the projection formula would make its image N_0-trivial, contradicting
maximal nef dimension. All properties of Theorem~thm:obstruction are
now established.


Proof. [Proof of Theorem~thm:main]
Theorem~thm:obstruction contradicts Proposition~prop:prepared.
The maximal-nef-dimension case is therefore impossible. By
Proposition~prop:reduction, it remains to apply abundance in nef
dimension at most two cite[Theorem~1.8]{XuNonvanishing}. This gives
semiampleness, and faithful descent along the algebraic closure extension
gives it over the original field.
