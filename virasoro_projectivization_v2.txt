# Introduction

## What is being proved, informally

Let $Y$ be a smooth complex projective variety. Gromov--Witten
(GW) theory ``counts'' maps from compact curves into $Y$; each
such map contributes a number that also remembers two pieces of
data:

item a homology class $din H_2(Y;Z)$ telling us which curve
class of $Y$ we are counting;
item at each of the $n$ marked points $x_i$ on the domain
curve, a cohomology class $b_iin H^*(Y;C)$ together with a
power $psi_i^{k_i}$ of the first Chern class of the cotangent
line at $x_i$.

The resulting descendant integral is
[
 int_{[mathcal Mbar_calMbar_{g,n}(Y,d)]^{vir}}
 psi_1^{k_1}ev_1^* b_1...
 psi_n^{k_n}ev_n^* b_n.
]
Stacking every such number (all $g$, $d$, $n$, all
$k_i$, all $b_i$) into a single formal generating series gives
the total descendant potential $Z_Y(t)$, defined
precisely in Section~sec:conv.

The Virasoro constraints are a sequence of differential
equations
$L_k^Y Z_Y=0$, $kgeq -1$, in the sources $t$.
They were proposed by Eguchi--Hori--Xiong and refined by
Getzler, and are known for target curves, toric targets, and
targets with semisimple quantum cohomology, but remain open in
general. This paper proves that they pass from a base to the
projectivization of any rank $geq 2$ vector bundle over it. The
transfer needs no semisimplicity of quantum cohomology and no
positivity or splitness of the bundle.

The precise statement is:


Let $B$ be a smooth connected projective variety over $C$,
let $E$ be an algebraic vector bundle of rank $rgeq 2$ on
$B$, and set $X=mathbb P_B(E)$.bbP_B(E)$. If the ordinary descendant
potential of $B$ is annihilated by every Virasoro mode,
$L_k^B Z_B=0$ for all $kgeq -1$, then the same holds for $X$:
$L_k^X Z_X=0$ for all $kgeq -1$.


Here $Z_Y$ is the ordinary descendant potential of $Y$
(no ancestor classes, no twisting), on the full cohomology
$H^*(Y;C)$, with each actual integral curve class kept
separate. The conclusion includes every genus, every individual
integral curve class, every finite list of descendants, and
primitive and odd insertions. Applying the theorem repeatedly
covers towers $Xto X'to...to B$ of projective bundles
over any base satisfying the hypothesis.

## Why this is not obvious

If $E$ splits as $L_1 ⊕  ... ⊕  L_r$, mirror-symmetry
arguments prove the transfer (Coates--Givental--Tseng), and if
$B$ has semisimple quantum cohomology, semisimplicity transfers
to $X$ (Iritani--Koto) and Givental--Teleman reconstruction then
gives the constraints on $X$. Neither assumption is available in
general. Non-split bundles and non-semisimple bases require an
argument that does not go through mirror symmetry.

Our proof uses a model geometry that sees both $B$ and $X$ as
fixed loci, plus a $C^*$-equivariant shift operator that turns
the ``change of fixed component'' into ``change of spectral
block.'' The shift and its spectrum do not depend on
semisimplicity. A separate step (Quantum Riemann--Roch) carries
the constraints from the local neighborhood of each fixed
component to its ordinary theory. The sum over the fiber's
spectral cluster assembles the constraints on $X$.

## The master space and the five ideas

Twist $E$ by a sufficiently negative line bundle on $B$. This
does not change $X=mathbb P_B(E)$,bbP_B(E)$, but makes $E^*$ globally
generated, hence makes tautological degrees nonnegative on
effective curves. Set
[
 W=mathbb P_B(EbbP_B(E ⊕ mathcal O_B).calO_B).
]
Let $T=C^*$ act with weight $0$ on $E$ and weight $1$ on
$mathcal O_B$,calO_B$, and let $lambdain H_T^2(pt)$ be the
equivariant parameter. The fixed scheme is a disjoint union
$W^T=Bsqcup X$: the section $[0:1]$ is $B$, and the section
$[1:0]$ is $X$.

The proof has five steps:


item Localize the master ancestors. The equivariant
descendant theory of $W$ factorizes, via the stable-graph sum of
virtual localization, into the two fixed-component theories,
each twisted by the inverse Euler class of its normal bundle,
plus an upper-triangular symplectic transformation $R(z)$: see
Proposition~prop:loc-factorization below. The $R$ and its
inverse are explicitly quantizable.

item Identify the Iritani shift and its spectrum.
Iritani's equivariant shift operator $T_a$ translates
$lambdamapstolambda+z$. It has a closed formula
$T_a=S_W T_d S_W^{-1}$, and on the fiber part of the degree-zero
cohomology, its leading term is multiplication by $p+lambda$
in the quantum algebra of the fiber. Hence the distinct
eigenvalue branches are $lambda+h$, with
$h^r(h+lambda)=y$ and $y$ the fiber class. There are
$r+1$ branches; near $y=0$, one tends to $0$ (the $B$
branch), and the other $r$ tend to $lambda$ (the $X$
cluster).

item Isolate spectral blocks. Contour integrals against
the resolvent of $T_a$ produce projectors $P_i$ and logarithms
$mathscr L_i$scrL_i$ on each branch. Because the graded operator
$G_a$ (which has $lambda∂_lambda$ in it) commutes with
$T_a$, the formula
$D_i=P_iG_a-(lambda/z)mathscr L_i$scrL_i$ has no
$lambda$-differentiation. The induced loop operators on blocks
are therefore quantizable over the coefficient ring.

item Continue around the branch cover. The cover
$hmapsto h^r(h+lambda)$ is connected away from its critical
values, so monodromy in $y$ transports every equation from the
$B$ branch to every other branch. Ancestor coefficients are
polynomial in $y$, and their cotangent powers are bounded
independently of fiber degree; consequently the interchange of
``formal identity'' and ``identity of germs'' is legal
coefficientwise. Adding the $r$ branches of the $X$ cluster
gives the constraints on $X$ after going back to $y=0$.

item Fix the scalar normalization. Quantized transports
are only defined projectively. Two commutators,
$[L_{-1}^Y,L_1^Y]=-2L_0^Y$ and $[L_0^Y,L_k^Y]=-kL_k^Y$, remove
the insertion-independent scalars and give exactly the
prescribed constant $C_Y$ in $L_0^Y$.


Two ingredients of the argument are worth flagging, since they
are reusable:
(1) subtracting a block logarithm from a differential operator
in $lambda$ converts a derivative into a loop operator over the
coefficient ring; and
(2) ancestor bounds make every coefficient test finite, so
analytic continuation between branches is legitimate
coefficientwise.
Neither ingredient is special to projective bundles.

# Conventions: descendants, quantization, Virasoro
% ==================================================================

Throughout the paper, $Y$ is a smooth connected projective
variety over $C$ of dimension $d$. The two examples to keep in
mind are $Y=B$ and $Y=X=mathbb P_B(E)$;bbP_B(E)$; every definition in
this section applies verbatim to both.

## State space and grading

The state space is the full super-vector space
$H_Y=H^*(Y;C)$ with parity given by cohomological degree
modulo $2$ and Poincar'e pairing
$eta_Y(a,b)=(a,b)_Y=int_Y acup b$. Tensor products,
permutations, contractions and derivatives follow the Koszul
rule; the coevaluation tensor is the categorical inverse of
$eta_Y$, without an extra parity sign.

Two even endomorphisms of $H_Y$ organize the Virasoro
construction:

 mu_Ybig|_{H^{a,b}(Y)}=(a-d/2)id,
 qquad
 R_Y=c_1(TY)cup(-).
 

Here $a$ is the first Hodge index, so $mu_Y$ uses the first
Hodge grading (which is the correct choice for targets with odd
cohomology; see Getzler). Poincar'e duality gives
$mu_Y^*=-mu_Y$ and $R_Y^*=R_Y$, and the Hodge type of $c_1$
gives $[mu_Y,R_Y]=R_Y$.

## Descendant potentials and Novikov completion

Fix a homogeneous Hodge basis $(phi_a)$ with $phi_0=1_Y$. Let
$t_j^a$ be formal supercommuting variables with the parity of
$phi_a$, $jgeq 0$, and put
$t_j=sum_a t_j^aphi_a$, $t(z)=sum_{jgeq 0}t_jz^j$. Define
the connected ordinary unreduced descendant potentials and
total potential by

 F_g^Y(t)
 =sum_{substack{beta effectivemgeq 0}}
     (Q^beta)/(m!)
     int_{[mathcal Mbar_calMbar_{g,m}(Y,beta)]^{vir}}
     prod_{i=1}^m(sum_{jgeq 0}psi_i^j
     ev_i^* t_j),
 qquad
 Z_Y(t)=expsum_{ggeq 0}hbar^{g-1}F_g^Y(t).
 

Only stable maps contribute. In particular, $psi_i$ is the map
cotangent class at the $i$-th mark, not an ancestor class. The
exponential records disconnected domains and the empty domain.

Novikov variables remember the actual effective integral class
$betain H_2(Y;Z)$, not just its numerical degree:
$Q^{beta_1}Q^{beta_2}=Q^{beta_1+beta_2}$ and $beta=0$
corresponds to $Q^0=1$. Fix any ample integral class on $Y$ to
give the Novikov completion: only finitely many effective
$beta$ lie below any fixed ample degree. All identities below
are read coefficientwise in this completion, at finite order in
the descendants, and coefficientwise in $hbar$ (which is a
formal Laurent variable).

## Loop space and ordinary Virasoro modes

Set
[
 cH_Y=H_Y((z^{-1})),quad
 Ω_Y(f,g)=Res_{z=0}(f(-z),g(z))_Y,dz,
]
with polarization
$cH_Y=H_Y[z] ⊕  z^{-1}H_Y[[z^{-1}]]$.
The infinitesimal symplectic operators are

 ell_{-1,Y}=z^{-1},quad
 ell_{0,Y}=z∂_z+tfrac12+mu_Y+(R_Y)/(z),quad
 ell_{k,Y}=ell_{0,Y}(zell_{0,Y})^k,quad kgeq 1.
 

The symplectic identity is exactly the adjoint triple
$mu_Y^*=-mu_Y$, $R_Y^*=R_Y$, $[mu_Y,R_Y]=R_Y$ combined with
the sign of $z$ in the residue pairing; we check this in the
proof of Lemma~lem:commutators below, where we use it.

## Quantization and Virasoro operators

Write $q_j^a$ for the positive loop coordinates (dual to
$t_j^a$ in the standard positive part) and $p_{j,a}$ for the
dual negative coordinates. Quantize a quadratic Hamiltonian
$mathcal Q_A(f)=calQ_A(f)=frac12Ω_Y(f,Af)$ by the rule: a $qq$
monomial becomes $q^top q/(2hbar)$, a $pq$ monomial becomes
the first-order operator with left multiplication before left
derivative, and a $pp$ monomial becomes $hbar$ times the
second derivative; all reorderings have their graded signs.
Then perform the dilaton translation

 q(z)=t(z)-z1_Y.
 

Denote the resulting operator by $op_{eta_Y}(A)$.

With these conventions:

 L_{-1}^Y
 &=op_{eta_Y}(ell_{-1,Y})
  =-(∂)/(∂ t_0^0)
  +sum_{jgeq 0,a}t_{j+1}^a(∂)/(∂ t_j^a)
  +((t_0,t_0)_Y)/(2hbar),
 
 L_k^Y&=op_{eta_Y}(ell_{k,Y})quad(kneq 0),
 qquad
 L_0^Y=op_{eta_Y}(ell_{0,Y})+C_Y,
 
 C_Y&=(chi(Y))/(16)-frac14str(mu_Y^2).
 nonumber

The supertrace counts even minus odd cohomological parity.
Positive modes have no scalar correction.

Write $V(Y)$ for the conjunction of identities

                     L_k^Y Z_Y=0,qquad kgeq -1.
 

These identities are read coefficientwise: separately at each
genus, at each actual integral class, at each number of marks,
and at each monomial in the descendants. The main theorem is
$V(B)⇒V(X)$.

## Projective satisfaction and covariance under frame change

Quantization of an infinitesimal symplectic mode is defined only
up to an additive scalar. Consequently a change of frame can
change the mode equation by a constant. For transfer purposes
we track the weaker projective version, then remove the scalar
ambiguity at the end.

[Projective satisfaction]
An even infinitesimal symplectic $A$ is satisfied
projectively by $Z$ if $Z^{-1}op_eta(A)Z$ is independent of
the descendants (it may still depend on the Novikov variables,
$lambda$, and $hbar$).


[Projective covariance]
Let $M$ be a symplectic map between paired loop spaces
$(cH_Y,eta_Y)$ and $(cH_{Y'},eta_{Y'})$, with both $M$ and
$M^{-1}$ admitting quantized actions $U_M,U_{M^{-1}}$ in the
coefficientwise completion. If $A$ and $MAM^{-1}$ are
infinitesimal symplectic and quantizable, then

 U_Mop_{eta_Y}(A)U_M^{-1}
  =op_{eta_{Y'}}(MAM^{-1})+scalar.
 

In particular, $A$ is satisfied projectively by $Z$
iff $MAM^{-1}$ is satisfied projectively by $U_MZ$; multiplying
either potential by an invertible scalar does not affect the
projective condition.


Proof. 
The commutator of two normally ordered quadratic operators is
the quantization of their Hessian/Hamiltonian bracket plus a
scalar double contraction, which in super Darboux coordinates
is a supertrace. Exponentiating this identity gives
(eq:covariance); equivalently this is Givental's
projective-quantization formalism. Neither $U_M$ nor $MAM^{-1}$
differentiates $hbar$ or the parameters, so the projective
condition is preserved.


## Coefficientwise finiteness

Some of the mode matrices defined below have infinitely many
positive powers of $z$, or finite-order $z∂_z$
derivatives. The following lemma collects the formal facts we
need.

[Coefficientwise finiteness]
Suppose a potential has finitely many descendant coefficients
at each fixed curve class, $hbar$-power, and total descendant
degree, and has $hbar$-powers bounded below at each fixed curve
class and total degree. Let $A$ be an even infinitesimal
symplectic mode with a finite $z$-lower-bound and finite order in
$z∂_z$ at every filtration step. Then $op_eta(A)$ acts
coefficientwise on the potential. The same holds with additional
formal filtration variables; and quantized actions $U_M$ of
transformations equal to the identity modulo positive Novikov
filtration are defined by their formal exponential and inverse
series.


Proof. 
At fixed filtration, the $pp$ part differentiates only finitely
many coefficients. In the $pq$ part a bounded differentiated
index and a lower bound on loop powers leaves only finitely many
multiplied indices. In the $qq$ part the order in $z∂_z$
is finite. Euler differentiation $z∂_z$ multiplies mode
coefficients by polynomials in their indices and preserves these
bounds. At fixed positive filtration, only finitely many terms of
an exponential can occur.


Ordinary descendant potentials satisfy the required support:
for fixed genus, class and number of marks, high enough powers
of $psi_i$ vanish on the finite-dimensional stable-map space.
Ancestor potentials satisfy a stronger bound from the
stable-curve moduli space (Lemma~lem:ancestor-bound
below).

Categorical inverse and signs.:  All splitting and
cotangent-class identities used later are tensor identities
built from the categorical inverse of $eta_Y$. Their standard
marked-point proofs therefore apply to full cohomology with
these signs. Quantum Riemann--Roch and the two-mark shift
identities likewise use arbitrary evaluation classes and this
diagonal.

# The master space and its fixed theories
% ==================================================================

We put $B$ and $X=mathbb P_B(E)$bbP_B(E)$ into one smooth projective
variety $W$ with a torus action, so that both are fixed loci.
Virtual localization will then express the theory of $W$ in
terms of the theories of $B$ and $X$. This section sets up the
geometry, curve labels, pairings, and the ancestor formalism.

## Geometry and integral curve labels

Tensoring $E$ by a line bundle on $B$ does not change
$X=mathbb P_B(E)$.bbP_B(E)$. Choose such a twist so that $E^*$ is
globally generated, and (abusing notation) continue to write
$E$ for the twisted bundle. Set
[
 X=mathbb P_B(E),bbP_B(E),qquad
 W=mathbb P_B(EbbP_B(E ⊕ mathcal O_B).calO_B).
]
Let $T=C^*$ act with weight zero on $E$ and weight one on
$mathcal O_B$.calO_B$. Write
$lambdain H_T^2(pt)$ for the weight-one
representation's class. The fixed scheme is
$W^T=Xsqcup B$, where the second component is the section
corresponding to the summand $mathcal O_B$.calO_B$.

Write $pi$ for the projective-bundle projection of either $X$
or $W$ to $B$, and put $p=c_1^T(mathcal O_W(1))$.calO_W(1))$. The normal
bundles, their ranks, weights, and the integers $W_F,j_F$ that
will appear in the shift formula below are:

{c|c|c|c|c|c}
 F&N_F&n_F=rk N_F&w_F&W_F=n_Fw_F&j_F hline
 X&mathcal O_X(1)&1&+1&+1&0calO_X(1)&1&+1&+1&0
 B&E&r&-1&-r&1

 

The restrictions of the equivariant divisor are
[
              p|_X=c_1(mathcal O_X(1)),calO_X(1)),qquad p|_B=-lambda.
]
Here $w_F$ is the common $C^*$-weight on the normal fibers.
Both descriptions come from the standard relative tangent
sequence: at a line $Lsubset V$ one has
$T_{W/B}congHom(L,V/L)$.


For $P=X$ or $P=W$, the map

 H_2(P;Z)to H_2(B;Z) ⊕ Z,quad
 dmapsto(pi_*d,int_d c_1(mathcal O_P(1)))calO_P(1)))
 

is an isomorphism, including torsion. An effective class has
effective base pushforward and nonnegative tautological degree.


Proof. 
The structure group of a projective bundle acts trivially on
the integral homology of the fiber, so the Serre spectral
sequence in total degree two is
$H_2(B;Z) ⊕  H_2(mathbb P^bbP^{s-1};Z)$ with $sgeq 2$ the
rank. The fiber generator survives integrally because
$c_1(mathcal O_P(1))$calO_P(1))$ pairs to one with the fiber line: its
image in total homology cannot be zero or a nontrivial multiple.
Thus we have an exact sequence
[
 0toZ[fiber line]to H_2(P;Z)
   xrightarrow{pi_*}H_2(B;Z)to 0,
]
and pairing with $c_1(mathcal O_P(1))$calO_P(1))$ provides a retraction on
the first term, giving (eq:integral-splitting). Finally,
$E^*$ and $E^* ⊕ mathcal O_B$calO_B$ are globally generated, so the
tautological line bundle is globally generated and has
nonnegative degree on effective curves; proper pushforward
preserves effectivity.


Henceforth we write $Q^beta y^l$ for the actual class
$(beta,l)$. Under $X↪ W$ the pair $(beta,l)$ is
unchanged; under $B↪ W$ a class $beta$ becomes
$(beta,0)$.

Completion.:  Fix an ample integral class $H$ on $B$;
for $M$ large enough, $c_1(mathcal O_P(1))+McalO_P(1))+Mpi^*H$ is ample on
both projective bundles. We complete in the degree
$l+Mint_beta H$. Only finitely many effective labels occur
below each fixed degree. Unless otherwise noted, ``positive
Novikov filtration'' means positive degree in this completion
(so it includes positive fiber degree even for zero base class).

## Equivariant pairings and inverse-Euler twists

The equivariant projective-bundle formula makes $H_T^*(W)$ free
over $C[lambda]$ with basis
$pi^*phi_acdot p^j$, $0leq jleq r$, for a homogeneous
Hodge basis $(phi_a)$ of $H_B$. Equivariant integration pairs
this basis perfectly over $C[lambda]$: fiberwise integration
gives an antitriangular matrix whose antidiagonal blocks are
the ordinary Poincar'e pairing of $B$.

After extending scalars to $C(lambda)$, restriction to the
fixed locus is an isomorphism of paired spaces

 (H_T^*(W),eta_W) ⊗ _{C[lambda]}C(lambda)
   cong
 ⊕_{F=B,X}
   (H_F ⊗ C(lambda),eta_F^t),
qquad
 eta_F^t(a,b)=int_F(acup b)/(e_T(N_F)).
 

The unit restricts to the sum of the two fixed units. The
Euler classes are invertible over $C(lambda)$ because both
normal weights are nonzero.

The corresponding inverse-Euler-twisted Gromov--Witten
theory on $F$ inserts

 e_T!(R^bulletrho_*f^*N_F)^{-1}
 

in each stable-map integral, extended multiplicatively to the
indicated $K$-theory class. Here $rho$ and $f$ are the
universal curve and universal map. We use subscripts
$F,tw$ for its potentials and fundamental solutions.
Its degree-zero three-point pairing is exactly $eta_F^t$.

Hodge bidegrees.:  Give $p$ and $lambda$ Hodge
bidegree $(1,1)$. In a homogeneous polynomial basis, $mu$ acts
on basis vectors by the first Hodge degree minus $dim_C/2$,
and differentiation in $lambda$ records the degree of
equivariant coefficients. Virtual dimension identities can be
expressed in this first degree: algebraicity of evaluation
maps and the virtual class forces the sum of Hodge-degree
differences of insertions to be zero in a nonzero invariant.
This remains true equivariantly (check on finite-dimensional
approximations or after fixed-locus integration). This is a
separate grading from the cohomological degree that later
bounds the fiber degrees.

## Ancestors and the fundamental solution

We now fix notation for ancestors, applicable to an ordinary
theory, an equivariant theory, or one of the twisted fixed
theories. Denote its pairing by $eta$, its Novikov monomial
for class $d$ by $mathsf q^d$,sfq^d$, its stable-map integrals by
brackets. Let $u$ be a formal even primary background. For
distinguished insertions $a_1,...,a_m$ set
[

    (mathsf q^d)/(n!)sfq^d)/(n!)
    bigl< a_1,...,a_m,
      {u,...,u}_{n}bigr>_{g,m+n,d}.
]
Here $d$ runs over effective classes and only stable-map terms
are included. Descendants in these brackets use map cotangent
classes.

For $2g-2+m>0$ there is a stabilization map
[

 mathcal Mbar_calMbar_{g,m+n}(Y,d)to
 mathcal Mbar_calMbar_{g,m}
]
forgetting the map and the $n$ background marks. Set
$barpsi_i=st_{g,m}^*psi_i$, $1leq ileq m$.
The ancestor potential at background $u$ is

 overlineoverline F_g(u;t)F_g(u;t)
 =sum_{substack{d eff., m,ngeq 02g-2+m>0}}
    (mathsf q^d)/(m!n!)sfq^d)/(m!n!)Bigl<
     prod_{i=1}^mhspace{-3pt}(sum_{jgeq 0}
       barpsi_i^jev_i^* t_j)
     prod_{a=m+1}^{m+n}ev_a^* uBigr>_{g,m+n,d},

[Fundamental solution]
The fundamental solution $S(u,z)=id+O(z^{-1})$ is defined by

 eta(b,S(u,z)a)=eta(b,a)
   +Bigl< b,(a)/(z-psi)Bigr>_{0,u},
qquad

The pairing term supplies the unstable two-point
contribution.


The genus-zero splitting and cotangent-class identities give

 S(u,-z)^*S(u,z)=id,qquad
 z∂_v S(u,z)=(vstar_u)S(u,z),
 

where $star_u$ is the quantum product and $∂_v$
differentiates the primary background. Recall that the
genus-zero cone $mathcal L$calL$ is the formal Lagrangian graph of
$dF_0$ in Darboux coordinates $(q,p)$ with $q=t-z1$. Its point
with descendant input $ε$ has positive coordinate
$ε-z1$, and the tangent space there is the graph of the
Hessian of $F_0$. The string, dilaton and topological recursion
identities make this tangent space tangent along $zTsubset
mathcal L$;calL$; at primary coordinate $u$ the tangent space is
$S(u,z)^{-1}cH_+$ (Givental). The ancestor--descendant
correspondence in our group-action convention is

 mathcal A(u)=c(u)U_calA(u)=c(u)U_{S(u)}Z,
 

with $c(u)$ independent of the ancestor variables. Both
formulas follow from the splitting identity for $psi_i-barpsi_i$
and its genus-zero specializations (Kontsevich--Manin,
Getzler, Givental), in the super conventions of
Section~sec:conv.

We use (eq:ancestor-descendant) only on the fixed theories,
with $u$ of positive total Novikov filtration. On such $u$,
$S(u,z)-id$ has positive filtration, and every Novikov
coefficient has finitely many negative $z$-powers: finitely
many background marks at that coefficient, and $F$'s cotangent
classes are nilpotent. The quantized action is then covered by
Lemma~lem:finiteness.

For the master space we abbreviate
$S_W=S_W(0,z)$ and $mathcal A_W=calA_W=mathcal A_W(0)$.calA_W(0)$. Its
loop-variable rationality and its quantized action against the
fixed theories will be established directly by localization in
the next section.

# Ancestor localization for the master space
% ==================================================================

The torus action on $W$ splits its Gromov--Witten theory into
contributions from $B$ and $X$. This section proves the resulting
factorization of the master-space ancestor potential. We
abbreviate
[
 H^{fix}=H_B ⊕  H_X,qquad
 eta^{fix}=eta_B^t ⊕ eta_X^t.
]
Restriction identifies this paired space with $H_T^*(W)$ after
inverting $lambda$. All series use full curve labels
$Q^beta y^l$.

[Ancestor localization]
There are classes $u_Fin H_F$ of positive Novikov filtration
for $F=B,X$, and a symplectic power series
$R(z)inEnd(H^{fix})[[z]]$ with $R-id$ of positive
Novikov filtration, such that, writing
$S_f(z)=S_{F,tw}(u_F,z)$ and
$S_{bl}(z)=⊕_F S_f(z)$,

 S_W(z)&=R(z)S_{bl}(z),
 mathcal A_W(0)&=ccalA_W(0)&=c,U_R
   prod_{F=B,X}mathcal A_calA_{F,tw}(u_F),
   

with $c$ an invertible scalar independent of ancestor variables.
For every curve coefficient, $S_W(z)$ is the expansion of a
rational function of $z$ (at $z=0$ the identity is in Laurent
series). Both $R$ and its inverse admit quantized actions in the
coefficientwise completion used here.


Source of the argument.:  The same master-space geometry
underlies Fan's reconstruction of projective-bundle invariants;
for ancestors we adapt Coates--Givental--Tseng's localization,
with an additional gluing argument required because the orbit

## Moving components and their gluing

A moving leg is an irreducible component of a
torus-fixed stable map whose image is not contained in
$W^T=Bsqcup X$. Connected pieces mapping into $B$ or $X$ are
kept as stable-map factors for that component (including their
internal boundary strata).

[Leg geometry]
A moving leg has degree $mgeq 1$ on a line joining
$xin X$ to $b=pi(x)in B$. Its map from $mathbb P^1$bbP^1$ is
totally ramified at the two endpoints, and its markings and
nodes lie at these endpoints. The leg moduli is the smooth
proper $mu_m$-gerbe $q_m:mathfrak L_mfrakL_mto X$ of $m$th roots of
$mathcal O_X(-1)$,calO_X(-1)$, with endpoint evaluation maps $q_m$ and
$picirc q_m$.

At an endpoint in $F$, the tangent line of the leg has
equivariant first Chern class
[
        a=(w_Flambda)/(m)+nu,
]
with $nu$ an ordinary degree-two class on $mathfrak L_m$.frakL_m$.
Every node involving a moving leg has nonzero smoothing
character.


Proof. 
After a finite cover of the torus, the action lifts to the domain
of a fixed stable map. A nontrivial $C^*$-action on a complete
irreducible curve is trivial on its normalization; since $T$ acts
trivially on $B$ the projection of a moving component is
constant. In a fiber $mathbb P(E_bbbP(E_b ⊕ C)$ the closure of any
nonfixed orbit is the line joining $[0:1]$ to a unique
$[v:0]inmathbb P(E_b)$.bbP(E_b)$. An equivariant finite map to this
closure is, at the two endpoints, $tmapsto t^m$; a self-node
would identify distinct fixed points, so the moving component is
smooth and its special points all lie at the endpoints.

Varying the endpoint $[v]$ gives $X$ as the parameter space of
orbit lines. With $q_m:mathfrak L_mfrakL_mto X$ the root gerbe of
$mathcal O_X(-1)$calO_X(-1)$ and $M$ the universal $m$th-root line, the
universal cover
[
 mathbb P(MbbP(M ⊕ mathcal O)calO)to
 mathbb P(q_m^*bbP(q_m^*mathcal O_X(-1)calO_X(-1) ⊕ mathcal O)calO)to W,qquad
 [s:t]mapsto[s^m:t^m],
]
is locally the moving leg, and its automorphisms are exactly
the deck group $mu_m$. This identifies the leg moduli. It is
smooth and proper over the projective variety $X$, and its
ordinary class coincides with its fixed virtual class: on a
given fiber line $L$,
[

so $H^1(mathbb P^1,f^*T_W)=0$;bbP^1,f^*T_W)=0$; the fixed part of the ambient
deformation space is exactly $T_{mathfrak L_m}frakL_m$.

The universal cover gives tangent classes
$a_X=(lambda+q_m^*p|_X)/m$ at $X$ and $a_B=-a_X$ at $B$, with
scalar characters $+lambda/m$ at $X$ and $-lambda/m$ at $B$. A
node joining a leg to a fixed-map factor has the corresponding

## Gluing and virtual localization

Localization is applied as follows. Distinguish all cut branches
at nodes, then divide by graph automorphisms. Since every
smoothing character at a node cut from a moving leg is nonzero,
fixed deformations cannot smooth those nodes; fixed loci are
described by matching leg endpoints and stable-map pieces to
$B$ or $X$ along evaluation maps. This description holds in
families: degree zero over $B$ forces the projection of a
rational family to factor through its base, and the local
monomial form applies after a trivialization.

Obstruction theory matching.:  Normalize pieces
$C_alpha$ at the cut nodes $q$. The map-deformation complexes
fit into the compatible triangle
[
 RGamma(C,f^*T_W)to
 ⊕_alpha RGamma(C_alpha,f_alpha^*T_W)to
 ⊕_q T_{W,f(q)}.
]
Passing to absolute map deformations adds pointed-domain
deformation and automorphism complexes, and releasing a cut node
adds its smoothing line $T_{q,+} ⊗  T_{q,-}$. The ambient
virtual tangent class of the fixed gluing locus is thus

 [mathbb T^bbT^{vir}_Gamma]
 =sum_alpha[mathbb T^bbT^{vir}_alpha]
  -sum_q[ev_q^*T_W]
  +sum_q[T_{q,+} ⊗  T_{q,-}].

The identity comes from the displayed triangle, so the fixed
part gives the diagonal-matched obstruction theory, not merely
an equality of virtual ranks. On a fixed-map piece the fixed
part is the ordinary theory in $F$; on a leg it is
$T_{mathfrak L_m}frakL_m$. The matching terms are $T_F$; every
smoothing line is moving. The fixed virtual class is the
diagonal virtual pullback of these classes, including when two
legs meet or a graph has a cycle.

Cutting a leg off a fixed-map vertex contributes the normal
matching numerator $e_T(N_F)$ and the smoothing denominator

                  frac1{a-psi},

with $psi$ the cotangent class at the vertex. The numerator
exactly turns the contraction into the inverse-Euler-twisted
$eta_F^t$. All remaining leg data are cohomology
correspondences independent of the vertex descendants. For an
external marked point at the leg endpoint, there is no
smoothing denominator; its cotangent class is $-a$, and
contraction with $eta_F^t$ again cancels the matching
numerator.

## Nilpotent denominators and dummy derivatives

The ordinary class $nu$ in $a$ need not descend to $F$, so we
expand in $mathfrak L_m$frakL_m$ before pushing forward. If $a=a_0+nu$
with $a_0=w_Flambda/mne 0$, then

 (1)/(a-s)=sum_{jgeq 0}frac{(-nu)^j}{(a_0-s)^{j+1}}

(finite sum). Let $C$ be a correspondence coefficient on a cut
piece with endpoint map $e$ to $F$, and put
$h_j=e_*(nu^j C)$ (with virtual and Euler factors included).
For any flag function $K$, define

 K(a)h:=sum_{jgeq 0}(1)/(j!),
        ∂_alpha^j K(alpha)big|_{alpha=a_0},h_j,

a dummy scalar $alpha$: the derivative acts only on $K$.
For example $S_f(a)h/(a+z)$ means (eq:dummy-derivatives)
with $K(alpha)=S_f(alpha)/(alpha+z)$. At several flags use
joint moments and independent dummy scalars. A pure ordinary
cohomology vector is the special case with only $h_0$.

Coinvariance and finiteness.:  All graph sums below are
coefficientwise finite: each moving leg has positive tautological
degree, so its multiplicity and the number of legs is bounded at
fixed ample degree; there are finitely many splittings into
effective full curve labels; stability bounds remaining degree
zero pieces once genus and distinguished markings are fixed.

## The genus-zero factor $R$

Fix $Fin{B,X}$. An end at $F$ is a genus-zero unmarked
tree attached to a fixed-map vertex by a moving leg, with the
vertex itself omitted. Its contribution, including the
$(a-s)^{-1}$ denominator at the attachment, is a cohomology-valued
power series $ε_F(s)$ of positive Novikov filtration. A
tail has one external primary insertion $b$ and contributes
$h_b/(a-s)$; the moments $h_b$ absorb curve monomials, stack and
symmetry factors, and the linear dependence on $b$.

End denominators have infinite Taylor expansions; in identities
involving these backgrounds we use the completion
$widehatcH_+=H_F[[z]]$ of the positive space, with localized
Novikov coefficients, and $[,cdot,]_+$ the nonnegative part.
The usual cone identities extend coefficientwise (positive
filtration bounds the number of insertions; nilpotence of
cotangent classes bounds indices).

Let $u_F$ be the primary parameter of the tangent space to the
genus-zero cone of the $F,tw$ theory at background
$ε_F$; equivalently it is determined by

 x_F(z):=[S_f(z)(ε_F(z)-u_F)]_+in zwidehatcH_+,quad
 S_f(z)=S_{F,tw}(u_F,z).

Applying $S_f$ makes the cone point belong to
$zwidehatcH_+$, and $[S_f(z)(-z+u_F)]_+=-z$; the linearization
of the constant term is $-id$ at zero Novikov degree, so $u_F$
exists uniquely and has positive filtration.

The tangent space being the Hessian graph implies that its
two-point function depends on $ε_F$ only through $u_F$.
The genus-zero two-point identity (dimension-counted from
topological recursion) is

 (eta_F^t(b,c))/(w+z)
 +Bigl<
    (b)/(w-psi),(c)/(z-psi)
 Bigr>_{0,ε_F}^{F,tw}
 =(eta_F^t(S_f(w)b,S_f(z)c))/(w+z).

It is rational in $w,z$ at each curve coefficient.

Localize the master matrix element $eta(b,S_W(z)c)$ for $c$
supported on $F$. If the input lies on a fixed-map vertex, the
unmarked outgoing trees provide the background $ε_F$; the
insertion $b$ is either on that vertex ($b|_F$) or on a tail with
attachment $h_b/(a-psi)$. If $b$ is a marked leg endpoint, the
endpoint rule supplies the metric term in (eq:two-point),
with denominator $a+z$. Taking the $w^{-1}$ coefficient of
(eq:two-point) handles a primary slot, and we get

 eta(b,S_W(z)c)=eta_F^t(b|_F,S_f(z)c)
   +sum_{tails}
      (eta_F^t(S_f(a)h_b,S_f(z)c))/(a+z),

giving

 (R(z)^*b)_F=b|_F+
   sum_{tails}(S_f(a)h_b)/(a+z)

as a power series at $z=0$. Consequently $R$ is upper triangular
and

 R(-z)^*R(z)=id,

since both $S_W$ and $S_{bl}$ are symplectic and their
symbolic identities are rational at each curve coefficient.
Every nonidentity term in (eq:R-tail) has a moving leg, so
$R-id$ has positive Novikov filtration.

Translation and bridge.:  The one-point function is
$J_W(-z)=-zS_W(z)^{-1}1$. The string equation identifies it as

where only stable one-marked terms occur. Its regular part on

## From descendants at flags to ancestors

Two ancestor identities convert surviving vertices to ancestor
theory. For a fixed target with pairing $eta^t$, let
$mathcal A(ε;t)$calA(ε;t)$ denote the mixed potential with
distinguished descendants $t(barpsi)$ and additional insertions
$ε(psi)$; the ancestors forget the additional marks.
Parameter $ε$ of positive filtration; $u$ and $x(z)$ as
in (eq:u-equation).

[Vertex conversion]
In summed vertex correlators with background $ε$, an
insertion at a distinguished flag transforms as

 (h)/(a-psi)⟼(S(u,a)h)/(a-barpsi),

also with additional ancestor-class powers at the flag and with
further distinguished flags (all flag evaluation classes in $h$
before applying $S(u,a)$). Moreover, up to a scalar independent
of $t$,

              mathcal A(ε;t)=calA(ε;t)=mathcal A(u;t+x).calA(u;t+x).



Proof. 
These are the ancestor identities of Getzler and
Kontsevich--Manin, in the form used by Coates--Givental--Tseng;
we spell out the arguments because the background has
descendants.

The identity
$(1)/(a-psi)=(1)/(a-barpsi)
+(psi-barpsi)/((a-psi)(a-barpsi))$
reduces (eq:flag-dressing) to the splitting divisor of
$psi-barpsi$. That divisor separates a genus-zero twig at the
mark, a subset of background marks, and an attaching node.
Splitting the virtual class contracts the twig's two-point
function with the rest of the vertex. Summing all twigs plus the
direct term gives $S(u,a)h$ by the primary-slot instance of
(eq:two-point). Ancestor classes at the original markings
are pulled back from the stabilized curve, so they do not enter
the twig calculation. For a tangent class with nilpotent part,
apply the scalar identity then the finite dummy derivatives of
(eq:dummy-derivatives).

For (eq:mixed-potential), write $ε=u+(ε-u)$
and expand multilinearly. Forget the primary $u$ marks alone; the
splitting argument converts the remaining descendants to
$x(barpsi)=[S(u,z)(ε(z)-u)]_+|_{z=barpsi}$. The
original ancestors still forget the $x$ marks; the new ones
retain them. Their cotangent-class difference has a boundary
divisor with a rational tail carrying one original mark and
$kgeq 1$ marks to be forgotten; its stable-curve factor is
$mathcal Mbar_calMbar_{0,k+2}$ of dimension $k-1$; every $x$
insertion has positive ancestor power (since $xin
zwidehatcH_+$), so the product restricts to at least degree
$k$ and vanishes. Applying this at every distinguished flag
replaces original ancestors by those retaining all $x$ marks;
multilinearity gives (eq:mixed-potential). Terms that only
become stable after adding $x$ contribute only a scalar (in
genus zero, positive ancestor powers exceed the stable-curve
dimension; in genus one, no original distinguished mark leaves a
scalar).


## Assembly

Fix a stable map contributing to a stable ancestor invariant of
$W$. Forget the map and stabilize the distinguished curve. Every
moving leg disappears (it has at most two special points).
Prune unmarked rational trees and suppress rational chains with
two remaining flags. Surviving fixed-map factors are kept with
their own stabilization and internal boundary. Removed pieces
between surviving vertices are bridges; removed pieces
ending in a distinguished marking are tails; all others
are ends.

Ends give the background $ε_F$. Lemma~lem:vertex-conversion
converts bridge and tail denominators to ancestor denominators and
inserts the factors $S_f(a)$. Bridges become exactly
$D(barpsi,barpsi')$. Direct markings together with all tails
become, using (eq:R-tail),
[
 R(-z)^*t(z)=R(z)^{-1}t(z),qquad z=barpsi.
]
Finally (eq:mixed-potential) replaces $ε$ by $u_F$
and adds $x_F$. Combined with (eq:translation) the vertex
substitution is

 t(z)⟼ R(z)^{-1}t(z)+z(1-R(z)^{-1}1).


Quantize this substitution. If
$D(s,s')=sum_{i,jgeq 0}D_{ij}s^i(s')^j$, the upper-triangular
contraction operator is $hbar/2$-times the second derivative
with coefficients $D_{ij}$, using the categorical inverse of
$eta^{tw}$. Exponentiating it on the product of fixed
ancestors and applying (eq:Wick-substitution) is exactly
$U_R$: the kernel is (eq:bridge-kernel), and the signs
$-w,-z$ come from the negative Darboux modes $(-z)^{-i-1}$. The
graph sum reproduces this: labeling flags first and then dividing
by permutations produces the same factorials and graph
automorphisms; a bridge or self-bridge contributes one factor of
$hbar$; super signs and permutations are exactly those of the
tensor rules.

Components without distinguished markings may multiply
$mathcal A_W$calA_W$ by an invertible scalar; this is the $c$ in
(eq:A-factorization). The invertibility of $U_R$ is
coefficientwise: $R-id$ has positive filtration so its inverse
and logarithm are defined degree by degree; at each fixed
coefficient the number of contributing filtration-positive
factors is finite; ancestor powers are bounded by $3g-3+n$; fixed
descendant identities are bounded by fixed-target stable-map
dimensions. This completes the proof of
Proposition~prop:loc-factorization.

# Grading and the Iritani shift

We now build two commuting operators on the equivariant quantum
cohomology of $W$: the calibrated grading $G_a$, and Iritani's
shift $T_a$, which translates $lambdamapstolambda+z$. The
$T_a$ admits a simple closed formula on each fixed component, and
its spectrum on fiber classes has $r+1$ branches that will
organize the transfer.

Throughout $star$ means the small equivariant quantum product of
$W$ at primary background zero. Put
[
 c=c_1^T(TW),qquad kappa=c_1(TB)+c_1(E).
]
The equivariant projective-bundle formula gives

 c=(r+1)p+pi^*kappa+lambda,qquad
 int_{(beta,l)}c_1(TW)=(r+1)l+int_betakappa.
 

Choose a homogeneous Hodge basis of $H^*(B)$ and the resulting
polynomial basis $pi^*b,p^j$ ($0leq jleq r$) of $H_T^*(W)$
over $C[lambda]$. In this basis, $mu$ acts by the first Hodge
degree minus $dim_C(W)/2$, and does not differentiate the
$lambda$-coefficient.

## The fiber spectrum of the shift

Let $Q^{>0}$ be the ideal of positive base degree. Since an
effective nonzero base class has positive ample degree, $Q^{>0}$
retains exactly the maps with constant projection to $B$. The
$y$-variable is retained.


On $H_T^*(W)$ with the fiber quantum product,

 T_abmod(z,Q^{>0})=(p+lambda)star.
 



Proof. 
The fiber quantum product is linear over $H^*(B)$ (all
genus-zero fiber evaluations have the same projection to $B$,
so base classes pull out of pushforward), and $S_W$ in the
reduction is likewise $H^*(B)$-linear. So $T_a$ is
$H^*(B)$-linear.

Write $D_y=y∂_y$. The divisor equation gives
$S_W(zD_y-pcup)S_W^{-1}=zD_y-pstar$, and the left operator
commutes with $T_d$. On $X$, the multiplier has no $y$ factor
and $p|_X$ is independent of $lambda$. On $B$, $p|_B=-lambda$
and $[zD_y,T_d|_B]=zT_d|_B$, $[lambda,T_d|_B]=-zT_d|_B$, so
the two terms cancel there too. Reducing modulo $z$ proves that
$T_a|_{z=0}$ commutes with $pstar$.

Assign cohomological degree one to $p,lambda,z$ and degree
$r+1$ to $y$. Polynomiality in $lambda$ forces $pstar p^k$
to equal the classical power for $kleq r$; these powers form
an $H^*(B)[lambda]$-basis, so an $H^*(B)[lambda]$-linear
operator commuting with $pstar$ is determined by its value on
$1$. In this grading $T_a$ has degree one (equivalently,
(eq:section-dimension) at $beta=0$ is
$dim W+1+(r+1)l$, and each fiber inclusion raises insertion
degree by one); so $T_a(1)|_{z=0}$ has no positive-$y$
coefficient. At $y=0$ we have $S_W=1$, and
(eq:shift-fixed) applied to $1$ restricts to $p+lambda$
on $X$ and to zero on $B$; these are the restrictions of
$p+lambda$ to the fixed locus. Thus $T_a(1)|_{z=0}=p+lambda$,
and (eq:seidel) follows.


Let $I=H^{>0}(B;C)$; it is nilpotent, also in the presence of
odd classes. Reducing the fiber quantum algebra modulo $I$ gives

 C[lambda,y,p]big/(p^r(p+lambda)-y).
 

Indeed, the classical relation reduces to $p^r(p+lambda)=0$; in
degree $r+1$ the only possible positive curve correction is a
constant multiple of $y$. Its coefficient is the degree-one
fiber line invariant:
[

 =1,
]
since two general point conditions determine a unique line and
the remaining marked point meets a general hyperplane in one

coefficient has degree zero, so may be computed at $lambda=0$.


For generic $(y,lambda)$, the distinct eigenvalue branches of
$T_abmod(z,Q^{>0})$ are

 lambda+h,qquad h^r(h+lambda)=y.
 

Each branch may have nonzero multiplicity and nilpotent part. For
$lambdane 0$, near $y=0$ one branch tends to $0$ and a cluster
of $r$ branches tends to $lambda$.


Proof. 
On $H^*(B)[lambda,y,p]/(I)$, Lemmas~lem:fiber-operator
and (eq:fiber-relation) give the eigenvalues. The finite
filtration by powers of $I$ is preserved, and each graded piece
of the induced operator is the same fiber multiplication tensored
with $I^m/I^{m+1}$, so the characteristic polynomial has the same

Translating by $lambda$ gives the final assertion. The argument

cohomology.


Polynomial dependence of $G_a,T_a$ and finiteness of the spectral

still compare the individual descendant theories on $B$ and $X$.

# The ordinary theory on a fixed component
% ==================================================================

The operators of Section~sec:spec were built from the
equivariant theory of $W$. This section identifies what their
constraints mean near $y=0$: on a fixed component, Quantum
Riemann--Roch removes the inverse-Euler twist, and the logarithm
of the shift becomes a translation generator in $lambda$. Its
derivative term cancels the derivative term of the equivariant
grading, leaving the ordinary grading plus a scalar multiple of
$z^{-1}$.


Fix a germ at $lambdaneq 0$. Near $Q=y=0$, use the full Novikov
completion with a formal $log y$ adjoined, allowing polynomial
dependence on $log y$ in each coefficient. Let $F=B$ denote the

the eigenvalue $lambda$. Use the projectors and logarithms of
Proposition~prop:spec-zero, and let $A_{k,F}$ be their modes.
Then, over this coefficient ring,
[
 mathcal A_WcalA_W  satisfies every A_{k,F} projectively
 ;⇔;
 Z_F  satisfies every ell_{k,F} projectively,
 quad kgeq -1.
]
The operators on the left are infinitesimal symplectic for the
equivariant pairing of $W$. On the right, $Z_F$ is the ordinary
descendant potential with its full cohomology and actual integral

operators, after a change of paired space, become the ordinary
Virasoro modes plus a scalar shift. Then we show that this change
of space is realized by the quantum Riemann--Roch multiplier and,
after separating the rank factor, that the resulting quantized
equations are those of the actual Euler twist.

when $w=-1$, use $Phi(v-z)-Phi(v)=log(v-z)$ to cancel the

exponential in (eq:fixed-shift-conjugation).

For the logarithm (as opposed to the exponential form), set
$H=z∂_lambda-Θ_F'(lambda)$. Its evolution is

 e^{tH}=exp(frac{Θ_F(lambda)-Θ_F(lambda+tz)}
 {z})e^{tz∂_lambda};

differentiating the right side in $t$ gives $H$ times that side,
and the value at $t=0$ is the identity. The functional calculus
of Lemma~lem:calculus agrees (coefficientwise
differentiation of the contour integral gives the same evolution).
The $z$-constant term is $-Θ_F'(lambda)$; locally choose a
logarithm that inverts the exponential at that scalar; the
composition rule in Lemma~lem:calculus then identifies
$log(e^H)$ with $H$. Conjugating by $Delta_F$ is allowed by
Lemma~lem:gauge. For $F=B$, apply this to the divided shift
$y^{-1}T_F$ and restore $log y$; for $F=X$, apply it directly to
$T_F$. These are exactly the logarithm prescriptions of
Proposition~prop:spec-zero, giving
(eq:fixed-log-conjugation) up to the logarithm-branch
scalar.


Subtracting $lambda/z$ times (eq:fixed-log-conjugation)
from (eq:fixed-grading-conjugation) removes
$lambda∂_lambda$. Thus the operator $D_F$, after the
descendant change of space and then $Delta_F$, becomes

 ell_{0,F}+(b_F)/(z),qquad
 b_F=lambda(nw-jlog y-nwlog(wlambda)),

up to a scalar multiple of $lambda/z$ from a logarithm choice.
In particular it differentiates neither $lambda$, $y$, nor the
Novikov variables.

For a scalar $b$ independent of $z$, write
[
 ell_{-1,F}^{(b)}=z^{-1},qquad
 ell_{k,F}^{(b)}=z^{-1}(zell_{0,F}+b)^{k+1}quad(kgeq 0).
]
The binomial identity

 ell_{k,F}^{(b)}=sum_{m=-1}^{k}binom{k+1}{m+1}
 b^{k-m}ell_{m,F}

is triangular with diagonal entries one. Consequently these
operators are infinitesimal symplectic, and their simultaneous
projective equations are equivalent to those of the ordinary
modes. This completes the classical identification. Now we lift
it to the potentials.

## Quantizing the comparison

The $Delta_F$ above contains $-log(wlambda)c_N/z$, a
classical multiplication that we cannot directly exponentiate in
$lambda^{-1}$. We therefore first use the normalized class

 c_0(N_F)=prod_x(1+x/a)^{-1},

with $Z_{F,ntw}$ and $eta_F^{ntw}$ the
corresponding descendant potential and pairing. Define
$Delta_{F,0}$ by (eq:Delta-F) with $-log(a+x)$ replaced by
$-log(1+x/a)$ and $tfrac12log(a+x)$ by $tfrac12log(1+x/a)$.
It maps the ordinary paired space to
$(cH_F,eta_F^{ntw})$ and is the identity modulo
$lambda^{-1}$.

[Quantum Riemann--Roch in the normalized twist]
With quantization and dilaton translations as in
Section~sec:conv,

 Z_{F,ntw}=(invertible scalar),
  U_{Delta_{F,0}}Z_F.

This is an invertible identity formal in $lambda^{-1}$ and
coefficientwise in the full Novikov variables and $hbar$.


Proof. 
Quantum Riemann--Roch of Coates--Givental says that for a
multiplicative class whose logarithm on a line with first Chern
class $x$ is $s(x)$, its multiplier (in the fixed ordinary
pairing) has exponent
[
 sum_xBigl{frac1zint_0^x s(t)dt
   +sum_{mgeq 1}frac{B_{2m}}{(2m)!}z^{2m-1}s^{(2m-1)}(x)Bigr},
]
and it identifies the twisted and ordinary pairings by
multiplication by $√(c_0(N_F))$. Expressing the conclusion
as a map to the actual twisted space adds $-s(x)/2$ to the
exponent. Taking $s(x)=-log(1+x/a)$ gives exactly
$Delta_{F,0}$.

The affine-coordinate bookkeeping matches: in the ordinary
Fock space the twisted potential uses
$widetilde q=√(c_0(N_F))(t-z1_F)$, and multiplication by
$c_0(N_F)^{-1/2}$ returns the twisted Darboux coordinate
$q_{ntw}=t-z1_F$. Thus $U_{Delta_{F,0}}$ has the
dilaton translations of (eq:qrr). The theorem applies to
$Rpi_*ev^*N_F$ on the ordinary stable-map spaces of the smooth
projective $F$, is invertible and formal in $lambda^{-1}$, and
uses the categorical inverse for nodal contractions. So it holds
in our Koszul conventions, for arbitrary Hodge types and parity.


The two classical multipliers are related simply:

 Delta_F=a^{n/2}exp(-log(a)c_N/z),Delta_{F,0}.

If $C$ is an ordinary divisor class, then
$[ell_{0,F},C/z]=[z∂_z,C/z]+[mu_F,C/z]=0$ and
$[z,C/z]=0$ (the two contributions being $-C/z$ and $+C/z$,
since $R_F$ commutes with $C$). So $C/z$ commutes with all
ordinary modes and with their scalar translates
(eq:fixed-binomial). After the coefficient derivatives are
removed in (eq:fixed-ordinary-grading), the scalar $a^{n/2}$
also cancels in conjugation. Consequently $Delta_F$ and
$Delta_{F,0}$ produce the same conjugated mode matrices. By
Lemma~lem:qrr and Lemma~lem:covariance, the equations
for those matrices on $Z_{F,ntw}$ are equivalent to the
ordinary projective equations on $Z_F$.

It remains to restore the rank factor omitted in
(eq:normalized-class). This changes the paired space as
well as the invariants, and both must be handled together.

[Rank scaling]
Put $m_F=a^{-n}$. The actual inverse-Euler twist is obtained from
the normalized twist by the substitutions

 hbar⟼hbar/m_F,qquad
 Q^beta y^l⟼ Q^beta y^l a^{-int_d c_N}

(where $d$ is the corresponding actual class on $F$). Its pairing
is $eta_F^t=m_Feta_F^{ntw}$. Under these substitutions
the quadratic quantizations of
$Delta_{F,0}ell_{k,F}Delta_{F,0}^{-1}$ (for every $kgeq -1$)
in the two paired spaces agree.


Proof. 
On $mathcal Mbar_calMbar_{g,m}(F,d)$, Riemann--Roch gives
$rank Rpi_*ev^*N_F=n(1-g)+int_d c_N$. The ratio of the
inverse-Euler class to the normalized class is therefore
$a^{-n(1-g)-int_d c_N}=m_F^{1-g}a^{-int_d c_N}$, exactly the
change in the coefficient of $hbar^{g-1}Q^beta y^l$ under
(eq:scaling).

The $qq$ part of quantization uses $eta/hbar$; the $pp$ part
uses $hbareta^{-1}$. In the actual twist these become
$m_Feta^{ntw}/hbar$ and
$(hbar/m_F)(eta^{ntw})^{-1}$, exactly the normalized
counterparts at $hbar/m_F$. The mixed part is unchanged. Since
$Delta_{F,0}ell_{k,F}Delta_{F,0}^{-1}$ has no Novikov
derivatives or Novikov dependence, the curve substitution
commutes with its action. Restore the scalar $b_F$ afterwards by
(eq:fixed-binomial). Each curve monomial is multiplied by a
nonzero scalar, so the substitution is injective and reversible
without identifying curve classes.


Rationality in $lambda$.:  The above used QRR only as a
formal identity in $lambda^{-1}$. To pass to germs at nonzero
$lambda$, first remove $b_F$ by (eq:fixed-binomial). Each
coefficient of the conjugated matrices
$Delta_{F,0}ell_{k,F}Delta_{F,0}^{-1}$ is then rational in
$lambda$; the matrices have finite $z$-lower bounds. The
coefficients of $Z_{F,tw}$ are also rational in
$lambda$ (the torus acts trivially on $F$, the inverse Euler
class expands only up to finite cohomological dimension, and
descendant indices are bounded at fixed genus, class and number of
marks). Then each nonconstant coefficient of
$Z_{F,tw}^{-1}op_{eta_F^t}
(Delta_{F,0}ell_{k,F}Delta_{F,0}^{-1})Z_{F,tw}$
is a finite sum of rational functions of $lambda$; vanishing of
its Laurent expansion at infinity is equivalent to vanishing as a
germ. At each genus and curve coefficient, (eq:scaling)
only shifts finitely many $lambda$-powers, so the argument runs
in both directions. We then restore $b_F$ and its logarithms via
(eq:fixed-binomial). No analytic value of the quantized
infinite multiplier is taken.

subsection{Proof of Proposition~prop:fixed-equivalence}

Let $F'$ be the other fixed component. Up to invertible scalars,
the potential chain is
[

 Z_Fxrightarrow{U_{Delta_{F,0}}}Z_{F,ntw}
    xrightarrow{(eq:scaling)}Z_{F,tw},
 Z_{F,tw}xrightarrow{U_{S_f}}
    mathcal A_calA_{F,tw}(u_F),
 mathcal A_calA_{F,tw}(u_F),mathcal A_calA_{F',tw}(u_{F'})
    xrightarrow{U_R}mathcal A_W.calA_W.

]
The arrow (eq:scaling) changes parameters and rescales the
pairing per Lemma~lem:rank-scaling. The final row first
adjoins the other fixed potential and then applies the upper
symplectic transformation.

(eq:fixed-binomial), Lemma~lem:qrr and
Lemma~lem:rank-scaling identify projective satisfaction of
all ordinary modes on $Z_F$ with projective satisfaction of the
corresponding descendant modes on $Z_{F,tw}$. The
rationality argument above gives the equivalence over the same
germs in $lambda$ used by the spectral construction.

Next, pass from descendants on $F$ to ancestors at $u_F$: the
ancestor--descendant identity (eq:ancestor-descendant) and
Lemma~lem:covariance conjugate the mode matrices by
$S_f=S_{F,tw}(u_F,z)$ and transport their projective
equations to $mathcal A_calA_{F,tw}(u_F)$. On the product of
the two fixed potentials, an operator supported on $F$ acts only
on that factor, so its projective equation is equivalent to the
equation on the single factor.

Finally, apply $R(z)$ of Proposition~prop:loc-factorization.
The identities
[
 S_W=R(z)⊕_F S_f,qquad
 mathcal A_W=(scalar)calA_W=(scalar),U_Rprod_Fmathcal A_calA_{F,tw}(u_F)

show, by Proposition~prop:spec-zero, that the resulting

All quantized changes are defined in the Novikov completion:

# Continuation and the ordinary constraints
% ==================================================================

The fixed-component comparison converts the hypothesis on $B$ into
equations on one spectral branch of the ancestor theory of $W$. We
continue them to every branch. Finiteness comes from three sources:
each coefficient test is a finite sum (via ancestor bounds);
continuation of the branch is holomorphic in each coefficient; and
the branch cover is connected outside its critical values. Then
adding the $r$ branches of $X$ and using the same comparison
gives the ordinary constraints on $X$.

## Polynomial coefficients and ancestor bounds

Write $logmathcal A_W=calA_W=sum_{ggeq 0}hbar^{g-1}mathcal F_g$calF_g$ for
the connected ancestor potential at background zero, taken in the
polynomial equivariant basis of Section~sec:geo (no
localization of invariants themselves).

[Polynomial coefficients and ancestor bounds]
Fix a genus $g$, an actual base class $beta$, and insertions
$b_ibarpsi_i^{a_i}$, $1leq ileq n$. Their connected invariant
in $W$, summed over fiber degree with weight $y^l$, is polynomial
in $y$ and $lambda$. It vanishes unless the distinguished curve
is stable and

                   sum_i a_i leq 3g-3+n.
 

The bound is independent of $beta,l,lambda$.


Proof. 
Curve-unstable ancestor terms are omitted by definition. For
stable data the cotangent-class product is pulled back from
$mathcal Mbar_calMbar_{g,n}$, of dimension $3g-3+n$, so
(eq:ancestor-bound) holds before integration, without any
equivariant degree input.

The virtual dimension of $mathcal Mbar_calMbar_{g,n}(W,(beta,l))$
is
[
 (1-g)(dim_C W-3)+n+int_betakappa+(r+1)l,qquad
 kappa=c_1(TB)+c_1(E),
]
and insertion degrees are fixed. Proper equivariant pushforward
lands in $C[lambda]$ (no negative cohomological degree), so the
integral vanishes once the virtual dimension exceeds total
insertion degree. Since $lgeq 0$, only finitely many $l$
contribute at fixed $beta,g,n$. Each remaining integral is
polynomial in $lambda$.


## Equations as germs and finite coefficient tests

Modes on the $B$ branch are infinitesimal symplectic at $y=0$ by
Proposition~prop:fixed-equivalence; their entries are finite
$log y$-polynomials with coefficients holomorphic at zero
(Proposition~prop:spec-zero). Taylor expansion is injective
on this ring, so the symplectic identities hold as germs on a
punctured sector; they continue entrywise along any path on
which the modes themselves continue.

For a continued branch and logarithm, set

 mathcal E_calE_{k,i}
 =mathcal A_W^calA_W^{-1}op(A_{k,i})mathcal A_WcalA_W ,
 

meaning the operator acts on $mathcal A_W$calA_W$ then multiplies by
$mathcal A_W^calA_W^{-1}$. For the entire $X$ cluster,
$mathcal E_calE_{k,X}$ uses $A_{k,X}=sum_{iin X}A_{k,i}$ with a
single logarithm. Projective satisfaction means every positive-
degree (insertion) coefficient of (eq:continuation-equation)
vanishes.

[Finite coefficient tests]
Fix $kgeq -1$, $hbar^{G-1}$, base class $beta$, and an
insertion monomial of total degree $N$ in
(eq:continuation-equation). Its coefficient is a finite sum
of holomorphic germs on the spectral covering of generic
$(y,lambda)$, with logarithms as chosen, and continues along
every path in that locus. Near $y=0$ the coefficient of
$mathcal E_calE_{k,B}$ is a finite $log y$-polynomial with
coefficients holomorphic through zero; the coefficient of
$mathcal E_calE_{k,X}$ is holomorphic through zero. Its Taylor
expansion is the corresponding formal Novikov coefficient test.


Proof. 
Let $F=logmathcal A_W$calA_W$ in positive loop coordinates $q$. A
normally ordered quadratic operator has schematic form
[
 (1)/(2hbar)C(q,q)
   +sum_{alpha,gamma}B_{alphagamma}q^gamma∂_alpha
   +(hbar)/(2)sum_{alpha,gamma}
     D_{alphagamma}∂_alpha∂_gamma,
]
with graded-symmetric tensors. Dividing its action on $e^F$ by
$e^F$ replaces $∂_alpha$ by $∂_alpha F$ and
$∂_alpha∂_gamma$ by
$∂_alpha∂_gamma F
+(∂_alpha F)(∂_gamma F)$.

At $hbar^{G-1}$: the first-derivative term involves
$mathcal F_G$;calF_G$; the second-derivative term involves
$mathcal F_calF_{G-1}$ and products $mathcal F_gcalF_gmathcal F_h$calF_h$ with
$g+h=G$; the multiplication term occurs only at $hbar^{-1}$. For
a fixed output monomial, the connected potential has at most
$N+2$ distinguished marks, and Lemma~lem:ancestor-bound
bounds every differentiated descendant index uniformly in $l$.

By Lemma~lem:modes, $A_{k,i}$ has loop powers at least
$-1$, finite $z∂_z$ order; at a fixed test monomial, the
$pp$ part uses only bounded indices; the $pq$ part uses a bounded
differentiated index plus a multiplied index in the output
monomial or the dilaton; the $qq$ part bounds index pairs by the
order in $z∂_z$. So each part uses finitely many loop
coefficients of the operator, and the $z$ derivatives only
multiply by polynomials in the mode indices.

At fixed $beta$, Novikov finiteness leaves finitely many
decompositions into base classes of the operator and the
$mathcal F_g$'s.calF_g$'s. The $mathcal F_g$'scalF_g$'s are polynomials in $y$
(Lemma~lem:ancestor-bound); operator entries are holomorphic
germs continuing with the branches
(Lemma~lem:modes); the pairing and its inverse are fixed
rational matrices in $lambda$ (we work at $lambdaneq 0$).
Finiteness, holomorphy and continuation follow.

Finally, Proposition~prop:spec-zero gives: $X$-cluster
coefficients holomorphic through zero; $B$-branch coefficients
polynomial in $log y$ with coefficients holomorphic through zero.
All the operations in the coefficient test are finite, so Taylor
expansion commutes with the test and yields the formal identity of
Proposition~prop:fixed-equivalence.


A finite sum $sum_{j=0}^Jf_j(y)(log y)^j$, $f_j$ holomorphic at
zero, whose formal series in $y$ and $log y$ vanishes is equal to
zero on a punctured sector (each Taylor coefficient of every $f_j$
vanishes). This is the bridge from formal to analytic (coefficient
by coefficient).

## Transport between fixed components

Fix $lambdane 0$. The branch cover is

               y=h^r(h+lambda).
 

Its critical values are $0$ and
$(-r)^rlambda^{r+1}/(r+1)^{r+1}$. Removing those gives an
unramified covering of degree $r+1$.


The monodromy of (eq:cover) acts transitively on its $r+1$
branches.


Proof. 
The inverse image of the complement of the critical values is the
$h$-plane with finitely many points removed; it is connected and
path connected. Given two points over a regular value, join them
by a path in this inverse image; its projection is a loop at that
value whose lift connects the two points. That is transitivity.



If the ordinary descendant potential of $B$ satisfies its ordinary
modes projectively, then the same holds for $X$.


Proof. 
By Proposition~prop:fixed-equivalence, the hypothesis gives
all projective equations for $A_{k,B}$ on $mathcal A_W$calA_W$ near
$y=0$. Lemma~lem:finite-tests turns their formal coefficient
identities into identities of germs on a punctured sector; the
$B$-branch infinitesimal symplecticity also holds there as germs
of the fixed comparison, and is entrywise linear so continues with
the operators.

Choose a regular value in this sector. Every coefficient of every
projective equation continues along any loop based there. The
ancestor coefficients themselves return unchanged (polynomial in
$y$ at each fixed base class). The only change is in the continued
spectral branch and logarithm of its modes. By
Lemma~lem:monodromy, the continued $B$ branch can be any of
the $r+1$ branches. Hence projective equations and symplecticity
hold on each branch.

Continuing a logarithm may add $2pi iZ$ times $P$-supported
$lambda z^{-1}$; by Lemma~lem:modes this is an invertible
triangular binomial change. The simultaneous equations therefore
hold for any chosen local logarithm on each branch.

Return to a punctured neighborhood of $y=0$ and choose one
logarithm on the whole $X$ cluster. Its projectors sum to $P_X$,
its logarithm to $mathscr L_X$;scrL_X$; in particular $D_X=sum_{iin X}D_i$.
Since $(zD_i)(zD_j)=0$ for $ineq j$, the sum's modes are the
sums of the branches' modes, and quantization is linear in the
modes, so the constant from projective satisfaction is
insertion-independent.

Proposition~prop:spec-zero extends the cluster operators
holomorphically through $y=0$. Taylor coefficients of the
equations are legitimate by Lemma~lem:finite-tests. Then
Proposition~prop:fixed-equivalence applied in the reverse
direction with $F=X$ gives the result for $Z_X$ with the full
curve labels.


## The prescribed scalar normalization

Projective equations carry insertion-independent scalars. For the
ordinary Virasoro operators, two commutators remove them, and the
characteristic constant $C_Y$ is produced.


For every smooth projective complex variety $Y$, with the
operators of Section~sec:conv,

 [L_{-1}^Y,L_1^Y]=-2L_0^Y,qquad
 [L_0^Y,L_k^Y]=-kL_k^Y
 quad(kgeq -1, kneq 0).
 



Proof. 
The classical identities
[
 [ell_{-1,Y},ell_{1,Y}]=2ell_{0,Y},qquad
 [ell_{0,Y},ell_{k,Y}]=kell_{k,Y}
]
follow from $[mu_Y,R_Y]=R_Y$ and $[ell_{0,Y},z]=z$. Normal
ordering in our Hamiltonian sign convention flips the commutator
sign and adds a contraction scalar between the two opposite
off-diagonal polarization blocks. We compute both cases.

For the first commutator, $z^{-1}$ crosses from nonnegative to
negative polarization only at mode zero. The nonnegative output of
$ell_{1,Y}$ applied to the mode $(-z)^{-1}a$ is
$(1/4-mu_Y^2)a$ in mode zero (the $R_Y$-terms still have
negative powers at that output). Double-contracting the mode-zero
quadratic terms in the two orders gives
[
 -frac12str(1/4-mu_Y^2)
 =-(chi(Y))/(8)+frac12str(mu_Y^2)
 =-2C_Y.
]
The sign is checked on Darboux summands: on an even coordinate
$[q^2/(2hbar),hbar m∂_q^2/2]=-mq∂_q-m/2$
(scalar $-m/2$ per even line); on an odd paired plane
$[thetapsi/hbar,-hbar m∂_psi∂_theta]
=m(1-theta∂_theta-psi∂_psi)$
(scalar $+m$ per odd pair). This is exactly $-tfrac12str(M)$
for $M=1/4-mu_Y^2$. Thus
$[L_{-1}^Y,L_1^Y]=-2(op(ell_{0,Y})+C_Y)=-2L_0^Y$.

For the second commutator with $k>0$, the only crossing of
$ell_{0,Y}$ is its $R_Y/z$ part at mode zero. The opposite
crossing of $ell_{k,Y}$ uses $k-1$ factors of $R_Y$ (in
$z^{-1}(zell_{0,Y})^{k+1}$, a term raising loop power by one needs
exactly this many zero-power $R_Y$ factors). Its contraction with
the first crossing raises first Hodge degree by $k$; such
endomorphisms have zero trace and zero supertrace. For $k=-1$
both crossings have the same direction, so no contraction. Thus
the second commutator has no scalar. Translating $q=t-z1_Y$
preserves all commutators.



Projective satisfaction of all ordinary modes by $Z_Y$ implies
$V(Y)$ with exactly the constant $C_Y$ in $L_0^Y$.


Proof. 
Projective satisfaction says $L_k^YZ_Y=c_kZ_Y$ with $c_k$
independent of insertion variables. The operators differentiate
neither Novikov nor $hbar$, so they commute with every $c_j$.
Hence their commutators annihilate $Z_Y$. By
Lemma~lem:commutators, $-2L_0^YZ_Y=0$ first; then
$-kL_k^YZ_Y=0$ in the remaining range.


## End of the proof of the main theorem

The hypothesis $V(B)$ gives projective satisfaction on $B$;
Proposition~prop:projective-transfer transports it to $X$;
Corollary~cor:exact finishes with the prescribed constant.

What was preserved.:  All constructions used full
cohomology with categorical inverse pairings. Curve splittings,
the shift correspondence, and the final Taylor expansion
preserved actual integral homology labels. The conclusion
therefore holds for every genus and curve class, with arbitrary
ordinary descendant insertions $b_ipsi_i^{a_i}$ for
$b_iin H^*(X;C)$ including primitive and odd classes.
Iteration over a tower of projective bundles is immediate.

Sanity check (a small example).:  Take $B=pt$, $rgeq 1$, so $X=mathbb P^r$.bbP^r$. The state
space $H^*(pt)$ is one-dimensional, so $V(pt)$
reduces to a trivial one-variable computation and holds; the
theorem then produces $V(mathbb P^r)$,bbP^r)$, matching the known toric
case. Iterating covers $V$ for towers of projective bundles
over projective spaces, over target curves, and over any base
known to satisfy $V$, without semisimplicity or splitness
assumptions.
