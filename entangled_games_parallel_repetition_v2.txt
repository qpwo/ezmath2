# Threshold parallel repetition for finite-dimensional entangled games


maketitle

Abstract: 
Let G be a finite two-player one-round game with entangled value
v=omega^*(G)<1. Suppose two players play k independent copies of
G in parallel and may entangle their answers across all copies. We
prove that, for every 0<delta<1-v, the probability that they win at
least a (v+delta)-fraction of the copies is at most
exp(-c,delta^5 k/(1+log d)), where d=|A|calA|,|B|calB|
and c>0 is universal. The bound is uniform over all finite-dimensional
joint strategies, holds at every repetition length kge1 (including
small k, where ceiling effects matter), and allows arbitrary
correlated question distributions with zero entries or disconnected
support. For each fixed question distribution we also prove the
sharper-in-form bound
exp(-c',delta^3 k/((1+kappa_mu)(1+log d))), in which the
constant kappa_mu depends only on the positives of the question
distribution. Either bound amplifies both completeness and soundness of
a one-round two-prover protocol without changing the number of provers,
the number of rounds, or the classical nature of the messages.

# Introduction

## The formal model

A finite two-player one-round game is a tuple
[
 G = (X,Y,A,B,mu,V)
]
consisting of:
 nonempty finite question alphabets X,Y;
 nonempty finite answer alphabets A,B;
 a probability distribution mu on X;
 a Boolean predicate V:X YcalYtimesA BcalBto{0,1}.


A finite-dimensional entangled strategy for G consists of a
unit vector psiinH_A ⊗ H_B in a
finite-dimensional tensor product of Hilbert spaces, together with:
 a positive operator-valued measurement (POVM) on H_A
        for each xinX, i.e. positive operators
        {M_x^a}_{ainA} with sum_a M_x^a = I;
 a POVM on H_B for each yinY, i.e.
        positive operators {N_y^b}_{binB} with
        sum_b N_y^b=I.

The strategy wins with probability
[
 p_psi(M,N)
 =sum_{x,y,a,b}mu(x,y),V(x,y,a,b),
   <psi|M_x^a ⊗  N_y^b|psi> .
]
The entangled value is
[
 v=omega^*(G)=sup_{psi,M,N} p_psi(M,N),
]
where the supremum is over all finite dimensions and all such data. It
need not be attained. Taking psi to be pure causes no loss: any mixed
finite-dimensional state can be purified by adjoining a finite ancilla.

## Repetition and the threshold event

The k-fold repetition G^k is the game in which the referee samples
(X_1,Y_1),...,(X_k,Y_k) independently from mu, sends the entire
list X_1,...,X_k to Alice and Y_1,...,Y_k to Bob, and accepts
(A_1,...,A_k) and (B_1,...,B_k) when all k coordinate pairs
satisfy V. A general k-coordinate strategy is any
finite-dimensional entangled strategy for this game; it does not need to
be a tensor product of single-game strategies.

Write
[
 I_i = V(X_i,Y_i,A_i,B_i)in{0,1},
 W_k = I_1+...+I_k,
]
and set d=|A|calA|,|B|.calB|. All logarithms are natural.

## Main theorem

[Threshold parallel repetition]
There is a universal constant kappa_0>0 with the following property.
Let G be a finite two-player one-round game with Boolean acceptance
predicate and entangled value v=omega^*(G)<1. For every
0<delta<1-v, every integer kge1, and every finite-dimensional
joint strategy for the k-fold repetition of G,

 Pr![W_kgelceil(v+delta)krceil]
 le exp!(-(kappa_0,delta^5)/(1+log d),k).

The same inequality holds for the supremum over all such strategies.


Four features of (eq:main) deserve emphasis:
 it is uniform over the dimension of the strategy;
 mu may be any distribution on X,
        including correlated mu with zeros or disconnected support;
 the rate depends on |A|,|calA|,|B|calB| only through
        the product d; it does not involve |X|calX| or
        |Y|;calY|;
 the bound holds for every kge1, not just asymptotically.
        The ceiling in lceil(v+delta)krceil matters at small k
        and is handled explicitly.


## A distribution-dependent cubic bound

We also prove a bound whose exponent is cubic in delta, at the cost
of a constant that depends on the positives of mu. Let
[
 H = {(x,y)inX Y:calY:mu(x,y)>0}
]
be the support of mu, and form its connection graph:
its vertices are the pairs in H, and two vertices are joined when they
share a coordinate. Let H_1,...,H_J be the connected components,
lambda_j=mu(H_j), mu_j=mu(·mid H_j) the conditional law on
component j, and
[
 mu_{j,min}=min_{zin H_j}mu_j(z).
]
Define

 kappa_mu = 8,max_{1le jle J}frac{|H_j|-1}{mu_{j,min}} .

Singletons contribute 0. The constant kappa_mu is finite because
each component is finite and every positive mass is positive.

[Cubic bound for fixed mu]
With the same hypotheses as Theorem~thm:main and the constant
kappa_mu of (eq:kappa-mu), for every 0<delta<1-v, every
kge1, and every finite-dimensional joint strategy,

 Pr![W_kgelceil(v+delta)krceil]
 le exp!(-frac{delta^3}
 {16384,(1+kappa_mu),(1+log d)},k).

The same holds for the supremum over strategies.


Theorem~thm:main is universal in mu and pays a fifth power of
delta; Corollary~cor:support-threshold is cubic in delta but
pays the distribution constant kappa_mu. Both bounds hold for the
same strategy, so one may use whichever rate is larger.

The conditioning cost tau(C) will be the central bookkeeping device

## The mean bound

The following direct consequence of the definition of v also covers
the case v=0, where the threshold bound becomes trivial.

[Marginal success probabilities]
Let G be a finite two-prover game with entangled value v and let
I_1,...,I_k be the coordinate win indicators of any
finite-dimensional joint strategy for the k-fold repetition. Then
[
 E I_ile vquad(1le ile k),
  E{i=1}^k I_ile kv .
]
In particular, if v=0 then every coordinate is lost almost surely.


Proof. 
Fix i and fix the question pairs at all coordinates other than i.
On a fresh input (x,y) to one copy of G, insert x and y at
coordinate i, use the fixed questions elsewhere, run the joint
strategy, and retain only the answers at coordinate i. What remains is
a finite-dimensional entangled strategy for G; its success probability
is at most v by definition of omega^*(G). Averaging over the fixed
questions under mu^{ ⊗ (k-1)} gives E I_ile v. Summing
gives the second claim, and when v=0 the nonnegative indicator I_i
has expectation zero, hence is zero almost surely.


# From conditioning bounds to threshold bounds

This section contains the combinatorial heart of the argument. It
converts a single analytic input --- an upper bound on the average
conditional win rate outside a tested set C, in terms of the
conditioning cost tau(C) --- into the exponential threshold decay of
(eq:main). The conversion uses only the joint distribution of the
binary win indicators, never the underlying quantum strategy. All
arithmetic below uses the symbols
[
 q=v+delta, q_0=v+delta/2,
 eta=delta/16, r=log(q/q_0), R=log(d/q),
]
and the target rate
[
 c=(delta,T)/(16(1+log d)).
]

coordinates with replacement produces such a C.

# The universal threshold bound

## Song's conditioning estimate

The sole external analytic input in the proof of Theorem~thm:main
is the following estimate.

[

# A common purification of effects

Conditioning on answers replaces the shared quantum state by local
positive operators applied to it. To compare the resulting vectors, we
need purifications of those operators that are close whenever an
operator entropy gap is small. For positive definite operators, this gap
is an average of Petz's operator Bregman divergence [cite];
related resolvent estimates appear in Kim [cite]. The
construction below furnishes all purifications in one common
finite-dimensional space. This preserves the answer measurements on the
purified side, not just the norms of the conditioned vectors. The
formulation and its Jensen estimate appear in
cite[Chapter~6, Definition~4.2, Lemma~4.3, and Appendix~A.4]{tenadvances2026};
the proof is included below, along with the finite-dimensional
compression.

For a finite-dimensional complex Hilbert space H, an
effect is a self-adjoint operator T on H with
0preceq Tpreceq I. Put f(t)=tlog t for t>0 and f(0)=0, and
define
[
 F_T:calF_T:H→calH→
 L^2((0,∞),ds;H),
 (F_T h)(s)=T(T+sI)^{-1}h.
]
The inverse exists for every s>0, including when T has a kernel.

[Resolvent purification]
For every effect T on H, F_T^*calF_T^*F_T=T. If
A_1,...,A_N are effects, q_1,...,q_N>0 sum to 1, and
B=sum_j q_j A_j, then

 sum_j q_j(F_{A_j}-F_B)^*calF_B)^*
                 (F_{A_j}-F_B)
 preceq sum_j q_j f(A_j)-f(B).

Finally, for any finite collection T of effects, all maps
F_T, TinT, can be compressed to a single
finite-dimensional subspace of L^2((0,∞),ds;H),
preserving every product F_S^*calF_S^*F_T for
S,TinT.


Proof. 
The scalar identity
[
 int_0^∞ (t^2)/((t+s)^2),ds = t (tge0)
]
and the spectral theorem give the first assertion
F_T^*calF_T^*F_T=T.

To prove (eq:resolvent-jensen), write D_j=A_j-B. If
hinker B, positivity and q_j>0 imply A_jh=0 for all j. This
common kernel reduces every operator in question and both sides of
(eq:resolvent-jensen) vanish there. We therefore restrict to
suppB, where B>0.

The scalar integral
[
 f(t)=int_0^∞
       ((t)/(1+s)-(t)/(t+s)) ds  (tge0)
]
converges (including at t=0, where the integrand is zero). Functional
calculus, together with sum_j q_j D_j=0, gives

 sum_j q_j f(A_j)-f(B)
 &=int_0^∞ ssum_j q_j
       big( (A_j+sI)^{-1} - (B+sI)^{-1}big),ds notag
 &=int_0^∞ ssum_j q_j(B+sI)^{-1}D_j
       (A_j+sI)^{-1}D_j(B+sI)^{-1},ds.
 

For the second equality use the exact identity

 (A_j+sI)^{-1}
 ={}&(B+sI)^{-1}-(B+sI)^{-1}D_j(B+sI)^{-1}
 &+(B+sI)^{-1}D_j(A_j+sI)^{-1}D_j(B+sI)^{-1},

and average over j. The final integrand in (eq:resolvent-gap)
is bounded near s=0 (since B>0 and s(A_j+sI)^{-1}preceq I) and is
O(s^{-2}) as sto∞. Thus these are norm-convergent operator
integrals.

The resolvent identity also yields
[
 A_j(A_j+sI)^{-1}-B(B+sI)^{-1}
 = s(A_j+sI)^{-1}D_j(B+sI)^{-1}.
]
Consequently the left-hand side of (eq:resolvent-jensen) has
integrand
[

]
Since s(A_j+sI)^{-2}preceq(A_j+sI)^{-1}, congruence by
D_j(B+sI)^{-1} bounds this integrand by the final integrand in
(eq:resolvent-gap). Integration proves the inequality.

For the finite-dimensional compression, the span
[
 K=span{F_T h:
              TinT, hinH}
]
has dimension at most |T|scrT|dimH. Viewing every map as
taking values in this same subspace preserves all inner products of
images, hence every stated product. An unused one-dimensional summand
can be added if a nonzero codomain is needed.


The common codomain is essential: compressing different operators
separately would not in general preserve their distances. In our
applications the number of effects is finite, although their number and
the resulting dimension need not have a uniform bound.

[Transporting answer measurements]

satisfy F^*F=T. Suppose A is a nonempty finite set and

always outputs a_*.


In particular, if Psi=(F_T ⊗ F_S) and

 <Psi|widetilde G_a ⊗ widetilde H_b|Psi>
 =<psi|G_a ⊗  H_b|psi>.

Thus the common purification preserves the exact answer probabilities,
not just the norms of the conditioned vectors.

# Conditioning on a tested set

Fix a finite-dimensional strategy for the k-fold game, with state
psi and POVMs {M_{mathbf x}bfx^{mathbf a}bfa} and
{N_{mathbf y}bfy^{mathbf b}bfb}. Let C⊆[k] be a set of tested
coordinates and let
[
 E_C={V(X_j,Y_j,A_j,B_j)=1 for every jin C},
  p=Pr(E_C)>0.
]
Assume m=k-|C|ge1, and put

 d_C=(|A|calA|,|B|)^calB|)^{|C|},
 L=log(d_C/p), tau=L/m.

We construct a vector and local measurements that reproduce the
conditional answers at one untested coordinate. Two approximating
vectors, each determined by one player's question and a transcript, will
have total squared error at most 2ptau on average. These comparisons
let Section~sec:root-rounding replace the conditional vector by a
fixed state on each support component. Throughout this section,
conditional expectations of operators use the original, unconditioned
question distribution mu^{ ⊗  k}. We adapt the weighted reveal
argument of cite[Chapter~6, Section~4]{tenadvances2026}, using a fixed
ordering of the untested coordinates.

## Exposing one question at each coordinate

Fix any ordering of the untested coordinates, and abbreviate their
questions as X_1,...,X_m and Y_1,...,Y_m, with answers
A_1,...,A_m and B_1,...,B_m. The tested questions retain the
notation X_C,Y_C. Position-indexed win indicators refer to these same
untested coordinates; sums over inotin C in the result statements
use the original coordinate labels. For 0le jle m define the flag
[
 Ω_j = (X_C, Y_C, X_{le j}, Y_{>j}).
]
Conditioned on a flag of positive probability, the full question vectors
mathbf XbfX and mathbf YbfY are independent. At a tested coordinate,
both questions are fixed. At each untested coordinate, one question is
fixed and the other has its conditional law under mu; these

## The conditional vector and its measurements

Apply the common finite-dimensional compression in
Lemma~lem:resolvent to all four families
F_x^alpha,F_y^alpha,H_x^gamma,H_y^gamma, over every mode, flag,
and positive-marginal question. This yields fixed finite-dimensional

including the maps used later for the local approximations.

Let R_i be the finite set of transcripts mathbf r=(r_0,bfr=(r_0,alpha,gamma),

they are independent of (x,y) and need not sum to one. Their sum is at
most d_C. The vector's squared norm supplies the remaining
probability of the tested answers.

We next exhibit measurements on Psi_{xy}^{mathbf r}bfr that reproduce
the conditional answers. Define the finer effects
[

  =E[M_bbE[M_{mathbf X}bfX^{alpha,,a_i=a}mid r_0,X_i=x],

  =E[N_bbE[N_{mathbf Y}bfY^{gamma,,b_i=b}mid r_0,Y_i=y],
]
where an unmentioned answer is summed out. They are positive and
satisfy sum_a G_x^{alpha,a}=F_x^alpha and
sum_b K_y^{gamma,b}=H_y^gamma. Lemma~lem:measurement-transport
provides POVMs {widetilde M_{mathbf r,x}bfr,x^a} on K_A and
{widetilde N_{mathbf r,y}bfr,y^b} on K_B such that
[

      F_{F_x^alpha}=G_x^{alpha,a},
 
 F_{H_y^gamma}^*widetilde N_{mathbf r,y}bfr,y^b
      F_{H_y^gamma}=K_y^{gamma,b}.
]
If p_{xy}^{mathbf r}bfr>0, write
phi_{xy}^{mathbf r}bfr=Psi_{xy}^{mathbf r}bfr/sqrt{p_{xy}^{mathbf r}bfr}.
Then

      widetilde M_{mathbf r,x}bfr,x^a ⊗ widetilde N_{mathbf r,y}bfr,y^b
       |phi_{xy}^{mathbf r}bfr>
  =frac{chi(G_x^{alpha,a},K_y^{gamma,b})}{p_{xy}^{mathbf r}bfr}.

To identify this probability, condition the original experiment on
r_0,X_i=x,Y_i=y. The unfixed Alice questions X_{>i} and Bob
questions Y_{<i} are independent, with conditional laws determined by
Y_{>i} and X_{<i} respectively. More explicitly, for positive-mass
conditioning data their joint law is
[
 Pr(X_{>i}=mathbf u,Y_bfu,Y_{<i}=mathbf zbfzmid r_0,X_i=x,Y_i=y)
 =prod_{j>i}mu(u_jmid y_j)
  prod_{j<i}mu(z_jmid x_j).
]
This is a statement about the original question law, before conditioning
on tested answers. Neither product depends on the fresh pair (x,y).
Since Alice's effect depends only on mathbf X,bfX, dropping the condition
Y_i=y leaves its average unchanged; the analogous assertion holds for
Bob after dropping X_i=x. Averaging the joint effect
M_{mathbf X}bfX^{alpha,,a_i=a} ⊗  N_{mathbf Y}bfY^{gamma,,b_i=b}
therefore gives exactly G_x^{alpha,a} ⊗  K_y^{gamma,b}. Its
coarse-grained probability is p_{xy}^{mathbf r}bfr. When
theta(mathbf r)=1,bfr)=1, conditioning further on the tested answers
(alpha,gamma) already implies E_C. Thus
(eq:exposure-fine-law) is the conditional distribution of the
untested answers (A_i,B_i) given E_C,mathbf r,X_i=x,Y_i=y.bfr,X_i=x,Y_i=y. Values
with theta(mathbf r)p_bfr)p_{xy}^{mathbf r}bfr=0 have no mass in that
conditional experiment.

In particular, the joint law of the transcript and fresh questions
conditioned on E_C is

 lambda_i(mathbf r,x,y)bfr,x,y)
   =frac{w_i(mathbf r)bfr),mu(x,y),|Psi_{xy}^{mathbf r}bfr|^2}{p}.

Thus lambda_i is a probability law even though the weights
w_i alone are not. The measurements in (eq:exposure-fine-law)
are local: Alice's depends only on (i,mathbf r,x)bfr,x) and Bob's only on
(i,mathbf r,y).bfr,y). The transcript is an analysis variable, not
information given to the players. A legal one-game strategy must start
with a shared resource independent of the fresh questions and obtain any
transcript information using only that resource and the player's own
question.

## Local descriptions and their approximation error

The conditional vector Psi_{xy}^{mathbf r}bfr depends on both fresh

# A cubic bound for a fixed question distribution

The exposure estimates also give a direct route to threshold repetition
when constants may depend on the question distribution. Within each
connected component of its support, we replace the conditional vector by
one reference vector. Adjacent question pairs have a common local
approximation, so the cost of this replacement is controlled along
paths in the support. The resulting conditioning error is of square-root
order, which gives a cubic threshold rate. The construction uses the
exposure vectors and transported answer measurements of
Section~sec:exposure. The connection-graph viewpoint of Dinur,
Harsha, Venkat, and Yuen [cite] motivated this fixed-support
approach; the quantum common-vector replacement by a component root and
the explicit cubic coefficient are proved here.

For a finite question distribution mu, let
[

]
Join two vertices of H when they share either coordinate. Let H_j be
the connected components, and let lambda_j,mu_j,mu_{j,min},
kappa_mu be as in (eq:kappa-mu). This constant depends on the
positive masses of mu, as well as on its support; in particular,
kappa_mu=0 when every component is a singleton.

[

# Two-sided error amplification

A threshold between completeness and soundness amplifies both guarantees
without changing the number of players or rounds. Tensor copies of a
single-game strategy give completeness; Theorem~thm:main controls
soundness even for joint strategies across all repetitions.

[Two-sided error amplification]
Let G be a finite two-player one-round game in the model above, and
fix 0le s<cle1. Put Delta=c-s, t=(c+s)/2, and
d=|A|calA|,|B|.calB|. For an integer kge1, let H_k sample
k independent question pairs from G, send each player the entire
local question string, and accept exactly when W_kgelceil tkrceil.
Then

 omega^*(G)ge c
 &⇒
 omega^*(H_k)ge1-exp(-Delta^2k/8),
 omega^*(G)le s
 &⇒
 omega^*(H_k)le
 exp!(-(kappa_0(Delta/2)^5)/(1+log d),k),

where kappa_0 is the universal constant in Theorem~thm:main.

Proof. 
For completeness, the supremum definition supplies a finite-dimensional
strategy with success p>c-Delta/4=t+Delta/4, whether or not the value
is attained. Its k tensor copies give independent Bernoulli win
indicators of mean p. Since W_k is integer valued,
{W_k<lceil tkrceil}={W_k<tk}, so Hoeffding's inequality
cite[Theorem~2]{hoeffding1963} bounds rejection by

Then deltageDelta/2>0 and delta<1-v, since t<1.

For polynomial-time verifier families with efficiently computable
rational bounds c,s, inverse-polynomial gap c-s, and polynomial
