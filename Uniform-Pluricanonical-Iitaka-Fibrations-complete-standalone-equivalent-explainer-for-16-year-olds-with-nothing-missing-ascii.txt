Exercise: Canonical divisor. For a smooth projective variety X, what divisor records the zeros and poles of a rational volume form?
K_X is the canonical divisor. For every positive integer m, H^0(X,O_X(mK_X)) consists of m-pluricanonical forms. The maximum dimension of their images in projective space is the Kodaira dimension κ(X). The uniform pluricanonical degree theorem says: for every d≥1 there is m(d)>0 such that, over every algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, |m(d)K_X| defines the Iitaka fibration of every smooth integral projective d-fold X with κ(X)≥0.

Exercise: Log discrepancies. For a normal projective X with an effective rational boundary B, what inequalities define log canonical and kawamata log terminal pairs?
Log canonical: every divisorial discrepancy is at least −1. Kawamata log terminal: every divisorial discrepancy is strictly greater than −1. The pair carries K_X+B. The log canonical index theorem says: for every dimension d and rational descending-chain coefficient set Φ, one fixed a(d,Φ)>0 makes a(d,Φ)(K_X+B) ~ 0 for every projective lc pair (X,B) of dimension d with coefficients in Φ and K_X+B ~_Q 0.

Exercise: Birational general type. In the general-type case, what does birationality of a pluricanonical map mean?
It separates two general points, so the map is birational onto its image. Exercise: Three induction statements. What are I_n, L_n, and K_n?

Exercise: Denominator theorem. State the denominator theorem for the moduli divisor.

Exercise: Weight rules. What three elementary rules govern weights?
A finite extension of the fibre function field with fixed base preserves the weight of a pulled-back form. A ramified curve extension of degree l multiplies the weight by l.
