A smooth integral projective variety X has a canonical divisor K_X. Think of K_X as the bookkeeping device for the zeros and poles of a rational volume form on X. For each positive integer m, the nonzero m-pluricanonical forms are the nonzero sections of O_X(mK_X); whenever these sections exist they send X into a projective space. The limiting growth of those images is measured by one integer, the Kodaira dimension k(X). If k(X) is nonnegative, then for all sufficiently divisible large m the pluricanonical maps stabilize: their birational images are equivalent and they package into the Iitaka fibration of X, whose base records every ratio of pluricanonical sections in one degree. The main theorem below says that a single degree m(d), depending only on the dimension d, already realizes this stable fibration for every smooth projective d-fold. Kodaira's elliptic surfaces are the model example: the canonical pluriforms of an elliptic surface distinguish the base of the elliptic pencil and ignore the elliptic fibre direction.

The companion statement controls singular pairs. A pair (X,B) consists of a normal projective variety X and an effective rational boundary divisor B with coefficients in [0,1]. Singularities are measured by discrepancies: log canonical means every divisorial discrepancy is at least -1, while kawamata log terminal means it is strictly greater than -1. The log canonical divisor is K_X+B. The theorem says that for each dimension d and each coefficient set Phi satisfying the descending chain condition, there is one integer a(d,Phi) with the following property: every projective log canonical pair (X,B) of dimension d with coefficients in Phi and K_X+B rationally linearly trivial satisfies a(d,Phi)(K_X+B)~0, so the multiple is an integral principal divisor, not merely a numerically trivial class. The descending-chain condition form follows from the finite-set form and the global ascending chain condition for lc thresholds, so the proof may reduce every coefficient set to a finite set.

For varieties of general type, k(X)=d and the pluricanonical systems already separate points eventually. Hacon-McKernan, Takayama, and Tsuji proved that a fixed multiple of K_X gives a birational map for every smooth projective variety of general type, the strongest possible statement for that case. The theorem needed here generalizes this from general type to every nonnegative Kodaira dimension. The cost is that the general fibre of the Iitaka fibration now contributes its own torsion, its own canonical index, and monodromy data. Those contributions cannot be ignored because in Kodaira dimensions between 0 and d-1 the pluricanonical sections see through to a base of smaller dimension.

Earlier effective Iitaka theorems had constants depending on the general fibre. Fujino-Mori proved a uniform degree for threefolds of Kodaira dimension one from a canonical bundle formula. Viehweg-Zhang handled two-dimensional Iitaka bases. Birkar-Zhang proved a general effective Iitaka theorem whose constant depends on the dimension, the least nonvanishing canonical degree of the general fibre, and a middle Betti number of a smooth model of a canonical cover. The missing step is to show those fibre-theoretic numbers have a uniform bound controlled only by the dimension; that is exactly what the denominator theorem and the index theorem below accomplish.

The canonical bundle formula is the machine that turns a fibration into data on the base. Let f:X->Z be a contraction of normal projective complex varieties with K_X rationally pulled back from Z. Then K_X is rationally equivalent to f^*(K_Z+B_Z+M_Z), where B_Z is the discriminant divisor recording singular fibres and M_Z is the moduli divisor recording the variation of the fibers. Ambro proved independence of the b-divisor and produced the klt-trivial fibration theorem that supplies B_Z and the moduli b-divisor. The uniform problem is that B_Z must have coefficients in a fixed descending-chain set, and M_Z must have a Cartier multiple whose size depends only on dim X. The section below obtains both uniform bounds without assuming a bound on middle Betti numbers of the fibres.

The induction runs on dimension and interleaves three statements. Write I_n for the uniform pluricanonical-degree theorem in dimension n, L_n for the uniform normal lc index theorem in dimension n for all rational descending-chain coefficient sets, and K_n for the special zero-boundary klt case of L_n. The step assumes I_j and L_j for all j<n. The denominator theorem is the main relative input. It fixes n>=1 and assumes L_j for every j<n. It says there are positive integers p0 dividing p, depending only on n, with the following property: if f:X->Z is a contraction with dim X=n, dim Z>0, X klt, and K_X rationally linearly equivalent to a pullback from Z, then there is a rational function psi on X and canonical bundle formula data satisfying
K_X+(1/p0)div(psi)=f^*D_Z, D_Z=K_Z+B_Z+M_Z,
where B_Z has coefficients in a rational descending-chain set depending only on n, the generalized pair (Z,B_Z+M_Z) is generalized klt, and pM_Z is b-Cartier for the moduli b-divisor. The proof first uses the geometric generic fibre. That fibre is klt of dimension less than n with rationally trivial canonical divisor, so the lower-dimensional index theorem gives a common integer p0 trivializing it. Cartierness and the one-dimensionality of the trivializing section space descend by flatness, so the same p0 works over the function field of Z.

Next choose psi so that p0K_X+div(psi) has no horizontal component. A rational function whose divisor is vertical is constant on the projective geometric generic fibre, so it comes from the base field; that produces D_Z. Ambro's rank-one condition holds because the relevant generic direct image has rank one when rounded discrepancies over the generic base point are allowed to be exceptional. The remaining denominator control is local over one prime divisor P of a smooth determination W of the base. Let alpha be the coefficient of the pullback of D_Z at P and let t be the threshold computed with the crepant total-space data. Because canonical divisors are integral, the denominator of M_W at P is the denominator of alpha+t. One computes alpha+t on a generic transverse curve C through a point c of P. After resolving the slice and cutting W with sufficiently general very ample divisors, the sliced equality is
K_V+B^c+(1/p0)div(psi_C)=h^*D_C, coeff_cD_C=alpha,
where h:V->C is the resolved slice and B^c is its crepant boundary. The geometric generic fibre of h is a normal klt variety with the original zero-boundary data. Let z be a local parameter at c, let theta be a rational top form defining K_V, and on the generic fibre set phi=(theta/dz)^{p0} psi_C. For a relative pluricanonical form omega of degree m define the weight
w_z(omega)=inf_E [ord_E(omega wedge dz/z)+m]/[m ord_E(z)],
where E runs over vertical divisorial valuations centered over c. The crepant equation gives
w_z(phi)=alpha-1+t.
The denominator is therefore controlled if a fixed multiple of w_z(phi) is an integer.

Three elementary rules govern weights. Finite extension of the fibre function field with the base fixed does not change the weight of a pulled-back form: ramification multiplies both the numerator and denominator by the same ramification index. A curve extension of ramification l at the marked point multiplies the pulled-back weight by l. Taking a tensor power leaves the weight unchanged, while multiplying a degree-m form by a base function a adds ord_z(a)/m. These rules convert geometric degenerations on a semistable model into arithmetic statements about units and roots of unity.

For an abelian block of dimension s, the normalized form is a local frame of the extended Hodge line in degree s under semistable reduction. The scalar estimates are quantitative preparations for the diagonal contradiction. For a big nef semiample rational Cartier divisor P on V and a smooth point x, let gamma(P;V) be the very general value of the normalized vanishing order of complete plurisections through x after placing V over a smooth projective model. Let epsilon(P;V) be the very general Seshadri constant, the infimum of P*C/mult_xC over curves C through x. Asymptotic Riemann-Roch gives gamma(P;V)>=(P^n)^{1/n}, because a section vanishing to order ell imposes at most binomial(d+ell-1,d) conditions. The scalar theorem says: if V is normal projective canonical with K_V~_Q0, a finite group G acts on V preserving the class of an ample rational Cartier divisor P, and every G-equivariant intermediate rational fibration has a general-type fibre, then gamma(P;V)<=C_n(P^n)^{1/n} and epsilon(P;V)>=c_n(P^n)^{1/n}, with constants depending only on n.
