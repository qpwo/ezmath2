Exercise: Canonical divisor. Let X be a smooth projective variety. Name the divisor that records the zeros and poles of a rational volume form on X.
Solution. That divisor is the canonical divisor K_X. For each positive integer m the nonzero sections of O_X(mK_X) are the m-pluricanonical forms. Their images in projective space have a maximum dimension, the Kodaira dimension kappa(X). The uniform pluricanonical degree theorem states: for each integer d>=1 there is an integer m(d)>0 such that, over every algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, the complete system |m(d)K_X| defines the Iitaka fibration of every smooth integral projective d-fold X with kappa(X)>=0.

Exercise: Log discrepancies. For a normal projective variety X with an effective rational boundary B, what inequalities define log canonical and kawamata log terminal singularities in terms of discrepancies?
Solution. Log canonical means every divisorial discrepancy is at least -1; kawamata log terminal means every divisorial discrepancy is strictly greater than -1. The log canonical divisor is K_X+B. Exercise: Uncountable birational maps. How does the chain leaf produce uncountably many birational transformations?
Solution. For fixed T and sufficiently large covering degree r, low-cost curves define leaves H_t with P_t^{h_t}H_t<=(h_tB)^{h_t}. Single-slot projections are generically finite, so 1<=h_t<=n and the projection degree satisfies d_t<=KT^{2n}. A long constant stretch yields one adjacent projection of degree one, so permutation invariance gives a birational coordinate-forgetting map. The tangent distribution projects isomorphically to each slot, giving a rational plane distribution A(x) and rational transport isomorphisms R(x,y) with cocycle R(y,z)R(x,y)=R(x,z). Choose independent rational vector fields v_1,...,v_h and their local flows E_z. Rational recovery gives E_z(y)=ev(E_z(u),xi(u,y)); since the derivative of z->E_z(y0) is nonzero, the resulting birational self-maps are uncountable, contradicting countability of Bir(V).
