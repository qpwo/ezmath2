A smooth projective variety X has a canonical divisor K_X. For each positive m, the nonzero m-pluricanonical forms map X into projective space; the limiting dimension of those images is the Kodaira dimension k(X). When k(X)>=0, sufficiently divisible degrees stabilize to the Iitaka fibration, whose base field is generated by ratios of pluricanonical forms. Kodaira's elliptic surfaces are the prototype.
Iitaka 1971, On D-dimensions of algebraic varieties: https://projecteuclid.org/journals/journal-of-the-mathematical-society-of-japan/volume-23/issue-2/On-D--dimensions-of-algebraic-varieties/10.2969/jmsj/02320356.pdf
    > The D-dimension of an algebraic variety is defined and studied.
Kodaira 1963, On compact complex analytic surfaces I: https://doi.org/10.2307/2373157
    > Elliptic surfaces and their canonical bundles organize the fibre structure.

The first uniform statement fixes dimension d. One integer m(d) works for every smooth projective d-fold X with k(X)>=0: |m(d)K_X| defines the Iitaka fibration and recovers the whole Iitaka base field. The second statement is an index bound: for each descending chain condition set Phi of rational coefficients, one integer a(d,Phi) makes a(d,Phi)(K_X+B) an integral principal divisor for every projective log canonical pair (X,B) of dimension d with coefficients in Phi and K_X+B rationally trivial.
Birkar-Zhang 2016, Effectivity of Iitaka fibrations and pluricanonical systems of polarized pairs: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.1098
    > The effective Iitaka fibration conjecture is reduced to polarized pairs.
Hacon-McKernan-Xu 2014, ACC for log canonical thresholds: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1212.1354
    > Log canonical thresholds satisfy the ascending chain condition.

The general-type case was settled by Hacon-McKernan, Takayama, and Tsuji: a fixed multiple of the canonical divisor gives a birational map for every smooth projective variety of general type.
Hacon-McKernan 2006, Boundedness of pluricanonical maps of varieties of general type: https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0509057
    > A bounded pluricanonical degree separates a bounded family.
Takayama 2006, Pluricanonical systems on algebraic varieties of general type: https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0306410
    > Pluricanonical maps are birational in uniformly bounded degree.
Tsuji 2007, Pluricanonical systems of projective varieties of general type II: https://ir.library.osaka-u.ac.jp/repo/ouka/all/6697/ojm44_03_12.pdf
    > The boundedness statement is completed for projective varieties of general type.

For lower Kodaira dimensions the general fibre contributes torsion and monodromy. Fujino-Mori treated threefolds of Kodaira dimension one via their canonical bundle formula. Viehweg-Zhang handled two-dimensional Iitaka bases. Birkar-Zhang obtained a general effective Iitaka theorem depending on dimension, the least nonvanishing canonical degree of the general fibre, and a middle Betti number of a canonical cover.
Fujino-Mori 2000, A canonical bundle formula: https://projecteuclid.org/journals/journal-of-differential-geometry/volume-56/issue-1/A-Canonical-Bundle-Formula/10.4310/jdg/1090347529.pdf
    > A canonical bundle formula relates the total space to the base.
Viehweg-Zhang 2009, Effective Iitaka fibrations: https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.4287v3
    > Effective Iitaka fibrations are obtained for two-dimensional bases.
Birkar-Zhang 2016, Effectivity of Iitaka fibrations and pluricanonical systems of polarized pairs: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.1098
    > The general effective Iitaka theorem depends on explicit invariants of the general fibre.

The canonical bundle formula turns a fibration into data on the base. For a contraction f:X->Z with K_X pulled back from Z, K_X=f^*(K_Z+B_Z+M_Z), where B_Z is the discriminant divisor and M_Z is the moduli divisor. Ambro proved the klt-trivial fibration theorem producing B_Z and the moduli b-divisor.
Ambro 2005, The moduli b-divisor of an lc-trivial fibration: https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0308143
    > The moduli b-divisor of an lc-trivial fibration is constructed.

The induction runs on dimension. The discrepancy and adjunction conventions follow Kollar; the minimal model program and cone machinery go back to Mori, Kawamata, and Shokurov. Write I_n for the uniform pluricanonical-degree statement, L_n for the normal log canonical index statement, and K_n for the zero-boundary klt case. The proof assumes I_j and L_j for j<n, then proves K_n, L_n, and I_n. A recent log abundance theorem supplies good minimal models in all dimensions.
Kollar 2013, Singularities of the Minimal Model Program: https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781139547895

A uniform Cartier denominator for the moduli b-divisor uses the geometric generic fibre, lower-dimensional index bounds, and the singular Beauville-Bogomolov decomposition. Druel and Greb-Guenancia-Kebekus supplied the splitting and holonomy parts; Horing-Peternell supplied algebraic integrability.

Degenerations of the individual Beauville-Bogomolov blocks clear the moduli denominator. Prokhorov's equivariant minimal model program handles finite groups. For a nonabelian block, a good special-fibre model produces a normal log canonical component with a degree-one log generator. A fixed-point argument on the possibly singular central fibre uses Donovan's Lefschetz-Riemann-Roch theorem; lower-dimensional index bounds then bound the associated character.

Torsion over rationally connected bases is handled separately. Birkar's boundedness theory for generalized Calabi-Yau pairs works in every fixed dimension. An unbounded-index sequence reduces to terminal varieties with countable birational groups. Countability uses the Picard group, finite stabilizers, and Matsumura's theorem excluding positive-dimensional linear algebraic groups as birational automorphism groups of varieties with nonzero pluricanonical systems.

The final contradiction uses compatible chains of low-cost curves on diagonal quotients. Large covering degree forces leaves to project finitely onto every slot, and projection degrees cannot grow exponentially under a polynomial bound. A degree-one forgetting map turns holomorphic flows into uncountably many birational self-maps, contradicting countability.

Finally, the uniform pluricanonical degree follows. On a good minimal model the semiample canonical divisor realizes the Iitaka fibration. If the base is a point, the proved index gives nonemptiness. If the base is positive-dimensional, the denominator bound and Birkar-Zhang polarized effective birationality recover the base field, and a common multiple over the finitely many base dimensions completes the induction.

    > Polarized effective birationality supplies a uniform birational rounded system.
