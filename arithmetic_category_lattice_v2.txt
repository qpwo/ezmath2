THE ARITHMETIC CATEGORY AND STABLE RECOVERY OF LATTICE FACTORS


================================================================


The whole paper in one paragraph:

A group builds an infinite-dimensional algebra of operators
called its group factor. A scalar twist builds a richer version.
The question: if you are handed an isomorphism between two such
factors, possibly after enlarging each by a positive scale, can
you recover the group, the twist, and even the exact map? For a
big class of groups coming from lattices in simple Lie groups
over the reals and the p-adic numbers (think SL_3(Z)), the
answer is yes, completely and explicitly. The engine is a
"category of correspondences" that we compute down to the last
finite-dimensional fibre.

================================================================
PART 1. GROUPS AND THE ALGEBRAS THEY BUILD
================================================================

1.1 Groups.

A group is a set G with an associative multiplication, an
identity e, and inverses. Two elements g and k are conjugate if
k = x g x^{-1} for some x in G. The conjugacy class of g is the
set of all such k.

A group is ICC ("infinite conjugacy classes") if every element
other than e has an infinite conjugacy class. Free groups, most
lattices in simple Lie groups, and the examples below are ICC.

Useful fact: in an ICC group, the only element that centralizes
a finite-index subgroup is the identity. Reason: if g centralizes
a finite-index subgroup H, then g has only finitely many
conjugates (one per coset of H), so its class is finite, so g = e.

1.2 The group factor.

Let G be a countable group. Form the Hilbert space l^2(G) of
square-summable complex functions on G, with orthonormal basis
e_g (the indicator of g). Left multiplication,

  lambda(g) e_h = e_{gh},

is a unitary. Let L(G) be the von Neumann algebra generated by
all the lambda(g): the smallest algebra of bounded operators on
l^2(G) closed under weak limits and containing each lambda(g).

Write u_g = lambda(g). These "canonical unitaries" multiply by
u_g u_h = u_{gh}. The vector e_e gives a trace

  tau(x) = <x e_e, e_e>,

so that tau(u_g) = 1 if g = e and 0 otherwise. A von Neumann
algebra with a trace like this, whose centre is just scalars, is
a II_1 factor.

Fact (Fourier argument). L(G) is a II_1 factor exactly when G is
ICC. The reason is short and worth knowing. A central vector in
L^2(L(G)) has Fourier coefficients that are constant on conjugacy
classes. If the class of g is infinite, those coefficients cannot
be square-summable unless they are zero. So the only central
vectors are constants, and the centre is trivial.

1.3 Scalar twists.

A normalized scalar 2-cocycle on G is a map

  mu : G x G -> T   (T = unit circle in C)

satisfying mu(e,g) = mu(g,e) = 1 and the cocycle identity

  mu(g,h) mu(gh,k) = mu(h,k) mu(g,hk).

Define a twisted multiplication on the same Hilbert space by

  u_g u_h = mu(g,h) u_{gh}.

This is associative precisely because of the cocycle identity,
and it again gives a II_1 factor L_mu(G) when G is ICC. The
trace is unchanged: tau(u_g) = 1 if g = e, else 0.

Two twists mu and mu' are cohomologous if

  mu(g,h) = b(g) b(h) b(gh)^{-1} mu'(g,h)

for some cochain b : G -> T with b(e) = 1. Cohomologous twists
give the same factor, because the map u_g -> b(g) u_g extends to
an isomorphism. So the twist really measures a class, not a
function.

================================================================
PART 2. THE GROUPS WE STUDY: PROPERTY (T) LATTICES
================================================================

2.1 Property (T).

A unitary representation of G is a homomorphism into the unitary
group of a Hilbert space. It has almost-invariant vectors if, for
every finite set S and every epsilon > 0, there is a unit vector
xi with ||pi(g) xi - xi|| < epsilon for all g in S.

G has property (T) if every unitary representation with
almost-invariant vectors actually has a nonzero invariant vector.
Property (T) is a strong rigidity property: it forbids "almost"
symmetries that are not exact. It passes to lattices and to
finite-index subgroups.

2.2 Lattices and local fields.

A local field is R, C, or a finite extension of the p-adic
numbers Q_p. For each such field k there is a notion of a simple
algebraic group over k; familiar examples are SL_n over R or over
Q_p.

Let G_i = H_i(k_i)^+ be a noncompact, property-(T), connected,
adjoint, absolutely simple group over a local field k_i. Take a
finite product G = G_1 x ... x G_r. The "+" means "generated by
the unipotent radicals of the proper parabolics"; for the
classical groups below it is the expected group.

A lattice in G is a discrete subgroup of finite Haar covolume.
Two groups are abstractly commensurable if they contain
isomorphic finite-index subgroups.

2.3 The class K.

Definition. K is the class of countably infinite ICC groups
abstractly commensurable with a lattice in a finite product G as
above, with each G_i noncompact and property (T).

No irreducibility, cocompactness, product decomposition, or
torsion-free hypothesis is imposed on the lattice.

2.4 Named examples.

All of the following belong to K:

  SL_3(Z), and every finite-index subgroup of it, including the
  principal congruence subgroup Gamma(3) = kernel of the map
  SL_3(Z) -> SL_3(Z/3Z);

  PSL_n(Z) for n >= 4 (the quotient of SL_n(Z) by its centre);

  PSp_4(Z) (the quotient of Sp_4(Z) by its centre);

  finite-index subgroups of any of these;

  and finite products of these discrete groups.

Why these are ICC. A finite conjugacy class gives a finite-index
centralizer. By Borel density that centralizer is Zariski dense,
so the element centralizes the connected ambient group. In the
adjoint form the ambient centre is trivial, so the element is
the identity. A finite-index subgroup of an ICC group is ICC.

The real factors here have real rank at least 2 (SL_3(R) has rank
2, Sp_4(R) has rank 2), and product with a semisimple group of
property (T) is again property (T). The p-adic factors have
relative rank at least 2.

================================================================
PART 3. WHAT "THE SAME FACTOR" MEANS
================================================================

3.1 Amplification.

Given a II_1 factor M and a real number t > 0, choose n with
t <= n and a projection p in M_n(M) whose unnormalized matrix
trace is t. Set

  M^t = p (M_n(C) tensor M) p,

with its normalized trace. This is again a II_1 factor, and it
does not depend on the choices up to isomorphism. Intuition:
"blow M up by a factor t."

A stable isomorphism from M to N is an isomorphism between M^t
and N^s for some positive t, s. The fundamental group of M is

  F(M) = { t > 0 : M^t is isomorphic to M }.

3.2 The central question.

Fix a group Lambda in K and an arbitrary countably infinite ICC
group Pi. Fix normalized scalar cocycles omega on Lambda and nu
on Pi, and real numbers t, s > 0. When is

  L_omega(Lambda)^t  isomorphic to  L_nu(Pi)^s ?

And if they are isomorphic, can we recover Lambda, the twist
omega, and the actual isomorphism?

================================================================
PART 4. CORRESPONDENCES: THE RIGHT LANGUAGE
================================================================

4.1 Definition.

A bifinite M-N correspondence is a Hilbert space H with two
commuting normal actions:

  a unital normal *-homomorphism  M -> B(H)   (left),
  a unital normal *-anti-homomorphism N -> B(H)  (right),

such that H is a finite module over M on the left and over N on
the right. Finite module means: H is isomorphic, as a left
M-module, to p L^2(M)^n for some projection p and integer n.

Its dimensions are

  dim_M H = (Tr_n tensor tau_M)(p),
  dim H_N  = the analogous right number.

These are positive reals; for factors of trace one they behave
like sizes.

4.2 Why correspondences.

An isomorphism M -> N is the same thing as a correspondence
with a unit vector that is cyclic for both actions (equivalently,
an equivalence correspondence of dimensions 1 and 1). More
important: a nonzero correspondence between M and N means the
two factors are "Morita-linked." The correspondence language
turns "are these factors isomorphic?" into a linear-algebra
question about bimodules, which we can fully classify.

4.3 Fusion.

Given an M-N correspondence H and an N-P correspondence K, the
Connes fusion (or relative tensor product)

  H tensor_N K

is a well-defined M-P correspondence. Finite modules fuse to
finite modules, and dimensions multiply: dim_M = dim_M H
times dim_M of the result, and similarly on the right.

4.4 Equivalence and scale.

For a corner M^t = p M_n(M) p, the column C_p = p L^2(M)^n is
an M^t - M correspondence with dimensions (1/t, t). It is an
equivalence: fusing it with its conjugate gives back L^2(M) or
L^2(M^t). This is the exact bookkeeping that will force scales
to match in the main theorem.

================================================================
PART 5. THE ARITHMETIC MODELS
================================================================

5.1 The building block.

Let Gamma, Lambda be two groups with normalized twists mu and
omega. Let A <= Gamma and B <= Lambda be finite-index subgroups,
and let delta : A -> B be an actual group isomorphism.

Let V be a finite-dimensional complex vector space carrying a
projective unitary representation sigma of A, meaning

  sigma(a) sigma(b) = beta(a,b) sigma(ab)

for a scalar beta. We require the specific multiplier

  beta(a,b) = mu(a,b) / omega(delta(a), delta(b)),

and sigma(e) = I (normalized).

Define the arithmetic model

  E(A,B,delta,sigma)
    = L^2(M)  tensor_{L_mu|A(A)}  (V  tensor  L^2(N)),

where M = L_mu(Gamma), N = L_omega(Lambda). Concretely: start
with the big Hilbert space L^2(M) tensor L^2(N), let the twisted
group factor of A act on the left of the second factor through

  u_a (xi tensor eta) = sigma(a) xi  tensor  v_{delta(a)} eta,

and balance. The multiplier condition is exactly what makes
this an action of the subgroup with the twist mu restricted.

The outcome is a bifinite M-N correspondence whose left and
right dimensions are

  dim_M = dim_C(sigma) [Lambda : B],
  dim_N  on the right = dim_C(sigma) [Gamma : A].

Interpretation. The isomorphism delta "glues" a finite-index
chunk of Gamma to a finite-index chunk of Lambda. The projective
representation sigma adds a finite-dimensional twist on top, and
it is exactly the twist needed to reconcile mu with omega on the
matched chunks. Nothing else is required.

5.2 Exhaustion.

The geometric companion paper proves the key input:

  Every nonzero bifinite correspondence from L_mu(Gamma), with
  Gamma in K, to L_omega(Lambda) for any countably infinite ICC
  group Lambda, is a closed bimodule summand of a finite direct
  sum of these arithmetic models.

So: the models are not just examples, they are everything. The
rest of the paper is bookkeeping inside them.

================================================================
PART 6. GERMS AND FULL STABILIZERS
================================================================

6.1 Virtual isomorphisms.

A virtual isomorphism germ from Gamma to Lambda is an actual
isomorphism A -> B between finite-index subgroups, where two
such isomorphisms that agree on a common finite-index subgroup
are declared equal.

Let D = Gamma x Lambda act on germs by conjugation:

  (g,h) . [delta] = [ Ad(h) o delta o Ad(g)^{-1} ].

For a germ d, its full stabilizer is

  C_d = { (g,h) in D : (g,h).d = d }.

6.2 Key lemma.

Because Gamma is ICC, the full stabilizer C_d is the graph of an
actual isomorphism A_d -> B_d between finite-index subgroups. It
contains the graph of any representative delta on a finite-index
subgroup, and extends it. The proof uses the ICC trick from
Part 1: if one coordinate of a stabilizer element were trivial,
the other would be too.

Second half of the lemma, the "finite-orbit test": if a
finite-index subgroup of C_d fixes some other germ x, then
x = d. Equivalently, the only finite C_d-orbit in the germ
space is the single point {d}.

6.3 Why full stabilizers matter.

A naive model E(A,B,delta,sigma) presents the germ d by a small
subgroup A. But a summand of that model can mix the several
cosets of C_delta sitting inside C_d. Grouping them over the
full stabilizer C_d exposes the actual pieces. Concretely,

  E(A,B,delta,sigma)  =  H(d, Ind_{C_delta}^{C_d}(sigma)),

i.e. the presenting fibre is replaced by the induced
representation. Skipping this step loses projections: for
instance, the identity germ inside E(A,A,id,1) with A of index
k already contains L^2(M) as a summand, and selecting it uses a
projection with all matrix entries 1/k.

6.4 The fibre picture.

For a germ d and a finite-dimensional projective representation
sigma_d of C_d with multiplier

  alpha((g,h),(g',h')) = mu(g,g') conj(omega(h,h')),

write H(d, sigma_d) for the full-germ bundle. Its Hilbert space
is an orthogonal sum of copies of the representation space,
indexed by the cosets D/C_d. Conjugation moves the labels by the
D-action, and the transports are the exact projective-induction
phases

  c(s,x) = r_{sx}^{-1} s r_x,
  U_s(x) = alpha(s, r_x) / alpha(r_{sx}, c(s,x)) * gamma(c(s,x)).

These are the standard Mackey phases; the paper verifies them
line by line so that no scalar is lost.

PART 7. THE CLASSIFICATION THEOREM
================================================================

THEOREM C (All finite correspondences).

Let Gamma be in K, Lambda countably infinite and ICC, and mu,
omega arbitrary normalized scalar cocycles. Then:

 (i) Every nonzero bifinite L_mu(Gamma)-L_omega(Lambda)
     correspondence is a closed bimodule summand of a finite
     direct sum of the arithmetic models E(A,B,delta,sigma).

 (ii) Every such correspondence decomposes orthogonally into
      full-germ orbit bundles, and all bounded bimodule maps
      are blockwise:

        Hom( sum_d H(d,sigma_d), sum_d H(d,tau_d) )
          = sum_d Hom_{C_d,alpha}(sigma_d, tau_d).

      Norm is the maximum over fibres, adjoint and composition
      are fibrewise, and projection summands are exactly
      projective-representation summands in the fibres.

      The dimensions of a correspondence K are

        dim_M K = sum_d dim_C(sigma_d) [Lambda : B_d],
        dim K_N = sum_d dim_C(sigma_d) [Gamma : A_d].

      Both are positive integers whenever K is nonzero.

 (iii) Conjugation and fusion are computed by explicit
       double-coset rules with exact scalar phases:

         E(A,B,delta,sigma) tensor_N E(C,F,eps,tau)
           = sum_{m in B\Lambda/C} E(A_m,F_m,theta_m,gamma_m),

       where A_m = delta^{-1}(B cap m C m^{-1}),
       theta_m = eps o Ad(m^{-1}) o delta, and the fibre
       representation gamma_m carries a specific correction

         q_m(b) = omega(b,m) / omega(m, m^{-1} b m).

Why part (ii) is true, in one line. Take an intertwiner T and
look at its matrix blocks T_{y,x} between fibres. Square
summability of the columns says sum_y ||T_{y,x}||^2 is finite.
Equivariance keeps that sum constant along a C_x-orbit, so a
nonzero block can only occur when the orbit of y is finite. The
finite-orbit test (Part 6) then forces y = x. So T is diagonal
in the germ labels, and what remains is the fibrewise intertwiner.

Consequences to keep in your pocket:

  - dimensions of actual summands are positive integers,
    computed from the fibre ranks and subgroup indices;
  - the correspondence of an isomorphism has reciprocal
    dimensions, so here both are 1;
  - only the matched ratio mu/omega needs a finite-dimensional
    projective representation. Neither endpoint cocycle alone is
    assumed to be of finite type.

================================================================
PART 8. STABLE CANONICAL RECOVERY
================================================================

THEOREM A (Stable canonical recovery).

Let Lambda be in K and Pi any countably infinite ICC group. Let
omega, nu be arbitrary normalized scalar cocycles and t, s > 0.
Then

  L_omega(Lambda)^t  is isomorphic to  L_nu(Pi)^s

if and only if t = s, and there exist a group isomorphism
delta : Lambda -> Pi and a normalized cochain b : Lambda -> T
with

  omega(g,h) = b(g) b(h) b(gh)^{-1} nu(delta(g), delta(h)).

Moreover, at unit scale every specified isomorphism has the form

  theta(u_g) = w b(g) v_{delta(g)} w*

for a unitary w in the target.

The specified-corner version is stronger. Put M = L_omega(Lambda)
and N = L_nu(Pi), and let

  theta : p M_n(M) p  ->  q M_m(N) q

be a given isomorphism, where the unnormalized traces of p and q
are t and s. Then t = s, and we can choose delta, b as above and
a rectangular matrix z in M_{m,n}(N) with

  z* z = phi^(n)(p),   z z* = q,   theta(x) = z phi^(n)(x) z*,

where phi(u_g) = b(g) v_{delta(g)}. Whenever such an isomorphism
exists, the recovered group Pi also lies in K. In particular
every factor L_omega(Lambda) in this class has trivial fundamental
group.

Proof idea, in four steps.

Step 1. A specified isomorphism theta gives an equivalence
correspondence H_theta from M to N, built by fusing the columns
of the source and target corners around the graph of theta. Its
left and right dimensions are t/s and s/t.

Step 2. By Theorem C the dimensions are positive integers, and
their product is 1. So both equal 1, hence t = s.

Step 3. A correspondence with both dimensions 1 contributes, on
each side, a positive integer fibre rank times a positive integer
subgroup index, and those must sum to 1. So there is one germ,
one dimension-one fibre, and both stabilizer projections are the
whole groups. That germ is an actual isomorphism delta : Lambda ->
Pi. Group isomorphism invariance puts Pi in K.

Step 4. The one-dimensional fibre is a cochain b. The arithmetic
model then says H_theta is isomorphic to the trace module with
left action phi(u_g) = b(g) v_{delta(g)}. Comparing the two
column presentations gives a unitary correction w, and absorbing
it into z gives the corner formula.

Trivial fundamental group follows by taking Lambda = Pi, the same
cocycle, and scales 1 and r: if L_mu(Lambda)^r were isomorphic to
L_mu(Lambda), then r = 1.

================================================================
PART 9. ALL FINITE-INDEX FACTOR ENDPOINTS
================================================================

9.1 What a neighbor is.

A finite-index factor neighbor of M is a II_1 factor P joined to
M by a nonzero bifinite correspondence. The name comes from the
case P = p M_n(M) p, where P sits inside an amplification of M
at finite index.

9.2 The theorem.

THEOREM B (All factor endpoints).

Let Gamma be in K, mu any normalized scalar cocycle on it, and P
a II_1 factor with separable predual. There is a nonzero bifinite
L_mu(Gamma)-P correspondence if and only if

  P is isomorphic to L_omega(Lambda)^t

for a countably infinite ICC group Lambda, a normalized scalar
cocycle omega on Lambda, and t > 0, for which there exist:
finite-index subgroups A <= Gamma and B <= Lambda, an actual
isomorphism delta : A -> B, and a nonzero finite-dimensional
projective unitary representation of A with multiplier

  mu|A / delta*(omega|B).

In particular the reconstructed group Lambda belongs to K.

9.3 Meaning.

The unknown factor P is not assumed to have a group presentation
or any preferred basis. Its hidden group and twist are recovered
from a finite-index chunk. When mu = 1 (untwisted source), the
ratio condition becomes exactly "omega is finite type on Lambda",
recovered by projective induction. For arbitrary mu the ratio is
essential: the identity correspondence shows that P = L_mu(Gamma)
itself is always an endpoint, whatever mu is.

9.4 Proof idea.

From the correspondence, the commutant Q of the right action is
a positive amplification Q = P^r of P, and M embeds in Q as a
finite-index subfactor. Grade L^2(Q) by virtual-isomorphism germs
of Gamma. The degree-zero part K_1 is a finite-dimensional unital
star-algebra; pick a minimal projection p. Then pQp is a factor
with a natural twisted group basis, so pQp is isomorphic to some
L_omega(Lambda). Boundedness of the graded pieces comes from the
Pimsner-Popa index inequality, and the identity matrix block of
K_1 gives the finite-dimensional projective representation of
the ratio. Finiteness of [Lambda : B] and ICC of Lambda follow
from the ICC trick and from the grading support being contained
in finitely many double cosets. Undoing the amplification gives
P isomorphic to L_omega(Lambda)^t.

================================================================
PART 10. CONSEQUENCES AND EXAMPLES
================================================================

10.1 Commensurability from untwisted correspondences.

Corollary. Let Gamma be in K and Lambda any countably infinite
ICC group. There is a nonzero bifinite L(Gamma)-L(Lambda)
correspondence if and only if Gamma and Lambda are abstractly
commensurable.

10.2 Untwisted neighbors.

Corollary. For Gamma in K, a separable II_1 factor P is joined to
L(Gamma) by a nonzero bifinite correspondence if and only if
P is isomorphic to L_omega(Lambda)^t for some t > 0, some ICC
group Lambda commensurable with Gamma, and some finite-type
cocycle omega on Lambda.

For an arbitrary source twist the ratio in Theorem B replaces
"finite type."

10.3 The explicit examples.

For Gamma = SL_3(Z), or Gamma(3), or PSL_n(Z) (n >= 4), or
PSp_4(Z), or any finite-index subgroup, and any countably
infinite ICC group Lambda, any specified isomorphism

  theta : L(Gamma) -> p M_k(L(Lambda)) p

satisfies

  (Tr_k tensor tau)(p) = 1,
  theta(u_g) = chi(g) W v_{delta(g)} W*,
  W* W = 1, W W* = p,

with W in M_{k,1}(L(Lambda)), delta : Gamma -> Lambda an actual
group isomorphism, and chi : Gamma -> T a character. Notice: the
scale is forced to 1, the corner is forced to be a column, and
the map is pinned down to a character and an inner correction.

10.4 Outer automorphisms.

Corollary. For Gamma in K,

  Out(L(Gamma)) = Hom(Gamma, T)  semidirect  Out(Gamma),

where Out means automorphisms modulo inner automorphisms, and
the action is [delta].chi = chi o delta^{-1}.

Idea: at unit scale the theorem says every automorphism is a
character twist composed with a group automorphism. The kernel
of that description is exactly the inner automorphisms, checked
with the same ICC argument.

10.5 Recovery from the reduced C*-algebra.

Write C*_r(Gamma) for the operator-norm closure of the group
algebra inside L(Gamma).

Corollary. Let Gamma be in K and suppose C*_r(Gamma) has a
unique tracial state. If Lambda is any discrete group and there
is a complex-linear star-isomorphism C*_r(Gamma) = C*_r(Lambda),
then Gamma is isomorphic to Lambda.

This applies to Gamma = SL_3(Z), whose reduced group C*-algebra
has a unique tracial state. So SL_3(Z) is recoverable from its
reduced C*-algebra alone.

10.6 Finite-index endomorphisms of L(SL_3(Z)).

Lemma (classical covolume fact). An injective homomorphism
alpha : SL_3(Z) -> SL_3(Z) whose image has finite index is
surjective.

Proof idea: strong rigidity extends alpha to an analytic
automorphism Phi of SL_3(R). The differential preserves the
Killing form, so the covolume is preserved. But covolume scales
by the index [SL_3(Z) : alpha(SL_3(Z))]. So the index is 1.

Corollary. Every normal unital complex-linear star-endomorphism
theta of M = L(SL_3(Z)) with [M : theta(M)] finite is surjective.

Proof idea: theta makes L^2(M) into a bifinite M-M correspondence
with dimensions 1 and [M : theta(M)]. The classification shows
that right dimension 1 leaves a single germ whose graph is an
isomorphism SL_3(Z) -> B of finite index. The covolume lemma
forces B = SL_3(Z). The left dimension is then 1, so theta is
onto.

================================================================
PART 11. HOW THIS SITS IN THE LITERATURE
================================================================

Connes proved in 1980 that the fundamental group of a
property-(T) group factor is countable, and in 1994 asked whether
nonisomorphic ICC property-(T) groups can have isomorphic
factors, and what their fundamental groups can be. Popa gave the
strong stable formulation: recover the given isomorphism up to a
character and inner conjugacy, at scale one.

Property (T) alone does not force recovery. There is a countably
infinite family of pairwise nonisomorphic ICC property-(T) groups
with isomorphic group factors (OpenAI, 2026), and independently a
pair by Zhou (2026). Outside the property-(T) world, the free
groups F_2 and F_3 have isomorphic factors, and these factors have
fundamental group all of R_{>0}. That is the exact opposite of
the trivial fundamental groups in Theorem A.

The deformation/rigidity line runs through Ioana's
W*-superrigidity for Bernoulli actions (2011), Ioana-Popa-Vaes on
superrigid group factors (2013), and Chifan-Ioana-Osin-Sun on
property-(T) wreath-like products (2023). Vaes (2008) computed
all finite correspondences for generalized Bernoulli crossed
products, with the projective-induction and double-coset calculus
this paper adapts. Donvil-Vaes (2025) proved W*-superrigidity for
cocycle-twisted group factors with arbitrary source cocycles and
the finite-type ratio conclusion, and gave the homogeneous-space
and minimal-corner construction adapted in Theorem B. CFOT (2026)
obtained stable canonical recovery for certain property-(T)
wreath-like products with a trivial-cocycle hypothesis.

What is new here: the full unrestricted source cocycle, the
mixed local-field lattice class, complete all-maps and
all-summands classification, and the fusion and the finite-index
neighbor theorem in that class. The geometric exhaustion is the
companion paper's job; everything else is the algebraic calculus
of this one.

================================================================
PART 12. WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED
================================================================

  - These are structural classifications. They do not give a
    decision procedure for arbitrary group presentations or
    arbitrary cocycle classes.
  - Nothing here says that every property-(T) group factor is
    rigid; the counterexamples above show otherwise.
  - Nothing here is claimed for non-property-(T) groups beyond
    the general algebraic calculus.

================================================================
PART 13. GLOSSARY
================================================================

2-cocycle (normalized): a map mu : G x G -> T with
  mu(e,g) = mu(g,e) = 1 and mu(g,h) mu(gh,k) = mu(h,k) mu(g,hk).

Ad(g): the automorphism x -> g x g^{-1}.

amplification M^t: the corner p M_n(M) p with
  (Tr_n tensor tau)(p) = t.

bifinite: finite as a module on both sides.

commensurable: contain isomorphic finite-index subgroups.

correspondence M-N: a Hilbert space with commuting normal left M
  and right N actions and finite module dimensions both sides.

Connes fusion: the relative tensor product H tensor_N K.

full-germ stabilizer C_d: the stabilizer in Gamma x Lambda of the
  virtual-isomorphism germ d.

fundamental group F(M): the set of t > 0 with M^t isomorphic to M.

germ (virtual isomorphism): an isomorphism between finite-index
  subgroups, modulo agreement on a common finite-index subgroup.

ICC: every nonidentity conjugacy class is infinite.

lattice: a discrete subgroup of finite covolume.

L(G): the von Neumann algebra generated by the left regular
  representation of G.

L_mu(G): the twisted version with u_g u_h = mu(g,h) u_{gh}.

property (T): every unitary representation with almost-invariant
  vectors has a nonzero invariant vector.

projective representation: sigma(a) sigma(b) = beta(a,b) sigma(ab)
  for a scalar beta.

II_1 factor: a von Neumann algebra with trivial centre and a
  faithful normal finite trace tau(1) = 1.

2. A correspondence is the linear-algebra version of
   "these two factors are linked."
3. The arithmetic models are built from finite-index subgroup
   isomorphisms plus a finite-dimensional representation of the
   ratio of the two twists.
4. Their dimensions are positive integers, and that integrality
   forces the amplification scale to be 1.
5. For property-(T) lattices over local fields, all of this is
   complete: you can recover the group, the twist, the scale,
   and the exact isomorphism, including the implementing partial
   isometry in the given matrix corners.
