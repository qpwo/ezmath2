The Virasoro conjecture ascribes to the total descendant potential of a
smooth projective variety a Lie algebra of second order differential
operators, built from the Poincare pairing, a Hodge grading, and cup
product with the first Chern class of the tangent bundle, whose positive
elements annihilate the potential. Its prototype is two dimensional
gravity: the Witten conjecture, proved by Kontsevich, identifies the
generating series of top intersections on the moduli spaces of stable
curves with the tau function of the KdV hierarchy, and the Virasoro
constraints of that moduli problem follow from the KdV equations.
./pdf/ref-witten-1991.pdf

For a general target the formulation on Fano varieties is due to Eguchi,

proposal of Katz to grade descendant insertions by a Hodge index. The
normalization takes the first Hodge index in a superspace,
the dilaton shift of Givental, and the central charge of Getzler.

The conjecture asserts L_k^X Z_X=0 for every kge -1. The string
equation gives the case k=-1. The case k=0 is the Hori equation; it
also follows from the string equation, the case k=1, and the
commutator [L_1,L_{-1}]=2L_0 with this normalization. What
remains is the family of positive constraints.

Two obstructions separate a numerical degeneration formula from a
transport theorem for Virasoro equations. Primitive insertions of X
need not extend individually through the degeneration, and the
operators weight contracted indices by first Hodge degrees, so their
contractions are not ungraded diagonal pairings. The response is to
regard a coefficient of L_k^XZ_X, at fixed genus, degree and
descendant orders, as a multilinear error tensor in its cohomology
inputs, and to test it by scalar contractions with coherently extending
classes of fixed Hodge type, graded inverse pairings, and ordinary
descendant tensors of independent replicas. Vanishing of every such
scalar test is shown to force the tensor to vanish, using Hodge-Riemann
positivity on primitive middle cohomology.

Two algebraic facts convert these constructions into numbers. First, a
closed tensor diagram built from flat Hodge tensors of prescribed types,
flat singles of fixed Hodge type, pairings, inverse pairings, and
functions of the first Hodge index on its legs, whose total type is
scalar, is locally constant on smooth fibers and equals its value on the
associated Hodge and weight gradeds of the limiting structure with all
Tate shifts retained. The derivative of a projector Pi_p onto a Hodge
summand is off-diagonal: differentiating Pi_p^2=Pi_p gives
Pi_p(dPi_p)Pi_p=0, and differentiating Pi_pPi_q=0 gives the
other diagonal blocks. In a derivative of a closed diagram exactly one
factor becomes off-first-index, every other vertex balances its
prescribed index, and the leftover index change at that one factor
forces the scalar contraction to vanish; a function on the finite index
spectrum is a linear combination of projectors. The canonical extensions
of the Hodge filtrations in a semistable degeneration are locally free
and flat morphisms of variations extend as filtered morphisms, so the
same scalar extends to the graded objects, and strictness of morphisms
of mixed Hodge structures lets the weight graded be taken without
changing the value. Second, if C is a finite complex with a perfect
chain pairing and T a chain functional on a tensor power of C with
fixed cycles and fixed degree shifts, contracting paired slots against
the closed inverse-pairing tensor gives the contraction of the induced
functional on cohomology against the induced inverse pairing; the
Kunneth map identifies the tensor cohomology, the inverse cycle
represents the inverse induced pairing, a chain functional kills
boundaries, and Koszul symmetry gives the same statement for loops as a
categorical supertrace.

Degrees separate curve classes. The ordinary and blowup configurations
can be equipped with coherent numerical divisor tests distinguishing
every curve class with a possibly nonzero ordinary Gromov-Witten
contribution, after choosing very general smooth fibers where necessary,
and the same holds for disconnected total classes. If a
positive-dimensional complete intersection has dimension different from
two, its integral curve homology is detected by the hyperplane degree:
for curves this follows from H_2(Y,mathbb Z)=bbZ)=mathbb Z,bbZ, and for
dimension at least three from the integral Lefschetz theorem, with no
torsion ambiguity.
Complete-intersection surfaces are simply connected, so Poincare duality
and the universal coefficient theorem show that their H_2 is torsion
free. For a complete-intersection surface with all linear equations
eliminated, the very general rational algebraic cohomology is ambient
unless the surface is mathbb P^2,bbP^2, a quadric, a cubic, or an intersection of
two quadrics, which is the complete-intersection Noether-Lefschetz
statement; in the nonexceptional case ambient degree distinguishes all
effective classes on a very general fiber, and a class with a nonzero
primitive part is not algebraic there, so it cannot carry a stable map,
and deformation invariance makes its ordinary invariant vanish on any
special smooth fiber with the curve class and insertions transported
locally. An effective disconnected total is itself an algebraic class,
so the argument covers disconnected coefficients.

Deformation invariance of the tested scalars completes the induction.
Fix a coefficient error and its Hermitian-square test. All contractions
in the test have balanced Hodge type, and the only nonflat ingredients in
a flat cohomology trivialization are first-Hodge-index projectors and
functions of them; the derivative of such a projector is first-index
off-diagonal, since differentiating its idempotence and its constant
eigenspace decomposition makes its diagonal blocks vanish. Replacing one
projector by its derivative in the balanced contraction changes the
total first Hodge degree while leaving every other tensor morphism at its
fixed type, so the resulting scalar is zero, and the product rule shows
that the Hermitian-square scalar is locally constant. The same argument
applies to the numerical tests used in the degeneration calculation.

The remaining work is a two-parameter induction. A zero-dimensional smooth
complete intersection is a disjoint union of points; the point
constraints are the Witten-Kontsevich theorem, and for a disjoint union
the potential is the product and each Virasoro operator the sum of the
component operators, so dimension zero holds. Assume the assertion in
dimensions below n, and in dimension n induct on kappa. For
kappa=0 the target is projective space after eliminating linear
equations, a toric bundle over a point, and the toric bundle transfer
proves the base case. At positive complexity choose d_rge2 and a
splitting d_r=a+b. Both U and M have smaller complexity, and D
and S have smaller dimension, so their full constraints are available
by induction. To obtain the constraints for widetilde M: if S is
empty this is the assertion for M; otherwise E and Q_S are
projectivizations of sums of line bundles over S and C_E is one more
such projectivization over E, so the constraints for S together with
the toric transfer give the full constraints for Q_S and C_E, and
all shorter targets required by transport are known. Applying transport
gives every tested positive constraint on widetilde M, with the
hyperplane degree pulled back from M and the additional degrees
retained, including individual exceptional degrees when the centre is
disconnected; the coherent subspace just described meets all the
hypotheses of detection, so every positive constraint holds for
widetilde M with arbitrary insertions, the degree tests distinguish
its relevant curve classes, and deformation invariance covers special
smooth choices of the nested intersections. The string equation
L_{-1}Z_X=0 and the grading equation, or the string equation together
with the commutator [L_1,L_{-1}]=2L_0 and the central normalization,
supply the nonpositive constraints; the two lower operators are the
string operator and the quantization of the grading with that scalar,
and the commutator with L_1 gives L_0Z_X=0 with that
normalization, equivalently through the dilaton, homogeneity, divisor,
and genus-one Riemann-Roch identities. Applying transport once more, now
to the ordinary degeneration with main smoothing X and shorter targets
U, V, and the ruled bundle over D, gives the positive constraints
for X itself. Both induction parameters strictly decrease where
invoked, and no restriction on genus or on the number or parity of
primitive insertions was imposed, so every positive constraint holds for
every smooth projective complete intersection, in every genus and curve
class, with arbitrary insertions from H^*(X;mathbb C).bbC).
