Let $t_+ = \max\{t,0\}$, $t_- = \max\{-t,0\}$.

For $\gamma > 1/2$ and nonnegative $W \in L^{\gamma+1/2}(\mathbb{R})$, consider the quadratic form $h_{-W}[v] = \int_{\mathbb{R}} |v'|^2 dx - \int_{\mathbb{R}} W|v|^2 dx$ on the Sobolev space $H^1(\mathbb{R})$. The form is closed and bounded below, and its associated self-adjoint operator $H_{-W} = -d^2/dx^2 - W$ has negative spectrum consisting of eigenvalues of finite multiplicity whose only possible accumulation point is zero. Write
$$\operatorname{Tr}(H_{-W})_-^{\gamma} = \sum_{\lambda_j(H_{-W}) < 0} |\lambda_j(H_{-W})|^{\gamma}$$
counting multiplicity; the sum is permitted to be infinite before the inequality is proved.

For every $1/2 < \gamma < 3/2$ and every nonnegative $W \in L^{\gamma+1/2}(\mathbb{R})$,
$$\operatorname{Tr}(H_{-W})_-^{\gamma} \le 2\left(\frac{\gamma-1/2}{\gamma+1/2}\right)^{\gamma-1/2} L_{\gamma,1}^{\mathrm{cl}} \int_{\mathbb{R}} W^{\gamma+1/2},$$
where
$$L_{\gamma,1}^{\mathrm{cl}} = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{\mathbb{R}}(1-\xi^2)_+^{\gamma} d\xi = \frac{\Gamma(\gamma+1)}{2\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(\gamma+3/2)}$$
is the semiclassical constant. The constant is optimal. It is attained by the one-bound-state potential $W(x) = (r+1)\operatorname{sech}^2(rx)$ with $r = 1/(\gamma-1/2)$, whose only negative eigenvalue is $-1$.

Numerical experiments of Levitt confirmed the one-state profile or its splitting into increasingly separated copies throughout the strict interval (A. Take $\delta > 0$ fixed, send the penalty parameter $\varepsilon > 0$ to zero, and only then remove $\delta$; only with $\delta$ fixed do the compactness estimates from the regularized field hold uniformly in $\varepsilon$. Let $I = (x_-, x_+)$ be a bounded interval throughout, write $\sigma = \gamma - 1/2 \in (0, 1)$, and put $p = 1+\sigma$, $q = 1+1/\sigma$.

For a positive diagonal matrix $K = \operatorname{diag}(k_1, \ldots, k_N)$ and $a \in H^1_0(I;\mathbb{R}^N)$, put
$$\mathcal{E}_K(a) = \int_I \left(|a'|^2 + a^T K^2 a - |a|^{2q}\right) dx.$$

The action inequality holds for every $u \in H^1_0(I;\mathbb{R}^N)$ with $\int_I uu^T dx = I_N$ and every $k_i > 0$:
$$\sup_{C \text{ lower triangular}} \mathcal{E}_K(Cu) \ge \sum_{i=1}^N \int_{-k_i}^{k_i} (k_i^2 - s^2)^{\sigma} ds.$$
The supremum runs over real lower triangular $N \times N$ matrices.

The restriction to lower triangular $C$ links the action inequality to spectral data. If the $u_i$ are ordered Dirichlet eigenfunctions, $k_1 \ge \cdots \ge k_N > 0$, then $a_i = \sum_{j \le i} C_{ij} u_j$ stays in the span of the first $i$ eigenfunctions. The $i$th eigenfunction equation, in form sense, integrates to a finite sum of $(k_i^2 - k_j^2)$ with nonnegative coefficients, and the quadratic part of the action is bounded above by $\int W|a_i|^2$.

The lower bound for the action is built from three ingredients: a regularized matrix field, its rank-one comparison, and a continuation argument producing a path with prescribed matrix endpoints.

Put $\beta = \sigma - 1/2 \in (-1/2, 1/2)$ and fix $\delta>0$. Let $f$ be the $C^1$ function on $\mathbb{R}$ with $f(0) = 0$ and
$$f'(y) = (\max\{y,0\} + \delta)^{\beta-1}.$$
Since $\beta-1<0$, $f'$ is nonincreasing and $f$ is concave on $\mathbb{R}$. The integral $c = \sigma/\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+s^2)^{\beta-1} ds$ converges because $\beta<1/2$; a substitution $s \mapsto \sqrt{z+\delta}\,s$ gives, for $z \ge 0$,
$$c \int_{\mathbb{R}} f'(s^2+z)\, ds = \sigma (z+\delta)^{\beta-1/2} = \sigma(z+\delta)^{\sigma-1};$$
integration in $z$ produces $(z+\delta)^{\sigma} - \delta^{\sigma}$. The definition keeps $f'$ constant for negative arguments.

For symmetric $N \times N$ matrices $B$, put $X_s(B) = K^2 - 2sB + s^2 I_N$ and
$$M_R(B) = c \int_{-R}^{R} (f(X_s(B)) - f(s^2) I_N)\, ds, \quad M(B) = \lim_{R \to \infty} M_R(B).$$
The symmetric cutoff is part of the definition, and $f$ of a symmetric matrix is defined by the spectral theorem. The limit is locally uniform and $M$ is locally Lipschitz.

The needed estimate is Hilbert-Schmidt calculus. If $h$ is $L$-Lipschitz on an interval containing the spectra of two Hermitian matrices $A, A'$, then $\|h(A) - h(A')\|_{\mathrm{HS}} \le L\|A - A'\|_{\mathrm{HS}}$. Choosing orthonormal eigenbases $v_i$, $w_j$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_i$, $\nu_j$, the entries of $h(A) - h(A')$ and $A - A'$ are $(h(\lambda_i) - h(\nu_j))\langle v_i, w_j\rangle$ and $(\lambda_i - \nu_j)\langle v_i, w_j\rangle$; squaring and summing over $i, j$ gives the claim.

Fix a bounded set of $B$'s. For large $|s|$ the spectrum of $H_s(B) = K^2 - 2sB$ lies in $[-A|s|, A|s|]$ with $A$ independent of $B$. Set $h_s(t) = f(s^2+t) - f(s^2) - f'(s^2)t$. On this interval all arguments of $f$ are positive; since $f''(y) = (\beta-1)(y+\delta)^{\beta-2}$ for $y>0$, the mean value theorem gives $\operatorname{Lip}(h_s) = O(|s|^{2\beta-3})$ and $\sup_{|t| \le A|s|}|h_s(t)| = O(|s|^{2\beta-2})$. Thus
$$f(X_s(B)) - f(s^2)I_N = -2s f'(s^2)B + R_s(B),$$
with $R_s(B) = f'(s^2)K^2 + h_s(H_s(B))$ of norm $O(|s|^{2\beta-2})$ and with Lipschitz constant $O(|s|^{2\beta-2})$ in $B$: the factor $2|s|$ from $H_s$ multiplies $O(|s|^{2\beta-3})$. The first term is odd in $s$ and cancels at every symmetric cutoff; the remainder is integrable since $2\beta - 2 = 2\sigma - 3 \in (-3, -1)$. On bounded $s$-intervals $f$ is Lipschitz and the same lemma applies directly.

To construct the primitive, let $F$ be any $C^2$ primitive of $f$. The trace formula $d\operatorname{tr}F(A)[H] = \operatorname{tr}(f(A)H)$ follows for polynomials by cyclicity and for general $f$ by polynomial approximation of $F$ and $F'$ on a compact spectral interval. For $s \ne 0$, $B \mapsto f(X_s(B)) - f(s^2)I_N$ is the gradient of $-\frac{1}{2s}\operatorname{tr}F(X_s(B)) - f(s^2)\operatorname{tr}B$; at $s=0$ the field is constant and again a gradient. Each $M_R$ has zero integral on every closed curve. The function $J_R(B) = -\int_0^1 \operatorname{tr}(B M_R(tB))\, dt$ primitivizes $-M_R$; local uniform convergence gives $J(B) = -\int_0^1 \operatorname{tr}(B M(tB))\,dt$ as the limit, and passing to the limit in $J_R(B+H) - J_R(B) = -\int_0^1 \operatorname{tr}(M_R(B+tH)H)\,dt$ via continuity of $M$ proves that $J$ is $C^1$ with $dJ(B)[H] = -\operatorname{tr}(M(B)H)$.

Define
$$\mu(z) = c \int_{\mathbb{R}} (f(s^2+z) - f(s^2))\, ds, \quad z \in \mathbb{R}.$$
The integral is absolutely convergent (its tail is $O(|s|^{2\beta-2})$) and $\mu$ is locally Lipschitz. It is strictly increasing because $f$ is strictly increasing at every $s$, $\mu(0)=0$, and for $z \ge 0$,
$$\mu(z) = (z+\delta)^{\sigma} - \delta^{\sigma}, \quad \mu'(z) = \sigma(z+\delta)^{\sigma-1};$$
differentiation under the integral gives $\mu'(z) = c \int_{\mathbb{R}}(s^2+z+\delta)^{\beta-1}\, ds = \sigma(z+\delta)^{\sigma-1}$.

The shift identity is: for every fixed $b, z \in \mathbb{R}$,
$$c \lim_{R \to \infty} \int_{-R}^{R} (f((s-b)^2+z) - f(s^2))\, ds = \mu(z).$$
The function $g(s) = f(s^2+z)$ is even and $g'(s) = 2s f'(s^2+z) = O(|s|^{2\beta-1}) \to 0$ as $|s| \to \infty$; the difference $\int_R^{R+b} g - \int_{R-b}^{R} g$ has absolute value at most $b^2 \sup_{|t-R| \le b}|g'(t)| \to 0$, and evenness handles $b<0$.

For every symmetric $B$ and every real unit $e$,
$$e^T M(B) e \le \mu(e^T K^2 e - (e^T B e)^2).$$
Scalar concavity of $f$ and the spectral decomposition of $X_s(B)$ give $e^T f(X_s(B)) e \le f(e^T X_s(B) e)$; put $b = e^T Be$ and $z = e^T K^2 e - b^2$, integrate at symmetric cutoff, and use the shift identity.

If $e_i$ is a standard basis vector and $B e_i = b_i e_i$, then
$$M(B) e_i = \mu(k_i^2 - b_i^2) e_i;$$
$e_i$ is also an eigenvector of $X_s(B)$ with eigenvalue $(s-b_i)^2 + k_i^2 - b_i^2$, and the shift identity applies. Restricting $dJ = -\operatorname{tr}(M\,dB)$ to diagonal matrices and integrating along the diagonal segment from $K$ to $-K$ gives
$$J(-K) - J(K) = \sum_{i=1}^N \int_{-k_i}^{k_i} \mu(k_i^2 - s^2)\, ds.$$
The field satisfies $M(K) = M(-K) = 0$, and for every diagonal sign matrix $S$ with entries $\pm 1$, $M(SBS) = SM(B)S$; both follow from the definition at each cutoff. The matrix $K$ stays fixed throughout.

Let $B \in \operatorname{Sym}_N$ and $a \in \mathbb{R}^N$ with $M(B) = aa^T$. Then
$$a^T (K^2 - B^2) a \ge |a|^{2q}.$$

For $y \ge 0$, $f$ has the integral representation
$$f(y) = c_\beta \int_0^{\infty} v^{\beta} \left(\frac{1}{\delta+v} - \frac{1}{y+\delta+v}\right) dv, \quad c_\beta^{-1} = \int_0^{\infty} \frac{t^{\beta}}{(1+t)^2}\, dt.$$
The integrand is $O(v^\beta)$ near zero and $O(v^{\beta-2})$ at infinity; the range $-1 < \beta < 1$ ensures convergence. Both sides vanish at $y=0$ and their derivatives agree: $c_\beta \int_0^{\infty} v^\beta (y+\delta+v)^{-2}\, dv = (y+\delta)^{\beta-1} = f'(y)$.

Let $X, Y \ge 0$ be Hermitian. Then
$$f(X) \le f(Y) + T_Y(X-Y),$$
where, in an eigenbasis of $Y$ with eigenvalues $y_i$,
$$(T_Y Z)_{ij} = f[y_i, y_j] Z_{ij}, \quad f[x,y] = \int_0^1 f'(\tau x + (1-\tau)y)\, d\tau,$$
and $f[x,x] = f'(x)$ for repeated eigenvalues. To verify, put $A = Y + (\delta+v)I$ and $Z = X - Y$. Since $A$ and $A+Z$ are positive definite, the resolvent identity gives
$$-(A+Z)^{-1} = -A^{-1} + A^{-1}ZA^{-1} - A^{-1}Z(A+Z)^{-1}ZA^{-1} \le -A^{-1} + A^{-1}ZA^{-1};$$
integration in $v$ together with $\int_0^{\infty} v^\beta((y_i+\delta+v)(y_j+\delta+v))^{-1}\, dv = f[y_i, y_j]$ proves the tangent inequality.

Suppose $m = |a|^2 > 0$. The directional comparison and strict increase of $\mu$ together with $e^T M(B) e \ge 0$ give $e^T K^2 e \ge (e^T B e)^2$ for every real unit $e$. Therefore
$$e^T X_s(B) e = (s - e^T B e)^2 + e^T K^2 e - (e^T B e)^2 \ge 0,$$
so $X_s(B) \ge 0$ for every real $s$.

Choose $d>0$ so that $\mu(d) = m$, i.e. $d = (m+\delta^{\sigma})^{1/\sigma} - \delta$, and set $D = K^2 - B^2$, $Y_s = (sI-B)^2 + dI$, so that $X_s(B) = Y_s + (D - dI)$ and $Y_s$ is diagonal in an eigenbasis of $B$. The tangent inequality at $Y_s$, the shift identity (which gives $c\int_{\mathbb{R}}(f(Y_s) - f(s^2)I)\,ds = mI$ in an eigenbasis of $B$), and the substitution identity $\mu'(d) = c\int_{\mathbb{R}} f'((s-z_i)^2+d)\,ds$ combine to give
$$M(B) \le mI + \mu'(d) \mathcal{P}(D - dI), \quad \mathcal{P} = \frac{c}{\mu'(d)} \int_{\mathbb{R}} T_s\, ds,$$
where $T_s$ is the tangent map at $Y_s$. The integral of the tangent maps converges absolutely.

The map $\mathcal{P}$ has the form
$$(\mathcal{P} Z)_{ij} = P_{ij} Z_{ij}, \quad P_{ij} = \int_0^1 \left(1 + \frac{\tau(1-\tau)(z_i-z_j)^2}{d+\delta}\right)^{\sigma-1} d\tau$$
in an eigenbasis of $B$ with eigenvalues $z_i$, and there is an even probability measure $\nu$ on $\mathbb{R}$ such that
$$\mathcal{P}(Z) = \int_{\mathbb{R}} e^{itB} Z e^{-itB}\, d\nu(t).$$
The kernel formula follows from $f[y_i, y_j]$ evaluated at $y_i = (s - z_i)^2 + d$ together with Tonelli. The probability representation follows from
$$(1+hx^2)^{\sigma-1} = \frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\sigma)} \int_0^{\infty} v^{-\sigma} e^{-v} e^{-vhx^2}\, dv$$
applied with $h = \tau(1-\tau)/(d+\delta)$ and $\tau$ uniform on $[0,1]$: the factor $e^{-vhx^2}$ is the characteristic function of a centered Gaussian of variance $2vh$ (point mass at zero when $h=0$), and the mixture over $v, \tau$ is an even probability measure $\nu$, independent of $i, j$, whose characteristic function at $z_i - z_j$ is $P_{ij}$. The diagonal is one; hence $\mathcal{P}(I) = I$, and $\mathcal{P}$ is self-adjoint for the trace pairing.

For every $B$ and $D = K^2 - B^2$,
$$\operatorname{tr}(M(B)D) \ge \operatorname{tr}(M(B) \mathcal{P}(D)).$$
Klein's inequality, in the form used by Petz, gives for any real increasing $g$ and Hermitian $A_1, A_2$,
$$\operatorname{tr}\left((g(A_1) - g(A_2))(A_1 - A_2)\right) \ge 0,$$
as is immediate from eigenbases of $A_1$ and $A_2$. Apply with $g=f$, $A_1 = U X_s(B) U^*$, $A_2 = X_s(B)$ and $U = e^{itB}$. Since $U$ commutes with $(sI-B)^2$,
$$0 \le \operatorname{tr}\left(f(X_s(B))(2D - UDU^* - U^*DU)\right).$$
The coefficient $2D - UDU^* - U^*DU$ has trace zero, so $f(s^2)I$ may be subtracted inside the trace; integrating over $[-R,R]$ and passing to the symmetric cutoff in $M$ is uniform in $t$ because the coefficient has norm at most $4\|D\|_{\mathrm{HS}}$, whereas $M_R(B) \to M(B)$ in matrix norm. Averaging over $\nu$ and using $\int UDU^*\, d\nu = \mathcal{P}(D) = \int U^*DU\, d\nu$ (via evenness) proves the claim.

Multiplying $M(B) \le mI + \mu'(d)\mathcal{P}(D-dI)$ by the positive semidefinite $M = aa^T$ and taking the trace, $M^2 = mM$ and $\mathcal{P}(I) = I$ give
$$m\operatorname{tr}M \le m\operatorname{tr}M + \mu'(d)\left(\operatorname{tr}(M\mathcal{P}(D)) - d\operatorname{tr}M\right).$$
Since $\mu'(d) > 0$, trace monotonicity yields $a^T D a = \operatorname{tr}(MD) \ge \operatorname{tr}(M\mathcal{P}(D)) \ge dm$. For $0<\sigma<1$ and nonnegative $x, y$, $(x+y)^\sigma \le x^\sigma + y^\sigma$; applied with $x=d$, $y=\delta$ this gives $m = (d+\delta)^\sigma - \delta^\sigma \le d^\sigma$, so $dm \ge m^{1+1/\sigma} = |a|^{2q}$.

Fix $K = \operatorname{diag}(k_i) > 0$, a bounded interval $I=(x_-, x_+)$, and $u \in H^1_0(I;\mathbb{R}^N)$ with $\int_I u u^T = I_N$. For every $\varepsilon > 0$ there are a real lower triangular $C$ and a path $B \in C^1(\bar I; \operatorname{Sym}_N)$ with
$$\varepsilon B' = M(B) - (Cu)(Cu)^T, \quad B(x_-) = K, \quad B(x_+) = -K,$$
bounded by $\|B(x)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \|K\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ and with $\|C\|_{\mathrm{HS}}$ uniformly bounded for $0<\varepsilon \le 1$.

Let $Q$ be a smooth $d$-dimensional manifold without boundary, $E \to Q$ a smooth real vector bundle of rank $d$, and $s_\theta$, $0 \le \theta \le 1$, a continuous homotopy of sections whose zero set $\{(q, \theta): s_\theta(q) = 0\}$ is compact. If $s_0$ is smooth with an odd number of zeros, all transverse, then $s_1$ has a zero. This is the compact-one-manifold form of mod-two degree; see Milnor's Topology from the Differentiable Viewpoint, 1965, Sections 2-4.

Suppose $s_1$ has no zero. Choose a relatively compact smooth domain $\Omega \subseteq Q$ containing the projection of the joint zero set, and a smooth fiber metric. On $(\partial \Omega \times [0,1]) \cup (\bar\Omega \times \{1\})$, the norm of the section has a strictly positive minimum. Reparametrize the homotopy so it equals $s_0$ on a small initial collar. Approximate it smoothly on $\bar\Omega \times [0,1]$, keeping agreement with $s_0$ on a smaller initial collar: use finitely many bundle charts, approximate the continuous coefficient functions by smooth functions, and combine with a smooth partition of unity; a cutoff supported inside the original collar splices the approximation to $s_0$. Choose the approximation error below a quarter of the boundary minimum, so no zeros appear near the spatial boundary or the final boundary.

Make the smooth section transverse to zero while keeping it unchanged near the initial boundary. Inside an initial collar the zeros are already transverse; any remaining zeros sit in a compact interior set. Choose finitely many compactly supported local frame sections whose values span every fiber on a neighborhood of the interior set, with supports away from the initial boundary and the forbidden boundaries. Adding small linear combinations gives a finite-dimensional family whose derivative in the family parameters is onto wherever transversality is still needed. Restrict the parameters to a small open ball in $\mathbb{R}^m$; smallness preserves the positive gap on the complement of the spanning neighborhood and on the initial collar, so no new zeros appear there.

The universal zero set in the interior is a smooth manifold of dimension $m+1$. Applying Sard's theorem to its projection onto $\mathbb{R}^m$ gives arbitrarily small regular parameters. At a zero, write the universal derivative as $Av + Db$, where $v$ is a $($base$\, \times\, \theta)$ variation and $b$ a parameter variation. Because perturbations are only required where transversality is missing, $D$ is onto there; surjectivity of the projection on the kernel says $Av = -Db$ is solvable for every $b$, equivalent to $A$ being onto. Elsewhere the section is already transverse. Thus regular parameters make the fixed-parameter section transverse. Smallness in the smooth topology preserves the transversality already present in the initial collar, and smallness in norm preserves the boundary gap.

The zero set of the resulting section is a compact smooth one-manifold. Its boundary consists exactly of the $s_0$-zeros inside $\Omega \times \{0\}$. A compact one-manifold has an even number of boundary points, contradicting the odd count.

For the path-existence statement let $\kappa = \|K\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ and $\ell = x_+ - x_-$. Choose a smooth nonnegative $\chi$ on $\operatorname{Sym}_N$, invariant under orthogonal conjugation, compactly supported, and equal to one on $\{\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \kappa\}$; for instance $\chi(B) = \eta(\operatorname{tr}B^2)$ with $\eta \equiv 1$ on $[0, N\kappa^2]$ and $\eta = 0$ outside a larger interval. Then $\widehat M = \chi M$ is bounded and globally Lipschitz.

Let $\mathcal{L}$ be the real vector space of lower triangular $N \times N$ matrices. For $C \in \mathcal{L}$ and $0 \le \theta \le 1$, solve
$$\varepsilon B' = \theta \widehat M(B) - (Cu)(Cu)^T, \quad B(x_-) = K.$$
A bounded globally Lipschitz field and continuous forcing give a unique solution on $\bar I$, and the integral equation with Gronwall gives continuous dependence in $(C, \theta)$. Thus $\Phi_\theta(C) = B(x_+) + K$ is a continuous homotopy of maps from $\mathcal{L}$ to $\operatorname{Sym}_N$.

At a zero of $\Phi_\theta$, integrating and using orthonormality of $u$ gives
$$CC^T = 2\varepsilon K + \theta \int_I \widehat M(B(x))\, dx,$$
so $\|C\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 \le 2\varepsilon \operatorname{tr}K + \ell \sup_B |\operatorname{tr}\widehat M(B)|$. For fixed $\varepsilon$ the joint zero set in $\mathcal{L} \times [0,1]$ is compact.

Let $G$ be the group of diagonal sign matrices with entries $\pm 1$. The equivariance $M(SBS) = SM(B)S$, invariance of $\chi$, and uniqueness of the flow give $\Phi_\theta(SC) = S\Phi_\theta(C)S$ for $S \in G$. Suppose row $i$ of $C$ vanishes and $S_i$ changes only that row's sign; then $S_i C = C$, so uniqueness forces $S_i B S_i = B$ throughout the flow and $B e_i = b_i e_i$. Consequently $\varepsilon b_i' = \theta \chi(B(x)) \mu(k_i^2 - b_i^2)$ with $b_i(x_-) = k_i$. Regarding $\theta \chi(B(x))/\varepsilon$ as a known continuous coefficient, local Lipschitz uniqueness for the scalar equation gives $b_i \equiv k_i$, and since $k_i>0$ the terminal value cannot be $-k_i$. Hence no zero of $\Phi_\theta$ has a vanishing row.

Let $\mathcal{L}^{\times}$ be the open subset of $\mathcal{L}$ where every row is nonzero. The joint zero set lies in $\mathcal{L}^{\times} \times [0,1]$. The action of $G$ on $\mathcal{L}^{\times}$ is free, so $Q = \mathcal{L}^{\times}/G$ is a smooth manifold of dimension $d = N(N+1)/2$. Sign conjugation on $\operatorname{Sym}_N$ turns $(\mathcal{L}^{\times} \times \operatorname{Sym}_N)/G \to Q$ into a smooth rank-$d$ vector bundle $E \to Q$, and $\Phi_\theta$ descends to a continuous homotopy of sections of $E$ with compact joint zero set.

At $\theta = 0$, $\Phi_0(C) = 2K - \varepsilon^{-1} CC^T$. The lower triangular solutions of $CC^T = 2\varepsilon K$ are exactly $\operatorname{diag}(\pm\sqrt{2\varepsilon k_1}, \ldots, \pm\sqrt{2\varepsilon k_N})$, one $G$-orbit: the first row determines $C_{11}$, the rest of the first column then vanishes, and induction proceeds. At any such $C = \operatorname{diag}(c_i)$, $D\Phi_0(C)[H] = -\varepsilon^{-1}(HC^T + CH^T)$ has diagonal entries $-2c_i H_{ii}/\varepsilon$ and entries with $i>j$ equal to $-c_j H_{ij}/\varepsilon$; since all $c_i \ne 0$, this map is an isomorphism onto $\operatorname{Sym}_N$. The initial quotient section has exactly one transverse zero, and the parity lemma gives a zero of $\Phi_1$.

Take a path from such a zero, and for a real unit $e$ put $h(x) = e^T B(x) e$. Whenever $|h(x)|>\kappa$, the directional comparison gives
$$\varepsilon h'(x) \le \chi(B(x))\mu(e^T K^2 e - h(x)^2) - |e^T C u(x)|^2 \le 0,$$
where $e^T K^2 e \le \kappa^2 < h(x)^2$ and $\chi \ge 0$. Both endpoint values of $h$ lie in $[-\kappa, \kappa]$. A component of $\{h > \kappa\}$ cannot start from the left endpoint, because $h$ is nonincreasing there; similarly, a component of $\{h < -\kappa\}$ cannot return to the right endpoint. Hence $|h(x)| \le \kappa$ on $I$. Since $e$ was arbitrary, $\|B(x)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \kappa$ and $\chi(B(x)) = 1$ everywhere, so the path solves the uncut equation. Integrating the ODE once more and using orthonormality of $u$ gives
$$\|C\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 \le 2\operatorname{tr}K + \ell \sup_{\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \kappa}|\operatorname{tr}M(B)|,$$
uniformly in $0<\varepsilon \le 1$.

Fix $\delta>0$ and use the resulting $M$, $J$. For $0<\varepsilon \le 1$ take the lower triangular $C_\varepsilon$ and path $B_\varepsilon$ from the connecting-path proposition, and put $a = C_\varepsilon u$. The path is $C^1$, $a$ is absolutely continuous, and the chain and product rules give, almost everywhere,
$$(J(B) + a^T B a)' = 2(a')^T Ba - \operatorname{tr}((M(B) - aa^T) B') = 2(a')^T Ba - \varepsilon^{-1}\|M(B) - aa^T\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2.$$
Since $a$ vanishes on $\partial I$, integration together with $2(a')^TBa \le |a'|^2 + a^T B^2 a$ gives
$$J(-K) - J(K) \le \mathcal{E}_K(a) + \int_I F_\varepsilon(B(x), a(x))\, dx,$$
where
$$F_\varepsilon(B,a) = |a|^{2q} - a^T(K^2 - B^2)a - \varepsilon^{-1}\|M(B) - aa^T\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2.$$

The compactness bound from the connecting-path proposition places every $(B_\varepsilon(x), a_\varepsilon(x))$, $x \in \bar I$, $0<\varepsilon \le 1$, in a single compact set $\mathcal{K}$; the continuous representative of $u$, the bound on $\|C_\varepsilon\|_{\mathrm{HS}}$, and the fixed $\delta$ enter, but no bound uniform in $\delta$. Put
$$\eta_\varepsilon = \max_{(B,a) \in \mathcal{K}} \max\{F_\varepsilon(B,a), 0\}.$$
Then $\eta_\varepsilon \to 0$ as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. Otherwise $F_{\varepsilon_n}(B_n, a_n) \ge \eta > 0$ for some $\varepsilon_n \downarrow 0$, and compactness produces a limit $(B_*, a_*)$ with $M(B_*) = a_* a_*^T$ and $|a_*|^{2q} - a_*^T(K^2 - B_*^2)a_* \ge \eta$, contradicting the rank-one comparison.

Hence $J(-K) - J(K) \le \sup_C \mathcal{E}_K(Cu) + |I|\eta_\varepsilon$. Passing to the limit $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ at fixed $\delta$ and inserting
$$J(-K) - J(K) = \sum_{i=1}^N \int_{-k_i}^{k_i} \mu(k_i^2 - s^2)\, ds = \sum_{i=1}^N \int_{-k_i}^{k_i} ((k_i^2 - s^2 + \delta)^{\sigma} - \delta^{\sigma})\, ds$$
gives
$$\sup_C \mathcal{E}_K(Cu) \ge \sum_{i=1}^N \int_{-k_i}^{k_i} ((k_i^2 - s^2 + \delta)^{\sigma} - \delta^{\sigma})\, ds.$$
Letting $\delta \downarrow 0$, the integrand converges on each interval to $(k_i^2 - s^2)^{\sigma}$ and is dominated, by subadditivity of the power $\sigma$, by the same function. Dominated convergence yields the action inequality.

For $0 \le W \in L^p(I)$, $p = 1+\sigma$, let $-k_1^2 \ge \cdots \ge -k_N^2$ be the first $N$ negative Dirichlet eigenvalues of $-d^2/dx^2 - W$, with $k_1 \ge \cdots \ge k_N > 0$. Then
$$\sum_{j=1}^N k_j^{2\gamma} \le \ell_\sigma \int_I W^p, \quad \ell_\sigma = \frac{A_\sigma}{B(1/2, 1+\sigma)}, \quad A_\sigma = \frac{\sigma^{\sigma}}{(1+\sigma)^{1+\sigma}}.$$

Let $u_1, \ldots, u_N \in H^1_0(I)$ be real orthonormal Dirichlet eigenfunctions; put $u = (u_i)_{i=1}^N$, $K = \operatorname{diag}(k_i)$, and $a = Cu$ for a real lower triangular $C$. Since $a_i = \sum_{j \le i} C_{ij} u_j$ and
$$\int_I (u_i' u_j' - W u_i u_j)\, dx = -k_j^2 \delta_{ij},$$
the $i$th eigenfunction equation in form sense gives
$$\int_I (|a_i'|^2 - W|a_i|^2 + k_i^2 |a_i|^2)\, dx = \sum_{j \le i} C_{ij}^2 (k_i^2 - k_j^2) \le 0.$$
Summing over $i$ and putting $m = |a|^2$,
$$\int_I (|a'|^2 + a^T K^2 a - Wm)\, dx \le 0, \quad \text{so} \quad \mathcal{E}_K(a) \le \int_I (Wm - m^q)\, dx.$$
Maximizing $Wm - m^q$ over $m \ge 0$ gives $m = (\sigma W/(1+\sigma))^{\sigma}$ and $Wm - m^q = A_\sigma W^{1+\sigma}$. The action inequality therefore gives
$$\sum_{j=1}^N \int_{-k_j}^{k_j} (k_j^2 - s^2)^{\sigma}\, ds \le A_\sigma \int_I W^{1+\sigma}.$$
Substituting $s = k_j t$ produces
$$\int_{-k_j}^{k_j}(k_j^2 - s^2)^{\sigma}\, ds = k_j^{2\sigma+1} B(1/2, 1+\sigma) = k_j^{2\gamma} B(1/2, 1+\sigma),$$
so $\sum_{j=1}^N k_j^{2\gamma} \le \ell_\sigma \int_I W^p$.

The beta-gamma identity identifies $\ell_\sigma$ with the semiclassical constant:
$$\ell_\sigma = \left(\frac{\sigma}{1+\sigma}\right)^{\sigma} \frac{\Gamma(\sigma+3/2)}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(\sigma+2)} = 2\left(\frac{\sigma}{1+\sigma}\right)^{\sigma} L_{\gamma,1}^{\mathrm{cl}}.$$

To exhaust the negative spectrum, first record that the operator is well-defined. Fix $p = \gamma+1/2 > 1$. Sobolev's inequality on $\mathbb{R}$ reads $\|v\|_\infty \le 2^{1/4}\|v'\|_2^{1/2}\|v\|_2^{1/2}$, and Holder's inequality at $p' = p/(p-1)$ together with this bound gives $\|v\|_{2p'}^2 \le 2^{1/p}\|v'\|_2^{1/p}\|v\|_2^{2-1/p}$. For every $\eta>0$ there is therefore a constant $b_{\eta,W}<\infty$ with $\int_\mathbb{R} W|v|^2 \le \eta\|v'\|_2^2 + b_{\eta,W}\|v\|_2^2$ for all $v \in H^1(\mathbb{R})$, and the KLMN theorem makes $h_{-W}$ closed and bounded below on $H^1(\mathbb{R})$.

Negative spectral projections of $H_{-W}$ have finite rank. Choose bounded, compactly supported $W_n \to W$ in $L^p$. Multiplication by $\sqrt{W_n}$ is compact from $H^1(\mathbb{R})$ to $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ by Rellich, and $\|(\sqrt W - \sqrt{W_n})v\|_2^2 \le C_p\|W - W_n\|_p \|v\|_{H^1}^2$; multiplication by $\sqrt W$ is consequently the operator-norm limit of a compact sequence and is compact.

If the spectral subspace for $(-\infty, -\alpha]$ with $\alpha>0$ contained an orthonormal sequence $v_j$, that sequence would be bounded in $H^1$ by the relative form bound, would have a subsequence converging weakly to zero in $H^1$, and would therefore force $\int W|v_j|^2 \to 0$. But $h_{-W}[v_j] \le -\alpha$ together with $\|v_j'\|_2^2 \ge 0$ gives $\int W|v_j|^2 \ge \alpha$, a contradiction.

On a bounded interval $I$, the Dirichlet form is equivalent to the $H^1_0(I)$ norm and $H^1_0(I) \subseteq L^2(I)$ is compact, so the associated operator has compact resolvent. The correspondence between closed semibounded forms and self-adjoint operators, and the form-domain min--max principle, are treated in M. Reed and B. Simon, Methods of Modern Mathematical Physics IV (Academic Press, 1978), and G. Teschl, Mathematical Methods in Quantum Mechanics (AMS Graduate Studies in Mathematics 99, 2009).

Let $0 \le W \in L^p(\mathbb{R})$. Fix finitely many negative eigenvalues $\lambda_1 \le \cdots \le \lambda_N < 0$, counted with multiplicity, and corresponding orthonormal eigenfunctions $\phi_1, \ldots, \phi_N \in H^1(\mathbb{R})$. Choose smooth $\chi_R$ equal to one on $[-R, R]$, supported in $I_R = (-2R, 2R)$, with $0 \le \chi_R \le 1$ and $\|\chi_R'\|_{\infty} \le C/R$. Then $v_{j,R} = \chi_R \phi_j \to \phi_j$ in $H^1(\mathbb{R})$; Holder's inequality together with the embedding $H^1(\mathbb{R}) \subseteq L^{2p'}(\mathbb{R})$ passes this convergence through the potential form.

Let $G_R$ and $Q_R$ be the Gram and form matrices of $v_{1,R}, \ldots, v_{N,R}$. Then $G_R \to I_N$ and $Q_R \to \Lambda := \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_N)$ in matrix norm. Set $g_R = \|G_R - I_N\|_{\mathrm{op}} < 1$ and $e_R = \|Q_R - \Lambda\|_{\mathrm{op}}$; Rayleigh quotients over the span of $v_{1,R}, \ldots, v_{N,R}$ differ from coefficient-space quotients uniformly by at most $\eta_R = (e_R + \|\Lambda\|_{\mathrm{op}} g_R)/(1 - g_R) \to 0$: for $v = \sum a_i v_{i,R}$ the form quotient is $a^TQ_Ra/a^TG_Ra$, which is within $\eta_R$ of $a^T\Lambda a/a^Ta$ when $\|G_R - I_N\|_{\mathrm{op}} < 1$. The form-domain min-max principle gives, for $1 \le j \le N$,
$$\lambda_j(I_R) \le \lambda_j + \eta_R,$$
where $\lambda_j(I_R)$ is the $j$th Dirichlet eigenvalue on $I_R$; these are negative for large $R$. The Dirichlet estimate gives
$$\sum_{j=1}^N (-\lambda_j - \eta_R)^{\gamma} \le \sum_{j=1}^N |\lambda_j(I_R)|^{\gamma} \le \ell_\sigma \int_{I_R} W^p \le \ell_\sigma \int_{\mathbb{R}} W^p.$$
Letting $R \to \infty$ gives the bound for every finite partial sum; the supremum over $N$ gives $\operatorname{Tr}(H_{-W})_-^{\gamma} \le \ell_\sigma \int_{\mathbb{R}} W^p$. The full moment is finite even if infinitely many negative eigenvalues exist.

The same argument handles sign-indefinite potentials. Let $V$ be real measurable with $V_- \in L^p(\mathbb{R})$ and let
$$\mathcal{D}_V = \{v \in H^1(\mathbb{R}): \int_{\mathbb{R}} V_+ |v|^2 < \infty\}$$
be dense in $L^2(\mathbb{R})$; this holds, for instance, when $V_+ \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})$. The form
$$h_V[v] = \int_{\mathbb{R}} |v'|^2 + \int_{\mathbb{R}} V_+|v|^2 - \int_{\mathbb{R}} V_-|v|^2, \quad v \in \mathcal{D}_V,$$
is closed and bounded below; its associated operator $H_V$ has negative spectrum of finite multiplicity, and
$$\operatorname{Tr}(H_V)_-^{\gamma} \le \ell_\sigma \int_{\mathbb{R}} V_-^p.$$
The positive form $\int|v'|^2 + \int V_+|v|^2$ is closed on $\mathcal{D}_V$ by completeness of $H^1$ and of the weighted $L^2$-space; the relative bound with $W = V_-$ gives closure and semiboundedness after subtracting the negative part, and $C_c^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}) \subset \mathcal{D}_V$ when $V_+ \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$.

On a negative spectral subspace bounded away from zero, the same relative form bound controls the $H^1$-norm of unit vectors, since the positive term may be discarded; compactness of multiplication by $\sqrt{V_-}$ then excludes an infinite orthonormal sequence.

On $\mathcal{D}_V$, $h_V \ge h_{-V_-}$; the min-max principle with the inclusion of form domains bounds each negative eigenvalue of $H_V$ below by the corresponding eigenvalue of $H_{-V_-}$, and summing the line bound gives the display.

Sharpness of the constant is checked on an explicit potential. Set $r = 1/\sigma > 1$, $W(x) = (r+1)\operatorname{sech}^2(rx)$, $\psi(x) = \operatorname{sech}^{1/r}(rx)$. Direct differentiation gives $\psi'/\psi = -\tanh(rx)$ and $\psi''/\psi = 1 - (r+1)\operatorname{sech}^2(rx)$, so $\psi, \psi', \psi''$ decay exponentially; $\psi \in H^2(\mathbb{R})$ lies in the operator domain of $H_{-W}$, and
$$H_{-W} \psi = -\psi'' - W\psi = -\psi.$$
With $t = rx$ and $z = \tanh t$,
$$\int_{\mathbb{R}} W^p = \frac{(r+1)^p}{r} B(1/2, p), \quad \left(\int_{\mathbb{R}} W^p\right)^{-1} = \ell_\sigma.$$
The eigenvalue $-1$ gives $\operatorname{Tr}(H_{-W})_-^{\gamma} \ge 1$; the upper bound gives the reverse inequality, so equality holds. The constant $2(\sigma/(1+\sigma))^{\sigma} L_{\gamma,1}^{\mathrm{cl}}$ equals $\ell_\sigma$ and is optimal.
