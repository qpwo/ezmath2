# A positive solution to Tingley's problem



maketitle

Abstract: 
Every surjective isometry between the unit spheres of two real Banach spaces
extends uniquely to a surjective real-linear isometry of the spaces. This gives
an affirmative answer to Tingley's problem in full generality, with no
restriction on dimension or geometric regularity. If distances between points
of different radii could fail to be preserved, we magnify that failure into a
maximal positive defect in a possibly larger pair of spaces. Extremal chords
are then aligned by a fixed-point argument for a map that strictly contracts
Kuratowski's measure of noncompactness; a support estimate and a convexity
argument finally rule the aligned configuration out.


# Introduction and main result

Let X be a real normed space. Its unit sphere is
S_X={xin X:norm{x}=1}, equipped with the distance coming from the norm.
Tingley's problem [cite] asks:

if f:S_Xto S_Y is a surjective isometry, must it extend to a
real-linear isometry of X onto Y?

A surjective sphere isometry always maps antipodal points to antipodal points,
so the only possible linear extension is the radial map
[
T(0)=0, T(x)=norm{x},f!(frac{x}{norm{x}}) (xne0).
]
This map matches f on the sphere and preserves distances between points at the
same radius. The whole difficulty is a single point: the metric of the sphere
knows nothing directly about pairs of points at different radii, yet the
extension must preserve those distances too.

[Main theorem]
Let X,Y be nonzero real Banach spaces and let f:S_Xto S_Y be a surjective
isometry. Then T above is a surjective real-linear isometry of X onto Y,
and it is the unique linear extension of f.


The spaces are arbitrary: no separability, reflexivity, smoothness or strict
convexity is assumed, and the dimension may be infinite. Applied to complex
spaces regarded as real spaces, the conclusion is real linearity only.

Background.:  The result belongs to the extension theory of metric
isometries. Mazur and Ulam [cite] proved that a surjective isometry
between normed spaces is affine, and Mankiewicz [cite] extended
isometries of convex bodies with nonempty interior. A sphere has empty ambient
interior, so neither applies directly. Positive solutions were first obtained for
concrete classes: C_0-spaces [cite], ell^p(Gamma) and L^p with
pne2 [cite], then, through the Mazur--Ulam property
of Cheng and Dong [cite], for operator algebras
[cite]. Facial methods
[cite], low dimensions [cite] and specific families
[cite] cover further cases.
The present argument is uniform: it isolates the largest possible failure to
preserve a cross-radius distance and shows that this failure cannot exist.

The proof in five moves.:  The argument has five moves.
(1) We measure the worst error in preserving a distance between two radii and
show that, if it is positive, it becomes an attained maximum after passing
to an ultrapower (Section~sec:attainment).
(2) From that maximum we manufacture two ``extremal chords'', one in S_X and one
in S_Y, whose endpoints line up around common base points (Section~sec:alignment).
(3) A return map on sphere directions, which pairs the far end of one chord with
the far end of the other, strictly shrinks Kuratowski's measure of noncompactness;
Darbo's theorem then gives a fixed direction, i.e. truly aligned chords.
(4) Support estimates and a convexity comparison show that aligned chords are
impossible (Section~sec:comparison).
(5) With the defect zero, the radial map is an isometry, and the classical
Mazur--Ulam reflection trick upgrades it to a linear map (Section~sec:extension).

Notation.:  Supports are finite-dimensional throughout
(Lemma~lem:support). We write co and clco for convex hull and closed
convex hull, and drop the norm subscript when the space is clear. Section
numbers are referenced in the text; all auxiliary results are proved, the
fixed-point inputs in Appendix~sec:appendix.

# Supports and radial contact

A support at a vector a in a finite-dimensional normed space is a
linear functional phi with norm{phi}le1 and phi(a)=norm{a}.
The next lemma is finite-dimensional Hahn--Banach [cite]; we prove it
because it is used constantly.


Supports exist and extend, with the same norm bound, to any prescribed
finite-dimensional enlargement of their domain. If unit vectors v,w and numbers
alpha,beta>0 satisfy norm{alpha v+beta w}=alpha+beta, then v and w
share a support: a norm-bounded functional equal to 1 at both.

Proof. 
Start with the norming functional on the line through a nonzero vector. To extend
L from a subspace V to V+R e, pick its value at e in
[
∩_{ain V},[,L(a)-norm{a-e}, L(a)+norm{a-e},].
]
The intervals meet pairwise because L(a)-L(b)lenorm{a-b}lenorm{a-e}+norm{b-e};
they have the finite intersection property, and the interval indexed by a=0 is
compact, so they share a point. That value gives |L(a)-L(e)|lenorm{a-e} and
hence the norm bound on V+R e; finitely many extensions reach any
finite-dimensional enlargement. Finally, a support phi at
alpha v+beta w has phi(alpha v)lealpha and phi(beta w)lebeta
with total alpha+beta, forcing equality, so phi(v)=phi(w)=1.



Let norm{c}<1. Each ray c+R_+v, vin S_X, meets S_X in a unique point
c+rho(v)v. The radius rho(v) is continuous and satisfies
1-norm{c}lerho(v)le1+norm{c}.

Proof. 
The norm is continuous and the unit ball is bounded, so a first contact exists.
Every point strictly between an interior point of the ball and a point of the ball
is interior (a small ball sits inside the appropriate convex combination), which
forces uniqueness. The bounds are the triangle inequality. For continuity, a
convergent subsequence of the bounded scalars rho(v_n) must converge to
rho(v) by continuity of the norm and uniqueness of contact, so the whole
sequence does.


# Attaining the maximal defect

For a surjective sphere isometry put

D_q(x,y)=norm{f(x)-qf(y)}-norm{x-qy},
M=sup_{x,yin S_X, 0le qle1}|D_q(x,y)|.

Because f is bijective, the inverse map has defect

D_q^{f^{-1}}!(f(x),f(y))=-D_q(x,y),

so the inverse has the same absolute bound. Each D_q(·,·) is
2-Lipschitz in q uniformly in x,y, and D_0=D_1=0. Hence

|D_q(x,y)|le2min(q,1-q), 0le Mle1.


[Attainment]
If some surjective sphere isometry has M>0, there are nonzero real Banach spaces,
a surjective sphere isometry (still called f:S_Xto S_Y), and x_0,yin S_X,
0<t<1, with

|D_q(a,c)|le Mquad(a,cin S_X, 0le qle1),

norm{f(x_0)-tf(y)}-norm{x_0-ty}=M>0.


Proof. 
Take triples approaching the supremum, pass to a subsequence of one sign and, if
needed, replace f by f^{-1}. A further subsequence has radius parameters
converging to tin[0,1]; the 2-Lipschitz bound lets us replace them all by
t while the signed defects still tend to M. By (eq:defectbound),
M/2le tle1-M/2, so 0<t<1.

We build the limit as a Banach ultrapower [cite]. Fix a free
ultrafilter U on N; every bounded real sequence has a unique
limit along U, and these limits respect sums, inequalities and
continuous functions. On bounded sequences in X set
norm{(a_n)}_{U}{U}{a_n}, let Q_X be the quotient
by the kernel, and define Q_Y likewise; constant sequences embed X,Y
isometrically, so the quotients are nonzero. Every unit vector of Q_X or Q_Y
has a representative made of unit vectors (normalize each nonzero term, replace
zeros by a fixed unit vector).

Apply f termwise to unit representatives to get F:S_{Q_X}to S_{Q_Y}. The
identity lim_{U}{f(a_n)-f(b_n)}=lim_{U}{a_n-b_n}
shows F is well defined and preserves distances, and applying f^{-1} termwise
gives its inverse, so F is a surjective sphere isometry. For fixed
qin[0,1],
[
norm{F([a_n])-qF([b_n])}-norm{[a_n]-q[b_n]}=lim_{U},b_n),
]
which lies in [-M,M] since each term does; the chosen maximizing sequences give
equality at t. Completing both quotients extends F and F^{-1} by continuity
and density to a surjective sphere isometry of the completions, and continuity
keeps the bound and the equality.


It remains only to rule out the attained configuration (eq:maximal), retained
through Sections~sec:alignment and~sec:comparison.

# Aligning extremal chords

For a bounded set H in a normed space, the Kuratowski measure of noncompactness
[cite] is
[
chi(H)=inf{delta>0: H is covered by finitely many sets of diameter ledelta}.
]
We use the strict-contraction fixed-point theorem of Darbo [cite], proved
in Appendix~sec:appendix.


Let C be a nonempty closed bounded convex subset of a Banach space and
g:Cto C continuous with chi(g(H))legammachi(H) for all Hsubset C and

[Aligned chords]

All four radii lie in [1-t,1+t].

Proof. 
Fix a direction at ty, map its sphere contact to S_Y, take the opposite sphere
contact on the line through tb, pull it back to S_X, and reverse its direction
from ty. A fixed direction of this return map aligns the two chords.

For vin S_X define p=p(v)>0 by x(v)=ty+pvin S_X (Lemma~lem:radial).
Then set successively

f(x(v))=tb+sw, f(z)=tb-uwin S_Y,

where win S_Y and u>0 is the radial contact in direction -w. All these
quantities are continuous in v, and each radius lies in [1-t,1+t].

Let E={vin S_X: s(v)-p(v)=M}, closed in X. It is nonempty: with
v_0=(x_0-ty)/norm{x_0-ty}, uniqueness of radial contact gives x(v_0)=x_0, and
(eq:maximal) puts v_0in E. For vin E, distance preservation and the
bound (eq:maximal) give
[

]
Since s=p+M, both are equalities:

Thus v and g(v) share a support.

Indeed, for the fixed z,r,u attached to v, the shared support gives
norm{x(l)-z}ge p(l)+r (both summands are supported to the value 1 along
l and g(v)), while distance preservation gives
[

]
Hence s(l)-p(l)ge r-u=M, and the bound (eq:maximal) makes it equal, so

co{v_0,...,v_n}subset E. Take a strictly positive convex combination a_n
of v_0,...,v_n and set v_{n+1}=g(a_n). A common support phi of

by strict positivity, so for every l in the enlarged hull,
1=phi(l)lenorm{l}le1. Thus l is a unit vector sharing phi with
g(a_n) and lies in E by propagation.

Choose the coefficients so that the a_n are dense in C=clco{v_0,v_1,...}:
enumerate finite rational probability vectors, mix each (zero-padded) with the

Strict contraction. For v,v'in E, with primes marking associated

[

With a=1-t>0, U=1+t, all radii lie in [a,U] and M<r,s, so

# The linear extension

Proof. [Proof of Theorem~thm:main]
If the sphere isometry had positive defect, Lemma~lem:attainment and
Proposition~prop:no-defect would clash; hence M=0. For a=alpha x and
c=beta y with x,y unit and alphagebeta>0, put q=beta/alpha. Then
[
norm{T(a)-T(c)}=alphanorm{f(x)-qf(y)}=alphanorm{x-qy}=norm{a-c},
]
and symmetry handles betagealpha; distances to 0 follow from norm
preservation. For cin Y∖{0} pick yin S_X with
f(y)=c/norm{c}; then T(norm{c},y)=c, so T is a surjective isometry onto
Y fixing 0.

It remains to upgrade a surjective isometry to a linear map; this is the affine
step of Mazur--Ulam [cite] in the reflection form of Nica
[cite], who credits V"ais"al"a and Vogt [cite].
Let F:Eto E' be a surjective isometry of real normed spaces and a,cin E. With
m=(a+c)/2, m'=(F(a)+F(c))/2 and delta=norm{F(m)-m'}, the triangle
inequality gives deltalenorm{a-c}/2. The reflection
R(z)=2m'-z is a surjective isometry of E', so H=F^{-1}RF is a surjective
self-isometry of E swapping a,c with midpoint defect
norm{H(m)-m}=2delta. Iterating produces surjective self-isometries of E
whose midpoint defects double while staying lenorm{a-c}/2; thus delta=0,
and every surjective isometry preserves midpoints.

So T((a+c)/2)=(T(a)+T(c))/2. With T(0)=0 this gives first T(2a)=2T(a) and
then T(a+c)=T(a)+T(c); additivity plus continuity gives real homogeneity.
Finally, a linear extension is fixed on each nonzero vector by its norm and unit
direction, so it is unique.

# The fixed-point ingredients

We prove Theorem~thm:condensing. The measure-of-noncompactness mechanism is
Darbo's [cite]; the compact case is Schauder's [cite],
obtained from the simplex argument via Sperner's lemma [cite].


For every bounded nonempty H in a normed space,
chi(overlineoverline H)=H)=chi(H) and chi(clco H)=chi(H). If C_1supset C_2supset...
are nonempty closed bounded subsets of a Banach space with chi(C_n)to0, their
intersection is nonempty and compact.

Proof. 
Closing a finite diameter cover preserves diameters, giving the first identity.
For the second, cover H by finitely many H_1,...,H_N of diameter ledelta,
with R a norm bound. Each co H_i has diameter ledelta. Write a point of
co H as sum_ilambda_i z_i, z_iinco H_i, sum_ilambda_i=1, and
partition the coefficient simplex into finitely many pieces of ell_1-diameter
leeta. Two sums in one piece differ by at most delta+Reta. Hence
chi(co H)ledelta+Reta; let eta↓0, minimize over delta, and use
monotonicity plus the closure identity for the reverse inequality.

For the intersection, pick x_nin C_n. For each k a late C_n has a finite
cover of diameter <2^{-k}; successive subsequences and a diagonal choice give a
Cauchy subsequence of the x_n with indices going to infinity. Its limit lies in
every C_n, so the intersection is nonempty; it is closed with
chi=0, hence totally bounded, hence compact.



Every continuous self-map of a finite-dimensional probability simplex has a fixed
point.

Proof. 
Let P be a continuous self-map of
Delta_m={ainR^{m+1}:a_ige0, sum_i a_i=1} (m=0 trivial). Subdivide into
simplices and label each vertex a by an index i with a_i>0 and
a_ige P(a)_i (it exists, else summing over positive coordinates gives
1<sum_i P(a)_i=1); the label of a vertex lies among the vertices of every
original face containing it.

Sperner's parity fact: in a finite face-compatible triangulation of an m-simplex
with labels 0,...,m obeying that boundary condition, the number of small
m-simplices carrying all labels is odd. Count, mod 2, incidences of small
m-simplices with facets labeled 0,...,m-1: internal facets count twice; a
boundary facet lies only in the original face missing label m, whose count is
odd by induction (starting from the single vertex in dimension 0). A small
m-simplex contributes one if fully labeled, two if it carries 0,...,m-1 with
a repeat, and none otherwise.

Subdivisions of arbitrarily small mesh exist: subdivide faces, cone to the
barycenter, and note that barycenters of nested faces with kle l vertices differ
by at most (1-k/l) times the diameter, so dimension mge1 shrinks diameters by
at most m/(m+1) per round. Pick a fully labeled small simplex in each round; by
compactness and shrinking mesh a subsequence of vertices converges to one point
a. The label-i vertices satisfy a_ige P(a)_i in the limit for every i,
and summing forces equality, so a=P(a).



A continuous self-map g of a nonempty compact convex set K in a normed space
has a fixed point.

Proof. 
For delta>0 choose a finite strict delta-net P_1,...,P_Nin K and set
lambda_i(x)=max{delta-norm{P_i-g(x)},0}/sum_jmax{delta-norm{P_j-g(x)},0},
continuous probability weights. The simplex self-map
(a_i)|->(lambda_i(sum_j a_jP_j)) has, by Lemma~lem:simplex, a fixed
coefficient vector; its convex combination xin K satisfies
norm{x-g(x)}lesum_ilambda_i(x)norm{P_i-g(x)}<delta. Let delta↓0
and pass to a convergent subsequence in K.


Proof. [Proof of Theorem~thm:condensing]
Let C_0=C and C_{n+1}=clco(g(C_n)); these are nonempty closed bounded convex
sets with C_{n+1}subset C_n. By Lemma~lem:chi,
chi(C_{n+1})=chi(g(C_n))legammachi(C_n), so K=∩_n C_n is nonempty,
compact and convex. If xin K then g(x)in g(C_n)subset C_{n+1} for all n,
so g(K)subset K; Lemma~lem:compactfixed applies.
