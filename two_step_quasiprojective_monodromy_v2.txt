Campana's theory of special varieties relates the absence of fibrations
of general type to restrictions on topology and monodromy. Let X be a
connected smooth complex quasi-projective variety with a smooth
projective compactification barbar XX whose boundary D=barbar XX - X is a
reduced simple normal crossing divisor. Let f : barbar XX to Z be a
fibration, meaning a surjective algebraic map with connected general
fibre. For a prime divisor Q in the smooth projective base Z, the
orbifold multiplicity is
 m_f(Q) = inf_{f(P)=Q} r(f,P) m_P(D), 
where the infimum ranges over prime divisors P of barbar XX that map onto
Q, the integer r(f,P) is the multiplicity of P in the divisor
f^*Q, and m_P(D) is the multiplicity of P in the boundary. Removed
components carry m_P(D) = ∞; components outside the boundary carry
m_P(D) = 1. The orbifold base divisor on Z is
 Delta(f, D) = sum_Q (1 - 1/m_f(Q)), Q. 
The quotient 1/∞ is 0, so boundary components contribute coefficient
one. A fibration is of general type when the Kodaira dimension of
K_Z + Delta(f,D) equals dim Z. The pair (barbar X,X, D) is special when
every fibration from every smooth projective birational model of
(barbar X,X, D), with the same boundary and every allowed elementary
birational orbifold morphism, has orbifold base of Kodaira dimension less
than the base dimension. This pair convention and the open-variety
convention of Cadorel, Deng, and Yamanoi are compatible on smooth
compactifications of the same open variety.

./pdf/campana-2011-orbifoldes-geometriques-speciales.pdf

The linear monodromy of the ordinary topological fundamental group of a
special quasi-projective variety is bounded at nilpotency class two. For a
group G put gamma_1 G = G and gamma_{r+1} G = [G, gamma_r G]. The
class is at most two when gamma_3 G = 1, equivalently when every
commutator [a,b] = a b a^{-1} b^{-1} is central. A property holds
virtually for G when it holds in a subgroup of finite index.

Theorem 1 (two-step linear monodromy). For every connected smooth special
complex quasi-projective variety V, every positive integer d, and
every representation rho : pi_1(V) to GL_d(ℂ), the
image rho(pi_1(V)) has a subgroup of finite index and nilpotency class
at most two.

Cadorel, Deng, and Yamanoi showed that the identity component of the
Zariski closure of rho(pi_1(V)) is a product of a unipotent group and
a torus, hence virtually nilpotent, but left the class of the unipotent
part unconstrained. The class-two bound was subsequently announced by Cao,
Deng, Hacon, and Paun on the strength of a different method. The
argument uses a structural completion theorem that does not depend on
specialness of the fibre.

Theorem 2 (completion under quasi-Albanese exactness). Let V be a
connected smooth complex quasi-projective variety with base point v and
algebraic quasi-Albanese map a : V to A = Alb(V). Suppose a
is dominant. Choose a smooth connected general fibre F through v, and
suppose the sequence of ordinary fundamental groups
 pi_1(F,v) → pi_1(V,v) xrightarrow{a_*} pi_1(A,a(v))
→ 1 
is exact. Then every canonical rational unipotent quotient of
pi_1(V,v) has nilpotency class at most two; the full rational unipotent
completion is finite-dimensional and of class at most two; and every
finite-dimensional complex unipotent representation has image of class at
most two.

The quasi-Albanese map is the universal algebraic morphism from V to a
semiabelian variety, in the sense of Fujino's construction. Exactness at
the first arrow identifies the image of the fibre group with the entire
kernel of a_*; it does not require the fibre group to inject. No
specialness of V, no specialness of F, and no prior nilpotence of
pi_1(V) is assumed. The proof uses two geometric facts: the
quasi-Albanese map induces an isomorphism H_1(V,ℚ) cong
H_1(A,ℚ) modulo torsion, and the algebraic inclusion of the
smooth fibre F ↪ V is a morphism of pointed mixed Hodge
structures.
./pdf/fujino-2024-quasi-albanese-maps.pdf

A second consequence, when the logarithmic Kodaira dimension of V
vanishes, is virtual class-two monodromy for every linear representation.

A different question concerns the general Albanese fibre of a compact
special variety: Campana asked whether it is special, and Campana and
Claudon answered in the affirmative for smooth projective varieties.
With reduced open boundary the analogous statement fails, and the
following open surfaces exhibit the failure.

Theorem 3 (special surface with nonspecial fibre). Let E be a complex
elliptic curve, let q_1,...,q_n in ℙ^1 be distinct with
n geq 3, and choose arbitrary points e_1,...,e_n in E. Let
 b : Y = Bl_{{(e_i,q_i)}}(E times ℙ^1)
→ E times ℙ^1 
be the blowup at the n distinct points. Let D_i be the strict
transform of E times {q_i}, let D = sum_i D_i, and let
X = Y - D. Then:
(i) the pair (Y,D) is special in the orbifold-pair sense: on every
smooth projective birational model carrying the same reduced boundary,
every fibration has orbifold base of Kodaira dimension less than the
base dimension;
(ii) the morphism a = (pr_E circ b)|_X : X to E is the
algebraic quasi-Albanese map after choosing origins, every fibre of a is
connected, and the general fibre is F simeq ℙ^1 -
{q_1,...,q_n};
(iii) the induced map a_* : pi_1(X) to pi_1(E) is an isomorphism, so
pi_1(X) simeq ℤ^2 and the map pi_1(F) to pi_1(X) is the
zero map.

The general fibre F has logarithmic canonical degree n - 2 > 0, so F
is not special. By (i) and the pair-to-open comparison, the total space
X is special. A special smooth quasi-projective surface therefore has a
general quasi-Albanese fibre that is not special, and the fibre-
specialness assertion of Cao, Deng, Hacon, and Paun fails on this example.
The fibre's ordinary group is free of rank n-1, but its image in
pi_1(X) = ℤ^2 is trivial, so the total space still meets the
monodromy bound of Theorem 1.

The geometric mechanism behind the failure lies in the orbifold
multiplicities. At each q_i the complete scheme fibre of
j = pr_{ℙ^1} circ b is the union D_i + A_i with
both components of multiplicity one; the curve A_i is the exceptional
ℙ^1 introduced by the blowup. The retained A_i makes the
direct orbifold multiplicity at q_i equal to one, whereas contracting
A_i first discards its contribution. Campana's composition rule records
exactly the exceptional-divisor hypothesis under which the direct and
iterated bases agree. The compound passage in the CDHP proof of Claim 7.17
treats flatness over the Iitaka base J as flatness over a contracted
intermediate space, and it is the latter that fails.

Recall the terminology of mixed Hodge structures. A rational mixed Hodge
structure is a finite-dimensional ℚ-vector space with
increasing weight filtration W_bullet and decreasing Hodge filtration
on the complexification, whose graded pieces are pure. It has weights at
most r when W_r is the entire space; it is pure of weight r when
additionally W_{r-1} = 0. Morphisms are strict: the image of a morphism
receives the filtration it has as a subquotient of the source. Tensor
products add weights. For smooth quasi-projective V, the first homology
H_1(V,ℚ) carries weights -1 and -2, and for the smooth
general fibre F the same bound holds; weights add under tensor product.
./pdf/deligne-1971-theorie-de-hodge-II.pdf

Hain's construction assigns to m_V a pro-mixed Hodge
structure compatible with the bracket, in such a way that the closed
lower central ideals gamma_r m_V are subobjects
and every g_c inherits an ordinary mixed Hodge structure.
Based algebraic morphisms induce morphisms of these structures: in
particular the algebraic inclusion F ↪ V yields a
morphism g_c(F) to g_c(V). Morgan's foundational
version had a functoriality assertion that was later corrected; the
pointed statement required is due to Hain.

An orbifold pair packages a smooth variety together with a ℚ-
divisor
 D = sum_P (1 - 1/m_P(D)), P, 
m_P(D) in ℤ_{geq 1} cup {∞}, 
with only finitely many nonzero coefficients. The convention
1/∞ = 0 gives a component of infinite multiplicity boundary
coefficient one and a component outside the boundary zero. For a
fibration f : Y to Z with Y smooth projective and Z smooth, and a
prime divisor Q subset Z, the orbifold multiplicity and base divisor
are
 m_f(Q) = inf_{f(P)=Q} r(f,P), m_P(D), 
Delta(f,D) = sum_Q (1 - 1/m_f(Q)), Q, 
where r(f,P) is the multiplicity of P in f^* Q. A divisor P
with codim_Z f(P) geq 2 is f-exceptional and is omitted
from the immediate infimum, though it may enter a later composition. The
infimum convention is the one fixed by Cao, Deng, Hacon, and Paun.
./pdf/cdhp-2026-two-step-nilpotent-monodromy.pdf

For the specific surface, the fibre identification j^*(q_i) = D_i + A_i
gives
 m_j(q_i) = min(1 cdot ∞, 1 cdot 1) = 1, 
the first term from the deleted component D_i with multiplicity
∞ in D and the second from the retained exceptional A_i with
multiplicity 1. Similarly every t notin {q_i} receives
multiplicity one from the unchanged complete elliptic fibre. Hence
 Delta(j, D) = 0, 
so the orbifold base of j is (ℙ^1, 0) even though kappa(Y,
K_Y + D) = 1.

A dominant morphism u : (Y', D') to (Y, D) of smooth orbifold pairs is
an orbifold morphism when, for every prime divisor P subset Y and
every component P' of u^{-1}(P) with multiplicity r in u^* P,
 r, m_{P'}(D') geq m_P(D). 
It is an elementary birational orbifold morphism when additionally u is
birational and u_* D' = D. An upper model of (Y, D) is a smooth
projective orbifold pair (Y', D') equipped with an elementary
birational orbifold morphism u : (Y', D') to (Y, D).

A fibration f : (Y, D) to Z is neat when it fits into a commutative
birational diagram with smooth spaces and horizontal fibrations, where
the source modification w is an elementary birational orbifold
morphism and every f-exceptional divisor is also w-exceptional.
Neatness gives the equality
 kappa(f, D) = kappa(Z, K_Z + Delta(f, D)), 
which identifies the canonical dimension of f with the Kodaira
dimension of the orbifold base K_Z + Delta(f, D).

Definition (pair specialness). A smooth projective orbifold pair (Y, D)
is special when, for every elementary birational orbifold morphism u :
(Y', D') to (Y, D) with (Y', D') smooth projective, and every neat
fibration g : (Y', D') to Z with dim Z > 0,
 kappa(Z, K_Z + Delta(g, D')) < dim Z. 

The pair convention implies the open-variety convention of Cadorel,
Deng, and Yamanoi. Start with an open fibration g : X to B and a
proper birational modification allowed in that convention. Compactify
over (Y, D), resolve the graph of the fibration, and choose a neat
upper model (Y', D') to (Y, D). Let D_{open} be the reduced
inverse image of D and D_{high} the boundary of the upper
orbifold pair. The orbifold morphism condition over the reduced divisor
D forces D_{open} leq D_{high} on components, and
the infimum formula then gives
 Delta(g', D_{open}) leq Delta(g', D_{high}) 
on the same resolved fibration g' : Y' to B'. Kodaira dimension is
monotone in the divisor, so
 kappa(B', K_{B'} + Delta(g', D_{open})) leq
kappa(B', K_{B'} + Delta(g', D_{high})) < dim B' 
by pair specialness. The infimum over models in the open-variety
definition is at most this value.

For a smooth quasi-projective curve C = barbar CC - B, specialness
reduces to deg(K_{barbar C}C + B) = 2g(barbar C)C) - 2 + #B leq 0. The
general fibre F simeq ℙ^1 - Q of a has g(barbar F)F) = 0 and
#Q = n, so deg(K_{barbar F}F + Q) = n - 2. For n geq 3 this is
positive, and F is not special.

Specialness on every upper model. Fix an elementary birational orbifold
morphism u : (Y', D') to (Y, D) with (Y', D') smooth projective,
and a neat fibration g : (Y', D') to Z with dim Z > 0. Since
u_* D' = D and D is the reduced sum sum_i D_i, every component of
D' is either a strict transform D_i' of some D_i (with multiplicity
∞) or a u-exceptional curve. Let Σ subset Y be the
finite set of images of exceptional curves; on Y' - u^{-1}(Σ) the
map u restricts to an isomorphism onto Y - Σ. The strict
transform A_i' of A_i is not in the support of D' and carries
multiplicity 1.

Case 1, dim Z = 2. Since the general fibre is connected, g is
birational. Comparing g with the identity fibration on (Y', D'),
 kappa(g, D') leq kappa(Y', K_{Y'} + D'). 
Choose m geq 1 making m D' integral. Pushforward along u
identifies u_*(m(K_{Y'} + D'))|_{Y - Σ} = m(K_Y + D)|_{Y - Σ}.
Restricting a global section s in H^0(Y', m(K_{Y'} + D')) to
Y' - u^{-1}(Σ), pushing forward to Y - Σ, and extending
across the finite set Σ by Hartogs (a section of a line bundle on
a smooth surface over the complement of a finite set extends), gives an
injection
 H^0(Y', m(K_{Y'} + D')) lhookjoinrel→ H^0(Y, m(K_Y + D)). 
The right side grows linearly in m by the identification H^0(Y,
m(K_Y+D)) simeq H^0(ℙ^1, O_{ℙ^1}(m(n-2))).
The left side therefore grows at most linearly, so kappa(Y', K_{Y'} +
D') leq 1. Neatness gives kappa(Z, K_Z + Delta(g, D')) =
kappa(g, D') leq 1 < 2 = dim Z.

Case 2, dim Z = 1. Then Z = C is a smooth projective curve, and it
suffices to rule out deg(K_C + Delta(g, D')) > 0. Choose t in
ℙ^1 avoiding {q_i} and the images under j of the finitely
many exceptional curves of u. Then C_t := (j circ u)^{-1}(t) is a
smooth complete elliptic curve in Y', disjoint from Supp D' and from
the exceptional locus of u.

Suppose h = g|_{C_t} : C_t to C is nonconstant. Then h is finite of
degree d > 0. For each c in C let m_c = m_g(c) be the orbifold
multiplicity. Every component P of g^* c meeting C_t lies outside
Supp D', so m_P(D') = 1 and r(g, P) geq m_c. For x in C_t
above c, the ramification index e_x geq m_c. If m_c = ∞, this
is impossible since e_x is finite; hence every m_c is finite under
nonconstancy. For finite m_c,
 e_x - 1 geq e_x (1 - 1/m_c). 
Summing over c in Supp Delta and applying Riemann-Hurwitz,
 0 = deg K_{C_t} = d deg K_C + sum_{x in C_t} (e_x - 1) geq
d deg(K_C + Delta). 
Since d > 0, this forces deg(K_C + Delta) leq 0, contradicting
positivity.

So g is constant on the fibre of j circ u over every sufficiently
general t, hence on a dense union of complete fibres. Descent on
function fields gives a rational map r : ℙ^1 dashrightarrow
C with g = r circ j circ u on a dense open. Since ℙ^1 is
smooth projective, r extends to a morphism. Connectivity of the general
fibre of g forces deg r = 1, hence C simeq ℙ^1 and g =
j circ u up to isomorphism.

The boundary of this possible curve base is computed on the arbitrary
model Y'. The strict transform A_i' of A_i has coefficient zero in
D'. Since u is an isomorphism at the generic point of A_i', the
multiplicity of A_i' in (j circ u)^* q_i equals the multiplicity of
A_i in j^* q_i, which is one. So
 m_{j circ u}(q_i) leq r(j circ u, A_i'), m_{A_i'}(D') =
1 cdot 1 = 1. 
The coefficient of q_i in Delta(j circ u, D') is 1 - 1/1 = 0, and
for every t notin {q_i} the same computation with the strict
transform of E times {t} gives coefficient zero. Adding boundary on
further exceptional components can only decrease the infimum, which is
already one. Therefore
 Delta(j circ u, D') = 0 
and deg K_{ℙ^1} = -2 < 0, contradicting positivity.

Both possible base dimensions are excluded, so (Y, D) is special in the
pair sense on every upper model.

Units lemma. Every invertible regular function on X is constant.

Proof. A unit v in Gamma(X, O_X)^* has rational extension
to Y whose divisor div_Y(v) = sum_i b_i D_i is supported on
D. Intersecting with A_j and using D_i cdot A_j = delta_{ij},
 0 = div_Y(v) cdot A_j = b_j 
because div_Y(v) is principal and has degree zero on every
complete curve. Hence div_Y(v) = 0, the function v is
nowhere-vanishing on Y, and v is constant on the connected

Fujino's quasi-Albanese universal property identifies a with a_X.
Let f : X to G be an algebraic morphism to a semiabelian variety G
fitting into
 1 to T to G xrightarrow{rho} B to 1, 
T simeq 𝔾_m^r, 
with B abelian. For e in E - {e_1,...,e_n} the fibre
a^{-1}(e) is the punctured line ℙ^1 - {q_1,...,q_n},
contained in the complete curve {e} times ℙ^1 subset Y. So
rho circ f extends to a morphism ℙ^1 to B over this
fibre, and every such morphism is constant: an invariant one-form on B
pulls back to a holomorphic one-form on ℙ^1, the module
Ω^1(ℙ^1) has no nonzero sections, so the differential of
the extension vanishes and constancy follows in characteristic zero.
Hence rho circ f is constant on each fibre a^{-1}(e) with e notin
{e_i}.

Comparing rho circ f with (rho circ f circ s) circ a on the
dense open set X - ∪_i a^{-1}(e_i) gives equality on all of X:
 rho circ f = (rho circ f circ s) circ a. 
The morphism h(x) = f(x) - f(s(a(x))) in G satisfies rho circ h =
0, so h takes values in T. Each coordinate of h : X to
𝔾_m^r is invertible on X and hence constant by the units
lemma. On s(E) the composition s circ a circ s = s, so h
vanishes on s(E) and therefore vanishes identically on X. This gives
the unique factorization
 f = (f circ s) circ a 
of every algebraic morphism f : X to G through a. Uniqueness follows
by precomposing with s.

For the based universal property, take s(0) as base point with 0 in
E the group identity, and suppose f(s(0)) = 0. Then g = f circ s :
E to G is a homomorphism of algebraic groups. Its projection rho
circ g : E to B is a homomorphism by the rigidity theorem for abelian
varieties. The group-law defect
 Phi : E times E to G, 
(e,e') mapsto g(e+e') - g(e) - g(e') 
takes values in T because rho circ Phi = 0, and is constant
because E times E is a connected projective variety and T simeq
𝔾_m^r is affine. Evaluating at (0,0) gives Phi(0,0) =
-g(0) = 0, so Phi equiv 0 and g is a homomorphism. This completes
the identification
 Alb(X) = E,  a_X = a, 
and hence proves part (ii) of Theorem 3.

The ordinary fundamental group. Put U = X - ∪_i A_i^circ. Since
A_i^circ = A_i cap X, the identity X - A_i^circ = X - A_i holds,
and
 U = X - ∪_i A_i = Y - D - ∪_i A_i
= Y - ∪_i b^{-1}(H_i) = E times (ℙ^1 - Q), 
with Q = {q_1,...,q_n}. The last step uses that b restricts to
an isomorphism away from the blowup centres, all of which lie on the
H_i. Therefore
 pi_1(U) = pi_1(E) times pi_1(ℙ^1 - Q)
= ℤ^2 times F_{n-1}, 
with F_{n-1} free of rank n - 1.

Each A_i^circ is a smooth complex submanifold of X of complex
codimension one. The inclusion U ↪ X induces a surjection
on pi_1 whose kernel is the normal closure of the meridians of
A_i^circ: a loop in X can be pushed off A_i^circ, and a
null-homotopy of the pushed-off loop can be chosen transverse to
A_i^circ, meeting it in finitely many points; a small disc around each
such point rewrites the boundary loop as a product of conjugates of
meridians.

The meridian is identified in the blowup chart. Take local coordinates
(x, t) on S near p_i with x = e - e_i and t = q - q_i, and use
the blowup chart b(u, t) = (ut, t), in which A_i is {t = 0} with
coordinate u in ℙ^1, A_i^circ is {t = 0, u in
ℂ}, and the strict transform D_i of H_i meets the chart
only at u = ∞. For u = 0 the disc {u = 0, |t| leq
ε} lies entirely in X, since its only point with t = 0 is
the centre u = 0, t = 0 in A_i^circ; its boundary is the loop t =
ε e^{2pi i theta}. Under b, this loop maps to
 (e, q) = (e_i, q_i + ε e^{2pi i theta}), 
a loop winding once around q_i with e fixed. Its class in pi_1(U)
is therefore the pair (identity in ℤ^2, m_i), where m_i
is the standard puncture loop around q_i in pi_1(ℙ^1 - Q).
Since the loop bounds an actual disc in X, the puncture loop m_i is
trivial in pi_1(X) for every i.

Therefore
 pi_1(X) = (pi_1(U))/(<!< m_1, ..., m_n
>!>) = frac{ℤ^2 times F_{n-1}}{1 times F_{n-1}}
simeq ℤ^2. 
Under the identification U simeq E times (ℙ^1 - Q) via b,
the map a is the projection to E, so a_* : pi_1(X) to pi_1(E)
is the resulting isomorphism; the section s also gives a right inverse.

The general fibre F = ℙ^1 - Q has pi_1(F) = F_{n-1} freely
generated by the m_i, so the map pi_1(F) to pi_1(X) is the zero
map. This proves part (iii) of Theorem 3.

The orbifold base of the composition. For a composition of fibrations
Y xrightarrow{c} barbar XX xrightarrow{barbar j}j J in the orbifold
setting, two divisors have to be distinguished:
 Delta(barbar jj circ c, D)  and 
Delta(barbar j,j, Delta(c, D)). 
Campana proved that on smooth orbifold pairs
 Delta(barbar jj circ c, D) leq Delta(barbar j,j, Delta(c, D)), 
with equality as soon as
c : (Y, D) to (barbar X,X, Delta(c, D)) is an orbifold morphism; a
c-exceptional divisor that dominates a divisor of J enters the direct
infimum but not the iterated one.
./pdf/campana-2011-orbifoldes-geometriques-speciales.pdf

On the specific surface, take c = b : Y to S and barbar jj =
pr_{ℙ^1} : S to ℙ^1. The iterated base is
 Delta(b, D) = sum_i H_i, 
Delta(pr_{ℙ^1}, sum_i H_i) = sum_i q_i. 
Indeed, for each H_i the only Y-divisor mapping onto it is its strict
transform D_i with r(b, D_i) = 1 and m_{D_i}(D) = ∞, so
m_b(H_i) = 1 cdot ∞ = ∞ and H_i enters Delta(b, D) with
coefficient 1 - 1/∞ = 1; and for each q_i the only S-divisor
mapping onto it is H_i with r(pr_{ℙ^1}, H_i) = 1 and
m_{H_i}(sum H_i) = ∞, so q_i enters Delta(pr_{ℙ^1},
sum H_i) with coefficient one.

The direct base is computed on Y directly from j = pr_{ℙ^1}
circ b. The fibre over q_i is D_i + A_i, so
 m_j(q_i) = min(r(j, D_i) m_{D_i}(D), r(j, A_i) m_{A_i}(D))
= min(1 cdot ∞, 1 cdot 1) = 1, 
the second term from the retained exceptional A_i outside the support of
D. Similarly every t notin {q_i} has m_j(t) = 1 from the
unchanged complete elliptic fibre. Hence
 Delta(j, D) = 0. 
The two answers differ at every q_i: the direct boundary is trivial,
whereas the iterated boundary is sum q_i with deg(K_{ℙ^1} +
sum q_i) = n - 2 > 0, hence general type.

The CDHP proof asserts equality Delta(barbar jj circ c, D) = Delta(barbar
j,j, Delta(c, D)) (Claim 7.17). Its justification treats every
widetilde c-exceptional divisor as having infinite boundary

to barbar X'.X'. The preceding construction arranges flatness over J', not
over barbar X';X'; these are different morphisms. In the instantiated case,
widetilde c = b : Y to S is the blowdown, whose fibres are points away
from the centres and curves over the centres, so b is not flat. The
divisors A_i are exceptional for widetilde c = b but not for
nu = id_Y, and carry boundary multiplicity 1, not ∞.
Omitting A_i from the direct infimum is what turns the true direct value
Delta(pr_{ℙ^1} circ b, D) = 0 into the spurious value
sum q_i.

Proposition. If pi_1(V) is virtually solvable for V smooth
quasi-projective, then pi_1(V) is virtually nilpotent.
./pdf/rogov-2025-bns-quasi-projective.pdf

Proof. Rogov proves that for normal quasi-projective V a virtually
solvable quotient of pi_1(V) forces V to admit a fibration over a
quasi-projective hyperbolic orbicurve, and derives as corollary that
pi_1(V) is virtually solvable if and only if it is virtually
nilpotent. Mal'cev's theorem then provides a torsion-free nilpotent
subgroup of finite index, which admits a faithful rational unipotent
representation (Segal, Polycyclic Groups, Cambridge Tracts in
Mathematics 82, 1983, Chapters 1 and 5, Chapter 6 p. 100).

Consequently the class-two bound of Theorem 1 is not accessible from
virtual nilpotence of pi_1(V) alone: virtual solvability must come
from somewhere, and it is not a consequence of specialness.

Theorem 3 shows that virtual nilpotence of pi_1(V) cannot be derived
by induction on the quasi-Albanese fibration. On the surface X the
group pi_1(X) = ℤ^2 is abelian, hence virtually nilpotent;
the general fibre F = ℙ^1 - Q has pi_1(F) = F_{n-1}, the
free group of rank n - 1. The map pi_1(F) to pi_1(X) is trivial
though the source is not nilpotent. This is precisely where control
over the image of pi_1(F) in pi_1(V) and control over pi_1(F)
itself diverge.

The completion theorem addresses the image in every canonical unipotent
quotient of pi_1(V). Control over the kernel of the completion map --
the subgroup of pi_1(V) that no unipotent representation sees --
remains necessary for a proof of virtual nilpotence of pi_1(V)
itself, and is not addressed by the completion theorem. The kernel is
not vacuous: a finitely generated group with trivial abelianization has
trivial rational unipotent completion, since a nonzero nilpotent Lie
algebra has nonzero abelianization. The entire group can therefore be
invisible to rational unipotent completion. Specialness of the total
space does not eliminate this phenomenon.

Comparison with the compact case. Campana asked whether the general
Albanese fibre of a compact special variety is special, and Campana and
Claudon answered affirmatively for smooth projective varieties (their
Theorem 2.4). For quasi-projective varieties with reduced boundary,
Theorem 3 shows that the analogue fails: a smooth special quasi-
projective surface has a general quasi-Albanese fibre that is not
special.
