% Ionization energies for full Coulomb atoms

An atom with $Z$ protons binds on the order of $Z$ electrons. The cost of
removing $m$ of them, while the nucleus is held fixed, is the $m$-th
ionization energy $I_m(Z)$. For large $Z$, but with $m$ small compared
with $Z$, the sharp asymptotic is $I_m(Z)sim a_{TF}m^{7/3}$, where
$a_{TF}$ is a universal Thomas--Fermi coefficient. Theorem~thm:main
below establishes this in the joint limit $mto∞$, $Z/mto∞$.

The ionization energy of an atom measures the cost of removing electrons while
leaving the nucleus fixed. The first electrons removed come from the outer,
dilute part of the cloud; the leading total energy is dominated by the much
denser inner region. Thomas--Fermi theory, introduced by L.~H.~Thomas (Proc.
Cambridge Philos. Soc. 23 (1927), 542--548,
doi:10.1017/S0305004100011683) and E.~Fermi (Rend. Accad. Naz. Lincei
6 (1927), 602--607), describes the leading large-charge total energy.
Lieb and Simon (Phys. Rev. Lett. 31 (1973), 681--683,
doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.31.681; Adv. Math. 23 (1977), 22--116,
doi:10.1016/0001-8708(77)90108-6, Theorem~III.1) connected it to the
many-body Schr"odinger theory. Their energy theorem concerns terms of order
$Z^{7/3}$. Subtracting two such total-energy asymptotics does not control a
much smaller ionization energy: the remainder may exceed the difference being
sought.

The generalized ionization conjecture asserts that Thomas--Fermi theory
nevertheless fixes the leading cost of removing many outer electrons, as long
as their number is small compared with the nuclear charge. J.~P.~Solovej
(Mol. Phys. 114 (2016), 1036--1040,
doi:10.1080/00268976.2015.1130273, Section~3 Equation~(1)) formulated the
statement as an upper and a lower large-$Z$ limit followed by a large-$m$
limit. The energy part of the conjecture is sharpened below to the joint
regime in which both the number removed and the ratio $Z/m$ tend to infinity.

Earlier full-model work covers other ionization regimes. Seco, Sigal and
Solovej (Comm. Math. Phys. 131 (1990), 307--315,
doi:10.1007/BF02161416, Theorem~1 and p.~315) bounded the first ionization
energy of a neutral atom by $CZ^{20/21}$. Ivrii (arXiv:1210.1132v2,
Theorem~6.2.1) obtained successive one-electron ionization energies of positive
atoms that match the Thomas--Fermi chemical potential when $Z^{5/7}=o(Z-N)$
and $Z-N=o(Z)$, where $N$ is the electron count. Both leave open the joint
neutral-to-positive-ion energy asymptotic established in the present work. Uniform excess-charge
bounds were proved in Thomas--Fermi--von Weizs"acker theory by R.~Benguria and
E.~H.~Lieb (J. Phys. B 18 (1985), 1045--1059,
doi:10.1088/0022-3700/18/6/006), and Solovej proved uniform ionization bounds
in reduced (Invent. Math. 104 (1991), 291--311,
doi:10.1007/BF01245077) and unrestricted (Ann. of Math. 158 (2003),
509--576, doi:10.4007/annals.2003.158.509, Theorems~3.8 and~1.5)
Hartree--Fock theory. Those are different variational problems; going to
arbitrary correlated fermionic states needs additional screening estimates,
obtained below through conditional many-body states.

noindentThe theorem and its normalization.quad Work in atomic units.
For a positive integer $Z$ let

 H_{Z,N}
 =sum_{i=1}^N(-tfrac12Delta_i-(Z)/(|x_i|))
   +sum_{1le i<lle N}frac1{|x_i-x_l|}

act on $∧^N L^2(R^3;C^2)$ (two spin states). Write
$E_Z(N)=infsigma(H_{Z,N})$ and $E_Z(0)=0$. For integers $Z>mge1$ define the
$m$-th ionization energy
[
 I_m(Z)=E_Z(Z-m)-E_Z(Z).
]
The Thomas--Fermi kinetic coefficient for this model (two spins, kinetic
$-tfrac12Delta$) is $T=tfrac3{10}(3pi^2)^{2/3}$. The corresponding
functional on a nonnegative density $rho$ is
[
 mathcal T_Z(calT_Z(rho)
 =T!int_{R^3}!rho^{5/3}
 -Z!int_{R^3}!(rho(x))/(|x|),dx
 +frac12iint_{R^3timesR^3}!(rho(x)rho(y))/(|x-y|),dx,dy .
]
Let $E_Z^{TF}(N)$ be its infimum over nonnegative densities of mass $N$ with
displayed terms finite, and set
$I_m^{TF}(Z)=E_Z^{TF}(Z-m)-E_Z^{TF}(Z)$. The constant in the conjecture is
fixed by the classical Thomas--Fermi weak-ionization law of P.~B'enilan and
H.~Brezis (J. Evol. Equ. 3 (2004), 673--770,
doi:10.1007/s00028-003-0117-8, Section~6 Corollary~5):

 lim_{Zto∞}I_m^{TF}(Z)=a_{TF}m^{7/3},qquad m>0 .

The coefficient $a_{TF}$ is recomputed below in the present normalization,
which is what fixes $a_{TF}$ on both sides.

[Generalized ionization energy]
For the full nonrelativistic Coulomb atoms with two spin states,

 frac{I_m(Z)}{m^{7/3}}→ a_{TF}>0
 quadwheneverquad m→∞,
 qquad frac Zm→∞,

with integers $Z>mge1$. In particular,

 lim_{mto∞}frac{limsup_{Zto∞} I_m(Z)}{m^{7/3}}
   &=a_{TF},
 lim_{mto∞}frac{liminf_{Zto∞} I_m(Z)}{m^{7/3}}
   &=a_{TF}.

The inner limits are over integer $Z$ with $m$ fixed. No convergence of
$I_m(Z)$ at a fixed $m$ is asserted or assumed.


The iterated limits (eq:iterated-upper)--(eq:iterated-lower) are
exactly the energy part of the conjecture above; the joint statement
(eq:joint-limit) strengthens the order of limits. The energy is
the exact spectral infimum of (eq:hamiltonian), with no restriction to
Slater determinants. For the same model, ecpre, Uniform excess charge
for Coulomb molecules and the outer radius of neutral atoms (2026), also
proves $0<cle E_Z(Z-1)-E_Z(Z)le C<∞$ uniformly in $Z$. That
fixed first-ionization bound does not assert convergence at fixed $m$ and is
distinct from the large-$m$ limits above.

medskip
noindentA price per electron.quad The proof allows the electron
number to float. Fix $Z$, write $E_n=E_Z(n)$, and introduce the unconstrained
energy and the priced minimum
[
 E=inf_{nge0}E_n,qquad
 P(lambda)=min_{nge0}(E_n+lambda n)quad(lambda>0).
]
The minimum is attained (Lemma~lem:priced-rough). Locality is its point:
replacing the exterior electrons by a trial state need not preserve their
number, because the price charges the change exactly. At the length scale
$s=lambda^{-1/4}$ the removed charge is of order $s^{-3}$. The main
intermediate theorem identifies its coefficient, for every minimizing sector,
including prices at which several sectors tie.

[Deficit at a prescribed price]
There is a universal number $q>0$ such that for every sequence with
[
 lambda=s^{-4},qquad s→0,qquad Zs^3→∞,
]
and every choice of $n$ minimizing $E_n+lambda n$, the scaled deficit
$s^3(Z-n)$ tends to $q$. Moreover $q$ is the exterior Coulomb charge of the
unique classical profile $F$ solving
[
 Delta F=4pi k((F-1)_+)^{3/2}  on R^3∖{0},
 qquad k=(5T/3)^{-3/2},
]
tending uniformly to zero at infinity and bounded above and below by positive
multiples of $|x|^{-4}$ near $0$. The density $k((F-1)_+)^{3/2}$ has bounded
support and $F(x)=q/|x|$ outside it. Finally $a_{TF}=tfrac37q^{-4/3}$.


medskip
noindentAnalytic precedents and proof structure.quad The local
kinetic comparison merges two classical constructions: coherent wave packets
give the upper semiclassical energy (E.~H.~Lieb, Rev. Modern Phys.
53 (1981), 603--641, doi:10.1103/RevModPhys.53.603, Section V.A.1;
also Solovej 2003, Lemma~8.2), and Neumann cube eigenvalues with their
lattice count give the matching lower comparison at the same leading
coefficient (Lieb--Simon 1977, Theorems III.10--III.13). Their remainders are
controlled by local particle counts. This is finer than the coarse
Lieb--Thirring inequality (E.~H.~Lieb and W.~E.~Thirring, Phys. Rev. Lett.
35 (1975), 687--689, doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.35.687), whose universal
positive constant suffices only for preliminary density bounds. The spectral
split follows M.~Rumin (Duke Math. J. 160 (2011), 567--597,
doi:10.1215/00127094-1444305, Theorem~1.4 and Section~3.1).

Screened exterior potentials and outward Thomas--Fermi comparisons are central
to Solovej's Hartree--Fock argument (Ann. Math. 158 (2003),
Sections~6, 10, 12). The conditional states below require a separate transfer
from local quantum energies to conditional fields, while tracking changes in
particle number. The eventual singular profile belongs to the classical
Sommerfeld comparison theory (Lieb--Simon 1977, Section~IV), including the
nonspherical and chemical-potential comparisons of Solovej 2003 (Lemma~4.4,
Remark~4.5, Theorem~4.6, Corollary~4.7). The issue is to extract that profile
from the correlated quantum state and preserve its positive singularity
through conditioning.

The localization, screening, and Thomas--Fermi existence results used below
are drawn from ecpre, Uniform excess charge for Coulomb molecules and
the outer radius of neutral atoms (2026). Two of its consequences matter at
once: the uniform neutral-sector bound $E_Z(Z)-Ele C$ fixes the zero-price
integration constant, and the local-field estimates keep explicit control of
an arbitrary energy offset (FNeutralOffset, FLocalField). The positive-price
conditional comparisons and barrier propagation are proved below. The offset
dependence is essential: a minimizing priced state has energy at most
$E+Cs^{-7}$, so the allowed offset grows as $sto0$, and a fixed-offset
conclusion from the neutral-atom theory cannot by itself yield
Theorem~thm:main.

The argument proceeds in four stages. The price per electron and the rough
size of the deficit are analysed, and the rescaled exterior electron mass is
shown to vanish beyond a fixed radius. Noisy observations then permit a local
conditional comparison at controlled energy cost: a Thomas--Fermi trial state
in a small ball is compared with the priced many-body state, a fresh cut
records the local particles and leaves a core whose conditional screened
potential is harmonic on the ball, and matching this with the observation law
yields simultaneous upper and lower implications between local density and
local potential; two posterior laws occur and must be kept distinct. A
positive subsolution is then built at the innermost scale and propagated
through the observation scales, so the singular inner charge survives the
successive averaging; the error paid for exceptional conditional data has
vanishing expected mass, and the weak inequality is proved on open
neighborhoods of the retained branches, not across an activity interface.
Finally, conditional data are selected, rescaled by $s$, and passed to the
limit, producing a decaying solution of the unit-price Thomas--Fermi equation;
the barrier prevents loss of the singularity, a maximum-principle argument
fixes the Sommerfeld coefficient, and uniqueness forces the same deficit for
every minimizing sector. Only monotonicity of $E_Z(N)$ is then needed to
bracket the fixed particle number between two minimizing sectors (convexity
in $N$ is not used); the matching Thomas--Fermi calculation identifies
$a_{TF}$, and a sequential argument gives the stated order of limits.

Proof.

($0<ale b<∞$). Take points approaching the upper limit, radii $t$, and
dilates $u_t(x)=t^4F(tx)$ satisfying

First, fixed-sector energy differences are recovered from the priced deficit of
the limit analysis. This uses monotonicity of the sector energies, not
convexity, and does not require every sector to minimize at some price. The
priced minimum is concave in the price, and its minimizing sectors bound its
secant slopes even when the minimizer changes. Integrating the deficit law
therefore pins the energy at those sectors; monotonicity brackets the wanted
particle number. The coefficient is then identified by the matching Thomas--Fermi
calculation.

Monotonicity in the electron mass.:

Proof. [Completion of the proof of Theorem~thm:main]
The joint conclusion (eq:joint-limit) follows from
Proposition~prop:fixed-sector-limit and
Proposition~prop:tf-identification. For the iterated limits, suppose first
that along unbounded integers $m_j$ the normalized inner upper limit exceeds
$a_{TF}$ by a fixed $eta>0$. By definition of limsup, for each $j$ one may pick
arbitrarily large integers $Z_j>m_j$, in particular $Z_j/m_jge j$, with
$I_{m_j}(Z_j)/m_j^{7/3}>a_{TF}+eta/2$, contradicting (eq:joint-limit)
(the same selection applies if the inner upper limits are infinite). Likewise a
normalized inner lower limit below $a_{TF}-eta$ along unbounded $m_j$ allows
$Z_j/m_jge j$ with energies below $a_{TF}-eta/2$, again a contradiction. Hence
[
 limsup_{mto∞}frac{limsup_{Zto∞}I_m(Z)}{m^{7/3}}le a_{TF},qquad
 liminf_{mto∞}frac{liminf_{Zto∞}I_m(Z)}{m^{7/3}}ge a_{TF}.
]
Since an inner lower limit never exceeds the inner upper limit, both outer limits
exist and equal $a_{TF}$. The construction always chooses $Z$ only after $m$ is
fixed; no convergence in $Z$ at fixed $m$ is assumed.
