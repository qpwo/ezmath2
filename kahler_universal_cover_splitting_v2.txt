A product X = Y1 x Y2 of complex manifolds has holomorphic tangent bundle
  T_X = pr1^* T_Y1 (+) pr2^* T_Y2,
pr_i being the projection onto the i-th factor. A holomorphic splitting of a compact complex manifold is a fixed decomposition T_X = E1 (+) E2 into holomorphic subbundles of positive rank. A subbundle E of T_X is integrable when the Lie bracket of any two local holomorphic sections of E is again a section of E.

./kobayashi-nomizu-foundations-of-differential-geometry-vol2.pdf

Holomorphic Frobenius gives integrability a concrete local meaning: every point of X has coordinates (z1, z2) in a product of polydiscs D^r1 x D^r2 in which E1 is the z1-direction and E2 the z2-direction. The question is whether those local pieces fit together globally, not whether they exist.

./kobayashi-nomizu-foundations-of-differential-geometry-vol2.pdf

Two obstructions keep an integrable splitting from producing a global product. The holonomy of one foliation along leaves of the other may fail to close; and even when X itself is a product, a given splitting may not come from any product decomposition of X. The second obstruction is why integrability is assumed rather than derived.

Beauville's example makes the second obstruction concrete. Take X = A x P^1 with A a complex abelian surface. On A choose independent translation-invariant vector fields U, V with [U, V] = 0; on P^1 choose vector fields S, T with [S, T] != 0; view both families on the product. Set E1 to be the rank-two span of U + S and V + T, and E2 = T_(P^1). Transversality gives T_X = E1 (+) E2, but
  [U + S, V + T] = [U, V] + [S, T] = [S, T] in T_(P^1)
is not in E1. The splitting exists, X is a product, and yet no product decomposition of X realises E1 and E2 as its coordinate tangent bundles. Integrability of the specific summands is a real constraint.

./beauville-2000-complex-manifolds-with-split-tangent-bundle.pdf

Beauville stated the compatible universal-cover conjecture for compact Kaehler manifolds with integrable partial sums of the tangent summands (Beauville 2000, Section 2.3) and settled the Kaehler-Einstein and compact-Kaehler-surface cases there (Theorems A and C). For two summands the partial-sum hypothesis amounts to simultaneous integrability of E1 and E2. Hoering later proved automatic integrability on non-uniruled projective manifolds (Hoering 2007, Theorem 1.3), which turns the conjecture into a theorem in that class.

./hoering-2007-uniruled-varieties-with-split-tangent-bundle.pdf

Main theorem. Let X be a compact connected Kaehler manifold of complex dimension n >= 2, and T_X = E1 (+) E2 a holomorphic splitting into integrable subbundles of positive ranks r1, r2. Let pi : Xtilde -> X be the ordinary universal covering. There are connected simply connected complex manifolds Y1, Y2 and a biholomorphism Phi : Xtilde -> Y1 x Y2 with
  dPhi(pi^* E_i) = pr_i^* T_Yi, i = 1, 2.
In particular dim_C Yi = ri.

The equation dPhi(pi^* E_i) = pr_i^* T_Yi asks more than existence of some product structure on Xtilde: it asks that the specified subbundles become the two coordinate directions after lifting. The factors are the universal covers of the two foliation leaves through a chosen basepoint and can be noncompact. The conclusion concerns the ordinary universal cover and says nothing about finite covers of X.

Two consequences. On a smooth connected non-uniruled projective complex manifold every holomorphic splitting into positive-rank subbundles is integrable by Hoering's theorem (Hoering 2007, Theorem 1.3), and the main theorem applies. On a smooth connected projective X of dimension >= 2 with K_X nef and big, every holomorphic splitting into positive-rank subbundles is integrable by the second-fundamental-form calculation for the canonical Kaehler-Einstein metric, and the same compatible product follows.

For the analytic part let X be a compact connected Kaehler manifold of complex dimension n >= 2 and T_X = E1 (+) E2 a splitting into integrable subbundles of ranks r1, r2. Write D for the open unit disk.

./kobayashi-nomizu-foundations-of-differential-geometry-vol2.pdf

Product charts. Every point of X has a holomorphic coordinate neighbourhood (D^r1 x D^r2, z = (z1, z2)) in which E_i is tangent to the z_i-direction. Changes between two such charts are locally of the separated form
  z1' = h1(z1), z2' = h2(z2),
with h1, h2 local biholomorphisms.

Proof. Fix a point x. Choose a local holomorphic coordinate projection pi: V -> C^r1 whose differential is nonsingular on E1 near x. Lift the ambient coordinate vector fields to sections of E1. Integrability keeps the brackets of the lifts inside E1, so their projections to C^r1 vanish; the projection is an isomorphism from E1 to the base tangent, so those brackets vanish. The commuting local flows of the lifts give holomorphic coordinates (z1, z2) with E1 = ker(d z2). Repeating for E2 and combining both coordinate systems on a smaller polydisc gives the required pair. A chart change preserves E1 and E2, so its off-diagonal blocks vanish on small product neighbourhoods; invertibility of the chart change then makes the diagonal blocks invertible.

./kobayashi-nomizu-foundations-of-differential-geometry-vol2.pdf

A first plaque is a slice of a product chart with z2 fixed; a second plaque is a slice with z1 fixed. A continuous path is a first (respectively second) plaque path if every point of it has a neighbourhood on which the path lies in a first (respectively second) plaque. Transitions have to be separated only locally; disconnected overlaps need not admit a single formula.

Holomorphic box. Let U be open in C^a x C^b with parameter blocks (s, t). A holomorphic map f: U -> X is a box if at every point of U
  df(T C^a (+) 0) is contained in E1,   df(0 (+) T C^b) is contained in E2.
The conditions ask for tangency only; f need not be an immersion. In a product chart a box reads f(s, t) = (f1(s), f2(t)) locally, and this local separation conversely implies the two tangencies. The scalar case a = b = 1 appears first.

Fix a Kaehler form omega on X with Hermitian metric g(u, v) = omega(u, J v), J denoting the complex structure. Real and holomorphic tangent bundles are identified as complex vector bundles, so multiplication by i on real tangent vectors is J. The projection P_i: T_X -> E_i determined by the splitting is complex-linear. Compactness gives
  sup_{x in X} ||P_i(x)|| < infinity, i = 1, 2,
and no orthogonality of E1 and E2 is assumed.

Hartogs extension for boxes. Let (s, t) be coordinates on C^2 and (w, v) any invertible complex-affine coordinate system. For real numbers 0 < e < a and 0 < c < b, put
  H = {|w| < e, |v| < b} union {|w| < a, c < |v| < b},
  Q = {|w| < a, |v| < b}.
Every holomorphic box f: H -> X with df(partial_s) in E1 and df(partial_t) in E2 extends uniquely to a holomorphic box Q -> X with the same tangencies.

./ivashkovich-2008-extra-extension-properties.pdf

Proof. Step 1: meromorphic extension. Scalar holomorphic functions on H extend to Q: expand a function on the shell {c < |v| < b} in Laurent series in v; the coefficients of the negative powers are holomorphic in w, vanish on {|w| < e}, hence by continuation on {|w| < a}; Cauchy estimates on an intermediate circle give normal convergence on compacta of Q. So Q is the envelope of holomorphy of H. Ivashkovich's meromorphic extension theorem (Ivashkovich 2008, Theorem 2.2; originally Ivashkovich 1992) extends any meromorphic map from a domain in a Stein manifold into a compact Kaehler manifold meromorphically to the envelope of holomorphy. Since H is a domain in C^2 and f is holomorphic, f extends meromorphically to Q.

./ivashkovich-1992-hartogs-type-extension-for-meromorphic-maps.pdf

Step 2: graph. Let Gamma in Q x X be the meromorphic graph with reduced analytic structure and rho: Gamma -> Q the first projection. Gamma is a proper analytic subset of Q x X, so rho is proper. There is a proper analytic A in Q, disjoint from H, with the graph over U = Q minus A being the ordinary graph of a holomorphic map F: U -> X. The ordinary graph is dense in Gamma and U is connected. Applying the identity theorem to P2 dF(partial_s) and P1 dF(partial_t) gives
  P2 dF(partial_s) = 0, P1 dF(partial_t) = 0 on U,
with P_i: T_X -> E_i the projections of the splitting.

Step 3: no positive-dimensional fiber of rho. On every local irreducible surface branch of Gamma both s and t are nonconstant: choose a relatively open part of the branch away from the other branches; that part meets the dense ordinary graph, where rho is a local isomorphism and s, t are coordinate functions of Q. Hence for every s0 the analytic set Gamma cap {s = s0} has local dimension at most one; sym for t.

Suppose a fiber over (s0, t0) had positive dimension. It is contained in Gamma cap {s = s0}, so it has a curve component C. Take a small piece of C inside a product chart z = (z1, z2) of X. Some scalar component h of z1 or z2 is nonconstant on this piece, else the curve piece, contained in {(s0, t0)} x X, would be a point.

Suppose h is a component of z1. The curve piece is a component of the reduced set Gamma cap {s = s0}; its smooth points carry dimension one. Choose a general q where the reduced set is smooth and h nonconstant. Then (s, h) has an isolated zero at q: locally q is the only point of Gamma with (s, h) = (s0, h(q)). Work in ambient coordinates on Q x X near q and choose a closed Euclidean ball Bbar around q small enough that Gamma meets the (s, h)-level of Psi(q) = (s0, h(q)) only at q. The image of Gamma cap (partial B) under Psi is compact and misses Psi(q). Choose q_j on the ordinary graph tending to q. For large j, Psi(q_j) misses that compact image, so
  K_j = Gamma cap Bbar cap Psi^{-1}(Psi(q_j))
is a compact analytic subset of the open ball B. Near q_j, on the ordinary graph, the identity P2 dF(partial_s) = 0 says h is independent of t; varying t with s fixed produces a one-dimensional germ in K_j through q_j. This contradicts the standard fact that a compact analytic subset of an open subset of C^N has no positive-dimensional component (maximize the strictly plurisubharmonic squared coordinate norm on a component and restrict to a curve germ through the maximum).

If h is a component of z2, the same argument uses Psi = (t, h) and varies s. Hence every fiber of rho has dimension zero; properness makes it finite.

Step 4: singleton fibers. Fix p in Q and write its fiber as {(p, x1), ..., (p, xk)}. Pick pairwise disjoint neighbourhoods V1, ..., Vk of the x_i with pairwise disjoint closures. Properness gives a base ball D around p such that every point of rho^{-1}(D) has its target in the union of the V_i: otherwise a sequence of graph points over points tending to p, outside that union, would have a limit in the central fiber, contradicting the choice of V_i. The ordinary locus D minus A is connected, so its image under F lies in a single V_i. Density then places every x_j in the closure of V_i, and the disjoint closures force k = 1.

The proper bijection rho is a homeomorphism; its inverse followed by the second projection defines a continuous Fhat: Q -> X. Near each source point the image lies in a target coordinate chart, and its coordinates are continuous and holomorphic off A. The removable-singularity theorem for locally bounded holomorphic functions across analytic subsets of a complex manifold makes them holomorphic; Fhat is holomorphic. The tangencies extend to all of Q by the identity theorem. Uniqueness follows from the identity theorem on the connected bidisk Q.

Two features of the extension are used by the mixed-boundary argument: no rank condition on the box is needed, and the Hartogs figure may be aligned with any invertible complex-affine change of the box coordinates (s, t).

Small-area lemma. Let M be a compact Hermitian manifold with positive (1,1)-form omegaM, and U a domain in C. If h_j: U -> M are holomorphic with int_U h_j^* omegaM -> 0, then sup_{z in K} ||d h_j(z)|| -> 0 on every compact K in U. Closedness of omegaM is not required.

./brody-1978-compact-manifolds-and-hyperbolicity.pdf

Proof. It suffices to treat a disk D with Dbar in U. Put d(z) = dist(z, partial D) and Mj = max over Dbar of d(z) ||d h_j(z)||. If Mj does not tend to 0, pass to a subsequence with Mj >= epsilon. Let z_j be a maximizer and set a_j = ||d h_j(z_j)||, d_j = d(z_j). For |zeta| < epsilon/4 put k_j(zeta) = h_j(z_j + zeta/a_j). On that disk d(z_j + zeta/a_j) >= d_j/2 and maximality gives ||d k_j(zeta)|| <= 2, ||d k_j(0)|| = 1. After subsequence, k_j(0) converges to a point of M. The derivative bound puts images of a fixed smaller disk in a relatively compact coordinate neighbourhood of that point; Montel's theorem and the Cauchy estimates give a further subsequence converging there with its derivatives. The limit has derivative norm 1 at the origin and therefore positive area on a smaller disk. This contradicts the vanishing areas of k_j, which are bounded by those of h_j and unchanged by the affine reparametrization. Hence Mj -> 0; on each smaller disk d is bounded below, so derivatives tend uniformly to zero. A finite cover proves the assertion on any compact subset.

Mixed-boundary extension. Let B in C^2_(s,t) be convex and f: B -> X a holomorphic box. Suppose p in partial B has a C^2 defining function with nonzero differential. If the complex tangent line to partial B at p is generated by a vector whose components in the s and t directions are both nonzero, then f extends as a holomorphic box to a neighbourhood of p, agreeing with f on its intersection with B.

Proof. Use the fixed omega, its Hermitian norm, and the bound on the projections P_i. Write p = (s*, t*).

Complex-affine coordinates at p. Choose (u, v) with
  (s, t) = (s*, t*) + (a1, a2) u + (b1, b2) v, b1 b2 != 0,
where the v-direction is complex tangent and the positive u-direction points outward from B. Constants may depend on p and these coordinates.

Let rho be a local defining function with B = {rho < 0} near p. Taylor's theorem, using that both real derivatives of rho in the complex tangent direction vanish, gives rho(x, v) <= c x + M(|x|^2 + |v|^2) with c > 0. Choose k > 0 small enough that for all x < 0 close to zero the closed disks
  D_x = {u = x, |v| <= r(x)}, r(x) = k sqrt(-x),
are compactly contained in B.

Let eu = partial_{Re u} and ev = partial_{Re v}. Box tangencies and complex linearity give
  df(eu) = sum_{i = 1, 2} (a_i / b_i) P_i df(ev).
Hence
  ||df(eu)|| <= C ||df(ev)||, C = sum_{i = 1, 2} |a_i / b_i| sup_X ||P_i||.
Holomorphicity makes ||df(ev)|| the speed in every unit real direction of the v-plane, including radial.

Set Omega = f^* omega and A(x) = int_{D_x} Omega. Closedness of Omega and smoothness of D_x in x let Stokes be applied to the moving disks:
  A'(x) = int_0^{2 pi} Omega(eu + r'(x) partial_r, partial_theta) |_{u = x, v = r(x) e^{i theta}} d theta.
On a boundary circle put q = ||df(ev)||. Since partial_theta = r J partial_r,
  Omega(partial_r, partial_theta) = r q^2, |Omega(eu, partial_theta)| <= C r q^2,
hence
  A'(x) <= int_0^{2 pi} r(x)(C + r'(x)) q^2 d theta.
With r'(x) = -k / (2 sqrt(-x)). Choose x0 < 0 close enough to zero that r'(x) <= -C for x0 <= x < 0. Then
  0 <= A(x) <= A(x0) < infinity, x0 <= x < 0.
No product property of the Kaehler metric appears.

Let x_j = -delta_j with delta_j -> 0. Among the scales lambda = 5^l delta_j^{3/4}, integer l >= 0, contained in [delta_j^{3/4}, delta_j^{5/8}], the annuli lambda/2 < |v| < 2 lambda are pairwise disjoint and their number tends to infinity. For large j they lie in D_{x_j}. The area bound gives one of them, at a scale lambda_j, area tending to zero. The scales satisfy
  delta_j / lambda_j <= delta_j^{1/4} -> 0, lambda_j / sqrt(delta_j) <= delta_j^{1/8} -> 0.

Apply the small-area lemma to h_j(zeta) = f(x_j, lambda_j zeta) on 1/2 < |zeta| < 2. Derivatives tend uniformly to zero on a neighbourhood of 3/4 <= |zeta| <= 3/2. This compact annulus has bounded intrinsic Euclidean diameter, so the image of
  u = x_j, 3 lambda_j / 4 < |v| < 3 lambda_j / 2
has diameter tending to zero. A finite cover of X by product charts has a positive Lebesgue number, so for large j the whole image lies in a single product chart z: V -> V1 x V2. Restrict the box to the slice u = x_j in this chart, writing it as (g1(v), g2(v)).

Put w = u - x_j and c_i = a_i / b_i. Choose eta > 0 independent of j with eta max(1, |c1|, |c2|) < 1/8. On
  S_j = {|w| < eta lambda_j, lambda_j < |v| < 5 lambda_j / 4}
define
  F_j(w, v) = z^{-1}(g1(v + c1 w), g2(v + c2 w)).
Both shifted arguments stay in 3 lambda_j / 4 < |v| < 3 lambda_j / 2, so F_j is well defined. The range of z is a product, so combining the two factor coordinates needs no extra target-chart margin. Moreover
  v + c1 w = (s - s* - a1 x_j) / b1, v + c2 w = (t - t* - a2 x_j) / b2,
so F_j is a box in the original (s, t).

F_j agrees with f on an open germ at every point of S_j with w = 0. Continuity puts f into the same chart V on a neighbourhood, where its separated expression is (phi1(s), phi2(t)). Restriction to u = x_j gives the local expressions for g1, g2, and the displayed identities show that F_j reproduces f on a full neighbourhood, not only on the slice.

The closed slice disk {u = x_j, |v| <= 5 lambda_j / 4} lies in B, so there is 0 < e_j < eta lambda_j with f defined on
  C_j = {|w| < e_j, |v| < 5 lambda_j / 4}.
The overlap C_j cap S_j is a connected product of a disk and an annulus. The identity theorem for maps into X extends the open-germ agreement to this overlap, so f and F_j together give a box on the Hartogs figure C_j union S_j.

Hartogs extension gives a box on Q_j = {|w| < eta lambda_j, |v| < 5 lambda_j / 4}. Both Q_j and B are convex in the underlying real-affine space, their intersection is connected and contains C_j, so the extension agrees with f throughout Q_j cap B. Finally p has (w, v) = (delta_j, 0), which belongs to Q_j for large j. This extends the box across p.

Two things matter for the disk-axes argument: no product property of omega is used, and the annulus scale lambda_j sits strictly between delta_j and sqrt(delta_j). The upper inequality lambda_j / sqrt(delta_j) -> 0 keeps lambda_j well inside D_{x_j}; the lower inequality delta_j / lambda_j -> 0 makes the shell wide enough in the u-direction to reach past delta_j. The exponent 3/4 is the geometric mean of the two constraints.

Disk axes. Let alpha, beta: D -> X be holomorphic with d alpha(T D) in E1, d beta(T D) in E2 and alpha(0) = beta(0). There is a unique holomorphic box F: D^2 -> X with
  F(s, 0) = alpha(s), F(0, t) = beta(t)
for all s, t in D.

Proof. Product coordinates at alpha(0) = beta(0) give a unique box germ at (0, 0): first coordinate from alpha, second from beta. Any two boxes extending this germ agree on a connected common domain by the identity theorem.

Fix an integer m >= 1 and let B_m(R) = {(s, t) in C^2 : |s|^{2m} + |t|^{2m} < R^{2m}} for 0 < R <= 1; these domains are convex. Let R* be the supremum of radii to which the germ extends as a box. Nested extensions agree and combine to a box on B_m(R*), with values alpha, beta on the axes by the one-variable identity theorem. Suppose R* < 1.

Boundary points with s t != 0. At such a point of partial B_m(R*) the complex tangent has both components nonzero: the real defining function of the boundary is |s|^{2m} + |t|^{2m} - R^{2m} and its complex s- and t-derivatives are both nonzero when s t != 0. The mixed-boundary extension extends the box across the point.

Boundary points (s*, 0). The complex tangent degenerates and the mixed-boundary extension does not apply. Reconstruct on a small product using one coordinate from alpha and one from a known interior point. Choose a disk U around s* with closure in D and alpha(U) in a single product chart z = (z1, z2). Fix s' in U with |s'| < R*, so F is defined at (s', t) for small t. For epsilon > 0 small enough,
  G(s, t) = z^{-1}(z1(alpha(s)), z2(F(s', t))), (s, t) in U x D_epsilon,
is a holomorphic box: first coordinate from alpha, second from F, product range of z needs no target-chart margin. Near (s', 0), G agrees with F by local separation. Since (U x D_epsilon) cap B_m(R*) is convex, they agree on the whole intersection. This extends across (s*, 0). The points (0, t*) are treated symmetrically.

Gluing. Each boundary extension is defined on some neighbourhood of its point. Restrict each to an ordinary Euclidean ball centred at its boundary point. If two such balls intersect, their intersection contains a point of the segment joining the centres; the segment lies in the closure of B_m(R*) by convexity. Since the intersection is open, it also meets B_m(R*), where the two extensions agree; hence they agree on their whole connected intersection. A finite subcollection covers the compact boundary partial B_m(R*), giving an extension to a neighbourhood of the closure of B_m(R*). This neighbourhood contains B_m(R') for some R* < R' < 1, a contradiction.

Hence the germ extends to B_m(1) for every m. The domains B_m(1) increase with m to D^2, and the resulting boxes agree on connected overlaps; their union is a holomorphic box F: D^2 -> X. Uniqueness follows from the initial uniqueness observation.

Continuous box. A continuous map R: [0, 1]^2 -> X is a continuous box if it is locally separated in product coordinates: every horizontal slice locally follows an E1-plaque and every vertical slice locally follows an E2-plaque. If a whole subrectangle maps into one product chart its first coordinate depends only on the horizontal parameter and its second only on the vertical parameter; the unwanted coordinate is locally constant along connected slices.

Uniqueness. Two continuous boxes on [0, 1]^2 with the same bottom and left edges are equal.

Proof. Choose a Lebesgue number lambda > 0 for a finite product-chart cover of X. Uniform continuity gives a finite rectangular grid such that each closed tile, under either candidate, has diameter < lambda/3. Whenever the two maps agree at a tile's lower-left corner, the union of their tile images has diameter < 2 lambda/3, so both lie in one product chart. In that chart a box is determined by its bottom first coordinate and left second coordinate. Starting with the prescribed edges and proceeding by increasing sum of tile indices proves equality on the whole grid.

Polydisk axes. Let S, T be unit polydisks of positive dimensions. Suppose alpha: S -> X, beta: T -> X are holomorphic with alpha tangent to E1, beta tangent to E2, and alpha(p0) = beta(q0) for some (p0, q0) in S x T. There is a unique holomorphic box F: S x T -> X with
  F(p, q0) = alpha(p), F(p0, q) = beta(q).

Proof. Polydisk automorphisms reduce to p0 = q0 = 0. For fixed (p, q) in S x T the maps sigma -> alpha(sigma p), tau -> beta(tau q) are holomorphic on disks of radius greater than one. After rescaling, disk axes give a holomorphic box; restrict it to the closed real square and call the result R_(p, q): [0, 1]^2 -> X. Set F(p, q) = R_(p, q)(1, 1). Constant-axis cases and uniqueness of continuous boxes give the prescribed axis values; parameter dependence remains.

Fix (p*, q*) and a finite grid on the parameter square such that closed tile images of R_(p*, q*) lie in product charts. Reconstruct the square for nearby (p, q) using these fixed charts, setting tile-by-tile values equal to R_(p, q). Begin with the axes and process tiles from bottom and left. Maintain three properties: (a) each constructed tile depends jointly continuously on its real parameters (sigma, tau) and on (p, q); (b) each is a continuous box in the real parameters; (c) at each fixed (sigma, tau), the tile is holomorphic in (p, q) with p-directions in E1 and q-directions in E2. These hold on the initial axes because alpha and beta are holomorphic.

To fill one tile, write B(sigma; p, q) and L(tau; p, q) for the constructed bottom and left edges. At (p*, q*) their images lie in the chosen product chart z = (z1, z2). Joint continuity and compactness of the edge images put both images in the same chart after shrinking the parameter neighbourhood. Define the new tile by
  z^{-1}(z1(B(sigma; p, q)), z2(L(tau; p, q))).
The edges meet at a common corner; plaque constancy on each edge shows the formula reproduces both. Joint continuity and separation in (sigma, tau) follow. For fixed (sigma, tau) the formula is holomorphic in (p, q), and the induction hypothesis gives
  partial_q (z1 o B) = 0, partial_p (z2 o L) = 0,
so the parameter tangencies carry through. At (p*, q*) the reconstructed tile is the original one, so the induction continues. Only finitely many tiles, hence finitely many shrinkings.

Tiles agree on common edges and form a continuous box across seams: in a product chart around the common image, the unwanted coordinate along a horizontal or vertical slice is constant on each tile piece and agrees at shared endpoints, hence constant across the seam. Uniqueness of continuous boxes identifies the reconstruction with R_(p, q) for every nearby parameter pair; its upper-right value is F(p, q). F is a holomorphic box near (p*, q*) and hence everywhere. Uniqueness follows from the axes determining the box germ at (0, 0) together with the identity theorem.

The construction produces the box from full-polydisk representatives of its edges, not only existence. A transported edge retains its holomorphic parametrisation on an entire polydisk; that property, not a size bound on the edge image, is what permits the finite-grid fill in the composition argument.

Path rectangles. (1) If alpha, beta: [0, 1] -> X are continuous and locally follow E1-, E2-plaques with alpha(0) = beta(0), then there is a continuous box R: [0, 1]^2 -> X with R(s, 0) = alpha(s), R(0, t) = beta(t). (2) Every continuous path gamma: [0, 1] -> X is the diagonal of a continuous box: there is a continuous box R: [0, 1]^2 -> X with R(s, s) = gamma(s).

Proof. (1) Subdivide the horizontal interval into pieces I_i = [u_{i-1}, u_i] and the vertical into J_j = [v_{j-1}, v_j] on which the paths lie in single plaques. Each horizontal piece reads alpha(s) = A_i(a_i(s)) for s in I_i, with a_i: I_i -> D^r1 continuous and A_i: D^r1 -> X holomorphic tangent to E1. Similarly beta(t) = B_j(b_j(t)) for t in J_j, b_j: J_j -> D^r2 continuous and B_j holomorphic tangent to E2; r_i = rank E_i.

Fill tiles I_i x J_j from bottom and left. Inductively the known edges have the forms A(a_i(s)) and B(b_j(t)) with A, B holomorphic on the entire polydisks D^r1, D^r2 and tangent to their bundles, and agreeing at the common corner. Polydisk axes at origins a_i(u_{i-1}) and b_j(v_{j-1}) gives a holomorphic box G: D^r1 x D^r2 -> X. Its evaluation R(s, t) = G(a_i(s), b_j(t)) fills the tile. The opposite horizontal and vertical edges have holomorphic representatives
  a -> G(a, b_j(v_j)), b -> G(a_i(u_i), b)
on the whole original polydisks, so the induction needs no shrinking of edge domains.

(2) Subdivide so each gamma(I_i) lies in a product chart with inverse theta_i: D^r1 x D^r2 -> X. On that interval write gamma(s) = theta_i(a_i(s), b_i(s)). Define the diagonal tile by R(s, t) = theta_i(a_i(s), b_i(t)) for (s, t) in I_i^2. These tiles agree at diagonal vertices and their edges have full-polydisk representatives.

Fill the remaining tiles I_i x I_j in increasing |i - j|. If i < j use the filled bottom and right edges; if i > j use the top and left edges. Polydisk axes works from any corner because its origins are arbitrary. The two input edges have matching corner values: for |i - j| = 1 this is the prescribed diagonal value; for |i - j| > 1 it follows from the intervening tile closer to the diagonal. Tiles filled at the same stage share no edge; shared vertices lie on prescribed input edges. Every tile is consistent and its output edges retain full-polydisk representatives.

In either construction the finite tile collection glues continuously. Box behaviour holds across seams: in a product chart near a seam point, the unwanted coordinate is constant on consecutive slice pieces and agrees at common endpoints; the resulting map is a continuous box.

Proof. The leaf consists of points reachable from o by finite chains of E_i-plaques. Second countability. Take a countable ambient product atlas. Start with a plaque through o. For each reached plaque and each ambient chart the intersection has at most countably many components (open subset of a second-countable plaque), and each component lies in a single plaque of the new chart because the transverse coordinate is locally constant. Adjoin these plaques and repeat. Only countably many plaques appear at finite stages, and every plaque path from o is covered by a finite chain of them. Their countable coordinate bases form a countable basis for the leaf.

The usual covering-space construction applies because a manifold is locally path connected and semilocally simply connected. For second countability of the universal cover, refine the countable atlas to simply connected coordinate disks; there are countably many components of pairwise overlaps; a based loop is described up to homotopy by a finite sequence of these charts and components; the fundamental group is countable; the lifted countable chart basis has only countably many sheets and is a countable basis for the universal cover. Lifting the holomorphic charts gives the complex structure.

Rational boxes. Let M be a smooth projective complex manifold with a holomorphic splitting T_M = E1 (+) E2 into integrable subbundles. Let F_i be the corresponding regular foliations. Definition. Let A, B be locally path connected. A continuous R: A x B -> M is a box if, near every parameter pair, there are a product neighbourhood A' x B' and a product chart on its image with
  z(R(a, b)) = (r1(a), r2(b)).
First-parameter slices locally follow F1 plaques and second-parameter slices locally follow F2 plaques; the slices are continuous into their leaves with the leaf topology. A rational tree is a connected nodal curve with every irreducible component a copy of P^1 and dual graph a finite tree.

For continuous maps on products of rational trees, holomorphic on each product of components, the box condition follows from separate tangencies: in a product chart the wrong-coordinate function is constant on every branch of its parameter slice; continuity makes these constants agree at a node.

Holonomy. A continuous plaque-wise path in an F2 leaf transports first transverse coordinates by composing the first-coordinate changes in a finite chain of product charts, giving a biholomorphic germ between first transversals. Reversing the path gives the inverse germ. The germ is unchanged under a leafwise homotopy with fixed endpoints (subdivide the compact homotopy square into pieces mapping into foliation charts, and cancel transverse changes around their boundaries). The same holds with the two foliations interchanged.

One-tile rule. Suppose two prescribed edges alpha: I -> X, beta: J -> X meet at a corner p, follow the first and second foliations respectively, and each have diameter at most delta. Their union has diameter at most 2 delta; choose a crossing map by the uniform bound and set R(s, t) = c(alpha(s), beta(t)). This is a box and agrees with both prescribed edges: for fixed s, varying the second input along the part of alpha joining p to alpha(s) is constant by d_2 c(E1) = 0, so c(alpha(s), p) = c(alpha(s), alpha(s)) = alpha(s); the other edge follows from d_1 c(E2) = 0. The argument applies to continuous plaque paths because the corresponding functions are locally constant on parameter intervals. The Lipschitz inequalities show every horizontal or vertical partial slice of this tile has diameter at most delta, so the same bound is available for its two new edges.

Boxes exist. (1) For every continuous path lambda: [0, 1] -> X there is a box R: [0, 1]^2 -> X with R(s, s) = lambda(s). (2) For continuous paths alpha, beta in leaves of the first and second foliations with alpha(0) = beta(0) there is a box on [0, 1]^2 with R(s, 0) = alpha(s), R(0, t) = beta(t). Continuity of prescribed leaf paths is in the intrinsic leaf topology.

Proof. (1) Subdivide so each lambda(I_i) has diameter at most delta. On each diagonal tile put R(s, t) = c_i(lambda(s), lambda(t)); diagonal is lambda and slices have diameter at most delta. Tiles agree at common vertices. Fill remaining tiles I_i x I_j in increasing |i - j|: above the diagonal the bottom and right edges are defined; below it, the top and left. In either case they are one horizontal and one vertical edge meeting at a corner. Apply the one-tile rule. The induction invariant: every partial slice of each completed tile has diameter at most delta; the tile rule gives agreement on old edges and preserves the invariant. Tiles at the same stage share no edge; a shared vertex is already fixed by an intervening tile from an earlier stage. The finite filling is consistent.

(2) Subdivide each prescribed axis so every edge segment has diameter at most delta. Fill the rectangular array in increasing order of the sum of the two indices; the bottom and left edges of a new tile are prescribed. The same rule and invariant apply.

In both constructions tile maps glue continuously. The box property holds across seams: near a seam value, continuity places adjacent path pieces in one foliation chart, and both follow the plaque through their common endpoint.

The construction uses only continuity and diameter control. In particular it applies to paths inside a collapsed fiber, whose pseudodiameter is zero, without assigning those paths any finite length for the limiting metric.
