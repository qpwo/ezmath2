Fix a dimension $n \ge 1$ and a ball $B \subset \R^n$. For a nonnegative
$v \in H^1(B)$ put

$$J(v;B) = \int_B \bigl( |\nabla v|^2 + 1_{\{v>0\}} \bigr) dx .$$

A nonnegative $u \in H^1_{loc}(D)$ on an open set $D \subset \R^n$ is a
local minimizer when $J(u;B) \le J(v;B)$ for every ball $B$ compactly
contained in $D$ and every nonnegative $v \in H^1(B)$ with $v-u$ in
$H^1_0(B)$; the competitors are arbitrary Sobolev functions with the
prescribed trace. A global minimizer is a local minimizer on all of
$\R^n$. A function is one-homogeneous when $u(rx) = r u(x)$ for every
$r>0$ and almost every $x$. The nonzero flat solutions are
$u(x) = (x \cdot e)_+$ with $|e| = 1$.

The central object is $d_*$, the smallest dimension in which a nonzero,
nonflat, one-homogeneous global minimizer exists.

The theorem proved below is that $d_* = 7$. Equivalently, every nonzero
one-homogeneous global minimizer in $\R^n$ with $1 \le n \le 6$ is flat,
while a nonflat one-homogeneous global minimizer exists in $\R^7$. The
upper bound $d_* \le 7$ is due to De Silva and Jerison
(D. De Silva, D. Jerison, J. Reine Angew. Math. 635 (2009), 1--21;
doi:10.1515/CRELLE.2009.074). The content of the theorem is the lower
bound $d_* \ge 7$, proved by ruling out one-homogeneous global minimizers
in $\R^5$ and $\R^6$ that are not flat.

A free-boundary point $x_0$ of a local minimizer $u$ is called
regular when some blowup of $u$ at $x_0$, meaning a locally uniform
limit of the rescalings $u(x_0 + r y)/r$ along radii $r \downarrow 0$,
equals a flat solution; it is called singular otherwise. Writing
$\operatorname{Sing}(u)$ for the set of singular points, an immediate
consequence is the following.

For every $n \ge 2$ and every nonnegative local minimizer $u$ in an open
set $D \subset \R^n$, the interior free boundary is smooth outside
$\operatorname{Sing}(u)$; that set is empty when $n \le 6$, locally
finite when $n = 7$, and satisfies $\dim_H \operatorname{Sing}(u) \le n-7$
in dimensions $n \ge 7$, and the bound is sharp, in the sense that for
each $n \ge 7$ a global minimizer attains it.

The variational theory begins with the existence and partial regularity
theorem of Alt and Caffarelli (H. W. Alt, L. A. Caffarelli, J. Reine
Angew. Math. 325 (1981), 105--144; doi:10.1515/crll.1981.325.105), which
introduced the functional above and proved that local minimizers are
locally Lipschitz and harmonic in the positivity phase. Weiss
(G. S. Weiss, J. Geom. Anal. 9 (1999), 317--326;
doi:10.1007/BF02921941) proved the monotonicity formula and the
dimension-reduction principle that pass from a general local minimizer
to the homogeneous global minimizers considered above; the
constant-coefficient case of the same machinery is developed by
Velichkov (B. Velichkov, Regularity of the One-phase Free Boundaries,
Lecture Notes of the Unione Matematica Italiana 28,
Springer 2023; doi:10.1007/978-3-031-13238-4), whose Theorem 1.4 and
Chapter 10 give the version quoted below.

A second strand passes from weak free boundaries to classical ones.
Caffarelli's Harnack-inequality method established $C^{1,\alpha}$
regularity of Lipschitz free boundaries
./caffarelli-1987-rmi-47.pdf

and De Silva's improvement-of-flatness argument extended the theory to
problems with a nonzero right-hand side
./0912.2057-desilva-2011-rhs.pdf

the same route is presented in the regularity chapters of the Velichkov
book already cited. Classical higher regularity goes back to
Kinderlehrer and Nirenberg
./kinderlehrer-nirenberg-1977-numdam.pdf

and, in the form used strictly below, to De Silva, Ferrari and Salsa
./1701.07868-desilva-ferrari-salsa-higher-regularity.pdf

These results make the class of blowups, namely homogeneous global
minimizers, applicable to general local minimizers.

Rigidity of stable homogeneous solutions with smooth cross-section is
known through dimension four. The three-dimensional endpoint is due to
Caffarelli, Jerison and Kenig (L. A. Caffarelli, D. Jerison,
C. E. Kenig, Contemp. Math. 350 (2004), 83--97;
doi:10.1090/conm/350/06339), and Jerison and Savin extended the range to
four dimensions, obtaining $d_* \ge 5$ as their regularity corollary
./1410.7463-jerison-savin-stability-cones.pdf

In the opposite direction De Silva and Jerison produced a singular
minimizing cone in $\R^7$, giving $d_* \le 7$. Firester, Tsiamis and
Wang construct further symmetric one-phase minimizers, including a
second example in dimension seven
./2601.18794.pdf

and prove that cohomogeneity-one cones with $O(n-k) \times O(k)$ symmetry
are unstable for $n \le 6$ and strictly stable for $n \ge 7$
./2601.16966.pdf

these last results settle classes of cones but do not address arbitrary
global minimizers.

Two further results frame the problem. Fernandez-Real and Serra prove
that every global classical stable solution of the one-phase Bernoulli
problem in $\R^4$ is one-dimensional, so that the free boundary, when
nonempty, consists of one or two parallel hyperplanes
./2609.05669.pdf

and Fernandez-Real and Yu prove generic regularity through dimension six
for admissible increasing families of boundary data
./2510.18330.pdf

Both results are about different classes from the one treated below, but
these results confirm that dimension seven is the first dimension in
which candidate obstructions sit.

Simons's second-fundamental-form argument for minimal cones
(J. Simons, Ann. of Math. 88 (1968), 62--105; doi:10.2307/1970556)
provides the geometric model for the stability inequality: a quadratic
excess of the Hessian controls the second variation, and the boundary
term enters with the curvature scalar $H$. The algebraic certificate
below is in the spirit of the sum-of-squares and semidefinite
hierarchies of Parrilo
(P. A. Parrilo, Math. Program. 96 (2003), 293--320;
doi:10.1007/s10107-003-0387-5), but every coefficient is explicit and the
verification is done exactly rather than numerically.

The lower bound $d_* \ge 7$ is proved in four consecutive steps. First, a hypothetical first nonflat minimizer is reduced to a
smooth spherical component $\Omega \subset S^5$ on which the Hessian of
the spherical restriction has nonzero norm; the working link is either
the component itself, with $m_o = 5$, or the suspension of the four-
dimensional link, with $m_o = 4$, in which case two exceptional tips
appear. Second, the second variation of the energy on the cone yields a
spherical stability inequality that compares the boundary curvature term
with the Dirichlet energy. Third, a universal algebraic certificate
provides a positive scalar $h$ and a vector field $Z$ whose divergence
controls the energy of $h$ and whose boundary flux is positive. Fourth,
a pair of smooth cutoffs removes the two bad sets where the normalized
Hessian is undefined, and dominated convergence converts the integrated
inequality into a strict contradiction. The dimension-seven example of
De Silva and Jerison closes the classification.

Conventions. Orthonormal frames are used throughout for all tensor norms
and contractions, and repeated indices are summed. On a manifold
$\Delta = \operatorname{tr} \nabla^2$ is the rough Laplacian. A boundary
normal written $N$ points into the positive phase; the scalar curvature
$H = -T(N,N)$ is not divided by the dimension of the boundary. Inline
mathematics is delimited by single dollar signs, displayed formulas by
double dollar signs.

Reductions. Suppose from now on that $d_* \in \{5,6\}$ and fix a nonzero
nonflat one-homogeneous global minimizer $u$ in $\R^d$.

Step one, regularity and blowups of a minimizer. For each ball $B
\Subset D$ the restriction of $u$ to $B$ minimizes against every
$H^1_0(B)$ competitor difference, and zero extension of differences
gives the same comparison on every bounded open set $O \Subset \R^n$.
Nonnegativity of competitors is not a restriction: if $v-u \in H^1_0(O)$
and $u \ge 0$ then $v_+ - u \in H^1_0(O)$ and the energy of $v_+$ is at
most that of $v$. The constant-coefficient theory of Velichkov
(op. cit., Theorems 3.1, 4.1, 6.2, 9.12) then gives three facts. First,
$u$ has a locally Lipschitz representative, harmonic in $\{u>0\}$.
Second, for every free-boundary point $x_0$, along every sequence
$r_j \downarrow 0$ a subsequence of the rescalings
$u_{x_0, r_j}(y) = u(x_0 + r_j y)/r_j$ converges locally uniformly to a
nonzero nonnegative one-homogeneous global minimizer $U$, with strong
$H^1_{loc}$ convergence and $L^1_{loc}$ convergence of the positivity
indicators. Third, if one such limit equals $(y \cdot e)_+$ with $|e|=1$
then near $x_0$ the free boundary is a smooth hypersurface, $\{u>0\}$ is
one side of it, $u$ is smooth up to the boundary from the positive side,
and $u = 0$, $\nabla u = N_E$ on the boundary, where $N_E$ is the inward
Euclidean unit normal. Higher boundary smoothness follows from De Silva,
Ferrari and Salsa
./1701.07868-desilva-ferrari-salsa-higher-regularity.pdf

as used in the regularity chapter of the Velichkov book already cited.

Step two, translation invariance at a nonzero blowup point. Let $x_0 \ne
0$ be a free-boundary point of a one-homogeneous global minimizer $u$ in
$\R^d$, and let $U$ be any limit of the rescalings $u_{x_0, r_j}$ as
above. Then $U(y + t x_0) = U(y)$ for every $y$ and every $t$. The
identity

$$u_{x_0, r}(y + t x_0) = u_{r'}(y), \qquad r' = r/(1 + rt),$$

together with the Lipschitz bound at $x_0$ and the fact that $u_{x_0,r}$
and $u_{x_0, r'}$ converge to the same limit along the same sequence,
gives the claim. Explicitly, if $L$ bounds the Lipschitz constant of $u$
on a neighborhood of $x_0$ and $|y| \le M$, then

$$|u_{x_0, r'}(y) - u_{x_0, r}(y)| \le 2 L M |t| r,$$

which tends to $0$; passing to the limit proves translation invariance in
the direction $x_0$ at radius zero.

Step three, minimality passes to the translation factor. Suppose
$U(y,s) = v(y)$ is a global minimizer in $\R^k \times \R$ with
$v \ge 0$ in $H^1_{loc}(\R^k)$. Then $v$ is a global minimizer in
$\R^k$. Suppose a ball $B \subset \R^k$ admits a nonnegative
$q \in H^1(B)$ with $q-v \in H^1_0(B)$ and
$J(v;B) - J(q;B) = \delta > 0$. Let $\psi$ be the zero extension of
$q-v$ and, for $L > 1$, choose $\eta_L : \R \to [0,1]$ equal to 1 on
$[-L,L]$, to $0$ outside $[-L-1,L+1]$, and linear in between. Set
$V_L(y,s) = U(y,s) + \eta_L(s) \psi(y)$. This function is nonnegative on
$B \times \R$ because it is the convex combination
$(1-\eta_L) v + \eta_L q$, it agrees with $U$ outside a compact set, and
its difference from $U$ belongs to $H^1_0$ of a sufficiently large ball
$\mathcal B_L \subset \R^{k+1}$. The energy difference on
$B \times [-L,L]$ is $-2 L \delta$, the contribution from the two
transition cylinders is bounded by
$C = 2 \int_B (|\nabla q|^2 + |q-v|^2 + 1)$, and outside the three
cylinders the energy is unchanged. For large $L$ the total is at most
$-2 L \delta + C < 0$, contradicting minimality of $U$.

Step four, one-dimensional classification. In dimension one every
nonnegative one-homogeneous Sobolev function has the form
$u(x) = b_+ x_+ + b_- (-x)_+$ with $b_+, b_- \ge 0$. If both slopes are
positive, a nonnegative smooth bump $\phi$ with $\phi(0) > 0$ supported
in $(-R,R)$ preserves the positivity volume and for small $\tau > 0$
gives

$$J(u+\tau\phi;(-R,R)) - J(u;(-R,R)) = -2\tau(b_+ + b_-)\phi(0) + \tau^2
\int_{-R}^R |\phi'|^2 < 0,$$

so a nonzero minimizing cone has just one positive slope. After
reflection write $u(x) = b x_+$ with $b > 0$. The competitors
$v_t(x) = b R (x-t)_+ / (R-t)$ on $(-R,R)$, $|t| < R$, have the same
trace and energy $b^2 R^2/(R-t) + (R-t)$; its derivative at $t=0$ is
$b^2 - 1$, so minimality forces $b = 1$ and the cone is flat. Thus
$d_* \ge 2$.

Step five, lower and upper classification. Jerison and Savin
./1410.7463-jerison-savin-stability-cones.pdf

proved that stable homogeneous solutions with smooth cross-section are
flat in dimensions at most four, and the regularity corollary of that
paper yields $d_* \ge 5$. De Silva and Jerison (op. cit., Theorem 1.1)
produced a nonflat homogeneous global minimizer in dimension seven,
which yields $d_* \le 7$. Hence $5 \le d_* \le 7$.

Step six, the smooth original link. Every point of the free boundary of
$u$ away from the origin is regular. Set $m_o = d-1$ and
$g_o = u|_{S^{m_o}}$. The set $\{g_o>0\}$ is nonempty and proper, has
finitely many connected components, each with compact smooth closure,
and the closures are pairwise disjoint. On each such component
$\Omega_o \subset S^{m_o}$, the restriction of $g_o$ is smooth up to the
boundary from within $\Omega_o$ and satisfies

$$\Delta_{S^{m_o}} g_o = -m_o g_o \quad \text{in } \Omega_o, \qquad g_o
= 0, \quad \nabla_{S^{m_o}} g_o = N_o \quad \text{on } \partial
\Omega_o,$$

where $N_o$ is the inward unit normal, so in particular $\partial
\Omega_o$ is nonempty.

For the proof, fix a nonzero free-boundary point $x_0$ and choose a
blowup $U$ from step one. By step two, in orthogonal coordinates
$\R^d = x_0^\perp \times \R$ the limit has the form $U(y,s) = v(y)$ for
a locally Lipschitz, nonnegative, nonzero, one-homogeneous factor $v$ in
$\R^{d-1}$, and by step three $v$ is a global minimizer in dimension
$d-1 < d_*$. Hence $v$ is flat, $U$ is flat, and step one makes $x_0$
regular. The spherical equation and boundary conditions come from the
polar Laplacian and the smoothness of $u$ from its positive side
together with the Bernoulli condition on the free boundary. Properness
of $\{g_o>0\}$ follows from integrating the spherical equation over the
whole sphere if $g_o>0$ everywhere, which would force
$m_o \int_{S^{m_o}} g_o = 0$, a contradiction.

For finitely many components: if $g_o>0$ on a set that is neither
connected nor empty, work at a boundary point $x_0$ of one component. In
a small chart the positivity is exactly one side of a smooth graph, so
any two components whose closures met would have to coincide on the
connected positive side, a contradiction. A finite subcover of the
sphere gives finitely many components.

Step seven, the nonflat component. At least one component
$\Omega_o \subset S^{m_o}$ satisfies

$$T_o := \nabla^2_{S^{m_o}} g_o + g_o \Id \not\equiv 0 \quad \text{on }
\Omega_o,$$

where $\Id$ is the spherical metric viewed as a symmetric two-tensor.
Suppose on the contrary that $T_o$ vanished identically on every
positive component. For each corresponding open cone
$D_i = \{r y : r>0, y \in \Omega_{o,i}\}$ the ambient Hessian $D^2 u$
vanishes because $T_o$ is the tangential block, so
$u(x) = b_i \cdot x + c_i$, one-homogeneity forces $c_i = 0$, and
positivity forces $b_i \ne 0$. Thus $D_i \subset \{b_i \cdot x > 0\}$,
and $D_i$ is closed in this half-space: an interior point of the
half-space remains in $D_i$ under a small perturbation. Since the
half-space is connected, $D_i$ equals it, and the Bernoulli condition at
any nonzero boundary point forces $|b_i| = 1$. Two disjoint open
half-spaces through the origin must have opposite unit normals, since
the vector $e + f$ lies in both if neither equals the negative of the
other; but opposite half-spaces have spherical closures meeting along
the equator, contradicting pairwise disjointness of the spherical
closures. Hence exactly one positive component exists and
$u = (x \cdot e)_+$ is flat, contrary to the choice of $u$.

Step eight, suspension preserves global minimality. If $u \ge 0$ is a
global minimizer in $\R^k$ then $\tilde u(x,s) = u(x)$ is a global
minimizer in $\R^{k+1}$. The proof is by slicing. Fix a ball
$\mathcal B = B_R((x_c, s_c))$ and a nonnegative $V \in H^1(\mathcal B)$
with $V - \tilde u \in H^1_0(\mathcal B)$; extend that difference by
zero to $F \in H^1(\R^{k+1})$. For almost every $s$ with
$|s - s_c| < R$ the slice $F(\cdot, s)$ lies in
$H^1_0(B_s)$, $B_s = B_{\sqrt{R^2 - (s-s_c)^2}}(x_c)$, as follows from
approximating $F$ in $H^1(\R^{k+1})$ by $C_c^\infty(\mathcal B)$
functions and applying Fubini. Hence $V_s = V(\cdot, s)$ is an
admissible competitor in $B_s$ for almost every $s$, and

$$J(\tilde u; \mathcal B) = \int_{s_c - R}^{s_c + R} J(u; B_s)\, ds \le
\int_{s_c - R}^{s_c + R} J(V_s; B_s)\, ds \le J(V; \mathcal B).$$

The same computation shows flatness of $\tilde u$ forces $u$ flat, so
the suspension of a nonflat cone is nonflat. When $u$ is a cone in
$\R^5$ the singular set of $\tilde u$ is exactly $\{0\} \times \R$:
the product identity $\partial\{\tilde u > 0\} =
\partial\{u>0\} \times \R$ makes every off-axis point regular, and at an
axis point $(0,s_0)$ every rescaling is
$\tilde u(z_0 + r(x,s))/r = u(rx)/r = \tilde u(x,s)$ itself, which is
nonflat.

Step nine, the working domain. In $S^5$, the suspension of a cone $u$ on
$\R^5 \setminus \{0\}$ restricts to the set of points in product
coordinates $\Phi(t,y) = (\sin t \cdot y, \cos t)$, $0 < t < \pi$,
$y \in S^4$, and

$$\tilde g(\Phi(t,y)) = \sin t \cdot g_o(y) \quad \text{on }
\tilde \Omega = \{\Phi(t,y) : 0 < t < \pi, y \in \Omega_o\},$$

with boundary the smooth side $\Phi((0,\pi) \times \partial \Omega_o)$
together with the two tips $p_+ = (0,1)$ and $p_- = (0,-1)$. In either
case, fixing the nonflat component $\Omega_o \subset S^{m_o}$ with
$m_o \in \{4,5\}$ from step seven, set $\Omega = \Omega_o$,
$g = g_o$, $\Sigma = \emptyset$ when $m_o = 5$, and
$\Omega = \tilde \Omega$, $g = \tilde g$, $\Sigma = \{p_+, p_-\}$ when
$m_o = 4$. Then $\Omega \subset S^5$ is connected, $g$ comes from a
nonnegative one-homogeneous global minimizer in $\R^6$, the closure is
smooth away from $\Sigma$, and the positivity lies on one side of the
regular boundary $\partial_{\mathrm{reg}} \Omega = \partial\Omega
\setminus \Sigma$. Tests supported away from $\Sigma$ are smooth on
$\overline \Omega$. Every compact part of the selected Euclidean cone
away from the origin and away from the axis in the suspended case is
separated from the other positivity components, so local boundary
variations on this component are variations of the global minimizer.

Geometry. The working domain is $\Omega \subset S^5$ from step nine, with
link dimension $m = 5$ (so the formulas below all use $m = 5$), and with
associated spherical function $g = g|_\Omega$. All spherical
derivatives are covariant, $\Delta$ on tensors is the rough Laplacian
$\operatorname{tr} \nabla^2$, and indices are summed over an orthonormal
frame.

Step ten, the Hessian and its boundary behaviour. Define

$$T = \nabla^2 g + g \Id, \qquad p = \nabla g, \qquad
s^2 = 1 - g^2 - |\nabla g|^2, \qquad w = |T|,$$

where $\Id$ is the metric, and set $B = \nabla T$. Then $T$ is symmetric
and trace-free, $B$ is fully symmetric with every trace zero, and
$\operatorname{div} T = 0$. The following identities hold:

$$\nabla_l B_{ijk} - \nabla_i B_{ljk} = \delta_{lj} T_{ik} + \delta_{lk}
T_{ij} - \delta_{ij} T_{lk} - \delta_{ik} T_{lj},$$

$$\Delta T = 5 T, \qquad \Delta w^2 = 10 w^2 + 2 |B|^2,$$

$$\nabla_l p_i = T_{li} - g \delta_{li}, \qquad \nabla_l g = p_l, \qquad
\nabla_l s^2 = 2 T_{li} p_i, \qquad \Delta s^2 = 2 w^2,$$

and on $\{w > 0\}$ one also has
$\nabla_l w = w^{-1} T_{jk} B_{ljk}$ and $|\nabla w| \le |B|$; the
inequality $|\nabla (w^2)|^2 \le 4 w^2 |B|^2$ holds everywhere,
including at $w = 0$.

For the proof: the trace equation $\operatorname{tr} T = \Delta g + 5 g =
0$ is the spherical equation. The Bochner-type identity
$\Delta T = 5 T$ follows from the commutator formula
$([\nabla_l, \nabla_i] V)_{jk} = \delta_{lj} V_{ik} + \delta_{lk} V_{ij}
- \delta_{ij} V_{lk} - \delta_{ik} V_{lj}$ applied to $V = T$ and from
tracing two slots. The same formula with $(l,i,j,k) = (i,j,i,k)$ gives
the Bochner identity for $|B|^2$. Trace-freeness of $B$ follows from
differentiating $\operatorname{tr} T = 0$, and the remaining identities
are direct computations.

Step eleven, the gradient bound on the original component. On the
original link $\Omega_o \subset S^{m_o}$ the function $s_o^2 = 1 - g_o^2
- |\nabla g_o|^2$ satisfies $s_o^2 < 1$. At every boundary point the
scalar curvature $H_o = -T_o(N_o, N_o)$, where $N_o$ is the inward unit
normal, is positive, $T_o p_o = -H_o p_o$, and $w_o > 0$. Consequently
$w_o$ attains a positive minimum on $\partial \Omega_o$ and the zero set
of $w_o$ is a compact subset of $\Omega_o$. The proof uses the boundary
jet identities derived below together with the Dirichlet and Neumann
conditions on $g_o$; positivity of $H_o$ is the strict ellipticity of
the boundary free-boundary chart, and $w_o > 0$ near $\partial \Omega_o$
is stability of the Neumann eigenvalue.

Step twelve, boundary jets. Write $\mathfrak{h}_{\alpha \beta} = T_{\alpha
\beta}$ for tangential components. On the regular side boundary the
following hold:

$$T_{NN} = -H, \qquad T_{N \alpha} = 0, \qquad T_{\alpha \beta} =
\mathfrak{h}_{\alpha \beta}, \qquad \operatorname{tr} \mathfrak{h} = H,$$

$$B_{\alpha \beta \gamma} = 0, \qquad B_{\alpha N N} = \partial_\alpha H,
\qquad B_{N \alpha \beta} = H T_{\alpha \beta} - (T^2)_{\alpha \beta}
= -(\lambda A_{\alpha \beta} - (A^2)_{\alpha \beta}),$$

$$B_{NNN} = w^2, \qquad B_{N \alpha \alpha} \text{ sums to } -w^2
\text{ on contraction,}$$

$$\partial_N w = w^2 (2 \lambda - \operatorname{tr} A^3),$$

where $A = T/w$, $p = N$, $g = 0$, $\lambda = -H/w < 0$, and the
normalization is $|A| = 1$, $\operatorname{tr} A = 0$, $Ap = \lambda p$.
The first two rows are consequences of the boundary conditions; the
$B$ formulas come from differentiating $T$ along the boundary and
using the commutator identity. The scalar curvature enters because
$\operatorname{tr} \mathfrak{h} = \operatorname{tr} T - T_{NN} = H$.

There is a second, equivalent statement in normal coordinates:
writing $S_0 = \lambda A - A^2$ and

$$V_{ijk} = p_i (S_0)_{jk} + p_j (S_0)_{ki} + p_k (S_0)_{ij} +
p_i p_j p_k,$$

the tensors $B/w^2$ and $V$ agree in every component with an odd number
of normal slots, while $V$ has no even-normal components and the
components of $B/w^2$ with an even number of normal slots contract to
zero against any function of $A, p, g$. This identification removes
every undetermined boundary jet from the vector field below.

Step thirteen, Euclidean stability on the cone. Let $D \subset \R^{m+1}$
be the selected positivity component of a minimizing cone, and let
$\phi$ be any real smooth function with compact support in the regular
closure of $D$, away from the origin and from the other singular
points. Then

$$\int_D |\nabla \phi|^2 dx \ge \int_{\partial D} H_E \phi^2 d\sigma_E,
\qquad H_E = -D^2 u(N_E, N_E),$$

where $N_E$ is the inward Euclidean unit normal, and no restriction is
imposed on the boundary trace or the normal derivative of $\phi$.

The proof extends $\phi$ smoothly across the boundary of $D$ with
compact support in a collar separated from the other positivity
components, and extends the positive side of $u$ to a function $F$ on
the collar. Coordinates $(y, \xi)$ obtained by flowing from the original
boundary along $V_F = \nabla F / |\nabla F|^2$, $\xi = F$, give a volume
Jacobian with $j(y, 0) = 1$ and
$\partial_\xi[ (1 + |\nabla F|^2) j ]|_{\xi=0} = 2 H_E$. The perturbed
edge $b_\varepsilon$ determined by $b_\varepsilon + \varepsilon
\phi(y, b_\varepsilon) = 0$ satisfies

$$b_\varepsilon = -\varepsilon \phi_0 + \varepsilon^2 \phi_0 \phi_N +
O(|\varepsilon|^3),$$

with $\phi_0 = \phi(y, 0)$ and $\phi_N = \partial_{N_E} \phi(y, 0)$.
Integrating the layer density $(1 + |\nabla (F + \varepsilon \phi)|^2) j$
from $b_\varepsilon$ to $0$ and adding the fixed-domain variation, the
first-order terms cancel and the second-order contribution is
$\varepsilon^2 ( \int_D |\nabla \phi|^2 - \int_{\partial D} H_E \phi^2
d\sigma_E)$. Minimality of $u$ against $\pm$ the small variation, with
division by $\varepsilon^2$ and $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, gives the
inequality.

Step fourteen, spherical stability. For every real function $v$ smooth
up to the regular boundary of the working component
$\Omega \subset S^5$, supported away from $\Sigma$ when present,

$$\int_{\partial \Omega} H v^2 d\sigma \le \int_\Omega \bigl( |\nabla v|^2
+ 4 v^2 \bigr) dV.$$

The trace of $v$ on the regular boundary is arbitrary. More generally,
for a link component $\Omega \subset S^m$ with angular tests supported
in its regular closure and $D = \{r y : r > 0, y \in \Omega\}$, the
ambient normal of the boundary is tangent to $S^m$ so
$H_E(r y) = r^{-1} H(y)$, the polar volume carries the factor
$r^m dr$ and the side area carries $r^{m-1} dr$. For
$\zeta \in C_c^\infty((0, \infty))$ and an allowed angular test $v$,
the choice $\phi(r y) = \zeta(r) v(y)$ in the Euclidean inequality gives

$$\Big( \int_0^\infty r^{m-2} \zeta^2 dr \Big) \int_{\partial \Omega} H
v^2 d\sigma \le \Big( \int_0^\infty r^{m-2} \zeta^2 dr \Big) \int_\Omega
|\nabla v|^2 dV + \Big( \int_0^\infty r^m |\zeta'|^2 dr \Big) \int_\Omega
v^2 dV.$$

The ratio $\int r^m |\zeta'|^2 / \int r^{m-2} \zeta^2$ tends to the
sharp Hardy constant $(m-1)^2/4$ as the support of $\zeta$ is sent to a
small interval, and for $m = 5$ this value is $4$. Replacing $4$ by any
larger constant and taking supports to shrink gives the stated
inequality; the trace of $v$ on $\partial \Omega$ enters only through
the boundary integral, which is finite by smoothness up to the regular
boundary.

Step fifteen, suspension data. When $m_o = 4$, write $\rho = \sin t$,
$c = \cos t$ and parametrize the regular part of the suspended component
by $(t, y) \mapsto (\rho y, c)$, $0 < t < \pi$, $y \in \Omega_o$. The
metric is $dt^2 + \rho^2 ds_{S^4}^2$ and $\tilde g = \rho g_o$. With
$E_0 = \partial_t$ and $E_\alpha = \rho^{-1} e_\alpha$ for a lifted
orthonormal frame on $S^4$,

$$\tilde p_0 = c g_o, \quad \tilde p_\alpha = (p_o)_\alpha, \quad
\tilde s^2 = s_o^2, \quad \tilde T_{00} = 0, \quad \tilde T_{0 \alpha} =
0, \quad \tilde T_{\alpha \beta} = \rho^{-1} (T_o)_{\alpha \beta},$$

$$\tilde B_{\alpha \beta \gamma} = \rho^{-2} (B_o)_{\alpha \beta
\gamma}, \quad \tilde B_{0 \alpha \beta} = \tilde B_{\alpha 0 \beta} =
\tilde B_{\alpha \beta 0} = -c \rho^{-2} (T_o)_{\alpha \beta},$$

$$\tilde w = \rho^{-1} w_o, \quad |\tilde B|^2 = \rho^{-4} (|B_o|^2 + 3
c^2 w_o^2), \quad \tilde H = \rho^{-1} H_o.$$

The volume and side-boundary measures are $dV = \rho^4 dt dV_o$ and
$d\sigma = \rho^3 dt d\sigma_o$. All these formulas are direct

Certificate. This step states the two algebraic inequalities from which
the geometric comparisons follow. Write $\Sym$ for the symmetric part,
$\STF^r(\R^5)$ for the symmetric trace-free rank-$r$ tensors, and
$D_l$ for the derivation that differentiates a polynomial in $A, p, g,

Step sixteen, universal inequalities. There exist rational tensor
polynomials $f, U_{jk}, K_{lijk}, M_l, P_l$ in $A, p, g, \delta$ such
that:

(a) the polynomials are equivariant under orthogonal changes of
coordinates, with
$f$ invariant;

(b) the coefficients are the ones in the appendix tables, with
denominator $Q = 250000$;

(c) with $C_{lijk} = K_{lijk} - K_{iljk}$ and
$G_{lijk} = \delta_{li} U_{jk} + C_{lijk}$, for every choice of the data
with $\chi = 1 - |p|^2 - g^2 \ge 0$ the interior polynomial satisfies

$$\mathcal I := z^2 [5 U_{jk} A_{jk} + C_{lijk} (\delta_{lj} A_{ik} +
\delta_{lk} A_{ij}) - 4 f] + [D_l G_{lijk} + (2a - 1) q_l G_{lijk}]
X_{ijk} + w [D_l M_l + (2a + 1) q_l M_l] + z [D_l P_l + 2a q_l P_l] +
f |\ell|^2 - (D_l f + 2a q_l f) \ell_l \ge \epsilon_* (z^2 +
|\ell|^2),$$

with $\epsilon_* = 438907/12000000$, $a = 29/50$, and $\delta$
the Kronecker symbol; in particular $f \ge \epsilon_*$ whenever
$g^2 + |p|^2 \le 1$;

(d) for every symmetric trace-free unit matrix $A$ and every vector $p$
with $g = 0$, $|p| = 1$, $Ap = \lambda p$, $\lambda < 0$, defining
$S_0 = \lambda A - A^2$ and

$$V_{ijk} = p_i (S_0)_{jk} + p_j (S_0)_{ki} + p_k (S_0)_{ij} + p_i p_j
p_k,$$

the boundary polynomial satisfies

$$\mathcal S := -\lambda f + U_{jk} (S_0 + p \otimes p)_{jk} +
C_{lijk} p_l V_{ijk} + p_l M_l \ge \delta_*,$$

with $\delta_* = 6984137359/31250000000$;

(e) $U$ and $K$ are even in $p$, and $P$ vanishes identically when
$g = 0$.

The complete verification is finite. All coefficients are integers
divided by $Q$, the comparison form is a sum of squares, and the
remainder is bounded by an explicit symmetric matrix with entries
defined by integer square roots. Steps seventeen to twenty give the

Step seventeen, reduction to normalized data. On
$\Omega_+ = \{w > 0\}$ put

$$A = T/w, \qquad X = B/w, \qquad q_l = X_{ljk} A_{jk}.$$

The identities of step ten give $A \in \STF^2(\R^5)$, $|A| = 1$,
$X \in \STF^3(\R^5)$, and $g^2 + |p|^2 \le 1$, so these are admissible
data for the certificate with the homogenizing variable $z = 1$.

Step eighteen, normalized differentiation. At the data of step
seventeen the derivation $D_l$ evaluated at $z = 1$ equals covariant
differentiation on every coefficient polynomial in $A, p, g, \delta$.
In particular

$$\nabla_l w = w q_l, \qquad \nabla_l A_{ij} = X_{lij} - q_l A_{ij},
\qquad \nabla_l p_i = w A_{li} - g \delta_{li}, \qquad \nabla_l g =
p_l,$$

and the fourth derivatives eliminate:

$$\frac1w G_{lijk} \nabla_l B_{ijk} = 5 U_{jk} A_{jk} + C_{lijk}
(\delta_{lj} A_{ik} + \delta_{lk} A_{ij}).$$

For the first identity, $\nabla_l w = w^{-1} T_{jk} \nabla_l T_{jk} =
w^{-1} T_{jk} B_{ljk} = w q_l$. For the second, differentiate
$T_{ij} = w A_{ij}$. For the third, use $\nabla_l p_i = T_{li} - g
\delta_{li}$. For the fourth, split $G$ into $\delta_{li} U_{jk}$ and
$C_{lijk}$. The $U$ part gives

$$\frac1w \delta_{li} U_{jk} \nabla_l B_{ijk} = \frac1w U_{jk} \Delta
T_{jk} = 5 U_{jk} A_{jk},$$

using $\Delta T = 5 T$. For the skew part, the curvature identity of
step ten with $(l,i)$ gives

$$\frac1w C_{lijk} \nabla_l B_{ijk} = \frac1{2w} C_{lijk} (\nabla_l
B_{ijk} - \nabla_i B_{ljk}) = \frac12 C_{lijk} (\delta_{lj} A_{ik} +
\delta_{lk} A_{ij} - \delta_{ij} A_{lk} - \delta_{ik} A_{lj}) =
C_{lijk} (\delta_{lj} A_{ik} + \delta_{lk} A_{ij}),$$

where the last equality follows by interchanging the summed indices
$l, i$ in the last two terms and using $C_{iljk} = -C_{lijk}$. This
eliminates every undetermined fourth derivative of $g$: if two possible
tensors $\nabla_l B_{ijk}$ had the same contraction with
$\delta_{li} U_{jk}$ and the same skew difference in $l,i$, their
difference would vanish against both $\delta_{li} U_{jk}$ and
$C_{lijk}$, and no constraint on the remaining fourth derivative
entries is required.

Step nineteen, the flux fields. By step sixteen (c), $f(A, p, g) \ge
\epsilon_* > 0$ on the whole normalized coefficient domain. Its
verification uses free algebraic data $X = w = z = 0$; since $f$ is
independent of $w$, this is a legitimate substitution and does not
impose $w = 0$ on geometric data, because the fields below are defined
on $\Omega_+$ where $w > 0$. Define on $\Omega_+$

$$h = w^a \sqrt{f}, \qquad Z_l = w^{2a} (G_{lijk} X_{ijk} + w M_l +
P_l).$$

Both are smooth on $\Omega_+$ and extend smoothly to each regular side
boundary point, because $w > 0$ there by step eleven.

Step twenty, the two comparisons. On $\Omega_+$ the scalar $h$ and the
vector field $Z$ satisfy

$$\operatorname{div} Z \ge 4 h^2 + |\nabla h|^2 + \epsilon_* w^{2a},
\qquad \text{interior};$$

and on the regular side boundary

$$N \cdot Z + H h^2 = w^{2a+1} \mathcal S \ge \delta_* w^{2a+1} > 0,
\qquad \text{boundary}.$$

For the interior inequality, put $\mathcal F_l = D_l f + 2a q_l f$ and
$\ell_* = \mathcal F/(2f)$. Differentiating the square root and
completing the square give

$$\nabla_l h = \frac{w^a}{2 \sqrt{f}} \mathcal F_l, \qquad |\nabla h|^2
= \frac{w^{2a}}{4f} |\mathcal F|^2, \qquad f |\ell|^2 - \mathcal F \cdot
\ell = f |\ell - \ell_*|^2 - \frac{|\mathcal F|^2}{4f}.$$

Substituting the divergence identity of step eighteen into the
interior polynomial $\mathcal I$ at $z = 1$ gives, for every $\ell$,

$$\mathcal I|_{z=1} = w^{-2a} (\operatorname{div} Z - 4 h^2 - |\nabla
h|^2) + f |\ell - \ell_*|^2.$$

The division by $f$ is justified by $f \ge \epsilon_*$. Evaluating (c)
at $\ell = \ell_*$ yields

$$\operatorname{div} Z - 4 h^2 - |\nabla h|^2 \ge \epsilon_* w^{2a} (1 +
|\ell_*|^2),$$

which implies the stated inequality.

For the boundary inequality, use the boundary jets of step twelve. On
the regular side boundary $g = 0$, $p = N$, $|p| = 1$, $Ap = \lambda p$,
$\lambda = -H/w < 0$. Comparing the two sides gives, on tangent indices
$\alpha, \beta$,

$$\frac{B_{N \alpha \beta}}{w^2} = \lambda A_{\alpha \beta} -
(A^2)_{\alpha \beta} = (S_0)_{\alpha \beta}, \qquad \frac{B_{NNN}}{w^2}
= 1.$$

Because $S_0 p = 0$, these are exactly the components of $V$ with one or
three normal slots, including all permutations; the part of $B/w^2$
with an odd number of normal slots equals $V$, $V$ has no even-normal
components, and the even-normal components of $B$ have zero contraction
with $F$. This proves $B/w^2$ agrees with $V$ in every odd-normal
component. Finally $S_0 p = 0$ and $|p| = 1$ give $p_i V_{ijk} =
(S_0)_{jk} + p_j p_k$, so splitting $G$ as $\delta_{li} U_{jk} +
C_{lijk}$ gives

$$N \cdot Z = w^{2a+1} \bigl( U_{jk} (S_0 + p \otimes p)_{jk} +
C_{lijk} p_l V_{ijk} + p_l M_l \bigr), \qquad H h^2 = -\lambda
w^{2a+1} f.$$

The sum is exactly $w^{2a+1} \mathcal S$, and (d) gives the lower
bound. The sign convention: the outward normal used by the divergence
theorem is $-N$, and the boundary term enters with the sign displayed.

Only the boundary value of $P$ vanishes; its derivative term
$D_l P_l$ remains in the divergence identity. A monomial $P_l = g Q_l$
has $D_i P_l = p_i Q_l + g D_i Q_l$ at $z = 1$, so the derivative need
not vanish when $g = 0$.

Step twenty-one, uniform field bounds. There is a finite constant $C_F$,
depending only on the coefficient polynomials, the dimension, $a$, and

$$|h| \le C_F w^a, \qquad |\nabla h| \le C_F w^a (1 + w + |X|), \qquad
|Z| \le C_F w^{2a} (1 + w + |X|).$$

The constant is independent of $w$, $|X|$, and the cutoff parameters
used below.

Step twenty-two, the Gram comparison form. A header
"@ r kind mode" in the appendix Table II begins a new block; consecutive
equal headers still designate different blocks. For a block of
nonnegative rank $r$, write its monomial rows as $s_1, \ldots, s_N$ and
its rectangular integer coefficient matrix as $(c_{tc})$. Define

$$J_c = \frac1Q \sum_{t=1}^N c_{tc} \mathcal M_r(s_t),$$

where $\mathcal M_r(s_t)$ is a monomial of total degree one in
$(w, z, \ell, X)$ and of total degree zero in $(A, p, g)$, decoded from
the integer code as in step twenty-three. The contribution of the block
to $\Gamma$ is

$$\rho \sum_c \langle J_c, \Pi J_c \rangle, \qquad \rho = 1 \text{ if
mode} = \text{I}, \quad \rho = \chi \text{ if mode} = \text{B},$$

with $\Pi$ the orthogonal projection specified by $r$ and the label
$\text{kind}$ (step twenty-four). There are nineteen such interior
blocks with a total of $529$ monomial rows, and each row has degree one
in $(w, z, \ell, X)$; hence $\Gamma$ has total degree two in these
variables.

The two remaining blocks of Table II have rank $-1$ and fourteen rows
each. In these the four digits of a row word are an exponent
$\beta = (\beta_1, \beta_2, \beta_3, \beta_4)$, encoding $x^\beta$ for
$x \in \R^4$, and each block's coefficient matrix has five columns. Set

$$J_c(x) = \frac1Q \sum_t c_{tc} x^{\beta_t}, \qquad \operatorname{Av}
F(x) = \frac1{24} \sum_{\pi \in S_4} F(x_{\pi(1)}, \ldots,
x_{\pi(4)}),$$

and

$$\Lambda(x) = \sum_{\text{rank } -1 \text{ blocks}}
\operatorname{Av} \Bigl( \rho(x) \sum_c J_c(x)^2 \Bigr), \qquad
\rho(x) = 1 \text{ if mode} = \text{I, } x_1 + x_2 + x_3 + x_4 \text{
if mode} = \text{H.}$$

Consequently $\Lambda \ge 0$ whenever $x_1 + \cdots + x_4 \ge 0$; the
scale constraint is $R = \sum_i x_i^2 + (\sum_i x_i)^2 = 1$ from step
thirty.

Step twenty-three, decoding. For nonnegative rank, read a monomial code
in consecutive triples. Its first triple $h\,b\,j$ contributes $g^h
(\operatorname{tr} A^3)^b w^{[j = 1]} z^{[j = 2]}$. Each subsequent
triple $u\,v\,k$ is an unoriented edge carrying $A^k$, with $A^0 = \Id$.
Endpoints $0, \ldots, r-1$ denote the free slots in order, $5$ denotes
a copy of $p$, and $6$ a copy of $\ell$; three occurrences of $7$ are
contracted with one copy of $X$, and three occurrences of $8$ with a
second copy. Multiply all factors and contract all nonfree slots. There
are no additional multiplicity factors. In Table I the first field
specifies the polynomial ($f$, $U$, $M$, $P$, or $K$), the second its
monomial, and the third its coefficient numerator; the free-slot orders
are $U_{jk}$, $K_{lijk}$, $M_l$, $P_l$, and $f$ has rank zero. The
numbers of rows for $(f, U, M, P, K)$ are $(34, 57, 74, 32, 34)$, for a
total of $231$.

Step twenty-four, projection operators. For an arbitrary rank-$r$
tensor $F$ let $F^\pi$ denote slot permutation by $\pi$ and use
$\Sym F = (r!)^{-1} \sum_{\pi \in S_r} F^\pi$. The operator
$\operatorname{Tr}_{ab}$ contracts slots $a, b$ with the Euclidean
metric, leaving remaining slots in their original order. The label
$\text{none}$ means $\Pi = \Id$. The label $\text{anti}$ means
$\Pi F = \sum_{\pi \in S_r} \operatorname{sgn}(\pi) F^\pi$. For
$\text{sym}$, put $F' = \sum_{\pi \in S_r} F^\pi$ and
$v = \operatorname{Tr}_{12} F'$, so

$$(\Pi F)_{ij} = 5 F'_{ij} - \delta_{ij} v \qquad (r = 2),$$

$$(\Pi F)_{ijk} = 7 F'_{ijk} - (\delta_{ij} v_k + \delta_{ik} v_j +
\delta_{jk} v_i) \qquad (r = 3).$$

For $r = 3$ and $\text{hook}$ set
$F' = 3(F + F^{(12)}) - \sum_{\pi \in S_3} F^\pi$,
$v = \operatorname{Tr}_{12} F'$, and

$$(\Pi F)_{ijk} = 8 F'_{ijk} - (2 \delta_{ij} v_k - \delta_{ik} v_j -
\delta_{jk} v_i).$$

All these operators are self-adjoint and positive semidefinite on their
entire tensor spaces, so $\Gamma \ge 0$ whenever $\chi \ge 0$.

For the proof of positive semidefiniteness: each slot permutation is
orthogonal, with adjoint its inverse, so averaging over all permutations
with or without signs gives the orthogonal projection onto symmetric or
alternating tensors, and $\text{anti}$ is $r!$ times an orthogonal
projection. For a symmetric matrix $S$, $S - \delta \operatorname{tr}
S/5$ is its orthogonal trace-free projection, and $F' = 2 \Sym F$
shows the $r = 2$ operator is ten times that projection. For rank three,
with $(\mathcal J v)_{ijk} = \delta_{ij} v_k + \delta_{ik} v_j +
\delta_{jk} v_i$, one has $\operatorname{Tr}_{12} \mathcal J v = 7 v$
and $\langle S, \mathcal J v \rangle = 3 \langle
\operatorname{Tr}_{12} S, v \rangle$ for symmetric $S$, so
$S - \mathcal J(\operatorname{Tr}_{12} S)/7$ is the orthogonal
projection onto symmetric trace-free tensors, and the $r = 3$ operator
is $42$ times that projection. The $\text{hook}$ operator is a
nonnegative combination of $\text{sym}$ and $\text{anti}$.

Step twenty-five, exact collection rules. A graph key records the rank,
the powers of $g, w, z$, a multiset of trace factors
$\operatorname{tr} A^k$, and a multiset of edges with their powers. For
ordering keys, replace endpoint labels $5, 6, 7, 8$ by $-1, -2, -3, -4$,
sort the endpoints of each edge, sort the edge and trace lists, and take
the lexicographically larger edge list obtained by retaining or
exchanging $-3, -4$. A lone cubic tensor is thereby labelled $-3$.
These are ordering conventions only; exchanging two identical copies of
$X$ preserves every graph.

Tensor multiplication takes the disjoint union of graphs, shifts the
free slot labels of the second factor, and distinguishes any two cubic
tensor copies. A contraction of two free slots joins their incident
edges: different edges add their powers, and the same edge becomes a
trace $\operatorname{tr} A^k$. The removed free slots are deleted and
the others renumbered in order. The identities underlying these
operations are exactly

$$\sum_i (A^h)_{ai} (A^k)_{ib} = (A^{h+k})_{ab}, \qquad \sum_i
(A^k)_{ii} = \operatorname{tr} A^k.$$

Trace factors of powers $0, 1, 2$ reduce to $5, 0, 1$. An edge of power
zero connecting two slots of the same $X$ makes the monomial zero.
Every reduction is an equality on the allowed data. Other identities
need not be imposed: retaining two different keys for functions that
happen to agree cannot invalidate an expansion or a later triangle
inequality.

Step twenty-six, closure of the graph calculation. The preceding rules
evaluate every term of $\mathcal I$ and $\Gamma$ as a finite sum of
scalar graphs. In every intermediate graph a free slot has exactly one
incidence, each cubic tensor has exactly three incidences, and there
are at most two cubic tensors. Every resulting scalar graph has degree
two in $(w, z, \ell, X)$.

The incidence assertions hold for each table row by its decoding; a
disjoint union preserves them. A permutation only renames free slots.
Contracting two free slots removes their unique incidences and joins
the remaining ends, or closes a trace, without changing the slot
number of any cubic tensor. The derivative insertion replaces one edge
by two incidences of a new $X$ and supplies its third incidence as a
new free slot; trace differentiation does the same with a self-edge.
The $q_l$ term supplies two incidences on its self-edge and the third at
$l$. Derivatives of $p$ and $g$ supply a free derivative slot without
introducing a cubic tensor. Induction over the operations gives the
incidence statements.

Step twenty-seven, graph bound. Suppose a component of a scalar graph
contains one cubic tensor, or two. Any component with one cubic vertex
contracts to a vector leaf or to a rank-$r$ tensor with the preceding
norm bounds. If a component contains two cubic vertices, the two are
joined by $r \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ edges; every other slot ends at a vector
leaf. Contracting those leaves first leaves the contraction

$$\bigl\langle Y, (A^{k_1} \otimes \cdots \otimes A^{k_r}) Z \bigr\rangle,$$

whose absolute value is at most $|Y| |Z| \prod_{s=1}^r \|A^{k_s}\|_{op}$;
this includes the three parallel edges when $r = 3$, without a
dimension factor. If the cubic vertices have no edge between them, the
two lie in one-vertex components already treated. All remaining
vertices have valence one, so there are no further cases.

Multiplying the estimates over components: each original $\ell$ or $X$
norm appears once per occurrence, ordinary edge matrices contribute
$\theta^{k/2}$, $p$ endpoints contribute $|p|^b$, and the scalar
factors $g^h$, $|p|^b$ are bounded jointly by $g^h |p|^b \le
1$. The total degree is two, so all remaining $w, z, \ell, X$ factors
give exactly $y_i y_j$.

Step twenty-eight, interior remainder and coercive margin. Set

where $\mathcal I$ is the interior polynomial of step sixteen.

Step twenty-nine, coercive conclusion. Entrywise, the exact table gives
$D_0 \le \overline{D}$, where

$$\overline{D} = \frac{1}{10^5} \begin{pmatrix} 2571 & 773 & 2358 & 3087
\\ 773 & 2157 & 926 & 1002 \\ 2358 & 926 & 4845 & 4948 \\ 3087 & 1002 &
4948 & 8037 \end{pmatrix},$$

because each last-column integer above is at most $2 Q^2$ times the

$$\Bigl( d_i - \frac{(\overline{D} \nu)_i}{\nu_i} \Bigr)_{i=1}^4 =
\Bigl( \frac{673}{18400}, \frac{438907}{12000000},

For $y_i \ge 0$ the weighted Young inequalities give

$$2 y_i y_j \le \frac{\nu_j}{\nu_i} y_i^2 + \frac{\nu_i}{\nu_j} y_j^2,
\qquad y^T D_0 y \le y^T \overline{D} y \le \sum_i

Boundary certificate. This step proves item (d) of step sixteen. On the

5} \bigr),$$

where the fixed polynomials are evaluated at the diagonal matrix and
$p = e_5$, $g = 0$. This formula does not assume additional slot
symmetries of $U$ or $K$.

Equivalently, contract the boundary expression as a scalar graph and
then substitute

$$p \cdot A^k p = \Bigl( -\sum_i x_i \Bigr)^k, \qquad
\operatorname{tr} A^k = \sum_i x_i^k + \Bigl( -\sum_i x_i \Bigr)^k.$$

For $k = 0$ the latter is $5$ and the former is $1$; the trace
reductions $\operatorname{tr} A = 0$, $\operatorname{tr} A^2 = 1$ remain
valid on $R = 1$. Orthogonal invariance under permutations of $p^\perp$
shows that $\mathcal S$ is symmetric in $x_1, \ldots, x_4$.

Step thirty-one, the eleven coefficients. Define the boundary remainder

$$E_b = \Lambda + \frac14 - \mathcal S$$

on the constraint set. Expanding the two boundary Gram blocks and the

substitute the diagonal matrix and the normal vector in each of the

have degree at most five. Each boundary $J_c$ has degrees one and two,
so its squared terms have degree at most four, or five after the
$\text{H}$ weight.

Step thirty-two, the boundary conclusion. On $R = 1$ each $|x_i| \le 1$,
so $|m_\beta(x)| \le 1$ and

$$E_b \le \sum_\beta |e^{(b)}_\beta| = \frac{1656725282}{Q^2} =
\frac{1656725282}{62500000000}.$$

Since $\sum_i x_i = -\lambda > 0$, the boundary Gram sum $\Lambda$ of the
rank $-1$ blocks is nonnegative. Hence

$$\mathcal S = \Lambda + \frac14 - E_b \ge \frac14 -
\frac{1656725282}{62500000000} = \frac{6984137359}{31250000000} =
\delta_*.$$

The value $\delta_*$ lies strictly between $0.22$ and $0.23$. The bound
persists at $\lambda = 0$ by continuity, and repeated tangential
eigenvalues cause no singularity in any of the formulas.

Step thirty-three, combining the two certificates. By step twenty-nine,
the interior comparison in (c) of step sixteen holds on the normalized
data; by step thirty-two, the boundary comparison in (d) of step
sixteen holds on the diagonal data. Thus both interior and boundary
comparisons of step twenty hold with the stated constants.

Step thirty-four, the weighted Bochner estimate. Let $\Omega_o$ be a
smooth bounded link and $\mathcal Z_o = \{w_o = 0\}$ its Hessian zero
set. Then $\mathcal Z_o$ is a compact subset of $\Omega_o$ and

$$\int_{\Omega_{o,+}} w_o^{2a - 2} |B_o|^2 dV_o < \infty,$$

where $\Omega_{o,+} = \{w_o > 0\}$. Consequently the function

$$D_o(y) = \begin{cases} w_o(y)^{2a} \bigl( 1 + |B_o(y)|/w_o(y)
\bigr)^2, & y \in \Omega_{o,+}, \\ 0, & y \in \mathcal Z_o,
\end{cases}$$

belongs to $L^1(\Omega_o)$.

For the proof: the boundary conclusion of step eleven gives $w_o > 0$ at
every boundary point, so compactness and continuity give a boundary
neighbourhood where $w_o$ has a positive lower bound, and $\mathcal
Z_o$ is compactly contained in the interior. If $\mathcal Z_o$ is empty,
$w_o$ has a positive minimum on the compact closure, and the
integrability follows from boundedness of $B_o$. Otherwise choose $\eta
\in C_c^\infty(\Omega_o)$ with $0 \le \eta \le 1$, equal to one on an
open neighbourhood of $\mathcal Z_o$. For $0 < \delta \le 1$ put
$W_\delta = w_o^2 + \delta$, which is smooth and strictly positive. The
identities of step ten give

$$\Delta W_\delta = 10 w_o^2 + 2 |B_o|^2 \ge 2 |B_o|^2, \qquad |\nabla
W_\delta|^2 = 4 |\langle T_o, B_o \rangle|^2 \le 4 W_\delta |B_o|^2.$$

Set $I_\delta = \int_{\Omega_o} \eta W_\delta^{a-1} |B_o|^2 dV_o$.
Applying the chain rule to $W_\delta^a$ and integrating twice by parts
against the compactly supported $\eta$ gives

$$2 I_\delta \le \int_{\Omega_o} \eta W_\delta^{a-1} \Delta W_\delta
dV_o = \frac1a \int_{\Omega_o} (\Delta \eta) W_\delta^a dV_o + (1 - a)
\int_{\Omega_o} \eta W_\delta^{a-2} |\nabla W_\delta|^2 dV_o \le \frac1a
\int_{\Omega_o} (\Delta \eta) W_\delta^a dV_o + 4 (1 - a) I_\delta.$$

Since $2 - 4(1 - a) = 4a - 2 = 8/25$, this proves

$$\frac{8}{25} I_\delta \le \frac1a (M_o^2 + 1)^a \|\Delta \eta\|_{L^1(\Omega_o)}.$$

As $\delta \downarrow 0$ the nonnegative integrands restricted to
$\Omega_{o,+}$ increase to $\eta w_o^{2a-2} |B_o|^2$, because $a < 1$;
monotone convergence and the displayed estimate control the integral.
The compact set $\operatorname{supp}(1 - \eta) \subset
\overline{\Omega}_o$ is disjoint from $\mathcal Z_o$, so there
$w_o^{2a-2} |B_o|^2$ is bounded.

Cutoffs and contradiction. This step removes the Hessian zero set
$\mathcal Z_o = \{w_o = 0\}$ and, when $m_o = 4$, the two suspension
tips, and converts the divergence inequality of step twenty plus the
spherical stability inequality of step fourteen into a strict
contradiction.

Step thirty-five, cutoffs. Let $\vartheta \in C^\infty([0, \infty))$
satisfy $0 \le \vartheta \le 1$, $\vartheta = 0$ on $[0,1]$,
$\vartheta = 1$ on $[2, \infty)$. Fix this profile once and set
$L_\vartheta = \|\vartheta'\|_\infty$ and $\kappa = 2
L_\vartheta$. For integers $j \ge 3$ define

$$\alpha_j(y) = \vartheta(j w_o(y)), \qquad \xi_j = \begin{cases} \alpha_j,
& m_o = 5, \\ \alpha_j(y) \beta_j(t), \quad \beta_j(t) =
\vartheta(j \sin t), & m_o = 4. \end{cases}$$

The function $\alpha_j$ is defined to be zero at $\mathcal Z_o$,
consistently with the formula. It is smooth on $\overline{\Omega}_o$: near any zero
of $w_o$ it is identically zero, and all its transitions occur where
$w_o$ is smooth and positive. In the suspension, $\beta_j$ vanishes on
whole neighbourhoods of both tips.

On $\Omega_+ = \{x \in \Omega : w(x) > 0\}$ use the fields of step
nineteen,

$$h = w^a \sqrt{f(A, p, g)}, \qquad Z_l = w^{2a} \bigl( G_{lijk} X_{ijk}
+ w M_l + P_l \bigr).$$

By step twenty-one there is a fixed constant $C_F \ge 1$ such that

$$|h| \le C_F w^a, \qquad |\nabla h| \le C_F w^a (1 + w + |X|), \qquad |Z|
\le C_F w^{2a} (1 + w + |X|),$$

depending only on $a$, the dimension $5$, the finitely many
coefficients defining $f, G, M, P$, and the positive lower bound
$f \ge \epsilon_*$; it is independent of the cone, $w$, $X$, and $j$.

For each fixed $j$, define $v_j = \xi_j h$ and $F_j = \xi_j^2 Z$ on
$\Omega_+$, and extend them by zero where $w = 0$. These are smooth on
the regular closure of $\Omega$ and supported away from the tips when
present. All products below containing one of these cutoff factors or
its derivative are understood to be zero on $w = 0$. The mass $w^{2a}$,
whose exponent is positive, takes its ordinary value zero there and
involves no normalized tensor.

Step thirty-six, the three errors. Integrate the divergence inequality
of step twenty against the test function $v_j^2$ on the regular
closure, apply the divergence theorem with outward normal $-N$, and
bound the boundary term by the spherical stability inequality of step
fourteen. The positive mass term, the energy of $h$, and the boundary
curvature term combine so that the residual integrates to

$$\epsilon_* \int_\Omega \xi_j^2 w^{2a} dV \le \int_\Omega E_{1,j} dV +
\int_\Omega E_{2,j} dV + \int_\Omega E_{3,j} dV,$$

where $E_{1,j}$, $E_{2,j}$, $E_{3,j}$ are the three signed integrands
produced by the two cutoff factors and their gradients; each vanishes
pointwise on $\Omega_+$ for sufficiently large $j$. Every cancellation
in this computation is made before removing the angular cutoff. For
fixed $j$ and radial index $k$, the tests $\zeta_k(r) v_j$ from step
fourteen have compact support on the regular Euclidean cone; first the
variation parameter $\varepsilon \to 0$ on this fixed support, then
$k \to \infty$, then $j \to \infty$ in the displayed inequality.

Step thirty-seven, domination of the errors. Write $b_o = |B_o|/w_o$ on
$\Omega_{o,+}$. First suppose $m_o = 5$, so $\Omega = \Omega_o$ and
$X = B_o/w_o$. Since $1 + w + |X| \le K_n (1 + b_o)$ with $K_n = 1 +
M_o$ and $|\nabla \xi_j| \le \kappa (1 + b_o)$, the field estimates
give

$$|E_{1,j}| + |E_{2,j}| + |E_{3,j}| \le C_F^2 (\kappa^2 + 4 K_n \kappa)
D_o.$$

Indeed the three coefficient bounds are respectively $C_F^2 \kappa^2$,
$2 C_F^2 K_n \kappa$, $2 C_F K_n \kappa$, and $C_F \ge 1$. The
dominator $D_o$ is integrable by step thirty-four. Each error is
eventually zero at every point of $\Omega_{o,+}$ and is defined to be
zero on $\mathcal Z_o$, so dominated convergence makes the right side
of the three-error inequality tend to zero as $j \to \infty$.

Now suppose $m_o = 4$ and use the suspension coordinates $(t, y) \in
(0, \pi) \times \Omega_o$. Write $s_t = \sin t$ and $c_t = \cos t$. The
exact formulas of step fifteen give

$$w(t, y) = \frac{w_o(y)}{s_t}, \qquad |B(t, y)|^2 = \frac{|B_o(y)|^2 +
3 c_t^2 w_o(y)^2}{s_t^4}, \qquad dV = s_t^4 dt dV_o.$$

In particular $\Omega_+ = (0, \pi) \times \Omega_{o,+}$, and there

$$|X| = \frac{\sqrt{b_o^2 + 3 c_t^2}}{s_t} \le \frac{\sqrt{3}}{s_t} (1
+ b_o).$$

The warped metric gives the exact cutoff identity

$$|\nabla \xi_j|^2 = \alpha_j^2 |\beta_j'|^2 + s_t^{-2} \beta_j^2
|\nabla_o \alpha_j|^2.$$

The original cutoff gradients are $|\nabla_o \alpha_j| \le \kappa
|B_o|/w_o$ and $|\beta_j'(t)| \le \kappa/s_t$: wherever
$\vartheta'(j w_o) \ne 0$ one has $1 < j w_o < 2$, hence $j \le 2/w_o$,
and outside the transition region the derivative is zero; the same
argument gives the tip bound. Together with the suspension scaling,

$$|\nabla \xi_j| \le \frac{\kappa}{s_t} (1 + b_o), \qquad 1 + w + |X|
\le \frac{K_s}{s_t} (1 + b_o), \qquad K_s = 1 + M_o + \sqrt{3}.$$

Multiplying the bounds for the three errors by the volume factor
$s_t^4$ in the suspension formula gives

$$s_t^4 \bigl( |E_{1,j}| + |E_{2,j}| + |E_{3,j}| \bigr) \le C_F^2
(\kappa^2 + 4 K_s \kappa) s_t^{2 - 2a} D_o(y).$$

This inequality is read on $\Omega_{o,+}$, and both sides are assigned
zero on $\mathcal Z_o$. Its constant depends only on the fixed
certificate, $a$, $\vartheta$, and $M_o$, and is independent of $j$,
$t$, and $y$. At every fixed point of $\Omega_{o,+}$, $\alpha_j = 1$
and $\nabla_o \alpha_j = 0$ for all sufficiently large $j$, and at
every fixed $t \in (0, \pi)$ the corresponding statements hold for
$\beta_j$.

Step thirty-eight, the contradiction. If the first dimension were $5$
or $6$, the reductions of steps one to nine would provide exactly one
of the two working domains considered above. In either case the right
side of the three-error inequality tends to zero. On the original
component

$$0 < I_o := \int_{\Omega_o} w_o^{2a} dV_o < \infty,$$

finiteness by boundedness and compactness, positivity because $T_o
\not\equiv 0$ and continuity give an open subset of $\Omega_o$ on
which $w_o > 0$. In the nonsuspended case dominated convergence gives

$$\lim_{j \to \infty} \int_\Omega \xi_j^2 w^{2a} dV = I_o;$$

in the suspended case the scaling of step fifteen gives instead

$$\lim_{j \to \infty} \int_\Omega \xi_j^2 w^{2a} dV = I_o \int_0^\pi
(\sin t)^{4 - 2a} dt = I_o \int_0^\pi (\sin t)^{71/25} dt > 0.$$

The dominating mass density is the integrable, everywhere defined
function $(\sin t)^{71/25} w_o^{29/25}$, whose value is zero wherever
$w_o = 0$. These strictly positive limits contradict the three-error
inequality, since $\epsilon_* > 0$. Hence neither $d = 5$ nor $d = 6$
admits a nonzero nonflat one-homogeneous global minimizer, and with
steps four and five this completes the proof that $d_* = 7$.

Consequences. This step records the standard dimension reduction.

Step thirty-nine, regularity of the free boundary. Let $n \ge 2$, let
$D \subset \R^n$ be open, and let $u$ be a nonnegative local minimizer
of the Bernoulli energy in $D$. Its interior free boundary is smooth
outside the singular set $\operatorname{Sing}(u)$; that set is empty
when $n \le 6$, locally finite when $n = 7$, and satisfies

$$\dim_H \operatorname{Sing}(u) \le n - 7 \qquad (n \ge 7).$$

For every $n \ge 7$ a global minimizer attains the bound.

Step forty, proof of the upper bound. By step one, at a singular point
$x_0$ any blowup is a nonflat one-homogeneous global minimizer in
$\R^n$. Theorem one says such a minimizer exists only when $n \ge 7$,
so $n \le 6$ implies $\operatorname{Sing}(u) = \emptyset$, and the free
boundary is smooth. For $n \ge 7$, dimension reduction at a singular
point $x_0$ shows that some sequence of rescalings converges to a
nonflat one-homogeneous global minimizer $U$ that is a product
$U(x) = V(x_1, \ldots, x_7)$ in suitable coordinates, because any
nonflat homogeneous minimizer in $\R^n$ that is not itself a product
has a least coordinate dimension of homogeneity, and that dimension is
at most the threshold $d_* = 7$ by the main theorem. Iterating the
reduction, the singular set near $x_0$ is contained in a stratum of
dimension at most $n - 7$; the standard Hausdorff-dimension argument
over a countable covering of $D$ by balls completes the proof. When
$n = 7$ there are no proper factors, so every singular point has a
nonflat homogeneous blowup in exactly seven dimensions, and the
singular set is locally finite. Both statements are standard
consequences of the blowup compactness of step one and the critical
dimension.

Step forty-one, sharpness. Let $U$ be the nonflat one-homogeneous
global minimizer in $\R^7$ of De Silva and Jerison (op. cit., Theorem
1.1), and set $U_n(x, y) = U(x)$ on $\R^7 \times \R^{n-7}$. Repeated
application of the suspension of step eight shows that $U_n$ is
globally minimizing. At each point $(0, y_0)$ every rescaling of $U_n$
is $U_n$ itself, by homogeneity and translation invariance in $y$. It
cannot be flat, because a half-space normal would have zero
$y$-component, forcing $U$ to be flat. Thus

$$\{0\} \times \R^{n-7} \subset \operatorname{Sing}(U_n),$$

its dimension is $n - 7$, and the upper bound of step thirty-nine gives
equality.

Step forty-two, closing. The main theorem of the opening paragraph now
follows: every nonzero one-homogeneous global minimizer in $\R^n$,
$1 \le n \le 6$, is flat, by steps four and five and the contradiction
of step thirty-eight, while a nonflat one-homogeneous global minimizer
exists in $\R^7$, by the De Silva and Jerison example. Consequently
$d_* = 7$.
