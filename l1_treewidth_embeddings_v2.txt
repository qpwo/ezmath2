
================================================================================
L1 EMBEDDINGS OF GRAPHS OF BOUNDED TREEWIDTH
a complete self-contained proof in plain ASCII
================================================================================

WHAT IS PROVED.

   For every integer k >= 2 there is a constant C_k >= 1 with the
   following property.  Let G = (V,E) be any finite connected simple
   undirected graph with a tree decomposition whose bags have size at
   most k.  Give each edge e of G an arbitrary positive real length
   ell(e).  Let d(u,v) be the resulting shortest-path distance.  Then
   there is a map F from V into a real L1 space such that

        d(u,v)  <=  ||F(u) - F(v)||_1  <=  C_k * d(u,v)
                                                        for all u,v in V.

   The constant C_k depends on k only.  It does NOT depend on the
   number n = |V| of vertices, on the decomposition tree T, on how
   deep T gets, or on the ratios ell(e')/ell(e'') between the shortest
   and longest edge lengths.

The same theorem can be rephrased: the family of shortest-path metrics
of graphs of treewidth at most k-1 embeds into L1 with uniformly bounded
distortion.  Since the excluded-planar-minor theorem of Robertson and
Seymour says "bounded treewidth" = "does not contain a fixed planar
graph as a minor" up to a constant, we also get: any family of graphs
excluding some fixed planar graph as a minor embeds into L1 with
uniformly bounded distortion.  The bound is existence only.  We do not
estimate C_k in terms of k.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY IT MATTERS AND WHY IT IS HARD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT "L1" MEANS.  A natural way to cut a finite set V of n points is
to choose a subset S of V and split V into S and its complement V\S.
That cut contributes the elementary metric

   delta_S(u,v) = |1_S(u) - 1_S(v)|,

where 1_S = 1 on S, 0 outside.  delta_S(u,v) is 1 if the cut separates
u from v (that is, exactly one of u,v lies in S), and 0 otherwise.
Every nonnegative combination

   D(u,v) = sum over S subset of V of  w_S * delta_S(u,v),
   w_S >= 0,

is a finite "cut metric".  It is well known (and we explain it in
Part 0) that a finite metric is a cut metric with distortion bound C if
and only if it can be mapped into real L1 with distortion at most C.
So the theorem can be restated like this:

   Find a nonnegative weighting w_S of cuts of V so that:
     (upper)   for every edge e = uv, the sum
               sum_S w_S * delta_S(u,v)  charges at most
               (some constant U) times ell(e);
     (lower)   for every pair u,v, the sum separates u and v by at
               least (some constant c) times d(u,v).

The upper bound "extends by triangle inequality" from edges to all
pairs: if every edge uv satisfies D(u,v) <= U * ell(uv), then every
shortest path u = x_0, x_1, ..., x_m = v satisfies
D(u,v) <= sum_{i<m} D(x_i, x_{i+1}) <= U * sum_{i<m} ell(x_i x_{i+1})
= U * d(u,v).  So the whole content is:
   UPPER: control D(edge) <= U ell(edge) [easy if cuts are small].
   LOWER: D(u,v) >= c d(u,v) for all pairs [the hard part].

The Linial-London-Rabinovich theorem ties the SAME cut data to the
nonuniform sparsest-cut problem: the worst flow-cut gap on a fixed
graph equals the worst L1 distortion of its weighted shortest-path
metrics (Gupta-Newman-Rabinovich-Sinclair, Section 3, Theorem 3.2).
So a C_k-bounded distortion bound also gives a C_k-bounded flow-cut
gap on these graphs.

WHY BOUNDED TREEWIDTH IS NOT OBVIOUS.  A tempting shortcut: bounded
treewidth means the graph "looks like a tree of small pieces"; maybe
we can sample a dominating tree metric, and trees embed isometrically
into L1.  This fails.  Series-parallel graphs have treewidth 2, yet
some of their shortest-path metrics need expected distortion
Omega(log n) against ANY random dominating tree (Gupta-Newman-
Rabinovich-Sinclair, Theorem 5.6).  So the tree-sampling route cannot
give a uniform bound for treewidth 2, let alone general k.

The right idea is to use the tree decomposition to keep many cut
measures "in flight" simultaneously, in a way that does not lose
information as we cross bags.  Because treewidth is bounded, the
only state needed at any bag is a bounded-size object.  This makes
the construction tractable.

WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN.
  - k = 2 (treewidth 1, i.e. up to treewidth 1) is classical and
    easy; treewidth 2 (series-parallel) is Gupta et al.; optimal
    distortion 2 (Chakrabarti-Jaffe-Lee-Vincent upper, Lee-
    Raghavendra lower).
  - k-outerplanar and other bounded-size pieces: known.
  - Bounded pathwidth: Lee-Sidiropoulos, then O(sqrt(p)) by
    Abraham-Filtser-Gupta-Neiman.
  - Chlamtac-Krauthgamer-Raghavendra (CKR) get bounded L1 distortion
    when the metric's restrictions to (t+3)-vertex subsets admit
    consistent isometric cut representations.  We do not assume such
    consistency; we build it.
  - Filtser et al. prove optimal "padded partitions" for bounded
    treewidth and derive a size-dependent O(sqrt(log(2+t) log n))
    distortion bound.  Our bound does not grow with n.

THE SHAPE OF OUR PROOF (bird's eye).  There are three reusable
ingredients:

   (1) CONSISTENT BAG LAWS  (Part 1, Part 2).  On each bag B_t of
       a fixed tree decomposition assign a finite measure on cuts
       of B_t.  If adjacent bag measures agree on the intersection
       of their bags, they glue canonically by a Markov procedure
       to one global measure of cuts of V, which we call
       "Markov gluing".  This is a measure-theoretic version of
       conditional sampling.  Consistency is what makes the local
       data global.

   (2) TRANSPORT OF UNCERTAINTY  (Part 2).  If one small cut
       measure appears as a summand in EVERY bag along a path of
       bags, and if it is genuinely uncertain at the far end z
       with respect to a testing bag P, then the glued metric
       separates the ends of the path by a definite fraction of
       that uncertainty.  The proportion is a function of k only,
       not the path length.

   (3) PARTICLES AND RESERVATIONS  (Part 3).  Each bag law is carried
       by a fixed-size list of "particles", that is, real functions
       on V.  Sweeping a threshold through a particle's values gives
       a cut measure.  Swapping a pair (F_i, F_j) for (f+g, f-g)
       where f = (F_i+F_j)/2 and g vanishes on the current bag leaves
       the bag's cut measure UNCHANGED but gives the pair a "head
       start" of |g(z)| in distant parts of the graph.  A uniform
       bound shows that any finite number of such swaps still keeps
       every particle uniformly Lipschitz.  A "reservation" freezes a
       pair so its cut measure is unchanged along a stretch of bags.
       At most a bounded number of reservations are held at once.

   Assembling (1) (2) (3) we build TWO cut pseudometrics:

   - D_bag  (Part 4):  has an upper bound D_bag <= U_bag * d and, if
                       u,v share a bag, D_bag(u,v) >= a_bag * d(u,v).
   - D_anc  (Part 5):  has an upper bound D_anc <= U * d and an
                       "anchor" separation for distant vertices.

   Then (Part 6) if D_bag + D_anc failed to be a real lower bound, we
   would get a sequence of normalized counterexamples.  Using a
   compactness device (a nonprincipal ultrafilter), the bounded cache
   of anchors would produce a "youngest reservation crossing from
   bounded distance to vanishing distance"; the noise carried by the
   reservation then forces a younger crossing reservation to exist,
   contradicting "youngest".  So D_bag + D_anc >= lambda_k * d for
   some lambda_k > 0 depending on k only.

   Part 7 assembles the two cut pseudometrics into one cut measure.
   Any finite cut metric embeds isometrically into finite-dimensional
   L1 (revisit the cut representation in Part 0), so the theorem
   follows with C_k = (U_bag + U) / lambda_k.

================================================================================
PART 0.  BACKGROUND: METRICS, L1, AND TREE DECOMPOSITIONS
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0.1  A GRAPH, ITS EDGE LENGTHS, ITS SHORTEST-PATH METRIC
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Let G = (V,E) be a finite, connected, simple, undirected graph.  Simple
means no loops and no two edges connect the same pair.  Connected means
every pair of vertices can be joined by a walk in G.  Give every edge
e in E a positive real length ell(e) > 0.  Nothing else is assumed
about these lengths; they may be wildly different from one another.

For vertices u,v in V, define

     d(u,v) = the smallest total length of a walk in G from u to v.

Because all ell(e) are positive and G is finite and connected, the set
of walk-lengths between u and v is a nonempty set of positive numbers
that descends to some minimum; d(u,v) is that minimum.  Then d is a
metric: d(u,v) = d(v,u), d(u,u) = 0, and the triangle inequality
d(u,v) <= d(u,w) + d(w,v) holds (concatenate a shortest u-to-w path
and a shortest w-to-v path).  Distinct vertices u != v have d(u,v) > 0.
Note: d(u,v) may be much bigger than the length of a single edge on it,
and may be smaller than the straight ratio ell(u,v)/ell(any) suggests;
the metric only "knows" shortest-path sums.

Two quantities appear constantly.

     Delta = max_{u,v in V} d(u,v).     (the diameter)

     d(x,A) = min_{a in A} d(x,a)      for x in V, A subset of V nonempty.

The function x -> d(x,A) is 1-Lipschitz with respect to d:

     |d(x,A) - d(y,A)| <= d(x,y)       for all x,y in V.

That is a one-line triangle inequality (choose a nearest a in A for x;
swap roles).  We will use this often.

We will also use the phrase "1-Lipschitz" in general: a function
g : V -> R is 1-Lipschitz (or Lip(g) <= 1) if |g(x)-g(y)| <= d(x,y)
for every x,y in V.  If we say "g has Lipschitz constant at most L",
that means |g(x)-g(y)| <= L d(x,y).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0.2  L1 SPACES, CUTS, AND THE LAYER-CAKE IDENTITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

For a finite set V, a "cut" of V is a subset S of V.  It serves as a
binary labeling 1_S : V -> {0,1} (1 on S, 0 off S).  The distance
between labeling values is

     delta_S(u,v) = |1_S(u) - 1_S(v)|,   which is 1 if u,v separated by S, else 0.

A "cut combination" is a finite list of cuts S_1,...,S_m with weights
w_1,...,w_m >= 0:

     D(u,v) = sum_{i=1}^m  w_i * delta_{S_i}(u,v).                (0.1)

We claim (0.1) is the same as an embedding into the L1 space R^m.
Indeed, define F : V -> R^m by

     F(v) = ( sqrt(w_1) * 1_{S_1}(v), ..., sqrt(w_m) * 1_{S_m}(v) ).

Then

     ||F(u) - F(v)||_1
       = sum_i | sqrt(w_i) * (1_{S_i}(u) - 1_{S_i}(v)) |
       = sum_i w_i * |1_{S_i}(u) - 1_{S_i}(v)|
       = D(u,v).

So a cut combination is literally an L1 embedding.  Conversely, an
L1 embedding F : V -> R^M decomposes into M one-coordinate functions
F_1,...,F_M, and we can split D_nu(u,v) = sum_j |F_j(u) - F_j(v)|.
Each |F_j(u) - F_j(v)| is a "cut combination" via the LAYER-CAKE
identity:

     |a - b| = int_{-inf}^{inf}  |1_{a > t} - 1_{b > t}|  dt,     (0.2)

which holds for any real a,b (sweep a vertical line across the two
values: it separates them on an interval of length |a-b| exactly).
Apply (0.2) to a = F_j(u), b = F_j(v) and integrate; the left side is
the coordinate's contribution.  Because V is FINITE, the function
F_j takes only finitely many values, so (0.2) is a finite sum over the
finitely many thresholds where the labeling 1_{F_j > t} can change.
Thus the coordinate is a finite cut combination.

There is a more helpful version of this for us.  Define, for any real
function F on V and any "cutoff" M > 0 with F(V) subset of [-M, M],
the SWEEP MEASURE

     sigma_F^M   =   pushforward of Lebesgue measure on [-M, M]
                     under  t -> 1_{F > t}  in Omega_V = {0,1}^V.       (0.3)

That is, a Borel set A of labelings gets mass sigma_F^M(A) = the
Lebesgue measure of the set of thresholds t in [-M,M] for which
1_{F > t} lies in A.  Then by (0.2)

     the cut pseudometric  D( sigma_F^M )(u,v)  is exactly |F(u)-F(v)|

       (as long as [-M, M] contains every value of F, so that the
        Lebesgue run of thresholds pokes through both values).

The total mass of sigma_F^M is 2M.  We often symmetrize a labeled cut
measure nu on Omega_V by

     nu^{sym} = (nu + nu^c) / 2,    where  nu^c is the image
                                     of nu under omega -> 1 - omega.

Symmetrization leaves the cut pseudometric D_nu(u,v) = nu{omega: omega(u) != omega(v)} unchanged (the two sides of "!= " complement to each other).

So the following are equivalent on a finite set V, up to a
multiplicative scalar on the metric:

     (L1')  There exists F : V -> R^M with ||F(u)-F(v)||_1 = D(u,v).
     (CUT') There is a finite labeled cut measure nu on Omega_V with
            D(u,v) = nu{omega : omega(u) != omega(v)}.

Both contain the same information; the L1 coordinates are a
convenient way to store the cut weights.  When we say "we construct a
cut pseudometric" we mean we construct the cut measure nu, and the L1
realization is then immediate from (CUT').

Remark on pseudometrics.  If we only know "D(u,v) >= 0, symmetric,
D(u,u)=0, triangle inequality", but D(u,v) = 0 is permitted for
u != v, D is a PSEUDOmetric.  Any pseudometric with D(u,v)=0 for some
u != v collapses those points to a single point; this is harmless
here (and we do not want to distinguish coinciding vertices, so we
state results as pseudometrics; when put together with a strict lower
bound lambda_k d, they automatically become proper metrics).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0.3  TREE DECOMPOSITIONS AND SEPARATORS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A TREE DECOMPOSITION of a graph G = (V,E) is:
   - a finite tree T (its vertices are called NODES here, to
     distinguish them from the graph's vertices in V);
   - for each node t of T, a "bag" B_t  subset of V.

Two rules.
   (COVER)   Every v in V lies in at least one bag, and for every edge
             e = uv in E there is some bag B_t containing both u and v.
   (RUNS)    For every v in V, the set of nodes t with v in B_t induces
             a CONNECTED subtree of T.

The WIDTH of the decomposition is (max_t |B_t|) - 1.  The TREEWIDTH of
G is the minimum width of one of its tree decompositions.  In this
file, k is an upper bound on BAG SIZE, so treewidth(G) <= k-1.

Two important simplifications valid for the rest of the file.

   (S1) We may assume all bags are nonempty.  [Proof: if B_t = empty,
   delete node t; this cuts T into components.  Each component's union
   of bags has no graph edge to any other component's union (an edge
   uw needs a bag containing u,w; the edge's two endpoints lie in a
   single component), and (since G is connected) all but one component
   contain no vertices of V.  Delete those.  Repeat.]

   (S2) Bags are INDEXED OBJECTS.  Two nodes t,t' of T may have the
   same vertex set B_t = B_{t'}; they are still different bags, and we
   track them separately.  (Used for anchors in Part 5.)

LEMMA 0.1 (Separator facts).  In a tree decomposition of a connected
graph G = (V,E), the following hold.

   (a) If a bag B lies on the tree path from bag A to bag C in T,
       then every path in G from a vertex of A to a vertex of C meets B.

   (b) For adjacent bags A,C of T, the set A cap C separates the
       union of bags on one side of the tree edge from the union on
       the other side.  In particular, adjacent nonempty bags have
       nonempty intersection.

   (c) If bags A_0, A_1, ..., A_r along a tree path, and z is a
       vertex that belongs to A_r, then the sequence

             d(z, A_0), d(z, A_1), ..., d(z, A_r)

       is nonincreasing in the index i.  In fact, if B lies between
       some A and a bag that contains z, we can choose a vertex v in B
       on some shortest z-A path with the equality

             d(z,A) - d(v,A) = d(z,v).                    (0.4)

Proof of (a).  Delete node t_B (the node of T whose bag is B).
The tree T splits into components C_1, ..., C_m.  Every vertex x of
V \ B has its occurrence tree T_x inside one such component, by
(RUNS) and x not in B.  If two graph-adjacent vertices x,y in V \ B
had occurrence trees in DIFFERENT components, the edge xy has to lie
in a bag, so a bag containing both x and y exists; but then T_x and
T_y intersect at the node of that bag, and their connected subtrees
lie in the same component of T - t_B, contradiction.  So all edges
between V \ B stay within a single component.  A path in G avoiding B
therefore starts and ends in the same component; if it goes from a
vertex of A to a vertex of C, they must be on the same side of t_B,
but t_B was strictly interior by hypothesis (a), so A and C are on
opposite sides, contradiction.  Done.

Proof of (b).  Let A and C be adjacent bags; let "left side" be the
union of bags on A's side of the tree edge, similarly "right side" on
C's side.  If a vertex v occurs on both sides, T_v is a connected
subtree crossing the edge, so it contains both nodes (A's node and C's
node), so v in A and v in C, so v in A cap C.  Hence: a vertex lies on
both sides precisely iff it is in A cap C.  If there is an edge u'w'
joining a vertex u' strictly on the left (u' not in A cap C) to w'
strictly on the right (w' not in A cap C), the edge sits in some bag,
which sits on one side or the other; but then both endpoints sit in
that bag and hence on the same side, contradicting "strictly left
endpoint" and "strictly right endpoint".  So A cap C separates the
two sides.  If A cap C were empty, then since A and C are nonempty
and lie on the two sides, they would be disconnected from each other;
in particular no path from A to C in G would exist, contrary to G
connected and A,C subset of V nonempty.  So A cap C is nonempty.

Proof of (c).  Take a shortest path in G from z to A_i, then continue
to A_{i+1}: it starts at z and ends at A_{i+1}, but we can shorten it
by continuing to A_{i+1} via (a): the shortest z to A_i path hits
A_{i+1} (because A_{i+1} is between z's last bag and A_i's bag on the
tree path).  So d(z, A_{i+1}) <= d(z, A_i).  For the equality (0.4):
take any shortest z-to-A path; let v be any vertex of it that lies in
B.  Its initial segment ends at v and has length d(v,A): if there were
a shorter path from v to A, replacing the suffix z-to-v-to-A by
z-to-v-then-shorter-A would shorten z-A, contradiction.  The remaining
segment (v to z) has length d(z,v) (again shortest, otherwise shorten
z-A).  Their sum equals d(z,A) by definition.  Done.  []

A consequence used often.  Suppose A is a bag chosen earlier (that is,
closer to the root of T, once we fix a root) and B is a bag below it,
and p is a vertex of B.  Then

     d(p,A')  DECREASES  as the bag A' moves DOWN toward B along the
     tree path from A to B,

because every such A' sits between A and B, so (c) with z=p, A_r = B
shows d(p, A') is nonincreasing in the sequence.  This "monotonicity
of distance-to-a-fixed-upper-bag" will be used to argue that distance
lists are ordered (Part 5).

0.4  SUMMARY: WHAT PART 0 GIVES US
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

     * Every "cut combination" is an L1 embedding, and conversely every
       finite L1 embedding is a cut combination (on finite V).  Both
       are equivalent to a finite labeled cut measure on Omega_V.
     * Shortest-path distance d is a genuine metric, and d(x,A) is a
       1-Lipschitz function of x.
     * Tree decompositions, bags, and the three Separator facts (a),
       (b), (c).

Everything else in this file is built on these.  We construct cut
measures; the L1 embedding follows immediately.

================================================================================
PART 1.  BAG MEASURES ON A TREE DECOMPOSITION AND MARKOV GLUING
================================================================================

Throughout this part we fix:
   * a finite connected graph G = (V,E) with positive edge lengths,
   * its shortest-path metric d,
   * a tree decomposition (T, {B_t}) with bags of size at most k, all
     bags nonempty (Part 0, (S1)).

The plan is to construct bag cut measures that agree on overlaps, then
glue them into a single cut measure of V.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1  THE INGREDIENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

For a finite set S, write Omega_S = {0,1}^S: all binary labelings of S.
A LABELED CUT MEASURE on S is a finite nonnegative measure nu on
Omega_S.  (Omega_S is finite, so nu assigns a nonnegative number
nu(omega) to every labeling omega and everything is a finite sum.)

Given R subseteq S, define the TRACE nu|_R as the pushforward of nu
under restriction of labelings:

     nu|_R ( b )  =  nu( { omega in Omega_S : omega|_R = b } ),
        b in Omega_R.

So nu|_R remembers only what nu says about labels on R.

The COMPLEMENT of a labeling is omega^c defined by omega^c(v) = 1-omega(v);
N^c is the pushforward by omega -> omega^c.  The SYMMETRIZATION of a
labeled cut measure nu is

     nu^{sym} = (nu + nu^c) / 2.

A measure is SYMMETRIC if nu = nu^c.

The CUT PSEUDOMETRIC of a labeled cut measure nu on S is

     D_nu(u,v) = nu( { omega in Omega_S : omega(u) != omega(v) } ),  u,v in S.

Equivalently, D_nu(u,v) is the total mass of the labelings that put u
and v into different classes.  Symmetrization does not change D_nu:
D_{nu^{sym}}(u,v) = D_nu(u,v), because "omega(u) != omega(v)" is the
same as "omega^c(u) != omega^c(v)" for the complemented labeling.

Now a CONSISTENT FAMILY is a collection mu = (mu_t)_{t in V(T)} of bag
cut measures, one per bag, satisfying:

   (CM1) all mu_t have the SAME total mass m > 0;

   (CM2) for every tree edge ts in T (so t and t' are adjacent nodes
         of T, with bags B_t, B_{t'}), we have

              mu_t | (B_t cap B_{t'})  ==  mu_{t'} | (B_t cap B_{t'}).

        (The common trace on B_t cap B_{t'} will be called mu_{ts}.)

This is the "glue on overlaps" condition.  The next result is the only
place where we actually glue bag measures.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.2  MARKOV GLUING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

LEMMA 1.1 (Markov gluing).  Let mu = (mu_t) be a consistent family of
bag cut measures, with bags of size at most k and every bag nonempty.
Then there exists a LABELED CUT MEASURE rho_mu on Omega_V = {0,1}^V,
of the same total mass m, whose trace on every bag equals the
corresponding bag measure:

     (rho_mu) | B_t   ==   mu_t       for every node t in V(T).

Moreover:

   (MG1) rho_mu is independent of the choice of root of the tree T.

   (MG2) If every mu_t is symmetric, so is rho_mu.

   (MG3) Along any tree path B_{t_0}, ..., B_{t_r}, the successive
         intersection assignments

              (omega restricted to B_{t_i} cap B_{t_{i+1}})  for
              i = 0, ..., r-1

         form a Markov chain under rho_mu/m.

   (MG4) On the finite measure space (Omega_V, rho_mu), the coordinate
         functions F_v(omega) = omega(v) realize the cut pseudometric:

              rho_mu{omega: omega(u) != omega(v)} = D_mu(u,v)
              ||F_v - F_u||_1  =  D_mu(u,v)        (in L1(Omega_V, rho_mu)).

Proof.

Construction.  Root T at any node t_0.  Sample the root labeling
omega|_{B_{t_0}}  from mu_{t_0}/m.  Then process the tree top-down.
When node t has just been given its parent s (so omega on B_s is
known), keep omega on B_s cap B_t (which is known) and sample the
remaining labels on B_t \ B_s from the conditional law

     mu_t( . | omega restricted to B_s cap B_t ),

that is, the conditional law of mu_t given the labels that are already
fixed on S = B_s cap B_t.

Two facts make this work.

Fact (i): after the step at t, every vertex v that was already assigned
before processing t and lies in B_t belongs to S = B_s cap B_t.
Indeed, v was assigned in some earlier bag, so it belongs to that
bag; and it belongs to B_t; by (RUNS), its subtree of T contains the
path from the earlier bag to t, and this path passes through s (the
parent).  So v lies in B_s.  Hence v in B_s cap B_t = S.

Fact (ii): the sampling produced labelings on each bag with marginals
mu_t/m.  Base case: root is sampled from mu_{t_0}/m.  Induction: node
t is sampled conditional on its B_t cap B_s; by consistency, the
partial labeling on S is distributed as mu_s|_S = mu_t|_S.  By the
tower property of conditional expectation, the resulting marginal on
B_t is mu_t/m.  In fact, for any node t, "mu_t on B_t" is the
marginal by a simple calculation: conditioning on S at the second
last step and unconditioning (integrating) gives back mu_t/m on B_t,
because the conditional laws combine to mu_t/m = marginal of
unconditional sampling from mu_t.

Combined, after processing every node of T, we have an assignment of
labels to every vertex that appears in at least one bag, which is all
of V by (COVER).  Rule (COVER) ensures each vertex is in some bag; and
(RUNS) ensures the assignment is consistent (when a vertex occurs in
several bags it receives the same label, because each step keeps the
overlap labels).

This composition of constraint-preserving samplings yields a probability
measure on Omega_V; multiply by m to get rho_mu.  Its marginals on every
bag are mu_t, by induction.  So (rho_mu)|B_t = mu_t.

Independence of root (MG1).  This is a standard consequence of the
"Markov random field on a tree" factorization: the joint density, when
all traces on edges are positive, is

     rho_mu(omega)  =  ( prod_t mu_t(omega|_{B_t}) )
                     / ( prod_{ts in E(T)} mu_{ts}(omega|_{B_t cap B_s}) ),

and every factor is symmetric in the tree; every root gives the same
answer.  If some trace has zero mass, the same formula in the limit
gives zero for this assignment, and the already established marginals
suffice.  Root-independence of the pseudometric D_mu(u,v) is
immediate because D_mu is defined from rho_mu and the formula is
independent of root.

Symmetry (MG2).  Every factor is invariant under global complement;
prod is invariant.  So rho_mu = rho_mu^c.

Markov chain along a path (MG3).  Apply the tree construction rooting
at the first bag B_0 of the path.  When we process the next path bag
B_1, the step depends on the labels already set, which are exactly
those on B_0 cap B_1 plus those coming from side-branches of T.
Fact (i) above with t = B_1 and s = B_0 shows that side branches
contribute only labels on B_0; and inside B_0, the side-branch labels
are exactly those on B_0 (in the marginal).  Conditioning on the labels
of B_0 cap B_1 completely determines the sampling of the next
intersection: integrate out the rest of B_0 and then sample the rest
of B_1.  So successive intersections form a Markov chain.  (Any
side-branch contributions are integrated out because we look only at
the path.)

L1 realization (MG4).  On the probability space (Omega_V, rho_mu/m),
the coordinate functions F_v satisfy

     int |F_v - F_u| d(rho_mu/m)  =  (rho_mu/m) {omega: omega(u) != omega(v)}
                                  =  D_mu(u,v) / m.

After multiplying the measure by m, the same functions give
D_mu(u,v) as their L1 distance in L1(rho_mu).  Finally, rho_mu is a
measure on the finite set Omega_V; we can index it by cuts S subseteq
V, giving the cut representation in Part 0.  []

The canonical construction of Lemma 1.1 is the ONLY gluing procedure
used in this file.  Everything downstream is a bag-local operation;
the gluing is the automatic global closure.

NOTATIONAL CONVENTION.  We will often write D_mu for the glued cut
pseudometric, and treat it as attached to the family mu.  For a
specific labeled cut measure nu on V, the notation D_nu(u,v) already
meant "probability omega(u) != omega(v)".  This matches the glued
pseudometric when nu = rho_mu.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.3  TWO IMMEDIATE CONSEQUENCES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Consequence A (edge extension).  Suppose every edge e = uv has its
endpoints in some bag B_t and satisfies the bag-local bound

     D_{mu_t}(u,v)  <=  U * ell(uv)         for every edge uv.         (1.1)

Then the global glued pseudometric satisfies a path-sum bound:

     D_mu(u,v)  <=  U * d(u,v)               for every u,v in V.       (1.2)

Proof.  For the edge uv, its bag marginal is mu_t, so
D_mu(u,v) = D_{mu_t}(u,v) <= U ell(uv).  Sum along a shortest path
using the triangle inequality.  []

Consequence B (averaging over auxiliary randomness).  Suppose the
family mu depends measurably on an auxiliary random variable X taking
values in some probability space, and that for almost every realization
X = x the total mass of mu(x) equals a FIXED integer m (so is
integrable).  Define

     rho*  =  E_X [ rho_{mu(X)} ]      (as a finite measure on Omega_V),

where rho_{mu(X)} is the Markov gluing for realization X.  Then rho* is
a labeled cut measure on Omega_V of total mass m, and its cut
pseudometric

     D_{rho*}(u,v)  =  E_X [ D_{mu(X)}(u,v) ]

is itself a cut pseudometric.  In particular, if D_{mu(X)}(u,v) >= c d(u,v)
holds for almost every X, so does D_{rho*}(u,v) >= c d(u,v); and if
D_{mu(X)}(u,v) <= U d(u,v) for almost every X, so does D_{rho*}(u,v) <= U d(u,v).

Proof.  This is Fubini.  Each rho_{mu(X)} is a finite measure of mass
m; integrability (mass 2M in applications) makes the expectation well
defined.  Linearity of the cut representation and the fact that "omega(u)
!= omega(v)" is a bounded (0-1) random variable under each rho_{mu(X)}
give the expectation formula for D_{rho*}.  []

================================================================================
PART 2.  UNCERTAINTY TRANSPORT: A RIGIDITY OF MARKOV GLUING
================================================================================

The Markov gluing combines bag laws into ONE global law.  A subtle
but crucial issue: the glued measure on V need NOT be organized
"path-by-path" the way a naive product would suggest.  So if a cut
measure appears as a SUMAND of the bag laws along a path, we need an
additional estimate to say how much separation it forces in D_mu
between the two ENDS of the path.  This is the transport inequality
of Proposition 2.2.  It is where the external CKR theorem enters.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1  THE EXTERNAL SYMMETRIC MARKOV FLOW BOUND
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

We use the following theorem, proved in Chlamtac, Krauthgamer,
Raghavendra, "Approximating sparsest cut in graphs of bounded
treewidth", APPROX/RANDOM 2010 (Section 4, Theorem 4.4, and its
finite-measure form as stated there).

A MARKOV FLOW GRAPH for a finite Markov chain X_0, X_1, ..., X_r with
state sets L_0, ..., L_r is a directed graph: its vertices are
{(i, a) : i = 0,...,r, a in L_i}; edges only go from layer i-1 to layer i;
each such edge ((i-1,a),(i,b)) has capacity Pr[X_{i-1} = a, X_i = b].

SYMMETRIC Markov flow graph: each layer L_i has a fixed-point-free
involution theta_i (think theta_i(x) = "the complement of x", or "swap
the two states"), and the joint law of (theta_i(X_i))_{i=0}^r equals
that of (X_i)_{i=0}^r.

A FEASIBLE FLOW from s_0 to t_1 means a nonnegative function on the
edges that satisfies node conservation everywhere except at s_0 (source)
and t_1 (sink), and is bounded by the edge capacities.  Its VALUE is
the total flow out of s_0.

THEOREM 2.1 (CKR 2010, Theorem 4.4).  For every integer w >= 2 there is
a constant C(w) >= 1 with the following property.  Let a symmetric
Markov flow graph have at most w states per layer, first layer
{s_0, s_1}, and last layer {t_0, t_1}, and let its layer involutions
interchange the two states in each endpoint layer.  Then every feasible
flow from s_0 to t_1 has value at most

     C(w) * Pr[X_0 = s_0, X_r = t_1].

C(w) is a function of w alone.  It does not depend on r or on the
transition probabilities.

Two small enrichments, for convenience (and justified where we use it):

   * Deterministic transitions are allowed (the capacities include
     zero for impossible pairs).
   * Zero-probability states may be removed in involution-pairs
     (their incident capacities vanish, so neither feasible flows nor
     endpoint probabilities change; and involutions pair them up).

We fix once and for all:

     b_k = 1 / C(2^k) > 0.                                        (2.1)

The only feature of "2^k" is that it upper bounds the number of binary
labelings of a set of size k, and so upper bounds every layer size in
the transport argument below.  The constant b_k is therefore a
function of k only.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.2  UNCERTAINTY AND THE TRANSPORT PROPOSITION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Let nu be a labeled cut measure on V, P subseteq V, z in V.  Define
the UNCERTAINTY of nu at z given P:

     E_nu(z | P)  =   sum_{b in Omega_P}
                        min{ nu(omega|P = b, omega(z) = 0),
                             nu(omega|P = b, omega(z) = 1) }.       (2.2)

Read this as: for each possible labeling b on P, look at the two
masses of the two possible labels at z; take the smaller; sum over b.
Two properties are immediate:

   (UNC1) E_nu >= 0; E_nu is monotone in nu, positively homogeneous,
          and concave in nu.
          [All three follow from properties of min{a,b} and of sum.]

   (UNC2) E_{nu^{sym}}(z|P) >= E_nu(z|P).
          [The measure nu^{sym} may average the mass of labels at z
          by complement.  Under the reindexing b -> 1-b, "omega(z)=0"
          and "omega(z)=1" trade places; the min takes the smaller,
          so complementing cannot decrease the total less-frequent
          mass.]

PROPOSITION 2.2 (Transport of uncertainty).  Let mu be a consistent
family of SYMMETRIC bag cut measures, of bags of size at most k.  Let
B_0, B_1, ..., B_r be a path of bags in T, with B_0 containing a vertex y
and B_r containing a vertex z.  Let P = B_j for some 0 <= j <= r.
Suppose a labeled cut measure nu (NOT necessarily symmetric) satisfies

     nu | B_i   <=   mu_{B_i}     for every j <= i <= r,        (2.3)

where mu_{B_i} is the trace of mu_i on its bag (i.e. mu_i itself, but
written suggestively).  Then the Markov-glued pseudometric D_mu obeys

     D_mu(y,z)  >=  b_k * E_nu(z | P).                          (2.4)

Proof.  Fix a realization.  Write rho = rho_mu, m = rho(Omega_V).

If z in P then each assignment on P determines z's label; then
E_nu(z | P) = 0 (both sides of the min are the mass of the same
event minus itself), so (2.4) holds trivially.  Now z notin P.

The transport chain.  Consider the following sequence of vertex sets,
which will be layers of a Markov chain under rho/m:

   L_0     = { y },
   L_1     = B_0 cap B_1,
   L_2     = B_1 cap B_2,
   ...
   L_r     = B_{r-1} cap B_r,
   L_{r+1} = { z },

PLUS one additional layer for the full assignment on P, inserted at the
point where B_j is visited on the path.  More precisely:

   Let L(P) be the layer with states Omega_P (assignments on P).
   Put L(P) between L_j and L_{j+1} (or, if j = 0, immediately after
   L_0; if j = r, immediately before L_{r+1}).

The indexing vertex sets along consecutive layers are all subsets of
SOME bag:

   * L_0 = {y} subset B_0, L_1 = B_0 cap B_1 subset B_0 and B_1,
   * L(P) = B_j,
   * and so on.

So by Lemma 1.1 (MG3), the joint law of labels on consecutive layers is
a Markov chain under rho/m.  Every layer has at most 2^k states (since
each is a subset of a bag, or is P = B_j itself).  All layer vertex
sets are nonempty by Lemma 0.1(b).  The involutions are theta_L: an
assignment omega on L -> 1 - omega.  Because every bag measure mu_i is
symmetric, the joint law of the chain is invariant under the
complement of all layers.  So this is a symmetric Markov flow graph,
with w = 2^k.

The capacities.  Between consecutive layers L_{i-1}, L_i (of the
path-only chain, not counting L(P) yet), the capacity of an arc is
the mass in rho/m of the joint assignment on those layers, which is
the same as a projection of mu_{B_{i-1} cap B_i}: this is a
finite-measure statement.  After symmetrization, the total mass on
each pair of layers is exactly 2m * (probability).  We use the
PROBABILITY normalization rho/m from here on; capacities scale by m
in the end.

The transport measure lambda on Omega of paths.  We want a measure on
paths in the chain whose first layer is y and last is z and whose
successive joint distributions are dominated by the reference chain
capacities.  Take the SUFFIX (from L(P) onward) to be the projection
of nu.  For each assignment b on P (that is, b in Omega_P), let
n_b = nu(omega|P = b).  By (2.3), n_b <= m_b := rho(omega|P = b).
If m_b > 0, append an independent prefix with the conditional law under
rho/m given assignment b on P (this is well defined by the Markov
property).  If m_b = 0 then n_b = 0 (by (2.3)), and no prefix is
needed.

Feasibility.  For each prefix edge F (an event on some consecutive
layers before P), the mass is

     lambda(F) = sum_{b: m_b > 0} n_b * Pr_rho[ F | omega|P = b ]
             <= sum_{b: m_b > 0} m_b * Pr_rho[ F | omega|P = b ]
              = rho(F).

The inequality is term-by-term (n_b <= m_b), so every prefix edge
respects the capacity.  For the suffix edges, the law is the
projection of nu, and these edges correspond to subsets of a single
bag B_i (for i >= j) by construction of the layers, so the suffix also
respects capacities by (2.3).

Consistency at L(P).  A vertex that occurs both on a prefix layer and a
suffix layer must occur on P, because both sides keep only layers that
are subsets of bags that meet at P, and a vertex occurring nontrivially
outside P would give two assignments to a vertex in the graph
disagreeing on the same vertex on non-terminator layers.  Formally: any
coordinate of any layer is a vertex of V; vertices appearing both sides
of L(P) are exactly those in P, so the labels agree.  []

Now, the flow value.  Define, for i in {0,1} and b in Omega_P,

     a_i(b)  =  nu(omega|P = b, omega(z) = i),
     q_b     =  Pr_rho[ omega(y) = 0 | omega|P = b ]  (0 if m_b = 0).

We compute lambda{omega(y) != omega(z)}.  By construction,

     lambda{omega(y) != omega(z)}
       = sum_b q_b * a_1(b) + (1 - q_b) * a_0(b).

Two cases depending on sign (q_b >= 1/2 or <).  In both cases we have

     q_b * a_1(b) + (1 - q_b) * a_0(b)  >=  min{ a_0(b), a_1(b) }.

Summing over b:

     lambda{omega(y) != omega(z)}
       >=  sum_b min{ a_0(b), a_1(b) }  =  E_nu(z | P).

So the total mass of paths where the labels differ at the endpoints is
at least E_nu(z|P).

Restrict lambda to paths with endpoint labels (y=0, z=1); this set of
paths defines a feasible flow from s_0 = (y=0) in layer L_0 to
t_1 = (z=1) in layer L_{r+1}.  Conservation holds at intermediate
states of the flow; capacities dominate the arc masses, by the
feasibility above.  By Theorem 2.1 this flow has value at most

     C(2^k) * Pr[X_0 = (y=0), X_{r+1} = (z=1)]
       = C(2^k) * (rho/m){omega(y)=0, omega(z)=1}.

Similarly for the other orientation (y=1, z=0); apply Theorem 2.1 to
the COMPLEMENT of that flow (recall the chain is complement-symmetric).
Adding up the two orientations,

     lambda{omega(y) != omega(z)}
       <= C(2^k) * (rho/m){omega(y) != omega(z)}
       =  C(2^k) * D_mu(y,z) / m.

Combining the flow bound just derived with the lower bound
E_nu(z|P) <= lambda{omega(y) != omega(z)} and recalling
b_k = 1 / C(2^k), after multiplying by m and dividing by C(2^k):

     D_mu(y, z)  >=  b_k * E_nu(z | P).  []

This completes the proof of Proposition 2.2.  Note that nu may be
UNSYMMETRIC; only mu needs to be symmetric.  This is important: our
transport submeasures include paired perturbations that are not
individually symmetric.

Remark on the symmetry of nu.  The statement only needs mu to be
symmetric (so that the flow graph is symmetric); nu just has to be a
submeasure of mu on the relevant bags.  So we always apply the
proposition with nu allowed to be unsymmetrized.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.3  TWO "ENGINE" TESTS ON SWEEP MEASURES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The transport inequality is only useful if we can produce a
submeasure nu along the path with a definite uncertainty at the far
end.  The two following tests do exactly that.

Setup.  Fix a cutoff M > 0.  For a function F : V -> [-M, M],
recall sigma_F^M = push Lebesgue measure on [-M,M] through t -> 1_{F > t}.
This measure on Omega_V has total mass 2M, and its cut pseudometric is
D( sigma_F^M )(u,v) = |F(u)-F(v)|.  We define the SYMMETRIC sweep

     sigma_F^M, sym = (1/2) ( sigma_F^M + (sigma_F^M)^c ),

which has the same cut pseudometric.

We say F has SWEEP MARGIN a >= 0 if |F(v)| <= M - a for all v in V.
So F is at least distance a from the two endpoints -M, M of the sweep
interval.

LEMMA 2.3 (Height-gap test).  Let P subseteq V, z in V, and t > 0.
Suppose F : V -> [-M,M] satisfies:

   (HG1) F(z) is at distance at least t from both endpoints of [-M,M]
         (that is, F(z) is in [-M+t, M-t]); equivalently, F has sweep
         margin t at z;

   (HG2) |F(z) - F(s)| >= t for every s in P.

Then for nu := sigma_F^M, sym:

     E_nu(z | P)   >=   t.

Proof.  Let U = (F(z)-t, F(z)+t) = the open interval of radius t around
F(z).  By (HG2), no value F(s) with s in P lies in the open interval
(F(z)-t, F(z)+t); each F(s) is <= F(z)-t or >= F(z)+t.  As t varies
through U, the labeling s -> 1_{F(s) > t} is CONSTANT on P.  Indeed
(HG2) says that no F(s) lies inside U; so the label 1_{F(s) > t} is
determined by which side of U the value F(s) is on, and that side does
not change as t runs over U.  Hence the labeling on P is the same at
every t in U.

On the other hand, the label at z, 1_{F(z) > t}, flips exactly at
t = F(z), the midpoint of U.  So during the first half of U (length t)
the labels on P plus "z = 0" are seen; during the second half (also
length t) the same labels on P plus "z = 1" are seen.  For the fixed
assignment b on P equal to the labeling on U, the UNSYMMETRIZED sweep
gives each of the two labels at z at least mass t.

Since U is contained in [-M, M] by (HG1), these thresholds are all
inside the swept interval.  So E_{sigma_F^M}(z | P) >= t.  Apply
(UNC2): E_{sigma_F^M, sym}(z | P) >= E_{sigma_F^M}(z | P) >= t.  []

LEMMA 2.4 (Launch-gap test).  Let A subseteq V and let
f, g : V -> R satisfy g|A = 0 and f+g, f-g in [-M,M]^V.  Set

     nu := ( sigma_{f+g}^M )^{sym} + ( sigma_{f-g}^M )^{sym}
        (sum as a labeled cut measure on Omega_V).

Then for every z in V,

     E_nu(z | A)   >=   2|g(z)|,                                (2.5)

and for every u,v in V,

     D_nu(u,v)  =  |f(u)-f(v) + g(u)-g(v)| + |f(u)-f(v) - g(u)+g(v)|
                 >= 2|g(u) - g(v)|.                             (2.6)

Proof.  Since g|A = 0, we have (f+g)|A = (f-g)|A = f|A.  So the two
height functions agree on A.

Let h_+ = f+g and h_- = f-g.  On A, both equal f.  At z, h_+(z) and
h_-(z) = f(z) + g(z), f(z)-g(z) differ by 2|g(z)|.  Suppose w.l.o.g.
g(z) > 0 (the g(z) < 0 case is symmetric by swapping + and -).  Then
h_-(z) = f(z)-g(z) < f(z)+g(z) = h_+(z), a gap of 2g(z).

For thresholds t in the open interval (f(z)-g(z), f(z)+g(z)), we have
1_{h_-(z) > t} = 0 and 1_{h_+(z) > t} = 1 (labels at z are opposite).
Both h_+, h_- restricted to A equal f|A, so at each such t their
labelings on A agree; call that labeling b(t) in Omega_A.  For any b
in Omega_A, let I_b be the set of t in (f(z)-g(z), f(z)+g(z)) with
b(t) = b.  Then in the sum

     (sigma_{h_+}^M + sigma_{h_-}^M)( b, z = 0 ) =
       int_{I_b} [1_{h_+(z) > t} = 0] dt + int_{I_b} [1_{h_-(z) > t} = 0] dt
       >= int_{I_b} 1 dt = |I_b|  (since h_-(z) <= t on this interval),

and similarly for z = 1 on I_b (from h_+).  Taking min of these two,
then summing over b:

     E_{sigma_{h_+}^M + sigma_{h_-}^M}( z | A )
       = sum_b min{mass with z=0, mass with z=1} >= sum_b |I_b|
       = |(f(z)-g(z), f(z)+g(z))| = 2|g(z)|.

(All thresholds in (f(z)-g(z), f(z)+g(z)) lie in [-M,M] because both
h_+, h_- values are in [-M,M].)  Applying (UNC2) with nu = sym of
sigma_{h_+}^M + sigma_{h_-}^M proves (2.5).

For (2.6): "sigma_F^M cut pseudometric is |F(u)-F(v)|" applied to
F = h_+ and F = h_- gives the equality.  The inequality is the algebraic
|a+b| + |a-b| >= 2|b| with a = f(u)-f(v), b = g(u)-g(v).  []

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.4  PUTTING THE TWO TESTS INTO THE TRANSPORT INEQUALITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The following consequences are repeatedly used; they combine Prop 2.2
with Lemma 2.3 or 2.4.

CONSEQUENCE C1 (LAUNCH TRANSPORT).  Let A subseteq V with g|A = 0, and
let the two functions h_+ = f+g and h_- = f-g be in [-M,M]^V.  Set

     nu = (sigma_{h_+}^M)^{sym} + (sigma_{h_-}^M)^{sym}.

Suppose nu | B_i <= mu_{B_i} for every bag B_i on a path segment from
some bag containing a vertex y (at the "y end") to a bag containing
z.  Suppose also the launch bag A is on the bag path from the y bag to
the z bag.  Then, for the glued pseudometric D_mu and the constant
b_k = 1/C(2^k):

     D_mu(y, z)   >=   b_k * 2 |g(z)| = 2 b_k |g(z)|.            (2.7)

In particular, if |g(z)| >= d(z,A) / 2, then D_mu(y,z) >= b_k d(z,A).

Proof.  Apply Prop 2.2 with P := A.  By Lemma 2.4, E_nu(z | A) >= 2|g(z)|.
So D_mu(y,z) >= b_k * 2|g(z)|.  []

CONSEQUENCE C2 (AVERAGED HEIGHT TRANSPORT).  Let F : V -> [-M,M] with
sweep margin t at z (so F(z) is in [-M+t,M-t]).  Let P be a testing bag
on the bag path from a bag containing y to a bag containing z.
Suppose that, on some event of probability at least p, the trace of
sigma_F^M, sym is dominated by mu_{B_i} on every bag of the segment
from P to the bag containing z.  Suppose on that same event the gap
condition |F(z) - F(s)| >= t holds for every s in P.  Then:

     E_X [ D_mu(y,z) ]  >=  b_k * p * t.                        (2.8)

Proof.  On the dominant event, Lemma 2.3 + Prop 2.2 give a pointwise
lower bound D_mu(y,z) >= b_k * t (for each realization of auxiliary
randomness).  Off the dominant event we still have D_mu(y,z) >= 0.
Averaging and using that the dominant event has probability >= p:

     E D_mu(y,z) >= b_k * t * p.  []

Typical use: the auxiliary randomness is the fresh noise at a launch
of a particle; the trace domination is exactly what "reservation" in
Part 3 provides; the gap event is an event on which a fresh independent
uniform variable has amplitude at least some fixed fraction of a known
scale.  We will build exactly this in Parts 3-6.

================================================================================
PART 3.  PARTICLES AND RESERVATIONS
================================================================================

In Part 1 and Part 2, the bag laws mu_t were given.  In the rest of the
proof, we have to CONSTRUCT them.  They have to be consistent on
overlaps (so that Markov gluing applies) and must carry some specific
submeasures (so that we can apply the transport inequality).

The construction trick is to represent each bag law as a sum of sweeps
of real functions "particles" on all of V.  Because every particle is
a global function, its sweep gives a cut measure on the whole V; the
trace on any bag is automatically consistent between neighboring bags,
because it is a restriction of the same global measure.  Consistency
is FREE.  The real work is keeping particles in line while they get
modified.

Throughout PART 3 fix:
   * the same (V,d) and tree decomposition (T, {B_t}) as before,
     with bags of size at most k >= 2 and B_t nonempty;
   * a root of T (used to define parent/child);
   * the diameter Delta = diam(V,d).  We assume |V| >= 2 initially;
     for |V| = 1 the whole theorem is trivial and handled in the final
     Part 7.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1  SWEEPS AND THE COMMON CUTOFF M
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A PARTICLE is a real function on V.  We maintain a fixed-size LIST

     F_1, F_2, ..., F_N

of particles.  Start: all zero.

Fix a common CUTOFF M > 0.  As long as every particle takes values in
[-M, M], the sweep measure sigma_{F_i}^M is well defined (Part 0 (0.3)):

     sigma_{F_i}^M  =  push of Lebesgue measure on [-M, M]
                       through  t -> 1_{F_i > t}.

Each sigma_{F_i}^M is a labeled cut measure of total mass 2M.

The MEASURE CARRIED BY THE LIST (on a bag P, or any subset P of V) is

     (mu_list)|_P :=  (sum_{i=1}^N (sigma_{F_i}^M)^{sym}) |_P.        (3.1)

We will always use the SYMMETRIC versions sigma^{sym}.  If we want to
save writing, we can drop the word "sym" below; but the bookkeeping is
important: EVERY summand in every bag law we build below is a
symmetric sweep, so the bag laws themselves are symmetric.  This is a
hypothesis of the transport Proposition 2.2, so we must and will
maintain it.

So a bag law mu_t is always described by (3.1).  Consistency on tree
edges becomes the statement: for adjacent bags t,t', the trace of
sum_i (sigma_{F_i}^M)^{sym} on B_t cap B_{t'} depends only on the
functions F_i (not on which list we take it from).  If both copies
share the SAME F_i (as we will maintain while copying down a tree
branch), the traces agree.  So the entire juggling of bag laws reduces
to two rules:

   (P-consistency) Every update we make to the particle list changes
                   the LIST, but leaves the measure (3.1) on each
                   "current bag" UNCHANGED.
   (P-copy) Along a child of the current node, we may COPY the list and
            make different updates into different children, as long as
            each individual copy preserves (P-consistency) on the bag
            where the branch was made.

More precisely, we will update at a "current bag P" (or at the parent
bag of the branch root).  Each update preserves the trace on P; this
is what makes (P-consistency) work.

The crucial lemma is the following, which expresses "the affine trace
preservation".

LEMMA 3.1 (Affine trace update).  Let P subseteq V be any subset, and
suppose particles F_a, F_b admit a COMMON WEAK INCREASING ORDER on P:
that is, there is a listing P = {p_1, p_2, ..., p_r} of the vertices
of P such that

     F_a(p_1) <= F_a(p_2) <= ... <= F_a(p_r),  and
     F_b(p_1) <= F_b(p_2) <= ... <= F_b(p_r).

Let f = (F_a + F_b)/2, and let g : V -> R satisfy g|P = 0.  Suppose
the four functions F_a, F_b, f+g, f-g all take values in [-M,M].
Define

     F_a' := f + g,    F_b' := f - g.

Then the traces of the sum of the two sweep measures on P are EQUAL:

     (sigma_{F_a}^M + sigma_{F_b}^M) | P
       = (sigma_{f+g}^M + sigma_{f-g}^M) | P.                (3.2)

The equality remains true after symmetrization, and for nonempty P it
includes the total masses (which on P are 2M each, but weighted
identically).

Proof.  Write P = {p_1, ..., p_r}.  Consider an arbitrary real function
F with F(p_1) <= F(p_2) <= ... <= F(p_r).  Its sweep sigma_F^M
concentrates on the "monotone" assignments (threshold down-crossings
between successive p_i).  Precisely, the sweep assigns, on Omega_P,
masses to the (r+1) monotone labelings

     (p_1, ..., p_r) -> (1, ..., 1, 0, ..., 0),  cut between p_i and p_{i+1}

with mass given by the successive differences:

     F(p_1) + M
     F(p_2) - F(p_1)
     ...
     F(p_r) - F(p_{r-1})
     M - F(p_r).

These are all >= 0 (by the ordering), and they sum to 2M.

Every OTHER labeling in Omega_P receives zero sweep mass (because F is
monotone on P, and sweeping a threshold through monotone heights only
produces these monotone assignments).

The point is: each of these masses is AFFINE in the height vector
(F(p_1), ..., F(p_r)).  So if we replace (F_a, F_b) by (f+g, f-g) and
g vanishes on P, then (f+g)|P = f|P = (f-g)|P, and the trace of
sigma_{f+g}^M plus sigma_{f-g}^M is TWICE the trace of sigma_f^M (both
have the same height vector f|P, and their sweeps give the same masses,
so we add them).

Meanwhile, the sum sigma_{F_a}^M + sigma_{F_b}^M has trace on P that is
also equal to twice the sweep of ANY function whose height vector at P
equals the componentwise AVERAGE of (F_a(p_i)+F_b(p_i))/2 = f(p_i), by
linearity of the affine formulas applied component-wise (F_a(p_i) and
F_b(p_i) both appear; the sum telescopes).  So both sides equal

     2 * (sigma_f^M) | P.

Symmetrization and restriction to P are linear, so they preserve the
identity.  For P = empty, both sides are "one atom of mass 2M each",
and there is nothing to check.  []

The interpretation: replacing (F_a, F_b) by (f+g, f-g) at P does not
change the BAG LAW at P, as long as we preserve a common weak
increasing order.  So we can modify the pair, e.g. add useful
perturbations g that are 0 on P, without changing the measure carried
on the current bag.

For us, the useful perturbation g in later applications is a
nonnegative, nonzero function on V that vanishes on the current bag P.
Its role is exactly to be BIG somewhere else (on future bags where the
pair will be frozen, so the perturbation survives).

But we have to keep the particles uniformly Lipschitz: extracting
information via g each time could in principle inflate their slopes
without bound.  A numerical invariant saves us.

AN UPDATE AT P is an operation that replaces a pair (F_a, F_b) with a
common weak increasing order on P by

     (F_a, F_b) -> (f+g, f-g),     f = (F_a+F_b)/2,   g|P = 0.     (3.3)

We fix positive constants L, B once and for all (later L = B = 3) and
require every perturbation to satisfy

     Lip(g) <= L,       sup_{v in V} |g(v)| <= B * Delta.          (3.4)

We will see that only L and B appear in the bounds, so we can choose
them small.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.2  UNIFORM BOUNDS ON PARTICLES: THE INVARIANT Q_r
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A naive approach: after each update, use Lipschitz-triangle-inequality
to say Lip(f+g) <= Lip(f) + L, so after T updates Lip grows like T L.
This is unbounded in T (there could be arbitrarily many updates).

Surprisingly, there is a UNIFORM bound that does not depend on T.  The
reason: linear changes of a vector of N real coordinates that "average
and perturb" always leave the vector in a bounded region of R^N.

LEMMA 3.2 (Uniform particle bounds).  After ANY finite sequence of
updates starting from zero particles, every particle satisfies

     Lip(F_i) <= L(N-1),                                             (3.5)
     |F_i(v)| <= B(N-1) * Delta     for every v in V.                (3.6)

The bounds hold for ARBITRARY choices of updated pairs and perturbations
satisfying (3.4), REGARDLESS of the number of updates.

Proof.

   Step 1:  Numerical invariant.  Suppose a vector a = (a_1, ..., a_N) in R^N
   starts at zero and is changed by operations of the form

        a_a, a_b  ->  (a_a + a_b)/2 + e,   (a_a + a_b)/2 - e        (3.7)

   on two coordinates a,b (with |e| <= 1).  At every stage:

        (Sum)  a_1 + a_2 + ... + a_N = 0.                         (3.8)

        (Parti) for every I subseteq {1,...,N},
                sum_{i in I} a_i <= |I| (N-|I|).                   (3.9)

   (Sum) is immediate: the update (3.7) sends a_a + a_b to
   (a_a + a_b)/2 + e + (a_a + a_b)/2 - e = a_a + a_b, so the total is
   preserved at zero.

   (Parti) by induction.  Define Q_r = r(N-r) for r = 0, 1, ..., N.
   Q_0 = 0, Q_N = 0.  Base case: all a_i = 0, so the LHS of (3.9) is
   0 <= Q_r for every I.

   Induction step.  Let a new update happen at coordinates a, b,
   producing a'.  Fix I subseteq {1,...,N}.  Cases:
      - Both a, b in I: sum_{i in I} a_i is unchanged.
      - Neither: unchanged.
      - Exactly one, say a, is in I: write S = I \ {a}, so |S| = r - 1
        with r = |I|.  After the update, sum_{i in I} a'_i is

             sum_{h in S} a'_h  +  a'_a
           = sum_{h in S} a_h  +  (a_a + a_b)/2 + e   (S unaffected)

             Use the identity

                  sum_S a_h + (a_a + a_b)/2   =   (1/2) [ sum_S a_h + sum_{S+a+b} a_h ].

             WHY:  sum_{S+a+b} a_h = sum_S a_h + a_a + a_b.

             Apply the inductive hypothesis:  sum_S a_h <= Q_{r-1}  and
             sum_{S+a+b} a_h <= Q_{r+1}.  Then

                  new sum over S+a  <=  (1/2) [Q_{r-1} + Q_{r+1}] + |e|
                                   <=  (1/2) [Q_{r-1} + Q_{r+1}] + 1   (|e| <= 1).

             Algebra:  Q_{r-1} + Q_{r+1}
                     = (r-1)(N-r+1) + (r+1)(N-r-1)
                     = (r-1)(N-r) + (r-1) + (r+1)(N-r) - (r+1)
                     = (N-r)(2r) + (r-1) - (r+1)
                     = 2 r (N-r) - 2
                     = 2 Q_r - 2.

             Therefore

                  new sum over S+a  <=  (1/2)(2 Q_r - 2) + 1
                                   =  Q_r - 1 + 1
                                   =  Q_r.

             This is (3.9) for I = S+a in the new vector.

   Step 2:  Apply the invariant to differences.  Fix distinct u,v in V.
   Define the vector a_i := (F_i(u) - F_i(v)) / (L d(u,v)).  Each update
   at a bag P = some set, with perturbation g, sends a_a, a_b to
   (a_a + a_b)/2 + e and (a_a + a_b)/2 - e, where

        e = (g(u) - g(v)) / (L d(u,v)),     |e| <= Lip(g)/L <= 1.

   Apply the invariant (3.9) with I = {i} (single index) and also with
   I = complement of {i}.  Yields, using (3.8) zero-sum:
        a_i <= N - 1,        a_i >= -(N - 1).
   So
        |F_i(u) - F_i(v)|  <=  (N-1) L d(u,v),   for every pair u,v.
   That is Lip(F_i) <= L(N-1).  This is (3.5).

   Step 3:  Apply the invariant to values.  Fix v in V.  Define
   a_i := F_i(v) / (B Delta).  Each update produces
   e = g(v) / (B Delta), with |e| <= 1.  Same invariant gives
        |F_i(v)|  <=  (N-1) B Delta.   This is (3.6).

   The argument applies SEPARATELY to every distinct "copy" history (when
   different branches share the same starting point and the same list,
   then diverge, each branch's invariant is separate).  This is harmless.

   Note that the bound does not depend on T, and therefore is a genuine
   consequence of the update rule (uniform in the number of updates).  []

Remark.  With L = B = 3 (as later), the bounds read:
        Lip(F_i) <= 3(N-1),        |F_i(v)| <= 3 (N-1) Delta.

We can now choose the common cutoff M to leave a safety margin.

LEMMA 3.2 gives, immediately, the following.

   (Cutoff choice) Take N and any L,B > 0.  Set

        M  =  (B(N-1) + 2) * Delta.                                (3.10)

   Then every particle ever built (via any finite sequence of updates
   satisfying (3.4)) satisfies |F_i(v)| <= B(N-1) * Delta = M - 2 Delta.
   So the SWEEP MARGIN of every particle is at least 2 Delta:
   F_i(V) is at least 2 Delta away from each endpoint of [-M,M].

   (Upper bound on D)   At any bag B_t, the sweep pseudometric of the
   LIST carried by that bag satisfies, for any u,v in V

        D_{mu_t}(u,v)  =  sum_i |F_i(u) - F_i(v)|
                       <=  N * L(N-1) * d(u,v).                        (3.11)

   (In particular, U := N L(N-1) is an upper bound on the glued
   pseudometric in (1.2); see Consequence A in Part 1.)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.3  RESERVATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A RESERVATION is a constraint "freeze particles F_a and F_b": neither
may be used in another update, while the reservation is active.
Releasing a reservation makes them available again (but does NOT
delete or change them; they remain in the list).

Rationale.  Consider a pair (F_a, F_b) that we UPDATE at a bag A.  The
two resulting functions are f+g and f-g, where g vanishes on A.  Then
freeze them.  So long as the reservation is active, both particles are
unchanged along the current branch, and so their sweep measures are
unchanged on every bag along that branch.  The two-side submeasure
they generate is

     nu = (sigma_{f+g}^M)^{sym} + (sigma_{f-g}^M)^{sym}.

At any later bag B_l (of the same branch), the total trace mu_{B_l}
is a sum with all currently-carried sweeps, INCLUDING these two.  So
nu | B_l <= mu_{B_l} by monotonicity.  This is exactly the hypothesis
(2.3) of Proposition 2.2, and it says that the pair carries its
"launch uncertainty" all the way from the launch bag A to any later
bag in the branch, regardless of what other operations happen at the
intervening bags.  C1 then gives the lower transport bound
D_mu(y,z) >= b_k * 2|g(z)| for suitable y,z.

Now we formalize the bookkeeping.

At each tree node t, let
   (active)   a set of active reservations, each a pair (a,b) in
              {1,...,N}^2 with a != b;
   c_t  :=  the number of active reservations at node t.
        Each reservation occupies 2 particles, so it takes 2 c_t
        particles out of the pool.

The number of FREE particles at node t is N - 2 c_t.  To run an update
at t we need two free particles with a COMMON WEAK INCREASING ORDER on
the current bag.  There are at most r! <= k! such orders on a bag of
size r <= k, so at least one such order has at least ceil((N-2c_t)/k!)
free particles assigned to it; taking two of them suffices.

Therefore, if N - 2 c_t >= 2 k!  the update can always proceed.  We
choose N accordingly (see Prop 3.3).

A reservation along a descending path is an interval [launch, release]
of consecutive nodes, along an ancestor-descendant path in T.  During
this interval, the pair (a,b) is "reserved": no update touches F_a or
F_b.  So along the interval, the sweep measures (sigma_{F_a}^M)^{sym}
and (sigma_{F_b}^M)^{sym} are IDENTICAL as functions on V; the trace
of their sum on each bag of the interval is a submeasure of the bag
law built at that bag.  This gives the trace-domination hypothesis of
Proposition 2.2 for ANY sub-interval of the reservation.

Branching.  At a branching node of T (a node with several children),
copies of the whole state (list of particles + full reservation record)
are maintained separately for each child.  So c_t is the number of
active reservations in THAT COPY of the memory, not across all copies.
The number of simultaneous reservations we must bound is per-copy.

A REJECTED PROPOSAL: at a bag where we want to "consider" a new
reservation, we might reserve it for the duration of just that bag and
then release it if we decide not to keep it.  This "rejected" pair is
one-bag-only and does not persist; but for bookkeeping we allow it, and
still count it toward c_t (during its single node).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.4  REALIZATION: ALL-PURPOSE SCHEDULE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The following proposition is our central construction tool.  It
formalizes: "we have a finite schedule of launches and releases; run it".

PROPOSITION 3.3 (Realization by particles).  Suppose a finite schedule
requires at most q simultaneous reserved pairs IN EACH MEMORY COPY
(including any temporary one-bag reservation made before another is
released).  Updates may be made at the current bag, or on a
child-specific copy at its parent bag before moving to the child.
Before processing the root, updates at the empty interface are also
allowed (empty interface imposes no ordering constraints, so any two
free particles can be used).

Every update uses a perturbation satisfying (3.4) and vanishing on its
interface.

Choose

     N   =  2 q + k! + 2,
     M   =  (B(N-1) + 2) * Delta.                             (3.12)

Then all requested updates and reservations can be carried out.  They
produce symmetric bag cut measures mu_t with the following properties.

   (i)    Each mu_t has the same total mass m = 2 N M, and neighboring
          bag measures have equal traces on their intersection.

   (ii)   Every particle height is at distance at least 2 Delta from
          each endpoint of the sweep interval [-M, M].

   (iii)  The Markov-glued pseudometric D_mu satisfies

              D_mu(u,v)  <=  U * d(u,v)  for all u,v in V,          (3.13)
          with
              U  =  N L (N-1).

   (iv)   If a pair is reserved from a launch bag through a later bag
          on a descending path, its fixed symmetric two-particle cut
          measure has trace dominated by mu_t at every bag on that
          segment.  The segment includes the launch and the release
          bags, even if the particles are reused at the release bag.
          A reservation launched at the empty interface has the same
          property from the root onward.

The statements hold for each realization of any auxiliary randomness.
For measurable randomized choices, taking expectations also yields an
L1 pseudometric with the same upper bound.

Proof.

   (Free-particles bound) At every stage at most 2q particles are
   reserved, so at least N - 2q = k! + 2 particles are free.  For a
   bag of size r <= k, each free particle has a weak increasing order
   on the bag (with a fixed tie-breaking of the vertex labels for
   determinism).  There are at most r! <= k! possible orders, so two
   free particles get the SAME order.  So an update at the bag is
   always possible with two free particles.

   Choose the pair deterministically: order the pairs by some fixed
   ordering, and pick the first eligible pair in the current memory
   (BEFORE sampling any fresh perturbation randomness for that launch).
   This guarantees that which pair we pick depends only on the
   deterministic state, not on the launch noise.

   (Bounds) Lemma 3.2 with the choice (3.12) of M gives the 2 Delta
   sweeps margin at every stage; the cutoff is fixed at the very start
   and does not depend on the schedule length.

   (Bag law) At a bag t, record:

        mu_t := ( sum_i (sigma_{F_i}^M)^{sym} ) | B_t.              (3.14)
        (with F_i the CURRENT particle list at bag t).

   Updates at B_t leave mu_t unchanged (Lemma 3.1), and releasing a
   reservation leaves the functions unchanged.  Thus we may record mu_t
   either before or after updates at B_t (or before/after a release);
   the same trace is obtained.  Preliminary updates at the empty
   interface are performed before the root bag law is recorded.

   (Consistency) For a child t' of t, COPY the current list AND all
   reservation records.  On that copy, perform any child-specific
   updates still at B_t.  Its trace on B_t remains mu_t.  Then move
   that copy to B_{t'} and record its law there.  The two laws at B_t
   and B_{t'} agree on B_t cap B_{t'} because BOTH are restrictions of
   the SAME total cut measure on V carried by this COPY before any
   operations at B_{t'}.

   Operations at B_{t'} afterwards preserve the child law (Lemma 3.1).
   So consistency holds on every edge of T.

   (Mass) Each summand (sigma_{F_i}^M)^{sym} has mass 2M (the sym
   doubles nothing; both sides add up to 2M by (sigma)^{sym}(Omega) =
   M + M = 2M).  There are N of them, so mu_t total mass 2NM = m, same
   at every bag.

   (Markov gluing) With consistency over T, Lemma 1.1 applies and
   supplies the glued pseudometric D_mu.

   (Upper bound)  For an edge uv, choose a bag B_t containing BOTH
   endpoints u,v.  Its trace mu_t equals the glued one on the bag.  So
   D_mu(u,v) = sum_i |F_i(u) - F_i(v)| (since the right side is the
   sweep pseudometric on the bag).  By Lemma 3.2, this is at most
   N L(N-1) d(u,v) = U d(u,v).  Already for the edge; for arbitrary
   u,v use the triangle inequality along a shortest path (Consequence A
   in Part 1).  Gives (3.13).

   (Reservations dominate)  For a reservation launched at bag A with
   the pair ending as (f+g, f-g) with g|A = 0, let

        nu = (sigma_{f+g}^M)^{sym} + (sigma_{f-g}^M)^{sym}.

   These two functions do not change while the reservation is active
   (by definition).  So at any bag B_l on the reservation, they are
   two summands of the current trace mu_{B_l}; hence nu | B_l <= mu_{B_l}.
   At the launch bag this is read just after the launching update; at
   the release bag it can be read just before release.  So the
   domination also holds for the RECORDED law at either endpoint, even
   if the pair is immediately reused.

   (A reservation launched at the empty interface) The two sides are
   identical from the root onwards.  So domination holds from the first
   bag.

   (Aux randomness) All bounds and trace identities hold POINTWISE
   for each realization of the auxiliary randomness.  For measurable
   choices, taking expectation of the finite cut measures gives an
   L1 pseudometric (Part 1, Consequence B) with the same upper bound.

   []

Concrete constants.  With the common choice L = B = 3:

     Lip(F_i) <= 3(N-1)                    (uniform, for every particle)
     |F_i(v)| <= 3(N-1) Delta              (uniform)
     M        =  (3(N-1) + 2) Delta  =  (3N - 1) Delta
     U        =  N L (N-1)  =  3 N (N-1).

All depend on N (and hence on q, the per-copy reservation cap) only.
The NUMBER of updates over the whole tree, and the TOTAL number of
reservations anywhere, do not appear in U.  This is key: only the
MAXIMUM SIMULTANEOUS reservations in any memory copy matter.

The proposition says: as long as the schedules we design have a
BOUNDED PER-COPY simultaneous reservation count, we get consistent
bag laws and a glued pseudometric of bounded upper Lipschitz constant.
We do NOT yet say how to CHOOSE the schedules (that is the content of
Parts 4 and 5).  But the bounded-per-copy reservation machinery is
established: that will be the only bookkeeping stress in Parts 4-5.

We emphasize one point about "reservation".  Even though our
constructions repeatedly update particles and add noise, at ANY moment
of the process, EVERY active reservation contributes a fixed submeasure
nu to the current bag law; the trace of nu is the SAME for every bag
of the reservation segment; so Proposition 2.2 applies to any sub-path
of that segment.  This is what converts "particles" into "transport".

================================================================================
PART 4.  THE COMMON-BAG METRIC D_bag
================================================================================

Goal of this part: construct one symmetric consistent family of bag
measures whose glued cut pseudometric D_bag satisfies

   UPPER:  D_bag(u,v)  <=  U_bag * d(u,v)              (all u,v),
   LOWER:  D_bag(u,v)  >=  a_bag * d(u,v)              (u,v in a common bag),

with U_bag < infinity and a_bag > 0 depending only on k.  The
construction uses the same particle machinery as Part 3.  The
per-copy reservation cap will be R = 2k(k-1).  So the particle count
N, the cutoff M, and the upper bound U_bag will be exactly the "Part 3
realization" formulas with q = R.

Throughout this part, (V,d) is the shortest-path metric of a finite
connected graph with positive edge lengths and a tree decomposition
whose bags are nonempty, size at most k; d has diameter Delta; |V|>=2.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1  ACTIVE SCALES AND THE PER-BAG COUNT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

For a bag B (as a vertex set), say a POWER OF TWO  s = 2^j  (j in Z)
is ACTIVE at B if some two DISTINCT vertices u,v in B satisfy

     s/2  <=  d(u,v)  <=  4 s.                                (4.1)

The interval of scales [s/2, 4s] on which a fixed distance d(u,v) is
"active" corresponds to s in [d/4, 2d] (endpoint ratio eight).  A
positive distance therefore lies in at most FOUR such intervals.  A
bag of size at most k contains at most binom(k,2) unordered pairs, so

     (Bounded active scales)  |{s : s active at B}|  <=  4 binom(k,2)  =  2 k(k-1).

Define

     R   :=   2 k (k-1).                                      (4.2)

So every bag has at most R active scales.  We will reserve one
particle pair per active scale.

Two comments on WHAT ACTIVE MEANS.

   (A1)  The activity interval [s/2, 4s] is wider than the interval
   [s, 2s] that we use below to pick a scale FOR A TESTED PAIR.  This
   slack has a geometric purpose: if a pair at distance between s and
   2s lives in a bag, at least one endpoint must lie away from an
   INACTIVE upper bag A; that "one endpoint away" statement is what
   gives the bumps enough LIPSCHITZ ROOM to be pushed at one endpoint
   while vanishing at the other.

   (A2)  When a scale is launched at a parent bag A (so the pair will
   persist into A's child), we require s INACTIVE at A but active at
   the child.  The parent launch is made BEFORE visiting the child, at
   the interface A.  If we were to launch s AT the child (with
   interface = the child bag), the perturbation g would vanish on the
   child bag, giving no separation inside that bag; launching at A
   with interface A gives g nonvanishing INSIDE the child.

The bookkeeping about which partition sampler we call how many times
is important; we spell it out fully below.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.2  PADDED PARTITIONS (EXTERNAL INPUT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The whole D_bag construction (and, later, Part 5's local-noise step)
uses the following padded decomposition theorem for bounded-treewidth
graphs, proved by Filtser, Friedrich, Issac, Kumar, Le, Mallek, and
Zeif (TheoretiCS 2025, Theorem 1.2 and Definition 2.1).  The
"diameter" in its statement is the ORIGINAL shortest-path distance
(sometimes called WEAK diameter); the parts need not be geodesically
convex.

THEOREM 4.1 (Padded partitions for bounded treewidth).  There are
constants  alpha in (0, 1/8]  and  pi in (0, 1]  with the following
property, depending only on the bag-size bound k.  For every graph
metric (V,d) as above and every radius r > 0, there is a random
partition P of V (parts are subsets C subseteq V with pairwise-disjoint
union V) such that:

   (PP-diam)   diam(C) <= r       for every part C in support,
   (PP-pad)    Pr[ B_d(x, alpha r)  is contained in the part
                   containing x ]  >=  pi       for every x in V.

Here B_d(x, t) = {y in V : d(x,y) <= t}.

Choice of alpha and pi.  The cited scheme depends on t = treewidth, so
we need to fix k-UNIFORM constants.  Let t_max = max{2, k-1}.  For
each t in {2, ..., t_max} the source has constants beta_t > 0 and
delta_t > 0 such that, at every scale r and every vertex x, the
padding probability is at least exp(-beta_t * gamma) whenever
0 <= gamma <= delta_t.  Set

     alpha := min { 1/8,  min_{2 <= t <= t_max} delta_t },
     pi    := min_{2 <= t <= t_max} exp( - beta_t * alpha ).

These are positive and depend only on k.  So we take the constants
alpha and pi fixed for the rest of the file.

Special case: treewidth 1.  The cited padded decomposition is stated
for treewidth t >= 2.  Treewidth 1 (forests) can be reduced to
treewidth 2 by attaching a positive-length triangle at a single
vertex.  This enlarges the graph by at most three vertices but keeps
all original distances.  Every partition of the enlarged graph,
restricted to original vertices, preserves both (PP-diam) and
(PP-pad).  So the theorem extends to all t >= 1 with the SAME positive
alpha, pi (taking a common minimum across t = 1 and t >= 2).

Convention for subsets: for C subseteq V, we treat d(z, V \ C) as
finite whenever C != V (in which case d(z, V\C) is a genuine
minimum over a nonempty set); whenever we write
"min{ ..., d(z, V\C), ... }", this is always bounded above by a finite
constant that we control.

We also use the following elementary Lipschitz-bump lemma (from the
original Section 4).

LEMMA 4.2 (Disjoint nonnegative bumps).  Let E_1, ..., E_m subseteq V
be pairwise DISJOINT.  For each i, let psi_i : V -> [0, inf) be
nonnegative and 1-Lipschitz, with psi_i vanish off E_i.  Let
u_1, ..., u_m in [0,1].  Then  sum_i u_i psi_i  is 1-Lipschitz.

Proof.  Within one set E_i, the summand u_i psi_i is 1-Lipschitz
(scale of a 1-Lipschitz function).  For z,w in different sets, the
sum at either z or w is nonnegative and bounded by d(z,w) (each bump
vanishes off its own set, so the "other side" sees zero).  Their
absolute difference is at most d(z,w).  Same estimate covers points
outside all sets.  []

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.3  THE CO-BAG EMBEDDING: STATEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PROPOSITION 4.3 (Co-bag embedding).  For every rooted tree
decomposition of a finite connected graph as above, with bags of size
at most k and all bags nonempty, there is an L1 pseudometric D_bag on
V and constants U_bag < infinity and a_bag > 0, depending only on k,
such that for every u,v in V

     D_bag(u,v)  <=  U_bag * d(u,v),                         (4.3)
and
     D_bag(u,v)  >=  a_bag * d(u,v)        whenever u,v share a bag.  (4.4)

One may take

     R       =  4 binom(k,2),
     N       =  2 R + k! + 2,
     U_bag   =  3 N (N-1),
     a_bag   =  (pi * alpha) / 64.

The rest of PART 4 is the construction and the proof.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.4  THE RESERVATION SCHEDULE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

We root T at any node.  The schedule is defined node by node.

Root bag.  At the root bag B_root, launch one reserved particle pair
for every scale s ACTIVE at B_root, using the EMPTY INTERFACE (no
vanishing constraint, so any two free particles may be used).  Then
record the root trace.

A node t with parent s.  Make a fresh copy of the parent's particle
list and full reservation record.  This copy is dedicated to t.

   Step 1 (drop): release all reservations whose scale is INACTIVE at
   B_t.  Such pairs are just freed; the particles remain in the list.

   Step 2 (carry): the pairs whose scale is ACTIVE at BOTH B_s and B_t
   are unchanged; they continue from s to t.

   Step 3 (launch): for every scale s' ACTIVE at B_t but NOT active
   at B_s, launch a new reserved pair whose interface is B_s.  This
   update is performed AT B_s in the child-specific memory, BEFORE
   moving the memory to t.  The new pair therefore has trace on B_s
   that is compatible with the child law, and is available to be
   active at B_t and onward.

   Step 4 (record): record mu_t in the child's memory; then move to t.

   Step 5 (branch): if a node has multiple children, each child gets
   its own copy from Step 1 onward.  The reservations held in one
   copy are counted separately.

A nonroot node t whose (Step 1) left some scales active at B_t has
those scales' pairs carried passively; no fresh launch.  A pair whose
scale is active at a maximal consecutive run of bags gets
LAUNCHED at the top of that run and RELEASED after the bottom.

Reservation-count invariant.  In any memory copy at any moment:

     the number of reserved pairs is at most R (the per-bag count);

(and there is a brief overlap while "one old pair is dropped AND one
new pair is launched", but we can always reorder as drop-then-launch
which uses at most R simultaneously).  So the reservation cap q = R
is respected.  By Proposition 3.3 with q = R:

        N = 2R + k! + 2,     M = (B(N-1)+2) Delta,     m = 2NM.

All particles are uniformly Lipschitz by Lemma 3.2 with L = 3.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.5  THE PERTURBATION g FOR A NEW SCALE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

At a launch of scale s at interface bag A (possibly A = empty):

     r := s/16.

   Sample a random partition P from Theorem 4.1 at scale r (so parts
   have diameter at most r, and every point has probability >= pi of
   having a ball of radius alpha r around it inside its own part).

   For each part C in P, give an independent uniform coefficient
   U_C in [0, 1], and set the bump

                             min{ d(z,A), d(z, V \ C), r }    if z in C,
        psi_C(z)    =      {
                             0                                 if z notin C.

   (For a root launch with EMPTY interface A, omit the term d(z,A)
   entirely; the other two terms remain.)

   Define  g   :=  sum_{C in P}  U_C * psi_C.

PROPERTIES OF g:

   (G1)  Vanishes on A.  For z in A: if z is in some C, then
   d(z,A) = 0, so psi_C(z) = 0.  So g(z) = 0 for z in A.

   (G2)  Lipschitz.  The bumps psi_C are supported on disjoint sets C,
   and each psi_C is 1-Lipschitz (it is the minimum of 1-Lipschitz
   functions d(z,A), d(z,V\C), and the constant r).  So by Lemma 4.2
   with all u_i bounded by 1, the sum g is 1-Lipschitz.

   (G3)  Bounds.  Since the scale s is active at some bag, s <= 2 Delta
   (else no bag would have a pair at distance in [s/2, 4s] with
   s/2 <= Delta).  So r = s/16 <= Delta/8, and each bump is at most
   r; so 0 <= g <= r <= Delta/8.  Hence |g| <= Delta/8.

   (G4)  Amplitude at a padding point.  Fix w with d(w, A) >= s/8
   (when A is nonempty) and let C be the part of the random partition
   containing w.  On the padding event that B_d(w, alpha r) is inside
   C (probability >= pi, independent of the coefficients), the bump
   psi_C(w) satisfies

        min{ d(w,A), d(w, V\C), r }  >=  min{ s/8, alpha r, r }
                                       =  alpha r.

   (Since d(w,A) >= s/8 = 2r, and d(w,V\C) >= alpha r on the padding
   event, and alpha r <= r.)

   (G5)  Truncation cap is well below d(w,w').  If w' is the "other"
   endpoint at distance d(w,w') >= s > r = s/16, then w,w' lie in
   different parts of any partition at scale r.  So psi_C(w') = 0.

At each launch, replace two free particles with a common weak sorting
order on the interface by (f+g, f-g) as in Lemma 3.1, and reserve
them.  The empty-interface launch has no sorting constraint.  All
launches use fresh randomness (partition, coefficients); the particle
pair is chosen deterministically BEFORE the launch randomness is
sampled, by the first-eligible-pair rule of Prop 3.3.

The L = B = 3 choices.  Lip(g) <= 1 and |g| <= Delta/8, so any choice
of L >= 1 and B >= 1/8 works.  We use L = 3 and B = 3 to match Part 3,
giving U_bag = 3N(N-1) as in Prop 3.3 with L = 3.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.6  UPPER BOUND: D_bag <= U_bag * d
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Fix a bag B_t containing both endpoints of some graph edge uv.  Its
trace mu_t on B_t equals the glued one, so by Lemma 3.1 and the
definition of the reserved particle list (as in the proof of Prop 3.3),

     D_bag(u,v)  =  E_X [ D_{mu_t(X)}(u,v) ]
                =  E_X [ sum_i |F_i(u) - F_i(v)| ].

The inner sum satisfies, by the uniform particle bound of Lemma 3.2,
sum_i |F_i(u) - F_i(v)| <= N L(N-1) d(u,v).  Compute:

     N L(N-1)  =  (2R + k! + 2) * 3 * (2R + k! + 1)
              =  3 N (N-1)  =  U_bag.

So D_bag(u,v) <= U_bag d(u,v) for every edge uv.  By the triangle
inequality along a shortest path (Part 1, Consequence A, applied to
D_bag as a path-summed metric from the edge bound), the same inequality
holds for all u,v in V.  This proves (4.3).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.7  LOWER BOUND: D_bag >= a_bag * d(u,v) for pairs in a common bag
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Fix two distinct vertices u,v in a common bag B.  The goal is to
exhibit a definite mass of separating cuts of D_bag.

STEP 1: A dyadic scale for the pair.  Since d(u,v) > 0, there is a
unique integer j with 2^j <= d(u,v) < 2^{j+1}.  Let s = 2^j.  Then

     s   <=  d(u,v)  <  2s.                                    (4.5)

In particular, s is active at B: s/2 <= s <= d(u,v) < 2s <= 4s.

STEP 2: The reserved pair carrying s.  The pair reserved for scale s
at bag B is the unique pair launched at the beginning of the maximal
run of bags (along the current branch, from the root toward B) where s
was active.  Trace back to the start of that run:

   CASE 1 (root launch).  The pair was launched at the root bag with
   EMPTY interface.  Pick w := u (either endpoint works; empty
   interface imposes no constraint).  Let w' := v.

   CASE 2 (launched at a bag A where s was inactive).  The bag A sits
   STRICTLY ABOVE B on the tree path (A is a parent of some bag on
   the run).  At least one of u,v, say w, has d(w, A) >= s/8.  For,
   if both d(u,A) < s/8 and d(v,A) < s/8, then pick nearest points
   a, b in A: by triangle inequality,

        d(a,b)  >=  d(u,v) - d(u,a) - d(v,b)
                >  s - s/8 - s/8  =  3s/4,
        d(a,b)  <=  d(u,a) + d(u,v) + d(v,b)
                <  s/8 + 2s + s/8  =  9s/4.

   So 3s/4 < d(a,b) < 9s/4, hence s/2 <= d(a,b) <= 4s.  Then a != b
   (they are at positive distance), and a,b in A, so s would be active
   at A.  Contradiction.  Hence choose w as the endpoint with
   d(w,A) >= s/8, and w' := the other endpoint.

   In both cases, the interface A satisfies d(w, A) >= s/8 when A is
   nonempty; when A is empty, the term d(z,A) is dropped from the bump
   and the amplitude estimate below is even simpler.

STEP 3: w and w' lie in different parts of the launch partition.  The
launch partition was sampled at scale r = s/16.  By (4.5),
d(w,w') = d(u,v) >= s > s/16 = r.  So any part of the partition
contains at most one of w,w' (parts have diameter <= r).  Let C be
the part containing w; then w' notin C.

STEP 4: Amplitude at w on the padding event.  Let

   EV   :=  the event that the random partition at the launch puts
            B_d(w, alpha r)  inside  C,    where C is w's part.

By Theorem 4.1 (PP-pad), Pr(EV) >= pi.  On EV, d(w, V\C) >= alpha r.
Combined with d(w,A) >= s/8 = 2r (Case 2) or d(z,A) omitted entirely
(Case 1), the bump psi_C at w satisfies

     psi_C(w)  =  min{ d(w,A), d(w, V\C), r }
              >=  min{ 2r, alpha r, r }  =  alpha r.

(If Case 1, replace d(w,A) by "infinity" in the min; the bound
min{alpha r, r} = alpha r still holds.)

STEP 5: Conditional uniform coefficient.  Condition on the launch's
random partition, on the choice of the reserved pair (which is
deterministic from pre-launch state), and on all coefficients U_{C}
except the one for w's part.  Then g(w) - g(w') equals

     U_C * psi_C(w)  +  (a quantity F determined by the other
                         coefficients and the partition).

Let a := psi_C(w) >= alpha r (on EV) and let b := F.  Since U_C is
uniform on [0,1] and independent of everything else,

     E_{U_C} [ | a U_C + b | ]  >=  a / 4,                     (4.6)

where (4.6) is the elementary estimate

     int_0^1 | a s + b | ds  >=  a / 4       (a > 0, b real).

Why a/4: the function s -> |as + b| is convex, has minimum at
s_0 = -b/a, and equals a |s - s_0|.  Its integral over any unit
interval is at least the integral over [s_0, s_0+1] or [s_0-1, s_0],
whichever is inside [0,1]; either of those equals a/2, and on a
sub-interval of length 1/2 the integral is at least a/4.  (Simpler:
symmetric convex argument: |as + b| >= a|s - s_0|, and the integral
of a|s - s_0| over [0,1] is at least a/4 because the interval has
length 1 and s_0 is some real point.)

So, conditioned on the padding event EV (so a >= alpha r),

     E[ |g(w) - g(w')| | EV, partition, other coefficients ]  >=  alpha r / 4.

Averaging over EV (probability >= pi):

     E[ |g(w) - g(w')| ]  >=  pi * alpha r / 4  =  pi * alpha * s / 64.   (4.7)

STEP 6: Apply the paired perturbation to get a lower cut bound.  At
the launch we replaced two particles with (f+g, f-g).  Let
nu = (sigma_{f+g}^M)^{sym} + (sigma_{f-g}^M)^{sym}.  By Lemma 2.4,
equation (2.6):

     D_nu(u,v)   =   D_nu(w, w')
                >=  2 |g(w) - g(w')|.

The two functions f+g and f-g are FROZEN from the launch bag A (or
the empty interface, at root) down to B, so nu | B <= mu_B by the
trace-domination property of Prop 3.3(iv).

Taking expectation over launch randomness and using (4.7),

     D_bag(u,v)   =   E_X [ D_{mu_B(X)}(u,v) ]
                  >=  E_X [ D_nu(u,v)(X) ]                   (mu_B dominates nu)
                  >=  2 * E_X [ |g(w)-g(w')|(X) ]            ((2.6))
                  >=  2 * pi * alpha * s / 64
                  =  pi * alpha * s / 32.                    (4.8)

STEP 7: Relate s to d(u,v).  By (4.5), s > d(u,v)/2, so

     D_bag(u,v)  >=  (pi * alpha / 32) * (d(u,v) / 2)
                 =  (pi * alpha / 64) * d(u,v)  =  a_bag * d(u,v).    (4.9)

For u = v, both sides are 0.  This proves (4.4).  []

The proof of Proposition 4.3 is complete.

Two remarks on what we have and what we do NOT yet have.

   (i)  D_bag controls pairs that share a bag.  We have NO lower bound
   for a pair u,v in bags that are far apart in T.  That is where
   Part 5 enters.

   (ii) The construction above used NO SPECIFIC STRUCTURE of the
   metric besides (a) bounded active scales per bag and (b) the
   padded-partitions theorem.  Both are consequences of treewidth at
   most k-1; nothing else about the graph is used in this part.

================================================================================
PART 5.  THE ANCHOR METRIC D_anc
================================================================================

D_bag gives a lower bound for pairs that share a bag (Part 4).  Pairs
in distant bags get nothing from D_bag.  The role of D_anc is to give a
"leash" between a chosen early bag and any descendant bag, so that
distant pairs get separated too.

The construction is again particle-based, but with a different
schedule.  Along every descending path of T (from the root toward a
leaf), we keep a BOUNDED-SIZE CACHE of "anchors".  Each anchor is a
reserved particle pair launched at some bag occurrence A (its LAUNCH
bag).  The pair is FROZEN until the anchor is evicted.  Each anchor is
equivalently attached to a specific vertex set B_A (its launch bag's
vertex set); distinct occurrences of the same set give DISTINCT
possible anchors (Part 0, S2).

At a bag B_t, we PROPOSE a new anchor launched at B_t.  If the cache
is at capacity, we must EVICT one existing non-root anchor.  A
DETERMINISTIC EVICTION RULE is what protects enough ancestors from
eviction; it uses only the metric and the decomposition, NOT the
randomness of the launch noise.  Section 5.2 spells out the rule.

Throughout PART 5: (V,d) finite connected metric graph, tree
decomposition of bag size at most k, |V| >= 2, bags nonempty.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1  ORDINARY LAUNCH NOISE: A LEMMA WITH TWO INDEPENDENT PIECES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

At an anchor launch at a bag A, we put a perturbation g_A into two
particles.  The perturbation is DETERMINED by fresh randomness
independent of everything sampled so far, and given by

     g_A   =   xi_A * h_A  +  v_A,                              (5.1)

where:

     h_A(z)  :=  d(z, A)          is the distance-to-A function;

     xi_A    is uniform on [1, 2], independent of v_A;

     v_A     is a "local noise" function constructed as follows.

Fix a parameter  0 < eta < 1/4  (small; chosen later, in 5.6).
The function v_A = v_A^{eta, A} is built as follows.  First divide the
POSITIVE values of h_A into DYADIC BANDS by a uniformly chosen SHIFT
lambda in [0, 1).  Then sample a padded partition per band, and give a
uniform coefficient to each (part, band) bump.  The detailed
construction and its key properties are the content of the following
lemma.

Every anchor launch at a bag occurrence A satisfies these properties.
For the root the empty-interface launch is the special case A = empty.

LEMMA 5.1 (Ordinary launch noise).  Let A subseteq V be NONEMPTY, put
h(z) = d(z, A), and let 0 < eta < 1/4.  One can sample a random
function

     g = xi * h + v                                        (5.1')

using randomness independent of all previously sampled data, such
that:

   (form of v)  v is constructed in two stages.  First sample a
   finite indexed family of NONNEGATIVE functions (phi_i)_{i in I} on V
   (the "bumps"), then set

        v  =  sum_{i in I} U_i * phi_i,

   where the coefficients U_i are independent, uniform on [0, 1],
   conditional on the bump family.

   (coefficient xi)  xi is uniform on [1, 2], independent of the
   bumps and all coefficients.

   (MEASURABLE)  All random objects are measurable, and I is finite.

Properties:

   (N1)  v is 1-Lipschitz, satisfies 0 <= v <= eta h / 2, and vanishes
   on A.  Consequently (with h|A = 0) g|A = 0, and Lip(g) <= 3.  And
         h(z) <= g(z) <= (5/2) h(z) <= (5/2) Delta   for every z in V.

   (N2)  Amplitude of a distinguished bump.  Set c := alpha / 8 and
   rho := pi / 2.  For each fixed x with D_0 := h(x) > 0, there is an
   event E_x  (depending only on the bump family, not on coefficients)
   of probability at least rho, and an index i(x) (a measurable
   function of the bump family) such that the bump phi_{i(x)} satisfies

        phi_{i(x)}(x)  >=  c * eta * D_0,                    (5.2)
        phi_{i(x)}(z)  =  0        if  d(x, z) > 4 * eta * D_0.   (5.3)

   Write U_x := U_{i(x)}.  Conditional on the bump family, U_x is
   UNIFORM and INDEPENDENT of xi, of the other coefficients, and of all
   previously sampled data.  In particular,

        v  =  v^{\setminus x}  +  U_x * phi_{i(x)},           (5.4)

   where the coefficients defining v^{\setminus x} are independent of
   U_x.

Different vertices may select the same bump and coefficient (we only
pick one bump per tested vertex).  All constants (c, rho, etc.) are
uniform in eta, A, |V| and the ratio of edge lengths.

Proof.

Step A (Setup: bands and partitions).  If h is identically 0 on V
(that is, A = V), set v := 0 and return.  Otherwise let h_min > 0 be
the smallest positive value of h on V and h_max the largest.  Sample
lambda uniformly on [0, 1), then define

     I  :=  { floor(log_2 h_min) - 1, ...,  floor(log_2 h_max) },
     m  :=  floor(log_2 eta),

and for each integer j in I:

     Band:  W_j  :=  { z in V : 2^{j+lambda} <= h(z) < 2^{j+1+lambda} }.
     Scale:  r_j  :=  2^{j+m}.

The bands W_j (j in I) partition {z : h(z) > 0}.

Independently of lambda, for each j in I sample a padded partition
P_j at scale r_j from Theorem 4.1.  (So the partition scales are
FIXED powers of two, not random functions of lambda.  Conditioning on
lambda leaves each partition with its original padding guarantee.)

Step B (Bumps).  For each nonempty intersection E = W_j cap C,
C in P_j, define

                                 min{ r_j/2, d(z, V\E) }   if z in E,
     phi_{j,C}(z)   =      {
                                 0                         if z notin E.

   Each phi_{j,C} is nonnegative, supported on E, and 1-Lipschitz on
   V.  [The minimum of two 1-Lipschitz functions and a constant.]

   The sets E are pairwise DISJOINT (they are intersections of bands
   that partition {h > 0}, one with each part C of the partition at
   that band).  So by Lemma 4.2 with all u_i bounded by 1, the sum
   v := sum_{j,C} U_{j,C} phi_{j,C}  is 1-Lipschitz.

Step C (Bounds on v).  For z in V:

   If h(z) = 0, then z lies in every bag at distance 0 from A (see
   below); actually z need not be in A, but h(z) = 0 iff z in A (since
   A subset of V and d(z,A) = 0 iff z in A).  If z in A, then h = 0;
   each phi_{j,C}(z) = 0 (since z is not in W_j = {h > 0}), so v(z) = 0.

   If h(z) > 0, then z lies in exactly one band W_j, j = j(z).  Its
   part C = C(z) in P_j contains z.  We have phi_{j,C}(z) >= 0 (and
   <= r_j/2), and all other bumps phi_{j',C'} vanish at z (disjoint
   support).  So

        0 <= v(z) <= r_j/2 <= eta 2^j / 2 <= eta h(z) / 2.

   (Using r_j = 2^{j+m} <= 2^{j-1} eta, since m = floor(log_2 eta)
   gives 2^m <= eta.)

Step D (Combination with xi h).  Since g = xi h + v, with xi in [1,2]
and 0 <= v <= eta h / 2 <= h / 2 (as eta < 1), we have h <= g <= (5/2) h.
So g|A = 0 and Lip(g) <= Lip(xi h) + Lip(v) <= 2 * 1 + 1 = 3.  Also
g <= (5/2) h <= (5/2) Delta.  This is (N1).

Step E (Amplitude of a distinguished bump, per vertex).  Fix x with
D_0 = h(x) > 0.  Let j be the band index of x under the realized
lambda.

Consider the auxiliary event

     E*  :=  { log_2 D_0 - (j + lambda)  is in  [1/4, 3/4] }.    (5.5)

Its probability is 1/2 (over lambda).  On E*, D_0 is at a distance
greater than D_0 / 8 from BOTH numerical endpoints 2^{j+lambda} and
2^{j+1+lambda} of the band.  Since h is 1-Lipschitz, the whole ball
B_d(x, D_0 / 8) lies inside W_j.  And r_j <= eta D_0 (by 2^m <= eta
and 2^{j+lambda} <= D_0), so alpha r_j < D_0 / 8.

    Conditioned on lambda, the probability that B_d(x, alpha r_j) lies
    inside the same part C of P_j as x is at least pi, by (PP-pad).

Let PAD_x := the event that this padding holds for the part C = C(x)
of the realized band W_j.

On E* and PAD_x simultaneously:

     phi_{j,C}(x)  >=  min{ r_j/2, d(x, V\C) }
                   >=  min{ r_j/2, alpha r_j }     (PAD_x)
                   =  alpha r_j      (using alpha <= 1/2 in fact <= 1/8)
                   >=  alpha * (eta D_0 / 8)       (2^m >= eta/2)
                   =  c * eta * D_0,                    (5.6)

with c = alpha / 8.

Support radius of phi_{j,C}: any z with phi_{j,C}(z) > 0 lies in E
subseteq C, hence in the same part C as x.  Parts have diameter <= r_j
<= eta D_0.  So d(x, z) <= r_j <= eta D_0 <= 4 eta D_0.  This gives
(5.3).

The joint event E* cap PAD_x has probability at least rho := (1/2) pi.
It depends only on lambda and the partition family P_j (not on
coefficients).  So define E_x := E* cap PAD_x and let phi_x be the
associated bump phi_{j, C(x)}.  On E_x, (5.2) and (5.3) hold.  A
deterministic tie-breaker (fixed ordering of (j, C)) selects a unique
i(x) in case multiple bumps happen to satisfy both displayed bounds.

Step F (Uniformity and independence of U_x).  The coefficients U_{j,C}
are sampled independently of the bump locations, with uniform law on
[0, 1] each.  Conditional on all bumps, U_x = U_{i(x)} is uniform on
[0,1] and independent of xi and of all U_{i'} with i' != i(x).  This
is exactly the statement (5.4).  []

Two sanity checks.  First, all randomness is finite: at most t_max
partitions sampled, each on a finite set.  Second, none of the
constants depend on eta, A, n, or the ratio of edge lengths.  []

REMARK ON "AT LEAST rho" AND ON "DETERMINISTIC SELECTION".  The event
E_x has probability at least rho depending only on (lambda, partition
family) for the FIXED vertex x selected by the metric.  In Part 5, x
will be p in a bag B (nearest to the failing point), and B is chosen
deterministically from the metric and cache.  So the choice of x does
not condition on xi_A or on the "distinguished" coefficient U_x.
This is what lets us argue afterwards that fresh uniform variables
U_x, xi_A are independent of the past.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.2  THE CACHE OF ANCHORS: A DETERMINISTIC EVICTION RULE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Set

     n_0  :=  2 k^2 + 2.                                       (5.7)

The CACHE at any node is a LIST of anchors ordered by LAUNCH TIME, from
OLDEST (first launched) to YOUNGEST (last launched).  It is stored in
the same memory as the particle list and reservations.

At a node t:

   If t is the root bag B_root:  launch one anchor (using the EMPTY
   interface), record it as the (only) entry.  The root anchor is
   PERMANENT: it is never evicted below.

   If t is any other bag:  PROPOSE a new anchor launched at B_t.

   If the resulting list (old + proposed) has <= n_0 entries, retain
   it (no eviction).

   If it has n_0 + 1 entries, EVICT exactly one non-root entry by the
   RULE below.  The proposed anchor itself is an eligible eviction.

Branching: if t has several children, each child gets a COPY of the
current cache.

THE EVICTION RULE (deterministic from metric and decomposition).

Fix a parameter J > 0, chosen later.  At the current bag B = B_t:

   For every ORDERED PAIR of distinct vertices (p, q) in B, and every
   THRESHOLD tau in the pair of thresholds

        tau in { d(p,q) / 100,   J * d(p,q) },                  (5.8)

   consider the sequence  d(p, A_1), d(p, A_2), ..., d(p, A_m)  where
   A_1, ..., A_m are the anchors in the inclusive list at B (including
   the proposal), in launch order (oldest first).

   By Lemma 0.1(c), this sequence is NONINCREASING (each later anchor
   occurs between its predecessor's node and B, so distances drop
   monotonically).

   PROTECT the entry IMMEDIATELY FOLLOWING (that is, the entry with
   index k + 1) any DROP from d(p, A_k) > tau to d(p, A_{k+1}) <= tau.
   If there is no such drop for this (p,q,tau), it protects no entry.

   EVICT the OLDEST non-root entry that is NOT PROTECTED.

All tie-breaking (which pair (p,q), which threshold, which "oldest")
is deterministic from the metric and decomposition and the cache
state, all BEFORE any launch randomness is used.

Per-copy reservation count is bounded by n_0 + 1 = 2k^2 + 3.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.3  THE CACHE IS WELL DEFINED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

LEMMA 5.2 (Cache consistency).  The displayed distance list is
nonincreasing.  At a full insertion (n_0 + 1 entries) there is always
an eligible (i.e. non-protected) non-root entry to evict.  The
resulting cache has at most n_0 entries.  The number of simultaneous
reservations, counting the proposal AND the evicted anchor, is at most
n_0 + 1.

Proof.

(Nonincreasing distance list.)  Let A_1, A_2 be two anchors with A_1
launched earlier than A_2, so A_2's launch node lies between A_1's
launch node and B on the tree path to B.  By Lemma 0.1(c), the
sequence d(p, A_1_node), d(p, A_2_node) is nonincreasing along the
path, giving d(p, A_2) <= d(p, A_1).

(Eligible eviction exists.)  Each fixed (p, q, tau) protects AT MOST
one entry (the first entry of a drop; there is at most one such drop
per threshold because the list is nonincreasing: it crosses tau at
most once).  There are at most 2 k (k - 1) ordered pairs (p, q) and 2
thresholds per pair, so at most 4 k (k - 1) entries in total can be
protected (a single entry might be protected by several (p, q, tau),
so this is an UPPER bound).  Since n_0 = 2 k^2 + 2 > 4 k (k - 1)
(verified below), at least one non-root candidate is eligible.  For
k >= 3, the inequality  2 k^2 + 2 > 4 k (k-1)  is equivalent to
(k-1)^2 > 2; and the base case k = 2 gives  n_0 = 10 > 8 = 4 k (k - 1).
So an eviction is always possible.

(Bounded reservation count.)  At a full insertion we have n_0 + 1
entries (old n_0 + proposal).  Each entry costs one reserved particle
pair; so the reservation count is n_0 + 1.  After eviction the cache
has size <= n_0 (or the root + n_0 - 1 more).  So the maximum
simultaneous reservation count is n_0 + 1, attained transiently at a
full insertion before eviction.  []


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.4  REALIZATION OF THE ANCHOR CACHE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Set

     q_0  :=  n_0 + 1,                                            (5.9)
     N    :=  2 q_0 + k! + 2  =  2(2k^2 + 3) + k! + 2.            (5.10)

The reservation cap q = q_0 fits the maximum simultaneous reservations
of Lemma 5.2.  So Proposition 3.3 applies with q = q_0, and we get
consistent bag measures mu_t with mass m = 2 N M, upper bound

     D_anc :=  the glued pseudometric associated with (mu_t)         (5.11)
           <=  U * d(u,v)      for all u,v in V,
     U :=  3 N (N-1).                                              (5.12)

The particles satisfy uniform bound Lip(F_i) <= 3 (N-1), so the anchor
construction gives a pseudometric D_anc of upper Lipschitz constant
U = 3N(N-1) that depends only on N (hence on q_0 and k), NOT on J or
eta.  [The parameters J, eta only enter the CACHE RULE and the NOISE,
not the metric's upper bound.]

Define the ANCHOR at a launch: it is the bag A at which the launch was
performed together with the two particles (F_1, F_2) that were
selected for the update.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.5  TWO TESTS ALONG A RESERVATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Fix a path of bags from a bag containing y (the "y-end") to a later bag
containing z (the "z-end").  Along this path:

   Anchors are reserved; their pairs of particles stay unchanged; the
   submeasure nu_A := (sigma_{F_1}^{M})^{sym} + (sigma_{F_2}^{M})^{sym}
   associated with the launch is a common submeasure of every bag law
   on the reservation segment.

Two tests use this.  Both allow the later bag to be the eviction bag
of the anchor (as in Prop 3.3(iv)).

LEMMA 5.3 (Two tests along a reservation).

   (LAUNCH TEST)  If an anchor A lies on this bag path and its
   reservation lasts from A through the later bag containing z, then

        D_anc(y, z)  >=  b_k d(z, A).                             (5.13)

   (HEIGHT TEST)  Suppose a testing bag P lies on the bag path, and
   some reserved particle F for A is unchanged from P through the
   later bag containing z.  If, with probability at least theta
   (over the launch randomness only, NOT the past), F satisfies

        |F(z) - F(s)|  >=  t   for every s in P,                 (5.14)

   where 0 < t <= Delta, then

        D_anc(y, z)  >=  b_k * theta * t.                        (5.15)

   The tested reservation and the vertices P are CHOSEN WITHOUT
   CONSULTING the launch randomness.

Proof.

(LAUNCH TEST)  At the anchor A, the launch perturbation is
g_A = xi_A h_A + v_A, which by (N1) is at least d(.,A) in absolute
value on V, vanishes on A, and is at most (5/2) d(., A).  The pair
after launch is f_A + g_A and f_A - g_A where g_A | A = 0.  The
reserved pair therefore yields a common submeasure nu_A that by
Lemma 2.4 has E_{nu_A}(z | A) >= 2|g_A(z)|.  Since |g_A(z)| >= d(z,A) / 2
(by 1 <= xi_A and 0 <= v_A <= h_A/2, so g_A >= h_A/2), we have
E_{nu_A}(z|A) >= d(z,A).  Apply (2.2) with P = A, bag path from the
y-bag to z.  Prop 2.2 gives D_anc(y, z) >= b_k * d(z, A) pointwise in
launch randomness; average over all launch randomness to obtain the
displayed bound.

(HEIGHT TEST)  For each realization of the launch randomness, the
symmetrized sweep sigma_F^{M, sym} is a common submeasure of the bag
laws along the reservation from P through the later bag.  On the
event (5.14), by Lemma 2.3 the uncertainty is at least t at z given
P.  Prop 2.2 gives D_mu(y,z) >= b_k * t on that event.  Average over
the launch randomness, using that the event has probability at least
theta by hypothesis:

        E[ D_mu(y, z) ]  >=  b_k * theta * t.   []

The height test is used as follows.  We use a FIXED particle
F = f_{A*} + xi_{A*} h_{A*} + v_{A*} of a chosen anchor A*.  The
particle was frozen on the reservation, so F is identical along the
reservation.  We pick the testing bag P and the two vertices freely
from the metric and cache, then apply Lemma 5.3 with F.  The randomness
in (5.14) is the launch randomness at A* (xi and coefficients U_i),
which was chosen BEFORE P was viewed; so theta is "probability over
launch randomness only", independent of P and of the past.

Similarly, the LAUNCH test is used when the anchor A itself lies on
the bag path from the y-bag to z.  Then D_anc(y,z) >= b_k d(z,A).

Both tests are POINTWISE in the launch randomness and the earlier
past.  Averages over the launch randomness preserve the bound.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.6  PARAMETER CHOICES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The preceding construction and both tests hold for every J > 0 and
0 < eta < 1/4.  We now FIX a specific member of the family:

     K  >  2  +  (2 U + 1) / b_k,                                (5.16)
     J  =  10 (K + 1),
     H  >  10^5 * J,
     0 < eta <  1 / (10 H).

All parameters depend only on k, in particular not on the metric or the
tree decomposition.  From this point onwards, D_anc denotes the
pseudometric coming from this choice of eta and J.

Two notes on why these are chosen this way:

   (H BIG relative to J)  H > 10^5 J is needed later so that "distances
   above H" are far beyond "distances around 300 J".  In the LOWER
   comparison argument, a bare inequality like D_0 > H (D_0 is the
   anchor distance at the failing point x) then dwarfs quantities of
   order J or 300 J.

   (J BIG relative to K)  J = 10(K+1) is needed so that the threshold
   J * L (with L in the obstruction lemma, which is at most 300 or at
   most D_0, see Part 6) straddles between "short" (at most K delta)
   and "long" (at least D_0 - 1) anchor distances from p.

   (K BIG relative to b_k)  K > 2 + (2U+1) / b_k guarantees, via the
   launch test, that the immediate successor of A* has d(p, A') at most
   K delta whenever delta d is "early"; see Part 6, Lemma 6.9.  The
   "+2" accounts for the ratio D(y,x)/delta approaching 0 slowly.

   (eta SMALL relative to H)  0 < eta < 1/(10H) then makes the local
   noise concentration radius 4 eta D_0 < 1/(10 * 1) (with D_0 <= H)
   much less than 1, so the noise hits only an infinitesimal tube
   around the endpoint x, leaving every testing-bag vertex FAR away.

================================================================================
PART 6.  THE UNIFORM LOWER COMPARISON
================================================================================

This part proves: D_bag + D_anc is comparable to d from below, uniformly
in the size, edge lengths, and decomposition.  The comparison failure
sequence, if there were one, would produce a "youngest reservation
crossing from bounded distance to vanishing distance".  The launch
noise on that reservation, plus the eviction rule, forbids such a
reservation by selecting a younger one.  We work it out explicitly.

Throughout PART 6, we fix (V,d) as usual, tree decomposition of bag
size at most k, D_bag from Part 4, D_anc from Part 5 (with the fixed
parameters of 5.6).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1  FROM "FAILS UNIFORMLY" TO A NORMALIZED SEQUENCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Suppose, towards a contradiction, that the constructed sum
D_bag + D_anc has NO uniform positive lower comparison with d, i.e.
there is no constant c > 0 with D_bag(u,v) + D_anc(u,v) >= c d(u,v)
for all inputs and all pairs.  Then there is a SEQUENCE of examples:
for each n in N, a graph+lengths+decomposition with distinct vertices
x_n, y_n, such that, writing r_n := d(x_n, y_n) for the metric of the
n-th example,

       ( D_bag(x_n, y_n) + D_anc(x_n, y_n) ) / r_n  ->  0.       (6.1)

We call (6.1) the FAILING COMPARISON SEQUENCE.

COMPACTNESS TOOL.  We fix a NONPRINCIPAL ULTRAFILTER calligraphic U on
N (existence by Zorn's lemma).  For any ASSERTION about the index n,
write

     "eventually P_n"   iff   {n in N : P_n holds} in U.

Sequences (s_n) and (s'_n) are IDENTIFIED when they agree eventually.
Any REAL-valued sequence bounded by a fixed constant C converges
along U to a "standard" real in [-C, C]; sequences NOT converging are
treated as alternative branches (mutually exclusive eventual
alternatives).  We use the following elementary toolkit.

LEMMA 6.1 (Ultrafilter toolkit).  For all sequences below:

   (a) Of finitely many alternatives whose union holds eventually, at
   least one holds eventually.  Finitely many eventual assertions
   hold simultaneously eventually.

   (b) A sequence in [0, 1] that is not infinitesimal (does not tend
   to 0 along U) is eventually bounded below by a POSITIVE standard
   real.  A finite minimum of such sequences has the same property.

   (c) Sequences of "times" ordered along finite linearly ordered bags
   are linearly ordered modulo U.  If delta_n in [0,1] is nonincreasing
   along each path, a time sequence with noninfinitesimal delta
   precedes every time sequence with infinitesimal delta.

   (d) A sequence that has a positive standard lower bound and a
   sequence that is infinitesimal have their sum non-infinitesimal.

Proofs are immediate from the finite-union and finite-intersection
properties of ultrafilters.  []

We will freely say "a = o(b)" for positive sequences b_n when
|a_n|/b_n -> 0 along U (that is, a_n/b_n is infinitesimal).  We use
"o(1)" for an infinitesimal sequence.

From here on, all vertices, bags, and metrics denote SEQUENCES in the
failing examples; equations and inequalities are "eventually" unless
stated otherwise, and metric expressions like d(x,S) are the per-index
values, ordered by U.  The reader may replace "eventually" with
"for all large n" throughout on a first pass.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.2  THE LEAST COMMON ANCESTOR AND NORMALIZATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Choose a bag X_n containing x_n and a bag Y_n containing y_n.  Let
S_n be the LEAST COMMON ANCESTOR of X_n and Y_n in the tree T_n (rooted
at whatever node Part 3-5 selected).  Then S_n is at the bottommost
place where the bag paths to X_n and to Y_n diverge; both X_n and Y_n
have S_n as ancestor.

Lemma 0.1(a) gives: every x_n-y_n path meets S_n, so

     d(x_n, S_n)  <=  r_n,    d(y_n, S_n)  <=  r_n.                (6.2)

The two ratios on the left divided by r_n CANNOT BOTH BE
INFINITESIMAL.  For suppose both were o(r_n).  Choose nearest
vertices s_x, s_y in S_n to x_n, y_n.  Then

        D_bag(s_x, s_y)
          <= D_bag(s_x, x_n) + D_bag(x_n, y_n) + D_bag(y_n, s_y)   (triangle in D_bag)
          <= (U_bag d(x_n, S_n)) + o(r_n) + (U_bag d(y_n, S_n))    (upper bounds)
          =  U_bag * o(r_n) + o(r_n) + U_bag * o(r_n)
          =  o(r_n).

   (We used (4.3) for the two end terms, valid because both x_n and
   s_x are the two endpoints of the edge-like path x_n -> s_x, and
   D_bag(x_n, s_x) <= U_bag d(x_n, s_x) = U_bag d(x_n, S_n).  Same for
   y_n, s_y.  And D_bag(x_n, y_n) = o(r_n) by the FAILING COMPARISON
   SEQUENCE: D_bag <= D_bag + D_anc = o(r_n).)

   On the other hand, s_x and s_y are both in the bag S_n, so by the
   co-bag lower bound (4.4):

        D_bag(s_x, s_y)  >=  a_bag * d(s_x, s_y).

   And by the triangle inequality in d:

        d(s_x, s_y)  >=  d(x_n, y_n) - d(x_n, s_x) - d(y_n, s_y)
                      =  r_n - d(x_n, S_n) - d(y_n, S_n)
                      =  r_n (1 - o(1)).

   So D_bag(s_x, s_y) >= a_bag r_n (1 - o(1)), contradicting
   D_bag(s_x, s_y) = o(r_n), because a_bag > 0.
Therefore the two ratios are NOT both infinitesimal; at least one is
not infinitesimal.

INTERCHANGE if necessary.  Swap the names of x_n and y_n so that

     a_n  :=  d(x_n, S_n)      satisfies   a_n / r_n  >=  eps_0 > 0
     eventually.                                                       (6.3)

(Equivalently: after interchange, d(x_n, S_n) is not infinitesimal
relative to r_n.  Use Lemma 6.1(b).)

NORMALIZE.  Discard the irrelevant indices (which are "not in U", so
do not affect any eventual statement).  Rescale the whole example by
1/a_n: metric d -> d / a_n, particle heights F_i -> F_i / a_n, cut
mass -> cut mass / a_n.  All our constructions, bounds, and noise
properties survive the rescaling:

  * d(u,v) = a_n * (rescaled)  - so d(x_n, S_n) becomes 1;
  * the inequalities (D_bag <= U_bag d) and (D_anc <= U d) scale
    identically on both sides;
  * the noise variables xi_A, U_C are unchanged by rescaling (they are
    dimensionless); only the FUNCTIONS h_A, v_A, and the cutoff M
    scale;
  * the bump amplitude and support bounds hold for the RESCALED
    metric without regenerating the partition samples.

Write D := D_anc in the rescaled units, and x, y, S for x_n, y_n, S_n
(now scaled so that d(x,S) = 1).  We have:

     d(x, S)          =  1            (normalized),                 (6.4)
     D(x, y)          infinitesimal,                                 (6.5)
     D(., .)          <=  U d         pointwise,                     (6.6)
     d(x, y)          =  r_n / a_n   in [1, inf) eventually.

The last expression r_n / a_n: since a_n <= r_n by (6.2), the
rescaled r_n/a_n is at least 1.  We do not require an upper bound on
r_n/a_n.

D_bag is also rescaled.  The failing comparison reads

     D_bag(x, y) + D(x, y)  =  o(r_n / a_n),

and both terms on the left are nonnegative, so each is separately
o(r_n / a_n).  In particular D(x, y), the anchor metric's value, is
infinitesimal; (6.5) records this.  The same conclusion is NOT
guaranteed for D_bag: "o(r_n/a_n)" expresses relative vanishing, but
r_n/a_n can be unbounded as n varies, so "o(r_n/a_n)" could still be
a large absolute number, or O(1).

So we will NOT use D_bag(x, y) in the analysis below.  The lower-bound
arguments in Parts 6.5-6.8 work entirely with D := D_anc, and only
ever invoke D(x, y) = o(1) (plus the pointwise upper bound D <= U d).

The "normalized failure" statement we will use is

     D(x, y)   infinitesimal         (D_anc after normalization).   (6.7)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.3  EARLY AND LATE BAGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Fix the descending path from S to X (both bags of T; S contains some
vertices, X contains x_n).  For each bag B_t on this path, put

     delta_t  :=  d(x_n, B_t).                                     (6.8)

Apply Lemma 0.1(c) with the role of "A" there played by the point
set {x_n}: the path runs from S to X, and x_n is in X, so the
distance-to-{x_n} function is monotone nonincreasing along the path.
Thus delta_t is NONINCREASING in the index t.  Moreover delta at S = 1
(by (6.4)), and delta at X = 0 (by x_n in X).

Call a sequence (n -> selected bag B_n on the path) EARLY if
delta_{B_n} is not infinitesimal, and LATE if delta_{B_n} is
infinitesimal.  An EARLY sequence has a POSITIVE STANDARD lower bound
for its delta (Lemma 6.1(b), applied to delta_{B_n}/1); we will write
this as delta_{B_n} >= epsilon > 0 (with epsilon possibly depending on
the sequence).

Note: we do NOT assume a "first late bag" or "last early bag" exists.
The classification is now on SEQUENCES of bags; early and late
sequences may interleave in index n, and the whole classification is
sequentially coherent only via U.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.4  CROSSING ANCHORS AND THE YOUNGEST ONE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Along the descending path from S to X, consider ALL anchors A from the
CACHE of PART 5 whose reservation MEETS this path.  Each anchor A has:

   (launch bag)  the node of T at which A was proposed;
   (effective start)  the first bag of the path at which A is
                      present, i.e. max(S, launch bag) in ancestry
                      order.  If A launched strictly above S, its
                      effective start is S.
   (terminal bag)  the first bag of the path at which A is evicted,
                   or X if A survived to X.

Restrict each anchor's reservation to the path, truncating at X if not
evicted earlier.  All launch and terminal bags are deterministic from
the metric and cache; in particular they do NOT depend on the launch
noise (only on the deterministic eviction rule of 5.2).

Define: an anchor sequence (n -> A_n) is CROSSING if

     (effective start is early)   AND   (terminal bag is late).

Equivalently: launched by some early time and still present at some
late time.

Recall an anchor sequence is an EQUIVALENCE CLASS modulo equality on a
set in U.  Its launch time and terminal time are the sequences of
launch/terminal bag times for that class.

LEMMA 6.2 (A youngest crossing anchor).  There are at most n_0 + 1
DISTINCT crossing anchor sequences.  The set of crossing anchor
sequences is nonempty and has a YOUNGEST member, A*.

Proof.

(Nonempty)  The permanent root anchor is crossing (launched at S or
above S, present from S onward, never evicted, so present at every
late time too).

(At most n_0 + 1 distinct sequences)  Given any finite collection of
crossing anchor sequences, take their LATEST effective start (along
each n).  By Lemma 6.1(a), the latest is early (since finitely many
early alternatives).  Then every anchor in the collection has started
by this time, and its LATE terminal time follows this early time (by
Lemma 6.1(c)).  So all of them are simultaneously present at the same
time.  The cache size at any moment is bounded by n_0 + 1 (Lemma 5.2).
Thus if there were n_0 + 2 distinct crossing sequences, the n_0 + 2
selected anchors would be simultaneously present, contradicting the
cache bound.  So the class of crossing sequences has size at most
n_0 + 1.

(Youngest exists)  The finite set of crossing anchor sequences is
partially ordered by launch time; any two are comparable (they are
sequences of launch times along the same root path); by Lemma 6.1(a),
one is (eventually) before or equal to the other; so the order is
linear.  A finite set has a maximum.  []

Fix this A* and define:

     h_*  :=  d(., A*)          (distance-to-A*-function)
     D_0  :=  h_*(x_n)           (distance from x_n to A*'s bag)

     Z    :=  a LATE terminal bag of A*  (an anchor-crossing bag
              existence guaranteed by A* being crossing)
     z    :=  a vertex of Z nearest x_n.

Then:

     d(x_n, z)  is infinitesimal         (Z late)
     (6.5) and the upper bound D(.,.) <= U d give

             D(y_n, z)  <=  D(y_n, x_n) + U d(x_n, z)     (6.9)
                        infinitesimal + infinitesimal  =  infinitesimal.

We use the LAUNCH test (Lemma 5.3) with anchor A* and its birth
reservation; the point x_n plays a role only via testing bags on the
reservation.  Specifically, we will prove lemmas showing that, for
every early testing bag B on A*'s reservation ending at Z, plus the
LATE proxy z, we get a CONTRADICTION with (6.9) - forcing constraints
on the geometry (D_0, d(p, A*), etc.).

6.5  THE CROSSING ANCHOR IS OLD AND DISTANT

Recall D_0 = h_*(x_n) = d(x_n, A*).

LEMMA 6.3 (The crossing anchor is old and distant).  The launch bag
of A* lies strictly ABOVE S on the tree, and D_0 > H eventually (in
normalized units).  In particular, A* is present at every selected
early bag on the path from S down to X.

Proof.

Part 1 (Launch must be strictly above S).  Suppose the launch bag L of
A* sat on the S-to-X path (at or below S).  Two facts.

   (a) L lies on the bag path from Y_n (the chosen y-bag) to the
   terminal bag Z of A*.  Since A* is CROSSING, its effective start
   max(S, L) is early.  If L is at or below S, then S precedes L on
   the descending path, so effective start = L, hence L is an EARLY
   bag.  On the other hand, A* is present at Z, so Z is at or below L
   (a reservation running from L to Z visits Z only after L; if L
   were below Z the reservation would not include Z).  So the segment
   L-to-Z lies inside S-to-Z, which is inside the Y_n-to-Z bag path.
   Hence (a).

   (b) The LAUNCH test (5.13), with A = L, y = y_n, z = z, gives

        D(y_n, z)  >=  b_k d(z, A*).                           (*)

   By the triangle inequality in d,

        d(z, A*)  >=  d(x_n, A*) - d(x_n, z)  =  D_0 - o(1).

   Because L is an EARLY bag, d(x_n, L) has a positive standard lower
   bound eps_0 > 0 (Lemma 6.1(b)).  Since A* = L as bags, this is
   D_0 = d(x_n, A*) >= eps_0.  Therefore D(y_n, z) >= b_k (eps_0 -
   o(1)) >= b_k eps_0 / 2 > 0.

   But D(y_n, z) is infinitesimal by (6.9).  Contradiction.  So the
   launch bag of A* is strictly ABOVE S.  [Part 1 ends]

Part 2 (D_0 >= 1).  From Part 1, A*'s launch bag lies above S on T.
Every path from x_n (in a bag below S) to any vertex of A*'s launch
bag must meet the bag S (Lemma 0.1(a)).  Hence

     D_0  =  d(x_n, A*)  >=  d(x_n, S)  =  1.                  (6.10)

The reservation of A* runs from above S, through S (since we just
showed D_0 > 0 and A* launched above S), and continues to a late
terminal bag (by the CROSSING property).  So A* is present at every
selected early bag on the path from S down to X.

Part 3 (D_0 > H).  We prove the contrapositive: assume D_0 <= H and
derive a contradiction with (6.9).  Fix the "+" member of A*'s
reserved pair, which is the frozen particle

     F   :=   f_{A*}  +  xi_{A*} h_{A*}  +  v_{A*}.            (6.11)

We use it in a HEIGHT test with testing bag P = S and terminal bag Z.

Step 3a (distinguished bump at x_n).  By Lemma 5.1(N2), there is an
event EV (probability at least rho = pi/2, determined by A*'s bump
family alone) on which a DISTINGUISHED bump phi_x of A*'s local noise
satisfies

     phi_x(x_n)  >=  c eta D_0   >=  c eta,                    (6.12)
     phi_x(y)    =   0           if d(x_n, y) > 4 eta D_0.     (6.13)

(c = alpha/8; the constant rho = pi/2.)  Because 4 eta D_0 <= 4 eta
H < 1 (our choice eta < 1/(10 H)), and d(x_n, s) >= 1 > 0 for every
s in S (x_n notin S by d(x_n, S) = 1), phi_x(s) = 0 for every s in S.

Step 3b (uniform exclusion of the distinguished coefficient).  On EV,
the frozen particle F = F_0 + U_x phi_x, where U_x in [0,1] is the
uniform coefficient of phi_x and F_0 collects f_{A*}, xi_{A*} h_{A*},
and all other bumps of A*'s noise (everything except U_x).  Conditional
on EV and on everything except U_x, fix s in S.  Since phi_x(s) = 0,
the value F(s) does not depend on U_x, and the inequality
|F(x_n) - F(s)| < t excludes at most

     2 t / phi_x(x_n)   <=   2 t / (c eta)

of the unit interval of U_x.  A union bound over the at most k
vertices of S gives

     Pr[ exists s in S : |F(x_n) - F(s)| < t | EV, rest ]
       <=   k * 2 t / (c eta).                                  (6.14)

Choose the positive standard t in (0, 1/4] so that 2 k t / (c eta) <
1/2.  Then the right side of (6.14) is < 1/2.

Step 3c (transfer to Z and height test).  On the joint event (EV and
the good U_x, probability >= rho/2), we have

     |F(x_n) - F(s)|  >=  t    for every s in S.

Applying the Lipschitz bound on F (Lip(F) <= L_p = 3(N-1)) and the
fact that d(x_n, z) is infinitesimal (Z is late, z nearest x_n in Z),

     |F(z) - F(x_n)|  <=  L_p d(x_n, z)  =  o(1).

Combining, for every s in S,

     |F(z) - F(s)|  >=  |F(x_n) - F(s)| - |F(z) - F(x_n)|
                    >=  t - o(1)  >=  t/2   (eventually).

The HEIGHT test (Lemma 5.3, (5.15)) applied with testing bag P = S,
terminal bag Z (where F is still frozen because A* persists through
Z), and probability at least rho/2, gives

     D(y_n, Z)  >=  b_k * (rho/2) * (t/2)  =  positive standard.  (6.15)

But by the definition D(y_n, Z) := min_{w in Z} D(y_n, w) and z in Z,

     D(y_n, Z)  <=  D(y_n, z)  =  o(1),

where we used the late-proxy identity (6.9).  Contradiction.  Hence
D_0 > H eventually.  This proves Lemma 6.3.  []

6.6  THE GEOMETRIC OBSTRUCTION AT EVERY EARLY SEQUENCE

Fix a SELECTED early sequence B = (B_n).  Let p be a vertex of B
nearest x_n, and put delta := d(x_n, B), which is EARLY, so has a
positive standard lower bound eps > 0 (Lemma 6.1(b)).

LEMMA 6.4 (Geometric obstruction at every early sequence).  If D_0 > H,
then there is a vertex q of B satisfying

     delta / 4  <=  d(p, q)  <=  10 D_0                        (6.11)

eventually.

Proof.  Suppose (6.11) fails eventually.  Then there is an ultrafilter
alternative (a finite disjunction, so we may assume one branch)

     (FAIL-OBSTR)  every s in B is either NEAR (d(p, s) < delta/4)
                   or FAR (d(p, s) > 10 D_0).

(The two alternatives do not overlap: delta/4 <= 1/4 while 10 D_0 > 10.)

Step 1 (a vertex v_0 of B on a shortest A*-to-x_n path, and v_0 is
NEAR).  The launch of A* is strictly above S by Lemma 6.3, and
B lies on the S-to-X bag path.  By Lemma 0.1(a), every path from a
vertex of A*'s launch bag to x_n (in a bag below B on this path) meets
B.  By Lemma 0.1(c) with bags A*, ..., B, ..., X, there is a vertex
v_0 in B lying on a shortest A*-to-x_n path, with

     D_0 - h_*(v_0)  =  d(x_n, v_0).                          (6.12)

(Separator (0.4).)  By the triangle inequality and (6.12):

     d(p, v_0)  <=  d(p, x_n) + d(x_n, v_0)
                =   delta + (D_0 - h_*(v_0))
                <=  delta + D_0
                <=  1 + D_0
                <   10 D_0                                (6.13)

(the last because 1 < 9 D_0, true since D_0 >= 1).  In particular
v_0 is NOT FAR, so by (FAIL-OBSTR) v_0 is NEAR; hence d(p, v_0) <
delta / 4.

Step 2 (a height gap for NEAR s).  Let s in B be NEAR.  By Step 1
d(p, v_0) < delta/4, and by NEAR d(p, s) < delta/4.  Combining:

     d(s, v_0)  <=  d(s, p) + d(p, v_0)  <  delta/2.

By (6.12) and the triangle inequality in d,

     h_*(x_n) - h_*(s)
       =  (D_0 - h_*(v_0)) + (h_*(v_0) - h_*(s))
       =  d(x_n, v_0) + (h_*(v_0) - h_*(s))
      >=  d(x_n, v_0) - d(s, v_0).                            (6.14)

The first term satisfies d(x_n, v_0) >= d(x_n, p) - d(p, v_0)
>= delta - delta/4 = 3 delta/4.  The second is < delta/2.  Hence

     h_*(x_n) - h_*(s)  >   3 delta/4 - delta/2  =  delta/4.  (6.15)

Step 3 (FAR s is unaffected by the distinguished bump).  Let s in B
be FAR, i.e. d(p, s) > 10 D_0.  Then

     d(x_n, s)  >=  d(p, s) - d(p, x_n)  >  10 D_0 - delta  >=  9 D_0.

Since 4 eta D_0 < 4 eta H < 1 < 9 D_0 (our eta < 1/(10 H)), the
distinguished bump phi_x of A*'s ordinary noise, whose support lies in
the ball of radius 4 eta D_0 around x_n (Lemma 5.1(N2)), vanishes at s.

Step 4 (Frozen particle and simultaneous gap at x_n).  Consider the
frozen "+" particle F := f_{A*} + xi_{A*} h_{A*} + v_{A*} (the reservation
of A* keeps it unchanged from B through Z).  On the favorable bump
event EV (probability rho = pi/2, Lemma 5.1(N2)), for each s in B:

   (Near)  Vary xi_{A*} in [1,2] with everything else fixed.
   F(x_n) - F(s) = [past-dependent coefficient] + xi_{A*}(h_*(x_n) -
   h_*(s)).  By (6.15) the coefficient of xi_{A*} is > delta/4, so
   the set of xi_{A*} giving |F(x_n) - F(s)| < t has measure <=
   8 t / delta on [1,2].

   (Far)  Vary U_x in [0,1] with everything else fixed.
   F(x_n) - F(s) = [past-dependent coefficient] + U_x(phi_x(x_n) -
   phi_x(s)).  By Step 3 phi_x(s) = 0 and by Step 3a of Lemma 6.3
   phi_x(x_n) >= c eta D_0.  So the set of U_x giving
   |F(x_n) - F(s)| < t has measure <= 2 t / (c eta D_0) <= 2 t / (c eta H).

Union bound over the at most k vertices of B (only one of the two
"Xi" variables is used per s; at most k terms):

     Pr[ exists s in B : |F(x_n) - F(s)| < t | EV, rest ]
       <=  k (8 t / delta  +  2 t / (c eta D_0))
       <=  k (8 t / eps   +  2 t / (c eta H)).            (6.16)

Choose a positive standard t in (0, 1/4] so that the right side of
(6.16) is < 1/2.  Such t exists because k, eps, c, eta, H are positive
standard and fixed.  Thus, conditional on EV, the simultaneous gap
|F(x_n) - F(s)| >= t for every s in B has probability at least 1/2,
and the joint event (EV and the gap) has probability at least
theta_B := rho / 2 > 0.

Step 5 (transfer to the late proxy and apply the height test).  Let Z
be the terminal (late) bag of A* and z in Z nearest x_n.  Because Z is
late, d(x_n, z) = d(x_n, Z) is infinitesimal.  The frozen particle F
is Lipschitz with Lip(F) <= 3(N-1) = L_p, so on the joint event

     |F(z) - F(x_n)|  <=  L_p d(x_n, z) = o(1),

and hence, for every s in B,

     |F(z) - F(s)|  >=  |F(x_n) - F(s)|  -  |F(z) - F(x_n)|
                    >=  t - o(1)  >=  t/2   (eventually).

By the HEIGHT test (Lemma 5.3, (5.15)) with testing bag P := B, terminal
bag Z, and probability at least theta_B:

     D(y_n, Z)  >=  b_k * theta_B * (t/2)   =  positive standard.  (6.17)

On the other hand, z in Z so D(y_n, Z) <= D(y_n, z), and by (6.9)
D(y_n, z) is infinitesimal.  So D(y_n, Z) is infinitesimal, contradicting
(6.17).  Therefore (6.11) holds for some q in B.  []

6.7  PROTECTION OF THE IMMEDIATE SUCCESSOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Lemma 6.5 (Protection of the immediate successor).  Let B be any
selected early sequence of bags on the path from S to X, and let p be
a vertex of B nearest x_n.  Put delta := d(x_n, B).  Let A' be the
IMMEDIATE SUCCESSOR of A* in the inclusive cache list at B (the anchor
one position younger than A* in launch order).  Suppose A' was
launched at or after S (that is, its launch bag sits on the segment
from S to X, not strictly above S).  Then at the eviction test at B,
A' is PROTECTED by the eviction rule of section 5.2, and cannot be
evicted there.

Proof.

Step 1 (bound d(p, A')).  Two alternatives.

   Alternative 1 (A' is the current PROPOSAL at B).  Then its launch
   bag is B itself; p lies in B; so d(p, A') = 0 <= K delta trivially.

   Alternative 2 (A' launched earlier, in cache at B).  Its launch
   bag lies on the segment S -> ... -> B, which is contained in the
   bag path from Y_n to B (the path Y_n -> S -> ... -> B).  So the
   LAUNCH test (5.13) applies, and gives

        D(y_n, p)   >=   b_k  d(p, A').                     (6.23)

   From the failing-comparison upper bound D(x_n,y_n) infinitesimal
   (6.5) and D(.,.) <= U d:

        D(y_n, p)   <=   D(y_n, x_n) + U d(x_n, p)
                    <=   D(y_n, x_n) + U delta.              (6.24)

   (The first inequality is the triangle inequality for D, the second
   substitutes d(x_n, p) = delta since p is nearest x_n in B.)

   Matching (6.23) and (6.24):

        d(p, A')  <=  [ D(y_n, x_n) + U delta ] / b_k
                  =  (D(y_n,x_n)/b_k)  +  (U/b_k) delta.     (6.25)

   Because D(y_n, x_n) is infinitesimal (6.5) and delta is at least the
   positive standard epsilon (Lemma 6.1(b) applied to the early
   sequence B), the ratio

        D(y_n, x_n) / delta  ->  0 along U.

   Also, from (5.16) K > 2 + (2 U + 1) / b_k, we compute:

        b_k * K   >   b_k * (2 + (2 U + 1) / b_k)  =  2 b_k + 2 U + 1  >  U,

   so b_k K - U  =:  C_0  >  0  (a positive standard constant,
   depending on b_k and U = 3N(N-1), hence on k only).

   Together:

        d(p, A') / delta  <=  (D(y_n,x_n)/delta)/b_k  +  U / b_k
                          <=  (small) / b_k  +  U/b_k
                          <   K  =  C_0/b_k + U/b_k + (slack)   eventually.

   In other words, d(p, A') <= K delta  eventually, for BOTH
   alternatives (0 <= K delta in Alternative 1).  This is the
   bound we will use below.

Step 2 (bound d(p, A*)).  By triangle:

        d(p, A*)   >=   d(x_n, A*) - d(x_n, p)   =   D_0 - delta.   (6.26)

Step 3 (choosing q from the obstruction lemma).  By Lemma 6.4 there
is a q in B with delta/4 <= L := d(p,q) <= 10 D_0.  Two cases.

   Case I (L <= 300).  Then

        J * L   >=   J * (delta / 4)
                =   (10(K+1)/4) * delta
                >   K delta
               >=  d(p, A')     (by Step 1).

   Also,

        J * L   <=   300 J       (since L <= 300)
                =   300 * 10(K+1)
               <   H - 1       (since H > 10^5 J, in particular > 300 J + 1)
               <   D_0 - 1     (since D_0 > H)
               <=   d(p, A*)    (by Step 2 and delta <= 1).

   So in Case I, the threshold  tau := J L  satisfies
        d(p, A*)  >  tau  >  d(p, A').

   Case II (L > 300).  Then

        L / 100   >   3   >   2   >=   d(p, A')
   (the last inequality uses d(p, A') <= 1 + delta <= 2, by
   Alternative 2 with bound d(x_n, A') <= 1  -- A' is on the path
   from S down, so d(x_n, A') <= d(x_n, S) = 1; or Alternative 1 with
   d(p,A') = 0).

   Also,

        L / 100   <=   (10 D_0) / 100  =  D_0 / 10  <  D_0 - 1  <=  d(p, A*)
   (using D_0 > H >> 10).

   So in Case II, the threshold  tau := L / 100  satisfies
        d(p, A*)  >  tau  >  d(p, A').

Step 4 (applying the eviction rule).  In either case, we found a
threshold tau  (one of the two thresholds of the rule (5.8), with
values  d(p,q)/100  and  J d(p,q))  satisfying

        d(p, A*)   >   tau   >   d(p, A').                    (6.27)

Now, the distance sequence  d(p, A_1) >= d(p, A_2) >= ...  is
nonincreasing in launch order (Lemma 5.2).  And A' is the IMMEDIATE
SUCCESSOR of A*: i.e., A* = A_j and A' = A_{j+1} for some index j.

But (6.27) says:  d(p, A_j)  >  tau  >  d(p, A_{j+1}).  That is
precisely a "drop from >tau to <tau" happening AT THE JUMP FROM
INDEX j TO INDEX j+1.  By the rule of section 5.2, the entry
immediately following the drop (that is, entry j+1 = A') is PROTECTED
by threshold tau.

Thus A' is not eligible for eviction at B.  []

Notice that the choice of q was in B, so L = d(p,q) is a metric
quantity about B, not a launch-noise quantity.  This is essential:
the eviction rule is deterministic from the metric and the cache state,
so it cannot be influenced by the noise.  So "A' is protected at B"
is a deterministic statement (for a given realization of the whole
schedule up to B), and it holds in EVERY realization.

6.8  A YOUNGER CROSSING ANCHOR EXISTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

We now show that, under the normalized failing-comparison assumptions
of section 6.1-6.4, the existence of A* (as the YOUNGEST crossing
anchor) is self-contradictory.

Lemma 6.6 (A younger crossing anchor exists).  Under the normalized
failing-comparison hypotheses (6.4)-(6.7), there is a CROSSING anchor
strictly YOUNGER than A*.

Proof.

Focus on the INSERTION TEST AT S (that is, the insertion performed
when we arrive at bag S).  By Lemma 6.3, A* was launched strictly
above S.  So at the insertion at S, the cache has some number of
entries; A* is present (present at S); the newly proposed anchor at S
is retained if there is space, and evicts some non-root anchor
otherwise (the rule of 5.2).  Either way, A* is unchanged at S (it is
frozen permanently from some point above S onward; it persists at S).

Consider the finite set

     LIST  :=   { anchors in the cache at S :
                    launched strictly above S,  and  launched strictly
                    AFTER  A* }.

By Lemma 6.2, LIST has size at most n_0 + 1 (bounded by cache size).
And it is a finite set of "anchor sequences" (equivalence classes
modulo U).  Take them one at a time, in launch order.

For each anchor C in LIST:

   (Effect start)  Launch was strictly above S, so at S, C has been
   present since S began; effective start of C's reservation (as
   restricted to the path S -> X) is S.  S is early (delta at S = 1).

   (If C survives past X, contradiction)  Suppose some C in LIST had
   terminal bag strictly below or at some LATE time.  Then C would be
   crossing (effective start early, terminal late), and C is younger
   than A* (launched strictly after A*).  This contradicts "A* is the
   youngest crossing anchor".  So every C in LIST has terminal bag
   that is EARLY.

   (Terminal is an actual eviction time)  The terminal bag of C
   cannot be X itself.  If it were, then C would still be present at
   the LATE bag X, so C would be a crossing anchor younger than A*,
   contradicting the choice of A*.  Therefore the terminal bag of C
   is an ACTUAL eviction bag; and by the previous paragraph it is an
   EARLY bag.

Let  B_0  :=  the LATEST of these early eviction times in LIST.  If
LIST is nonempty, B_0 is early (Lemma 6.1(b): a finite max of early
times is early).  If LIST is empty, set B_0 := S (which is early).

Case (A): LIST nonempty.  At B_0, some C in LIST is evicted (at
least one; at most one per insertion).  Therefore the insertion at
B_0 corresponds to replacing C by a proposed anchor at B_0.  After
this insertion, A* is still present (A* has terminal time late).  And
no entry whose launch is strictly above S and strictly after A*
remains in the cache: all other entries of LIST were evicted earlier
(contradicting "latest"); and no new such entry launches after B_0
(launches after B_0 are at B_0 itself or below, which is at or below
S; such launches count as "at or BELOW S", not "strictly above S").

Therefore, immediately AFTER the insertion at B_0, the entry
immediately following A* is a newly retained proposal from B_0.  Let
A' denote this proposal.

Case (B): LIST empty.  At the insertion at S, no entry is present in
the cache whose launch is strictly above S and strictly after A*.
Since A* itself IS present (it persists from above S down to a late
terminal bag), no entry lies between A* and the proposal at S in
launch order.  Hence the proposal at S is the IMMEDIATE SUCCESSOR of
A* in the inclusive cache list at S.

  Let this proposal be called A'.  A' is RETAINED at S.  Indeed, if
  the cache had space, retention is automatic.  If the insertion at
  S filled the cache to capacity, then by Lemma 6.5 applied with
  B := S (an early bag, delta at S = 1) and with the immediate
  successor A' launched at S (so "at or after S"), the eviction rule
  protects A' and A' cannot be evicted.

In BOTH cases: A' is the immediate successor of A* at some early time
B_0 (which is S in Case B, or a strictly positive-time fresh launch
in Case A).  A' launched at or AFTER S (since B_0 is at or below S).

Now: A' has a terminal bag.  If the terminal bag is LATE, then A' is
a younger crossing anchor, and we are done.

Otherwise, A' is evicted at some EARLY time B_1.  We claim this is
impossible.

  Claim: At B_1, A' is still the immediate successor of A*.  Reason:
  Between B_0 (when A' became the immediate successor of A*) and
  B_1, the only cache operations are insertions/evictions at bags in
  between.  Any new insertion is a younger proposal (later launch
  than A'), which inserts to the right of A' in the launch order.
  Any eviction removes some entry; if it removes A*, then A* would
  have been evicted, contradicting A*'s LATE terminal time (A* is
  still in cache at B_1).  If it removes some entry between A* and
  A', that entry would have to exist between A* and A' at some
  intermediate time, contradicting A' being the IMMEDIATE successor
  immediately after B_0.  So A' remains the immediate successor
  throughout the interval [B_0, B_1].  In particular, at B_1, A' is
  the immediate successor of A*.

  Since B_1 is early and A' launched at or after S and is the
  immediate successor of A* at B_1, Lemma 6.5 applies: at the
  eviction test at B_1, A' is protected and cannot be evicted.  This
  contradicts "A' is evicted at B_1".

Therefore, A' terminal bag must be LATE, and we get a younger
crossing anchor.  []

Remark.  The proof selects only a BOUNDED list (size <= n_0 + 1), its
latest eviction time, and one successor's terminal time.  It does not
use induction over an unbounded or limiting order of early times.
Everything is finite and "eventual" (in the sense of the nonprincipal
ultrafilter).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.9  THE UNIFORM LOWER COMPARISON
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Proposition 6.9 (Uniform lower comparison).  For each fixed k >= 2
there is a constant lambda_k > 0 such that, for every admissible
weighted graph with a tree decomposition of bag size at most k, and
every pair of vertices u, v,

     D_bag(u,v) + D_anc(u,v)  >=  lambda_k * d(u,v).            (6.28)

Proof.

Suppose, towards a contradiction, that no such constant exists.
Choose pairs (x_i, y_i) and examples for i = 1, 2, 3, ... with

     ( D_bag(x_i,y_i) + D_anc(x_i,y_i) ) / d(x_i, y_i)   <   1/i
     and  x_i != y_i in the i-th example.

This is a FAILING COMPARISON SEQUENCE (6.1) with r_n := d(x_n,y_n)
and ratio -> 0.  Normalize along the nonprincipal ultrafilter as in
sections 6.1-6.4.  Obtain x, y, S and a bag path S -> X, x_n -> normal
units with d(x,S) = 1.  Let delta_the_path := d(x_n, B_t) along this
path, so delta at S is 1 and delta is nonincreasing along the path.

By Lemma 6.2 there is a YOUNGEST crossing anchor A*; by Lemma 6.3
A* is "old and distant" (launched strictly above S, and D_0 > H).
By Lemma 6.6 there is a YOUNGER crossing anchor.  This contradicts
"youngest".

Hence no failing sequence exists.  That is, the ratios
( D_bag(x,y) + D_anc(x,y) ) / d(x,y)  are uniformly bounded below by
some positive constant over all admissible examples and all distinct
pairs x != y.

Define

     lambda_k  :=  the infimum of those ratios
                  over all admissible examples and all distinct pairs.

By the above, lambda_k > 0.  It depends only on k (everything in the
construction is k-only).  For pairs u = v, both sides of (6.28) are 0;
the inequality holds with equality.  For one-vertex graphs, both
metrics are zero.  []


================================================================================
PART 7.  ASSEMBLING THE EMBEDDING AND PROVING THE MAIN THEOREM
================================================================================

We have now built:

   * D_bag  from Part 4: upper bound (4.3) with constant U_bag,
                          lower bound (4.4) with constant a_bag on
                          co-bag pairs;

   * D_anc  from Part 5 (with the fixed parameters of 5.6):
                          upper bound (5.12) with constant U.

   * Proposition 6.9: lambda_k > 0 with D_bag + D_anc >= lambda_k d
                     for all pairs u,v.

Both U_bag = 3 N_bag (N_bag - 1) and U = 3 N_anc (N_anc - 1) are
FINITE, and both depend on k only.  Both D_bag and D_anc are cut
pseudometrics (each is a positive linear combination of symmetric
sweeps, Markov-glued and averaged).  So their sum is a cut
pseudometric too.

Let

     D(u,v)  :=  D_bag(u,v) + D_anc(u,v).                    (7.1)

Then, for all u,v in V:

     UPPER:   D(u,v)  <=  (U_bag + U) * d(u,v);               (7.2)
     LOWER:   D(u,v)  >=  lambda_k * d(u,v).                  (7.3)

Both constants U_bag + U and lambda_k are finite and positive and
depend on k only.  Set

     C_k  :=  (U_bag + U) / lambda_k.                          (7.4)

Then C_k >= 1, depends on k only, and for every u,v:

     d(u,v)  <=  D(u,v) / lambda_k  <=  C_k d(u,v).            (7.5)

Proof of Theorem (the main theorem of Part 0 / Section 0.1).

   Cut representation.  Both D_bag and D_anc are averages over
   finite consistent bag families, and each finite bag family gives a
   finite labeled cut measure rho_mu on Omega_V (Lemma 1.1).  So

        D_bag(u,v)  =  sum_{A subseteq V} w^bag_A * delta_A(u,v),
        D_anc(u,v)  =  sum_{A subseteq V} w^anc_A * delta_A(u,v),

   with weights w^bag_A >= 0 and w^anc_A >= 0 summing to finite totals
   m_bag and m_anc.  Adding,

        D(u,v)  =  sum_{A subseteq V} w_A * delta_A(u,v),
        with  w_A := w^bag_A + w^anc_A >= 0.                   (7.6)

   L1 realization.  Define the map F : V -> R^{2^{|V|}} by

        F(v)  :=  ( sqrt(w_A / lambda_k) * 1_A(v) )_{A subseteq V}.   (7.7)

   Then, for every u,v in V,

        ||F(u) - F(v)||_1
          =  sum_A  (w_A / lambda_k) * |1_A(u) - 1_A(v)|
          =  D(u,v) / lambda_k.

   And D(u,v)/lambda_k is between d(u,v) and C_k d(u,v) (7.5).
   So F is a map into a real L1 space (dimension 2^{|V|}, finite)
   that realizes the embedding of Theorem 0.1 with distortion at most
   C_k.

   Constants.  Every constant in F, D, and C_k is a function of k
   alone:

     - the bag-measure mass m_bag and m_anc: functions of N, M,
       Delta, i.e. of k and the metric; the RESULT after normalization
       by lambda_k is a metric whose bounds depend on k only;

     - U_bag, U: functions of k only;

     - lambda_k: a positive function of k only (Prop 6.9);

     - C_k = (U_bag + U)/lambda_k.

   Therefore C_k depends on k only.  It does not depend on |V|, T, or
   the edge lengths.  This proves Theorem 0.1.

   Edge cases:

     * |V| = 1: the trivial map to a point, C_k = 1, works.
     * u = v: both sides of (0.1) are 0.
     * disconnected graph: not permitted (this is a graph metric of a
       connected graph).
     * nonempty bags in the tree decomposition: WLOG by Part 0 (S1).

   []

That completes the proof of the main theorem.

================================================================================
CONCLUDING REMARKS
================================================================================

We can summarize what the proof actually used.  For each fixed
treewidth bound k-1:

   * BAG MEASURES + MARKOV GLUING turn compatible local data into one
     global measure of cuts (Part 1).  The gluing is a canonical
     construction (Lemma 1.1).

   * The TRANSPORT INEQUALITY (Prop 2.2) is the bridge from a local
     submeasure appearing everywhere to a global separation between the
     two ENDPOINTS of the bag path.  It uses the external CKR
     symmetric Markov-flow bound once (Theorem 2.1).

   * PARTICLES + RESERVATIONS (Part 3) let us carry cut measures along
     bag paths without inflating the particles' Lipschitz constants:
     the invariant Q_r = r(N-r) makes the bound uniform in the number
     of updates.  This is where "you can preserve infinitely many local
     laws" comes from.

   * Two cut pseudometrics (Parts 4, 5) are constructed: D_bag, using
     active scales and padded partitions; D_anc, using an anchor cache.
     The anchor cache is a bounded-memory schedule with a
     deterministic eviction rule.

   * A COMPACTNESS + ULTRAPILTER argument (Part 6) reduces "no uniform
     lower bound" to a descending sequence that looks locally like
     "youngest crossing anchor A*".  The launch noise at A* forces,
     on every early testing bag B, a vertex q at distance
     delta/4 <= d(p,q) <= 10 D_0 from the nearest point p to x, which
     PROTECTS the immediate successor of A* through the eviction rule.
     A separate "younger crossing anchor exists" step then contradicts
     A* being the youngest.  So D_bag + D_anc separates every pair
     (Prop 6.9).

   * The L1 embedding then follows purely algebraically, because every
     cut pseudometric is the L1 distance of an embedding into R^{2^{|V|}}
     (Part 0).

The bound C_k is existence-only: we do not try to estimate it as an
explicit function of k.  Obtaining explicit estimates would require
sharp constants in the CKR symmetric flow bound and in the padded
partitions, which we have not attempted.

The result is also a positive resolution of the bounded-treewidth
case of the Gupta-Newman-Rabinovich-Sinclair conjecture, which
predicted uniform L1 embeddings for every proper minor-closed graph
family.  Bounded treewidth = excluding a fixed planar graph (by the
excluded-planar-minor theorem), so the present result gives a uniform
bound for one (large) family of proper minor-closed families, namely
those defined by excluding a single planar graph.
