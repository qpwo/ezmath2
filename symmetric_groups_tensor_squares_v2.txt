The problem. For a partition lambda of n, let S^lambda denote the
irreducible complex representation of the symmetric group S_n indexed
by lambda. The Kronecker coefficients are
    g(lambda,mu,nu) = dim Hom_{S_n}(S^nu,S^lambda (x) S^mu),
the dimension of the space of S_n-equivariant maps from S^nu to
S^lambda tensor S^mu, where the tensor carries the diagonal S_n
action. A COVERING SQUARE for n is a partition lambda of n satisfying
g(lambda,lambda,nu) > 0 for every partition nu of n. A single
irreducible S^lambda then has a tensor square S^lambda tensor S^lambda
that contains every irreducible S^nu as a direct summand. The tensor
square conjecture for symmetric groups, formulated by Pak, Panova and
Vallejo, asserts that a covering square exists for every n at least
three other than 4 and 9. The ingredients are triple Kronecker
coefficients, not comparisons of Young diagrams. The underlying
problem is "one of the last major open problems" in algebraic
combinatorics (Section 1).

The precise form of the assertion is the following.

    MAIN THEOREM. For every positive integer n other than 2, 4 and 9,
    there exists a partition lambda of n such that g(lambda,lambda,nu)
    is strictly positive for every partition nu of n.

The partition lambda can be chosen to depend only on n, not on the
desired constituent nu. The construction that produces lambda is
explicit and is fixed before any particular nu is considered.

Necessary self-conjugacy. The sign representation on S_n forces a
strong structural condition on any covering lambda. Put nu = (1^n),
the sign partition. Then nu itself equals its own conjugate
nu^t = (n), and S^nu is the sign character of S_n. For a general
partition lambda, vanishing of the Kronecker coefficient
g(lambda,lambda,(1^n)) is equivalent to inequality of lambda and its
conjugate lambda^t. In detail, S^lambda tensor sign is isomorphic to
S^{lambda^t}, so
    g(lambda,lambda,(1^n))
      = dim Hom_{S_n}(S^lambda, S^lambda tensor sign)
      = dim Hom_{S_n}(S^lambda, S^{lambda^t}).
Schur's lemma gives the value 1 when lambda = lambda^t and 0
otherwise. Every covering square therefore comes from a
SELF-CONJUGATE partition, one equal to its own transpose.

For a covering square, positivity of g(lambda,lambda,nu) is equivalent
to nonvanishing of the inner product
    g(lambda,lambda,nu)
       = (1/n!) sum_{rho} chi^lambda(rho)^2 chi^nu(rho) / z_rho,
where chi^lambda(rho) is the character of S^lambda on the conjugacy
class of cycle type rho, and z_rho = prod_j j^{m_j(rho)} m_j(rho)!.
Real-valuedness of symmetric-group characters justifies the square.
This is the form used for the finite character computation of small
degrees, and it also gives every nonvanishing test its meaning.

The related Saxl conjecture. Saxl conjectured in 2012 that, for the
staircase rho_m = (m, m-1, ..., 1) at the triangular degree
N_m = m(m+1)/2, the square S^{rho_m} tensor S^{rho_m} contains every
S^nu for nu of N_m. Only triangular degrees and only the staircase
partition are permitted; all other degrees, and all other candidate
partitions, are excluded.

Why adaptation is delicate. For general n a small staircase placed
inside a larger partition is not enough: the cells added around it
contribute cancelling alternating terms, so the resulting
contractions may vanish identically. Two sufficient tests, BAND and
BALANCE, avoid this by projecting a single alternating tensor of a
fixed self-conjugate lambda and verifying nonvanishing contractions.
Together the two tests cover every target once the candidate
is chosen. Residual intermediate parameters and all small degrees are
settled by two finite recursive enumerations in exact integer
arithmetic.

Partitions. Write lambda |- n for a partition of n. Its transpose

The Specht modules via column alternation. Fix a basis e_1, ..., e_m
of C^m with m at least the number of rows of lambda. For an ordered
block A = (a_1, ..., a_k) of positions, set
    v_A = sum_{pi in S_k} sgn(pi) e_{pi(1)} tensor ... tensor e_{pi(k)},
placed at a_1, ..., a_k in the given order. For a set partition
A = {A_1, ..., A_k} of {1, ..., n}, put v_A = v_{A_1} tensor ... tensor
v_{A_k}. If |A_i| equals the i-th column height of theta, then
C[S_n] v_A is isomorphic to S^theta as S_n-modules. Changing the order
of blocks changes only the overall sign. Two tensors related this way
generate the same submodule, so the construction is robust to
reordering.

Character formula. For partitions lambda, mu, nu of n,
    g(lambda,mu,nu)
       = (1/n!) sum_{rho} chi^lambda(rho) chi^mu(rho) chi^nu(rho)
                / z_rho,
where chi^alpha(rho) is the character of S^alpha on the conjugacy class
of cycle type rho, and z_rho = prod_j j^{m_j(rho)} m_j(rho)!. Symmetric
group characters are real, hence positivity of g(lambda,lambda,nu) is
exactly positivity of the sum without absolute values. This identity
underpins every nonvanishing test.

Littlewood-Richardson rule. For partitions alpha, beta, gamma of
sizes a, b, a+b, the coefficient c^gamma_{alpha,beta} counts
Littlewood-Richardson skew tableaux of shape gamma/alpha with content
beta. Two consequences appear repeatedly:
    (Pieri) c^gamma_{alpha,(t)} > 0 iff gamma/alpha is a horizontal
    strip of size t, and c^gamma_{alpha,(1^t)} > 0 iff gamma/alpha is
    a vertical strip of size t.
    (Rectangle complement) if alpha and beta are complementary shapes
    inside a rectangle gamma = (w^j), then c^gamma_{alpha,beta} = 1.
The induction form: for a finite list A = (a_1, ..., a_k) of sum n,
and a partition nu |- n, the multiplicity of S^nu in
Ind^{S_n}_{S_{a_1} x ... x S_{a_k}} (1 (...) 1) is
    sum_{alpha_1, ..., alpha_k} prod_i c^{beta_i}_{alpha_i, (a_i)},
taken over chains nu = beta_1 >= beta_2 >= ... >= beta_{k+1} = empty
in which beta_i/beta_{i+1} is a horizontal strip of size a_i. The
partition obtained by sorting A into nonincreasing order contributes
multiplicity one.

The candidate partition. Let n be outside {2, 4, 9}. Choose
M = M(n) to be the largest integer with N_M = M(M+1)/2 <= n and
N_M congruent to n mod 2; set r = (n - N_M)/2. For n >= 45 with n odd,
or n >= 66 with n even, M >= 9. Set b = floor(r/2), s = 1 + (r mod 2),
and K = 2M - 1. Maximality of M constrains r through the parity
period of N_M = M(M+1)/2 modulo 2, which cycles odd, odd, even,
even. If M is congruent to 1 or 3 mod 4, then N_{M+1} has the
parity of n, so maximality forces N_{M+1} > n and r <= (M-1)/2;
if M is congruent to 0 or 2 mod 4, then N_{M+3} has the parity
of n, so maximality forces N_{M+3} > n and r <= 3M/2 + 2. In
particular,
r < 2M+5 always. Define the partition L |- n by its row lengths, and,
by self-conjugacy, its column lengths, both equal to
    (M+b+s-1, M+b-1, M-2, M-3, ..., 3, 2^{b+1}, 1^s),
where 2^{b+1} denotes b+1 columns of length two and 1^s denotes s
columns of length one. Direct counting of cells verifies L = L^t.

Two structural features are used repeatedly. The BIG PAIR is the pair
of columns of heights M+b+s-1 and M+b-1; their total size is
2M + 2b + s - 2 = 2M + r - 1 = K + r = T. The STAIRCASE D consists of
the cells (i,j) of L with i, j >= 1 and i + j <= M - 1; as a partition
D is the staircase rho_{M-2} = (M-2, M-3, ..., 2, 1). The BAND is the
complement F = L \ D, of size K + 2r.

The cyclic staircase theorem. For an integer m >= 1 let rho_m =
(m, m-1, ..., 1) and B_m = {(i,j) : i, j >= 1, i + j <= m + 1}.
Let R_m and C_m be the row and column set partitions of B_m, ordered
by increasing j within each row and by increasing i within each
column. Use the ordered alphabet e_1, ..., e_m in both tensor factors
and set
    w_m = v_{R_m} tensor v_{C_m} in S^{rho_m} tensor S^{rho_m},
    W_m = C[S_{B_m}] w_m,
with the diagonal action of S_{B_m}.

    CSA THEOREM. For every integer m >= 1 and every partition
    alpha |- N_m, S^alpha occurs in W_m.

A random vector of the ambient square S^{rho_m} tensor S^{rho_m}
has the whole square in its cyclic span with probability one, so
its orbit contains every constituent. The cyclic staircase theorem
identifies an explicit vector with this property: the tensor w_m
built from the ordered row and column alternations at their
natural interleaving.

The band criterion. Let lambda |- n = |L| have column heights
h_1 >= h_2 >= ... and row lengths w_1 >= w_2 >= ..., with zeros
appended. Suppose integers d, q satisfy
    1 <= d <= 4,   0 <= q <= 8,
    sum_{i=1}^{d} w_i                     >= K,
    d q + 7 + d                           <= K,
    sum_{i=1}^{q} max(0, floor((h_i-d)/2)) >= r.
Then S^lambda occurs in S^L tensor S^L.

The three inequalities have separate jobs. The first reserves
enough row length at the top of the target for a path of length
K. The second confines the path away from the two ends of the
staircase so attachments cannot cancel against endpoint
alternations. The third provides q even pieces of total capacity
2r that feed the two extra attachments.

Reduction to F. Regard L as its set B of cells. Let v_R, v_C be
the alternating block tensors for its rows and columns, and put
    W_L = C[S_B](v_R tensor v_C), a submodule of S^L tensor S^L
by self-conjugacy of L. Put
    D = {(i,j) : i, j >= 1, i + j <= M - 1},   F = B \ D.
D is the staircase rho_{M-2} of size N_{M-2}; F has size K + 2r.

    LEMMA (Triangle quotient). There is an S_B-equivariant quotient
    W_L -> Ind^{S_B}_{S_D x S_F} (W_{M-2} boxtimes U_F),
    where W_{M-2} is the cyclic module of CSA and U_F is generated
    by the row and column block alternations on F, with the initial
    alphabet on every remaining block.

The proof is a single projection. Set ell = M + b + s - 1, the
largest row and column length of L. In both alphabets mark
    {3, 4, ..., M-1} union {M+b},
comprising M-2 letters. For each i the initial alphabet of row
or column i contains
    t_i = max(M-1-i, 0)
marked letters, exactly the number of D-cells in that row or
column. Project the pair-letter tensor onto words whose two
letters at every position share marking status. The projected
space is a direct sum T = oplus_{A subset B, |A| = |D|} T_A, with
g T_A = T_{g A}. The row-column generator v_R tensor v_C lies in
T_D and its projection is, up to overall sign, w_{M-2} tensor u_F.

    LEMMA (Sector induction). Let G act transitively on a finite
    set Omega, let T = oplus_{A in Omega} T_A with g T_A = T_{g A},
    fix D in Omega with stabilizer H, and let z in T_D. The map
    Ind^{G}_{H}(C[H] z) -> C[G] z is an isomorphism of G-modules.

Sector induction turns the projected module into the Ind of the
two local modules, proving the triangle quotient. The proposition
therefore reduces to: for every target lambda satisfying the
three inequalities, exhibit a subpartition mu subseteq lambda of
size |F| with S^mu in U_F. Young branching then puts S^mu in
Res_{S_F} S^lambda, Frobenius reciprocity gives c^lambda_{alpha,mu}
> 0 for some alpha |- |D|, and CSA gives S^alpha in W_{M-2} for
every such alpha.

Odd-path support. For an odd integer k = 2t+1, put alternating pairs
in the first tensor layer at positions (2,3), (4,5), ..., (k-1,k),
with the singleton letter 1 at position 1. In the second layer put
alternating pairs at positions (1,2), (3,4), ..., (k-2,k-1), with the
singleton letter 1 at position k. Denote the resulting pair-letter
tensor by u_k in (C^4)^{tensor k}, viewed as (Mat_2(C))^{tensor k}.

    LEMMA (Odd-path support). The cyclic S_k-span of u_k contains
    every S^alpha for alpha |- k with at most four rows. Moreover,
    for any finite family of ordered lists a_1, ..., a_c with
    1 <= a_i <= 4 and sum a_i = k, and any simultaneous placement of
    their dual polytabloid columns along positions 1..k, a single
    ordered basis of Mat_2(C) can be chosen so that all contractions
    with u_k are nonzero at once.

Proof of the first statement. Use the ordered basis
    M_1 = I,    M_2 = Z = diag(1,-1),
    M_3 = T = offdiag(1,1),    M_4 = J = Z T.
The last three are traceless, invertible, and pairwise anticommuting.
Adjugation fixes I and negates the other three. For a segment of
length a <= 4 starting at global position p, put D_j(Y) = Y for odd
j and D_j(Y) = adj(Y) for even j, and define the alternated block
matrix
    A_{a,p} = sum_{pi in S_a} sgn(pi) D_p(M_{pi(1)}) ... D_{p+a-1}(M_{pi(a)}).
Let e be the number of even positions in this segment and epsilon its
left endpoint parity. Then
    A_{a,p} = a! (-1)^{e - epsilon} M_1 ... M_a.
Indeed, if the identity occupies the local slot j, its displacement contributes
(-1)^{j-1} to the sign, and reordering the remaining three matrices
reproduces the permutation sign by anticommutation. The adjugations
contribute (-1)^{e - epsilon_j}, and epsilon_j = epsilon + j - 1 mod 2,
so the total sign is (-1)^{e - epsilon} independently of pi. Every
right hand side is invertible.

Contracting the tensor u_k with the dual polytabloid of a partition
alpha inside 4 rows now reads off as (-1)^t [B]_{11}, where B is the
ordered product of the invertible matrices A_{a_i, p_i}. Choose an
eigenvector of B with nonzero eigenvalue beta and a matrix Q in
GL_2(C) sending it to the first coordinate vector. Replacing every
M_i by Q M_i Q^{-1} preserves the anticommutation relations and the
adjugation identities, and yields a basis in which the contraction
equals (-1)^t beta != 0. The second assertion follows by iterating
over the finite list of placements: the nonvanishing locus of each
contraction is a nonzero polynomial in the entries of the common
basis, and GL_4(C) is irreducible, so the intersection of the finitely
many nonempty Zariski-open loci is nonempty. The dual-polytabloid
test then gives S^alpha in the cyclic span of u_k for every such
alpha.

    LEMMA (One extra copy). Let E = (b+delta, b) with b >= 0 and
    delta in {0,1}. Let Z_E be the cyclic span of the row-column
    alternating generator of E on its two alphabets. Then the sign
    twist Z_E tensor sgn contains S^alpha for every partition
    alpha |- 2b+delta with at most four rows, all even row lengths
    when delta = 0, and exactly one odd row length when delta = 1.

Proof. Suppose b >= 1; boundary cases b = 0 are immediate. Let v
be the long-row alternating vector and p the short-column
alternating vector of E; the two generate S^{E^t} and S^E. Let H act
by permuting positions within each short column. Signed averaging
of the tensor product gives
    sum_{h in H} sgn(h) h(v tensor p) = v_0 tensor p,
where v_0 = sum_{h in H} h v is nonzero because the word placing
letter j in short column j is H-fixed with coefficient one.
Regrouping the product by positions, each short column repetition
becomes
    H_0 = e_11 tensor e_22 + e_22 tensor e_11
         - e_12 tensor e_21 - e_21 tensor e_12,
a nondegenerate symmetric form on the four-dimensional span of
{e_ac = e_a tensor e_c}. If delta = 1, an extra singleton e_11
remains.

For alpha |- 2b with even row lengths, pair adjacent columns of
its dual tableau; the paired columns have equal height. Place the
b copies of H_0 in the corresponding row pairs, contract against
the paired dual columns: the result is a product of factorials
times a common sign, hence nonzero. Lemma (Dual-polytabloid test)

Attaching without cancellation. Fix a target lambda satisfying
the three inequalities of the band criterion. The proof exhibits
a specific subdiagram mu subseteq lambda of size |F| and computes
its dual-polytabloid contraction with U_F. Let
e_1 >= ... >= e_t > 0 be the even list with t <= q,
sum_{i=1}^t e_i = 2r, e_i <= h_i - d, produced by the greedy
rule on max(0, floor((h_i - d)/2)). The third band inequality
guarantees such a list exists.

    LEMMA (Band subdiagram). There is a subdiagram mu subseteq lambda
    of size |F|, together with a placement of its dual polytabloid
    columns on positions 1..K of the path and on the attachment
    positions, such that the dual-polytabloid contraction with U_F
    is nonzero.

Proof. Set mu's column heights to
    mu_i = h_i - e_i (1 <= i <= t),
    mu_i = h_i - f_i (t < i <= d + q),
    mu_i = 0       (i > d + q),
where the integers f_i >= 0 are chosen by the greedy rule so that
sum_{i=t+1}^{d+q} f_i = N_{M-2} - 2r, making |mu| = |F|. All f_i
are bounded by h_i and by the excess of lambda over the staircase
skeleton, so mu subseteq lambda. The path portion of mu is the
specific subdiagram of rows and columns indexed 1..K with column
heights mu_i for i <= d + q and zeros thereafter; the extra
portion is the diagram eta with columns e_1, ..., e_t.

Now place the dual tableau columns of mu along the flag
    N_1, ..., N_d, Q_1, ..., Q_dimX, N_{d+1}, ..., N_4.
For every nonempty path column take its first d positions from
N_1, ..., N_d, its next a_i positions (down to the column height of

The balance criterion. The second sufficient test uses the full
square S^L tensor S^L, with both factors realized by column-
alternating tensors on the SAME column blocks. Each column of
length k is projected onto words whose pair-letter sum a + a' - 1
equals k. The projection commutes with all position permutations.
Two columns with disjoint output ranges can be combined as a
tensor product, and then induced via Young's rule. This is slower
than the band criterion (its local supports involve whole column
blocks) but it addresses targets the band test misses.

Proof. Retain from the full pair-word space the words whose
outputs lie in U_i I_i and for which the outputs in I_i have

projecting, the tensor splits as a product over i of the

D_i are disjoint and the output ranges I_i are disjoint. Summing
over all re-orderings of the factors produces the induced module.

    H = sum_{i=1}^j e_i tensor e_i. A diagram with even row

    alternating dual tensor. For a pair of columns of height h,

    tensor z_3 tensor H^{tensor(b+1)} tensor (e_11)^{tensor s},

    P_3(c, s). In particular for s = 1, the length-three block

The balance bound. Assume M >= 9 and let h_1 >= h_2 >= ... be
column heights of a target nu |- n. If
    sum_{i=1}^4 h_i <= 2M - 2,
then S^nu occurs in S^L tensor S^L. The same conclusion holds if
the four-small condition is met for the column heights of nu^t.

Proof. Decompose L into local blocks (the big pair; the length-two
columns; the length-three block; the middle lengths 3, ..., M-2;
the singletons) so that each block's support from the local table
applies. The output ranges are disjoint:
    singletons: output 1;
    length-two and length-three: outputs 2 and 3;
    adjacent middle pair (u, u-1): outputs u-1 and u;
    big pair: outputs M-1 or above.
The separation lemma makes these into independent induced pieces;
reassociation combines the big pair with the singleton strip (when
s = 2) and the length-two and length-three blocks with each other
(when c > M). This produces a multiset A of trivial-factor sizes
of total n.

Two uniform bounds on A:
    |A| >= 2M - 5, and max A <= M + 1.
When c <= M there are p = M - 4 middle lengths; a run of p
consecutive pairs contributes 2p - (p mod 2) >= 2p - 1 parts;
with at least five other parts this gives 2M - 5. When c > M, the
length-three block with the twos contributes two balanced parts
of total c+3; then p = M - 5 is odd and the run contributes
2M - 11, plus at least six more from the big and short parts,
so |A| >= 2M - 5. The largest middle or short part is at most M.
The largest big-component part is at most ceil((T+2)/4) <=
ceil((7M/2+3)/4) <= M + 1, using r <= 3M/2+2. Hence
n / max A >= N_M / (M+1) = M/2 > (2M - 2)/4.

Verify the Young support lemma. Target has at least four columns
(otherwise four-small gives n <= 2M - 2 < N_M), so h_1 <= 2M - 5
and P_A(1) = |A| >= 2M - 5 >= h_1. The four big-component parts
are each at least two, so P_A(2) >= |A| + 4 >= 2M - 1, and
P_A(3) is the same. For k >= 4, decreasing heights and four-small
give sum_{i=1}^k h_i <= min(n, k(2M-2)/4). With B = max A, the
inequality min(k, a) >= ka/B for k <= B gives
P_A(k) >= min(n, kn/B). Since n/B >= M/2 > (2M-2)/4, dominance
holds for every k. Young's rule gives S^nu in the induced trivial
module, hence in S^L tensor S^L. The sign twist from self-
conjugacy of L handles transposed targets.


Capacity exhaustion. With the band and balance criteria
established, it remains to show that the criteria cover every
target at every degree. Three regimes are distinguished by M.

Large M. Take d = 4 and q = 8, which satisfy 0 <= q <= 8 and
4q + 7 + d = 43 <= K = 2M - 1 for M >= 22. If the balance bound
already handles the target (h_1 + h_2 + h_3 + h_4 <= 2M - 2, or
the same for nu^t), then containment is direct. Otherwise
H_4 >= 2M - 1 and W_4 >= 2M - 1.

If the band test failed in both orientations,
    sum_{i=1}^8 max(0, floor((h_i - 4)/2)) <= r - 1,
and likewise for w. The termwise inequality
    x <= 2 max(0, floor((x - 4)/2)) + 5   (x >= 0)
then gives H_8, W_8 <= S := 2r + 38. The following bound converts
this into a contradiction.

    LEMMA (Rectangle bound). Let i, j >= 1 and suppose H_i <= U
    and W_j <= V. Then
        n <= max(U + V - 1, floor(U V / (i j))).

Proof. If the cell at row j and column i is absent, every cell
lies in the first i columns or the first j rows (these two strips
cover the diagram and share only the corner cell), so
n <= U + V - 1. Otherwise the intersection is an i-by-j
rectangle. Put x = h_i >= j and y = w_j >= i. Every cell outside
both strips lies in the x-by-y corner, and that corner is missing
the i-by-j intersection plus the two strips within it, giving at
most (x - j)(y - i) = (U/i - j)(V/j - i) = UV/(ij) - U - V + ij
remaining cells outside. Therefore (i j) n <= UV. Combined with
the first case, n <= max(U + V - 1, floor(UV/(ij))).

Applying the rectangle bound to H_8, W_8 <= S with i = j = 8 gives
n <= max(2S - 1, S^2/64). On the other hand N_M + 2r = n with
N_M = M(M+1)/2, and r <= 3M/2 + 2 by maximality of M, so
    n - 2S + 1 >= (M^2 - 5M - 158)/2 > 0,
    64 n - S^2 >= 23 M^2 - 28 M - 1508 > 0,
both positive at M = 22 and increasing thereafter. Contradiction.
The band criterion therefore succeeds for at least one orientation
of every target at M >= 22.

Intermediate M. For 9 <= M <= 21, let d be the least integer
with H_d >= K and e the least with W_e >= K, both in {1, 2, 3, 4}.
Set
    k_f = min(8, floor((2M - 8 - f) / f))    (1 <= f <= 4)
and the finite set of prefix bounds
    E_{M,r}(d, e) = {(d - 1, 2M - 2) : d > 1}
                    union {(k_f, 2r - 2 + k_f (f + 1)) :
                            e <= f <= 4, k_f > 0}.
If neither band test succeeds for the two orientations, every pair
(i, U) in E_{M,r}(d, e) satisfies H_i <= U, and the transposed
set bounds row prefixes. The termwise inequality
    x <= 2 max(0, floor((x - f)/2)) + f + 1    (x >= 0)
combined with the rectangle bound gives a contradiction for
every target outside the finite set
    {(10, 8), ..., (10, 17)} union {(12, 14), ..., (12, 20)}
    union {(14, 22), (14, 23)}.

Exceptions. Each of the nineteen residual pairs is settled by a
recursive enumeration gen. The algorithm allocates integers
delta_i with 0 <= delta_i <= min(4, h_i) and sum_i delta_i = T =
K + r (with at least one odd delta_i when s = 2), tests the
residual diagram (column heights h_i - delta_i) against the lower-
component supports via the packing scheme of the balance bound,
and recurses. A successful allocation certifies the target by
Young's rule support closure; the recursion enumerates every
target up to the pruning implied by the local supports.
The arithmetic is exact.


Small degrees. For degrees n <= 64 the capacity arguments do not
directly apply (M can fall below 9), but a finite character
calculation settles every such n directly.

    PROPOSITION (Finite check). For every integer 1 <= n <= 64
    other than 2, 4, 9, there is a self-conjugate partition
    lambda of n with g(lambda, lambda, nu) > 0 for every nu |- n.

    Proof. Write chi^lambda(rho) for the character of S^lambda on
    the conjugacy class of cycle type rho. The character inner-
    product formula gives
        g(lambda, lambda, nu)
            = sum_{rho |- n} chi^lambda(rho)^2 chi^nu(rho) / z_rho,
    where z_rho = prod_j j^{m_j(rho)} m_j(rho)! and
    m_j(rho) = number of parts of rho of size j. Symmetric-group
    characters are real, so the square is legitimate.

    Invariant vector. In a call calc(s, k, e, v), s is the
    remaining cycle type size, k the current bound on part sizes,
    e the count of size-k parts already selected. Reached states
    have 0 <= s <= 64, k >= 1, e >= 0. Set rho_1 = 0 for the empty
    partition and m_j = m_j(rho). Define
        F_{s,k,e}(v)_nu
            = sum_{rho |- s, rho_1 <= k}
                  chi_v(rho)^2 chi^nu(rho)
                  e! / (k^{m_k} (e+m_k)!)
                  prod_{1<=j<k} 1 / (j^{m_j} m_j!).
    Only states with e+m_k <= 64 are reached, so all denominators
    are invertible. For 2 <= k <= s, separating m_k = 0 from
    m_k > 0 gives
        F_{s,k,e}(v) = F_{s,k-1,0}(v)
                       + (1/(k(e+1))) R_k F_{s-k,k,e+1}(R_k^T v),
    where R_k is the operator that multiplies the nu-coordinate by
    chi^nu(rho') for a single new part of size k added to rho.
    When m_k = 0, the counter resets. Each call decreases (s, k)
    lexicographically. Base cases: F_{0,k,e}(v) = v_empty^2;
    F_{s,s,0}(v) is used when s < k (since no size-k part remains);
    and F_{s,1,0}(v) = (1/s!) d_s (d_s^T v)^2 for the dimension
    vector d_s of S_s irreducibles (computed via Young-lattice
    chains on the empty partition).

    Bit encoding. Let B_lambda = {64 + lambda_j - j : 1 <= j <= 64}
    subset {0, ..., 127} be the beta set of lambda. Moving one bead
    from x to an empty position x+k adds a border strip of size k;
    the number of beads passed along the way gives its leg length,
    and the sign is (-1)^leg. With b the bit mask of B_lambda,
    b & ((~b) >> k) selects the permitted starting positions. With
    m = 2^x and t = 2^{x+k}, the mask (t - 2m) contains precisely
    the bits strictly between x and x+k, so cnt(((t - 2m) & b))
    gives the strip leg length. The routine op implements R_k or
    R_k^T according to its direction argument. All operations are
    exact 128-bit integer; the program treats modular arithmetic
    with an explicit prime, then confirms output again without
    reduction.

    Execution. The computation for n = 1, ..., 64 other than
    2, 4, 9 was performed with GNU C++ 13.3.0. All 61 invocations
    returned successfully, establishing the existence statement
    in every eligible degree of the finite range.
