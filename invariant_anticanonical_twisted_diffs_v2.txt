An anticanonical bundle governs two separate questions. The first asks,
for a compact real torus T acting holomorphically on a compact complex
manifold F, whether the torus-invariant Euler characteristic of
anticanonical powers (-K_F)^m can be nonzero at one favourable exponent
yet vanish at all larger ones. The second asks, on a smooth connected
projective variety S whose anticanonical bundle L = -K_S carries a
smooth Hermitian metric of nonnegative curvature, whether an unbounded
sequence of effective twists m_j L - D with fixed pseudoeffective error
D forces some positive multiple of L to be effective.

Both answers are yes. The proofs share a shape: each converts an
alternating sum at one exponent into a polynomial in the global
exponent, then reads off a value at the zero exponent. Torus invariance
prevents the first polynomial from collapsing; Kollar-Nadel vanishing
prevents the second. Neither argument needs rational connectedness.

Let T be a compact real torus acting on a compact complex manifold F by
holomorphic automorphisms. The differential of T acts on the
anticanonical line L_F = -K_F = det T_F. The natural linearization is
the one whose fibre character at each fixed point is the sum of the
normal-bundle characters at that point. Write Lambda = Hom(T, S^1) for
the character lattice and t^lambda for the value of a character at t in
T. The invariant alternating sum

  I_T(m) = sum over q of (-1)^q dim ( H^q(F, L_F^m) )^T

is the trivial-character coefficient of the T-equivariant Euler
characteristic of L_F^m.

Invariant anticanonical index. Let a compact torus T act holomorphically
on a compact complex manifold F, and give -K_F the natural
linearization. There exist a positive integer a and a polynomial P in
Q[t] such that I_T(m) = P(m) at every positive multiple m of a, and
P(0) = I_T(0).

Polynomiality on a divisibility ray is a statement about denominators of
locally constant rational characters; it is already visible in
localization formulas and in Ehrhart theory. The endpoint condition is
not of that kind. It needs the anticanonical fibre character
w = sum over i of nu_i at each fixed component, where nu_i are the
normal characters; that identity converts the top-degree strict
inequalities of a lattice count into a deletion that preserves Euler
characteristic. Without it, twisting the trivial line on a point by a
nontrivial character gives I_T(m) = 0 at all positive m and 1 at m = 0.

A geometric consequence follows once F is Kaehler and L_F carries a
smooth Hermitian metric of semipositive curvature. Averaging the Kaehler
form over T produces an invariant Kaehler form omega. The theorem of
Demailly, Peternell, and Schneider on Hard Lefschetz with semipositive
coefficients, in the smooth-metric case with trivial multiplier ideal,
yields for each 0 <= q <= n = dim F a surjection

  wedge(omega^q): H^0(F, Omega_F^{n-q} x L_F^{m+1}) -> H^q(F, L_F^m).

The map depends on the averaged form and on the natural linearization.
Compact averaging of a preimage over Haar measure preserves the
invariant target class. Thus an invariant class at unbounded exponent in
a fixed degree q forces invariant twisted (n-q)-forms at the same
unbounded set of exponents. Hard Lefschetz combined with a nonzero Euler
characteristic had already produced infinitely many canonical twisted
forms in the work of Demailly, Peternell, and Schneider; the invariant
anticanonical index above is what retains the zero exponent and gives
an anticanonical counterpart.

Two counting lemmas drive the index theorem. The first counts weighted
lattice points on a closed rational polytope; the second compares the
closed count with the same count after certain coordinate faces are
removed.

Counting lemma. Let D be a nonempty closed rational polytope in R^s and
let W(x, m) be a polynomial in x and m. For sufficiently divisible
positive integers m, the sum

  sum over x in (mD cap Z^s) of W(x, m)

is a polynomial in m, and its polynomial value at zero equals W(0, 0).
The same statement holds with coefficients in any finite-dimensional
real vector space.

Proof of the counting lemma. Choose a positive integer a clearing all
vertex denominators of D. Write m = a n, replacing D by a D, a lattice
polytope, and W by W(x, a n). The value of the modified weight at (0, 0)
is unchanged, so proving the assertion for lattice polytopes suffices.
Triangulate D into closed lattice simplices with lattice vertices;
nonempty intersections of closed simplices in the triangulation are
lattice faces, whose contributions will combine through
inclusion-exclusion without breaking the constant term.

Suppose therefore that Delta is a lattice polytope that is a
d-dimensional simplex with vertices v_0, ..., v_d. The vectors
(v_i, 1) in R^{s+1} are linearly independent. Use the lattice
Z^{s+1} in their real span and the half-open fundamental parallelepiped
spanned by (v_0, 1), ..., (v_d, 1). Every lattice point in the
nonnegative cone has a unique expression

  p + sum over i of l_i (v_i, 1), each l_i >= 0 an integer,

with p a lattice point of the half-open parallelepiped. The height h of
p is an integer with 0 <= h <= d, and the height-m slice imposes
sum over i of l_i = m - h.

For fixed p, substitute the corresponding coordinates into
W(p + sum_i l_i (v_i, 1), m) and expand in the binomial basis
prod_i C(l_i, u_i), with coefficients that are polynomials in m; the
expansion terminates because each l_i is nonnegative and only finitely
many monomials appear. The generating function identity

  sum over l_i >= 0, sum_i l_i = m - h of prod_i C(l_i, u_i)
    = C(m - h + d, d + sum_i u_i)

follows by multiplying the geometric series
z^{u_i} / (1 - z)^{u_i + 1}. For m >= h this is exactly the required
counting identity. For 0 <= m < h the upper entry on the right lies
between 0 and d - 1, so the binomial polynomial vanishes there as well.
The polynomial expression is therefore valid at every m >= 0.

At m = 0 every term with h > 0 vanishes. The only parallelepiped point
of height zero is the origin; the only binomial with a nonzero value at
l_i = 0 for all i has u_i = 0, giving W(0, 0). Summing over the
triangulation by inclusion-exclusion and using chi(Delta) = 1 (a
nonempty convex polytope is contractible) gives W(0, 0) again. The
vector-valued statement follows coefficientwise.
