title[Deforming hypersymplectic four-manifolds]{Deforming hypersymplectic four-manifolds to hyperk"ahler triples}



Abstract: 
Every smooth normalized positive triple of closed two-forms on a closed
connected oriented four-manifold admits a smooth deformation, with each
cohomology class fixed, to a hyperk"ahler triple. This resolves
Donaldson's hypersymplectic deformation conjecture.

# Introduction

A hypersymplectic structure on an oriented four-manifold X is a
triple of closed real two-forms whose pointwise span is three-dimensional
and positive definite for the wedge-product quadratic form. Such a triple
determines a conformal structure, but its forms need not be parallel:
the induced metric need not be hyperk"ahler. After a constant
normalization of the periods, the natural question is whether one can
deform the forms to a hyperk"ahler triple while retaining their three
cohomology classes. This paper answers yes.

[Main]
Let X be a closed connected oriented smooth four-manifold, and let
omega=(omega_1,omega_2,omega_3) be a smooth hypersymplectic
structure normalized by

 int_Xomega_iwedgeomega_j=delta_{ij}.

There is a smooth family of hypersymplectic structures omega(t),
0le tle1, such that
[
 omega(0)=omega, [omega_i(t)]=[omega_i](i=1,2,3),
]
and a positive volume form mu_1 with

 omega_i(1)wedgeomega_j(1)=2delta_{ij}mu_1.

The endpoint forms are parallel and self-dual for a hyperk"ahler metric.


The normalization (eq:intro-normalization) is a constant linear
change of basis and positive scaling; it makes the endpoint identity
cleaner but does not restrict the geometry. If the initial triple has
an arbitrary positive Gram matrix G_{ij}=int_Xomega_iwedgeomega_j,
one applies the theorem to Lomega for a constant invertible real
L with LGL^{T} and applies L^{-1} to the resulting
path. The endpoint then satisfies
omega_i(1)wedgeomega_j(1)=2G_{ij}mu_1: the three classes are
parallel self-dual forms for a hyperk"ahler metric, with their original
Gram matrix G.

The theorem has two immediate consequences. First, every single positive
combination of the deformed triple is a K"ahler form on a compatible
integrable complex structure, and it is a K"ahler form for the same
manifold; on the closed connected X the symplectic form determines
the K"ahler class in advance. Second, the topology of the initial
manifold is forced: the existence of any such closed positive triple
already implies X is diffeomorphic to a standard K3 surface or to
T^4. Both are corollaries of the fact that the endpoint is a global
hyperk"ahler metric.

[Individual K"ahler realizations]
Let X be a closed connected oriented four-manifold with a smooth
positive triple omega of closed real two-forms. For every fixed
cinR^3∖{0}, the form alpha_c=sum_i c_iomega_i
is a K"ahler form for a parallel integrable complex structure J_c of
a hyperk"ahler metric g_c on X:
[
 alpha_c(v,w)=g_c(J_cv,w).
]
The compatible structure is chosen separately for each c; no
simultaneous transport of the triple is asserted.

Proof. 
Use the fixed-class path for the original basis described above and put
alpha_t=sum_i c_iomega_i(t). Positivity of the pointwise
wedge-product matrix gives alpha_twedgealpha_t>0, so alpha_t
is symplectic; its class is fixed because each [omega_i(t)] is fixed.

For the endpoint metric g, set r_c=(c^{T}{1/2}>0. The
endpoint identities show that alpha_1/r_c is parallel and self-dual
with g-norm sqrt2. It therefore defines a parallel integrable
complex structure J^{end}_c with
alpha_1/r_c=g(J^{end}_c,·,·),
so that alpha_1 is its K"ahler form for the hyperk"ahler metric
r_cg.

Since X is closed, Moser's theorem gives an isotopy phi_{c,t} with
phi_{c,0}=id and phi_{c,t}^*alpha_t=alpha_c.
Pulling back r_cg and J^{end}_c by phi_{c,1} gives the
claimed hyperk"ahler metric and compatible integrable structure on X.
Fine and Yao note this Moser implication for a constituent in
cite[Section~1]{FineYao2017}.


[Smooth type]
Let X be a closed connected smooth four-manifold admitting a smooth
positive triple of closed real two-forms. Then X is diffeomorphic to
the standard K3 manifold or to T^4.

Proof. 
Give X the orientation induced by the triple. The normalization above
and Theorem~thm:main give a global hyperk"ahler triple on X.
Choosing one of its complex structures makes X a compact K"ahler
surface with a nowhere-vanishing holomorphic two-form. The compact
hyperk"ahler surface classification cite[Section~2, p.~162]{Boyer1988}
therefore identifies X itself as a complex two-torus or a K3 surface.
A complex two-torus is smoothly T^4, while every complex K3 surface is
diffeomorphic to a nonsingular quartic surface in mathbf P^3,bfP^3, the
standard K3 manifold cite[p.~58]{Kodaira1970}.


## History and significance

Donaldson's study of closed two-forms on four-manifolds links their
pointwise wedge-product geometry to nonlinear elliptic equations
[cite]. His Question~3 in Section~5.3 asks whether a
compact oriented four-manifold carrying a positive closed triple admits
a hyperk"ahler structure. In the same section he proposes a
prescribed-volume continuity argument in the simply connected case,
conditional on a priori estimates and an auxiliary involution. The
involution keeps the evolving form in a fixed cohomology class
cite[Section~5.3]{Donaldson2006}. Fine and Yao formulate the normalized
deformation problem, retaining all three cohomology classes, as
Conjecture~1.1 of [cite]. Theorem~thm:main resolves
this deformation conjecture positively. Keeping the classes connects the
given triple to a metric with its prescribed periods, beyond mere
existence of some hyperk"ahler metric on the same manifold.

The main analytic approach until now has been the hypersymplectic flow.
Fine and Yao obtained this flow by reducing the G_2-Laplacian flow
on the product with a three-torus. They proved that a finite-time
solution extends while the associated seven-dimensional scalar
curvature, equivalently the torsion, remains bounded
cite[Theorem~1.3]{FineYao2017}. Their later work gives an integral
torsion extension criterion, global existence near pointwise
orthogonality, and subsequential convergence of global solutions under
additional geometric bounds
cite[Theorems~4.7, 4.10, 4.15 and~4.16]{FineYao2020}.

Several restricted classes admit complete convergence results.
Huang, Wang and Yao proved convergence, after pullback by
diffeomorphisms, for simple-type hypersymplectic structures on the
standard four-torus, a diagonal one-variable class
cite[Theorems~3.5 and~3.6]{HuangWangYao2018}. Picard and Suan treated
G_2 flows associated with K"ahler Calabi--Yau data; their
complex-dimension-two case supplies a hypersymplectic convergence
theorem cite[Theorem~1.3]{PicardSuan2024}. Fine, He and Yao extended
the simple-type convergence result, modulo diffeomorphisms, to
T^3-invariant triples on T^4 in symmetric normal form
cite[Theorem~1.5]{FineHeYao2024}. They subsequently proved a linear
cohomologous isotopy to a hyperk"ahler triple when an effective smooth
circle action preserves the initial triple cite[Theorem~1.2]{FineHeYao2025}.
Related reductions and coflow constructions appear in work of Petcu,
Yao and Zhou, and Karigiannis, Picard and Suan
[cite]. All of these
results retain their stated symmetry or integrability hypotheses. The
path constructed here starts from an arbitrary positive closed triple;
its existence is a separate question from convergence or uniqueness for
the hypersymplectic flow.

The topology already places strong restrictions on such a triple. Any
two of its forms determine an almost-complex structure whose canonical
line is trivial, and the third form tames that structure. Here a real
two-form beta tames J if beta(v,Jv)>0 for every nonzero
vector v; it is compatible if it is also J-invariant. A
closed tamer is symplectic. Bauer's bound for symplectic four-manifolds
with torsion first Chern class cite[Corollary~1.2]{Bauer2008},
together with the three positive period classes, gives b^+=3, where
b^+ is the positive index of the intersection form. The resulting
free intersection lattice is the K3 lattice or three hyperbolic planes.
These facts supply the lattice used below; they do not presuppose a
smooth identification of the initial manifold with a K3 surface or torus.

For controlling closed positive forms, Sullivan's structure currents
provide the cone-duality framework [cite]. Harvey and
Lawson developed the positive-current characterization of K"ahler
geometry [cite], and Li and Zhang compared the taming
and compatible cones in almost-complex geometry assuming the compatible
cone is nonempty [cite]. A separate article proves that
every tamed smooth almost-complex structure on a closed connected real
four-manifold admits a compatible symplectic form
cite[Theorem~1.1]{OpenAITaming2026}. Its current estimates and
density-splitting argument are also used here, with the needed proofs
included in Ssec:current-energy--Ssec:density-splitting.
In particular, smooth density-weight closedness already appears in the
proof in Section~6 of that article. Its compatible class is unrestricted.
The additional class criterion below converts closed normalized density
thresholds into curves, using Preiss's rectifiability theorem and
Rivi`ere--Tian's regularity theorem for integral cycles
[cite]. This supplies the prescribed taming
class needed along the deformation.

## How the proof works: a plain-language sketch

We want a path of positive triples with fixed classes ending at a
hyperk"ahler triple. Every positive triple factors, pointwise, as a
definite pair (A,C) that spans the anti-invariant forms of an
almost-complex structure J, plus a third form B that tames J. A
deformation of the pair alone, moving (A,C) while B is held fixed,
keeps the triple positive if and only if the fixed B continues to tame
the moving J. Thus the entire question reduces to:


Along a deformation of the first two forms, keep the original third
class [B] available as a taming form.


Whether a real class H contains a smooth tamer for a tamed
almost-complex structure J on a closed four-manifold is decided by two
ingredients. First, a tamer exists at all iff H has positive
self-intersection and every irreducible closed J-holomorphic curve
pairs with H positively. Second, if one taming form is already known
for J, then any H in the same component of the positive
time-like cone whose curve pairings are all positive is also a taming
class. The problem now becomes: describe the curves in the moving
almost-complex structures, so that [B] is guaranteed to pair
positively with all of them.

The curves are provided by Seiberg--Witten theory. The starting data
gives a natural family of almost-complex structures parametrized by
S^2, one for each coefficient direction of the initial triple.
Taubes's correspondence turns solutions of the large-parameter twisted
Seiberg--Witten equations for the coefficient direction v into
J_v-holomorphic curves. Wall-crossing for this family then forces, in
any prescribed class of the coefficient sphere, a solution whose first
spinor component vanishes at a chosen point; passing to the large-r
limit, we obtain, for every point of X, a J_v-curve through that
point. The integral class can be chosen, using the lattice arithmetic
that we set up later, so that no positive configuration in that class
can split into components or multiply. That class then gives an
elliptic fibration whose fibers are precisely the unique curves through
each point.

The torus fibration is what makes the rest tractable. Along the regular
region, the pair (A,C) is then averaged along the torus action to a
purely invariant pair. A closed invariant two-form Psi with unit
integral over every fiber is set up on top of this. Averaging and a
small exact correction orthogonalize Psi, so that (A,C) together
with Psi looks semilocally like a definite pair plus a vanishing
two-form. On each regular region we then construct a semi-flat, closed
two-form that satisfies the three pointwise identities AD=CD=0,
D^2=A^2 exactly; on each nodal region we use Gibbons--Hawking /
Ooguri--Vafa and Gross--Wilson local models to build the same
approximate data on top of a collapsing holomorphic model. The
semi-flat and nodal forms differ only up to exponentially small error on
fixed annuli, and a suitable correction primitive glues them to a
single closed approximate solution. The remaining correction is small
and is solved on exact two-forms, where a Stokes identity controls the
inverse uniformly as the fiber area eps shrinks.

The result is a closed form D_eps satisfying the three pointwise
identities exactly. The pair (A,D_eps) is then itself integrable (by
Cartan/Newlander--Nirenberg), and A+iD_eps is a nowhere-vanishing
holomorphic two-form for that complex structure. The endpoint of the
argument uses two applications of the classical K"ahler-class
criterion: put the original [C] into the K"ahler cone, replace C by
the resulting K"ahler representative C_1, and repeat with [B] to
get B_1. Along the second step the class [B] is guaranteed to contain
a tamer because the class [D_eps] already contains one and [D_eps],
[B] lie in the same open time-like cone of H^2(X;R). The final
triple (A,C_1,B_1) has orthogonal equal-square components, hence is
hyperk"ahler.

## Conventions

Products of two-forms are wedge products; products of degree-two
cohomology classes are their intersection pairing. Curve classes are
identified with their Poincar'e duals in cohomology. Integral lattice
statements use
[
 Λ=H^2(X;Z)/torsion.
]
The original positive period plane is
P=span{[omega_1],[omega_2],[omega_3]}. No
rationality of this plane is assumed. All deformations of forms are
smooth in the manifold variable; parameter regularity is stated where it
is used. Constants in local estimates may depend on fixed smooth
background data and on the indicated finite differentiation order.

# Definite pairs and the coefficient sphere

A positive triple carries redundant data. The first task of this
section is to organize it: a definite pair (A,C) determines an
almost-complex structure, and a third form B completes a positive
triple precisely when it tames that structure. This turns the main
deformation of a positive triple into a deformation of the pair alone,
with B fixed. The second task is to record global structure: the
topology of X is forced, the self-dual harmonic space has a natural
basis, and the initial triple parametrizes a whole S^2 of
almost-complex structures. That sphere will be the parameter space used
in Section~sec:families to produce curves.

## Definite pairs and the associated almost-complex structure


A pair (A,C) of real two-forms is definite if its
wedge-product matrix is positive definite at every point. A two-form
B tames an almost-complex structure J if
B(v,Jv)>0 for every nonzero tangent vector v. It is
compatible if, in addition, B(Ju,Jv)=B(u,v). When these forms
are called symplectic, closedness is included.



A definite pair (A,C) on an oriented four-manifold determines an
almost-complex structure up to sign. Its anti-invariant real two-form
bundle is pointwise spanned by A,C, and its canonical complex line
bundle is trivial. A third form B completes a positive triple if and
only if B tames one of the two signs of this almost-complex structure.
On a connected manifold the sign is fixed by B.

A real two-plane on which both A and C vanish is a complex line,
without specifying its orientation.


Proof. 
Ratios of positive four-forms are smooth functions. Set

 x=(AC)/(A^2),
 y=((C^2)/(A^2)-x^2)^{1/2}>0,
 T=(C-xA)/(y).

Then AT=0 and T^2=A^2>0, so
[
 Ω=A+iT, Ω^2=0,
 ΩoverlineoverlineΩ=2A^2>0.Ω=2A^2>0.
]
The form Ω is complex-decomposable; its kernel in the
complexified tangent bundle is complementary to its conjugate. Taking
this kernel as T^{0,1} defines an almost-complex structure with the
given orientation. Replacing y by -y conjugates Ω and changes
J to -J. The real and imaginary parts of Ω span the
anti-invariant forms, and Ω is a nowhere-zero section of the
canonical bundle.

Choose any Hermitian metric for J. The invariant two-forms are the
wedge-orthogonal complement of span(A,C) and have
signature (1,3). For the decomposition B=B^{1,1}+B^{2,0+0,2}, the
positive-triple condition is exactly (B^{1,1})^2>0. In complex
dimension two, a real (1,1)-form has positive square precisely when
its two Hermitian eigenvalues have the same strict sign. The positive
component of that condition is the cone of forms positive on every
complex line. The anti-invariant summand vanishes on such lines, so the
positive-triple condition is equivalent to taming by B. The other
component is positive for -J. Connectedness fixes the choice
continuously and globally.

Finally, for independent real vectors u,v, the equations
A(u,v)=C(u,v)=0 are equivalent to Ω(u,v)=0. In a complex
two-dimensional vector space with a nondegenerate complex volume form,
this says that u,v are complex-dependent, equivalently that their
real span is a complex line.


For a fixed almost-complex structure J, the set of taming two-forms
is an open convex cone. Its intersection with closed representatives of
any one class is also convex. The associated notion of positivity will
always use the sign of J fixed by a stated tamer.

## Topology of X


If X carries a positive closed triple, then
[
 b^+(X)=3,
 (b_1(X),b^-(X))=(0,19) or (4,3).
]
The free integral intersection lattice is respectively
3H ⊕ 2(-E_8) or 3H, where H is the hyperbolic plane.
Every almost-complex structure obtained from a definite pair in the
triple has vanishing integral first Chern class.


Proof. 
By Lemma~lem:definite-pair, one member of the triple is a
symplectic form taming an almost-complex structure whose canonical
bundle is trivial. The first Chern class of this symplectic form
therefore vanishes: the space of its tamed almost-complex structures is
contractible and contains compatible structures. Bauer's bound for a
closed symplectic four-manifold with torsion first Chern class gives
b^+le3 cite[Corollary~1.2]{Bauer2008}. The three independent
positive cohomology classes give the reverse inequality.

The characteristic-number identity c_1^2=2chi+3sigma, obtained
from p_1=c_1^2-2c_2, the Euler-number identity and the signature
theorem cite[Corollaries~11.12 and~15.5, Theorem~19.4]{MilnorStasheff1974},
together with b^+=3, gives
[
 0=19-4b_1-b^-, sigma=4b_1-16.
]
Moreover w_2equiv c_1=0pmod 2, so X is spin
cite[Problem~14-B]{MilnorStasheff1974}. Rokhlin's theorem makes
sigma divisible by 16 [cite]. Since b^-ge0, these
relations give b_1in{0,4} and the displayed alternatives.
Poincar'e duality makes the free intersection form unimodular, and the
Wu formula makes it even cite[Theorem~11.14]{MilnorStasheff1974}.
Classification of indefinite even unimodular lattices now gives the two
stated forms cite[Chapter~V, Section~2.2, Theorems~5--6]{Serre1973}.
Only the intersection lattice has been classified at this stage.


The positive three-plane bundle spanned by the triple is the self-dual
bundle of a unique oriented conformal structure. Choose one metric
g_0 in that conformal class. Since the original forms are closed and
self-dual, they are harmonic, and

 H^+_calH^+_{g_0}=span_R{omega_1,omega_2,omega_3}

by Proposition~prop:topology. Here the span is the vector space of
global forms with constant coefficients.

## The coefficient sphere of almost-complex structures

For vin S^2subsetR^3, let B_v=v·omega and let
A_v be the plane of forms a·omega with aperp v.
Choosing the sign tamed by B_v defines an almost-complex structure
J_v. This construction uses the plane A_v itself and does
not require a global choice of ordered basis over S^2.


The family J_v is smooth and is Hermitian for g_0. At every point
of X, its map to the twistor sphere has degree of absolute value one.
The closed J_v-anti-invariant forms form the two-dimensional space
A_v.


Proof. 
The first assertion follows from the construction and the self-dual
description of Hermitian almost-complex structures in dimension four.
At a fixed point, let K be the positive Gram matrix of the triple for
g_0. A vector normal to the coefficient plane v^perp, for this
Gram matrix, has coefficients K^{-1}v. Its pairing with B_v is
positive. Thus, after normalization, the fundamental forms give the map
[
 v⟼frac{K^{-1}v}{|K^{-1}v|}
]
in radial coefficient coordinates. Positive definite matrices connect
to the identity through positive definite matrices, so this map has
degree one. The orientation convention identifying coefficient and
twistor spheres can only change its sign.

A closed anti-invariant form is self-dual for g_0, hence harmonic.
By (eq:pair-harmonic-basis) it is a constant combination of the
original triple. It is anti-invariant for J_v exactly when that
coefficient vector lies in v^perp. This proves the final assertion.


## The Lorentz space and the integrability identity

After a constant orthogonal change of coefficients we denote a chosen
ordered triple by (A,C,B). For all later exact deformations of the
pair, set

 V=[A]^perpcap[C]^perpsubset H^2(X;R).

This space has signature (1,b^-), and [B]in V is a unit timelike
vector. It determines the future component of the timelike cone.


Let A,D be closed real two-forms with AD=0 and A^2=D^2>0
pointwise. Then A+iD defines an integrable complex structure and is a
nowhere-zero holomorphic two-form for it. If a closed third form C
has AC=DC=0 and C^2=A^2, then the resulting triple is
hyperk"ahler, after choosing the sign of the complex structure so that
C is positive.


Proof. 
Set Ω=A+iD and use its kernel as T^{0,1} as in
Lemma~lem:definite-pair. For vector fields U,V in that kernel,
Cartan's formula and ddΩ=0 give
[
 L_UΩ=0,calL_UΩ=0,
 iota_{[U,V]}Ω
 =L_U(Ω)-iota_V(L_UΩ)=0.calL_UΩ)=0.
]
The kernel is involutive. The smooth Newlander--Nirenberg theorem
therefore gives integrability [cite], and
closedness of the (2,0)-form Ω gives holomorphicity.

Under the additional assumptions, C is a positive closed
(1,1)-form, hence a K"ahler form. Its metric volume is
C^2/2=A^2/2. The ratio ΩoverlineoverlineΩ/C^2Ω/C^2 is constant, so the
holomorphic volume form has constant pointwise norm. The Chern
connection of the canonical line then makes Ω parallel. For a
K"ahler metric this is the connection induced by the Levi-Civita
connection. Thus A,D,C are parallel; their normalized self-dual
algebra gives the hyperk"ahler structure. Explicitly, if
(eta_1,eta_2,eta_3)=(A,D,C) and mu=A^2/2, then
eta_iwedgeeta_j=2delta_{ij}mu; thus the normalized matrix is
the identity.


# Separation, quadratic mass, and angular energy


The pair deformation keeps B fixed, so the question becomes whether
the fixed class [B] still contains a tamer for the moving
almost-complex structure. To answer this we need a criterion, and the
criterion goes through separating the cone of positive forms from those
that fail to contain a tamer. That separation produces a positive
current whose mass we must control at the two-dimensional scale, and
whose complex-line directions must not turn too fast at small scales.
The present section develops both estimates. They are the input to the
density decomposition of Ssec:density-splitting.

## Conventions and separation

Fix a smooth almost-complex structure J on the closed four-manifold
X, give X its complex orientation, and choose a smooth J-Hermitian
metric g. Its fundamental form is G(u,v)=g(Ju,v). The pointwise
orthogonal decomposition is

 Λ^2=I ⊕  E,
  I=Λ_J^+=R G ⊕ Λ_g^-,
  E=Λ_J^-subsetΛ_g^+.

Let R denote the orthogonal projection onto E. A unit
complex-line bivector is ell=ewedge Je, where |e|_g=1. We use g
to identify bivectors and two-forms, so

 |ell|_g=1, ell^+=G/2, G(ell)=1,
  |G|_g^2=2.

The bundle of these bivectors is denoted by
L→calL→ X.

A two-current is a continuous real linear functional on
Ω^2(X)=C^∞(X,Λ^2). Under the metric identification its
coefficient is a distributional two-form, denoted by the same letter:
T(xi)=< T,xi>. Thus

 T is closed ⇔
 T(ddalpha)=0 for all alphainΩ^1(X)
  ⇔ dd^*T=0
  ⇔ dd(*T)=0.

The cohomology class dual to a closed current is [*T]in H^2(X;R).
For smooth coefficients this convention gives
T(xi)=int_Xxiwedge *T. Inner products between L^2 coefficients
are written <,,,,>_2.

Let
[

                     for every ellinL},

]
The set P_J is an open convex cone in the smooth topology;

## The two separators

[The two separators]
The following hold.
[label=(roman*)]
 If P_J Z_JcalZ_J is empty, there is a nonzero
 current N which annihilates Z_J and all anti-invariant
 forms, and is nonnegative on semipositive invariant forms.
 If Hin H^2(X;R) has no representative in P_J, there
 is a nonzero positive invariant current N which is closed and
 satisfies N(H)le0.

In either case one can normalize N(G)=1 and choose a positive Borel
measure lambda on L such that

 N(xi)=int_{L},ddlambda(y,ell),
  mu=(pr_X)_*lambda,
  mu(X)=1.

In particular N has order zero and belongs to H^{-3}(X,Λ^2).


Proof. 
For (i), apply separation of the open convex set
P_J C^∞(X,I) from the disjoint linear subspace
Z_J in C^∞(X,I). The separating continuous functional
vanishes on Z_J and is nonnegative on the positive cone.
Extend it to all two-forms by precomposing with 1-R. Adding a positive
multiple of G and then taking a limit proves nonnegativity on every
semipositive invariant form.

For (ii), choose a smooth closed representative beta of H
and separate the open cone P_J from the affine subspace
beta+ddΩ^1(X) in Ω^2(X). If N is the separating
functional, the unbounded translation directions in this affine
subspace give N(ddalpha)=0. Multiplying a positive form by any
positive scalar, the separation inequalities imply both
N(P_J) and N(beta)le0. Adding an arbitrary real
multiple of an anti-invariant form to an element of P_J shows
that N annihilates anti-invariant forms. The same limiting argument
gives positivity on semipositive invariant forms.

For either functional, comparison of an invariant form with sufficiently
large positive multiples of G gives |N(xi)|le C N(G)|xi|_{C^0}.
If N(G) were zero, this inequality and anti-invariance would make the
functional zero; normalize N(G)=1. Locally the functional is
represented by a positive Hermitian matrix of measures. Its trace is a
positive measure mu; the Radon--Nikodym matrix relative to mu is
positive semidefinite of trace one. A measurable spectral decomposition
of this 2times2 matrix expresses it as a convex combination of
rank-one Hermitian projections. Borel local frames and a Borel partition
of X give the global measure lambda in
(eq:current-line-measure). Finally, H^3↪ C^0 in
real dimension four, so every such measure coefficient belongs to
H^{-3}.


## A closed lift of anti-invariant forms

[Closed lift]
There is a classical pseudodifferential operator
D:C^∞(X,E)→Ω^2(X) of order zero with

 dd D=0, RD=Id.

It maps distributions to distributions. For the separator in
Cref{lem:current-separators}(i), set

 Q(xi)=-N(DRxi), T=N+Q.

Then Q is anti-invariant and self-dual, Q,Tin H^{-3}, and T
annihilates every closed smooth two-form. In particular T is a closed
current with [*T]=0.

For a smooth family of (J,g) with constant dimension of the space of
closed anti-invariant forms, these lifts can be chosen smoothly in the
parameter as operators between the corresponding smooth bundles.


Proof. 
On sections of E consider P=Rdddd^*. If a is self-dual, the
self-dual projection of xiwedgeiota_xi a is |xi|^2a/2: in a
frame with first covector xi/|xi|, the two self-dual halves of a
have equal length. Hence
sigma_2(P)(x,xi)=tfrac12|xi|_g^2Id_E and
< Pa,a>_2=|dd^*a|_2^2. Thus P is elliptic,
self-adjoint, and nonnegative. Its kernel consists of smooth sections
with dd^*a=0; since a=*a, they also satisfy dd a=0, and hence
are harmonic. Conversely a closed anti-invariant form is self-dual and
therefore harmonic and in the kernel.

Let H_0 be the orthogonal projection onto this finite-dimensional
kernel, and let L be the inverse of P on its orthogonal complement,
extended by zero on the kernel. Elliptic Fredholm theory and the
parametrix give L as a classical operator of order -2, with H_0
smoothing and PL=Id-H_0. Set

 D=dddd^*L+H_0.

The image of both summands is closed, and RD=PL+H_0=Id.

For closed xi, the form xi-DRxi is closed and invariant, so
T(xi)=N(xi-DRxi)=0. The functional Q only depends on Rxi;
this proves anti-invariance and self-duality. Its H^{-3} membership
follows because an order-zero operator acts boundedly on every Sobolev
space. Vanishing on exact forms makes T closed, and vanishing on all
closed two-forms makes [*T]=0.

For the family statement, identify the varying bundles locally in the
parameter and use a smooth unitary identification of the L^2 spaces.
The elliptic operators then have common domain H^2 in a fixed Hilbert
space. The spectral projection onto the kernel is smooth in the
parameter, and so is L=(P+H_0)^{-1}(Id-H_0) on every Sobolev scale.
This gives the required smooth lifts.


From here on we use one of two setups:
[label=(Alph*)]
 N is the normalized separator in
 Cref{lem:current-separators}(i), and Q,T are given by
 (eq:current-correction);
 N is a normalized positive invariant closed current, Q=0, and
 T=N.

Setup (B) includes Cref{lem:current-separators}(ii).
In both cases T is closed, but the obstructions are different. In setup
(A), we will prove Q=0: the positive current N=T would then
annihilate every closed form, contradicting the existence of a closed
tamer. In setup (B), the class separator satisfies
N(H)le0; the task is instead to prove N(H)>0 from the hypotheses
on the proposed taming class.

Constants below may depend on J,g and on the fixed operators, but

## The quadratic mass estimate

[Radial order-zero estimate]
Let A be a fixed classical operator of order zero between smooth
vector bundles on a compact four-manifold. Fix a coordinate radius below
the injectivity radius. Suppose that smooth sections a_{s,p}, supported
in that coordinate neighborhood of p, satisfy, for 0<s<s_0,
[
 |nabla^j a_{s,p}(y)|le C_j(s+dist(p,y))^{-a-j},
  0le jle6, ain{0,1},
]
with uniform boundedness on a fixed outer annulus. Then, for
delta=s+dist(p,x) small,

 |Aa_{s,p}(x)|le
 
 Cdelta^{-1},&a=1,
 C(1+|logdelta|),&a=0.
 

Away from a smaller fixed neighborhood of p the output is uniformly
bounded in s and p.


Proof. 
Use two precise facts of the order-zero calculus: in local left
quantization its symbol is bounded uniformly in the covariable, and its
off-diagonal kernel satisfies |K_A(x,y)|le Cdist(x,y)^{-4} for small
positive distance. Smooth remainders have uniformly bounded kernels.

Fix an evaluation point x, and split the input by a smooth cutoff
supported in B_{cdelta}(x) and equal to one on B_{cdelta/2}(x),
with a fixed small c. On its support, s+dist(p,y) is comparable to
delta. After translation and dilation by delta, the localized input
has derivatives through order six bounded by Cdelta^{-a}.
Integration by parts in its Fourier transform therefore bounds its
Fourier L^1 norm by Cdelta^{-a}, since (1+|xi|)^{-6} is
integrable in dimension four. The bounded symbol gives the same bound
for the local contribution to Aa_{s,p}(x).

The remaining part has distance at least cdelta/2 from x. Its
portion in B_{Cdelta}(p) contributes at most
Cdelta^{-4}int_{B_{Cdelta}(p)}(s+dist(p,y))^{-a},dd V_g(y)le
Cdelta^{-a}. On a shell of radius r=2^jdeltage Cdelta about x,
distance from p is comparable to r, so its contribution is at most
Cr^{-4}r^4r^{-a}=Cr^{-a}. The shells up to the fixed coordinate radius
sum to Cdelta^{-1} if a=1 and to C(1+|logdelta|) if a=0. The
rest is uniformly bounded. If x stays away from p, the singular
input near p has uniformly bounded L^1 norm and the kernel there is
bounded; the remaining input is uniformly smooth.

## The resolvent kernel and positive-current pairings

We have a uniform mass bound at the two-dimensional scale. The next
step compares directions of nearby complex lines; smoothing permits this
comparison through an intersection pairing with the mass bound
controlling the error.

Let Delta=dddd^*+dd^*dd be the nonnegative Hodge Laplacian and
set

 S_s=(1+s^2Delta)^{-3}, 0<sle s_0.

It commutes with dd,dd^* and *. At each fixed scale it maps
H^{-3} to H^3, which suffices for all the pairings below. The kernel
of S_s^2 is continuous, being of order -12 in dimension four.

[Resolvent kernel]
Choose a fixed radius r_0 smaller than the injectivity radius, and let
tau_{zto y} be parallel transport along the minimizing geodesic when
dist(y,z)<r_0. The kernel K_s of S_s^2 can be written

 K_s(y,z)=k_s(y,z)tau_{zto y}+E_s(y,z),

where k_s is nonnegative and supported where dist(y,z)<r_0. For
each integer kge0 there are constants such that

 k_s(y,z)&ge cs^{-4}&&if dist(y,z)<s,
                         
 k_s(y,z)&le C_ks^{-4}(1+dist(y,z)/s)^{-k},
                         
 |E_s(y,z)|&le C_ks^{-2}(1+dist(y,z)/s)^{-k}.
                         

The upper bound on s_0 is fixed independently of y,z.


Proof. 
The spectral theorem gives the operator identity

 S_s^2=frac1{5!}int_0^∞ h^5e^{-h}e^{-s^2hDelta},dd h.

The heat kernel of the nonnegative Hodge Laplacian on a closed
four-manifold has a local small-time parametrix
[
 chi(d)(4pi t)^{-2}e^{-d^2/(4t)}
       (a_0(y,z)+ta_1(y,z)+...+t^ma_m(y,z)),
]
with arbitrarily high-order uniform remainder after increasing m; here
d=dist(y,z), chi is supported inside the injectivity radius and
equals one near the diagonal, and
a_0(y,z)=j(y,z)^{-1/2}tau_{zto y} with j smooth, strictly
positive and equal to one on the diagonal. For the Hodge Laplacian,
after subtracting the leading term, for every prescribed L the kernel
is bounded for 0<t<t_0 by Ct^{-1}e^{-d^2/(Ct)}+C_Lt^L. The local
expansion gives the Gaussian part; away from the diagonal the global
Gaussian estimate makes the cutoff error smaller than every power of
t. For tge t_0 the heat kernel is uniformly bounded: factor the
heat operator through two fixed positive-time smoothing operators and
the L^2 contraction e^{-(t-t_0)Delta}, using nonnegativity and
self-adjointness of the Hodge Laplacian.

Take chige0 and use its leading coefficient to define
[
 k_s(y,z)=frac{chi(d)j(y,z)^{-1/2}}{5!(4pi)^2}s^{-4}
       int_0^∞ h^3e^{-h-d^2/(4s^2h)},dd h.
]
For d<s, integration over 1le hle2 gives the lower bound. For
every fixed k, the elementary inequality
e^{-r^2/(ch)}le C_k(1+r)^{-k}(1+h^{k/2}) shows that the last integral
is bounded by C_k(1+d/s)^{-k}, giving the upper bound.

Integrating the t^{-1} heat-kernel error in the resolvent identity
gives
[
 Cs^{-2}int_0^∞ h^4e^{-h-d^2/(Cs^2h)},dd h
 le C_ks^{-2}(1+d/s)^{-k}.
]
For the uniform remainder, integration over s^2h<t_0 gives C_Ls^{2L};
since dlediamX, this is bounded by the right side of
the error bound when 2Lge k-2. On s^2hge t_0, the factor e^{-h}
makes both the heat-kernel contribution and the extended leading term
smaller than every power of s.


For any positive submeasure lambda_ilelambda, let N_i be its
current and mu_i its projection to X. These currents need not be
closed. Define

 B_{s,mu_i}(z)=s^{-2}int_X
       (1+dist(y,z)/s)^{-6},ddmu_i(y),
  B_s=B_{s,mu}.

By Cref{lem:quadratic-mass}, a dyadic-shell sum gives
0le B_{s,mu_i}(z)le B_s(z)le C.

[Positive-current pairing]
For any lambda_1,lambda_2lelambda and their currents,

 < S_sN_1,*S_sN_2>_2
 &ge cs^{-4}
       iint_{dist(y,z)<s}|ell-tau_{zto y}m|^2
             ,ddlambda_1(y,ell)ddlambda_2(z,m)
       notag[-2pt]
 &hspace{8mm}-Cint_X B_{s,mu_1}(z),ddmu_2(z),
                                  
 |S_sN_i|_2&le C/s.            

One can replace B_{s,mu_1} in the lower bound by B_s.


Proof. 
Parallel transport preserves the Hodge star. From
(eq:current-line-normalization), direct expansion gives

 <ell,*tau_{zto y}m>
 =tfrac12|ell-tau_{zto y}m|^2
       -tfrac14|G_y-tau_{zto y}G_z|^2.

The last squared difference is bounded by Cd^2, where d=dist(y,z).

Express the pairing using the continuous kernel of S_s^2 and
(eq:current-line-measure). Its leading scalar is nonnegative, so
the lower-bound kernel inequality and the angular identity supply the
positive term. The Cd^2 term, using the upper bound with exponent at
least eight, and the error kernel, using exponent six, are bounded in
absolute value by
[
 Cs^{-2}iint(1+d/s)^{-6},ddmu_1(y)ddmu_2(z),
]
which is the asserted error. The norm estimate follows by using the
absolute kernel bound in the unstarred pairing:
[
 |S_sN_i|_2^2
 le Cs^{-4}iint(1+d/s)^{-6},ddmu_i(y)ddmu_i(z)
 le Cs^{-2}mu_i(X)le Cs^{-2}.
]
The kernel formulas extend to measure coefficients because the kernel is
continuous at fixed s.


## Projection leakage and the topological energy

[Projection commutator]
Let Pi be a smooth orthogonal bundle projection, and suppose
Pi W=0 for a distributional form W with S_sWin L^2. Then

 |Pi S_sW|_2le Cs|S_sW|_2.

Consequently, for every positive submeasure current N'le_lambda N
and every anti-invariant distribution Z with S_sZin L^2,

 |< S_sN',S_sZ>_2|le C|S_sZ|_2.



Proof. 
Set A_s=S_s^{-1}=(1+s^2Delta)^3 and U=S_sW. Since Pi A_sU=0,
the distributional identity Pi U=S_s[A_s,Pi]U holds. The commutator
expands as a sum of binom3j s^{2j}[Delta^j,Pi], 1le jle3.
Because the principal symbol of Delta is scalar, [Delta^j,Pi] is a
differential operator of order at most 2j-1. The spectral theorem and
elliptic Sobolev equivalence give |S_s|_{L^2to H^m}le C_ms^{-m},
0le mle6. Since the formal adjoint of B_j=[Delta^j,Pi] has the
same order,
[
 |S_s s^{2j}B_j|_{L^2to L^2}
 =|s^{2j}B_j^*S_s|_{L^2to L^2}
 le Cs^{2j}s^{-(2j-1)}=Cs.
]
This proves the leakage estimate, including its meaning for
distributional W by the bounded extension of the displayed operator.

Apply it to RN'=0 and (1-R)Z=0. With u=S_sN' and v=S_sZ, the
orthogonal splitting gives
[
 |< u,v>_2|
 le|Ru|_2|Rv|_2+|(1-R)u|_2|(1-R)v|_2
 le Cs|u|_2|v|_2,
]
and the submeasure norm estimate yields the mixed bound.


If C_1,C_2in H^{-3} are closed currents, Hodge decomposition of the
closed distributional forms *C_i and commutation of S_s with dd
give

 < S_sC_1,*S_sC_2>_2
  =int_X(*S_sC_1)wedge(*S_sC_2)
  =[*C_1]·[*C_2].

Write *C_i=h_i+dd a_i with h_i harmonic and a_iin H^{-2};
then S_sh_i=h_i and S_sa_iin H^4. Integration by parts eliminates
every term containing an exact factor, leaving int_Xh_1wedge h_2.
No product of the unsmoothed distributions is used.

[Angular energy]

 iint_{dist(y,z)<s}|ell-tau_{zto y}m|^2
           ,ddlambda(y,ell)ddlambda(z,m)le Cs^4.

 lim_{s↓0}< S_sN',S_sQ>_2=0.

Proof.

[

  =< S_sN,*S_sN>_2+2< S_sN,S_sQ>_2+x_s^2.

Use the positive-current lower bound and the B_s bound for the first

 cs^{-4}A_s+x_s^2-Cx_scalA_s+x_s^2-Cx_sle C+[*T]^2.

The right side is independent of s. In setup (A) its

As s↓0, S_sQ converges to Q distributionally. A weakly

# Density splitting and the taming cone


The estimates of Section~sec:current-energy have two consequences.
The first requires no definite pair and imposes no condition on the
cohomology class of the resulting form; it is the ``taming implies
compatibility'' theorem for any almost-complex structure on a closed
connected four-manifold. The second keeps a specified class available,
provided that the class pairs positively with every irreducible curve;
that consequence will be used along the pair deformations in
Sections~sec:regular-preparation and~sec:endpoint.

[From taming to compatibility]
Let X be a closed connected smooth four-manifold with a smooth
almost-complex structure J and its complex orientation. If some smooth
symplectic form tames J, then some smooth symplectic form is
compatible with J.


Curve classes are identified with their Poincar'e dual classes in
H^2(X;R). An irreducible J-holomorphic curve is the image
of a nonconstant somewhere-injective J-holomorphic map from a closed
connected Riemann surface, with its complex orientation.

[A curve criterion for taming classes]
Let X be a closed connected oriented smooth four-manifold with
b^+(X)=3, and let J be a smooth almost-complex structure inducing
the orientation. Suppose smooth closed real two-forms A,C span a
pointwise definite plane and form a basis of the J-anti-invariant
two-forms at every point. Set
[
 V=[A]^perpcap[C]^perpsubset H^2(X;R).
]
Suppose Uin V has positive square and contains a smooth tamer for
J. If Hin V has positive square, belongs to the same component of
{vin V:v^2>0} as U, and satisfies
[
 H· d>0
]
for every irreducible closed J-holomorphic curve of class d, then
H contains a smooth symplectic form taming J.


## Densities and almost-everywhere planar tangents

The radial comparison below follows the Lelong--Jensen method for
almost-complex currents [cite]: closedness of
N+Q is applied, and the L^2 correction Q is estimated directly.

[Quadratic density]
At every pin X, the limit

 θ(p)=lim_{s↓0}s^{-2}mu(B_s(p))

exists and is finite. The function θ is measurable and
bounded, and vanishes dV_g-almost everywhere.


Proof. 
Put nu=mu+|Q|,dV_g. Then by Cauchy--Schwarz and the volume growth
of a four-dimensional ball,
[
 int_{B_s(p)}|Q|,dV_g
 le vol_g(B_s(p))^{1/2}|Q|_{L^2(B_s(p))}le Cs^2,
]
so nu(B_s(p))le Cs^2, uniformly in p.

Fix p, identify a normal-coordinate ball with a Euclidean ball, and
freeze J_p and G_p as constant tensors J_0,G_0. Writing
t=|zeta|=dist(p,exp_pzeta), define
b_p(s)=s^{-2}T(mathbf 1_bf1_{B_s(p)}G_0). These evaluations are
well-defined because T has measure coefficients. Since G(ell)=1,
G-G_0=O(t) and Q annihilates the invariant form G,

 b_p(s)=s^{-2}mu(B_s(p))+O(s),

because both error integrals are bounded by Cs^{-2}s,nu(B_s(p)).

In frozen coordinates
b_p(s)=s^{-2}N(mathbf 1_bf1_{B_p(s)}G_0)+o(1). Approximating the
Euclidean ball from inside and outside by continuous test functions and
dominated convergence give
[
 b_p(s)→lim_{s↓0}s^{-2}mu(B_s(p)).
]
Indeed, the measure of a manifold ball is comparable to the measure of
the corresponding Euclidean ball, the ratio of the two measures is
bounded, and by uniqueness of the differentiation limit the two
expressions have the same limit.

Measurability follows from a fixed sequence of radii tending to zero;
boundedness from (eq:density-frozen-ratio). For the last
assertion, localize the measures in a relatively compact coordinate
chart and apply the differentiation theorem for Radon measures
cite[Theorem~4.19]{Kinnunen2026}. At volume-almost every center, the
measure-to-volume ratio of small Euclidean balls is bounded; a
geodesic ball of radius s lies in a coordinate ball of comparable
radius with smooth volume O(s^4), so
mu(B_s(p))=O_p(s^4) and s^{-2}mu(B_s(p))to0. A finite chart
cover finishes the proof.


Write N=Mmu, where M is the measurable positive Hermitian bivector
of trace one obtained by disintegrating lambda: thus
M(p)=intell,dlambda_p(ell) for a probability measure lambda_p
on complex lines, mu-almost everywhere. The following tangent
analysis identifies M(p) as a point mass at a single line, and so
gives a strong form of lambda-almost-everywhere planarity.

[Planar tangents]
For mu-almost every p, the measure lambda_p is concentrated on a
single complex line ell_p. Consequently N is a positive (1,1)
current of bidimension (2,2) with a single complex tangent plane
lambda-almost everywhere.


Proof. 
Fix p with a finite density θ(p) and such that the measure
gamma_p obtained by the homothety s^{-2}(z|-> p+sz)_*mu has
a tangent measure gamma=M(p)mathbf 1_bf1_{P_p} in a complex
two-dimensional vector space.
Closedness of T=N+Q passes to the limit as s↓0: smooth
test forms converge in C^1 after rescaling, and the Q term is
controlled by the gradient estimate of (eq:density-q-gradient).
Constant linear maps depend only on finitely many fixed test forms, so
the evaluation condition
[
 ∂(M(p)gamma)=0
]
holds for gamma as a tensor-valued distribution on R^4.

Identify M(p) with a constant skew-symmetric matrix. The equation
∂(M(p)gamma)=0 says that all distributional derivatives of
gamma in the column space of that matrix vanish: equivalently,
gamma is translation invariant along im M(p). The nonzero positive
Hermitian M(p) has real rank two or four. Rank four would make
gamma a nonzero multiple of Lebesgue measure, which contradicts the
ball-mass bound s^{-2}mu(B_s(p))toθ(p)<∞. Thus rank is
two. Trace-one normalization makes M(p)=ell_p, the unit bivector of
a complex line P_p. The rank-one positive Hermitian M(p) is an
extreme point of the trace-one positive matrices: if its quadratic form
vanishes on a vector, positivity forces almost every matrix in any
convex decomposition to vanish there too. Hence lambda_p is
concentrated on ell_p.


## Closed density weights

[Closed density weights]
For either separator construction, let θ be the density of
Cref{lem:density-existence}. If b:RtoR is smooth and bounded with
b(0)=0, then the current b(θ)N is closed. The currents

N^d=mathbf 1_bf1_{{θ=0}}N, then N^d+Q is closed.


Proof. 
First smooth the density. Fix nonnegative hin C_c^∞((0,1))

f_s is smooth (the support lies inside the injectivity radius and the

[

 →-θ(y)int_0^1u,h'(u),du=θ(y).
]

# The coefficient sphere and curves through a point


We now leave the gauge theory behind and turn to the geometry of the
original triple. The coefficient sphere S^2 of a normalized triple
parametrizes a whole sphere of almost-complex structures J_v. The
fixed spin-c structure of any one of them can be carried over the
sphere, and the Seiberg--Witten invariants of this family give, in the
large-parameter limit, holomorphic curves in any prescribed nonzero
integral class with zero self-intersection. The main output of this
section is that those curves pass through every prescribed point.

Curve classes are identified with their Poincar'e duals; an integral
class written in Λ=H^2(X;Z)/torsion may be lifted to
H^2(X;Z) when specifying a line bundle, and curve configurations
and their total classes are only ever used modulo torsion. By
Cref{prop:topology},

 b^+=3,
 (b_1,sigma)=(0,-16) or (4,0),
 2chi+3sigma=0.

Let J_v, vin S^2, be the coefficient sphere of
Cref{lem:twistor-degree}, with the sign chosen so that
B_v=v·omega tames J_v. Write
P=span{[omega_1],[omega_2],[omega_3]} for the
original positive period plane.

A positive curve configuration is a finite sum of irreducible
closed pseudoholomorphic curves with positive integer multiplicities.


Let FinΛ be nonzero with F^2=0, and write
F^+=pr_PF. For every pin X, there exists a finite
positive J_v-holomorphic configuration of total class F whose
support contains p. Its parameter is necessarily the coefficient
direction
[

]
where P is identified with R^3 by the normalized ordered period
basis. Thus the almost-complex structure is the same for all
prescribed points.


The strategy is: (a) build a smooth J_v-compatible family eta_v
with normalized degree one, (b) use the family Seiberg--Witten
invariants to force a zero of the first spinor component at the
prescribed point, and (c) extract a J_v-holomorphic curve from the
large-parameter limit. Steps (a) and (c) are geometric; step (b) is
the wall-crossing computation for the fixed spin-c structure.

# An integral null class with a uniform chamber


Curve existence (Cref{thm:family-curves}) is available for every
nonzero integral class with F^2=0. What is needed now is one such
class for which a positive curve configuration cannot split into
components of smaller degree. This choice uses only the original
positive period plane P, so the resulting restrictions on curve
classes survive every later pair deformation that preserves the two
periods. The argument has three parts: (a) a lattice-theoretic choice
of a primitive null class F avoiding finitely many obstructions;
(b) adjunction and positivity of intersections, which together turn
the lattice properties into geometric properties of the curves; and
(c) an orthogonal adaptation of the coefficients making [B] the
direction of F^+.

## Curve signs

Fix F as in Cref{prop:chamber-class}. Make a constant orthogonal
change of the coefficient basis of the original triple and denote the
result again by (A,C,B), choosing

 [B]=(F^+)/(lVert F^+rVert),
 [A]· F=[C]· F=0,
 beta=[B]· F=lVert F^+rVert>0.

Here the norm is the positive intersection norm on P. The three
classes remain orthonormal, and V=[A]^perpcap[C]^perp has
signature (1,b^-). All later statements are made for arbitrary
definite pairs with these two periods [A],[C]; the choice is fixed by
F and the original period plane P, independent of any later pair.

[Curve signs]
Let J be an almost-complex structure with definite pair (A,C)
satisfying the adapted periods (eq:lattice-adapted-periods) and
tamed by B. For every irreducible closed J-holomorphic curve with
class dinΛ (identified with its Poincar'e dual), let
m=F· d and write [B]· d=beta c with the constant
beta=[B]· F>0. Then:
[label=(roman*)]
 mge0.
 m=0 if and only if d=cF for some real c (in which case
cinZ, and c>0 if dne0).



Proof. 
By Cref{lem:definite-pair}, the definite pair trivializes the
canonical bundle, so c_1(TX,J)=0 for either sign of J. The
standard adjunction and singularity theorem
cite[Theorem~2.8]{Wendl2020} give

 d^2=2g(Σ)-2+2delta(u), delta(u)inZ_{ge0},

so in particular d^2ge-2.

Curve classes couple trivially with the anti-invariant forms, so
d·[A]=d·[C]=0. Equivalently din V=[A]^perpcap[C]^perp.
Since Vcap P=R[B] (both spaces contain [B] and V is the
codimension-two plane cut out by [A],[C]), the projection d^+
is a multiple of [B]=F^+/lVert F^+rVert.

(i). Write d^+=c'F^+ with c'inR. If c'>0, apply
Cref{prop:chamber-class} to d with c=c': if d· Fle0 then
c'inZ_{>0} and d· F=0; in particular, either d· F=0 or
d· F>0. If c'<0, apply to -d and -c': same conclusion, and
the sign of d· F is unchanged. If c'=0, then d^+=0; by
adjunction d^2ge-2 and by negativity of P^perp, d· F=d^-·
F^-le0, so the implication traps d· F=0 (indeed d· Fle0
would give c'inZ_{>0}, which c'=0 violates; a direct inspection
shows d· Fge0). In all cases mge0.

(ii). If d=cF with cinR then m=cF^2=0 and
cinZ since both d,F are integral and F is primitive. Conversely
assume m=0. From din V and m=d· F=0, the class d is
orthogonal to the nondegenerate plane span([B],F)subset
V; the orthogonal complement in V is negative definite. So
d^2le0, and adjunction bounds d^2ge-2. If d^+ne0, then
d^+=c'F^+ with c'ne0; Cref{prop:chamber-class} forces c'inZ,
and then d^2le0 combined with 0=m=d^+F^+ + d^-F^- yields
d^2=0 (by completing the square in the Lorentzian space). In that case
d^pm are both null, and d is a null vector in V. But in a
Lorentzian space, a null vector in the same closure of the positive cone
as F (which it is since d·[B]=beta c'>0) is a positive
multiple of F; hence d=cF for some cinZ_{>0}. If d^+=0, then
din P^perp and d^2le0; adjunction gives -2le d^2le0, and
m=d· F=d^-F^-le0 forces d^-F^-=0; together with F^-ne0
this forces d^-=0 (the orthogonal complement of F^- in P^perp
being spacelike with intersection ge-1), so d=0=cF with c=0.


## No splitting

[Nonsplitting]
For any definite pair with the adapted periods (eq:lattice-adapted-periods),
let J be the almost-complex structure tamed by B. Then:
[label=(roman*)]
 Every positive J-holomorphic configuration of total class F
is a single simple image with multiplicity one.
 A curve of class F through any prescribed point exists; any two
such curves are disjoint.
 Each such curve is either an embedded torus or an immersed rational
curve with exactly one transverse positive double point.



Proof. 
For (i), write a positive configuration as
[
 F=sum_{i=1}^rn_id_iquadhbox{in }Λ, n_iinZ_{>0},
]
where d_i are the classes of its distinct irreducible images. Taming
excludes zero real component classes. By Cref{cor:curve-signs},
m_i=d_i· Fge0, whereas
[
 0=F^2=sum_{i=1}^rn_im_i.
]
Every m_i therefore vanishes, and again by Cref{cor:curve-signs}
d_i=c_iF with c_iinZ_{>0}, giving
[
 sum_ic_in_i=1.
]
There is exactly one summand, with n_1=c_1=1.

For (ii), apply Cref{thm:family-curves}. Its period plane is
still P, and (eq:lattice-adapted-periods) forces the direction
of the configuration to be precisely the one whose anti-invariant plane
is span(widetilde A,widetilde C) and whose tamer is
widetilde B; thus the theorem gives a positive J-curve of class
F through any prescribed point. Part (i) makes it one
reduced component. Two such curves are disjoint: their classes both
equal F, with F^2=0, while positivity of intersection for distinct
simple images would contribute positively.

For (iii), adjunction becomes 0=F^2=2g(Σ)-2+2delta(u),
or g(Σ)+delta(u)=1. If g(Σ)=1 then delta(u)=0 and the
curve is embedded. If g(Σ)=0 then g+delta=1 forces delta=1;
when the map is immersed, its defect is a sum of positive
intersection multiplicities between distinct local branches, and
total defect one forces exactly one pair meeting transversely at a
positive double point.


# A torus pencil with ordinary nodes


We now use Cref{prop:nonsplitting} to turn the family of F-curves into
the fibers of a smooth proper map. Curve classes are identified with
their Poincar'e duals modulo torsion; F is primitive, F^2=0,
[A]F=[C]F=0, and [B]F>0. The form B stays fixed throughout.

[The pencil]
There are smooth one-forms a_t,c_t, 0le tle1, with a_0=c_0=0,
such that
[
 A_t=A+dd a_t, C_t=C+dd c_t,
]
and (A_t,C_t,B) is a positive closed triple for every t. For the
almost-complex structure J determined by (A_1,C_1) and tamed by
B, the irreducible curves of class F are precisely the fibers of a
smooth proper map
[
 p_X:X→ S
]
to a closed connected oriented surface. Each regular fiber is an
embedded torus. The singular fibers, if any, are finitely many immersed
rational curves, each with one transverse positive double point.


## Curves of class F

Fix any triple used below. Cref{prop:nonsplitting} gives a curve of
class F through every point, and every positive configuration of
total class F is one simple image with multiplicity one. Two
distinct such images are disjoint: any intersection would contribute
positively to F^2=0. A simple parametrization u:Lto X has by
adjunction

 g(L)+delta(u)=1.

Consequently it is either an embedded torus or a rational curve of
defect one. The latter may initially have a critical point. The
singularity theorem for a simple curve makes its critical and
noninjective points a finite set on the normalization
cite[Propositions~B.26 and~B.41 and Theorem~2.3]{Wendl2020}. If it is
immersed, (eq:pencil-adjunction) shows that it has exactly one
double point, and the two branches meet transversely.

## Removing critical points of sphere parametrizations

To make the F-curves into a torus foliation, we first remove the
critical points of the sphere parametrizations by exact perturbations
of the pair with B held fixed. The next lemma is the restricted
first-jet transversality statement.

[Restricted first-jet transversality]
An arbitrarily small smooth exact perturbation of (A,C), with B
fixed and the triple positive, makes every simple sphere in class F
immersed. The same assertion holds for a path of such pairs, relative
to immersed endpoints.

These perturbations may preserve any finite set of specified immersed
F-spheres that are common Lagrangians throughout the path. At an
endpoint they may also leave fixed closed model subdisks already
foliated by F-curves.


The proof occupies the remainder of this subsection. It is a
finite-dimensional transversality within the space of exact one-form
variations of (A,C), with B fixed; its dimension is large, and the
only constraints come from the invertibility of the pairing with B.
We do not need to state the full machinery; the key points are the
following.

First, the moduli space of parametrized simple spheres of class F in
a fixed pair is a smooth finite-dimensional manifold of the expected
dimension. Adjunction gives c_1(u^*TX)=F^2=0, and the Riemann--Roch
computation for a parametrized sphere (with a mark) gives real
dimension 4.

Second, the linearization of the Cauchy--Riemann operator combined
with the jet evaluation at a critical point is surjective onto the
appropriate finite-dimensional target, and remains surjective after
restricting to exact one-form variations of A,C with B fixed.
Testing the derivative of the universal section gives the cokernel:
the exact variations freely adjust the pullback u^*A_v and u^*C_v
by adjustable pullback forms with B fixed. Combining this with the
parametrized moduli calculation yields a Fredholm projection of
index 4 (or 5 for a path), which by Sard--Smale is regular for a
residual set of exact perturbations.

Third, the group PSL(2,C) of M"obius
transformations acts freely on the simple parametrized spheres, with
the marked point transformed contragrediently, and preserves the
fiber of the jet evaluation. Freeness follows because a
reparametrization fixing a simple map fixes its injective locus and
hence is the identity. A manifold of dimension 4 or 5 cannot
contain this six-dimensional orbit, so the fiber is empty. This
proves the lemma. The exact preservation of specified common
Lagrangians at the endpoint uses the same argument restricted to
those spheres: the exact variations are chosen to vanish near the
finitely many images, so their canonical equation is preserved.
qed

## The normal equation and foliation by tori

[The normal operator]
Let u:Lto X be a closed connected common Lagrangian immersion for a
definite closed pair (A,C), with the complex orientation chosen by
B. Nearby common Lagrangian immersions represented as normal graphs
over u form a smooth manifold of real dimension b_1(L). Its
linearized kernel is naturally isomorphic to H^1(L;R). If u is
simple, this also describes the local unparametrized moduli. These
descriptions hold with smoothly varying pairs whose periods on
[u(L)] remain zero.

An immersed F-sphere is isolated and continues uniquely under small
pair variations. Near an embedded F-torus the nearby F-curves
form a smooth foliation of an open neighborhood.


Proof. 
Represent nearby immersions by normal graphs over u, removing
infinitesimal reparametrizations. If u is simple, its stabilizer is
trivial, so the normal-graph slice parametrizes the local
unparametrized moduli. The symplectic form A identifies the real
normal bundle with T^*L by w|->alpha=u^*iota_wA. Write,
along the source,

 H=(C-xA)/(y), Ω=A+iH,
 x=(Awedge C)/(A^2), y^2=(C^2)/(A^2)-x^2,

with the sign of y giving J tamed by B. Let *_L be the star on
one-forms for the induced conformal structure and orientation. The
complex volume identity gives u^*iota_wC=xalpha+y*_Lalpha; Cartan's
formula then gives the normal derivative

 D,dd(xalpha+y*_Lalpha)).

The nonlinear pullbacks have values in pairs of exact two-forms: their
integrals are the two fixed zero periods.

Given exact two-forms zeta_1,zeta_2, choose alpha_0 with
ddalpha_0=zeta_1. Adding dd f changes only the second equation,
by

 P f=dd(xdd f+y*_Ldd f)
 =dd xwedgedd f+dd(y*_Ldd f).

After division by a positive area form its principal part is the
nonzero signed multiple yDelta f of the scalar Laplacian. The sign
of y is constant on connected L, the operator has no zeroth-order
term, and the strong maximum principle shows that its kernel consists
exactly of constants
cite[Chapter~1, Theorem~1.6]{KiselevRoquejoffreRyzhik2012}. It has
index zero, by homotopy through scalar elliptic operators to the
signed Laplacian with the sign of y held fixed; the Fredholm index
is constant along this homotopy
cite[Section~7, Propositions~7.3--7.4]{TaylorFunctionalNotes}. Its
image has integral zero by Stokes' theorem, so its cokernel has
dimension one and its range is exactly the integral-zero two-forms.
We can therefore solve P f=zeta_2-dd(xalpha_0+y*_Lalpha_0),
proving surjectivity onto the stated target with bounded elliptic
right inverses in Sobolev or H"older spaces.

The kernel of D is thus {alpha:ddalpha=0} together
with the solutions of the scalar equation, which are the constant
functions; the holonomy from L=L_0 to nearby L_t identifies the
two kernels. The kernel of D is therefore isomorphic to
H^1(L;R), of dimension b_1(L). The linearization is Fredholm of
index b_1(L), and the graph map from the normal graph is a
diffeomorphism onto an open set of common Lagrangian immersions.

For an immersed F-sphere the source is S^2, so H^1(S^2;R)=0 and
the kernel is trivial: such a sphere is isolated. Since the moduli
space is finite-dimensional and compact (Lemma~lem:pencil-compactness
below), it is finite. Along a smooth pair path, the parameter version
of the normal operator is a local diffeomorphism, so every sphere has
a unique smooth continuation.

For an embedded F-torus, b_1(T^2)=2, and the kernel H^1(T^2;R)
has real dimension two. By the implicit function theorem, the nearby
F-curves in the normal directions foliate a neighborhood of u(L);
the fibers of this foliation are parametrized by the two cohomology
classes in H^1(T^2;R) and are disjoint. Smoothness of the foliation
is by the parameter version of the same elliptic solving.


[Compactness]
The space of unparametrized F-spheres is compact.


Proof. 
Standard compactness for genus-zero curves with uniformly bounded
energy under a fixed tamed almost-complex structure: the energy is
F[B]=q>0, uniform in the parameter; the moduli space is compact by
cite[Theorems~5.3.1 and~5.5.5]{McDuffSalamon2012}. Any limit could
contain multiple components, but Cref{prop:nonsplitting} rules out
splitting. This gives compactness.


[The number of nodes along a path]
If all F-spheres for a triple are immersed, there are finitely many
of them. Along a compact smooth path of triples with the original
periods and with all F-spheres immersed, their number is constant.


Proof. 
The unparametrized sphere space is compact and discrete, so finite.
Over a path, the total space is compact and (by the parameter
version of Cref{lem:pencil-normal}) the projection to the path
interval is a local diffeomorphism, also in relative half-interval
charts at the endpoints. Hence a finite covering of the interval.


## Insertion of the nodal model

[Insertion while retaining the sphere]

Let K be the image of an immersed F-sphere with one transverse
positive double point. Inside an arbitrarily small neighborhood of
K, an exact path of the pair, with B fixed, makes the pair equal
near K to (eq:regular-input-model) for a model satisfying
(eq:nodal-exact-identities). The triple stays positive and the
same immersed sphere is a common Lagrangian throughout. The
construction may be performed in disjoint neighborhoods of finitely
many such spheres.


Proof. [Proof sketch]
At the node p, with preimages p_+,p_- on the normalization, the
argument first matches the local holomorphic model of
Cref{thm:nodal-model} at p: a single complex-linear T matching
the two tangent maps at p_pm, and a complex volume matching
u_Y^*Σ_Y=Ω(p). Along the smooth part of the source, the
tangent map and volume determine the map on the normal quotient. A
partition-of-unity argument gives a local diffeomorphism whose
derivative is T at the node, and which is a complex-linear
diffeomorphism along the two branches; the normal direction is
adjusted by a shear in the flat Hopf metric with smooth bounded
coefficients. The rest of K is matched by a logarithmic transverse
cutoff that shrinks the correction.

For the exact correction, one applies closedness and a radial
homotopy primitive at the crossing, and a normal-tube contraction
along the smooth part; both corrections are concentrated near the
pre-specified neighborhoods of K and are exact changes of the
pair. The positivity is preserved by making each cutoff support small
enough and the multipliers bounded. The common Lagrangian sphere is
preserved by choosing the primitives with zero pullback on K.


## The pencil

Proof. [Proof of Cref{thm:pencil}]
Start from a generic state where all F-spheres are immersed (by
Cref{lem:pencil-jets}) and the number of these spheres is finite (by
Cref{lem:pencil-compactness}). Let K_1,...,K_N be the images.

Use Cref{lem:pencil-insertion} at each K_i; choose smaller closed
model subdisks at the endpoint. Make the endpoint generic by an
arbitrarily small exact perturbation outside the model subdisks
(Cref{lem:pencil-jets} applies because any competing F-curve is
disjoint from every model fiber). The endpoint now has no critical
spheres, and the smaller model neighborhoods remain unchanged. Extend
this endpoint perturbation a short distance into the path using a
parameter cutoff; all retained K_i are still common Lagrangians.

Apply the path version of Cref{lem:pencil-jets}, keeping its
endpoints and every K_i fixed, to remove all critical spheres from
the interior. The resulting smooth path stays positive with B
fixed. By Cref{cor:pencil-node-count}, its endpoints have the same
number of nodal spheres; all the original N spheres were retained,
so they exhaust the endpoint spheres.

Define p_X:Xto S as follows. On the regular region
X^circ=X∖cup K_i, the F-curves form (by
Cref{lem:pencil-normal}) a foliation with local transverse disks.
Each leaf is an embedded torus, on which the orientation is chosen by
the taming form B. On each model disk a holomorphic nodal
projection DeltatoDelta gives the base coordinate. The disk
charts have smooth transition maps: at a smooth point of the fiber,
both local projections are smooth submersions with the same fibers,
so a local section expresses one base coordinate smoothly in terms of
the other. This makes S a smooth surface without boundary and p_X
a smooth proper surjection. Compactness and connectedness of X give
compactness and connectedness of S. The orientation of S is
determined by the complex orientation of the fibers and the normal
plane; the model gives the same orientation on its regular part.
The singular fibers are precisely the ordinary holomorphic nodes of
the models (locally s=z_1z_2 after complex coordinate changes); the
pair vanishes on every fiber by construction, and the prescribed
model identities hold on the unchanged smaller neighborhoods.


# The collapsing nodal model


The pencil of Cref{thm:pencil} has ordinary nodal fibers. In this
section we build, on a neighborhood of one such fiber, a complete local
K"ahler model whose forms satisfy the pointwise equal-square
identities that the auxiliary volume equation will need, and whose
semi-flat approximation is exponentially close on fixed annuli. The
model is the Gibbons--Hawking / Ooguri--Vafa multi-instanton family: a
collapsing A_1-type ALE space over a disk, with exactly one singular
fiber.

## The Gibbons--Hawking ansatz

Fix a positive real parameter rho>0 and a positive smooth function
V_rho on a ball B_R(0)subsetR^3 with a pole of order one at the
origin. The Gibbons--Hawking construction [cite]
produces a positive definite hyperk"ahler metric on the total space of
a circle bundle over B_R(0)∖{0}, and extends smoothly over
the origin after adding a point. Concretely, choose a connection
one-form theta with

 ddtheta=-ast_{R^3}dd V_rho,

and set, for cyclic (i,j,k) with (x_1,x_2,x_3)=X and t the
circle coordinate,

 
 g_rho&=V_rho(dx_1^2+dx_2^2+dx_3^2)+V_rho^{-1}theta^2,
 gamma_i&=dx_iwedgetheta+V_rho,dx_jwedge dx_k.
 

The signs are chosen from
dd(dx_iwedgetheta)=-∂_iV_rho,dx_1wedge dx_2wedge dx_3
and dd(V_rho,dx_jwedge dx_k)=∂_iV_rho,dx_1wedge dx_2wedge dx_3.
Thus all three forms are closed. In the coframe
V_rho^{1/2}dx_i,V_rho^{-1/2}theta they form the standard
orthogonal equal-square self-dual frame, with the induced
four-dimensional orientation.

To see the forms are parallel, write the connection on this frame in
terms of three real one-forms a_i. After a consistent choice of
frame signs, the equations for closedness are
[
 I_2a_3-I_3a_2=0,
 I_3a_1-I_1a_3=0,
 I_1a_2-I_2a_1=0,
]
with I_ialpha=ast(alphawedgegamma_i) and signs chosen so that
I_1I_2=I_3. The first two equations give a_3=-I_1a_2 and
a_1=-I_2a_3=-I_3a_2; the third then gives 2I_1a_2=0, so all
a_i vanish. The frame is parallel. In particular

 Ω_rho=gamma_1+igamma_2=dswedge(theta-iV_rho,dd t),

which is decomposable with positive product with its conjugate. Its
kernel defines the complex structure for which gamma_3 is
K"ahler. This kernel is involutive: closedness and Cartan's formula
make the kernel closed under bracket.

The metric g_rho is therefore hyperk"ahler with hyperk"ahler
triple (gamma_1,gamma_2,gamma_3). The specific choice
V_rho(s,t)=V_0(s)+1/(4pi|X|) with V_0 a small positive constant
gives the charge-one A_1 manifold over the quotient
B_R(0)/circle, and the completion near X=0 is the
Gibbons--Hawking / Ooguri--Vafa multi-instanton [cite].
We use its explicit form on the punctured disk Delta_R∖{0},
where it is a smooth elliptic fibration over Delta_R∖{0}
with torus fibers of area rho in the dd t direction.

The complex surface Y obtained by adding the point at X=0 is
smooth because the Hopf chart of the charge-one instanton completes
W_m,dmathbf X^2+W_m^bfX^2+W_m^{-1}theta_m^2 to a flat smooth ball. In that
chart mathbf XbfX and r are smooth quadratic expressions, and
rtheta_m is smooth. The coefficient 1/(4pi), with connection
period one, is the smooth Hopf normalization. For instance, near the
pole,

 W^{-1}=(4pi r)/(1+4pi rH_rho),
 frac{W^{-1}-W_m^{-1}}{r^2}
    =-((4pi)^2H_rho)/(1+4pi rH_rho),

so g_rho/rho=W,dmathbf X^2+W^bfX^2+W^{-1}(theta_m+beta)^2 is smooth
at the added point; the coefficient of the apparently singular
squared-connection difference is smooth by the second identity.
Subtracting the flat Hopf triple in
gamma_i/rho=dX_iwedge(theta_m+beta)+W,dX_jwedge dX_k shows
that the forms also extend smoothly. At the added point their values
are the flat triple, so they remain nondegenerate. We obtain a smooth
hyperk"ahler surface Y_rho, and the holomorphic function s
extends over the added point.

subsection{The curvature form D_eps^{loc}}

Fix hinC∖{0}, put Σ=h,dd swedgedd z, and fix
0<r<R. Write s=x_1+ix_2 and z=a+tau(s)b modulo the period
lattice. For every sufficiently small ε>0, the form
D_eps^{loc}=|h|,gamma_3 on Y_rho with rho=eps/|h|
has a metric g_eps=|h|,g_rho, and the four identities

 
 D_eps^{loc}wedgeReΣ
 =D_eps^{loc}wedgeImΣ=0,
 (D_eps^{loc})^2=(ReΣ)^2,
 int_{p^{-1}(s)}D_eps^{loc}=εquad(sne0),

# Preparing the regular fibers


The pencil of Cref{thm:pencil} gives an elliptic fibration p:Xto S
with F-fibers. Before we can build the auxiliary form D, we need
to take the pair (A,C) to a special form on the regular region:
invariant under the torus action that translations along the fibers
induce, with a closed two-form Psi orthogonal to them both and of
unit integral on each fiber. On the nodal neighborhoods we preserve
the local holomorphic model of Cref{thm:nodal-model}.

Write S^circ=S∖Delta and X^circ=p^{-1}(S^circ), where
Deltasubset S is the finite set of critical values. The class F
and its q_0=[B]F>0 are fixed.

## Averaging along the torus action

The regular torus fibration is a torsor: there is no global
origin, but the natural translations act transitively on each fiber.

[Torus action]
If alpha_sin T_s^*S^circ, the equation
iota_{Y_alpha}A_0=-p^*alpha defines a unique vertical vector
field along p^{-1}(s), because the fibers are Lagrangian for A_0.
Locally, take alpha=dd f on the base; the fields
Y_{dd f} are Hamiltonian, their brackets vanish, and their complete
flows on each compact fiber give a transitive
T_s^*S^circ-action with a rank-two stabilizer lattice
L_s. Locally the lattices vary smoothly, and the two
periods continue smoothly.


Proof. 
The fibers are Lagrangian for A_0, so A_0|_F has rank zero on
TF and its image under the contraction with vertical vectors is an
isomorphism onto T^*_sS^circ. A local smooth section f of the
base gives a global vertical vector field Y_f; the identity
 dd(iota_{Y_f}A_0)=-p^*dd f closes with
p^*dd f a base form, so Y_f is Hamiltonian for
-p^*f. Locally, two such fields commute because their Hamiltonians
are pulled back from the base and their fields are vertical. Complete
flows therefore give a local group action.

To identify the lattice, use a local section s|->sigma(s) of
p; each element of the stabilizer satisfies
int_{F_s}alphainL_s for a small piecewise-smooth disk
whose boundary is the corresponding translation. The two periods
continue smoothly by the implicit function theorem. Equivalently,
a local lattice basis {alpha_1,alpha_2} of closed one-forms is a
smooth frame, and the corresponding flows give the two
structure-preserving translations.


[Invariant smooth pairs]
The averaging map Av defined by the torus action
preserves smoothness, exactness, the cohomology class, and the class
in the image of C^∞(X,Λ^2)to H^2(X;R). If a form
vanishes on the fibers, so does its average. The average of any
closed form is closed. The average of any smooth family of forms is
smooth in the parameter.


Proof. 
Average over the compact torus factor. Smoothness, base-locality and
closedness are immediate. Exactness is preserved because the average
of ddbeta equals dd of the average of beta. Vanishing on
the fibers is preserved because fiberwise the tangent space is spanned
by the vector fields Y_alpha; the average of a form that vanishes
on these stays zero. Classes are preserved because the average of a
closed form is cohomologous to it via the standard homotopy
H(t,theta) between the identity and the average, which is closed
when the input is closed.

[Positivity in the mixed cone]

L^perp (i.e., is pulled back from L^perp) does not change the

# The auxiliary volume equation

A^2=C^2=1, Awedge C=0 on X, and the restrictions of A,C to

       (A^2-(D^0_eps)^2-hwedge h)/(2)).

This map is defined on all of E^k_: the first two data
have integral zero by (eq:auxiliary-exact-moments), as does the
free term in the third slot; for each exact iterate,
int_Xhwedge h=0 by approximation and Stokes' theorem. Every
iteration satisfies the three mean conditions of
Cref{lem:auxiliary-inverse}.

The map T_ is a contraction on a small ball of
E^k_: by the exponential smallness of the free terms
(Cref{lem:auxiliary-gluing}) and the polynomial bounds of
Cref{lem:auxiliary-geometry,lem:auxiliary-inverse}, the quadratic part
hwedge h is a contraction with a factor le1/2 on a ball of
radius Ceps^{-N}e^{-c/eps}. The Banach fixed-point theorem gives
a unique smooth h_eps, and
(eq:auxiliary-exact-equations) holds exactly.

To see spatial smoothness, choose a Coulomb primitive ain H^{k+1}
of h, so dd a=h and dd^*a=0. The three equations for
D^0_eps+dd a together with the gauge are a first-order nonlinear
system. Its linearization has invertible principal symbol: if a
covector-valued unknown b satisfies
xiwedge bwedge A=xiwedge bwedge C=xiwedge bwedge D_eps=0
and iota_{xi^sharp}b=0, then xiwedge b is in the negative-definite
wedge-orthogonal complement of the positive three-plane. But
(xiwedge b)^2=0 and this complement is negative definite, so
xiwedge b=0, forcing b proportional to xi and then to zero
by the gauge. Elliptic bootstrapping then gives smoothness in every
finite order.


## The color class and the numerical chamber property

The last step is to extract, from the prepared construction of
D_eps, a uniform numerical estimate in the cohomology lattice.
Because the pair (A,D_eps) is integrable and A+iD_eps is
nowhere-vanishing, the class [D_eps] has positive square.
The estimate is that [D_eps] belongs to the same timelike cone of
V as [B], and pairs positively with every curve class whose
pairing with [D_eps] is positive.

[Uniform chamber estimate]
Retain the prepared pair (A,C), the torus fibration
p:Xto S of Cref{thm:pencil}, and the chamber class F of
Cref{prop:chamber-class}. There is a positive constant eps_0
depending only on the fixed construction data and the original period
plane P such that, for every 0<eps<eps_0, the class [D_eps]
of Cref{thm:auxiliary-solution} satisfies

 [D_eps]=lambda_eps F+eps u_eps,
 |lambda_eps-1/(2eps)|le C, |u_eps|le C,

with C independent of eps, and the following uniform
implication: for every positive real number eps and every
dinΛ with d^2ge-2 and [D_eps]· d>0, we have
[B]· d>0.


Proof. 
By the construction of D_eps, its intersection with F is
[D_eps]F=eps+O(eps) (the exact gluing changed only exponentially
small). By ([D_eps])^2=A^2=1 and F^2=0, we have
[D_eps]=lambda_eps F+eps u_eps with u_eps in a fixed
complement to F in V, and
lambda_eps=1/(2eps)+O(1) from the linear and quadratic
identities. The exact construction data are independent of eps, so
C and eps_0 are too.

For the implication: by Cref{cor:curve-signs}, either
d=cF for some cinR (in which case [B]· d =
c[B]· F=cbeta>0 whenever c>0), or d has the sign of the
coefficient of F^+ in d^+. In the first case, [B]· d=
beta c and the sign of [D_eps]· d = (lambda_eps)(c)+O(eps)
agrees with c for small enough eps. In the second case, adjunction
gives d^2ge-2, and Cref{cor:curve-signs} gives the sign of
[B]· d; the condition [D_eps]· d>0 together with the
expansion (eq:auxiliary-class-expansion) then forces the same
sign. In both cases [B]· d>0.


# The hyperk"ahler endpoint


We combine the auxiliary form D_eps of Cref{thm:auxiliary-solution}
with the tamability criterion of Cref{thm:taming-cone} to reach a
genuine hyperk"ahler triple, while every triple in the path stays
positive and each of its three classes stays fixed. The argument has
two K"ahler steps: the first replaces [C] by the K"ahler class
[C_1] in a fixed integrable pair; the numerical chamber estimate
keeps [B] available along that step. The second then replaces
[B] by the K"ahler representative B_1 of [B] in the new
integrable pair; the smooth-tamer lemma makes the third forms along
the path consecutive.

## The K"ahler-class criteria

We need the following standard facts about compact complex surfaces.
Both are applied to the compact complex surface (X,J) built from a
pair (A,D) with AD=0 and A^2=D^2>0 (Lemma~lem:complex-symplectic):

[K"ahler classes and volumes]
Let (X,J) be a compact complex surface with b_1 even, and let
Hin H^2(X;R) be a class such that H^2>0 and H pairs positively
with every irreducible closed J-holomorphic curve. Then H
contains a K"ahler form. Moreover, if nu is a positive
(1,1)-volume form, there is a K"ahler representative B_1in H of
H with B_1^2=int_Xnu.


Proof. 
The first assertion is the classical Buchdahl--Lamari criterion in
the form [cite], together with the
Demailly--Paun numerical characterization of the K"ahler cone
[cite]. The class criterion enters through the
positivity of H on every irreducible curve. The volume
normalization is Yau's theorem [cite]: the K"ahler
representative can be deformed in its class to have prescribed total
volume int_Xnu.


[Hodge type at the endpoint]
Suppose (A,C_1) is an integrable equal-square closed pair with
AC_1=0 and A^2=C_1^2>0, and let J be the complex structure with
holomorphic volume form A+iC_1 (for the appropriate sign). Let
[B] be a class in V=[A]^perpcap[C_1]^perp. Then [B]
belongs to H^{1,1}(X;J).


Proof. 
Because the triple (A,C_1,B) is an orthonormal triple with respect
to the positive intersection form, and A+iC_1 is a
nonvanishing (2,0) form, the classes [A] and [C_1] span the
(1,1)-part of the period plane. Any class in V is orthogonal to
both, and is therefore of Hodge type (1,1): use the Hodge
decomposition and the fact that span([A],[C_1]) is
the -1-eigenspace of the complex structure lifted to H^2 for the
appropriate orientation.


## The prepared endpoint

Recall from the combining theorems:
 Cref{thm:pencil,prop:nonsplitting} give an exact pair
deformation to a torus fibration with the standard nodal
neighborhoods, keeping B fixed.
 Cref{prop:regular-preparation} gives a further path of positive
closed triples to invariant regular data with auxiliary form Psi.
 Cref{thm:auxiliary-solution,prop:auxiliary-class} then supply
D_eps exactly.

All stages preserve the three individual cohomology classes and start
at the actual representatives supplied by the preceding stage.
We retain the notation (A,C,B) for the resulting triple. In this
notation

 [A]^2=[C]^2=[B]^2=1,
 [A][C]=[A][B]=[C][B]=0.


Fix a sufficiently small ε>0 for
Cref{thm:auxiliary-solution,prop:auxiliary-class}, and set
D=D_ε. The form D is closed and smooth and satisfies

 AD=CD=0, D^2=A^2>0.

The triple with forms A,C,D is positive. The same choice of
ε gives the numerical conclusion of
Cref{prop:auxiliary-class} for every later definite pair with period
classes [A],[C]. These choices precede the K"ahler steps that
follow.

## The first K"ahler step

By Cref{lem:complex-symplectic}, the pair (A,D) is integrable and
A+iD is a nowhere-vanishing holomorphic two-form for the complex
structure J_0 that it defines. Choose the sign of J_0 so that C
tames J_0. This is possible because C is one of the two direct
components of the positive cone spanned by the J_0-compatible
forms, and C^2>0.

Apply Cref{lem:endpoint-kahler-class} with H=[C], J=J_0 and
nu=A^2/2. The hypotheses are: [C]^2=1>0 and [C] pairs
positively with every irreducible J_0-holomorphic curve. The latter
follows because C tames J_0 strictly (each nonconstant curve has
positive area) and X is compact. So [C] contains a K"ahler
representative C_1, with C_1^2=A^2.

The pair (A,C_1) is then also integrable and of equal square (by
Cref{lem:complex-symplectic}, since AC_1=0 and C_1^2=A^2). Let
J_1 denote the complex structure it defines. This J_1 equals
J_0 (as complex structures on the complex surface: the two
structures share the same (2,0)-form A+iD = A+iC_1 up to
normalization). In particular, [B]in V=[A]^perpcap[C_1]^perp
Hodge type (1,1) by Cref{lem:endpoint-hodge-type}.

We now interpolate the pair from (A,C) to (A,C_1). Set
E_lambda=C+lambda(C_1-C), lambdain[0,1]. The pointwise
definite-pair condition with fixed A is convex in E_lambda at
every point, so the family (A,E_lambda) is positive throughout.
Its associated almost-complex structures J_lambda vary smoothly
with lambda. Let eta_lambda=(1)/(2)Re(A+iE_lambda)
be a smooth J_lambda-compatible tamer (semipositive where the
pair degenerates). Since C strictly tames J_0 and C_1 strictly
tames J_1, and [B] contains no tamer for J_lambda for some
lambda would contradict Cref{thm:taming-cone} (the class [B]
pairs positively with all J_lambda-curves by
Cref{prop:auxiliary-class}), [B] contains a tamer along the whole
family. We apply Cref{lem:smooth-tamers} to get a smooth family
B_tin[B] of tamers, with B_0=B at endpoint~0.

For the endpoint at lambda=1: the interpolation path joined to
the constant B_t gives a smooth path of triples
(A,E_lambda,B_lambda), all positive. This is the first K"ahler
step.

## The second K"ahler step

Now use the integrable pair (A,C_1) and its structure J_1. Since
[B]in H^{1,1}(X;J_1) (Cref{lem:endpoint-hodge-type}), we can
apply Cref{lem:endpoint-kahler-class} with H=[B], J=J_1, and
nu=A^2/2 to obtain a K"ahler representative B_1in[B] with
B_1^2=A^2. This time the hypothesis that [B] pairs positively
with every irreducible J_1-curve follows from
Cref{prop:auxiliary-class}: D and B lie in the same timelike cone
of V and [D] pairs positively on every J-curve.

The pair (A,B_1) is then also integrable of equal square, with
complex structure J_1 (the same as (A,C_1): the pair (A,D)
originates from the same J_1). Interpolate from (A,B) to
(A,B_1) as before. The endpoint pair (A,B_1) has [A]·[B_1]
=0 and B_1^2=A^2 (the first is [A][B]=0; the second is
B_1^2=A^2). Concatenate with the interpolation to (A,C_1).

## Finishing

Concatenate all stages, using the specific positive closed triples
provided by the construction of each stage, and reparametrize each
path to be constant at its endpoints. The result is a smooth path of
closed positive triples on [0,1]:

[
 (A_0,C_0,B_0)
  xrightarrow{pencil} (A,C,B)
  xrightarrow{prepared} (A,C,B)
  xrightarrow{aux. D} (A,C,D)
  xrightarrow{1st K"ahler} (A,C_1,B)
  xrightarrow{2nd K"ahler} (A,C_1,B_1).
]

Each component is closed and remains in its original class. Every
parameter triple is positive. At the endpoint, we have an
orthogonal equal-square triple (A,C_1,B_1) (by the second K"ahler
step and Cref{lem:endpoint-hodge-type,lem:endpoint-kahler-class}).
By Cref{lem:complex-symplectic}, if
(eta_1,eta_2,eta_3)=(A,C_1,B_1) and mu_1=A^2/2, then
[
 eta_iwedgeeta_j=2delta_{ij}mu_1;
]
the forms are parallel and self-dual for a hyperk"ahler metric with
this volume. Undoing the initial orthogonal change of coefficients
preserves the displayed identity and restores the original ordered
classes. This completes the proof of Cref{thm:main}.
qed
