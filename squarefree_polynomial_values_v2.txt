Which polynomials take squarefree values, and how often? Take f(n)=n^2+1. Its
values begin 2, 5, 10, 17, 26, 37, 50, and 50 is not squarefree because 25
divides it. For f(n)=n^4+2 the values begin 3, 18, 83, 258, 627, and 9 divides
18. A prime power p^k divides f(n) only for n in certain classes modulo p^k.
When those classes never exhaust the residues, the product over p of the
fractions outside them is the predicted density; for every irreducible
polynomial of degree four through eight and k=d-2, provided no prime power
divides every value, it is the true density.

For an integer k >= 2, an integer a is k-power-free if no prime power p^k
divides a. The convention includes negative integers and excludes zero. For f in
Z[x] write
rho_f(q)= # { a in Z/qZ: f(a) = 0 mod q } , S_(f,k)(X)= # { 1 <= n <= X: f(n) is
k-power-free } .
The necessary local condition is rho_f(p^k)<p^k for every prime p: no prime kth
power divides every value of f. The candidate density is the product of the
proportions surviving these separate congruences.

Theorem. Let f in Z[x] be irreducible over Q of degree d with 4 <= d <= 8, and
put k=d-2. If rho_f(p^k)<p^k for every prime p, then
S_(f,k)(X)=c_(f,k)X+o_f(X), c_(f,k)=prod_p (1-(rho_f(p^k))/(p^k) )>0.
No primitivity or sign condition on the leading coefficient is required.

For quartics this is a squarefree-value density theorem. In degrees 5,6,7,8 the
prime powers excluded are p^3,p^4,p^5,p^6. The statement concerns positive
integer inputs and imposes no bound on the coefficient height of f; the error
term is not asserted uniform in f. In particular x^4+2 and x^4+1 have positive
squarefree-value density. The first is Eisenstein at 2; the second is the eighth
cyclotomic polynomial. For x^4+2 the value at zero excludes an odd prime square
and the value at one excludes a square of 2; the value at zero of x^4+1 is one,
so its local condition is immediate.

The problem has a long history. Ricci obtained the asymptotic in the range k >=
d. Hooley obtained the asymptotic at the exponent d-1 first treated for
infinitude by Erdos. Erdos singled out the unresolved squarefreeness of n^4+2 in
1953 and returned to the obstacle at exponent d-2 in his 1965 survey.

Nair obtained the asymptotic for k >= (sqrt(2)-(1)/(2))d, with further
developments by Nair and by Huxley and Nair. The 1976 bound reaches k=d-2 for d
>= 24. Heath-Brown's affine determinant method gave the range k >= (3d+2)/4,
reaching k=d-2 for d >= 10. Combining that method with Salberger's global
determinant estimates, Browning improved the range to k >= (3d+1)/4, which
reaches k=d-2 for d >= 9.

Granville showed that the abc conjecture yields the predicted squarefree-value
density for separable integer polynomials with no fixed prime-square divisor.
Greaves and Helfgott treated squarefree values of binary forms; a density in two
integer variables does not by itself give the density on the fixed line with
second coordinate one. Recent results of Browning and Shparlinski and of Sofos
study the averaged discrepancy over polynomial coefficient families, and those
averaged statements do not imply a theorem for each fixed polynomial. Carella's
preprint claims the asymptotics for n^4+1 and n^4+2; Zapata Ceballos and
Jalalvand claim positive density for certain monic degree-2q polynomials
including n^4+1. Neither claim enters as a theorem input: the large-prime

For higher degrees Browning's theorem gives a direct consequence. Let f in Z[x]
be irreducible over Q of degree d >= 4 and k=d-2. If f has no fixed prime
kth-power divisor, then
S_(f,k)(X)=c_(f,k)X+o_f(X), c_(f,k)=prod_p (1-(rho_f(p^k))/(p^k) )>0.
Indeed for 4 <= d <= 8 this is the density theorem for irreducible polynomials,
and for d >= 9 one has d-2 >= (3d+1)/4, so Browning's theorem in Xiao's form
applies to the primitive positive-leading part of f; removing sign and content
preserves the density.

A fixed-product consequence follows from factorwise estimates. A nonzero
polynomial is separable over Q if each nonconstant irreducible factor occurs
with multiplicity one. Fix k >= 2 and let F in Z[x] minus { 0 } be separable
over Q with every nonconstant irreducible factor of degree at most k+2. If
rho_F(p^k)<p^k for every prime p, then
S_(F,k)(N)=c_(F,k)N+o_(F,k)(N), c_(F,k)=prod_p (1-(rho_F(p^k))/(p^k) )>0.
More generally, let F=(F_1, ... ,F_r), r >= 1, be a fixed family of nonzero
integer polynomials, each separable with all nonconstant irreducible factors of
degree at most k+2, sharing factors allowed, and put Omega_p= { a in Z/p^kZ: p^k
| F_i(a) for some i } . If # Omega_p<p^k for every prime p then
S_(F,k)(N)=c_(F,k)N+o_(F,k)(N), c_(F,k)=prod_p (1-( # Omega_p)/(p^k) )>0,
where S_(F,k)(N) counts 1 <= n <= N with every F_i(n) k-power-free. The
single-polynomial form constrains the value of the whole product F; the family
form constrains the F_i(n) separately and uses the joint condition Omega_p. All
data are fixed; no uniform or power-saving error is claimed.

The finite-prime part reduces to the Chinese remainder theorem. Outside a fixed
finite set, separability and Hensel lifting give rho_f(p^k) <= d, so for fixed
large Y the primes Y<p <= X remove at most O_f(XY^(1-k)+pi(X)) integers in
(X,2X]. The hard part is the control of primes larger than the input interval.
The central estimate is that, with k=d-2 and 4 <= d <= 8, there is
delta=delta_f>0 with
# { n in Z: X<n <= 2X, p^k | f(n) for some prime p>X } << _f X^(1-delta).
The estimate does not require local admissibility. The sieve argument shows
explicitly that it implies the density theorem, including sign and content
generality, holding the finite cutoff fixed as X -> infinity and then letting
the cutoff tend to infinity; this produces the exact Euler product without a
power-saving error in the density asymptotic.

Proof strategy. Fix a dyadic prime interval P <= p <= 2P and choose one
qualifying prime for each counted input. Write P=X^eta and b=d-keta. The
relation f(n)=yp^k places the triples (n,y,p) on the surface f(x)=yz^k with
coordinate sizes X,X^b,X^eta; a weighted determinant argument on this surface
suffices when eta is large.

The remaining range uses the factorization of f. Let theta be a root of f and
K=Q(theta). Ideal factorization and the unit theorem produce integral alpha,beta
in K with
alpha^kbeta=mu(n-theta)
for a fixed nonzero integer mu, with conjugate sizes comparable to P^(1/d) and
X^(b/d). Integer coordinates in a fixed integral basis give two boxes; the
projective directions satisfy explicit polynomial equations, and near a balanced
point, after choosing one of finitely many local branches, either direction
determines the other analytically once 1/n is specified. The directions are
partitioned into small paired boxes; an archimedean determinant yields a
polynomial equation on each pair, and components defined by an equation in a
single coordinate block receive a second cut. The distinction between a mixed
equation and a pure equation matters, since the latter also reduces the
independent Taylor monomials in the local determinant. Two further steps convert
the cuts into counts: a reduced basis of the integer lattice of a direction box
shrinks its points in new integral coordinates, while an exceptionally short
lattice vector bounds the number of boxes in which it occurs; then the uniform
affine counting theorem applies through lower bounds for weighted spaces of
polynomial functions, its auxiliary varieties depending on earlier prime choices
with a random deletion argument controlling bad reduction.

In degree four the surfaces and curves need sharper geometry: on a surface the
difficult projections have degree one or two; a quadric supplies an additional
field degree; on a plane a deficient bound forces either a polynomial of degree
at most four on a general line or a degree-two map on the line of directions
with four distinct critical values; on a curve the limiting case is a
degree-five polynomial with four prescribed critical values, and after
normalizing two critical points only finitely many such polynomials occur, the
integer values forming one set of size O_f(X^(1/5)) removed once before summing
over boxes.

Consider now the reduction from a large-prime tail to the exact density. The
geometric argument estimates large prime-power divisors only for a primitive
polynomial with positive leading coefficient, and the finite-prime conditions
must still be imposed on the original polynomial because removing content
changes the corresponding Euler factors. Let f in Z[x] be irreducible over Q of
degree d >= 2 and k >= 2, with rho_f(p^k)<p^k for every prime p. Write f=scg
with s in { -1,1 } , c in N the positive content, and g in Z[x] primitive with
positive leading coefficient, and assume
T_g(X):= # { n in Z: X<n <= 2X, p^k | g(n) for some prime p>X } =o_(g,k)(X).
Then S_(f,k)(N)=c_(f,k)N+o_(f,k)(N) with c_(f,k)=prod_p(1-rho_f(p^k)/p^k)>0.

The proof normalizes and treats the local factors. Multiplication by s does not
affect divisibility; g is irreducible and inherits local admissibility; neither
polynomial has an integer root since d >= 2, so no counted value is zero. For a
prime p | c put e=v_p(c). Local admissibility forces e<k and
rho_f(p^k)=p^erho_g(p^(k-e)), 1-(rho_f(p^k))/(p^k)=1-(rho_g(p^(k-e)))/(p^(k-e)),
because p^k | f(a) is equivalent to p^(k-e) | g(a) and each class modulo p^(k-e)
has exactly p^e lifts modulo p^k; for p !| c the root counts agree. Let B
contain the prime divisors of c, the leading coefficient of g and the
discriminant of g. For p notin B the reduction of g has degree d and simple
roots, and each root modulo p^j lifts uniquely to p^(j+1) because
g(a+p^j u) = g(a)+p^j u g'(a) mod {p^(j+1)}
determines u modulo p when g'(a) is invertible. Hence
rho_f(p^k)=rho_g(p^k)=rho_g(p) <= d for p notin B. With q_p=rho_f(p^k)/p^k<1 one
has sum_p q_p<infinity since k >= 2, and for large p, q_p <= 1/2 and -2q_p <=
log(1-q_p) <= -q_p, so the product converges to a strictly positive number with
the finitely many bad-prime factors retained exactly.

A large-prime bound survives any fixed finite congruence restriction on n, since
the restricted exceptional set is a subset of the unrestricted one; in the
finite sieve those restrictions combine with the conditions modulo p^k using the
least common multiple, with no independence needed, and the exact content-prime
conditions can be retained while using the large-prime bound for the primitive
polynomial.

The geometric arguments place the counted points on varieties whose equations
vary with the box, so a counting theorem uniform for moving varieties is needed.
Its input is a lower bound for independent monomials on each subvariety; its
proof successively cuts residue classes by polynomial equations, with a deletion
argument controlling the primes at which the newly built equations have singular
reduction. The local prime-adic determinant principle is Heath-Brown's, the
ordered local Hilbert and divisibility estimates are Salberger's, and
simultaneous prime conditions appear in Salberger's later work; the patch
construction and deletion estimate handle the dependence on earlier prime
choices.

Throughout, degree means the ordinary degree of the projective closure, summed
over irreducible components for a reducible set; irreducibility is over C;
Bezout bounds are used for intersections and linear projections in fixed ambient
dimension. Fix positive weights omega_1, ... ,omega_N and let the weight of a
monomial z^alpha be omega * alpha. For an affine algebraic set A ⊆ C^N
put
H_A(T)=dim_(C)span { z^alpha|_A: omega * alpha <= T } ,
the supply of independent polynomial functions whose weighted size is at most a
constant times X^T.

Theorem. Fix integers 1 <= r <= N, a degree bound e_0, a constant C, positive
weights omega_l, and positive reals delta_1, ... ,delta_r. For X >= 2 let S
⊆ Z^N satisfy |z_l| <= CX^(omega_l) and lie on an algebraic set Z of
degree at most e_0 and dimension at most r. Suppose that for every fixed degree
bound e, every irreducible Q-defined subvariety A ⊆ Z of degree at most e
and dimension h>0 meeting S satisfies either
|S intersect A| << _(e,alpha)X^alpha for every fixed alpha>0,
or
H_A(T) >= (T^h)/(h!delta_h^h)-C_eT^(h-1) (T >= 1),
with constants uniform in X,Z,S,A. Put E_h=max_(h <= s <= r)delta_s. Then for
every epsilon>0,
|S| << _epsilon X^(E_1+ ... +E_r+epsilon),
the constant depending only on the fixed data and the uniform constants in the
hypotheses, with no bound required on the coefficients initially defining Z.

The weights are fixed in the statement; uniformity over a family follows when
the positive lower and upper bounds and the Hilbert constants are uniform. Empty
sets are trivial and zero-dimensional sets have at most as many points as the
degree bound, so 1 <= r <= N and S != (empty set) may be assumed.

A patch is a triple (A,G,Delta) where A is an irreducible Q-defined variety of
dimension h, the list G consists of N-h integer polynomials vanishing on A, and
Delta is a square Jacobian minor in N-h chosen coordinate columns; it covers the
points of A at which Delta != 0. For h=N the list is empty and Delta=1. A patch
need not give A globally as a complete intersection; its equations are local
coordinates where the minor is nonzero. For every algebraic set Z' ⊆ C^N
of degree at most e there is a list of boundedly many patches (A,G,Delta) with A
⊆ Z' covering S intersect Z', with bounded degrees, all coefficients of G
and Delta bounded by C'X^(C'), and, at a covered point z, Delta(z) a nonzero
integer of absolute value at most C''X^(C''), the bounds depending only on e,N,C
and the weights. The construction can be fixed as a function of Z' and S alone.

The patch lemma follows by splitting Z' into components and descending to
rational equations. If a component A is not defined over Q, some automorphism
sigma of C over Q has sigma(A) != A, and every rational point of A lies in A
intersect sigma(A), a proper bounded-degree intersection, so one replaces A by
the components there and recurses in smaller dimension.

For the descent: if every such sigma fixed A, each finite-dimensional space of
equations of degree at most e' would be preserved; the reduced row-echelon basis
in ordered monomials is unique, so the coefficients are fixed by all sigma and
hence rational; the fixed field is Q, since a nonrational algebraic number has a
conjugate and a transcendental number can be moved by translation in a
transcendence basis. For A defined over Q of dimension h and degree e', the
equations of ordinary degree at most e' in its ideal have generic Jacobian rank
N-h: choose h coordinate functions algebraically independent in C(A), project
onto these and in turn each remaining coordinate, each image closure being an
irreducible hypersurface of degree at most e' whose equation has nonzero
derivative in the extra coordinate by separability in characteristic zero, and
lift these equations to a diagonal generically nonzero minor.

Evaluating all monomials of degree at most e' on S intersect A gives an integer
matrix of bounded column count and entries bounded by a fixed power of X; its
nullspace W has an integer spanning set of polynomial height by choosing
independent rows and pivot columns, solving by cofactors, and clearing the pivot
determinant. Comparing its complexification with the degree-e' part of the
ideal: if the containment is strict, one bounded-height spanning polynomial does
not vanish on A but vanishes on S intersect A, so one intersects and recurses;
if equality holds, the spanning equations give the generic Jacobian rank, and
choosing N-h of these polynomials with a nonzero minor gives a patch, recursing
on Delta=0 for the rest. A rational zero-dimensional component through z in S is
the point z, with equations z_l'-z_l and minor 1. Every recursive intersection
is proper with Bezout-bounded degree, dimension decreases, and the list and
heights are controlled; fixing the choices once as functions of Z', its degree
allowance and S makes equal inputs give identical data.

For h >= 1 let s_h(L) be the sum of the first L total degrees of monomials in h
variables, ordered by degree and counted with multiplicity; counting lattice
points in a simplex gives s_h(L)=(h)/(h+1)(h!)^(1/h)L^(1+1/h)+O_h(L). Fix nu>0
and a degree bound for a patch with h=dim A>0 satisfying the Hilbert
alternative. Let z in S intersect A be covered by the patch and Q a set of
distinct primes with q !| Delta(z) and prod_(q in Q)q >= X^(delta_h+nu). For
large X the points of S intersect A congruent to z modulo these primes lie on a
bounded-degree polynomial not vanishing identically on A, uniformly in the patch
and primes with dependence on nu and the data; extra congruence conditions may
be imposed.

The proof fixes q !| Delta(z), translates to z with the h free columns as
coordinates, and solves G=0 as power series in the free differences over Z_q by
the invertible Jacobian, Hensel giving every nearby solution uniquely; hence
each coordinate monomial has an integral expansion. An L-by-L determinant of
monomial values there has q-adic valuation at least s_h(L): expanding in
free-difference monomials, a repeated expansion monomial gives zero and the
differences lie in qZ_q, the least total degree of L distinct expansion
monomials being s_h(L).

On the other hand the Hilbert bound permits independent monomials with the j-th
of weight at most delta_h(h!j)^(1/h)+O(1) (substitute T=delta_h(h!j)^(1/h)+C_1),
whose first L have total weight at most delta_h s_h(L)+O(L) by s_h, so the
evaluation determinant has absolute value at most C_LX^(delta_h s_h(L)+C_2L) and
is an integer divisible by (prod_(q in Q)q)^(s_h(L)). Choosing L with nu
s_h(L)>C_2L and then X large forces every such determinant to vanish, giving
deficient rank on the class, and a nonzero rational linear combination vanishing
on the class but not on A has bounded degree.

The patch itself may depend on earlier primes, so the entire finite sequence of
evaluation ranks is recorded. Fix small gamma>0 and set h_i=r+1-i. Choose degree

let
K_i= { F in Q[z_1, ... ,z_N]_( <= D_i): F(z')=0 for all z' in S intersect
A_(i-1), z' = z mod {Q_i} } ,
record its codimension rho_i, and take the first patch (A_i,G_i,Delta_i)
covering z in A_(i-1) intersect V(K_i); all Delta_i(z) are nonzero integers of

Lemma (paired boxes). Fix 0<w<1 and an integer M ~ X^w and partition each cube

every b_i of the y'_i of weight V), irrespective of coefficients; the numerical
size bounds used for counting are those on x',y'.

For d=4 a later Hilbert estimate needs n algebraic over the functions a_1, ...
,a_4, which is arranged by removing a small set of x'. Let Z be an initial
bounded-degree set from the cut proposition restricted to a_1a_2a_3a_4 != 0 in
coordinates (a,n), b_i=(n-theta_i)/a_i^2, and suppose either dim Z <= 3 or Z has
irreducible equation F(a,n)=0 of positive degree in n. Then the a for which the
n-fiber has dimension one have closure of dimension at most two and bounded
degree; deleting the selected points above it costs O_f(X^(2U)) in a box, and on
every irreducible subvariety of Z meeting the remaining points, n is algebraic
over Q(a_1, ... ,a_4). Substituting b_i=(n-theta_i)/a_i^2 into bounded-degree
equations and clearing denominators, a fiber has dimension one exactly when each
resulting polynomial in n vanishes identically, so its base is the common zero
set of the coefficient polynomials on the a-torus, of bounded degree, excluding
components in a_1a_2a_3a_4=0. If dim Z <= 3 the fiber-dimension ineuality gives
dimension at most two; otherwise the coefficients of F in n have no common
nonconstant factor, so the common zero set has codimension at least two in
four-dimensional a-space. The invertible linear change to x' preserves this, and
a bounded-degree set of dimension at most two has O(B^2) integer points in a box
of radius B (slice by a nonconstant coordinate and apply Bezout inductively),
giving O(X^(2U)) exceptional x', with O_f(1) selected points per x' by the
factorization lemma's fixed-x bound. If Y ⊆ Z is irreducible with n
transcendental over the a_i-field, every polynomial relation in n has zero
coefficients on Y, so Y's a-image lies in the removed base and meets no
remaining point.

The determinant cuts and lattice coordinates leave the selected points on an
arithmetic variety and on bounded-degree sets inside each box. A weighted
Hilbert function now supplies the last input of the counting theorem: a lower
bound on independent polynomial functions on each subvariety, uniform in
coefficients and in the integral changes.

Give every a_i weight U>0 and every b_i weight V>0, fixed rationals. In the
transformed coordinates these are the weights of the whole x' and y' blocks;
invertible linear changes preserve the weighted filtration even with varying
coefficients. Let Y be an irreducible subvariety of the affine equations
a_i^kb_i=n-theta_i, 1 <= i <= d, of dimension 2 <= h <= d, with a nonempty open
where all a_i != 0; assume n nonconstant. On this open its function field is
generated by the a_i and n. Choose h-1 of the a_i algebraically independent over
C(n) and one more distinct index; the image in these h coordinates and n has
dimension h, so its ideal is generated by one irreducible F_1(a_1, ...
,a_h,n)=sum_Ia^If_I(n). Let Y_(pair) be the closure of the projection onto the
selected pairs (a_i,b_i)_(i=1)^h; it has the same function field as the image.

Proposition (weighted Hilbert). For each nonzero f_I define
nu_(i,I)=ord_(n-theta_i)f_I, e_i=min_I(I_i+knu_(i,I)),
D=max_I { U|I|+(kU+V)deg f_I } -Usum_ie_i.
Then D>0 and
H_(Y_{pair})(T)=((kU+V)^(h-1)D)/(U^hV^h)(T^h)/(h!)+O(T^(h-1)+1),
a lower bound for H_Y(T); if the function-field degree of Y over Y_(pair) is at
least two the leading coefficient doubles. For fixed weights and bounded degree
of Y the error constants are uniform, independently of the coefficients of its
equations. The basic version for an h-dimensional irreducible Y gives H_Y(T) >=
((kU+V)^(h-1)min(U,V))/(U^hV^h)(T^h)/(h!)-O(T^(h-1)+1).

In the proof, factor f_I=prod_i(n-theta_i)^(nu_{i,I})g_I and define
C_1(a,b)=sum_Ia^(I+knu_I-e)b^(nu_I)g_I(a_1^kb_1+theta_1),
all exponents of a nonnegative. Impose also the h-1 equations
a_i^kb_i+theta_i=a_1^kb_1+theta_1 for 2 <= i <= h. On the open where all a_i !=
0 these equations and C_1=0 have coordinate ring C[a_1^( +-1), ... ,a_h^(
+-1),n]/(F_1), integral of dimension h. The boundary has smaller dimension: if
a_i=0 then n=theta_i, every other a_j != 0, and b_j=(theta_i-theta_j)/a_j^k;
before imposing C_1 the free coordinates are b_i and the h-1 nonzero a_j, of
dimension h. The terms of C_1 surviving there have I_i+knu_(i,I)=e_i and are
nonzero scalar multiples of a_(-i)^(I_{-i}-e_{-i})b_i^(nu_{i,I}), whose
exponents determine I; these Laurent monomials are distinct, C_1 restricts
nonzero, and the dimension drops to at most h-1. This checks every affine
boundary stratum, since two different a_i cannot vanish when the theta_i are
distinct.

Choose a positive integer L_(wt) with A=L_(wt)U, B=L_(wt)V integers, and put
Q=kA+B, E=L_(wt)D. Homogenize the relations and C_1 with a variable t of weight
one, giving h homogeneous equations in 2h+1 variables. On t != 0 scaling to t=1
gives the affine one times C^*, whose nonzero-a open is integral and generically
reduced of dimension h+1, the remaining affine strata having dimension at most
h. At t=0 the products a_i^kb_i must coincide: if all a_i != 0 then a_1, ...
,a_h,q are free before the last equation and the nonzero weighted top is
nonzero, giving dimension at most h; if some a_i=0 the common product is zero, a
union of coordinate h-planes of dimension h. So the homogeneous system has
dimension h+1 with no top component in either boundary, the
complete-intersection criterion makes its ideal prime (hence saturated in t),
and dehomogenization gives precisely the prime ideal of Y_(pair).

The degree-N part of the graded quotient is the space of affine functions
represented by monomials of weight at most N in the scaled weights; homogenizing
each such monomial with a power of t gives surjectivity, and injectivity holds
because a homogeneous element vanishing after dehomogenization and localization
at t is zero by saturation. The Hilbert series is
((1-z^Q)^(h-1)(1-z^E))/((1-z)(1-z^A)^h(1-z^B)^h).
At z=1 the pole order is h+1 with leading factor Q^(h-1)E/(A^hB^h); at a
nontrivial root of unity, if only one of 1-z^A,1-z^B vanishes the denominator
order is h, and if both vanish the order divides gcd(A,B) hence Q and E (a
monomial weight of the nonzero C_1), all h numerator factors vanish and the
order is at most h. Partial fractions give
(Q^(h-1)E)/(A^hB^h)(N^h)/(h!)+O(N^(h-1)+1), and taking N= floor( L_(wt)T) and
rescaling proves the pair estimate; lower-dimensional coordinate planes at
infinity change neither the leading coefficient nor introduce a period of order
T^h.

Pullback from the pair image is injective and preserves weights, so its Hilbert
function is a lower bound for that of Y. If [C(Y):K_(pair)] >= 2, one original
a- or b-coordinate xi lies outside K_(pair); the spaces of pair-image functions
of weight at most T and of xi times those of weight at most T-omega are linearly
independent (a relation A+xi B=0 with A,B in K_(pair) forces A=B=0), so H_Y(T)
>= H_(Y_{pair})(T)+H_(Y_{pair})(T-omega) for T >= omega, the bounded shift
leaving the leading coefficient and only changing the uniform error, and the
coefficient doubles. For the basic estimate, write the local parametrization at
a general point with h independent a-coordinates and replace each remaining a_j
by a_1^kb_1+theta_1-theta_j; clearing denominators and extracting powers of the
b-coordinates, as in the saturated determinant cut, gives a curve parameterized
by monomials b^beta of weight |B|V, and H_Y(T) is at least the number of such
monomials of total weight at most T, giving the stated coefficient.

The degree of F_1 is bounded in terms of d,k and deg Y: n=a_1^kb_1+theta_1 is
polynomial of fixed degree, and forming the graph of the map to the selected a_i
and n then projecting linearly gives the bound without coefficient heights; for
fixed U,V and a degree bound, only finitely many numerator degrees E occur, so
the errors are uniform even for moving equations and blockwise linear changes.

For an irreducible subvariety of an initial cut set: if dim Y=d it is a mixed

quartic surface case needs a sharper bound than the general one.

In degree four, d=4 and k=2. The number field K=Q(theta), its splitting field L,

improved after the infinite-fiber removal, and one fixed exceptional set of

Put D_0=2U+V and let Y be an irreducible surface component of an initial cut set

In the proof, where a_i,a_j are independent on Y, either D >= D_0 and the

For an a-image of degree at least three, a general linear projection to an

For a quadric a-image, deg S=2; the degree of an irreducible projective variety
is at least its codimension in its linear span plus one, a bound recorded by
Eisenbud, Green, Hulek and Popescu, so S spans an affine three-space and is a
quadratic hypersurface there (a surface spanning only a plane would equal it and
have degree one). The fibres of the projection to the (a_i,a_j)-plane are lines
parallel to the ruled direction v, the equation of S has nonzero quadratic
leading coefficient Q_2(v) on each fibre, so S has degree two over that plane
and a_i,a_j are independent. If D >= D_0 the weighted Hilbert proposition gives
the bound; if D<D_0 the projected-pair field is C(a_i,a_j) and the full field
has degree at least two over it, so the doubling clause applies; since D >= V
and 2D >= 2V >= D_0 the doubled coefficient suffices.

For a plane a-image, deg S=1, so S=A is an affine plane rational in the first
block; every a_i is nonconstant on A, since a nonzero rational direction is a
nonzero element of K with all embeddings nonzero, and the linear parts span the
dual of the two-dimensional direction space. Join i,j when the linear parts are
independent: the graph is complete multipartite on the parallelism classes (at
least two, connected, no isolated vertex), and an edge is exactly an independent
pair. Suppose every such pair has D<D_0; then the small relation holds on every
edge and makes n a rational function on A.

If n is polynomial, B_(ij) has degree exactly two for an independent pair while
the right side has degree at most six, so deg n <= 4. Every index has nonzero
coefficient in at least one relation: starting from one nonzero coefficient and
applying Galois automorphisms (which preserve Y, permute conjugate coordinates
and roots transitively, and preserve independence and the degree bound) gives
the full orbit. Fix a relation with c_i != 0; at the generic point of a_i=0 the
independent coordinate a_j is a unit, so polynomiality of n makes
b_j=(n-theta_j)/a_j^2 regular there and the relation makes b_i regular, whence
a_i^2 | n-theta_i in C[A] for each i. The four zero lines are distinct since the
theta_i are; restricting to a general affine line gives a polynomial of degree
at most four with four distinct critical points and four distinct critical
values, whose derivative would have four roots despite degree at most three (or
be zero with equal values), impossible.

If n is nonpolynomial, both coefficients in every relation are nonzero (else n
would be polynomial); writing n=P/Q in reduced form, an irreducible factor of
the nonconstant denominator Q divides every B_(ij) by the displayed identity.
Over C, B_(ij) is a product of two distinct affine linear forms a_j +- zeta a_i
with zeta != 0; the fixed factor defines a line ell through the common zero of
a_i,a_j transverse to both zero lines, and for each edge the two intersections
with ell coincide. Connectivity forces all four zero lines through one point O.
Centering at O makes each a_i homogeneous linear; scaling by t and taking the
rational-function limit at t=0 gives, for every independent pair, the same
function of the projective direction,
R=(c_itheta_ia_j^2+c_jtheta_ja_i^2)/(c_ia_j^2+c_ja_i^2),
the S_(ij) term acquiring an extra t^2. This is a fractional linear transform of
(a_i/a_j)^2 with nonzero determinant c_ic_j(theta_j-theta_i), of degree two,
ramified exactly at the two directions a_i=0,a_j=0 with values theta_i,theta_j.
Since every index occurs in an edge the same function would be ramified in four
directions with four distinct values, contradicting either coincident directions
(distinct values) or the displayed degree-two form (four distinct directions).
Hence some independent pair has D >= D_0, proving the bound; the extra
independent functions have bounded weighted degree, so shifts by such functions
preserve the uniform error.

For the quintic equality case, the polynomial line curves can have first
coordinates linear in a rational parameter and second coordinates polynomial;

quintics lie in one fixed finite family.

with distinct critical points tau_1, ... ,tau_4 in L and N(tau_i)=theta_i, every
integer value N(s) at s in L lies in E_f.

In the proof, over C introduce the six coefficients of Phi and z_3,z_4, impose
Phi(z_i)=theta_i, Phi'(z_i)=0, and require nonzero leading coefficient and
distinct z_i, a finite-type locus whose four critical points are simple
(distinct and exhausting Phi' of degree four). If a reduced irreducible
component had positive dimension, its function field admits a nonzero derivation

The two-dimensional alternatives are paired with a one-dimensional statement: a
rational function of bounded degree takes integer values at few integral inputs

Lemma (integral values of a rational function). Fix a positive integer D and
positive constants A,B,C_0. For every epsilon>0 and X >= 2, let R in Q(s) be a
nonpolynomial rational function whose reduced numerator and denominator have
degrees at most D. Then
# { s in Z: |s| <= C_0X^A, R(s) in Z, |R(s)| <= C_0X^B } <<
_(D,A,B,C_0,epsilon)X^epsilon,
the constant independent of the coefficients of R; inputs at which R has a pole
are excluded.

In the proof, clear denominators and write R=p/q with p,q in Z[s] primitive, deg
p,deg q <= D and gcd(p,q)=1. The resultant identity ap+bq=M with a,b in Z[s] and
M a nonzero integer shows that for every integer s with R(s)=m in Z one has q(s)
| p(s) and hence q(s) | M; also |q(s)| << X^(AD) on the range. The
integer-valued s are multiplicatively separated. For two such integers s<s',
(s-s')W(s,s')=q(s)q(s')(R(s)-R(s')),
where W in Z[s,s'] is the divided-difference numerator, bounded by X^(2D-1);
since R(s)-R(s') is a nonzero integer and q(s)q(s') | M^2, the left side is
divisible by q(s)q(s') and the gap s'-s is at least c |q(s)q(s')|/X^(2D-1) with
c>0 absolute. Partitioning the divisor-bounded integers into O(X^epsilon) dyadic
classes for |q(s)| and using the gap inside each class, the count is
O_(D,A,B,C_0)(X^epsilon); the dependence on M cancels because q(s) is bounded by
M but also by X^(AD), and only the divisor count of the fixed integer M up to
that size enters.

The verifier works in exact integer and rational arithmetic. Each
radical needed in delta_h is replaced by the least grid point whose
h-th power exceeds the exact argument, so the resulting comparison
is conservative. For every admissible z the verifier checks the
two strict cut ineualities, the sign condition, the min and max
bounds, the ratio range 3/20 <= v/u <= 5, and all the margin
ineualities. For z = z_d - 1 the interval endpoint eta_+ = z_d/K_0
is still on the small-eta side, so the closed interval
[z/K_0, (z+1)/K_0] is certified by the single check at z. The
verifier enumerates z, so the stopping indices and margins are
finite lists that can be read off from the output. The enlarged
quartic cell needed for the cyclotomic example uses

The weight-shift ineuality needed in the box summation states that
for either fixed mode and every U, V > 0 with 3/20 <= V/U <= 5,
delta_h(U+s, V+s) <= delta_h(U,V) + s (s >= 0, 1 <= h <= d).
The auxiliary function
q_(a,b)(U,V) = UV/(aU+bV), with a >= 0 and b >= 0, is
concave and increasing in each variable outside the origin, positively
homogeneous of degree one. The branch
F_(h,a,b) = q_(k,1)^((h-1)/h) q_(a,b)^(1/h) is a geometric mean of
such functions, so it is again concave, increasing, homogeneous of
degree one. Its differential in the direction (1,1) has absolute
value less than one on the ratio interval because the one-variable
ineuality
t (1 + (1)/(t) - (h-1)/(h)(k+1)/(k+t) - (1)/(h)(a+b)/(a+bt) ) <=
((k+t)^(h-1)(a+bt) )^(1/h)
holds for t = 3/20 and t = 5 (an integer check for
2 <= k <= 6, 2 <= h <= k+2, and (a,b) in { (1,0),(0,1),(1,1) } ).
Concavity gives the same for every intermediate t. Integrating
along the segment from (U,V) to (U+s, V+s) yields the ineuality
for each branch, and finite min/max operations inherit it. The
simple functions U/sqrt(3), U/2, U, V/g also have
directional slope at most one.

Coverage of the interval [z_d/K_0, d/k) is then transparent.
The function phi_d(eta) = eta(1 - keta/d) has derivative
phi_d'(eta) = 1 - 2keta/d <= (4-d)/d <= 0, so it is
nonincreasing. The stopping criterion therefore cuts the small-eta
range off exactly where the direct surface estimate becomes weaker
than the number-field argument. In the quartic case the last mode Q
interval ends at 4/3 and meets the first ordinary interval.

Two applications occur. The elementary-surface step uses ambient space C^3 with
coordinates (x, y, z) of weights (1, b', eta') and the surface f(x) = y z^k; its
Hilbert function is (D)/(b' eta') T^2/2 + O(T) with D = max { d, b'+keta' } , so
the surface threshold is delta_2 = sqrt(b'eta'/D) < 0.4999, and its curves have
threshold delta_1 = 1/2, giving the bound O(X^(delta_2 + 1/2 + epsilon)). The
number-field step uses ambient space C^(2d) with the mixed weight U on x and V
on y and the pair-image thresholds E_h(U, V), giving the bound O(X^(sum_h
E_h(U,V) + epsilon)) per box; after the box summation and range aggregation the
exponent becomes O(X^(1 - delta)) uniformly.

The range
phi_d(eta) <= 2495/10000
is handled by the elementary surface f(x) = y z^k. Fix a dyadic
P with P = X^eta and b = d - keta, and for each qualified
triple (n, f(n)/p^k, p) set (x, y, z) = (n, f(n)/p^k, p); this
is an integer point of the surface with coordinate sizes O(X),
O(X^b), O(X^eta). The surface is irreducible over C: it
is primitive in Z[x, z, y] and linear in y, and the two
coefficients z^k and f(x) are coprime in C[x,z], so Gauss's
lemma makes the defining polynomial irreducible. Fix upper weights
b', eta' with sqrt(b'eta'/d) < 0.4999. An explicit choice
is b' = e( floor( b/e) + 1), eta' = e( floor(eta/e) + 1)
with e = 1/10000, together with the terminal choice (e, d/k + e),
and these cover all (b,eta) with b + eta <= 3 and
beta/d <= 2495/10000, because such a pair satisfies
(b'eta')/(d) <= (2495)/(10000) + (3e+e^2)/(4) < ((4999)/(10000) )^2.
For each fixed triple of weights (1, b', eta') the filtered Hilbert
function of the surface is
H(T) = (D)/(b'eta')(T^2)/(2) + O(T), D = max { d, b' + keta' } ,
obtained by extracting the pole of the weighted Hilbert series
(1-t^E)/((1-t)(1-t^A)(1-t^B)(1-t^C)) at t=1; the assignments
A = L, B = Lb', C = Leta', E = LD clear denominators.
Every other root of unity has pole order at most two, because an
order dividing A, B, C also divides E and cancels against the
numerator. The threshold supplied by the counting theorem is
delta_2 = sqrt(b'eta'/D) < 0.4999.
An irreducible curve on this surface over Q has either nonconstant
x, in which case the function field is of degree at least two
over C(x) unless y and z are rational functions, and the
rational-function lemma handles the latter by a bounded-count
alternative or a constant z; or x constant, giving O_f(1)
triples. In every case the curve threshold delta_1 = 1/2 applies.
The affine counting theorem (ambient dimension three, dimension bound
two) therefore gives
O_(f,epsilon)(X^(delta_2 + 1/2 + epsilon)),
a strict power saving since delta_2 + 1/2 < 1 - delta for a fixed
delta > 0 depending only on d.

The remaining range phi_d(eta) > 2495/10000 is the number-field
range. Fix P = X^eta inside one of the parameter intervals
[eta_-, eta_+] and b = d - keta. Factor each qualified
n - theta by the balanced factorization into a^k b' = mu(n-theta)
inside the number field; the map (n, p) |-> (x, y) has bounded
order, so no multiplicity loss occurs. Place the pairs into paired
boxes using the pairing lemma; the normalized chart coordinates
s, t and the z = 1/n coordinate all lie in cubes of side
O(X^(-w)). Apply the archimedean cut to each box; the counted
points of a box lie on a bounded number of bounded-degree algebraic
sets of dimension at most d-1, plus the mixed d-dimensional
component. Group the boxes into ranges j using the lattice
proposition and apply the affine counting theorem (ambient dimension
2d, dimension bound d) with the pair-image Hilbert inputs. For
a fixed range j the box count is O(X^(m-dj)), the coordinate
bounds are |x'_i| << X^U, |y'_i| << X^V with U = u+j+tau,
V = v+j+tau, and the pair-image Hilbert thresholds supply
H_A(T) >= T^h/(h! delta_h(U,V)^h) - O(T^(h-1)) on every
dimension-h piece. The affine counting theorem produces
|S_j| << _(f,epsilon) X^(m - dj + sum_{h=1}^d E_h(U,V) + epsilon).
By the shift ineuality applied j times with increment
tau at each step, E_h(U,V) <= E_h(u, v) + j + tau, so
sum_(h=1)^d E_h(U,V) <= sum_(h=1)^d E_h(u,v) + d(j+tau),
and therefore
m - dj + sum_h E_h(U,V) + epsilon
<= m + sum_h E_h(u,v) + dtau + epsilon
< 24979/25000 + epsilon < 1 - delta
for a fixed delta > 0 independent of the input X. The mixed
d-dimensional component is treated by the mixed pair-image bound
with D = lambda U + s_0 V >= U+V, and gives the same saving;
the factor of boundedly many charts, ideals of the class group,
and irreducible components contributes only a constant.

In the quartic case the infinite-fiber removal costs an additional
O(X^(m - dj + 2(u+j+tau))) points per box, that is
O(X^(m + 2u - 2j + 2tau)) <= O(X^(m + 2u + 2tau)) uniformly
in j, which by the parameter certificate is at most X^(3122/3125),
still a strict power saving. The one-time removal of the quintic
set costs O(X^(1/5)), negligible in the same accounting. The
bounded integral input and output sizes at the curves and the degree
and weight data in the Hilbert alternatives are uniform, so the
discarded sets never reappear.

Summing over O_f(log X) dyadic prime intervals and over the
finitely many parameter intervals preserves a fixed minimum of the
two savings. The bounded multiplicity from the charts, ideals,
coordinate bases and components absorbs by adjusting that minimum.
The result is the large-prime estimate: for f primitive,
irreducible, positive-leading of degree 4 <= d <= 8 there is
a fixed delta_f > 0 with
# { X < n <= 2X : p^k | f(n) for some prime p > X }
<< _f X^(1-delta_f).
The content and sign normalization proceeds first; the sieve transfer
then converts the large-prime bound into the density theorem.

The whole scheme becomes completely explicit for f(T) = T^4 + 1.
Write zeta = e^(2pi i/8) and K = Q(zeta), and let
c_8 = prod_(p = 1 mod 8) (1 - (4)/(p^2) ).
Then
S_(f,2)(X) = c_8 X + o(X).
In particular for every sufficiently large N the interval
(N, 2N] contains at least N/2 positive integers n with
n^4 + 1 squarefree.

Irreducibility follows from the Eisenstein substitution
T |-> T + 1: (T+1)^4 + 1 = T^4 + 4T^3 + 6T^2 + 4T + 2 is
Eisenstein at 2. The congruence conditions are elementary.
Modulo 4 the values of T^4 + 1 are 1 for even inputs and 2
for odd, so rho_f(4) = 0. For an odd prime p, the congruence
T^4 + 1 = 0 mod p has a solution precisely when the group
(F_p)^* has an element of order 8, i.e. when
8 | p - 1; then it has exactly four solutions, each with
nonzero derivative 4T^3, and Hensel's lemma lifts each to exactly
one square root modulo p^2. Hence rho_f(p^2) = 4 for
p = 1 mod 8 and 0 otherwise. The content product is
empty because f is primitive and f(0) = 1.

The bound c_8 > 3/4 follows from prod(1-a_p) >= 1 - sum a_p
for 0 <= a_p <= 1; each p in the product is at least 17, so
c_8 >= 1 - 4sum_(p = 1(8)) (1)/(p^2)
>= 1 - 4sum_(m >= 17) (1)/(m^2) > 1 - (4)/(16) = (3)/(4).
Substituting in the main term gives c_8 N + o(N) squarefree
values on (N, 2N] and hence at least N/2 for large N.

Beyond the plain main term, an enlarged central-prime estimate uses
the single enlarged quartic cell quoted with the parameter
certificate. Because epsilon_0 < 1/2, every considered prime
satisfies p^2 >= N^(2-2epsilon_0) > N; the finitely many bad
primes (dividing discriminant or content) are absent for large N;
and the balanced-factorization lemma applies verbatim. The paired
charts, determinant cuts and lattice groups require only the displayed
coordinate upper bounds and the strict parameter ineualities, and
the Hilbert and curve alternatives impose no further condition
p > N. One power saving therefore holds on every dyadic prime
interval in [N^(1-epsilon_0), N^(3/2+epsilon_0)]. The one-time
removal of the quintic set and the O(log N) factor absorb into a
smaller fixed saving, and combining with the p > N bound yields
a fixed positive delta_0 for the range
N^(1-epsilon_0) <= p <= N^(3/2+epsilon_0).

The two complex embeddings of zeta are conjugate, every root of
unity in K has modulus one at both, and the integer ring is
O_K = Z[zeta]. The norm form is Euclidean on Z[zeta]; every
ideal is principal, so the balanced factorization takes the concrete
form
A^2 B = n - zeta, A, B in Z[zeta], N(A) = p.
The unit 1 + sqrt(2), with sqrt(2) = zeta - zeta^3, has
absolute values 1 + sqrt(2) and (1+sqrt(2))^(-1) at the two
complex places; multiplying A by a suitable power of it balances
the two absolute values. Consequently
|sigma(A)| ~ P^(1/4), |sigma(B)| ~ N P^(-1/2)
at every embedding. The balanced factorization and paired charts
of the main argument supply the same box structure, and the same
archimedean cuts and lattice grouping then apply. Moreover, when
P >= N^(1-epsilon_0) the arguments of every embedding are fixed
and the two branches s, t depend analytically on z alone with
derivatives uniformly bounded, so the quartic surface bound and the
quartic curve bound apply without further inspection of the
divisor-class group. The result is the enlarged central-prime
estimate.

Finally, for completeness, the elementary estimate controlling the
Pell-type equation
n^4 + 1 = D q^2
for fixed positive integer D states that the number of (n,q)
with N <= n <= 2N is O(1 + log N). Writing
q^2 = (n^2 + 1)^2 - 2n^2 and setting x = n^2 + 1, the
factorization in Z[sqrt(2)] gives a fixed number of exponential
families, so the pair (n,q) is determined up to O(log N)
choices. Combined with the divisor-class finiteness in K this
closes the Pell aspect, and no further analysis of the group of
solutions is needed.

The corollary for all d >= 4 follows from the classical and
recent cases. When 4 <= d <= 8 the main theorem gives the
density c_(f,d-2). When d >= 9 the exponent k = d-2
satisfies k >= (3d+1)/4, so the Browning-Xiao theorem for
power-free values of f applies after removing the content and sign
from f.

The sieve transfer restores the same Euler product from
the primitive case. The large-prime estimate goes through with f
in place of its primitive part at every stage; the density only
references c_(f,k). Thus
S_(f,k)(X) = c_(f,k) X + o_f(X),
c_(f,k) = prod_p (1 - (rho_f(p^k))/(p^k) ) > 0,
for every irreducible f in Z[x] with d >= 4 satisfying local
admissibility. The special cases f(T) = T^4 + 2 (Eisenstein at
2) and f(T) = T^4 + 1 (cyclotomic) therefore satisfy
c_(f,2) > 0 for full squarefree density.
The value at zero of T^4+2 is 2, so no odd prime square divides
it, and the value at one is 3, so 4 does not divide every
value, so local admissibility holds. The value at zero of
T^4+1 is 1, so no prime square divides it either, and local
admissibility holds for that polynomial as well.
