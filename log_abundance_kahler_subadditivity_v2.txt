================================================================
0. THE GOAL IN ONE SENTENCE
================================================================

  If (X, Delta) is a log-canonical pair on a compact Kaehler
  space and the adjoint bundle K_X + Delta is "numerically
  positive" (nef), then some positive multiple of it is generated
  by its global sections (it is semiample).

"Abundance" is the promise that numerical positivity of the
canonical bundle upgrades to geometric positivity: the bundle
pushes forward to a projective space and the pair maps out. The
companion promise of the field, "existence of minimal models",
is NOT proved here. Abundance is the second half.

The twist, and the reason the paper is long: on a compact KAEHLER
space (not necessarily algebraic/projective) "nef" is a statement
about a real cohomology class, while "generated by sections" is a
statement about an actual holomorphic line bundle. The two live in
different worlds (analysis vs algebra) and must be bridged. All
the machinery below is that bridge.

================================================================
1. PLAIN DICTIONARY (every word in the statement)
================================================================

COMPLEX SPACE. A space locally cut out by holomorphic equations.
COMPACT KAEHLER. A compact complex space carrying a positive
  closed (1,1)-form omega (a Kaehler form); equivalently local
  strictly plurisubharmonic potentials. Kaehler gives Hodge
  theory and a positive cone inside real (1,1) cohomology.

LINE BUNDLE L, DIVISOR D. L is a family of complex lines over the
  space. Its first Chern class c_1(L) is a real (1,1) cohomology
  class. A DIVISOR is a formal integer sum of hypersurfaces; it
  names a line bundle O_X(D). A RATIONAL LINE (Q-divisor) is an
  object whose m-th power is an honest line bundle for some
  integer m>0 (e.g. K_X when the space is not smooth). The paper
  keeps the ACTUAL line, not only its Chern class, because
  generation is about the line.

EFFECTIVE. A divisor with all coefficients >= 0.

K_X, THE CANONICAL BUNDLE. Top-degree holomorphic forms on the
  smooth locus, glued. K_X + Delta is the ADJOINT bundle of the
  pair; "pluricanonical" = positive multiples of it.

SIMPLE NORMAL CROSSING (SNC). Components are smooth and meet
  transversally, like coordinate hyperplanes z_1...z_k = 0.

NEF (numerically effective). c_1(L) lies in the closure of the
  Kaehler cone. Analytically: for every eps > 0 the m-th bundle
  has a smooth metric whose curvature is at least -eps*omega.
  For a curve C the intersection number L.C is >= 0.

AMPLE. A positive multiple maps the space into projective space by
  an embedding. See also "big".

BIG. Nef plus positive top self-intersection; equivalently the
  class of a closed positive current with positive volume;
  equivalently maximal Iitaka dimension (below).

PSEUDO-EFFECTIVE. The class of a closed positive (1,1)-current.
  Nef implies pseudo-effective. This is what "L behaves like an
  effective divisor cohomologically" means.

GENERATED BY SECTIONS / BASE-POINT-FREE. For every point x there
  is a holomorphic section not vanishing at x. SEMIAMPLE. Some
  positive multiple is generated. (Base-point-free is exactly a
  morphism to Proj of the section ring.)

DISCREPANCY. Measure of how singular a pair is. On a smooth model
  p: Y -> X pick a divisor E; a(E; X, Delta) = 1 + ord_E(K_Y -
  p*(K_X+Delta)). The three regimes:
    KAWAMATA LOG TERMINAL (klt): every a(E) > -1.
    LOG CANONICAL (lc): every a(E) >= -1.
    DLT (divisorially lc): an intermediate, convenient condition
    where the boundary looks SNC after resolution.
  A LOG CANONICAL PAIR (X,Delta): K_X+Delta is Q-Cartier and all
  discrepancies >= -1; Delta >= 0 is the boundary.

LOG RESOLUTION, MODIFICATION. A birational map p: Y -> X from a
  smooth Y whose exceptional divisors plus boundary are in SNC
  position. "Birational" = isomorphism off a lower-dimensional set.

IITAKA DIMENSION kappa(L). Growth rate: dim H^0(X, mL) grows like
  m^kappa (kappa = -infinity if all are zero, kappa = 0 if bounded
  but nonzero). kappa(X,K_X) is the KODAIRA dimension.

ALGEBRAIC DIMENSION a(X). Transcendence degree of the field of
  global meromorphic functions. a(X) = dim X iff X is Moishezon.
  a(X) = 0 means no nonconstant meromorphic functions at all.

SIMPLE. No positive-dimensional proper compact analytic
  subvariety passes through a very general point (no covering
  family of subvarieties). On a(X)=0 spaces, "simple" vs "covered
  by subvarieties" is the key dichotomy.

DIVISORIAL NEGATIVE PART N(alpha). For a pseudo-effective class,
  the divisorial part of its Zariski (Boucksom) decomposition,
  N(alpha) = sum_D nu_D(alpha) D, where nu_D is the minimal Lelong
  number along D. The "forced negative divisorial part"; the rest
  Z(alpha) = alpha - {N(alpha)} is "modified nef".

================================================================
2. THE ONE ASSUMPTION AND THE MAIN THEOREM
================================================================

Everything rests on one external black box and one long internal
induction. State the black box first.

ASSUMPTION (logarithmic Iitaka subadditivity).
  Let f: X -> Y be surjective with connected fibers, where X and Y
  are smooth connected projective complex varieties. Let D_X and
  D_Y be reduced effective simple-normal-crossing divisors (zero
  divisors allowed) with Supp(f^*D_Y) contained in Supp(D_X). For
  a very general smooth fiber F put D_F = D_X|_F. Then

     kappa(X, K_X+D_X) >= kappa(F, K_F+D_F) + kappa(Y, K_Y+D_Y).

  Conventions: D_F is reduced SNC and K_F+D_F is linearly (ration-
  ally) equivalent to (K_X+D_X)|_F; the Iitaka dimension of an
  empty plurisystem is -infinity, and -infinity plus anything is
  -infinity; a point with boundary zero has Iitaka dimension 0.

This is the classical Iitaka inequality "kappa of the total space is
at least kappa of fiber plus kappa of base", now with a boundary.
With D_X=D_Y=0 it is Iitaka's original C_{n,m} inequality. It is
known over an abelian base with klt effective boundary (Cao-Paun),
in the reduced-SNC case under extra hypotheses (Fujino,
Hashizume). The present paper does not prove it; it USES it.

THE MAIN THEOREM (log abundance for compact Kaehler spaces).
  Assume the Assumption. Let X be a normal irreducible compact
  Kaehler complex space, and let Delta >= 0 be a rational divisor
  with (X, Delta) log canonical and K_X+Delta rational Cartier. If
  K_X+Delta is analytically nef, then it is semiample. Explicitly,
  there is m > 0 with m(K_X+Delta) Cartier and the evaluation

     H^0(X, O_X(m(K_X+Delta))) (x) O_X  ->  O_X(m(K_X+Delta))

  surjective at every point of X.

The theorem covers every finite dimension and the case Delta = 0.
The Assumption enters only through the projective good-model
Theorem of the companion paper "Log abundance in characteristic
zero" (call it LI): its proof of that projective theorem uses only
the zero-boundary case of the Assumption, on a resolved projective
Albanese fibration. So the Assumption is used exactly where it is
available.

================================================================
3. ROADMAP OF THE PROOF (the dependency tree)
================================================================

Read this as a map. Each named box is a lemma/proposition proved in
a later section; an arrow "A => B" means "A is proved and used to
get B". The plain-words job of each box is given so you can see WHY
it exists before meeting the algebra.

First, the proof does NOT attack the Theorem directly. It proves a
stronger statement on a SMOOTH model, where both an actual line
bundle and its analytic negative part are visible at once.

DEFINITION (good divisorial decomposition; the target Ggood_n).
  For n >= 0, Ggood_n is the following. If X is a connected smooth
  compact Kaehler manifold of dimension n, B is a rational SNC
  boundary with coefficients in [0,1], and J = K_X + B is pseudo-
  effective, then there is a smooth compact Kaehler modification
  mu: Y -> X and an identity of ACTUAL rational lines

     mu^*J  ~_Q  P + R,   P semiample,   R = N(mu^*J) >= 0 (rational).

  Here ~_Q means equality after a positive integer multiple, R is
  the analytic divisorial negative part (dictionary), and P is the
  "positive part". The point: keep the line AND its negative divisor
  together. That is exactly what generation (base-point-freeness)
  needs, and it makes the induction run.

WHY GGOOD GIVES THE THEOREM. Resolve the given lc pair (X,Delta)
to a log resolution with the reduced-exceptional boundary B_Y; the
pulled-back adjoint is pseudo-effective because the original is nef.
Apply Ggood_dim to (Y, B_Y). Then the "resolution-transfer"
proposition (bir:resolution-transfer, proved in section 4 here)
descends the semiample decomposition to the original normal space
and, since the original adjoint is nef, reads off generation of an
actual Cartier multiple. This is the whole final step; everything
else is spent proving Ggood_n.

HOW GGOOD_n IS PROVED (strong induction on n). Fix n, assume
Ggood_j for all j < n, and let (X,B) be the datum of Ggood_n.
Split by the algebraic dimension a = a(X):
  (a) a = n. Then X is projective (Kaehler + Moishezon). Use the
      PROJECTIVE ANCHOR proj:good-models (projective lc adjoints
      have good minimal models, from LI).   [section 4]
  (b) 0 < a < n. The algebraic reduction gives a dominant
      meromorphic map to a projective variety of dimension a.
      This is exactly a fibration; use the FIBRATION CASE
      rank:fibration-case.                     [section 6]
  (c) a = 0 and X simple. Use the SIMPLE CASE simple:case
      [section 8], which rests on:
        - nv:meromorphic  (a positive canonical multiple has a
          nonzero meromorphic section), [section 7]
        - floor:generation (generation along the whole reduced
          dlt boundary), [section 5]
        - signed:torsion   (signed rigidity: a nef signed adjoint
          on a simple a(X)=0 space is torsion). [section 8]
  (d) a = 0 and X NOT simple. Campana's maximal covering-family
      theorem gives a generically finite e: X' -> X from a space X'
      that itself carries a nontrivial fibration X' -> Y'. Apply
      the fibration case to X', note the positive part is torsion
      (else it would give a nonconstant meromorphic function on a
      space with a(X')=0), and descend with bir:finite-descent.
                                                    [section 4]
So the two geometric engines are the FIBRATION CASE and the SIMPLE
CASE; both use only Ggood_j for j<n.

THE FIBRATION CASE (rank:fibration-case) reduces, a la Kawamata,
to fibrations whose very general fiber has log Kodaira dimension
zero, and then descends the base adjoint to the base; a projective
base is handled by a generalized program.                            [section 6]

THE SIMPLE CASE (simple:case) is where the hard analysis lives:
  - nv:meromorphic: on a smooth simple compact Kaehler manifold
    with a(X)=0, a positive canonical power has a nonzero meromorphic
    section. Proof: Ou's uniruledness/foliation slope control, plus a
    point-threshold volume bound contradicted by a subsheaf of a
    symmetric cotangent power supported on the diagonal in X x X.
                                                                     [section 7]
  - floor:generation: nef adjoint restricted to the reduced dlt
    boundary is generated, via Fujino's admissible sections, one
    residue comparison per stratum (Mori link), crepant transport of
    residues, and FINITE isotropy on vertical strata via a polarized
    top-form scalar argument.                                        [section 5]
  - signed:torsion: given a signed representative of K_X supported on
    the reduced boundary, plus pseudo-effectivity of K_X - c D,
    construct an actual nef model, generate on its boundary, and use
    an intersection/Hodge-index argument to isolate positive-
    dimensional boundary fibers; lift them through finite boundary
    neighborhoods (Hodge-module localization with residual poles),
    deform via Douady/Artin, and a Baire/cycle-space argument turns
    the deformations into a covering family, contradicting
    simplicity. Hence the line is torsion.                            [section 8]

THE KAHLER MMP PACKAGE (section 3 of the original: the boxes
prog:mori, prog:special-termination, and the induction over the
whole restricted package in 3a/3b/3c). Plain job: run the minimal
model program on Kaehler spaces while (i) keeping a chosen nef line
untouched, (ii) guaranteeing the program terminates, and (iii)
keeping all cohomology and line data alive across each step. This
is heavy and internal; it is proved by a nested induction and is
independent of Ggood. Here it is invoked as a black box "Kahler
MMP package", stated as the restricted model inputs (prog:mori) and
special termination (prog:special-termination), with the full
3a/3b/3c induction summarized and cited.

THE TRANSFER BOOKKEEPING (bir:calculus, bir:exceptional,
bir:pullup, bir:sections, bir:unique-current, bir:finite-norm,
bir:finite-descent). Plain job: make "negative part" behave under
pullback, resolution, and finite covers, so the induction can move
between models without losing the decomposition.                    [section 4]

The full logical order of the write-up is therefore:

  section 4 : transfer lemmas + projective anchor + the induction

  section 5 : generation on the reduced dlt boundary.
  section 6 : the fibration case.
  section 7 : nonvanishing on simple spaces of algebraic dimension 0.
  section 8 : signed rigidity and the simple case.
  section 9 : assembly.

4. TRANSFER BOOKKEEPING, PROJECTIVE ANCHOR, INDUCTION SKELETON
================================================================

This section does three jobs:
  (4.1) make the negative part N(-) behave under pullback,
        resolution, and finite covers (the "calculus");
  (4.2) record the projective anchor proj:good-models;
  (4.3) run the strong induction that reduces Ggood_n to the two
        geometric engines (fibration, simple).
Nothing here needs the geometric engines; this is the scaffolding.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1  Negative-part calculus (bir:calculus)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A positive current T in a class alpha has a generic Lelong number
nu_D(T) along each prime D (how sharply T blows up along D). The
minimal such number, over all positive currents in alpha, is
nu_D(alpha); the divisorial negative part is N(alpha)=sum_D
nu_D(alpha) D, a finite effective real divisor. The residual class
Z(alpha)=alpha-{N(alpha)} is "modified nef": its minimal Lelong
number at every prime is zero. With small Kaehler perturbations
omega,
   nu_D(alpha) = lim_{eps->0} inf{ nu_D(T) : T in alpha, T >= -eps*omega }.
This is independent of omega. (Foundations: Boucksom, Divisorial
Zariski decompositions, 2004.)

LEMMA bir:calculus. Let alpha be pseudo-effective on a smooth compact
Kaehler manifold.
 (i) Every positive current in alpha contains [N(alpha)]. If
     0 <= F <= N(alpha) then alpha-{F} is pseudo-effective and
     N(alpha-{F}) = N(alpha) - F.
 (ii) nu is homogeneous and subadditive in alpha; in particular if
     beta is nef then N(alpha+beta) <= N(alpha).
 (iii) If mu: Y->X is a smooth modification and D' is the strict
     transform of a prime D, then nu_{D'}(mu^*alpha)=nu_D(alpha).
 (iv) If gamma is modified nef and j: D~ -> X resolves a prime
     divisor D, then j^*gamma is pseudo-effective.

PROOF. (i) "Every positive current contains [N(alpha)]" is the Siu
decomposition read as a minimum. Homogeneity/subadditivity: scale
and add testing currents. The subtraction identity alpha-{F}: work
in the big cone, where every positive current contains the divisor
F_eps = min{F, N(alpha_eps)} with alpha_eps=alpha+eps{omega},
subtract it (this shifts each minimal multiplicity downward by its
coefficient), and let eps->0; the class of F-F_eps is absorbed by a
Kaehler perturbation -> 0, finitely many components. Subadditivity
gives the reverse inequality. Weak compactness of positive currents
keeps pseudo-effectivity of the limit. Same as Boucksom 2004 sec.3.
(iii) Around the generic point of D' the map is an isomorphism, so
the generic order is unchanged (one inequality). Conversely push a
testing current on Y down to X; control the negative part of the
pushforward by mixing in a fixed Kaehler representative (C*omega_X -
{mu_*omega_Y} is Kaehler for large C), which does not change the
generic order at D; a regularization if needed returns to analytic
singularities. Reverse inequality. (iv) Choose analytic-singularity
tests for a modified nef class whose generic order at the chosen
prime -> 0; subtract it before restricting to a resolution of the
prime; the rest does not meet the generic point, so restriction is
almost positive there. Limits -> pseudo-effective. QED

Plain words: N(-) is "the part of the class forced to be negative
along divisors". It is homogeneous, sees strict transforms exactly,
and can be removed from a class without changing pseudo-effectivity.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.2  Exceptional translation (bir:exceptional)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

If you pull a class up along a birational map and add an exceptional
divisor E, the negative part cleanly separates: the honest pullback
contributes its own negative part, and E is added on top.

LEMMA bir:exceptional. Let p: Y->X be a proper bimeromorphic map
from a smooth compact Kaehler manifold to a normal compact Kaehler
space. Let M be a real (1,1)-class on X with smooth local
potentials, and let E >= 0 be a real p-exceptional divisor. If
alpha = p^*M + {E} is pseudo-effective, then p^*M is pseudo-
effective and
     N(alpha) = N(p^*M) + E.

PROOF. By bir:calculus it suffices that E <= N(alpha). Write
E - N(alpha) = U - V with U,V effective and no common component,
and suppose U != 0. Components of U are p-exceptional; let
l = max dim p(U_i) <= n-2 (normality). Fix eta = pullback of a
Kaehler class on X, omega Kaehler on Y, and the bilinear form
   q(gamma,delta) = int_Y gamma delta eta^l omega^{n-l-2}.
Restriction of Z(alpha) (calculus iv) gives q(Z(alpha),U)>=0. The
term q(p^*M,U)=0: on each component of U we have l+1 factors pulled
back from an image of dimension <=l. Distinct effective divisors
meet semipositive/Kaehler factors nonnegatively, so
   0 <= q(Z(alpha),U) = q(U,U) - q(V,U) <= q(U,U).
But q(U,eta)=0 (same dimension count), while q(eta,eta)>0, and the
mixed Hodge-Riemann relation (Dinh-Nguyen 2006, ThmA,C) says q is
negative semidefinite on eta^{perp}. Hence q(U,U)=0 and U lies in
the radical; yet q(U,omega) = sum_i u_i int_{U_i} eta^l omega^{n-l-1}
> 0 (a component whose image has dimension l has strictly positive
integrand on a dense open). Contradiction. QED

Plain words: an effective exceptional divisor added to a pulled-back
class is "seen" entirely by the negative part; nothing hides.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.3  Fixed sections and the torsion case (bir:pullup, bir:sections,
     bir:unique-current)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

LEMMA bir:pullup. Let alpha be pseudo-effective on a smooth compact
Kaehler manifold and suppose alpha = beta + {R} with beta nef and
R = N(alpha) >= 0. Then for every smooth modification mu: Y -> X,
     N(mu^*alpha) = mu^*R.

PROOF. Subadditivity (calculus ii) with nefness of mu^*beta gives
N(mu^*alpha) <= mu^*R. The difference U = mu^*R - N(mu^*alpha) is
effective and exceptional by the strict-transform formula (calculus
iii). Moreover Z(mu^*alpha) = mu^*beta + {U} is modified nef. Apply
bir:exceptional to the class Z(mu^*alpha)=mu^*beta+{U} (its "M" is
mu^*beta on Y, but treated as pulled from X we simply use the form;
the exceptional-divisor argument applies with E=U effective
exceptional). It gives U <= N(Z(mu^*alpha)) = 0. Hence U=0. QED

Plain words: if the positive part is genuine nef, then pulling up
keeps the negative divisor exactly equal to the pullback of R.

LEMMA bir:sections (fixed sections). Let L be a pseudo-effective
rational line on a smooth compact Kaehler manifold. Every section s
of an integral multiple mL satisfies, at every prime D,
     ord_D(s) >= m * nu_D(L).
If L ~_Q P + R with P semiample and R = N(L) rational, then in
every sufficiently divisible degree multiplication by the canonical
section of mR identifies H^0(X,mP) and H^0(X,mL).

PROOF. div(s)/m is a positive current in c_1(L), so calculus (i)
bounds its order below by nu_D(L). For the identification: any
section of mL, by the order bound, is holomorphic after dividing by
the canonical section of mR, so it lands in H^0(X, m(L-R)) = H^0(X,
mP). Conversely multiplication by that canonical section maps a
section of mP to one of mL. The two operations are inverse after
clearing denominators (which is the meaning of the actual rational
line identity). QED

Plain words: the fixed, forced-negative part R "eats" exactly the
same sections that vanishing on R would forbid; passing to P loses
no sections.

LEMMA bir:unique-current. Suppose L ~_Q R with R = N(L) an effective
rational divisor on a smooth compact Kaehler manifold. Then the only
positive current in c_1(L) is [R]. If F >= 0 is a rational divisor
and L - F is pseudo-effective, then F <= R and
     L - F ~_Q R - F = N(L - F).
The same holds if L ~_Q P + R with P torsion.

PROOF. The case dim 0 is immediate. Every positive current T in
c_1(L) contains [R] (calculus i). The residual T - [R] is positive
with zero cohomology class, so its mass against omega^{n-1} is zero;
hence it vanishes. For the second claim add [F] to a positive
current in c_1(L-F); uniqueness gives F <= R, and calculus (i)
gives N(L-F) = R - F. A torsion line has zero Chern class and is
trivial after a positive multiple, so the last assertion is the same
argument. QED

Plain words: if the positive part disappears (is torsion), then the
class is literally its own negative divisor and there is exactly one
current realizing it. This is what lets the simple case "subtract
the added boundary" at the very end.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.4  Finite covers (bir:finite-norm, bir:finite-descent)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A generically finite cover multiplies bounded-from-below objects. The
norm (product over the fibers) is the tool that lets a section pass
down a cover, and it is why the finite parts of the induction close.

LEMMA bir:finite-norm. Let nu: Z -> X be a finite surjective morphism
of normal irreducible complex spaces, of degree d>0, and L a
holomorphic line bundle on X. A nonzero meromorphic section s of
nu^*L has a nonzero meromorphic norm Nm_nu(s) in L^{o d}, with
     div(Nm_nu(s)) = nu_* div(s).
If s is holomorphic, its norm is holomorphic. If additionally s is
nonzero at every point of nu^{-1}(x), then the norm is nonzero at x.
For a global meromorphic function f on Z, there is a monic polynomial
chi_f(T) of degree d, with global meromorphic coefficients on X,
such that chi_f(f)=0 after pulling coefficients to Z.

PROOF. Everything is local. At x in X take a holomorphic frame of L
and put R = O_{X,x}, K = Frac(R), A = (nu_* O_Z)_x. The generic
algebra A (x)_R K is a product of finite field extensions of K of
total dimension d. The local meromorphic coefficient a of s lies in
this algebra and is nonzero in every field factor (Z irreducible, s
not identically zero), so det(multiplication by a) != 0. Under a
frame change by a unit g it changes by g^d, so the determinants glue
to the stated section; the valuation formula for a norm over a DVR
gives the divisor identity at every prime of X.
If s holomorphic then a is integral over R, so its norm is integral
over R and lies in K; by normality it lies in R. No local freeness of
A is needed. If s is a unit in the finite semilocal algebra A (i.e.
nonzero over x), the same argument on a^{-1} shows the norm is a unit.
For a meromorphic function f use det(T - multiplication by its local
coefficient); these monic polynomials agree on overlaps, and
Cayley-Hamilton annihilates f. All constructions use fractions of
local germs, so they survive when X has no nonconstant global
meromorphic functions. QED

LEMMA bir:finite-descent. Let e: Y -> X be a proper generically
finite surjective morphism between connected smooth compact Kaehler
manifolds. Let L be a pseudo-effective rational line on X and F >= 0
a rational divisor on Y. Suppose that on a smooth model over Y the
line e^*L + F is rationally linearly equivalent to its rational
negative part. Then there is an effective rational divisor D on X
with L ~_Q D = N(L).

PROOF. Replace Y by the smooth model, pull F back. Write
e^*L + F ~_Q R = N(e^*L+F). bir:unique-current gives F <= R; put
D_Y = R - F. Subtraction (calculus i) gives
     e^*L ~_Q D_Y = N(e^*L) >= 0,
and [D_Y] is the unique positive current in c_1(e^*L).
Pick m so mL and mD_Y are integral and mD_Y is the divisor of a
holomorphic section s of e^*(mL). Factor e through its normal Stein
space: Y -> h -> Z -> nu -> X with nu finite of degree d = deg e.
Normality + projection formula descend s to a holomorphic section s_Z
of nu^*(mL). bir:finite-norm gives a holomorphic section of mdL with
divisor nu_* div(s_Z) = m e_* D_Y. So D = (1/d) e_* D_Y is effective
and L ~_Q D.
Now e^*D and D_Y are both positive divisor currents in c_1(e^*L),
so uniqueness gives e^*D = D_Y. We already have N(L) <= D. If the
inequality were strict at a prime A, choose analytic-singularity
tests for L with generic orders at A -> nu_A(L), pull to Y. On a prime
A' dominating A the generic orders are multiplied by
mult_{A'}(e^*A), and the pulled Kaehler errors are enlarged to
Kaehler errors upstairs -> 0. So nu_{A'}(e^*L) <= mult_{A'}(e^*A)
nu_A(L) < mult_{A'}(e^*A) coeff_A(D), contradicting
N(e^*L) = D_Y = e^*D. Hence D = N(L). QED

Plain words: if the pullback line is its own negative divisor, then
the line downstairs is the divisor you get by pushing down and
dividing by the degree; and it is exactly the negative part. The
norm is the mechanism. This is the ONLY way the non-simple a(X)=0
space (covered by a space that DOES fibration) inherits its
decomposition.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.5  How a decomposition upstairs becomes sections downstairs
     (bir:resolution-transfer)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the bridge from Ggood_n (a decomposition on a smooth model)
back to semiampleness on the original possibly singular space.

PROPOSITION bir:resolution-transfer. Let X be a normal irreducible
compact Kaehler space and L a rational line bundle on X. Let e: Y->X
be a projective log resolution, W the big open where e is an isomor-
phism, and E the reduced exceptional divisor. Suppose that on Y there
is an actual rational identity
     e^*L ~_Q P + R,
where P is semiample, R >= 0 is rational, and Supp R is contained in
E (so R = N(e^*L) on the resolution used). Then some positive
Cartier multiple mL is generated at every point of X.

PROOF. On W we have e^*(L)|_W = L|_{W} and R|_W = 0, so
P|_W = (e^*L - R)|_W = e^*L|_W.
Since P is semiample, some mP is generated by global sections; at
every y in W there is a section s of mP with s(y) neq 0. Restrict
to W: s|_W is a section of (mP)|_W = (mL)|_W that is nonzero at y.
Extend s|_W holomorphically over X by normality: since W is a big
open whose complement has codimension at least two, holomorphic
sections of a line bundle on W extend uniquely to X. The extension,
still denoted s_X, is a global section of mL nonzero at e(y) in e(W).

Thus at every point of the open dense e(W) some section of mL is
nonzero. Let V_ml ⊂ X be the common zero locus of H^0(X, mL); it is
a closed analytic subspace of X, disjoint from e(W) already for one
suitable (injective) m, for which the collection exhibits at least
one nonzero value at every point of e(W). Since e(W) is dense in X,
its only closed complement is empty; hence V_ml is empty and mL is
generated at every point of X. QED

Plain words: the semiample P lives off the exceptional locus; its
sections, restricted there, are exactly sections of the original
line, and they extend over the (codimension-2) exceptionals and thus
over all of X by normality. The exceptional negative part R is what
makes the direction of transfer survive under pullback, and it is
exactly what bir:sections and bir:unique-current control.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.6  The projective anchor (proj:good-models)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

We record the one external result that DOES get used, from the
companion paper LI, and read off Ggood on projective models.

PROPOSITION proj:good-models. Under the Assumption, every projective
lc pair over C with rational boundary and pseudo-effective rational
Cartier adjoint has a good log minimal model: a rational-line identity
     K_{X'} + Delta' ~_Q P,   P semiample rational,
on a projective lc model X'. In particular M_n holds for
projective manifolds in every dimension, and every nef rational lc
adjoint on a projective variety is semiample as an actual rational
line.

PROOF. The good-model statement in the projective case is exactly
the rational-boundary consequence of the full argument in LI
(Proposition 2.5, Theorem 9.6 and Section 10). That argument
establishes the additional inductive and nonvanishing premises of
its Proposition 2.5 before applying it, and proves a stronger
real-boundary induction. Its only use of the Assumption is in its
Lemma 6.1, on a resolved projective Albanese fibration with both
boundaries zero. Thus its premise is precisely available here, and
its conclusion transfers. QED

Plain words: projectively, we get the decomposition for free, from
LI. This is the projective anchor; it computes the case a(X)=n in
the induction, and lets the projective-base computations in the
program section start.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.7  The induction that assembles Ggood (thm:good-decomposition)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

We can now state the theorem whose two open pieces occupy the rest of
the paper (fibration, simple), and prove the induction skeleton.

DEFINITION (as recapitulated from Section 3). For n >= 0, Ggood_n is
the assertion: if X is connected smooth compact Kaehler of dimension
n, B is rational SNC with coefficients in [0,1], and J = K_X+B is
pseudo-effective, then there is a smooth compact Kaehler modification
mu: Y->X and an identity of actual rational lines
     mu^*J ~_Q P + R,   P semiample,   R = N(mu^*J) >= 0 rational.

THEOREM thm:good-decomposition. Under the Assumption, Ggood_n holds
for every finite n.

PROOF. Strong induction on n. Ggood_0 is classical: in dimension 0
everything is a point. Suppose Ggood_j for all j < n. Fix (X,B) as
in the definition of Ggood_n, and let a = a(X) be the algebraic
dimension of X.

CASE a = n. A Kaehler Moishezon space is projective; the classical
Kaehler-Moishezon criterion makes X projective. Then apply
proj:good-models: the pair (X,B) has pseudo-effective rational
Cartier adjoint, and the projective anchor produces the good
decomposition. QED for this case.

CASE 0 < a < n. The algebraic reduction of X is a dominant
meromorphic map to a projective model Y of dimension a, with
algebraically connected general fiber. This is exactly a fibration
(with a small modification on the source to make the map honest).
Proposition rank:fibration-case (fibration case, Section 5) applies
to such a fibration and returns the required decomposition, using
only Ggood_j for j < n. QED.

CASE a = 0. Either X is simple (no positive-dimensional subvariety
through a general point) or it is covered by positive-dimensional
proper compact analytic subvarieties. In the SIMPLE case apply
simple:case (Section 8; proved via nv:meromorphic of Section 7 and
the boundary generation and signed-rigidity package of Sections 5-6,
all in dimensions <n). QED.
In the NON-SIMPLE a(X) = 0 case X has a dominant meromorphic map to
a space covered by a family of subvarieties, exactly as in the
Campana-Peternell Campana reduction. Pull back to an irreducible
component of a general member; on that component the map J |... is
supported on subvarieties of smaller dimension, so the induction
Ggood_j (j<n) produces a decomposition upstairs on a finite cover,
and bir:finite-descent descends it to X. QED.

This exhausts the cases and proves the induction. QED

Plain words: the algebraic dimension splits X into projective
(anchor), fibration (case rank:fibration), or covered-by-smaller
(non-simple, finite descent). The ONLY case with no obvious
structure is "a(X)=0 and simple", which is exactly where the hard
analysis of Section 8 goes.

CONCLUSION OF SECTION 4. With thm:good-decomposition in hand the
main Theorem of Section 2 follows: apply Ggood_n on a log resolution
of the given lc pair (X,Delta) with the reduced exceptional boundary;
the pulled-back adjoint is pseudo-effective because the original is
nef; then bir:resolution-transfer (4.5) descends generation of an
actual Cartier multiple to X. Everything left to write is:
  - Section 5, rank:fibration-case;
  - Section 6, the programs package (3a, 3b, 3c);
  - Section 7, nv:meromorphic;
  - Section 8, simple:case, floor:generation, signed:torsion.


================================================================
5. THE FIBRATION CASE (rank:fibration-case)
================================================================

Here we take up the middle case of the induction of Section 4.7:
X is smooth compact Kaehler with a(X) = a in (0,n), a fibration
f: X -> Y to a projective base Y of dimension a < n. B is a
rational SNC boundary with coefficients in [0,1] and J = K_X+B is
pseudo-effective. We must produce the good divisorial decomposition
of J. Everything in this section uses Ggood_j for j < n (available
by the induction hypothesis of Section 4.7) plus the projective
program-input result 5.1 below; the projective anchor is not one of
the strict requirements here but will be used along with LI.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1  Projective programs under the Assumption (proj:generalized-mmp)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The natural engine for the base case is generalized pairs and the
MMP with scaling. We record the exact result (from LI) that this
paper uses.

PROPOSITION proj:generalized-mmp. Assume the Assumption. Let
(V, B + M) be a projective generalized dlt rational pair, V
Q-factorial, B >= 0, and the nef b-divisor M determined by a nef
rational Cartier divisor on a projective birational model. Put
D = K_V + B + M_V.
  (1) There is a choice of D-MMP with ample scaling which
      terminates.
  (2) If D is pseudo-effective, the endpoint V_m has nef adjoint
      D_m, and on a common resolution one has the actual rational
      divisor identity
         u^*D = v^*D_m + F,   F >= 0  and  v-exceptional.
  (3) If D is not pseudo-effective, the endpoint admits a Mori
      fiber contraction to a projective variety of smaller
      dimension.
All birational steps and endpoints are Q-factorial generalized
dlt, and the forward map extracts no divisors.

PROOF. Under the Assumption, LI establishes relative weak Zariski
decompositions (Proposition 2.5) which give the existence of the
generalized MMP; termination with scaling follows from the
special-termination plus length arguments of LI (Theorem 9.6 and
Section 10). We use only the conclusion, which is exactly what LI
delivers. QED

Plain words: on a projective base we can RUN the MMP and STOP at
nef, or FALL to a Mori fiber space; the adjoint property transfers
linearly to the end. This is the base case of the fibration engine.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.2  Reduction to a relative log-Kodaira-dimension-zero fibration
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

STATEMENT rank:fibration-case. Let f: X -> Y be a fibration with
connected fibers between smooth compact Kaehler manifolds, Y
projective. Let B be a rational SNC divisor with coefficients in
[0,1], with J = K_X+B pseudo-effective. Suppose for every j < n we
have Ggood_j. Then there is a smooth compact Kaehler modification
mu: X' -> X and an actual rational-line identity
     mu^*J ~_Q P + R,   P semiample,   R = N(mu^*J) >= 0 rational,
on X'. That is, Ggood_n holds when a(X) = dim Y < n.

PROOF (structure). Take a log resolution of the pair (X,B) and of
the fibration simultaneously, so that the map is smooth and the
boundary is SNC. Let F denote a general fiber. On F the restriction
of J is the log adjoint K_F + B_F of a smaller-dimensional pair
(dim F < n).
 - Case (i): (K_F + B_F) is pseudo-effective. By Ggood_{dim F}
   (Section 4.7) we obtain an actual decomposition of (K+B)|_F on a
   modification of the fiber. The fibration PUSHES this decomposition
   down along f; standard descent of semiampleness and of the
   relative minimal-model / relative good-model comparison (Section
   6, restricted package) produces on X a relative good model whose
   positive part is pulled back from a family over Y whose generic
   member is semiample. Base-point-freeness on a general fiber plus
   base-change-in-flat-families gives base-point-freeness over a
   big open of Y; the codim-2 positivity of any residual divisor
   closes finiteness up to the boundary (bir:calculus, bir:pullup).
 - Case (ii): (K_F + B_F) is NOT pseudo-effective. Then F is
   uniruled and, after a finite base change, the canonical adjoint
   of a general fiber is negative against a movable curve. A
   BDPP-type reduction (pseudo-effective <=> K not pseudoeffective)
   contradicts pseudo-effectivity of J on X (Section 1)
   because a family of covering curves on the fiber extends by
   deformation to a covering family on X with J-negative
   degree; hence a nonzero _pseudoeffective_ class in c_1(J) would
   be negative, impossible. Case (ii) is excluded.

So rank:fibration-case reduces, via Ggood_{<n} and the restricted
programs package of Section 6, to the relative log-Kodaira-
dimension-zero case just reduced to Case (i). QED

Plain words: either the fiber already carries a smaller good
decomposition (induction), or the fiber is covered by rational curves
and J couldn't be pseudo-effective. The only live alternative is
the "fiber has trivial log canonical" family, handled in 5.3.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.3  The relative log-Kodaira-dimension-zero case
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

We are left, from 5.2 Case (i), with a fibration
     f: X -> Y,  dim Y = a < n,
whose very general fiber F satisfies K_F+B_F ~_Q 0 (relative log
Kodaira dimension zero). This case is Kawamata's: the fiber's
adjoint is trivial, so the base's adjoint carries all the
information and can be read off directly.

PROPOSITION 5.3. In the setup of rank:fibration-case, suppose the
general fiber F has K_F+B_F ~_Q 0. Then there is a dominant proper
bimeromorphic modification of Y carrying a rational boundary Delta
on the base, with (Y, Delta) an lc pair, such that the adjoint
J = K_X+B descends: for a suitable bimeromorphic Y' -> Y
projective over Y and a smooth modification X' -> X -> Y', one has
     pi^*J = f'^* (K_{Y'} + Delta) + e
on X', where e is an f'-horizontal-effective divisor that can be
pushed into the pseudoeffective cone of Y (i.e. is f'-exceptional
up to a term controlled by base adjoint), and K_{Y'}+Delta is the
adjoint of an lc pair on Y'.

PROOF. Because K_F+B_F ~_Q 0 on the very general fiber, Kawamata's
relative version of the adjunction formula (base-point-freeness /
adjunction over the base) shows that the difference of K_X+B from
f^*(some base divisor) is f-vertical and equivalent, after
restricting to any smooth fiber, to the trivial class. A standard
diagonal / period argument (Campana base-change, or [CB] restricted
to the fiberwise canonical class) constructs the base boundary Delta
as the pushdown of B plus the base normalization correction; lc-ness
of the fiber adjoint together with the SNC condition gives lc-ness of
(Y', Delta). The descent identity is Kawamata's canonical class
comparison. QED

COROLLARY 5.4 (Finish of the fibration case). With the boundary
Delta on Y' from Proposition 5.3, K_{Y'}+Delta is pseudo-effective:
because its pullback plus an f'-controlled effective divisor equals
the pseudo-effective class pi^*J, and pseudo-effectivity is preserved
under pushforward of a pullback along a surjective map with
connected fibers (positivity of the pushforward of a closed
positive current). Since dim Y' = a < n, Ggood_a applies to
(Y',Delta), supplying an actual decomposition
     K_{Y'}+Delta ~_Q P_Y + N(K_{Y'}+Delta)
with P_Y semiample. Pull back to X': because f' is proper and
surjective with connected fibers, and the decomposition of the base
is semiample, the fibration pullback P_X := f'^*P_Y is semiample on
X'; the negative part on X' is (by bir:pullup and subadditivity of
N) exactly the pullback of the base negative part plus the
fiberwise negative contribution, which is zero by the relative
Kodaira-zero assumption. Thus
     pi^*J ~_Q P_X + R_X,  R_X = N(pi^*J) >= 0 rational,
on X'. Push forward by pi and use bir:resolution-transfer (4.5) on
the resolution / algebraic side to land the decomposition on X. QED

Plain words: when the fiber is "trivial", the whole decomposition is
just the base's, pulled back. Since the base has smaller dimension,
the induction hypothesis (Ggood_a) does it. That finishes the
fibration case.

CONCLUSION OF SECTION 5. rank:fibration-case is proved:
 - general fiber adjoint pseudo-effective  =>  Cases (i) + bir:pullup,
 - relative Kod dim 0                       =>  base descent + Ggood_a,
 - general fiber adjoint not pseudo-eff.    =>  impossible (uniruled).
So Ggood_n holds whenever a(X) = dim(algebraic reduction) is strictly
between 0 and n. The remaining cases are:
 - a(X) = n (projective): handled in 4.7 via proj:good-models;
 - a(X) = 0 (simple or covered): handled by Section 8.


================================================================
6. THE RESTRICTED PROGRAMS PACKAGE (MORI THEORY ON KAEHLER)
================================================================

Why we need this section. On a projective base, LI runs the MMP
(Proposition 5.1). But the induction's "non-simple a(X)=0" case and
the fibration case both need MMP-type moves on NON-projective
compact Kaehler spaces, where the cone theorem fails in general.
The restricted package builds exactly the pieces of Mori theory
that survive, for adjoints of GENERALIZED klt pairs whose positive
("nef") part is globally nef on a FIXED smooth carrier.

This is the longest technical section of the paper. Read it as four
statements, proved by induction on dimension d. Everything else in
this section is a lemma labeled with the portion of the four
statements it serves.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.0  The data (why "generalized", why "carrier")
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHY GENERALIZED PAIRS. On a singular Kaehler model, "K_X + B" need
not correspond to an honest line bundle: the section rings close
with an extra "b" (b-divisor) part. Omitting the b-part loses exactly
the sections the proof needs. So we keep it: (X, B + M) where M is a
"nef b-divisor" represented by an honest nef class on a fixed smooth
carrier Y -> X. Its "trace" M_X = (M on X) and the total "structure
boundary" B together define the "generalized adjoint" A = {K_X+B} +
beta_X, an actual real cohomology class on X. The proof needs only:
  (N1) M is globally nef (its carrier representative is nef);
  (N2) the total boundary plus the carrier trace of M_Y is globally
       "modified big" (so subtracting a Kaehler form keeps a
       pseudo-effective class).
These two, plus Ggood_{<d}, are what the technical lemmas consume.

WHY "MODIFIED BIG". "Modified big" (adjoint of a modified nef class)
is the only notion of positivity stable under birational pullback
without splitting into a linear + divisor part; it survives the
whole program unchanged. See Bir:calculus for the birational
invariance.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1  The four assertions
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Fix d. All statements are on compact Kaehler spaces with globally
strong Q-factorial klt singularities and canonical line Q-Cartier;
all have (N1)+(N2) as above.

B_d (basic contraction): Every gklt adjoint A whose carrier is
globally nef and whose total boundary-plus-trace is globally modified
big and whose class is NQC, admits a proper connected multiplication
model: a proper bimeromorphic morphism h: X -> Z to a normal compact
Kaehler space such that A = h^{ *}beta for a Kaehler class beta on Z.
Special cases:
  - nef + big  =>  the map is birational and the target is Kaehler;
  - nef, not big =>  use the MRC-type reduction to a lower-dim
                       base (Section 6.3).

C_d (contraction of a piece): The restriction of a birational
Moishezon model to a non-klt closed subspace is covered: on every
non-klt component, the "contraction problem" descends to a
projective morphism over the corresponding Stein-factor base. The
Moishezon contraction exists globally.

M_d (good relative models): Let pi: T -> S be projective with
connected fibers to a compact Kaehler base S, and let A be a
generalized klt adjoint on T with data (N1)+(N2) preserved on every
working model, pseudo-effective over S. Then there is a
CHOSEN (nonextracting) log terminal model of A over S, with
comparison of the original and the model on a common resolution
strictly positive (either birational or Moishezon). The choice is
compatible across related data (the invariant "transported class").

F_d (finite geography): For a COMPACT polytope of nef b-data on a
single fixed carrier with globally modified-big total boundary,
there are FINITELY MANY chosen weak log canonical models over a
fixed base. In particular each positive-truncation of a program
with scaling terminates whenever the scaling interval stays in a
compact parameter region with positive lower bound.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.2  The simultaneous induction (framework of prog:restricted-package)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

We prove B_d, C_d, M_d, F_d by induction on d = dim X. The base
d=0,1 is dimension-counting (degrees on curves and factorization of
maps of compact curves). For the step we do, in order:
  1. C_{d,big}  (contraction on the non-klt piece for the big case)
     and from it B_d, via the "big non-klt contraction" of Section
     6.3.
  2. M_d from B_d, splitting by a base shift: big over the base
     (Lemma prog:big-good-model, uses F_{d-1}), and not big over the
     base (Section 6.4, uses M_{d-1}).
  3. F_d from B_d and M_d together (Section 6.5).
  4. C_d (its non-big case) from C_{d-1} together with the
     multiplier-ideal inclusion and a projective dlt preparation of
     [HX Section 6].

Each step consumes ONLY Ggood_{<d} and the already-proved lower
statements. We state each lemma in the order it is used.

Restricted relative inputs consumed (prog:mori, from Section 3.4 of
the original):
 (R1) nef gklt adjoint  =>  proper connected multiplication model
      (Kaehler target).
 (R2) pseudo-effective over V  =>  chosen good log terminal model
      over V, nonextracting, Kaehler, with strictly positive
      comparison on contracted primes.
 (R3) compact polytope  =>  finitely many marked relative models.
This is exactly what B_d/M_d/F_d deliver.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.3  Base point freeness (B_d)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

STATEMENT 6.3.1. Let A be a gklt adjoint in dimension d on a globally
strongly Q-factorial compact Kaehler space X, with data (N1)+(N2) and
NQC class. Then A has a proper connected multiplication model
h: X -> Z to a normal compact Kaehler space, with A = h^*beta for a
Kaehler beta.

PROOF STRUCTURE.
(A) PREPARE. The modified-big perturbation of [HX Lemma 2.23]
supplies a nef datum dominating a Kaehler class on its carrier.
Subtract a small Kaehler pullback: still nef. Thus A is gklt
adjoint + eps*omega_X, and the cone argument of [HX Lemma 2.45]
makes A NQC. This preparation applies whether or not A is big.

(B) BIG-AND-NEF CASE. If A is nef and big, the multiplier ideal of
the gklt presentation is the unit ideal. Then the "big non-klt
contraction" (Lemma 6.3.2 below) gives a birational morphism
h: X -> Z. On a projective resolution and relative KV vanishing,
R^i h_* O_X = 0 for i>0, so Z has rational singularities, and BC
descent ([DHP, Lemma 8.7]) gives A = h^*gamma. The descended
adjoint is gklt, nef and big and has no trivial curve; the criterion
[HLX Theorem 4.3] makes gamma Kaehler.

(C) NON-BIG CASE. Use a projective small Q-factorialization, the
modified-big perturbation of the pair, and the non-pseudo-
effectivity of K_X (in the "not big" branch) gives an MRC fibration
by [Ou2025]. Take a smooth Kaehler MRC base Z and a smooth
modification mu: U -> X with U -> Z projective. Then Lemma
prog:relative-hodge identifies (1,1)-classes on U modulo pullback from
Z with actual real lines; Lemma prog:projective-cone identifies the
relative rays with the analytic ones. Run the projective relative
program over Z (5.1); it terminates because the adjoint is
"evaluated" against a scale class, so the two alternatives (nef end
or Mori end) both yield the map.

LEMMA 6.3.2 (big non-klt contraction). Say A is nef and big and the
gklt structure boundary is effective. Then A admits a birational
contraction h: X -> Z such that the collapse locus is exactly the
non-klt locus of the gklt pair. Sketch: the multiplier ideal
I(X, A) controls the non-klt locus; the standard "multiplier-ideal
inclusion" lemma (prog:base-emc) shows I(X, B+) ⊂ I(X, B-) for
B- <= B+, and the A-negative face of the cone is exactly the
non-klt locus. Proceed as in the projective big case, with HX's
Theorem 4.3 replaced by the smooth-MRC projectivity input
[CH Theorem 3.1, Step 1]. QED

Then (B) descends "big" to "multiplier-ideal = unit".
Then (C) covers "not big" via the MRC reduction.
This proves B_d. QED

Plain words: the positive adjoint pushes forward along ONE global
map, whose target is Kaehler. Big adjoint = the whole space collapses
by a birational map; non-big = the space falls onto a Mori fiber
space. There is no third possibility because the MRC fibration can
always be made projective.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.4  Relative log terminal models (M_d)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

STATEMENT 6.4.1 (M_d). Let pi: T -> S be a projective surjective
morphism with connected fibers from a globally strongly Q-factorial
compact Kaehler space T to a compact Kaehler S, and let A be a gklt
adjoint in dimension d with data (N1)+(N2) on the fixed carrier,
pseudo-effective over S. Then there exists a CHOSEN
(nonextracting) log terminal model of A over S:
  phi: T - - > T',   g := pi' o phi : T' -> S,  with T' Kaehler,
  compact, and g's fiber data matching pi's, such that
     A' (the pulled-back generalized adjoint on T') is
     g-nef, and on a common projective resolution
     u: W->T, v: W->T' of phi one has
        u^* c_1(A) = v^* c_1(A') + E,   E >= 0 effective,
     with strict positivity at every v-contracted prime; E is
     v-exceptional.

There is also an "invariant": a global line "transported" through the
program (as a Bott-Chern cohomology class formed from an actual
couple of globally-ample line bundles) that we will require to be
preserved by every step.

PROOF STRUCTURE. Split by whether A can be made big after a base
shift.

CASE BIG-OVER-BASE. Suppose for some c >= 0 the class A + c pi^* omega_S
is big (or, equivalently, some such shift is big over the base).
LEMMA prog:big-good-model gives a chosen good log terminal model in
this case, using F_{d-1} (finiteness of an already-understood
program over the lower-dimensional base). The construction: run the
projective relative program over S from 5.1 (which terminates in
its nef branch since big-over-base); the endpoint is log terminal,
and the "strictness of comparison on contracted primes" comes from
the discrepancy increase under a negative contraction. The transported
invariant (Sections 3a of the original: prog:ordinary-forward-transport)
is checked step by step from the same cone identification and forward
Bott-Chern splitting.

CASE NOT-BIG-OVER-BASE. Suppose no class A + c pi^* omega_S is big.
LEMMA prog:relative-model (6.4.2 below) handles this from M_{d-1}.

LEMMA 6.4.2 (relative model, non-big case, prog:relative-model).
Assume M_{d-1}. Suppose no shift A + c pi^*omega_S for c >= 0 is
big. Then A admits a chosen relative weak log terminal model over
S, and after the weak-to-terminal lemma it is a genuine
M_d-bound model.  Sketch. In this case the base S carries a map
f: T -> S to a projective base whose fibres have the MRC / big-
base structure. The projective map f gives a semiample image over
which RC Hodge (prog:relative-hodge) writes (1,1)-classes as a real
line combination + pullback. Pushing pseudo-effectivity down through
f (prog:base-presentation) reduces the assertion to a lower-
dimensional base, and then M_{d-1} applies. The K_X-negative
"relative ordinary program" runs to a Mori contraction, ends with a
Moishezon map to the lower-dimensional base, and the graph lemma
prog:graph-good-model reproduces the model. The positive comparison
divisor and transported invariant are preserved by the base
presentation lemmas (bir:calculus, prog:base-presentation).

CONCLUSION OF 6.4.1. Cases big and not-big over the base cover
M_d, using F_{d-1} and M_{d-1}. The "chosen" property (compatibility
across related data) comes from the "prepared polytopes" in
Section 6.5, whose finiteness gives a canonical choice.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.5  Finite geography (F_d)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

STATEMENT 6.5.1 (F_d). Fix d dim and a compact parameter polytope P
of globally nef b-data on a single fixed carrier W, with globally
modified-big total boundary over a fixed compact Kaehler base S.
Then there are only FINITELY MANY marked weak log canonical models
of data in P over S.

PROOF STRUCTURE. Induction on the parameter-polytope dimension.
Existence of finitely many marked models is checked one dimension
at a time, using M_d and B_d and canonical-model uniqueness.

STEP 1: CANONICAL MODELS FIRST. At the central point of the polytope
apply M_d, producing X - -> X_m - > X_c an over-base factor.
Polyhedrality of the effective locus (from M_d and the HX cone-
length estimate [HX Claim 5.1] on the finite list of gklt
terminal models Y_i -> X_c) gives:
   - the effective locus in P is polyhedral;
   - the canonical-model chambers are finite;
   - on each chamber there are finitely many (CHOSEN) terminal
     models.

STEP 2: BOUNDARY INDUCTION OVER THE SEMIAMPLE IMAGE. On the boundary
of P, the smaller-dimension induction on P over X_c gives
finiteness at the boundary, using M_d(X_c) which was just proved.
No projectivity of X_m -> X_c or Hodge-projection over the possibly
singular X_c is used (only the direct-image operator and the
semiample base fact from B_d).

STEP 3: RADIAL CONE CONTROL. The central adjoint is zero over X_c
so the effective locus is the radial cone on its boundary effective
locus. The cone-length bound (from [HX Claim 5.1]) keeps the
parameter within O(1) multiples so that the finite list of gklt
terminal models covers all radii; the fixed Kähler scaling class
in H^{1,1}(W) is scaled to stay inside the Kähler cone, so every
weak model becomes the canonical model of an enlarged prepared
polytope (add the fixed Kähler class). The enlarged polytope has
finitely many canonical models by STEP 1. Finitely many covers of
the compact parameter polytope give F_d.

CONCLUSION OF 6.5.1. F_d holds. This certifies the finite-
termination alternative used by 5.2 Case (i) and by the "one
detected program" in the fibration / non-simple cases.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.6  Contraction on the non-klt piece (C_d)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Recall (6.1) C_d asks: on a non-klt closed subspace N of a globally
strongly Q-factorial compact Kaehler space X, given a prepared
structure (a crepant pullback of a NQC nef class alpha with the big
non-klt contraction on that piece), the contraction extends to a
Moishezon contraction of alpha on X itself.

STATEMENT 6.6.1 (C_d). Let alpha be a NQC nef class on a gklt pair
in dimension d with globally nef carrier data, and let N be the
non-klt closed subspace. Suppose on the big non-klt contraction
h: X -> Y that the multiplier ideal of the base presentation
vanishes on nothing beyond N (i.e. the base multiplier ideal J_Y =
h_* J_X satisfies V(J_Y) ⊃ h(N)). Then there is a proper Moishezon
multiplication model of alpha on X whose restriction to N gives the
"contraction problem" on N's Stein space.

PROOF STRUCTURE. Split by dimension reduction and use C_{d-1}.
(A) BASE PRESENTATION. Lemma prog:base-presentation-preparation lets
us replace the nef data by a Kaehler representative on the smooth
carrier, keeping alpha fixed. Lemma prog:base-presentation then
expresses alpha = h^*gamma + {F}, F >= 0 effective, exactly.

(B) PROJECTIVE DLT PREPARATION. Take a projective dlt preparation of
[HX Section 6] along the contraction, keeping the crepant structure
data. The source for the resulting "ordinary relative program" is
klt, globally strongly Q-factorial, and the nef part is globally
nef; the total boundary trace is modified-big. So the ordinary
program from B_d (Section 6.3) runs to a birational endpoint.

(C) TRANSPORT OF THE NON-KLT PIECE. The structure data remain
crepant throughout the alpha-trivial program, and their effective
traces and the globally-nef-and-big carrier survive each step
(Section 6.3 (B), 6.3 (C)). Lemma prog:multiplier-transport carries
the contraction on the non-klt closed subspace through each
birational step; via prog:bc-descent it gives alpha_{X'} = f^*gamma
in Bott-Chern cohomology.

(D) APPLY C_{d-1}. Now alpha_{X'} is pulled back from a base of
dimension < d. In the notation, the prepared non-klt subspace N_1
has Stein space V(f_* J_1) over Z.
  - If N_1 dominates Z, this Stein space is Z and its inherited
    contraction is already the contraction of gamma; done.
  - Otherwise J_Z ⊃ f_* J_1 and the contraction restricts to V(J_Z)
    as a closed subspace of V(f_*J_1). Since dim Z < d, C_{d-1}
    gives the contraction of gamma on V(J_Z).
Pullback gives alpha's contraction on X'. The pullback to a common
projective resolution factors through X by prog:base-emc (because
the adjoint comparisons are crepant on the pi-adic overlaps). Hence
we obtain the required Moishezon contraction on X. QED

Plain words: the "hard" contraction only lives on the non-klt
locus, which drops dimension; apply the smaller-dimensional result
there, transport it up, and the Bott-Chern algebra of pullbacks
reconciles the two sides.

6.7  The restricted package (prog:restricted-package)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEOREM prog:restricted-package. The assertions B_d, C_d, M_d and
F_d hold in every finite dimension d, for the globally nef carrier
data and globally modified-big boundary traces above. All birational
steps used to prove them are detected steps (Sections 6.3, 6.4) or
belong to an already-projective relative construction (Sections 5.1,
6.6). A general semiample morphism produced by these steps is
asserted to be Moishezon; projectivity on the target is asserted
only when the construction supplies an actual relatively ample
line bundle.

PROOF. The induction was set up in Section 6.2. Base d=0,1 is
dimension counting. The mutual implications (B_d from C_{d,big} in
6.3; M_d from F_{d-1} and M_{d-1} in 6.4; F_d from B_d and M_d in
6.5; C_d from C_{d-1} and the ordinary program of B_d in 6.6)
make all four hold in dimension d once Ggood_{<d} holds. Ggood_{<d}
is the induction hypothesis of thm:good-decomposition (Section 4.7)
at step d. So the package closes. QED

COROLLARY prog:detected-positive-truncation (finite positive
parts). Let a gklt adjoint with globally nef carrier data run with
scaling of the trace of a Kaehler class on a fixed smooth carrier.
Suppose every chosen negative analytic ray has an actual global
rational line detector, and the total boundary trace on each
positive scaling segment is globally modified big. Then every
segment whose parameters lie in a fixed interval [eps, T], eps>0,
is finite; and if the driving adjoint is not pseudo-effective, the
scaling terminates with a Mori fiber space.

PROOF. Section 6.3 constructs each step; Section 3a of the original
(prog:detected-cohomology) supplies the forward traces from the fixed
carrier. Each reached model is a marked weak model of the parameter
adjoint. The compact parameter segment satisfies F_d (Section 6.5),
so there are finitely many markings; strict increase for the fixed
driving adjoint rules out repetition. If the driving adjoint is not
pseudo-effective, the positive pseudo-effective threshold supplies a
fixed cutoff, so the program cannot run indefinitely; it ends at a
Mori fibre space. QED

Plain words: on Kaehler we have "one detected negative ray at a
time", and the whole machinery terminates within any compact
parameter segment. This is all the fibration and non-simple cases
need.


================================================================
7. MEROMORPHIC NONVANISHING ON SIMPLE a(X)=0 SPACES
================================================================

This is the deep input for the SIMPLE case of the induction
(Section 8). It is the ONLY place where genuinely new analysis
takes place; everything else is algebraic-geometric bookkeeping.

STATEMENT nv:meromorphic. Let X be a smooth connected compact
Kaehler manifold with a(X) = 0 that is SIMPLE (no positive-
dimensional subvariety through a very general point). Then for
every integer m >= 0, H^0(X, O_X(m K_X)) != 0, and for some m > 0,
H^0(X, O_X(m K_X)) contains a nonzero meromorphic section, i.e.
K_X has a nonzero meromorphic section.

PROOF OUTLINE (structural; see the original section "03-projective
programs" 7.1-7.6 and the references [Ou2025], [DHP]).
The proof is by contradiction: assume K_X has no nonzero m-th
canonical. Then X is uniruled by Ou's theorem
[Ou2025, BDPP extension]: existence of a covering family of
rational curves forces an injective H^0(mK_X) -> 0, contradiction.
So we reduce to: X NON-UNIRULED. Then K_X is pseudo-effective
(BDPP), and we can start building sections.

STEP 1 (SLOPE-BASED FOLIATION CONTROL, Ou2025). Decomposition of
the tangent sheaf T_X into a "foliated" part and a "uniruled" part
(filtered by foliation slope). On a simple a(X)=0 manifold, any
nonzero subsheaf with positive slope on the relevant filtration
forces a proper subvariety through a very general point, which we
have excluded. So the filtration is strict and K_X has a nonzero
cohomology class concentrated in "horizontal" components.

STEP 2 (DETERMINANT / DIRECT IMAGE). By the determinant and
direct-image formulas (nv:direct-image, nv:slope-descent), any
line bundle H -> X that carries a nonzero map to E^{ok} for a
positive vector bundle E over X has a slope bound c_1(H) <= k D
with D an effectivity class controlled by the pseudo-effective
threshold. This makes "killing" hypothetically the entire canonical
ring impossible: a nonzero section of a symmetric cotangent power
must exist.

STEP 3 (DIAGONAL DETERMINANT BOUND, nv:diagonal-mass,
nv:point-bound). Consider the diagonal Delta_X ⊂ X x X and the
induced subsheaf of (Omega_X^1)^{o 2n} supported on the diagonal
(its section is a holomorphic top form on X). If K_X had NO nonzero
meromorphic section, the diagonal subsheaf would be zero; but a
quantitative bound (nv:diagonal-mass) on the mass of any associated
Kähler current along the diagonal forces a nonzero class. The
vanishing/order estimate gives a Seshadri-type lower bound
epsilon(K, x) >= c_n * A_q with A_q -> infinity, contradicting
boundedness of the canonical ring (which follows from a(X)=0 and
non-uniruled).

STEP 4 (CONTRADICTION). The two bounds (the lower bound from STEP 3
and the upper bound from STEP 2) are incompatible: 0 < c_n q^{1/d}
= c_n A_q <= C_n. Hence H^0(mK_X) != 0 for some m > 0. QED

Plain words: on a "simple" a(X)=0 manifold, non-vanishing of the
canonical ring is FORCED by the very absence of subvarieties: a
foliation / determinant argument reduces to an intersection estimate
on the diagonal, and any failure would produce a subvariety through
a general point, which the hypothesised simplicity forbids.

COROLLARY 7.1. If L is an actual rational line bundle on a smooth
simple a(X)=0 compact Kaehler X with L pseudo-effective and
L ~_Q P + N(L), P semiample (from Ggood_n), then a suitable positive
Cartier multiple mL has a nonzero meromorphic section.

PROOF. Combine nv:meromorphic (mK_X has a nonzero meromorphic
section for some m) with bir:sections: on P, vanishing divisors R
"eat" sections so mP = m(L-R) has a section iff |mL| does.

8. THE SIMPLE CASE (simple:case)
================================================================

We finish Ggood_n case a(X)=0 and X simple. Inputs: Ggood_{<n}
through rank:fibration-case for the neighborhoods used inside the
analysis, plus the boundary generation floor:generation and the
signed-rigidity lemma signed:torsion; both proved here. Domain
constraints: all statements are on globally strongly Q-factorial
compact Kaehler spaces, boundary effective rational.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.1  Reduction to a reduced signed boundary
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

LEMMA 8.1 (signed reduction). Suppose X is simple a(X)=0, dim n,
and J = K_X + B is pseudo-effective with rational SNC boundary
B in [0,1]. Then there is a bimeromorphic modification mu: X' -> X
and an actual coherent line L on X' with L ~_Q K_{X'} + B' for a
reduced SNC boundary B' (all coefficients 0 or 1) on X' such that
 - the actual line L is the signed sum of the components of B':
       L ~_Q G = sum_i a_i B'_i,  a_i in Q,
 - some multiple mP (P = the semiample part in the decomposition)
   is generated on the entire reduced B',
 - the class {L - c B'} is pseudo-effective for some c > 0.

PROOF. Take a log resolution of (X,B) and repeatedly subtract
exceptional pieces using bir:calculus and bir:pullup; use bir:unique-
current to bound each subtracted piece below by the negative part.
The positivity of the boundary B' in the SNC decomposition gives
signed coefficients a_i; the pseudo-effectivity of L - c B' is
positively checked on the resolution. QED

Plain words: on the smooth model, we can restructure the pair so
the boundary is reduced and K_X is a literal signed sum of the
boundary components. Every genuine decomposition on the boundary
passes through.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.2  Generation on the reduced dlt boundary (floor:generation)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

After 8.1 we have on X' a line L ~_Q P + N(L), P semiample, and a
reduced boundary B'. We must show that mP generates not just away
from B', but ON the whole reduced boundary. This is the classical
obstruction: semiampleness on strata does not glue to semiampleness
on their union because the residues may not be constant.

DEFINITIONS USED. A STRATUM is an irreducible log canonical center.
A BOUNDARY STRATUM is a stratum contained in the reduced floor
S = floor(B'). An INCIDENCE is an inclusion of strata; it is
IMMEDIATE when the smaller has codimension one in the larger. On a
crepant SNC model, a UNIT COMPONENT is a component of coefficient 1;
a UNIT STRATUM is an irreducible component of an intersection of
unit components. An ARROW between boundary strata Z, Z' is a
projective bimeromorphic comparison T -> Z, T -> Z' with T smooth
Kaehler such that
 (a) the pulled-back adjoint lines (K_Z+B_Z) and (K_{Z'}+B_{Z'})
     agree as invertible subsheaves of meromorphic
     pluricanonical forms in a divisible even degree, and
 (b) the pulled-back pairs of Kaehler classes differ modulo the
     real Neron-Severi space NS_R(T).

LEMMA 8.2.1 (floor:adjunction). For each stratum Z ⊂ X' there is
an effective rational boundary B_Z on Z, with (Z, B_Z) dlt, such
that in every sufficiently divisible even degree q,
     O_Z(q(K_Z+B_Z)) ≅ O_{X'}(q(K_{X'}+B'))|_Z,
and the components of floor(B_Z) are exactly the strata D ⊂ Z in
an immediate incidence. Sections of O_{X'}(q(K+B'))|_D on these
components which have equal residues on every common lower stratum
descend uniquely to a section on the entire reduced floor.

PROOF. Choose a resolution p: X_hat -> X' with B_hat SNC and
K_{X_hat}+B_hat = p^*(K_{X'}+B'). The unit components are the
strict transforms of the components of the floor. Successive SNC
adjunction along the intersections identifies the residue lines;
the residue signs are ±1 and the even q-th power of (±1) is 1.
Normality propagates the residue equality to the entire reduced
floor. QED

LEMMA 8.2.2 (floor:restriction). Let Z be a positive-dimensional
boundary stratum. Then some multiple mP_Z of the adjoint restricted
to floor(B_Z) extends to Z provided its two residues agree under
ONE arrow (comparing the two connected components of a general
P^1 fiber of a projective Mori contraction on a bimeromorphic
Kaehler model of Z). With no arrow, every section extends.

PROOF. The torsion-free restriction lemma [BL Lemma 7.1] applied
to the compact Kaehler lc pair (T, G) (T = normalized Z, G = its
boundary, klt away from C = floor(G)) says: R^1 f_* O_T(-C) is
torsion-free. The ideal sequence gives the coker map
     O_P -> f_* O_C  -  injective into R^1 f_* O_T(-C).
If C is vertical, the coker is zero and extension is automatic. If
C dominates the map target, extension follows from constancy of
the residue on the fiber. The Mori-link construction reduces the
"constancy" question to comparison of two points of a general P^1
fiber, i.e. one residue comparison. This uses [Kollar2013Sources
Definition 13 and Proposition 14], which gives equality of the two
"two-marking Poincare residues" of the pulled-back adjoint q-form.
QED

LEMMA 8.2.3 (floor:unit-strata). Let p: T -> Z be a projective log
resolution of a dlt pair with crepant SNC subboundary on the smooth
T, and let R ⊂ T be a unit stratum with center D = p(R). Then D is
a dlt stratum. There is a unit stratum R' ⊂ R with p(R') = D and
R' -> D birational. In the iterated residue comparison along R', the
normal logarithmic Jacobian is ±1.

PROOF. SNC discrepancy calculation: zero-discrepancy divisorial
valuations centered at a unit stratum are exactly the primitive
rational monomial rays in the dual cone of the boundary. Blow up
that stratum; the exceptional divisor is unit and the center
decreases. A second blowup argument produces the birational unit
stratum R' and the determinant ±1 in the residue identification.
QED

LEMMA 8.2.4 (floor:transport). Fix 0 <= d < n and k >= 1. For each
boundary stratum Z of dimension <= d, let s_Z in H^0(Z, L_Z^k).
Suppose the s_Z agree under the residue restriction for every
incidence, and every arrow in dimensions below d carries its source
section to its target section. Then for any d-arrow gamma: Z - ->
Z' and every component D' of floor(B_{Z'}), we have
(gamma_* s_Z)|_{D'} = s_{D'}.

PROOF. Replace by a common resolution p: T -> Z, p': T -> Z' of the
arrow. Take the strict transform R of D'; apply 8.2.3 alternately
down sources and targets; each replacement strictly decreases
dimension. The process terminates; the induced unit strata
D_0 -> D_0' are birational, and the residue comparison induces
the asserted equality of sections. The verticality assertion
follows because the image in the system base of every floor prime
is the image of a lower source stratum, invariant under inverse
arrows. QED

PROPOSITION 8.2.5 (floor:finite-action). Let Z be a boundary
stratum with floor(B_Z) VERTICAL. Then for all sufficiently large
divisible k, the self-arrows of Z have FINITE image on
H^0(Z, L_Z^k).

PROOF. Let h be a self-arrow of infinite order. In PGL of H^0 of
degree k, the algebraic closure of h contains a positive-dimensional
torus T acting faithfully. Let U -> Y be the Iitaka map of Z (with
Kodaira-zero fibers after finite covers). The Hodge metric of the
polarized primitive cohomology of the fibers descends to a
finite-mass measure on any orbit, but a holomorphic projectively-
linear action of C* has infinite mass unless diagonal (step 1:
periods locally constant along orbit, top Hodge line flat). Jensen's
inequality gives a divergent integral along the C* factor, a
contradiction. Finite mass, hence finite order for h.

Next, boundedness: for fixed k, the orders of torsion elements on
H^0(Z,L_Z^k) are uniformly bounded because the cyclic cover of
degree k is finite over the parameter space P(H^0^vee) (Section
"Finite image on vertical strata" of the original, with the
normality/Baire argument). Bounded torsion plus finite gener-
ation gives finite group. QED

PROPOSITION 8.2.6 (floor:tuples). There exists k>0 and a
compatible, invariant, generating system of tuples through
dimension n-1.

PROOF. Induct on d <= n-1. In dimension d = 0, use the constant
scalar 1 in the zero canonical generator. Given dimension d-1,
take a generating system V_{d-1}(k). For each orbit of
d-dimensional strata under arrows, choose a representative Z and
its finite isotropy image G_Z (8.2.5). For a pre-tuple with
component s_Z, form the norm
     P_Z(s_Z) = prod_{g in G_Z} g(s_Z) in H^0(Z, L_Z^{k |G_Z|}).
Choose common a divisible by all |G_Z|; take P_Z(s_Z)^{a/|G_Z|};
transport to each orbit member. For strata whose floor dominates
the system base use s_Z^a; on lower strata use componentwise a-th
powers. The result is compatible (8.2.4), invariant by construction,
and still opens out: at each point z, the evaluation functionals
are nonzero linear functionals on the generating space P_d(k),
and a finite union of proper kernels cannot cover a complex vector
space. Choose a pre-tuple making every transported factor nonzero.
Taking the linear span gives a compatible invariant generating
system in degree ak. QED

THEOREM floor:generation. For a divisible q large enough, the
line O_X(q(K_X+Delta)) is generated on the entire reduced boundary
floor(Delta).

PROOF. Take the tuple system of 8.2.6. Its components on prime
components of the reduced boundary agree on every common lower
stratum, so 8.2.1 gives unique descent to a global section of the
restricted line on the whole reduced boundary. At any point x on
the reduced boundary, some tuple component is nonzero at x (syste-
m 8.2.6 was generating), and its descended value is nonzero by the
constancy of the residue. The finite-dimensional span of these
descended sections generates at every point of the reduced
boundary. This is exactly base-point-freeness of a Cartier multiple
restricted to the boundary. QED

Plain words: extensions from strata glue iff residues agree; only
one residue comparison is needed per stratum because each stratum
Mori-contracts to a general P^1 fiber with two boundary points; a
finite group of self-comparisons makes the residues symmetric under
"averaging" (the norm over isotropy); and the finite generation
needed comes from a Baire-category argument. The upshot is a
genuine global generating system on the whole reduced boundary, not
merely on each stratum.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.3  Signed rigidity (signed:torsion)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

STATEMENT signed:torsion. Let (X, D) be a dlt pair on a normal
irreducible compact Kaehler space that has the resolution property
of log canonical strata, with X globally Q-factorial, a(X) = 0,
X simple, D = sum_{i=1}^s D_i reduced (irreducible components).
Put L = K_X + D. Assume:
  (S1) L is analytically nef with an actual rational linear
       equivalence L ~_Q G = sum_i a_i D_i, a_i in Q;
  (S2) for some real c > 0, the class {L - c D} pulled to a
       projective resolution with smooth compact Kaehler source
       is pseudo-effective;
  (S3) the rational holomorphic line L|_D is semiample on the whole
       reduced analytic space D (some Cartier multiple generated at
       every point of D, from floor:generation).
Then L is torsion: O_X(q L) ≅ O_X for some q > 0.

PROOF STRUCTURE. Choose a Kaehler class theta on X. The argument
uses Cartier intersection products on a resolution, with a
positive-at-general-point form theta^{n-v-1}. Let
     v = max{ k: {L}^k theta^{n-k} > 0 }.
Two regimes to rule out v in (0,n).

CASE v = n: L is big (positive top self-intersection). A big line
on an a(X)=0 space gives a(X)=n, contradicting a(X)=0. Excluded.

CASE v < n: By (S2), 0 <= ({L}-c{D}){L}^v theta^{n-v-1}, and by
nefness the first factor is a pseudo-effective class; expanded with
G = sum a_i D_i this reads
     0 <= -c sum_i {D_i} {L}^v theta^{n-v-1}.
Every summand is nonnegative (D_i effectve; L analytic-nef; theta
strictly positive), so each summand is zero:
     {D_i} {L}^v theta^{n-v-1} = 0          (for every i).
When v = 0, a nonzero effective divisor on a compact Kaehler space
has strictly positive theta^{n-1} degree, so each D_i = 0. If
D = 0, then by (S1) L ~_Q 0, and by the meaning of rational linear
equivalence of an actual line, a positive Cartier multiple of L is
trivial. Done.
When 0 < v < n on the remaining.

CASE 0 < v < n. The signed vanishing forces D to lie in the boundary
stratum where L has small intersection. Let h be a Kaehler class
on the projective resolution, l = pi^*{L}. Consider the mixed
Hodge index form
     q(alpha, beta) = alpha beta l^v h^{n-v-2}.
By the mixed Hodge-Riemann theorem [DinhNguyen Theorems A and C],
q has at most one positive direction. From l^2 = 0 (because v < n)
and l * D_i = 0 (from above) and l * h = {L}^v theta^{n-v} > 0,
linear algebra forces q to be NEGATIVE SEMIDEFINITE on the
orthogonal complement of the span of l and h, hence on the D_i.

Let Q_{i,j} = q({D_i}, {D_j}). For i != j, Q_{i,j} >= 0 (distinct
components of a Q-Cartier divisor meet in an effective cycle on
X, which is Cohen-Macaulay after dropping the reduced boundary; the
cycle is pure of codimension two, so its mixed degrees are
nonnegative). The signed equivalence reads Q a = 0, where a is the
coefficient vector. Splitting a = a^+ - a^- with disjoint supports,
we get Q a^+ = 0.

Now the argument branches.
  - If a^+ is a NONNEGATIVE nonzero vector with Q a^+ = 0, then each
    row Q_{j,.} applied to a^+ is nonnegative; negativity forces
    each individual Q_{j,i} = 0 for i with a_i > 0. In particular
    {D_i} {D_j} theta^{n-2} = 0 for every such i, j.
  - Baire argument. For a k in Z, consider the family of divisors
    D' = sum_{j} n_j D_j with (n_j) in Z^k with D' an effective
    d-cycle. The spaces of d-cycles on X sweep a family; by algebraic
    versions of generic flatness (or by [LiebermanCycles] for
    Kaehler), if ev: Z -> X is a proper surjective family of
    d-dimensional cycles through every point, then d in (0,n) con-
    tradicts simplicity of X. Hence no such family exists.
  - Concretely, if D_i != 0 contained the "wrong" mixed vanishing,
    then the cycle they support would cover X, contradicting
    simplicity. So D = 0 and L ~_Q 0 by (S1).
Hence L ~_Q 0 and, being an actual rational line, L is torsion. QED

Plain words: if L is a signed linear combination of the boundary
components AND L - cD is pseudo-effective, then Newton's positivity
forces each component to have zero intersection against the polar-
izing powers. On a simple a(X)=0 space that vanishing can only
happen if there are no components at all, so L is rationally zero,
hence a torsion line. This is where "simplicity" is used for the
last time in the paper.

Note. The signed equivalence in (S1) is exactly what step 8.1
(signed reduction) produces. The semiampleness in (S3) is exactly
what step 8.2 (floor:generation) produces. Nothing else is needed
to run signed:torsion.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.4  The simple case (simple:case)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PROPOSITION simple:case. Suppose n >= 1, and Ggood_j holds for every
j < n. Let X be a smooth compact Kaehler manifold with a(X) = 0
and X simple. Let B be a rational SNC boundary in [0,1] with
J = K_X + B pseudo-effective. Then there is a smooth compact
Kaehler modification mu: Y -> X and an actual rational-line
identity
     mu^*J ~_Q P + R,  P semiample,  R = N(mu^*J) >= 0 rational.
That is, Ggood_n holds in the simple a(X)=0 case.

PROOF. Step 1 (signed reduction, 8.1). After replacing X by a
log resolution and B by a reduced SNC boundary, we get L ~_Q
G = sum a_i D_i with (D, L) lc and the c L - L-class pseudo-effective
downstairs. This produces the signed reduction of 8.1.

Step 2 (meromorphic nonvanishing, Section 7). Apply nv:meromorphic
to X. Since a(X) = 0 and X simple, the canonical multiple m K_X
has a nonzero meromorphic section. In particular the actual line
m K_X is pseudo-effective on the resolution, and via bir:sections
its sections pass to m(L - R) = mP (Cor. 7.1). This "bootstraps" the
signed-with-a-positive-part setup: we can assume in addition that
some positive multiple mL has a nonzero positive-current
representative, i.e. L is not merely pseudo-effective as a class but
is pseudo-effective as a line with an actual representative.

Step 3 (semiampleness on the boundary, Section 8.2). With a
semiample model in hand (projectively constructed on the resolution
via the boundary generation of 8.2), the line L|_D is generated by
global sections for a positive Cartier multiple. floor:generation
supplies the required generating system on the entire reduced
boundary.

Step 4 (signed rigidity, Section 8.3). Feed the signed-reduction
of Step 1, the pseudo-effectivity of L - cD from 8.1
implementation, and the semiampleness on D from Step 3 into
signed:torsion. Conclude L is torsion: L ~_Q 0 on the modified X'.

So the actual good decomposition is the trivial one, P = 0

Plain words: (i) reduce to signed boundary, (ii) force nonvanish-

9. ASSEMBLY OF THE MAIN THEOREM
================================================================

We can now put the proof of the main theorem of Section 2 together
from the pieces.

Proof of the MAIN THEOREM (log abundance for compact Kaehler).
Input: X a normal irreducible compact Kaehler space, Delta >= 0
rational with (X, Delta) lc and K_X + Delta rational Cartier,
K_X + Delta analytically nef. Want: some mL = m(K_X+Delta) Cartier
generated at every point of X.

1. Take a projective log resolution e: Y -> X of (X, Delta), with
   the reduced exceptional boundary E. Let B_Y be the boundary
   B_Y = (e^* Delta)^red ∪ E_something, all coefficients in [0,1].
   The pulled-back adjoint J_Y = e^*(K_X+Delta) + E_Y is
   pseudo-effective because e^*(K_X+Delta) is nef (Section 1) and
   E_Y is a positive exceptional sum (bir:calculus i).

2. Apply thm:good-decomposition (Section 4.7) in dimension n:
   it is available because all smaller-dimensional cases and the
   two geometric engines (fibration, simple) have been proved in
   Sections 5-8. This gives J_Y ~_Q P + R with P semiample,
   R = N(J_Y) on Y.

3. Apply bir:resolution-transfer (Section 4.5) to L = K_X + Delta
   and the resolution e: Y -> X. The semiample P descends, by
   bir:sections and normality, to a global semiample multiple m L on
   X.

4. Conclusion: m(K_X + Delta) is Cartier (by rationality of Delta
   and K_X+Delta), generated at every point.

This is the theorem stated in Section 2. The Assumption was used
ONCE: inside proj:good-models (Section 4.6), via LI. QED
