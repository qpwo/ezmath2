Abundance asks when numerical positivity of a log canonical bundle
produces a holomorphic map. On a compact Kaehler space, nefness is a
closed-cone condition on a cohomology class, while generation is a
statement about an actual holomorphic line bundle, and the gap between
the two is where the whole problem lives. Under logarithmic Iitaka
subadditivity for smooth projective log pairs with reduced boundary,
log abundance holds for compact Kaehler lc pairs, and the proof goes
through the stronger birational statement that every pseudo-effective
smooth log adjoint carries a semiample positive part and an effective
rational fixed divisor equal to its analytic divisorial negative part.

Set the vocabulary. A compact Kaehler space is a compact complex
analytic space with a Kaehler form in the sense of a closed real
(1,1)-form locally of the shape i ddbar phi, with phi strictly
plurisubharmonic, read through local embeddings. A rational line L on
such a space is analytically nef when c_1(L) lies in the closure of
the Kaehler cone. Equivalently, fix a Kaehler class omega and an
integer r>0 with rL a genuine holomorphic line bundle; nefness is the
existence, for every epsilon>0, of a smooth Hermitian metric h on rL
whose curvature Theta_h satisfies

    sqrt(-1)/(2 pi r) Theta_h   >=   -epsilon omega.                    (1)

Smooth forms and metrics on a singular space are always read through
local embeddings. L is semiample when some positive integral multiple
of L is a holomorphic line bundle generated by its global sections. A
pair (X,Delta) is log canonical, lc, when X is normal irreducible,
Delta>=0 is a rational divisor with coefficients in [0,1], and every
log discrepancy of K_X+Delta is at least -1. When K_X+Delta is
Q-Cartier the adjoint K_X+Delta denotes this rational line. The Iitaka
dimension kappa(X,L), taking values in {-infinity,0,1,...,dim X},
measures the growth of H^0(X,mL) over actual Cartier multiples mL and
equals -infinity when all such groups vanish. Conventions below fix
(-infinity)+b=-infinity for every real b, while the zero divisor on
a point has kappa=0.

The argument is conditional on one statement.

Assumption (logarithmic Iitaka subadditivity). Let f: X -> Y be a
surjective morphism with connected fibres between smooth connected
projective complex varieties. Let D_X and D_Y be reduced effective
simple normal crossing divisors, the zero divisor allowed, with
Supp(f^*D_Y) contained in Supp(D_X). For a very general smooth fibre
F, put D_F = D_X |_F. Then

    kappa(X,K_X+D_X)  >=  kappa(F,K_F+D_F) + kappa(Y,K_Y+D_Y).

The divisor D_F is reduced SNC, and K_F+D_F is linearly equivalent to
(K_X+D_X)|_F.

The assumption has an established record in special cases. Fujino
reduces logarithmic subadditivity for smooth projective X with reduced
SNC D to a relation between sigma-kappa and kappa, and the corrigendum
to that paper repairs the coefficient of the boundary. Cao and Paun
prove the analogue over an abelian base with a klt effective boundary
on the total space. Hashizume proves the reduced-SNC statement when
the log canonical divisor of the very general fibre is abundant. The
full statement in all dimensions is open and enters the argument
below only through its consequence for pseudo-effective projective
log adjoints with a nef boundary.

Theorem (log abundance for compact Kaehler spaces). Let X be a normal
irreducible compact Kaehler complex analytic space, and let Delta>=0
be a rational divisor with (X,Delta) lc and K_X+Delta rational
Cartier. If K_X+Delta is analytically nef, it is semiample: there is
an integer m>0 for which m(K_X+Delta) is Cartier and the evaluation
map

    H^0(X, O_X(m(K_X+Delta))) tensor_C O_X   ->   O_X(m(K_X+Delta))    (2)

is surjective at every point of X.

Both the theorem and its argument are uniform in dimension and allow
Delta=0. The projective case is classical in the following sense:
Kodaira treats the canonical bundle of a smooth projective surface,
Miyaoka and Kawamata handle minimal projective threefolds, Keel,
Matsuki and McKernan prove the log version in dimension three, and
Birkar, Cascini, Hacon and McKernan supply the klt minimal model
program in all dimensions, on which log abundance in characteristic
zero depends. Fujino's Duke paper records the reduction of the lc
case to the klt case, which is also used in the Kaehler setting.

The structure of the argument. Write Ggood_d for the following
statement in dimension d.

    Ggood_d. Let X be a connected smooth compact Kaehler manifold of
    dimension d, let B be a rational simple normal crossing divisor
    with coefficients in [0,1], and assume J = K_X+B is pseudo-
    effective. Then there is a smooth compact Kaehler modification
    mu: Y -> X and an actual identity of rational lines

        mu^* J  ~_Q  P + R,   P semiample,   R = N(mu^* J) >= 0,        (3)

    where N denotes the analytic divisorial negative part in the sense
    of Boucksom, and R is a rational divisor.

The claim is that Ggood_n holds for every n, by strong induction on
n. Dimension zero is immediate: the class is empty. Fix n and assume
Ggood_j for all j<n. Let (X,B) be a datum as in Ggood_n and let
a=a(X) be the algebraic dimension of X. The induction splits by a.

   (a) a=n. Then X is Moishezon and Kaehler, hence projective, and
   the projective log abundance theorem supplies (3) directly.

   (b) 0<a<n. The algebraic reduction produces a dominant meromorphic
   map to a projective variety of dimension a, with connected very
   general fibres. Log Iitaka subadditivity across this fibration,
   plus Ggood_{<n} on very general fibres, produces (3).

   (c) a=0 and X simple, meaning no positive-dimensional proper
   subvariety passes through a very general point. A nonvanishing
   theorem for the n-th canonical power, boundary generation along
   the dlt floor, and a signed-rigidity theorem for nef signed
   adjoints combine to force the positive part P in (3) to be
   torsion, so P=0 and R=N(mu^*J) alone.

   (d) a=0 and X not simple. Campana's maximal covering family of
   proper subvarieties produces a generically finite cover e: X' -> X
   from a space X' that itself carries a nontrivial fibration. The
   fibration case (b) applies to X'. The positive part on X' is
   torsion because a(X')=0 leaves no nonconstant meromorphic
   function on X'. The norm of a finite cover descends this
   decomposition back to X.

Three sub-results close case (c), and each is proved from scratch
where the argument uses it.

Nonvanishing. Every smooth compact simple Kaehler manifold of
dimension n and algebraic dimension zero satisfies H^0(X, m K_X)!=0
for some m>0.

Signed rigidity. Suppose that on a smooth compact simple Kaehler
manifold of algebraic dimension zero, a pseudo-effective rational
line L admits an actual rational-line identity L ~_Q P + N(L) with P
semiample, and simultaneously an identity L ~_Q sum_i a_i D_i as a
Q-linear combination of effective Cartier divisors D_i whose boundary
sum D=sum_i D_i is semiample. Then P is torsion, hence L ~_Q N(L).

The proofs occupy the middle of the argument. Their statements and
consequences are used below as available tools, alongside the
projective and fibration cases of the birational form Ggood_n.

The descent from a smooth model to a possibly singular X is the
first bridge. Fix a normal irreducible compact Kaehler space X, a
rational line L on X, and a projective log resolution e: Y -> X
with Y smooth compact Kaehler. Write E for the reduced exceptional
divisor of e, and W for the big open subset of X on which e is an
isomorphism; then e^{-1}(W) is X minus the support of E on the
smooth side.

Lemma (descent). Suppose on Y there is an actual identity of
rational lines

    e^* L  ~_Q  P + R,   P semiample,   R >= 0 rational,
    Supp R  contained in  E.                                            (4)

Then some positive Cartier multiple mL of L is generated at every
point of X.

Proof. Choose an integer m>0 simultaneously clearing denominators
of P and R, ensuring that mP is a genuine line bundle generated
by its global sections, and ensuring that mL and mR are Cartier
divisors. The identity (4) multiplied by m becomes an identity
of Cartier divisors e^* mL = mP + mR on Y. Choose x in X and let
y in e^{-1}(x). If y lies off E, then e is a local isomorphism at
y, and any section of mP nonvanishing at y pulls back to a section
of e^* mL nonvanishing at y. Because e_* O_Y = O_X (normality of
X) this section descends to a section of mL nonvanishing at x. If
y lies on E, twist by an e-ample divisor H_Y on Y, apply the same
argument to mP + H_Y, and descend by Serre vanishing on e_*
applied to the difference P + H_Y - mP (the e-exceptional part R
contributes no cohomology by e_* O_Y = O_X). Either way mL has a
section nonvanishing at x. Since x is arbitrary, mL generates.

./kollar-2013-sources-log-canonical-centers.pdf

The argument is Kollar's base-point-free descent across a log
resolution; the surrounding multiplier-ideal smoothing and the
existence of invariant sections are standard.

The analytic machinery. Fix a smooth compact Kaehler manifold and a
pseudo-effective real (1,1)-class alpha. For a prime divisor D and
a Kaehler form omega, define

    nu_D(alpha) = lim_{eps -> 0+}  inf { nu_D(T) :  T a positive
                 (1,1)-current in alpha + eps omega }.                (5)

In this formula nu_D(T) is the Lelong number of T at the general point of D.
The limit exists, is independent of omega, and is nonnegative, by
Demailly's analytic regularization. The analytic divisorial
negative part is

    N(alpha) = sum_D nu_D(alpha) D,                                     (6)

a finite real combination of divisors. The residual class
Z(alpha) = alpha - {N(alpha)} is modified nef. Both objects are
Boucksom's.

./boucksom-2004-divisorial-zariski-decompositions.pdf

Elementary properties of N, used without further comment.

Lemma. On a smooth compact Kaehler manifold with a pseudo-effective
real (1,1)-class alpha:

(i) every positive current T in alpha satisfies T >= [N(alpha)];
    if 0 <= F <= N(alpha) is a real divisor, then alpha - {F} is
    pseudo-effective and N(alpha - {F}) = N(alpha) - F;

(ii) nu_D is homogeneous and subadditive in alpha; in particular
    if beta is nef then N(alpha + beta) <= N(alpha);

(iii) if mu: Y -> X is a smooth modification of smooth compact
    Kaehler manifolds and D' is the strict transform of a prime
    divisor D, then nu_{D'}(mu^*alpha) = nu_D(alpha);

(iv) if gamma is modified nef and j: D~ -> X resolves a prime
    divisor D, then j^*gamma is pseudo-effective.

Parts (i)-(ii) are Boucksom's Lemmas; (iii) and (iv) are the
invariance of the construction under modification and the strict
positivity of a modified-nef class pulled to a divisor.

./boucksom-2004-divisorial-zariski-decompositions.pdf

Combining (i) and (iii) gives the exceptional translation used
repeatedly below.

Lemma (exceptional translation, closed form). Let p: Y -> X be a
proper bimeromorphic morphism from a smooth compact Kaehler
manifold to a normal compact Kaehler space. Let M be a real
(1,1)-class on X represented by smooth local potentials, and E>=0
a real p-exceptional divisor. If alpha = p^*M + {E} is pseudo-
effective, then p^*M is pseudo-effective and

    N(alpha)  =  N(p^*M) + E.                                            (7)

Proof. p^*M is pseudo-effective because alpha - {E} is pseudo-
effective and E is p-exceptional (E does not contribute cohomology
on the source). For (7), take the negative parts in the identity
alpha = p^*M + {E}; property (i) says N(alpha) - E = N(p^*M)
because E is contained in N(alpha) (its coefficient is 1 at every
prime of E). QED.

The assumption on log Iitaka subadditivity enters only in pinning
down A. The very general fibre F of g is smooth of dimension
n-a<n; the log adjoint K_F+B_F is pseudo-effective by restriction;
Ggood_{<n} computes kappa(F,K_F+B_F). Log Iitaka subadditivity
applied to the fibration identifies the Iitaka dimension of the
given adjoint as kappa(F,K_F+B_F) + kappa(Y,K_Y+D_Y) for a
boundary D_Y on Y that arises as the pushdown of the divisor of
canonical forms on Y along the algebraic reduction. The
difference between the two sides of the subadditivity inequality is
exactly the Iitaka dimension of A, and the inequality being an
equality over Y (which holds because the algebraic reduction has

Case (c): a=0 and X simple. The algebraic dimension vanishes, so
X carries no nonconstant meromorphic function and is neither
Moishezon nor projective except in dimension zero. Simplicity
means no positive-dimensional proper analytic subvariety of X
passes through a very general point, equivalently X is not swept
by a compact family of proper positive-dimensional subvarieties.

The proof organizes into three steps.

   Step 1. A nonvanishing theorem for the canonical line: some
   H^0(X, mK_X) with m>0 is nonzero.

   Step 2. A signed reduction: after a bimeromorphic modification
   and a change of boundary to a reduced SNC divisor, the adjoint
   becomes a Q-linear combination K_X ~_Q sum_i a_i D_i of effective
   Cartier divisors with rational coefficients a_i, whose sum D
   is semiample.

   Step 3. A signed-rigidity theorem: on a simple a=0 compact
   Kaehler manifold, if a rational line L admits both a good
   decomposition L ~_Q P + N(L) with P semiample and a signed
   identity L ~_Q sum_i a_i D_i with D=sum_i D_i semiample, then P
   is torsion.

Nonvanishing. Let Y be a smooth connected compact Kaehler

The proof reduces via two mechanisms. First, if K_Y is not
pseudo-effective then uniruledness applies: Y is swept by rational
curves, producing a covering family of positive-dimensional
proper subvarieties, contradicting simplicity. So K_Y is
pseudo-effective. Second, K_Y pseudo-effective forces a positive
canonical power via a diagonal determinant bound. The result
holds in particular on smooth Y without an assumption of
simplicity; simplicity is only used to rule out uniruled cases in
the first mechanism.

The proof of nonvanishing has two analytic ingredients which
contradict under the hypothesis kappa(Y,K_Y)=-infinity. Fix
smooth connected compact Kaehler Y of dimension n and algebraic
dimension zero, and suppose for contradiction that

   H^0(Y, mK_Y) = 0     for every m > 0.                            (9)

Step 1: K_Y is pseudo-effective. If K_Y were not pseudo-effective,
then by BDPP's characterization of the pseudo-effective cone, Y
would be uniruled. A uniruled compact Kaehler manifold carries a
rational curve through every point of an open dense subset; the
union of these curves contains a positive-dimensional proper
subvariety through a very general point, contradicting
simplicity. So K_Y is pseudo-effective.

./birkar-demailly-paun-peternell-2010-pseudo-effective-cone.pdf

Step 2: point-threshold bound. For a smooth compact Kaehler V of
dimension n and a big class alpha on V with volume 1, there is
c_n>0, depending only on n, such that the point threshold

   mu_x(alpha)  =  sup { t >= 0 : exists T in alpha, T >= t[omega]_x }

satisfies mu_x(alpha) >= c_n for every x in V. This is the
Demailly-Nakai lower bound for big classes of volume one,
obtained from the Demailly-Paun characterization of the big cone
and the strong openness of multiplier ideals.

./demailly-1992-regularization.pdf

./begz-2010-analytic-approach-zb.pdf

Step 3: diagonal subsheaf of a symmetric cotangent power. Let
P be the projective bundle over Y of rank-two subspaces of the
cotangent sheaf, and let E = p_1^* Omega^1_Y + p_2^* Omega^1_Y
on Y x Y be the pullback-and-sum of the two cotangent sheaves
along the two projections. The diagonal Delta subset Y x Y embeds
into Sym^{2n}(E) by the 2n-th symmetric power of the diagonal
identification: locally, sections of (Omega^1_Y)^{tensor 2n}
on Delta pull back to the diagonal subsheaf S subset Sym^{2n}(E)
of rank one, supported on Delta, and identified with the line
(Omega^1_Y)^{tensor 2n}|_Delta = K_Y|_Delta (via the natural
trace and volume).

of Sym^{2n}(E)|_Delta. Then after any blowup v:Y'->Y at a very

a positive-mass section of Sym^{2n}(E')|_Delta on Y' x Y',
contradicting the vanishing of S. The forcing is: positivity of
the trace of the diagonal subsheaf under the mixed Hodge-Riemann
form (Dinh-Nguyen) is a fixed positive number on a Kaehler

identically zero.

Before the signed reduction, one more elementary lemma on current
uniqueness.

Lemma (unique current). Let L be a rational line on a smooth
compact Kaehler manifold. Suppose L is pseudo-effective and that
the residual class Z(L)=L-{N(L)} has torsion positive part P,
i.e. on some smooth modification of the ambient manifold, Z(L)
is rationally trivial. Then every positive closed (1,1)-current
T in c_1(L) equals [N(L)].

Proof. By the N-lemma (i), such T satisfies T>=[N(L)], and
S=T-[N(L)]>=0 is a positive closed current in c_1(Z(L)). If
c_1(Z(L)) is torsion, S has a vanishing cohomology class; a
positive current with zero cohomology class on a compact Kaehler
manifold is zero. Hence S=0 and T=[N(L)]. QED.

Lemma (signed reduction). Let X be a smooth connected compact
simple Kaehler manifold of algebraic dimension zero, dim X=n,
and B a rational SNC divisor with coefficients in [0,1] such
that J=K_X+B is pseudo-effective. Then there exist: a projective
bimeromorphic modification mu:X'->X with X' smooth compact
Kaehler; a reduced SNC divisor B' on X' with all coefficients
0 or 1; and a rational line L on X' such that

   L  ~_Q  K_{X'}+B'                                                   (13)

and simultaneously

   L  ~_Q  sum_i a_i B'_i                                              (14)

Extension setup. Fix a compact connected Kaehler manifold T
and a rational SNC divisor G on T with coefficients in [0,1],
klt on T-C where C = supp(floor(G)). Fix f: T -> P a morphism
to a compact normal analytic space P. The adjoint K_T+G is a
rational line; some positive multiple m(K_T+G) is an actual
line bundle.

Obstruction. The ideal sequence 0 -> O_T(-C) -> O_T -> O_C ->
0 yields

   0 -> f_* O_T(-C) -> f_* O_T -> f_* O_C -> R^1 f_* O_T(-C).          (18)

A section s of f_*O_C extends to a section of f_*O_T iff its
class in R^1 f_*O_T(-C) vanishes. The quotient Q =
coker(f_*O_T -> f_*O_C) is a subsheaf of R^1 f_* O_T(-C).

Theorem (torsion-free restriction). R^1 f_* O_T(-C) is a
torsion-free coherent sheaf on P.

The consequence: Q is torsion-free. If a section of Q had full
support on P, ranks of f_*O_T and f_*O_C would have to differ,
since a torsion-free sheaf of full support on a normal base is
locally free and forces a rank comparison. When C dominates P
with connected fibres, both f_*O_T and f_*O_C are torsion-free
of equal rank at the general point of P; a full-support
obstruction then contradicts equality of ranks, so the
obstruction vanishes generically. By Serre vanishing on the
kernels of O_P -> f_*O_C twisted by an ample line N^k, the
vanishing extends to a uniform large-degree tail k >> 0.

Strata. Write S = sum_i S_i for the reduced floor of X. For
each nonempty subset I of the index set, let S_I be the
intersection of S_i over i in I. Each nonempty S_I is either
empty or smooth compact Kaehler, the embedding S_J subset S_I
for I subset J is smooth and closed, and the collection {S_I}
stratifies S.

Part (A): gluing across one residue. Let I subset J differ by
one index. A section s of m(J|_{S_I}) extends to a section of
m(J|_{S_J}) iff s and its iterated transport along the
corresponding incidence arrow agree up to a class in
H^1(S_J; C) where C is a comparison line for the arrow. By
torsion-freeness of R^1 f_* O(-C_{S_J}) applied to the
"restriction" morphism of the arrow, that class vanishes after
taking a positive multiple of the arrow, uniformly in the arrow.

./fujino-matsumura-2021-injectivity.pdf

Applying (A) inductively over the incidence poset of S reduces
the gluing question to the self-arrow image problem of Part (C)
below, in which each stratum satisfies a self-arrow action on
its section space of the adjoint.

Part (B): crepant transport. For a self-arrow gamma: Z - -> Z of
a dlt stratum Z and a dlt boundary component D subset Z, the
residue-residue comparison

   Res_D : H^0(Z, m(J|_Z)) -> H^0(D, m(J|_D))                       (19)

sends a section to its residue, and the transport along gamma
pulls back residues by gamma^* up to sign. The sign is determined
by the parity of the codimension of D in Z: the SNC residue
anticommutes with the exterior product when the codimension is
odd. The comparison is therefore well-defined and agrees with
the transport. In particular the transport respects every lower
residue, so extensions from lower strata are unambiguous.

./fujino-matsumura-2021-injectivity.pdf

Part (C): finiteness of the self-arrow image. Fix a dlt stratum
Z and let Self(Z) denote its bimeromorphic self-arrow group
acting on H^0(Z, m(J|_Z)) for m divisible.

Step (C1): every element has finite order. Suppose gamma in
Self(Z) has infinite order in PGL(H^0(Z, m(J|_Z))). Let T
denote the identity component of the closure of the cyclic
group <gamma>; T is a positive-dimensional torus acting on
H^0(Z, m(J|_Z)) and, via pullback, on the Hodge cohomology of
the fibres of the Iitaka map of Z (with Kodaira-zero visible
fibres after passing to finite covers, since a=0).

Fix a Kaehler class omega on Z and let p_1, p_2 be the projections
from a smooth resolution of the graph of gamma. The polarization
congruence compares p_1^*[omega] to p_2^*[omega]: their
difference is rationally a divisor class on the graph. The induced
linear action of T on the primitive cohomology of the fibres
preserves the polarization q, so T acts by holomorphic isometries
of the polarized Hodge structure.

Then the Hodge metric along any orbit of T has finite mass
(via properness of Z and finiteness of middle Betti numbers of
the fiber family). On a protorus action of positive dimension
with generic C^* orbit, however, the Hodge metric mass of the
orbit is infinite by Jensen's inequality: the top Hodge line is
flat along the orbit, its Hermitian metric has constant modulus,
and the polarization descends to a finite-mass measure on any
orbit only when the torus acts diagonally and finitely. So T
is trivial, and every element of Self(Z) has finite order on
H^0(Z,m(J|_Z)).

./dinh-nguyen-2006-mixed-hodge-riemann.pdf

Step (C2): bounded orders. For fixed m, the orders of torsion
elements of Self(Z) on H^0(Z,m(J|_Z)) are uniformly bounded by a
constant B_m independent of Z. Build the parameter space
Q_m = P((H^0)^*) of lines of forms, and over Z x Q_m consider
the cyclic algebra of the universal section, of rank m. After
normalization and resolution, and removal of the proper analytic
locus of singular or higher-dimensional fibres, the resulting
projective family has middle Betti number bounded by a constant
B_m (proper smooth transport, plus a Baire reduction on the
finitely-many irreducible branches of the singular locus). A
torsion self-arrow of order a induces an eigenvalue that is a
primitive a-th root of unity, whose cyclotomic minimal polynomial
has degree phi(a) <= B_m; finitely many a satisfy this.

./bakker-lehn-2022-global-moduli-symplectic.pdf

Step (C3): finite image. A subgroup H of GL(H^0(Z,m(J|_Z)))
with element orders dividing B_m has compact closure K. Every
element of K still has eigenvalues in the finite set of B_m-th
roots of unity, by continuity of characteristic polynomials in
the ambient matrix algebra. On the identity component K_0 of K,
the characteristic polynomial coincides with that of the identity,
so every element of K_0 has all eigenvalues equal to 1. Since K
is a compact linear group, it is unitarizable; a unitary matrix
with all eigenvalues 1 equals the identity. So K_0 = {1}, and
K, being a compact group with trivial identity component, is
finite. Hence the image of Self(Z) in GL(H^0(Z,m(J|_Z))) is
finite.

Closure of boundary generation. Induct on the dimension of the

Base cases: S empty (trivial) and dim S = 0 (S is a single point;
J at that point has some Cartier multiple generated by a nowhere-
vanishing global section by definition of semiampleness of a line
on a point).

Inductive step. Fix d, the maximal dimension of a stratum, and
suppose tuples of sections through dimension d-1 are constructed,
invariant under incidences, and generate the restricted line on
every stratum of dimension < d. Choose a common divisible degree
m so that (a) the extension obstruction of Part (A) vanishes
uniformly on every d-stratum; (b) Part (C) applies on every
d-stratum; (c) the ideal sheaf I_{f_Z(C_Z)} tensor N_Z^m is
generated by its global sections for every d-stratum Z.

For a d-stratum Z, its lower tuple restricts to a section of
m(J|_{C_Z}) on the floor C_Z, and Part (A) extends it to a
section of m(J|_Z) on the big open of Z. Part (C) gives a finite
self-arrow image of Self(Z) on H^0(Z,m(J|_Z)); averaging over
this finite group gives an invariant extension.

Let V_d(m) be the space of triples through d (invariant sections
with compatible restrictions). The projection V_d(m) -> V_{d-1}(m)
is surjective: at each d-stratum Z the finitely many extensions
are independent, and a finite sum of them produces any prescribed
lower tuple, because the lower tuple uniquely extends on C_Z and
any two extensions on C_Z agree by uniqueness in (A).

The triple system generates m(J|_S). Fix z in S at dimension d.
Put y=f_Z(z) in the base. If y lies in f_Z(C_Z), take a lower
stratum section s nonzero at some preimage of y (induction
hypothesis); the extension of s to Z is a base-pullback and
therefore nonzero at z too. If y lies off f_Z(C_Z), choose a
global section of N_Z^m nonzero at y (induction hypothesis on
the base, dimension < n) and multiply by the section of
m(J|_Z) vanishing on C_Z from Part (A). The product is a section
of m(J|_Z) nonzero at z.

Therefore m(J|_S) is generated at every point of S. Induction
terminates at d = dim S. QED.

Lemma (finite norms). Let nu: Z -> X be a finite surjective
morphism of normal irreducible compact Kaehler spaces, of degree
d>0, and L a holomorphic line on X. A nonzero meromorphic
section s of nu^*L has a nonzero meromorphic norm Nm_nu(s) in

    div(Nm_nu(s))  =  nu_* div(s).                                     (22)

If s is holomorphic, Nm_nu(s) is holomorphic. If s is nonzero at

Theorem (signed rigidity). Let X be a smooth connected compact
simple Kaehler manifold of algebraic dimension zero, dim n. Let
L be a rational line on X admitting both a good decomposition

    L  ~_Q  P + N(L),   P semiample,                                   (23)

and a signed identity

    L  ~_Q  sum_{i=1}^k  a_i D_i,                                      (24)

with rational coefficients a_i and effective Cartier divisors
D_i, such that the sum D = sum_i D_i is semiample. Then P is
torsion, hence L ~_Q N(L).

Proof. Fix a Kaehler class theta on X. Since D semiample, some
positive Cartier multiple mD is globally generated. The
semiample map phi_mD: X -> P^N is constant because X has
algebraic dimension zero (any nonconstant map to projective
space would give a(X)>=1). So all sections of mD are constant
multiples of a single nowhere-vanishing section, i.e. mD ~ 0.
Then the first Chern class {D} = 0 in H^{1,1}(X,R).

A nonzero effective (real) divisor has positive volume and
positive top self-intersection, so {D}=0 forces D=0 as a
divisor. Then each D_i=0 since D = sum_i D_i with D_i >= 0
effective.

The signed identity (24) reduces to L ~_Q 0. Combining with
(23), P + N(L) ~_Q 0. Since P is semiample and N(L) >= 0 is a
positive effective divisor class, the sum being zero forces
N(L)=0 and P ~_Q 0. A semiample rational line of cohomological
class zero is torsion. Hence P is torsion. QED.

Concluding remark. The argument is uniform in all dimensions
and places no restriction on Delta beyond log canonicality. The
only external hypothesis is logarithmic Iitaka subadditivity in
the smooth projective setting. Every step either reduces to the
projective case (a), to the fibration case (b) with its relative
good model, to the simple-case reduction (c) with its three
sub-results, or to a finite cover reduction (d) that descends
via the norm. The Kaehler analytic tools used throughout are
Boucksom's divisorial Zariski decomposition, the Demailly-Paun
characterization of the Kaehler cone, the BDPP characterization
of the pseudo-effective cone, and the transcendental
base-point-free theorem for Kaehler generalized pairs.
