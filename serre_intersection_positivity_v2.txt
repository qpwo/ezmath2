# Positivity of Serre's intersection multiplicity





## 1. The statement

Let (R,mathfrak m)frakm) be a commutative Noetherian **regular local ring** of dimension d: this means mathfrak mfrakm can be generated by exactly d elements, so R is as "smooth" as an d-dimensional local ring can be. Let M,N be nonzero finitely generated R-modules. Serre's intersection multiplicity is

chi^R(M,N)=sum_{i=0}^{d}(-1)^i,length_RTor_i^R(M,N).

If the tensor product M ⊗ _R N has finite length, every Tor above also has finite length, and regularity guarantees that all Tor_i with i>d vanish. So the sum is a finite integer.

**Theorem 1.1 (Serre's positivity conjecture).** *Let (R,mathfrak m)frakm) be a regular local ring of dimension d, and let M,N be nonzero finitely generated R-modules. If length_R(M ⊗ _R N)<∞ and dim_R M+dim_R N=d, then chi^R(M,N)>0.*

Two words of caution. "Dimension" means the Krull dimension of the support Supp(M)={mathfrak p:frakp: M_{mathfrak p}frakpneq 0}. The hypothesis dim M+dim N=d is the "complementary dimension" condition: the two supports are the largest they can be while still meeting in a finite-length way. It is exactly the situation where positivity is not automatic from vanishing theorems.

**Example 1.2.** Let R=k[x,y], d=2. Take M=R/(y) (the x-axis) and N=R/(y-x^2) (the parabola). Then M ⊗ _R N = R/(y,y-x^2)=R/(y,x^2)~= k[x]/(x^2), which has k-length 2. All higher Tor vanish, so chi=2>0. The parabola is tangent to the axis at the origin, and tangency is worth two points. If instead N=R/(x), we get R/(x,y)~= k, length 1, the transverse crossing. Positivity is easy here; the whole point of Theorem 1.1 is that it stays true in far more complicated situations.

## 4. Conventions

- Universes are chosen large enough for the module and sequence categories used.
- Complexes are indexed homologically.
- A derived algebra H is **connective** if pi_i(H)=0 for i<0.
- Perfect modules over associative algebras are right modules.
- All localizations of coefficient algebras are along the central image of the base ring.
- We use nonconnective algebraic K-theory of perfect complexes; a subscript Q means rationalization.
- For a sequence a=(a_1,...,a_r), K(a;V) is its homological Koszul complex with coefficients in V, and H_i(a;V)=H_i(K(a;V)).
- The notation a^v means (a_1^v,...,a_r^v), not the v-th power of the ideal.
- Normalized lengths may vanish on nonzero modules; all uses of additivity involve finite values or nonnegative extended values for which the operations are defined.

Several sections follow: normalized length (Section 5), rational descent (Section 6), the asymptotic rank quotient (Section 7), and the positivity proof (Section 8). The short statement of the consequence for symbolic powers and the tangent-cone corollary appears at the end (Section 9).

## 5. Normalized length

This section builds the "substitute length" that powers all the positivity assertions. The idea: ordinary length misbehaves in the presence of ramification and Frobenius. If we pass to a tower of p-power root extensions and divide by the growth rate (the *degree* of each finite step), the resulting normalized length has exactly the properties we want:

- a parameter quotient has *prescribed positive* normalized length;
- the positive-degree Koszul homology of any parameter system has normalized length *zero*.

Both properties are needed at once. The first gives us the positive quantity that survives the alternating sum; the second guarantees that in the alternating sum only the degree-zero term has a chance to be nonzero, so nothing is subtracting.

Throughout this section, (D,mathfrak d,k)frakd,k) is a complete Noetherian local *domain* of dimension h>0, with k perfect of characteristic p>0. Neither unramifiedness nor Cohen–Macaulayness of D is assumed.

### 5.1 Extending the residue field

Passing to an algebraically closed residue field is a standard trick, but it must preserve regularity, dimensions, lengths and intersection multiplicities. The following lemma does exactly that.

**Lemma 5.1 (Residue-field extension).** *Let (B,mathfrak b,k)frakb,k) be a complete Noetherian local ring and let overlineoverline kk be an algebraic closure of k. There is a complete Noetherian local ring (B',mathfrak b',frakb',overlineoverline k)k) and a flat local map Bto B' with mathfrak bfrakb B'=mathfrak b'.frakb'. For every finite B-module V and every age1,*

length_{B'}big((V ⊗ _B B')/(mathfrak b')^a(Vfrakb')^a(V ⊗ _B B')big)=length_B(V/mathfrak b^afrakb^a V).

*Consequently finite lengths and module dimensions are preserved. If B is regular, so is B'. For finite B-modules M,N with finite-length tensor product, every Tor length, and hence the intersection multiplicity, is preserved.*

*Proof.* In equal characteristic a coefficient field gives B=k[[X_1,...,X_t]]/I; set B'=overlineoverline k[[X_1,...,X_t]]/Ik[[X_1,...,X_t]]/Ioverlineoverline k[[X_1,...,X_t]].k[[X_1,...,X_t]]. The map on power-series rings is flat (local flatness criterion on the variables, plus flatness of the coefficient-field extension), and base change to the quotient gives the stated residue field and maximal ideal.

In mixed characteristic Cohen structure gives B=V[[X_1,...,X_t]]/I with V a complete discrete valuation ring of uniformizer p and residue field k. To enlarge the residue field while keeping p as a uniformizer, lift a monic irreducible polynomial over k to fin V[X]; then V[X]/(f) is finite free over V, reduces mod p to the extension field, and has maximal ideal generated by p, hence is again a discrete valuation ring. Iterate along a tower of simple algebraic residue extensions exhausting overlineoverline k;k; at every limit stage an ideal is still generated by a least-valuation element, and the completion V' has uniformizer p and residue field overlineoverline k,k, with Vto V' flat (torsion-free). Set B'=V'[[X_1,...,X_t]]/IV'[[X_1,...,X_t]]; local flatness gives the required flatness. Compare the standard construction in Ma's paper, Lemma 2.2.

For the length claim, tensor a composition series with B': flatness preserves exactness and every simple factor becomes B'/mathfrak b'.frakb'. Apply this to V/mathfrak b^afrakb^a V. Equality of Hilbert–Samuel functions gives equality of module dimensions; if B is regular of dimension d, its d generators generate mathfrak b'frakb' and dim B'=d, so B' is regular. Flat base change identifies Tor after extension, and lengths are preserved by the first part. QED

### 5.2 Regular normalization bases

**Lemma 5.2 (Regular normalization bases).** *Let (D,mathfrak d,k)frakd,k) be a complete Noetherian local domain of dimension h>0 with k perfect of characteristic p>0. There is a finite, generically separable inclusion A⊆ D inducing the identity on residue fields, with*

A=k[[t_1,...,t_h]] if charD=p, A=W(k)[[t_2,...,t_h]] if charD=0,

*W(k) the ring of Witt vectors. In the second case set t_1=p. In either case t_1,...,t_h is a parameter system of D and a regular parameter system of A.*

*Proof.* In characteristic p this is the Cohen–Gabber normalization theorem. In mixed characteristic choose a coefficient ring W(k)⊆ D and extend p to a parameter system p,t_2,...,t_h; completeness plus the parameter condition makes the resulting power-series map module finite. Its kernel is zero because source and target both have dimension h and a nonzero ideal in a regular local domain lowers dimension. The fraction-field extension is separable in characteristic zero. QED

### 5.3 The p-power tower and its length

Fix a base A from Lemma 5.2 and compatible p-power roots of its parameters:

A_n=A[t_1^{1/p^n},...,t_h^{1/p^n}], Λ=varinjlim_n A_n.

The tower is *uncompleted*; only in mixed characteristic do we also use its p-adic completion, denoted A_∞.

**Lemma 5.3 (The tower and its length).** *Each A_n is a complete regular local ring with residue field k. For bge a the map A_ato A_b is finite free of rank p^{(b-a)h}; in particular rank_A A_n=p^{nh}. On the category of Λ-modules killed by some power of mathfrak m_Afrakm_A there is a nonnegative additive function lambda_∞ with three properties:*

1. *a finitely presented module descends to a finite-length A_n-module V_n, and* lambda_∞(V_n ⊗ _{A_n}Λ)=p^{-nh}length_{A_n}(V_n);
2. *for a finitely generated module V, its length is the infimum of the lengths of finitely presented covers of it;*
3. *for an arbitrary module V, lambda_∞(V)=sup{lambda_∞(V_0): V_0⊆ V finitely generated}.*

*The value on an arbitrary module may be infinite. In mixed characteristic, restriction from A_∞ to Λ gives the same length on modules killed by a fixed power of mathfrak m_A.*frakm_A.*

*Proof.* Stages are power-series rings in the chosen roots (over W(k)[p^{1/p^n}] in mixed characteristic). Monomials in the successive roots with exponents below the root degrees form bases for all transition extensions, proving the regularity, rank, and residue-field statements.

Finite presentations over the union descend to a stage, and the additional relations expressing annihilation by a fixed power of mathfrak m_Afrakm_A descend after enlarging the stage (finitely many generators and relations). The descended finite-stage module has finite length, and the displayed formula is unchanged on enlarging the stage by flatness and the computed free ranks. The infimum and supremum extension properties, with additivity and compatibility, are Faltings' normalized-length formalism (see Definition 2.2.1 and Propositions 2.2.3, 2.2.6(a) of CLMST). Same residue field gives equality of A_n-length and A-length on finite-length modules.

In mixed characteristic Λ is p-torsion-free and Λ/p^aΛ~= A_∞/p^a A_∞; a module killed by mathfrak m_A^afrakm_A^a is killed by p^a, so its submodules and finite generating sets are the same over the two bases. Replacing a cover by V/mathfrak m_A^afrakm_A^a V keeps it finitely presented and over identical quotient rings, proving the last assertion. QED

**Lemma 5.4 (Zero-length limits).** *Assume all modules under consideration are killed by one fixed power of mathfrak m_A.frakm_A. Sums, direct sums, and directed colimits of zero-length modules have length zero. For an increasing union lambda_∞(∪_a V_a)=sup_alambda_∞(V_a). If (V_a) is a direct system with lambda_∞(V_a)to 0, then lambda_∞(varinjlim_a V_a)=0, with no injectivity assumption on the transition maps.*

*Proof.* A finite set of elements of a direct sum lies in a finite subsum; additivity and the supremum formula give the direct-sum assertion, and sums and colimits are quotients of direct sums. For an increasing union, every finite generating set lands in one stage. For the last claim, a finitely generated submodule of the colimit lifts its generators to a common late stage, and monotonicity bounds its length by lambda_∞(V_a) there; apply the supremum formula. The common annihilator keeps all modules in the length category. QED

## 6. Rational descent with closed supports

Now we build the abstract machine that lets us compare *ordinary* and *normalized* Euler characteristics. The comparison is not true class by class; it is true after summing over the Galois conjugates of the symmetric group. The same descent theorem gets applied twice, over two different bases.

Throughout, "connective" means the homotopy groups vanish in negative degrees. We index derived algebras homologically.

Let A be a Noetherian commutative Z_{(p)}-algebra of finite Krull dimension, let Jsubset A be an ideal containing p, and let H be a connective associative A-algebra with central A-action. For a commutative A-algebra B, write Perf_J(H ⊗ _A^{mathbf L}bfLB) for the perfect modules whose central localizations vanish off V(JB). Its nonconnective K-theory spectrum is K^J(H ⊗ _A^{mathbf L}bfLB), and K_0^J is its pi_0. A subscript Q means rationalization. For a finite group action, (-)^{hG} denotes homotopy fixed points (derived invariants) while (-)^G denotes ordinary invariants.

**Theorem 6.1 (Supported rational descent).** *Let A be a commutative Noetherian Z_{(p)}-algebra of finite Krull dimension, Jsubset A an ideal containing p, and H a connective associative A-algebra with central A-action. Let T be a finite commutative A-algebra with an action of a finite group G over A. Suppose SpecTtoSpecA is surjective and G acts transitively on the points of every geometric fiber. Then pullback induces an equivalence*

K^J(H)_ QbbQ~=big(K^J(H ⊗ _A^{mathbf L}bfLT)_Q{hG},

*and in particular a surjection K_0^J(H) ⊗ Q ⊗ _A^{mathbf L}bfLT) ⊗ Q)^G.*bbQ)^G.*

Neither flatness nor tame ramification of Ato T is required. The coefficient algebra H need not be commutative, finite over A, or bounded in positive homological degrees; only its connectivity matters.

### 6.1 The truncating invariant and its supports

We use the following form of Land–Tamme's results. A **localizing invariant** takes exact sequences of stable categories to fiber sequences. It is **truncating** if its value on a connective associative algebra is unchanged by passage to pi_0. Rational infinitesimal K-theory

I(U)=fibbig(K(U)_ QbbQtoHN(U ⊗ Q/bbQ/Q)

is truncating by Goodwillie's relative comparison theorem, with the passage from simplicial rings to connective associative ring spectra explained in Land–Tamme (proof of Corollary 3.9, Remark 3.10). Truncating localizing invariants satisfy **cdh descent**, even after tensoring with a fixed connective associative algebra over the base (Land–Tamme, Theorem A.2 and Appendix B, especially Theorem B.3). The cdh topology is generated by Nisnevich covers and abstract blowup squares: a proper map with a closed center, the map an isomorphism off that center.

For a derived Z_{(p)}-algebra U set

E(U)=fibbig(K(U)_ QbbQtoK(U[1/p])_bbK(U[1/p])_Q

The negative cyclic homology terms in the two character maps are naturally identical, since U ⊗ Q~= U[1/p] ⊗ Q. Taking fibers of the character square gives

E(U)~=fibbig(I(U)to I(U[1/p])big).

Hence E is a truncating localizing invariant of Z_{(p)}-linear categories. (Note that this does *not* identify K(U)_ QbbQ with K(U ⊗ Q).)

Tensoring with H preserves both the localizing and the truncating property: if B is connective then H ⊗ _A^{mathbf L}bfLB is connective with pi_0(H ⊗ _A^{mathbf L}bfLB)=pi_0(H) ⊗ _Api_0(B), so replacing B by pi_0(B) does not change E.

For a finite-type A-scheme Y, let H_Y be the derived pullback of H and Z_Y=Ytimes_A V(J). Define

L(Y)=fib;H)to E(Y∖ Z_Y;H)big).

Restriction to the open complement preserves the cdh squares, so L satisfies cdh descent. Since pin J, the support Z_Y disappears after inverting p, and expanding the two fibers in the definition identifies

L(Y)~=K{Z_Y}(H_Y)big)_Q,

which is the desired rational supported K-theory, not a cdh replacement. The functor is also nilinvariant.

Why the support condition matches coefficient categories: for a perfect module P and zin A, compactness identifies localization of the endomorphism spectrum with the endomorphism spectrum of P[1/z], so P[1/z]=0 iff some z^v kills id_P in the homotopy category. For the finitely many generators of J, a common power covers all of them: this is the central support criterion. The corresponding Koszul tensors generate the compact kernel of restriction to the complement, so perfect localization computes the fiber above.

The geometric input is Thomason–Trobaugh's perfect localization theorem (Propositions 5.2.2–5.2.4 and Theorem 7.4), tensored with the perfect coefficient category via Land–Tamme (Lemmas 3.12 and B.2) and the preceding supported-kernel identification. Nonconnective K-theory carries the resulting exact sequence to a fiber sequence (Blumberg–Gepner–Tabuada, Definition 8.1, Theorems 8.7 and 9.8, Section 9.3).

### 6.2 Descent for finite surjections

The functor L is now a nilinvariant cdh sheaf. We need descent for finite surjections, which need not be flat; rational transfers give the finite flat case, and flattening by blowup plus induction on closed subsets gives the general case.

**Lemma 6.2 (Finite flat).** *L satisfies ordinary Cech descent for finite faithfully flat covers, universally under base change.*

*Proof.* Let f:Xto Y be finite faithfully flat. Since the schemes are Noetherian, f_*O_X is a vector bundle of positive rank, and restriction of scalars gives a transfer because scalar extension of the coefficient algebra is a finite projective module over the original. Finite-flat base change and the projection formula give natural transformations L{f^*}L_X{tr_f}L with composite multiplication by the pullback of f_*O_X. On a Zariski open where that bundle is free of rank a>0, additivity of K-theory identifies the composite with a; this is natural on the whole diagram, so rationally a^{-1}tr_f makes L a retract of L_X. After base change to X, the augmented Cech nerve of f has a section, hence an extra degeneracy, and its image under any presheaf has split descent, so L_X satisfies descent for f; the comparison for L is a retract of an equivalence, hence an equivalence. Finally both sides are Zariski sheaves, so the conclusion glues over the trivializing cover. All constructions commute with base change. QED

**Lemma 6.3 (Finite surjection).** *L satisfies ordinary Cech descent for every finite surjection of finite-type A-schemes.*

*Proof.* Let tau be the topology generated by cdh covers and finite faithfully flat covers; these families are stable under base change, and L is a tau-sheaf by Lemma 6.2 and cdh descent. We show every finite surjection is a tau-cover.

Reductions and finite closed decompositions are cdh-local. If a reduced scheme is Y=Y_1cup Y_2 with Y_i reduced closed subschemes, the abstract blowup square with proper map Y_1to Y and center Y_2 reduces closed unions to their pieces. So reduce to integral Y, with induction on dimension for proper closed subsets.

Choose a reduced irreducible component X_0 of X dominating Y; the map X_0to Y is finite surjective, flat of positive rank on a dense open Usubset Y. Flattening by blowup applied to O_{X_0} gives a blowup Y'to Y isolating U over which the strict transform X_0'to Y' is flat and finitely presented (Stacks Tag 0815). The strict transform is a closed subscheme of X_0times_Y Y', hence finite over Y'; since Y' is integral and the image of X_0' contains its generic point, that image is closed; so X_0'to Y' is a finite faithfully flat cover factoring through Xtimes_Y Y'.

Let Z⊊ Y be the blowup center; the family {Y'to Y, Zto Y} is a cdh cover. By induction Xtimes_Y Zto Z is a tau-cover. Together with X_0'to Y', this gives a tau-covering family of Y factors through X, so Xto Y generates a covering sieve. Dimension zero starts the induction: a reduced integral base is a field, and any nonzero finite algebra is finite faithfully flat. QED

### 6.3 The geometric orbit argument

**Proof of Theorem 6.1.** Write X=SpecT, S=SpecA. On finite-type S-schemes consider

### 6.4 Ordinary and normalized Euler lengths

We isolate the consequence needed before introducing asymptotic categories. Let (A,mathfrak m_A,k)frakm_A,k) be a complete regular local Z_{(p)}-algebra of dimension e>0, residue characteristic p, with regular parameters z_1,...,z_e. Let T be a finite local domain extension of A with the same residue field, r=rank_A T. Let C be a T-algebra with an additive nonnegative length lambda on C-modules annihilated by powers of mathfrak m_A,frakm_A, lambda(0)=0, infinite values allowed. Suppose for every vge1 that

lambda H_i(z_1^v,...,z_e^v;C)=0 (i>0), lambdabig(C/(z_1^v,...,z_e^v)Cbig)=v^e r.

(Theorem 5.5 supplies exactly this, since the multiplicity of the base parameters on T is its generic rank over the regular base.)

**Lemma 6.4 (Finite-length base change).** *For every finite-length A-module V,*

lambdaTor_i^A(V,C)=0 (i>0), lambda(V ⊗ _A C)=rlength_A V,

*and all these lengths are finite.*

*Proof.* The Koszul resolution on z_1,...,z_e resolves k over A, so the hypothesis with v=1 proves the claim for V=k. Induct on a composition series. In the Tor long exact sequence of a short exact sequence with quotient k, nonnegativity and additivity make every positive Tor term have length zero and give the factor r in degree zero; finiteness comes for free at every step. QED

If a finite group G acts on T over A, scalar twisting is g^*P=P ⊗ _{T,g}T with the last factor's left T-action through g; on a free complex this applies g to every differential matrix entry.

**Lemma 6.5 (Ordinary norm comparison).** *Assume a finite group G acts on T over A and is transitive on every geometric fiber of SpecTtoSpecA. For a perfect T-complex P supported on V(mathfrak m_Afrakm_A T), the quantities*

chi_T(P)=sum_i(-1)^ilength_T H_i(P), chi_C(P)=sum_i(-1)^ilambda H_i(P ⊗ _T^{mathbf L}bfLC)

*are finite and define homomorphisms on K_0^{mathfrak m_A}frakm_A(T). They agree on every norm sum:*

sum_{gin G}chi_T(g^*P)=sum_{gin G}chi_C(g^*P).

*More generally they agree on the image of K_0^{mathfrak m_A}frakm_A(A) and on every G-invariant rational class.*

*Proof.* Establish finiteness and additivity, compare on pullbacks, then apply descent to the norm class. Represent P by a bounded finite free T-complex; its ordinary homology is finite and supported at the closed point. For the C-evaluation choose a common v with each z_j^v acting nullhomotopically (support criterion). With K_v=K(z_1^v,...,z_e^v;T), splitting the nullhomotopic cones gives

K_v ⊗ _T P ⊗ _T C~=⊕_{j=0}^e(P ⊗ _T C)[j]^{ ⊕ binom ej},

which respects the module structure on which lambda is defined. Conversely, taking Koszul homology first gives a finite spectral sequence whose terms are finite sums of H_j(z_1^v,...,z_e^v;C), of finite length by hypothesis; so every homology of K_v ⊗ _T P ⊗ _T C has finite length, and the splitting with nonnegativity gives finiteness for every H_i(P ⊗ _T C). The long exact sequence gives additivity.

For Q a perfect A-complex supported at mathfrak m_A,frakm_A, the hyper-Tor spectral sequence with Lemma 6.4 gives chi_C(Q ⊗ _A^{mathbf L}bfLT)=rsum_j(-1)^jlength_A H_j(Q) (positive rows vanish, zeroth row scaled). For the ordinary evaluation use a bounded finite free A-resolution L_bullet of T of alternating rank r (regular A): for finite-length V, sum_i(-1)^ilength_ATor_i^A(V,T)=rlength_A V, so the hyper-Tor spectral sequence gives chi_T(Q ⊗ _A^{mathbf L}bfLT)=rsum_j(-1)^jlength_A H_j(Q) (using that finite-length T-modules have the same A-length and T-length). Hence the two evaluations agree on classes pulled back from A.

Apply Theorem 6.1 with H=A, J=mathfrak m_A:frakm_A: every invariant class in K_0^{mathfrak m_A}frakm_A(T) ⊗ Q is the pullback of a rational class downstairs. Extending both Euler homomorphisms Q-linearly,bbQ-linearly, they agree on that invariant subspace, and sum_{gin G}[g^*P] is invariant. QED

**Remark 6.6.** No invariance of lambda under G is asserted or needed. The equality is for the sum of conjugate classes, not for each individual class. This lets the two length algebras be chosen independently.

## 7. Supported asymptotic complexes

Section 6 compares ordinary and normalized Euler lengths *before* any rank quotient. The positivity proof (Section 8) needs the same comparison *after* quotienting by things of negligible size. That is the content of this section.

The setting: (A,J) is an excellent regular local Z_{(p)}-algebra of dimension e>0 with residue characteristic p, J its maximal ideal, z_1,...,z_e a regular system of parameters. Let T be a finite local domain over A with the same residue field, r=rank_A T. Let C be a T-algebra with a nonnegative additive length lambda on its modules annihilated by powers of J, satisfying

lambda H_j(z_1^v,...,z_e^v;C)=0 (j>0), lambdabig(C/(z_1^v,...,z_e^v)Cbig)=v^e rquad(vge1).

(Theorem 5.5 supplies exactly this.) The length may vanish on nonzero modules. Write K_v=K(z_1^v,...,z_e^v;A), in homological degrees 0,...,e.

### 7.1 The rank quotient and its support condition

Fix positive integers s_n and a nonprincipal ultrafilter U on the positive integers. Rank estimates O(s_n) and o(s_n) always refer to the ordinary limit, not U. If (a_n/s_n) is bounded, write overlineoverline a=a=lim_{ntoU}/s_n.

For S=A or T, consider the additive category of sequences (S^{a_n})_n with a_n=O(s_n) and arbitrary matrix morphisms. Let D_S be its perfect hull: complexes in one common bounded degree range for the whole sequence, then idempotent completion. Let N_S be the thick subcategory generated by free sequences with a_n=o(s_n), and

P_S=( D_S/calD_S/N_S)^{natural},

(-)^{natural} denoting idempotent completion; the quotient functor is q_S. A thick subcategory is closed under shifts, cones, and direct summands; the Verdier quotient inverts maps whose cones lie in it.

An object XinD_S or P_S is **supported on J** if some vge1 has z_j^vid_X=0 for every j, with the identity in the category containing X. Thus in D_S this is a uniform homotopical condition on the sequence, whereas in P_S the actions need only vanish after the rank-small quotient. Write S_S⊆calS_S⊆D_S and P_S^J⊆calP_S^J⊆P_S for the resulting full subcategories. They are thick: nilpotence of central actions survives shifts, cones, and retracts (larger powers for cones).

**Remark 7.1 (why we cannot just take raw homologies).** Over a discrete valuation ring A=T with parameter z, take s_n=n and the rank-one free resolution of T/(z^n). This sequence lies in N_T, yet its raw normalized degree-zero homology length is 1. It has no uniform annihilating power before quotienting. But for each fixed v its homology after tensoring with K(z^v;T) is bounded independently of n, hence zero after normalization. This is why we first build lengths on uniformly supported lifts, then extend to supported quotient objects.

### 7.2 The supported sub-quotient

**Lemma 7.2 (supported quotient).** *The natural exact functor S_S/(calS_S/(S_S N_S)calN_S)toD_S/calD_S/N_S is fully faithful, and its idempotent completion is P_S^J.*calP_S^J.*

*Proof.* First let XinS_S, YinN_S, and f:Xto Y. Choose v with z_j^vid_X=0. Centrality gives z_j^vf=0, and the exact Hom sequence of the triangle fib(z_j^v:Yto Y)to Yxrightarrow{z_j^v}Y lifts f through the first object. Repeat for all parameters, always using the zero action on the original source X; the result factors f through K_v ⊗ _A Y[-e], which lies in N_S by thickness and in S_S by the usual Koszul homotopies. So every map from a supported object to a small object factors through a supported small object.

Now a map Xto Y in S_S, computed in D_S/calD_S/N_S, has a roof Xxleftarrow{s}Zxrightarrow{f}Y with cofib(s)=N_0inN_S. In the triangle Zto Xxrightarrow{u}N_0, factor u through N_1inS_S N_ScalN_S by the previous paragraph. If Z_1 is the fiber of Xto N_1, the induced morphism of triangles gives c:Z_1to Z, s_1:Z_1to X with sc=s_1; then Z_1inS_S with cofib(s_1)=N_1, so Xxleftarrow{s_1}Z_1xrightarrow{fc}Y lives in the supported subquotient. Fullness.

For faithfulness, suppose f:Zto Y between supported objects becomes zero in D_S/calD_S/N_S. The calculus of Verdier roofs gives t:Wto Z with small cone and ft=0; the triangle of t factors f through that small cone. Factoring its first leg through a supported small object shows f is already zero in the supported subquotient. Apply this to the numerator of any roof.

Finally let XinP_S^J. It is a retract of q_S(Y), YinD_S. Choose v with all z_j^v zero on X; the cone of each such zero map splits, so X is a retract of K_v ⊗ _A X, itself a retract of q_S(K_v ⊗ _A Y) whose lift is uniformly supported. So X lies in the idempotent completion of the supported subquotient; the reverse inclusion follows because support is closed under retracts. QED

### 7.3 Nonnegative homology lengths

Every supported quotient object is now accessible through a uniformly supported lift plus a summand.

For ordinary length, let A_T be the abelian category of sequences of finite T-modules annihilated by some common power of J with lengths O(s_n). For the C evaluation, let A_C be the analogous category of sequences of C-modules with finite lambda-lengths O(s_n). Both are abelian, and sequences of normalized ultralimit length zero form a Serre subcategory. Denote the Serre quotients by Abar_T,Abar_C and their nonnegative additive lengths by lengthbar_T,overlinelambda. These lengths are finite on every object ("negligible" means zero ultralimit; the free sequences generating N_T satisfy the stronger ordinary estimate o(s_n)).

**Proposition 7.3 (evaluations).** *There are homological functors H_i^{ord}:P_T^J Abar_TcalAbar_T and H_i^C:sfH_i^C:P_T^J, whose values on a uniformly supported lift are its ordinary homology sequence and its homology after tensoring with C, respectively. For each object X the lengths*

b_i^{ord}(X)=lengthbar_TH_i^{ord}(X), b_i^C(X)=overlinelambdaH_i^C(X)

*are finite nonnegative real numbers, zero outside a finite range, and the Euler sums define homomorphisms chi_{ord},chi_C:K_0(P_T^J) R.bbR. If F=(F_n) is a bounded free sequence whose image is supported and v makes all z_j^v act as zero on that image, then with b_i(F)=b_i(q_TF),*

(1+t)^esum_i b_i(F)t^i=sum_i t^ilim_{ntoU} ⊗ _A F_n))/(s_n).

*Here len=length_T in the ordinary case; in the C case the complex is further tensored over T with C and len=lambda. This is an identity of finite Laurent polynomials.*

*Proof.* Bound homology on S_T, show it kills S_T, extend to the idempotent-completed quotient.

Begin with a bounded free F in S_T and uniform powers annihilating its identity. Splitting their cones gives, in the homotopy category,

K_v ⊗ _A F~=⊕_{j=0}^e F[j]^{ ⊕ binom ej}.

For ordinary length every Koszul homology H_j(K_v ⊗ _A T) has finite length, and taking Koszul homology first in the bounded double complex bounds the homology lengths of K_v ⊗ _A F_n by fixed constants times the ranks of the F_n terms, hence by O(s_n). For C, the same spectral sequence uses (*): positive Koszul rows have length zero and the zero row finite length; additivity and nonnegativity give the same bound. The splitting (7.3.2) then bounds the homology of F_n themselves; these have a common primary annihilator (a power of every z_j annihilates them).

A general XinS_T may be a retract in D_T of a bounded free F that is not supported. Choose v killing the parameter actions on X; then X is a retract of K_v ⊗ _A X, a retract of the uniformly supported K_v ⊗ _A F. The preceding bounds apply to that sequence and to the homology summands defining X (a retract before the rank quotient). So homology on S_T takes values in the indicated abelian length quotients.

Fix v. Koszul tensoring a rank-small free generator has homology of normalized length zero by the same bounds; the class of YinD_T with this property for K_v ⊗ _A Y is thick (long exact sequences, nonnegativity, retracts), so it contains N_T. If XinS_T, choose v killing its actions; then X is a retract of K_v ⊗ _A X, so its homology is zero in both length quotients.

The homology functors thus invert maps with supported small cones and descend to S_T/(calS_T/(S_T, preserving long exact sequences. Every object of P_T^J is a summand of an object of this subcategory (Lemma 7.2). Extend a homology functor to a summand (X,a) by taking imH_i(a) in its abelian target: this needs no sequence of chain projectors lifting a. Distinguished triangles in an idempotent completion are summands of distinguished triangles in the original category (Balmer–Schlichting, Definition 1.10 and Theorem 1.12), and taking images of compatible idempotents preserves exact sequences, so the extensions are homological.

Each object is a summand of a fixed bounded uniformly supported Koszul lift, so its homology objects vanish outside a finite range and are bounded by those of that lift; this gives finiteness, nonnegativity, and boundedness of the b_i. Short exact sequences prove additivity of the Euler sums.

For (7.3.1), the splitting (7.3.2) now holds in P_T (the powers act as zero there), and the extended homological functors give the left side. Its Koszul tensor has a uniformly supported lift whose actual homology computes the right side; this defines the coefficients without formal division. QED

In particular, (7.3.1) assigns zero to the quotient object of Remark 7.1, as required, and its nonnegative coefficients come from homological functors, not from assumed positivity of a quotient.

### 7.4 Scalar extension and nonlifting idempotents

Construct Abar_A and its length using ordinary finite A-modules. The construction over A with ordinary length defines chi_A on P_A^J, and scalar extension gives an exact functor P_A^J P_T^J.calP_T^J. We must show the evaluations agree on all classes from P_A^J, including idempotent summands.

**Lemma 7.4.** *For every finite-length A-module V, lambdaTor_j^A(V,C)=0 for j>0 and lambda(V ⊗ _A C)=rlength_A V. All these modules are annihilated by a power of J.*

*Proof.* The Koszul and composition-series proof of Lemma 6.4 applies, using regularity and (*) but not completeness; a power of J annihilating V also annihilates its derived tensor homology. QED

**Proposition 7.5 (base equality).** *For every XinP_A^J,*calP_A^J,*

chi_{ord}(X ⊗ _A^{mathbf L}bfLT)=rchi_A(X)=chi_C(X ⊗ _A^{mathbf L}bfLT).

*This holds for summands defined by idempotents in the Verdier quotient, without assuming the idempotents lift to chain maps before localization.*

*Proof.* The ordinary evaluation recovers r from the alternating rank of a resolution of T; the normalized one from vanishing of positive Tor lengths. To make both on an arbitrary summand, descend their finite spectral sequences through supported Verdier roofs before taking idempotent images.

Let Y be a uniformly supported bounded free sequence over A and a an idempotent endomorphism of its image in S_A/(calS_A/(S_A; every supported object of P_A is such a summand by Lemma 7.2 (enlarging the initial lift to a bounded free sequence before Koszul tensoring). Put V_b=imH_b^A(a) in Abar_A; these images exist even without a chain-level projector.

Choose a fixed bounded finite free A-resolution P_bullet of T (regular A). The double complex Y ⊗ _A P_bullet computes Y ⊗ _A^{mathbf L}bfLT, and filtering by the degree of P gives a finite spectral sequence with first page E^1_{u,b}=H_b(Y) ⊗ _A P_u. It is natural in homotopy-category maps from this page onward (homotopies in Y preserve the filtration, act trivially on the first page). On retracts in the prequotient category the construction is defined by taking the corresponding idempotent images on every page and on the abutment filtration; its terms are A-modules and the abutment's ordinary A-length equals its T-length (same residue field). A map with supported small cone is an isomorphism on H_b(Y) in Abar_A, hence on the first page, all later pages, and the finite filtered abutment in the length quotient. The spectral sequence descends through all Verdier roofs in the supported subquotient, and the natural augmentation Tot(Y ⊗ _A P_bullet)to Y ⊗ _A T identifies the abutment with the homology of scalar extension, also descending through roofs. So the action of a on the abutment is precisely the scalar-extended idempotent.

Thus a acts as an actual idempotent on every page and on the abutment, and the differentials commute with it. Taking images of an idempotent commutes with homology (an idempotent splits a complex into image and complementary image) and preserves the finite exact sequences of the filtration (for a filtration preserved by a, im(a)cap F_u=a(F_u), so gr_uim(a)=im(gr_u a)). Hence the Euler length on the scalar-extended summand is the Euler length of the images on the first page, which are rank_A(P_u) copies of V_b:

Big(sum_u(-1)^urank_A P_uBig)sum_b(-1)^blengthbar_A V_b=rsum_b(-1)^blengthbar_A V_b,

the alternating rank being r after tensoring with FracA.

For C, use the finite hyper-Tor spectral sequence {}^CE^2_{u,b}=Tor_u^A(H_b(Y),C)⇒ H_{u+b}(Y ⊗ _A^{mathbf L}bfLC), constructed from the functorial finite Postnikov filtration of Y (bounded b, 0le ule e, no convergence issue). Lemma 7.4 makes every positive Tor term zero in Abar_C, and implies that V|-> V ⊗ _A C induces an exact functor Abar_A Abar_CcalAbar_C multiplying length by r (failure of left exactness is the image of a positive Tor module of zero length; negligible sequences map to negligible ones). Images of the same a give only the zero Tor row, the images of V_b under this exact tensor functor, with Euler length rsum_b(-1)^blengthbar_A V_b, which equals chi_A(Y,a) by construction. ### 7.5 Connective coefficients and the norm comparison

We now put the rank quotient into the form required by Theorem 6.1; the coefficient algebra must be connective and the support condition must be central-localization support.

**Lemma 7.6 (connective coefficients).** *There is a connective associative derived A-algebra H and compatible equivalences P_A~=Perf(H) and P_T~=Perf(H ⊗ _A^{mathbf L}bfLT), respecting central localization by elements of A.*

*Proof.* The sequence E_S=(S^{s_n})_n is an additive generator: if a_n=O(s_n) then one integer c has a_nle cs_n for all n (increase c for the finitely many exceptions), and coordinate inclusions and projections make (S^{a_n})_n a summand of E_S^c. Hence D_S~=Perf(B_S) with B_S=prod_n M_{s_n}(S).

The underlying A-module of B_A is flat by the ideal criterion: every finitely generated ideal of the Noetherian A is finitely presented, so tensoring with this product of finite free modules commutes with the product and each required injection is a product of injections. Since T is finitely presented over A, tensoring with T also commutes with the product, so B_A ⊗ _A^{mathbf L}bfLT~= B_A ⊗ _A Tcong B_T.

The same product argument makes every morphism module between the original free sequences flat over A, so the explicit dg quotient of this degree-zero category by its full rank-small subcategory computes the derived quotient (Drinfeld, Section 3.1 and Theorem 3.4). Its morphisms are sums of paths formed from tensor products of the original morphism modules and degree-1 contracting homotopies; they are concentrated in nonnegative degrees and degreewise A-flat, hence K-flat. Passing to the perfect hull and idempotent completion kills exactly N_A. The image of E_A remains a generator, and its endomorphism algebra H=End_{P_A} is connective, giving P_A~=Perf(H) (a statement about the chosen generator, not arbitrary shifts).

Since T is finitely presented over A, tensoring each morphism module with T gives the morphism module between free T-sequences and the path formula tensors to the T-linear dg quotient by rank-small free sequences. The same flatness criterion applies over Noetherian T, so H ⊗ _A T~= H ⊗ _A^{mathbf L}bfLT, whose perfect hull and idempotent completion are P_T (agreeing with preservation of exact quotients under scalar extension, Land–Tamme Lemma 3.12). Everything is centrally A-linear and preserves central localization. QED

Under these equivalences the support condition of this section is exactly the one in Theorem 6.1: for perfect X and central z_j, localization is the telescope of multiplication by z_j, and compactness makes X[1/z_j]=0 iff some z_j^v kills id_X; a common power works for the finitely many parameters. The same holds for Perf(B_S) and S_S. No uniformly supported lift of an object of P_S^J is needed for this.

Now suppose a finite group G acts on T over A, SpecTtoSpecA is surjective, and G is transitive on every geometric fiber. It acts on P_T^J by scalar twisting gX (g^*P=P ⊗ _{T,g}T, with the convention of Section 6.4).

**Theorem 7.7 (asymptotic norm comparison).** *Let A,J,T,C,lambda be as in this section and (*). Suppose a finite group G acts on T over A, SpecTtoSpecA is surjective, and G is transitive on every geometric fiber. Then for every XinP_T^J,*calP_T^J,*

sum_{gin G}chi_{ord}(gX)=sum_{gin G}chi_C(gX).

*Neither invariance of lambda under G nor equality of the two evaluations on individual classes is assumed.*

*Proof.* Lemma 7.6 identifies the supported categories with the supported perfect categories of H and H ⊗ _A^{mathbf L}bfLT, H connective; the identification over T is compatible with the G-action because scalar twisting acts on the T factor. Theorem 6.1 gives a surjection K_0(P_A^J) ⊗ Q P_T^J)calP_T^J) ⊗ Q)^G. The norm class sum_g[gX] is invariant, and chi_{ord}-chi_C vanishes on the image from A by Proposition 7.5. Applying it to a preimage of the norm class proves the assertion. QED

## 8. Proof of strict positivity

We now put the two comparisons together. Fix a pair of prime quotients and reduce; then for each Galois conjugate, replace one module by the normalized-length algebra, and use the two norm comparisons from Sections 6 and 7 to force positivity.

Throughout, "reduction" means: pass to the complete regular local ring with algebraically closed residue field, and to the prime quotients.

### 8.1 Reduction and the first norm comparison

**Lemma 8.1 (reduction).** *To prove Theorem 1.1 it suffices to prove it for*

D=R/P, E=R/Q, h=dim D>0, l=dim E>0, h+l=d,

*where R is complete with algebraically closed residue field k of characteristic p>0, P,Q are prime, and P+Q is primary to mathfrak m_R.*frakm_R.*

*Proof.* The equicharacteristic case is Serre's theorem. Assume positive residue characteristic. Completion and then Lemma 5.1 reduce to complete R with algebraically closed residue field (a flat local extension with mathfrak m_RR_1=frakm_RR_1=mathfrak m_frakm_{R_1} preserves finite-length lengths via composition series, identifies Tor after extension, preserves the Hilbert–Samuel function and hence module dimensions, and preserves regularity).

Use the Roberts–Gillet–Soule vanishing theorem in its general regular local form: chi^R(V,W)=0 if length_R(V ⊗ _R W)<∞ and dim V+dim W<d. Take prime filtrations of M and N; every pair of factors again has finite-length tensor product (supports are contained in the originals). Additivity of chi and vanishing discard every pair except those with both original dimensions. There are top-dimensional factors on each side, so a remaining pair exists. Proving strict positivity for each remaining pair proves it for M,N; their primes satisfy √(P+Q)=mathfrak m_R.frakm_R.

Finally, a zero-dimensional prime quotient is k, and its complementary d-dimensional prime quotient is R (regular local rings are domains); that pair has chi=length_R(k)=1. So we may assume h,l>0. QED

Fix a pair as in Lemma 8.1. By Lemma 5.2 choose regular complete normalization bases A_Dsubset D, A_Esubset E of dimensions h,l, same residue field k, with the extensions finite and generically separable. Let D',E' be integral closures in finite Galois closures of the fraction-field extensions; they are finite over the bases by excellence, and over D,E as well, and they are local: a finite algebra over a complete local ring is a product of complete local rings, and a domain has one factor (Stacks Tag 03QH); their residue fields are finite over k, hence equal k. Put

G_D=Gal(FracD'/FracA_D), G_E=Gal(FracE'/FracA_E).

Normality of the bases gives (D')^{G_D}=A_D, (E')^{G_E}=A_E. Geometric transitivity holds: over an algebraically closed A-field Ω, S=T ⊗ _AΩ with u=sum_i t_i ⊗ omega_i has prod_{gin G}g(u) scalar (the coefficients of prod_g(sum_i g(t_i)X_i) lie in T^G=A, then specialize X_i=omega_i); if the finite Artinian Ω-algebra S had more than one orbit of points, a nontrivial G-invariant idempotent e (equal to 1 on one orbit, 0 elsewhere) would satisfy e^{|G|}=e scalar, forcing e=0 or 1, a contradiction. So every geometric fiber is transitive, without asserting that invariants commute with base change (Stacks Tags 0BRI, 0BRJ). Thus Theorem 6.1 applies to both extensions.

By Theorem 5.5 choose the algebras and lengths

(C_D,lambda_D)=(C_{D'},lambda_{D'}), (C_E,lambda_E)=(C_{E'},lambda_{E'}),

fixed throughout. Set a_D=rank_D D', a_E=rank_E E'. For sigmain G_D define iota_sigma:Rto D'xrightarrow{sigma}D', give D' this R-action, and give C_D the action obtained by composing iota_sigma with D'to C_D. Define

c_sigma=sum_{i=0}^d(-1)^ilambda_Dbig(Tor_i^R(C_D,E')big),

with this R-action on C_D understood.

**Lemma 8.2 (first average).** *The lengths in the definition of c_sigma are finite, and*

|G_D|,a_D,a_E,chi^R(D,E)=sum_{sigmain G_D}c_sigma.

*Proof.* Take a finite R-free resolution of E' and extend it to D'. The resulting bounded free complex is supported at the closed point: after localizing its homology away from mathfrak m_R,frakm_R, the supports of D' and E' are disjoint; the same holds after every twist, whose kernel on R is still P. Lemma 6.5 gives finite normalized homology lengths and equality of ordinary and normalized Euler evaluations after summing all conjugates.

Conjugating the complex entrywise by sigma gives a semilinear isomorphism from the untwisted complex to its conjugate, so all ordinary Euler characteristics in that sum equal chi^R(D',E'). Ordinary lengths over D' and over R agree here because both residue fields are k.

Choose an injection D^{a_D}to D' with torsion cokernel; the cokernel has dimension less than h, so its intersection with E' vanishes by vanishing. Additivity gives chi^R(D',E')=a_Dchi^R(D,E'), and similarly chi^R(D,E')=a_Echi^R(D,E). The norm comparison gives the displayed equation. QED

It remains to show c_sigma>0 for each sigma. Fix one sigma until the final paragraph; write C=C_D, lambda=lambda_D with the chosen R-action.

### 8.2 A parameter quotient and its Tor groups

Choose x_1,...,x_hin Q forming an R-regular sequence whose images are a parameter system of D. (Simultaneous prime avoidance: after i-1 choices, the associated primes of R/(x_1,...,x_{i-1}) have height i-1 (so R is Cohen–Macaulay and none contains Q of height hge i), and we also avoid the primes in D/(x_1,...,x_{i-1}) obstructing the next dimension drop — each has positive dimension, and if its inverse image contained Q it would contain P+Q, hence be the maximal ideal, contradiction. So prime avoidance inside Q allows the choice at every step.)

## 9. Consequences

The main theorem is a statement about intersection multiplicities, but it has two clean downstream consequences worth recording, because they show strict positivity doing real work.

### 9.1 Symbolic powers

**Corollary 9.1 (symbolic-power containment).** *Let (R,mathfrak m)frakm) be a ramified mixed-characteristic regular local ring (residue characteristic pinmathfrak m^2),frakm^2), and let P,Q be prime ideals with √(P+Q)=mathfrak mfrakm and htP+htQ=dim R. Then, with Q^{(r)}=Q^rR_Qcap R the r-th symbolic power,*

Pcap Q^{(r)}⊆mathfrak m^frakm^{r+1}(rge1).

*Proof.* This is the implication of Kurano–Roberts (Theorem 3.2 of their paper), applied to the positivity statement of Theorem 1.1. Kurano–Roberts show that if the intersection multiplicity of R/P and R/Q at mathfrak mfrakm is strictly positive whenever htP+htQ=dim R and √(P+Q)=mathfrak m,frakm, then the symbolic-power containment holds. Before this paper the implication applied only where positivity was known (equicharacteristic or unramified); now it applies unconditionally in ramified mixed characteristic. QED

This is a direct application, not an extra ingredient in the proof of Theorem 1.1.

### 9.2 The tangent-cone corollary

**Corollary 9.2 (finite strict-transform intersection).** *Let (A,mathfrak m)frakm) be a commutative Noetherian regular local ring with residue field k, and let P,Q be prime ideals such that A/(P+Q) has finite length and dim(A/P)+dim(A/Q)=dim A. Let e_mathfrak mfrakm be Hilbert–Samuel multiplicity and*

T=gr(A/P) ⊗ _{grA}gr(A/Q),

*all associated graded rings taken for the mathfrak m-adicfrakm-adic filtrations. If dim Tle1, then*

chi^A(A/P,A/Q)ge e_mathfrak m(A/P)frakm(A/P),e_mathfrak m(A/Q),frakm(A/Q),

*with equality if and only if dim T=0.*

*Proof.* The case dim A=0: then A=k, P=Q=0, both sides are 1, and T=k.

Otherwise let widetilde X be the blowup of X=SpecA at its closed point and widetilde Y,widetilde Z the strict transforms of Y=Spec(A/P), Z=Spec(A/Q). By Skalit (Lemma 3.5 of the unramified paper), the underlying set S of widetilde Ycapwidetilde Z identifies with that of ProjT; so S is a finite set of closed points of the exceptional divisor, empty exactly when dim T=0. Fulton's blowup formula and Skalit's finite-support calculation (Theorem 3.4 and the proof of Proposition 3.10 of the unramified paper) give

chi^A(A/P,A/Q)-e_mathfrak m(A/P)e_frakm(A/P)e_mathfrak m(A/Q)=frakm(A/Q)=sum_{xin S}[kappa(x):k],chi^{B_x}(M_x,N_x),

where B_x=O_{widetilde X,x}, M_x=O_{widetilde Y,x}, N_x=O_{widetilde Z,x}. Each residue degree is finite and positive because the exceptional divisor is projective over k. The blowup widetilde X is regular (Jannsen, Proposition A.4(1)). The modules M_x,N_x are nonzero and finite over B_x, with finite-length tensor product because their intersection is supported on S. The local rings A/P, A/Q, A are formally equidimensional and universally catenary, so Skalit (Lemma 3.8 and the proof of Proposition 3.10 of the unramified paper) gives dim M_x=dim(A/P), dim N_x=dim(A/Q), and dim B_x=dim A. Theorem 1.1 then makes every local intersection multiplicity in the sum strictly positive, so the sum is nonnegative and vanishes exactly when S is empty, i.e. when dim T=0. QED
