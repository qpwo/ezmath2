A COMPLETE LOCAL DOMAIN WITH NO SMALL COHEN-MACAULAY MODULE

Everything is over the complex numbers C.  The background needed is:
rings, ideals, modules, localization, and the first definitions about
projective varieties.  Everything else (depth, Cohen-Macaulay,
intersection numbers, Chern classes, Harder-Narasimhan filtrations) is
defined where it is first used, and the references at the end are
pointers, never a dependency of the proof.

1. THE QUESTION AND THE ANSWER
================================================================================

1.1 Depth, dimension, Cohen-Macaulay

A ring is local if it has exactly one maximal ideal; write (R,m) and
k = R/m for the residue field.  It is Noetherian if every ideal is
finitely generated, and complete if the natural map from R to the
inverse limit of R/m^n is an isomorphism.

Let M be a nonzero finitely generated module over a Noetherian local
ring (R,m).  An element x in m is M-regular if "multiply by x" is an
injective map M -> M.  A list x_1,...,x_t is an M-regular sequence if
each x_i is regular on M/(x_1,...,x_{i-1})M.  Then

    depth M := the maximal length of an M-regular sequence,

independent of all choices, and

    dim M := dim(R/Ann(M)),

the Krull dimension of the support of M.  Always depth M <= dim M.

M is Cohen-Macaulay (CM) if depth M = dim M.  A small CM module over R
is a nonzero finitely generated module M with depth M = dim R.  (The
word "small" contrasts with "big" CM modules, which may be infinitely
generated.)

Examples.  A regular local ring is CM over itself; if R is CM and x in
m is a nonzerodivisor then R/(x) is CM.  Hence C[[x,y,z]]/(f), for any
nonzero f, is a 2-dimensional CM ring.  Over a 3-dimensional ring,
"small CM module" means "nonzero finite module of depth 3".

1.2 The question

Hochster asked in the early 1970s: does every complete Noetherian local
domain have a small CM module?  Some answers are positive.  In
dimension at most 2 the normalization of a complete local domain is a
finite CM module.  For finitely generated graded domains of positive
characteristic there are graded small CM modules.  In general the
answer is no.

Theorem A.  There is a 3-dimensional complete Noetherian normal local
domain R containing C, with residue field C, such that every nonzero
finitely generated R-module M has depth M <= 2.  In particular R has
no small CM module.

Theorem B.  Let X be a smooth integral projective surface over C and
let H be an ample, globally generated divisor on X.  Write K_X for the
canonical divisor of X, c_2(X) for the second Chern number of X, and
R(X,H) for the completed cone of the pair (X,H) (Section 2).  If
R(X,H) has a small CM module then

    K_X^2 - 2 c_2(X) <= 6 H^2.                                       (1.1)

Theorem A is Theorem B applied to one surface for which (1.1) fails.

1.3 The idea in four sentences

A small CM module over a completed cone is a free module over the
power series ring, hence a vector bundle on the punctured cone.  Push
that bundle onto the exceptional surface X: it becomes a torsion-free
sheaf P on X.  Two inequalities then fight over one number attached
to P.  The triviality of the bundle at infinity forces that number to
be >= 0 (Section 4), while semistability of the pieces of P forces it
to be <= 1/2 - (K_X^2 - 2c_2(X))/(12H^2) (Section 5); comparing the
two gives (1.1).

1.4 The same idea with numbers

For a coherent sheaf F of positive rank N on the plane P^2 put

    v(F) = 2 ch_2(F)/N - (c_1(F)/N)^2,                               (1.2)

where c_1(F) is the integer coefficient of the line class and ch_2(F)
is the degree of the second Chern character.  If F is a direct sum of
N line bundles O(a_1),...,O(a_N), then c_1(F)/N is the average of the
numbers a_i and v(F) is exactly the variance of the numbers a_i.
Variance is never negative, and it is at most 1/4 when all the a_i lie
in an interval of length 1.  That is the whole mechanism: the
variance is nonnegative because it comes from a divisor (Section 4),
and it is small because semistability bounds it (Section 5).

1.5 What is new here

Earlier obstructions concern restricted classes of witnesses.  If R
contains Q and is normal, trace shows that a CM finite extension
algebra would force R itself to be CM, so an extension algebra is a
special kind of witness.  Ulrich sheaves and graded modules are also
special.  The argument below uses only that some finite module of
depth 3 exists: no grading, no algebra structure, no semistability and
no Ulrich condition on the module.  What replaces those hypotheses is
a comparison of coherent extensions across the exceptional divisor of
the cone (Lemma 4.1).

1.6 Plan of the paper

Section 2 builds the completed cone R of (X,H), a regular model W of
Spec R, and turns a small CM module into a torsion-free sheaf E on W.
Section 3 modifies E along the exceptional surface so that the
Harder-Narasimhan slopes of its restriction P lie in an interval of
length at most 1.  Section 4 proves that triviality of the pushforward
of E away from the exceptional divisor forces v(f_*P) >= 0.  Section
5 combines that with Bogomolov's inequality for the semistable factors
and deduces (1.1).  Section 6 constructs a surface with
K_X^2 - 2c_2(X) > 6H^2.  Section 7 assembles Theorems A and B and the
uncompleted form.

================================================================================
2. THE CONE OF A POLARIZED SURFACE
================================================================================

Fix for the rest of the paper:

    X : a smooth integral projective surface over C,
    H : an ample divisor on X that is globally generated,
    h : H^2, a positive integer,
    D : the plane P^2.

2.1 A finite map to the plane

Lemma 2.1.  There are sections s_0,s_1,s_2 of O_X(H) with no common
zero.  They define a morphism f: X -> D with f^*O_D(1) = O_X(H); this
morphism is finite and surjective, and deg f = h.

Proof.  Choose s_0 != 0.  Its zero divisor has finitely many irreducible
components; choose s_1 not vanishing identically on any of them.  Then
the zero divisors of s_0 and s_1 share no component, so their
intersection Z is finite.  Choose s_2 not vanishing at any point of Z.
Now s_0,s_1,s_2 have no common zero, and the ratio
p |-> [s_0(p):s_1(p):s_2(p)] defines f.

X is projective, so f is proper.  A fiber of positive dimension would
contain a curve contracted to a point, and then
H·C = (f^*O_D(1))·C = 0, contradicting ampleness of H.  So f is finite.
For a finite map of degree e, the pullback formula for intersection
numbers gives

    h = H^2 = (f^*O_D(1))^2 = e (O_D(1))^2 = e.

QED

2.2 The ring and the model

Let S = C[x_0,x_1,x_2], where x_i acts on sections as s_i, and let

    T = T(X,H) = direct sum over j >= 0 of H^0(X, O_X(jH)),

a graded ring containing S.  Let A = C[[x_0,x_1,x_2]] and let
n = T_{>0} be the irrelevant ideal.  The completed cone of (X,H) is

    R = R(X,H) = T (tensor over S) A,

which is also the completion of T at n; that identification is step (c)
of Proposition 2.3.

To resolve Spec R we use the following two schemes.  Let V_0 be the
total space of O_D(-1), which is also the blowup of A^3 = Spec S at the
origin, with exceptional divisor D.  Let W_0 = X x_D V_0, the total
space of O_X(-H).  Base change along Spec S -> Spec A:

    V = V_0 x_S Spec A,     W = W_0 x_S Spec A.

Lemma 2.2.  With this notation:

 (i) W_0 -> Spec T is an isomorphism over the complement of the vertex,
     and W_0 -> X is a line bundle;
 (ii) g: W -> V is finite, p: W -> Spec R is proper, and p is an
     isomorphism over the complement of the closed point of Spec R;
 (iii) X in W and D in V are effective Cartier divisors with ideal
     sheaves J and I satisfying

         J = g^*I,     I|_D = O_D(1),     J|_X = O_X(H);

 (iv) V and W are regular and integral;
 (v) the ring of global functions on W is R.

Proof.  (i) On X_i = {s_i != 0} the line bundle O_X(H) is trivialized by
s_i, and clearing poles along div(s_i) identifies

    T[1/s_i] = O(X_i)[s_i, s_i^{-1}].

Both the punctured line bundle over X_i and the corresponding open part
of Spec T have this ring of functions, so W_0 -> Spec T is an
isomorphism over the complement of the vertex.  Also the global
functions on the total space of a line bundle L are the sum of the
H^0 of all powers of L; for L = O_X(-H) this sum is T.

(ii) Spec T -> Spec S is finite, so W_0 -> V_0 is finite by base
change, and hence g: W -> V is finite.  V -> Spec A is proper, being a
blowup, so W -> Spec A is proper; Spec R -> Spec A is separated, hence
p: W -> Spec R is proper.  Over the punctured base, p and V -> Spec A
are the open immersions described in (i), by flat base change.

(iii) X in W_0 is the zero section of a line bundle, hence an effective
Cartier divisor, and base change preserves that.  The ideal I of D in V
satisfies I|_D = O_D(1), because the normal bundle of the exceptional
divisor of a blowup is O_D(-1).  Base change gives J = g^*I, and the
normal bundle of the zero section of the total space of O_X(-H) is
O_X(-H), so J|_X = O_X(H).

(iv) V is the blowup of A^3 at the origin, base changed to the
completion, hence regular.  Let w be a closed point of W and let t be a
local equation of X at w.  Then t is a nonzerodivisor on O_{W,w} and
O_{W,w}/(t) is the local ring of X at a closed point, which is regular.
Lifting generators of the quotient maximal ideal to O_{W,w} shows that
the embedding dimension of O_{W,w} is at most its dimension: there is
one more generator and, t being a nonzerodivisor, one more dimension.
Hence O_{W,w} is regular.  Every point of W is a localization of such a
closed point: take a closed point in its closure, which exists because
closures meet the closed fiber.  Localizations of regular local rings
are regular, so W is regular, and the same argument gives V regular.

A regular Noetherian scheme has disjoint irreducible components, and
every component of W meets the closed fiber of W -> Spec A, which is X
and is integral.  So W is integral; the same holds for V with closed
fiber D.

(v) W_0 -> Spec S is separated and quasi-compact, so flat base change
applied to the Cech complex of a finite affine cover gives

    O(W) = O(W_0) (tensor over S) A = T (tensor over S) A = R.

QED

2.3 The ring is a complete normal local domain

Proposition 2.3.  R is a 3-dimensional complete Noetherian normal local
domain containing C, with residue field C, and R is a finite A-module.

Proof.  (a) T is a domain.  Trivialize O_X(H) rationally by a nonzero
rational section tau.  Dividing a section of O_X(jH) by tau^j embeds
each graded piece in the field C(X) of rational functions, and the
multiplication is compatible, so T embeds in C(X)[t, t^{-1}], which is
a domain.

(b) T is a finite graded S-module.  By the projection formula the
graded piece T_j is H^0(D, F(j)), where F = f_*O_X is a coherent sheaf
on D.  Choose a surjection O_D(-a)^p -> F, with kernel K.  For j >= a
the sequence

    0 -> K(j) -> O_D(j-a)^p -> F(j) -> 0

has H^1(D, O_D(j-a)) = 0, so H^0(D, F(j)) is the image of H^0(D,
O_D(j-a))^p = (S_{j-a})^p.  That is, T_j is contained in S_{j-a} T_a.
Hence T is generated as an S-module by the finite-dimensional space
T_0 + T_1 + ... + T_a.

(c) R is the completion of T at n.  Let a = (x_0,x_1,x_2)T.  If the
homogeneous S-module generators of T have degrees at most a', then

    a^j is contained in T_{>= j},     T_{>= j+a'} is contained in a^j,

so the a-adic and n-adic filtrations are cofinal.  Therefore

    R = lim T/a^j = lim T/n^j = the product of the T_j for j >= 0,

with the Cauchy product as multiplication.  In particular R is
complete.  Moreover T_0 = H^0(X,O_X) = C and T_{>0} = n, so the
residue field of R is C.

(d) R is a domain, finite over A.  Let u = sum u_j and v = sum v_j be
nonzero, with u_j and v_k the first nonzero homogeneous parts.  The
first nonzero homogeneous part of uv is u_j v_k, which is nonzero
because T is a domain.  So R is a domain.  Since T is a finite
S-module, R = T (tensor over S) A is a finite A-module.  The map
S -> T is injective because f is surjective, and A is flat over S, so
A -> R is injective.  Hence dim R = dim A = 3, and R is Noetherian
because it is finite over the Noetherian ring A.

(e) R is normal.  By Lemma 2.2, W is regular and integral, so W is
normal, and O(W) = R.  If a fraction of R is integral over R then it is
integral over every local ring O_{W,w}, hence lies in every O_{W,w},
hence lies in R.

QED

2.4 From a module to a sheaf on the model

Proposition 2.4.  Let M be a nonzero finitely generated R-module of
depth 3.  Then:

 (a) M is a free A-module of some rank N > 0;
 (b) M is a free R_q-module for every prime q of R different from m;
 (c) the sheaf E = (p^*(M-tilde))^{**} is coherent and torsion-free on
     W, agrees with M away from X, and its restriction P = E|_X is
     torsion-free of positive rank r;
 (d) g_*E is torsion-free and agrees with O_V^N away from D.

Proof.  (a) M has depth 3 over R, so it is Cohen-Macaulay over R of
dimension 3, hence over A.  A system of parameters of A is therefore an
M-regular sequence, in particular x_0,x_1,x_2 is.  By the
Auslander-Buchsbaum formula, projective dimension plus depth equals the
depth of the ring, so M has projective dimension 0 over the regular
ring A: M is free.

(b) Let q be a prime of R other than m.  The localization inequality
gives depth_{R_q} M_q >= depth_R M - dim(R/q), and since depth_R M = 3
and dim R = 3 this is at least 3 - dim(R/q).  Concatenating a chain of
primes below q with one above q gives dim R >= dim(R/q) + dim R_q, so
3 - dim(R/q) >= dim R_q.  Thus depth_{R_q} M_q >= dim R_q.  By Lemma
2.2 the local ring R_q is regular, so another application of
Auslander-Buchsbaum shows that M_q is free.

(c) By (b), M-tilde is a vector bundle on the punctured spectrum of
Spec R, which p identifies with W - X.  So p^*(M-tilde) is a vector
bundle there and E is reflexive, agreeing with M away from X.  On a
regular integral scheme of dimension 3 a reflexive sheaf has depth at
least 2 at every point: dualizing a finite presentation writes the
sheaf as the kernel of a map between locally free sheaves, and the
depth lemma applies.

Let x be a point of X with dim O_{W,x} >= 2.  The local equation t of X
is a nonzerodivisor on E because E is torsion-free, so depth P_x >= 2-1
= 1; that is, P is torsion-free there.  At the generic point of X the
ring O_{W,x} is a discrete valuation ring, E_x is free of some positive
rank r, and t is again a nonzerodivisor, so P_x is free of rank r.
Hence P is torsion-free of rank r > 0.

(d) g_*E is a subsheaf of the constant sheaf of the function field of
V, so it is torsion-free.  Off D, g is an isomorphism onto V - D, and E
is trivial there of rank N by (a), so g_*E agrees with O_V^N away from
D.

QED
