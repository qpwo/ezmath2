A coordinate layer visits every pair and, independently on each, swaps the two cards with probability 1/2 and leaves them alone otherwise. A sweep applies the layers in the order 1,2,...,d.

One layer already makes every single card uniform over the N vertices, and no later layer disturbs that: the marginal law of one card is uniform from the first layer onward. The deck is not uniform. Two cards that have not yet met occupy perfectly correlated positions, and the law of the whole permutation is supported on at most 2^{tN/2} points after t physical shuffles, far fewer than N!. Randomising a single card and randomising the deck are different problems.

Thorp introduced this shuffle, under the name of the faro shuffle, while analysing nonrandom dealing procedures in card games.

./pdf/thorp-1973.pdf

A bounded number of sweeps suffices, independent of N. A physical shuffle performs one coordinate layer and then rotates the coordinate names cyclically; after d physical shuffles the rotating layers have run through all d directions exactly once, so d physical shuffles make one sweep. Hence O(log N) physical shuffles randomise the deck, and the counting bound shows this order is optimal. In detail, t physical shuffles toss tN/2 independent coins, so at most 2^{tN/2} permutations are reachable and
[
|mu_t-U_{S_N}|_{TV} ge 1-frac{2^{tN/2}}{N!}.
]
Requiring distance at most 1/4 forces 2^{tN/2}/N!ge 3/4, that is tge (2/N)log_2(3N!/4)=2d-O(1).

The history of upper bounds is a history of the exponents. Morris proved O(d^{44}) for N=2^d; Montenegro and Tetali reduced it to O(d^{29}); Morris then obtained O((log N)^4) for every even deck size and O(d^3) for dyadic decks. A four-row moment recursion reaches O(d), meeting the counting bound up to a factor.

The engine is a permanent ineuality on the symmetric group S_4, and the reason four rows appear is that removing two coordinate directions leaves four independent smaller cubes. To set up the ineuality, write
[
|f|_p=(frac14sum_{j=1}^{4}f(j)^p)^{1/p}
]
for f:{1,2,3,4}to[0,∞), and let u be the uniform law on S_4. The expectation E_uprod_{i=1}^4 f_i(pi(i)) equals perm(f_i(j))/4!. Carlen, Lieb and Loss proved
[
perm(f_i(j))le(4!)/(4^2)prod_{i=1}^{4}(sum_{j=1}^{4}f_i(j)^2)^{1/2},
]
which in the present normalisation reads E_uprod_i f_i(pi(i))leprod_i|f_i|_2, and the equality case is settled there: for nonzero nonnegative functions on four points, equality forces every f_i to be constant.

The needed statement is not the sharp one. It is a stable version: the uniform law may be perturbed slightly, provided every coordinate marginal stays exactly uniform.


There are absolute constants p_0in(4/3,2) and ε>0 with the following property. Let nu be a probability measure on S_4 with |nu-u|_{TV}<ε and

The marginal hypothesis is not a technical convenience. Setting three of the functions equal to one reduces the ineuality to sum_j a(j)f(j)le|f|_{p_0} with a(j)=nu{pi(4)=j}. Uniform coordinate marginals are forced by the ineuality itself.

Proof. [Proof of Theorem~thm:permanent]
The claim is homogeneous of degree one in each f_i. A zero function makes both sides zero, so assume all functions are nonzero and normalise |f_i|_2=1. The set of such quadruples is compact. On it the exponent-two ineuality holds, with equality only at f_iequiv1 for all i.

Two regions must be treated. Near the constant quadruple the exponent-two ineuality is useless, since equality holds there and any improvement must be extracted from a second-order expansion. Away from it there is room by compactness.

Near the constants. Since each f_i is nonnegative with mean m_i>0 close to one, divide by the mean and write f_i=1+g_i with E_j g_i(j)=frac14sum_j g_i(j)=0; the ineuality is unaffected by this rescaling. Put
[
V=sum_{i=1}^{4}|g_i|_2^2, b=max_i|g_i|_∞ .
]
On four points |g|_∞le 2|g|_2, because |g|_2^2gefrac14|g|_∞^2.

For ine j, the pair (pi(i),pi(j)) is a uniformly random ordered pair of distinct points, so
[
E_u,g_i(pi(i))g_j(pi(j))
=frac1{12}sum_{rne s}g_i(r)g_j(s)
=-frac13< g_i,g_j> .
]
Summing over pairs,
[
sum_{i<j}E_u,g_i(pi(i))g_j(pi(j))
=-frac13sum_{i<j}< g_i,g_j>
=frac16(V-Bigl|sum_i g_iBigr|_2^2)lefrac V6 .

Every linear term vanishes under nu and not merely under u, since E_nu g_i(pi(i))=frac14sum_j g_i(j)=0 by the marginal hypothesis. Replacing u by nu changes each quadratic expectation by at most 2ε|g_i|_∞|g_j|_∞le 8ε|g_i|_2|g_j|_2, so the total change is at most an absolute constant times ε V. A monomial of degree at least three involves at least three distinct g_i; bounding all but two factors by b in sup norm and applying Cauchy--Schwarz to the remaining two, whose squared expectations are controlled by the uniform marginals, bounds each such term by an absolute constant times bV once ble 1/2. Therefore
[

le 1+(frac16+Cε+Cb)V .

On the other side, Taylor's formula on [1/2,3/2], uniformly for pin[p_*,2], gives
[
E_j(1+g_i(j))^p=1+frac{p(p-1)}2|g_i|_2^2+O(b|g_i|_2^2),
]
and taking pth roots and multiplying the four factors,
[
prod_{i=1}^{4}|1+g_i|_p=1+(p-1)/(2)V+O(bV).
]
The two displays are compatible exactly when (p-1)/(2)>frac16, that is p>4/3. Fix p_*in(4/3,2) and then b_*>0, ε_*>0 small enough that the error terms fit inside the strict gap (p_*-1)/(2)-frac16. For every pin[p_*,2], every nu with |nu-u|_{TV}leε_* and uniform marginals, and every quadruple with b<b_*, the ineuality holds.

Away from the constants. The condition b<b_* contains a fixed open neighbourhood U of the constant quadruple in the normalised compact set. On its complement the exponent-two ineuality is strict everywhere, so by compactness it has a positive minimum gap. Both sides are continuous in the functions, in the exponent and in the probability masses of nu, so choosing p_0in[p_*,2) close enough to two and then εleε_* small enough keeps this gap positive on the complement. The local argument covers U with the same constants. The two regions cover everything.

Now the shuffle as an operator. Positions are binary strings, a permutation sends input positions to output positions, and gh applies h first. The average of all optional edge swaps in direction i is an orthogonal projection Pi_i of the group algebra, and the sweep operator is
[
T_N=Pi_d...Pi_1, B_N=T_NT_N^*,
]
so that B_N is a positive contraction. All statements about mixing concern T_N; the moment estimates concern B_N, and the two are separated at the end.

Physical time is bookkeeping. Let R(x_1,...,x_d)=(x_2,...,x_d,x_1) and let S_t be the layer in the currently first coordinate. A physical shuffle is RS_t. If Y_t is the accumulated permutation, then X_t=R^{-t}Y_t satisfies X_{t+1}=(R^{-t}S_{t+1}R^t)X_t; the conjugated layers visit all directions in one cyclic order, and since R^d=I relabelling that order as 1,...,d turns d physical shuffles into exactly T_N.

Two structural facts about a single sweep will be used repeatedly: it annihilates the sign representation and every diagram with more than N/2 rows, and at any fixed N its operator norm on each remaining nontrivial irreducible is strictly less than one.
