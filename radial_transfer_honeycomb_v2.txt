# Radial transfer estimates and polygon length lawsfor honeycomb walks


maketitle

Abstract: 
A self-avoiding polygon on the honeycomb lattice is a closed loop that
never visits a vertex twice.  Give a polygon the critical weight
w(gamma)=x_*^{,|gamma|}, where |gamma| is its number of edges
and x_*=(2+sqrt2)^{-1/2} is the honeycomb connective constant
raised to the power -1.  We prove that the total critical weight of
polygons of diameter at least r is r^{-2+o(1)}, that the same sum
carrying one power of the length is r^{-2/3+o(1)}, and that the
truncated second length moment is at most r^{2/3+o(1)}.  As a
consequence a polygon sampled at criticality and conditioned to have
length at least n has diameter n^{3/4+o(1)} with probability
tending to one.  The exponent 3/4 is the long-predicted size exponent
of planar self-avoiding walk; the proof here arrives at it through
positive polygonal estimates and does not assume a scaling limit.

# Introduction: what is being counted, and what is attacked

## The game

Place vertices at the points P_{ij}=ih+jv and R_{ij}=P_{ij}+(h+v)/3 of the plane, where
[
   h=(sqrt3,0), v=(sqrt3/2,3/2), i,jinmathbb ZbbZ .
]
Every P_{ij} is joined to the three R-vertices R_{ij},R_{i-1,j},R_{i,j-1}, and every edge has length one. This is the regular honeycomb (hexagonal) lattice; it is the lattice of a chicken-wire fence and of a single sheet of graphene.

A self-avoiding polygon gamma is a finite cycle p_1p_2... p_Lp_1 of distinct lattice vertices, each consecutive pair joined by an edge. Equivalently it is a round walk that never crosses itself and never reuses a corner. We write L=|gamma| for its number of edges and D=diamgamma for its Euclidean diameter, i.e. the largest distance between two of its points.

Polygons that differ only by a translate are the same object for us; we count each translation class once. This throws away the position and keeps the shape and size.

The critical weight of a class gamma is
[
   w(gamma)=x_*^{,|gamma|}, x_*=(2+sqrt2)^{-1/2}.
]
This particular number is not a coincidence: Duminil-Copin and Smirnov proved that on the honeycomb lattice the connective constant equals mu=√(2+sqrt2), i.e. the number of m-step self-avoiding walks grows like mu^m, so x_*=1/mu is exactly the critical value at which the length series sum_m (# m-step walks),x^m sits on the boundary between convergence and divergence. We work at exactly this value; it is the analog of the critical temperature of a magnet. Because x_* is the critical activity, the weights w(gamma) balance every possible length equally and the only thing left to measure is size.

## The three observables

For an unrooted polygon (one considered up to translation) define, for r>0 a length scale (thought of as large),

   A(r) &= sum_{diamgammage r} w(gamma) &&(mass of big-diameter polygons),
   P(r) &= sum_{diamgammage r} |gamma|, w(gamma) &&(the same with one length factor).

Also, summing over the complementary range (diameter below r) but carrying two length factors,

   M_2(r) = sum_{diamgammale r} |gamma|^2 w(gamma)
   (truncated second length moment).

All three are finite sums for each fixed r; we will prove they stay controlled as rto∞.

## The main result

Write f(r)=r^{a+o(1)} if for every ε>0 one has r^{a-ε}le f(r)le r^{a+ε} for all sufficiently large r. The one-sided notation f(r)le r^{a+o(1)} is read the same way with two inequalities removed.

[Diameter tails and second length moment]
For the translation classes of honeycomb self-avoiding polygons with the critical weight w(gamma)=x_*^{|gamma|},
[
   A(r)=r^{-2+o(1)}, P(r)=r^{-2/3+o(1)},
   M_2(r)le r^{2/3+o(1)} (rto∞).
]


The first reading of the theorem: most of the critical weight sits on small polygons, and the mass above diameter r decays like r^{-2}. The second reading, dividing P by A, says that among big polygons the average length is r^{4/3} (since r^{-2/3}/r^{-2}=r^{4/3}). Polygons conditioned on large diameter are long: the mean length is larger than the diameter, which is the geometric statement that in a good approximation the polygon looks like a fractal curve that wanders through its own bounding box repeatedly.

The second moment M_2 controls the opposite failure mode: it rules out the critical mass being concentrated on very long polygons that hug a tiny box. Together the three lines in Theorem~thm:main squeeze the size law from both sides.

## The consequence for length cutoffs

Condition now on the length of the polygon instead of its diameter. The long-standing prediction of the polymer literature (Nienhuis; Lawler, Schramm and Werner) is that a critical polygon of length n has typical diameter of order n^{3/4}. The next corollary turns Theorem~thm:main into exactly that.

[Length cutoffs, typical size 3/4]
The length tails are
[
   sum_{L(gamma)ge n} w(gamma) = n^{-3/2+o(1)},
   sum_{L(gamma)ge n} L(gamma),w(gamma) = n^{-1/2+o(1)}.
]
Under either normalized weight w(gamma) or L(gamma)w(gamma), conditioned on L(gamma)ge n, one has diamgamma=n^{3/4+o(1)} in probability. The same holds with the length restricted to nle L(gamma)<n^{1+delta} for any fixed delta>0. Under the length-weighted law conditioned on diameter ge r one has L(gamma)=r^{4/3+o(1)} in probability.

Finally, the mean length in the unrooted critical ensemble conditioned on diameter ge r is r^{4/3+o(1)}, and the same holds on the power-separated annulus rlediamgamma<r^{1+ε}.


``In probability'' means: for every ε>0, the value lies between t^{a-ε} and t^{a+ε} with probability tending to one as the cutoff tto∞. The exponents 3/4 (size at fixed length) and 4/3 (length at fixed size) are reciprocal under the relation Dsim L^{3/4}, exactly as they should be.

We also prove two boundary (half-plane and slab) results for arches and corridors; those are stated in their own section (Ssec:boundary) because they are logically separate and can be read independently.

# Finite transfer identities

This section builds the finite machine. On a cylinder of circumference N|h|, encode self-avoiding polygons in a vector space of noncrossing pairings of the N boundary stubs (the "link states"), with a transfer matrix that slides one row of cells up. A self-avoiding polygon on the cylinder is a linear combination that is a fixed vector of this transfer matrix. We compute those fixed vectors (a polynomial family V_N), specialize them to the physical honeycomb weights, and extract the identity
[
   E_N,tau_N=c_*,tau^v_{N-1}
]
that drives the rest of the paper.

## Local weights, braid, fusion and cup

We work with three spin labels: 0 is a vacant edge; +1 and -1 are the two orientations of an occupied edge. Take a spectral parameter lambda near d_*=pi/8 and put
[
 Q_lambda=e^{ilambda},
 chi=i,e^{-2ilambda},
 n=chi^2+chi^{-2}=-2cos(4lambda),
 X_lambda=(sinlambda)/(sin2lambda).
]
At the physical parameter lambda=d_* write Q=Q_{d_*}, q=Q^2=chi=e^{pi i/4}, x_*=X_{d_*}=1/(Q+Q^{-1})=(2+sqrt2)^{-1/2}, and n=0.

The local Hilbert space mathcal H=calH=mathbb C^3bbC^3 has basis {|0>,|1>,|-1>}. On mathcal HcalH ⊗ mathcal HcalH we define a matrix B(r), depending on a spectral argument r=e^{ix}. It preserves total spin (charge), is transpose-symmetric, is unchanged when both the order of the two tensor factors and the charges are flipped, and satisfies some elementary analytic facts we use throughout: it has poles only at r^2=Q_lambda^{-4},Q_lambda^{-6} (simple), the values B(1)=I, and the two factorizations
[
   B(Q_lambda^3)propto C,C^{mathsf T}sfT,
   B(Q_lambda^2)propto A,A^{mathsf T}sfT,
]
where, with c=sum_{a=pm1}chi^{a}|a,-a>,
[
   C=|00>+c,
   A|0>=|00>+X_lambda,c,
   A|a>=X_lambda(|a,0>+|0,a>) (a=pm1).
]
The symbols C (a "cup", gluing two legs) and A (a "fusion", merging two legs into one) are the two maps through which all the reductions below go. The entries of B in the diagonal pair (vacuum, both occupied) and the single-occupied block are
[
 t&Uc^{t}Uc&wI+Wc,c^{t},
 (t,u,v,U,w,W)=((p_0+a_0b_3, s_2b_3, a_0b_3, s_2a_0, b_2b_3, -a_0b_1))/(g(x)),
]
where s_j=sin(jlambda), a_j=sin(jlambda+x), b_j=sin(jlambda-x), p_0=s_2s_3, g(x)=a_2a_3, and the two single-occupied eigenvalues are u+v and u-v. This is a Yang–Baxter matrix for the dilute O(1) loop model with fugacity n, specialized at lambda=d_* to the honeycomb SAW weights.

[Braid, unitarity, reductions]
At d_* the following hold as identities of operators on mathcal H^calH^{ ⊗  3}, with B_{12}=B ⊗  I and B_{23}=I ⊗  B.
 Unitarity: B(r)B(r^{-1})=I.
 Braid relation (Yang–Baxter): B_{12}(z)B_{23}(rz)B_{12}(r)=B_{23}(r)B_{12}(rz)B_{23}(z).
 Crossing: g(3lambda-x)B_{23}(Q_lambda^3/r)(C ⊗  I)=g(x)B_{12}(r)(I ⊗  C), and the same with 12leftrightarrow 23 and Cleftrightarrow C^t.
 Deleting a pair (cup): B_{12}(r)B_{23}(Q_lambda^3 r)(C ⊗  I)=D(x)(I ⊗  C), where D(x)=g(-x)/g(3lambda+x) equals 1 at d_*; similarly with the two factors exchanged.
 Fusing a pair: B_{12}(r)B_{23}(Q_lambda^2 r)(A ⊗  I)=d_f(x)(I ⊗  A)B(Q_lambda r) with d_f(x)=b_3/a_5, and the mirror identity; both transport scalars equal 1 at d_*.



Proof. 
Unitarity follows from a_jb_j=s_j^2-a_0^2 in the single-occupied block plus w(r)w(r^{-1})=1 in the doubly occupied block. The braid relation is checked at the seven special arguments zin{1,1/r,Q^3,Q^3/r,0,∞,(degenerate)}; at each of those B collapses to a degenerate rank and the two sides reduce to the same expression, while generically the entries after clearing g(x)g(y) are polynomials of degree at most eight and parity-fixed, so seven distinct arguments (counted with signs) suffice. Crossing follows by comparing the two blocks in the single- and doubly-occupied entries and matching the chi^{a-b} phases. The cup and fusion identities are the "two special ranks of B" — B(Q^3)propto CC^t removes a pair, B(Q^2)propto AA^t replaces a pair by a single spin — applied between the two positions where the pair appears; the transport factors D(x)=1, d_f(x)=1 at d_* because a_5=b_3 and a_5=b_3, a_6=b_2 there. The transport can be verified entry by entry in the single-/double-occupied blocks (see [bookkeeping]: six scalar sine-addition identities d_ftildetilde t=tt'+nXUu't=tt'+nXUu' and its siblings, all elementary).


[Why we want A and C]
The matrix B acts on a row of N spins, but the SAW condition — never reuse a corner — is not visible in the spin labels alone. That is why we lift the spins to noncrossing pairings; the two maps C (collapsing a pair to a vacuum, i.e. closing a segment of the loop) and A (replacing a pair by a single occupied leg) are exactly the allowed fusion moves on noncrossing pairings. The braid relation says local sliding of spins past each other is consistent; the reductions say the two collapse moves C,A commute with sliding. The transfer matrix below enumerates noncrossing pairings, and A,C are the tools by which it is reduced column by column.


## Cylinder rows, twists, and the link image

Number the slots 1,...,N periodically, with a seam between N and 1. Let z=(z_1,...,z_N) be site parameters, t_i=z_i^2 the squared parameters, and u the auxiliary spectral parameter. The open row with inserted auxiliary spins p on the left seam and k on the right seam is M_{p,k}(z;u); the operator B(z_i/u) propagates through column i. Let etane0 be a twist and set
[
   T_eta(z;u)=sum_s eta^{s},M_{s,s}(z;u).
]
This is a closed transfer matrix on total charge zero on the spatial circle; T_eta is rational in y=u^2 (parity) and T_eta(z;u) commutes with itself for different u. The two reductions in Lemma~lem:braid yield the product identities
[
 T_eta(z;z_j)T_eta(z;Q_lambda^{-2}z_j)=Big(prod_i d_f(x_{ij})Big)T_eta(z;Q_lambda^{-1}z_j),
 T_eta(z;z_j)T_eta(z;Q_lambda^{-3}z_j)=Big(prod_i D(x_{ij})Big)I.
]
At lambda=d_*, crossing gives the transpose symmetry T_eta(z;u)^t=T_{eta^{-1}}(z^{-1};Q^{-3}/u) on total charge zero.

Noncrossing pairings.:  A link state is a noncrossing partial matching of the N spatial slots drawn below the cut of the cylinder, with each arc labelled by the winding class of its two endpoints; unmatched slots are vacant. The empty state (all vacancies) is allowed. This is a finite basis. It maps to charge-zero spin tensors by giving each arc independent spins s,-s (s=pm1) at its two ends, summing with the factor chi^seta^{ks}, where k counts whether the arc crosses the seam. Call the image the link image; it is a linear subspace of the spin space, transformed by Ω (rotation by one slot, with twist eta) and preserved by projections to any specific set of spin values.

The transfer T_eta intertwines the link image with the spinless diagrammatic transfer with loop weights n for a contractible boundary loop and eta+eta^{-1} for a winding one. This is the snake / Temperley–Lieb re-indexing: insertions of B locally act on two spatial slots and preserve the link image because B's local blocks are the six local connection weights t,u,v,U,w,W. At lambda=d_* and eta=pm i two facts hold: (i) the empty-cell weight t=1 identically, so the vacuum of the link-image diagram is uniform; (ii) all closed loops have n=0 weight at eta=pm i under the physical pull-back, so the sum over closed contractible polygons is killed and only winding ones survive at first order.

The two physical reductions.:  A pair t,q^2t of column parameters of type A fuses to the single column qt; a pair t,q^3t of type C deletes. These are the physical meaning of Lemma~lem:braid: B(Q^2)=AA^t fuses two columns of the polynomial vector to one, B(Q^3)=CC^t removes two columns. At the homogeneous point z_i=1 with auxiliary u=Q^{-1} these fusions are exactly the two grouping choices on each honeycomb cell: the bottom-left and top-right ports group together (one honeycomb trivalent vertex), the bottom-right and top-left ports group (the other). The line between the bottom and top row is a horizontal cut through the edge midpoints.

## The two polynomial vacua

The fixed vectors of T_eta have a rational structure in the site parameters that we now exploit. We work at lambda=d_* throughout and use two twists:
[
   p=2, eta=i=q^2;qquadqquad p=1, eta=-i=q^{-2}.
]
The two are related by the particle-hole exchange, and both are needed because the polygon mass Z_eta (next subsection) has different signs on contractible and winding polygons for eta=i vs. eta=-i.

[The two polynomial vacua]
For each choice (p,eta)in{(1,-i),(2,i)} there is a unique family of polynomial vectors V_N(z), Nge0, in the link image, with V_0=1, V_1=|0>, satisfying:
 homogeneity: the z-degree is pN(N-1), and after stripping the spin-s_l component by prod_l z_l^{|s_l|}, each coefficient is polynomial in the t_l=z_l^2 of degree at most p(N-1)-|s_l| in t_l;
 exchange/rotation: exchanging two adjacent arguments acts by B_{ij}(z_i/z_j), and a left rotation acts by Ω on the charges;
 deletion: if the spin at slot l is 0, the vacuum evaluates at t_l=0 and t_l=∞ to V_{N-1} with slot l deleted (up to the appropriate powers of t_k);
 pair fusions: at z_j=Q^3z_i the vector takes the value prescribed by inserting a cup C on V_{N-2} of the other slots and multiplying by an explicit prefactor Pi_{ij} (see below); for p=2 this holds for both orders ito j,jto i, for p=1 for i<j.

The empty coefficient D_p=V_N^{empty} is not identically zero, and
[
   T_eta(z;u)V_N(z)=V_N(z)
]
as a rational identity in u and z. The normalized vector V_N/D_p extends regularly to homogeneous site parameters and equals the empty-normalized stationary state obtained from the physical transfer-semigroup limit lim_{mto∞} T_eta^m e_∅.


Proof. [Proof sketch]
The proof is an induction on N. The base cases N=0,1,2 are displayed. For Nge3 one interpolates in the last site parameter t_N: prescribe the value of V_N on the pair slices t_N=q^{pm3}t_i (for p=2, both signs; for p=1, positive only), plus the deletion value at t_N=0, and Lagrange-interpolate. The prescribed inputs W_{ij} are polynomial on the pair slices (Lemma~lem:braid kills the exchange poles there), so the interpolation has only removable poles and yields a polynomial with the correct degree. Compatibility of two intersecting pair slices, and covariance of the prescriptions under the exchanges and rotations, come from the braid relation and the reduction identities B(Q^3)=CC^t, B(Q^2)=AA^t on the pair slices. This closes the induction for the pair values, exchanges, and rotation. The identity T_eta V_N=V_N is proved by comparing both sides as polynomials in one site variable t_l: the difference vanishes at the pair nodes by the Pi_{ij} prescriptions, at t_l=u^2 and t_l=q^3u^2 by the two special ranks of B, and at 0 by the deletion limit; a degree count then kills the difference. Uniqueness follows from the same degree count at every induction step. Regularity at the homogeneous point follows from the interpolation representation together with the physical transfer-semigroup argument of Sc4:cylinder: the physical transfer is nonnegative, contracts coefficientwise to a rank-one eigenvector, and this normalized eigenvector agrees with V_N/D_p by generic uniqueness.


The empty component is a Pfaffian.:  For the p=1 vacuum (the -i twist) with N even, the empty coefficient is an alternant of 2times 2 minors of a single Cauchy-like kernel:

   D_1(X)=prod_{i<j}(p_K(x_i,x_j))/(x_j-x_i),
      Pf![(x_j^2-x_i^2)/(p_K(x_i,x_j))]_{i,j=1}^N,
      p_K(x,y)=x^2+sqrt2,xy+y^2 .

The prefactor clears the apparent poles of p_K in the rows and columns of the Pfaffian, so this is a symmetric polynomial of the required degree. Its two crucial values on pair slices are
[
   D_1(x_1,x_2)=x_1+x_2,
   D_1(Y,q^3x)=sqrt2,xprod_{tin Y}(t-x),D_1(Y)  (the q^3 deletion),
]
and the q^{pm1} fusion identity
[
   D_1(Y,q^{-1}x,qx)=sqrt2,xprod_{tin Y}(t+x),D_1(Y,x).
]
For the p=2 vacuum (the +i twist) the empty component is the squared Vandermonde form

   D_2(X)=P^Nprod_{i<j}[(p_K(t_i,t_j))/(t_i-t_j)]^2det[k(t_i,t_j)],
   k(t_i,t_j)=(t_it_j)/(p_K(t_i,t_j)),

with P=2+sqrt2. Both expressions are nonzero; in particular the transfer has a unique physical stationary state and both continuations are well defined.

## A marked row and its polygon interpretation

A closed transfer kills every closed loop when eta=pm i: that is why the unmarked stationary state is a bare vacuum. To measure the polygon mass that sits in one particular edge, freeze the orientation of a single auxiliary edge instead of summing it. For an operator H on the N-site spin space write the bilinear bracket
[
   < H>=Psi_eta(z^{-1})^t,H,Psi_eta(z),
   Z_eta(y)=big< M_{-,-}(z;sqrt y)big> ,
]
where Psi_eta=V_N/D_p is the normalized vacuum and M_{p,k} is the open row with fixed auxiliaries p on the left seam and k on the right seam. Write P_{l,s} for the projector to spin s at slot l.

[Marked-edge count]
At homogeneous site parameters and y=q^{-k}, k=1,2, the value Z_eta(q^{-k}) is a sum over self-avoiding polygons through the sampled edge, with weight x_*^{|gamma|}, of signs +1 or -1 depending on the parity of the loop. Precisely, at the minus twist eta=-i the three values Z_{-i}(1),Z_{-i}(q^{-1}),Z_{-i}(q^{-2}) each equal x_*^2 times the sum over positive (both essential and contractible) polygon mass through one fixed edge; the three edges 1,q^{-1},q^{-2} sample one of each honeycomb edge type, up to lattice translations on the same cylinder. At the plus twist eta=+i the same sum is essential mass minus contractible mass. The values pass to the limits because the minus twist makes the sum positive and bounded.


Proof. 
The projection to the fixed auxiliary edge closes one polygon at that edge. All remaining closed loops cancel in pairs by the local turn phase: a contractible simple loop contributes pm1 with squared total zero, a winding one contributes pm i with squared zero; the surviving loop is the unique one through the mark, because its orientation is uniquely fixed by the mark. This residual sign is +1 at eta=-i and pm1 depending on whether the surviving loop is contractible or essential at eta=+i, as computed by the seam-crossing count N_L,N_U and q^{pm2}eta^{pm1}. Summing over all three edge types gives positive mass at the minus twist; the sum over essential loops alone at +i follows by subtracting.


The important point is that at eta=-i the marked scalar Z_{-i} is a positive geometric quantity, whereas at eta=+i it is the difference of essential and contractible parts. Since the polymer mass is positive, the combination
[
   S_a := c_*+Z_{+i}(a)+Z_{+i}(q^{-1}a)+Z_{+i}(q^{-2}a)=Z_{-i}(a)+Z_{-i}(q^{-1}a)+Z_{-i}(q^{-2}a)-c_*
]
is real and its size gives the winding contribution; here c_*=2-sqrt2=2/P and a is a distinguished site parameter.

## Finite identities for marked polygon masses

We now write the marked scalar in terms of finite "color sums," which are the algebraic objects whose asymptotics we will compute.

Let d=q+q^{-1}=sqrt2, P=2+d, c_*=2-d=2/P. For a site list X=(t_i) with a distinguished parameter ain X, define the summing object
[
   S'_{eta,a}=Z_eta(a)+Z_eta(q^{-1}a)+Z_eta(q^{-2}a), S_a=c_*+S'_{i,a},
]
where in the subscript i=√(-1). Note the three values sit at three distinct arguments.

The unmarked color sum.:  Introduce three colors {-,0,+} with weights
[
   ell_-=(-1-d+i)/(2), ell_0=P, ell_+=qell_-,
    ell_-+ell_0+ell_+=1, ell_-ell_+=P^2/beta,quadbeta=G(1)=2P,
]
and a pair weight between two sites of colors s,t, in a fixed order (x,y)mapsto factor g_{st}(y/x) given by the symmetric table
[
   g(r)=1&G(r/q)&G(r/q)G(r/q^2)G(rq)&G(r)&G(r/q)G(rq)G(rq^2)&G(rq)&1,
   G(t)=frac{(1+t)^2}{(t-q)(t-q^{-1})}.
]
The symmetry g_{st}(r)=g_{ts}(1/r) makes the pair product reordering-invariant. Sum over all 3^N color assignments; the result is the unmarked color sum tau_X. Also let F_X(y) be the same sum with one site decorated by an extra explicit rational factor L_sigma(t/y), where
[
   L_-=L=(f_5f_6f_7)/(f_2f_3f_4),
   L_0(r)=(L(r/q))/(L(r/q^2)),
   L_+(r)=frac1{L(r/q^3)}, f_j(t)=1-q^jt .
]
Define
[
   R_X=frac{D_+(X)^2}{prod_{{x,y}subset X}delta(x,y)},
   delta(x,y)=p_K(x,y)(x^2+y^2),
]
where D_+=D_2 is the Pfaffian of (c4:D2-det). Both tau_X and R_X are symmetric rational functions of the t's. The central identity is that they coincide.

[Determinant equals color sum]
As rational functions, R_X=tau_X.


Proof. 
Both sides are rational, so it suffices to check that their poles agree (residue by residue) and that both have the same limits at t_i=0,∞. The only possible poles of tau_X are at t_j=q^{pm2}t_i (fusion of two colors) and t_j=q^{pm3}t_i (deletion), each simple and computed by a short table evaluation: two colors combine to a fused color, with weight alpha P at the q^2 fusion and alpha G(q^2)P^2/beta at the q^3 deletion. The determinant R_X has the same simple poles at the same arguments (the identity k_Y^perp(s,t)=H(s/a)H(t/a)k_{Y'}^perp(s,t) for the Pfaffian Schur complement, with H(t)=(t^4-1)/(t^4+1), shows the squared determinant transforms by prod G exactly as the color pair table), with the same residues. Both sides have the same prod_{x<y}t_x^{-2(N-1)} behaviour under t_xmapsto1/t_x (the inversion identity), and both tend to det-level or colored-vacuum constants at tto0,∞. The induction is closed at the initial values N=0,1,2.


Decorating the distinguished site.:  The three values F_X(a),F_X(q^{-1}a),F_X(q^{-2}a) coincide; denote the common value F_a(X). Each equals one half of the sum over the other sites with the additional explicit pair factor H_s^v displayed in [bookkeeping]. Concretely, 2F_a(X)=tau^v where tau^v is the color sum on the N-1 other sites with one distinguished site's worth of decoration. The central algebraic identity is

   S_a,R_X=4c_*,F_a .


Proof. [Proof of (c4:marked-scalar) (sketch)]
Both sides are rational and symmetric in the generic spectra; it suffices to identify them via their residues at the special arguments t_j/a=q^{pm1,pm2,pm3} and at -1. The pair-reduction identities from Lemma~lem:braid (fusion q^2 and deletion q^3) give the matching at q^{pm2},q^{pm3}; the cancellation at q^{pm1} comes from the two distinct sums in S_a. All this leaves a small polynomial ambiguity in the residues of b/a at pm3,-1; symmetry reduces it to at most one unknown constant. The cases N=1,2,3 are computed explicitly and pin the unknown constant. This is the "three initial evaluations" carried out in detail in the original.


The finite polygon identity.:  Let tau_N be the value of tau_X at all N homogeneous sites, and tau^v_{N-1} the value of the decorated sum on N-1 homogeneous sites.

[Finite polygon identity]
For the honeycomb cylinder of horizontal period Nh and even N, let E_N be the sum of x_*^{|gamma|} over winding self-avoiding polygons through three sampled edges, one of each honeycomb edge type, counted once for each sampled edge used. Then

   E_N,tau_N=c_*,tau^v_{N-1}, c_*=2-sqrt2.

Moreover, the total mass of all self-avoiding polygons (winding plus contractible) through the three sampled edges is exactly c_*.


Proof.

Substitute S_a=2E_N, R_X=tau_N (Proposition~prop:det-color) and F_a=tau^v_{N-1}/2 into the marked-scalar identity (c4:marked-scalar). Dividing by 2 gives (a:finite-winding). This is ingredient (1): a positive winding mass expressed as a ratio E_N=c_*tau^v_{N-1}/tau_N of two finite color sums. The denominator is nonzero (Proposition~prop:polynomial-vacua), and Sc4:angular--Sc4:plateau evaluates the ratio. It remains only to extract asymptotics.

# Two pressures on the cylinder

The finite identity (a:finite-winding) expresses E_N as a ratio of tau^v_{N-1} and tau_N. To know the ratio is real and positive we need an independent positivity input, because tracking tau^v and tau_N separately through the analytic continuation of Sc4:angular--Sc4:plateau lets complex phases cancel. This section supplies two such inputs as derivatives of a positive partition function.

Fix a cylinder of circumference N|h|. The closed transfer on the link image carries two loop weights: a factor n=-2cos(4lambda) for each contractible closed loop and a factor s=eta+eta^{-1} for each winding one (the book-keeping is Sc4:cylinder). At lambda=d_*, n=0 and s=0 at eta=pm i; we work near eta=i and use s as a small parameter.

Let Λ(y) be the eigenvalue of the cylinder transfer on the link image, chosen continuously near 1 at the physical point; the notation is y=u^2 for the squared auxiliary parameter. As a function of the twist s it has a Taylor expansion at s=0; define the responses
[
   J_m(y)=[s^m]Λ(y)Big|_{lambda=d_*}, mge1;
   J(y)=∂_lambdaΛ(y)Big|_{lambda=d_*, s=0}.
]
The first class is a twist-derivative (moving the winding loop weight), the second is a lambda-derivative (moving the local cell weights while keeping the twist zero).

[Physical evaluations]
At homogeneous site parameters z_i=1 and y=q^{-1},
[
   J_1(q^{-1})=A_N,
   J(q^{-1})=N(log t_{rm phys})'(d_*)+8A_N^0,
]
where
[
   A_N=sum_{winding gamma/v}x_*^{|gamma|},
   A_N^0=sum_{contractible gamma/v}x_*^{|gamma|},
]
are the winding and contractible masses (sums over axial translation classes, "gamma/v"), and t_{rm phys}(lambda) is the empty-cell weight at u=Q_lambda^{-1}.


Proof. 
On a finite-height cylinder the partition function is exactly a gas of vertex-disjoint polygons with step weight X_lambda, loop weights n,s and empty scalar t_{rm phys} per cell. Its logarithm per row converges to the leading log-eigenvalue by the fixed-N spectral gap: the vacuum matrix element of the leading projection is analytic and nonzero nearby. At s=0 (physical twist) the factor Λ=1 identically in y, so the first derivative of logΛ is the first derivative of Λ. Differentiating in s with lambda=d_*, n=0 selects exactly one polygon (the ∂_s pulls down one winding loop). Differentiating in lambda at s=0 splits into the empty-cell scalar N(log t_{rm phys})'(d_*) and a one-polygon term with weight n'(d_*)=8, i.e. 8A_N^0. Both are monotone in the height cut-off (extra height adds positive mass); the limits exist by monotone convergence.


So the two responses compute the two sums we care about. The remainder of this section shows that both leading constants are of definite sign, by writing each response as the convolution of a positive boundary function with an explicit kernel.

## Thin cylinders suppress large transverse spans

The last ingredient of the pressure section is the statement that cylinder polygons of tall transverse row-span are negligible at any polynomial order.

[Thin cylinders]
Let H(gamma) be the row span of a cylinder polygon of period Nh. For every fixed delta>0, pge0 and integer K>0,
[
   sum_{gamma/v: H(gamma)ge N^{1+delta}}(1+|gamma|)^p,w(gamma)=O(N^{-K}).
]


Proof. 
Connected tuples. The m-th log coefficient in s of the winding partition function is 1/m! times a weighted sum over ordered m-tuples with a factor sum(-1)^{#edges} over connected spanning subgraphs of their incompatibility graph. For a connected graph this factor has sign (-1)^{m-1} and magnitude at least 1 (induction by deletion/contraction of a single edge); for disconnected it is zero. Passage to axial classes and (c4:Press-6) bound the total connected-tuple weights by C_m/N.

Winding spans. For an absolute winding polygon gamma let M_gamma be the total weight of all absolute winding polygons sharing a vertex with it, including itself. If H(gamma)>2, a subpath between two same-sublattice vertices has displacement D_gamma=i'h+j'v with |j'|ge H(gamma)-2. Periodize by D_gamma: every coset of the lattice modulo Nh,D_gamma meets gamma at some translate of any other winding type (a fine-winding argument on a large circle), giving at least (H(gamma)-2)A_N neighbors in the full translation orbit. Restricting the tuple bound to stars gives
[
   sum_{ess/v}w(gamma)(1+H(gamma))^{m-1}le C'_m N^{m-2}.
]
Since |gamma|le 2N(H+1), this proves the claim for windings.

Contractible spans. For a tall contractible polygon delta', lock an R_{i,j} in its highest row. Windings with lowest row j+1 occupying P_{i,j+1} weigh at least A_N/N. Take the symmetric edge difference with the hexagonal face spanned by the two rows; the overlap with the face is a single short path in each polygon, so this splices into a single simple winding polygon zeta with H(zeta)ge H(delta')-1 and length within 2. Moreover at most C|zeta| inverse joins per output class, giving a bound A_N/Ncdot(tall contractible) by a relaxed tall essential sum with a linear weight, which is already negligible. This proves the claim for contractibles.


# The unmarked determinant scale

We must know the size of the denominator tau_N in the finite identity (a:finite-winding). This is the value of the unmarked color sum tau_X at the homogeneous point. Proposition~prop:det-color writes it as R_X=tau_X, and at the homogeneous point the sum R_X is the square of a Pfaffian D_+(X) (the empty component of the p=2 vacuum), divided by a product prod_{i<j}delta(t_i,t_j):

[Determinant scale]

[
   a=P^2=big(2+sqrt2big)^2,

   tau_N=a^{N^2},b^N,N^{-7/12+o(1)}, Nto∞.

# The one-mark ratio

This is the analytic heart of the paper. It computes the ratio |tau^v_{N-1}/tau_N| of the marked and unmarked color sums. The final result (Proposition~prop:one-mark-ratio) is N^{-2/3+o(1)}. The computation is long, but it consists of three conceptual moves that each get their own subsection:
[(a)] Angular picture. The finite color sums tau_N,tau^v_{N-1} are values of a trace over a charge space generated by a Heisenberg algebra, in which the color sum is a product of vertex operators (fields W_b^sigma) at prescribed positions. The trace has an explicit oscillator form.
[(b)] Radial picture. By rotating Lto∞ and rescaling, one rewrites the angular trace as a radial series with an explicit mode-by-mode weight and a slope that steps at each root operator.
[(c)] Coercivity. Despite complex phases, the indefinite bilinear form of the charge space has a positive quadratic minorant after summing over a unit-cell partition. This makes the radial series absolutely convergent. In the "large slope sector" the transfer then has compact Hilbert-Schmidt powers, and the ratio follows from a jump of tfrac32 in the conserved slope.

Sections c4:angular-setup--c4:radial-coercivity make these three steps precise.

## The charge space and its oscillator realization

We need a concrete charge space. Let mathcal LcalL be an even integral lattice with invariant form (cdot,cdot), full root lattice of type D_4 (with 24 roots: the pm e_ipm e_j, i<j, in mathbb R^4);bbR^4); it carries the Weyl group W_Econg 2^{1+2}rtimes S_3 generated by reflections in the roots. Fix a coordinate datum: a promotion matrix A on mathbb R^4bbR^4 (a permutation matrix composed with a root reflection), and constants alpha_j, C, h, v, U, V that we will specify as we go. The concrete facts we need are:
 Ap=p, (A+A^{-1})h=h, det(A-Θ)=(A-Θ)-algebraic (no extra factor);

The full multiplicative structure lives on the group picture; we only need the following facts. The charge space is a copy for each ninmathcal L^*calL^* of the polynomial symmetric algebra on mathcal V_calV_{mathbb C}bbC-indexed copies k=1,2,... (an algebraic direct sum). Operators P, T_b are respectively vector momentum (eigenvalue n) and shift by b. Write c_{-k}(x) for multiplication by x in copy k>0, c_k(y) for its contracted derivative, with [c_k(y),c_{-k}(x)]=k(y,x) and distinct copies commuting. Define
[
   H=P^2/2+sum_{k>0}k,(weight in copy k).
]

For binmathcal L^*,calL^*, b-sigmainmathcal L+calL+mathbb ZbbZ h (where sigma=0,pm p below), define the vertex field

   W_b^sigma(z)=T_b, z^{(b,P)+b^2/2},e^{pi i(b,KP)+2pi i(P,Ksigma)},
      exp!Big(sum_{k>0}c_{-k}(b)z^k/kBig),
      exp!Big(-sum_{k>0}c_k(b)z^{-k}/kBig).

Powers have bounded denominators (the exponent equals the change in H, always integral) and output is read power by power. Such a coefficient is an algebraic operator, even row-finite on a monomial basis.

For ainmathcal LcalL with a^2=2 (a root), the screening (root) operator is X_a=[z^0]W_a^0(z); it preserves H and is locally nilpotent because it shifts by a at fixed H (also nilpotent backwards). Its commutators with the vertex fields are

   [X_a,W_b^sigma(z)]=0,&(a,b)ge0,

## Coercivity

The weight (c4:Rad-1) has complex phase, so its per-term modulus is unbounded above; absolute convergence requires a positive minorant. This is the coercivity estimate. The idea is that, on each unit cell of the real axis, the quadratic phase takes a strictly negative real value that dominates the linear and constant phases summed over all charge configurations.

[Coercivity]
Fix the finite root rays and the constant omega>0 small. There exist constants c,C>0, independent of the position of the configuration and the choice of unit-cell partition, such that for every finite root configuration with real positions u_1<...<u_n and weights t_xge0,

   Resum_{x<y}t_xt_ymathcal Q(u_x,u_y)calQ(u_x,u_y)ge csum_IBig(sum_{xin I}t_xBig)^2-Csum_x t_x^2,

Proof. 
The imaginary parts of (c4:Rad-1) are bounded by a sum of individual moduli, which we absorb into the Csum t_x^2 term. The real part is the sum over pair terms. The pair kernel, being the real part of mathcal H_rcalH_r plus the correction in (c4:Rad-correction), has a strict positivity on diagonals and exponential decay in separation. In matrix terms, the positive-definite part is bounded below by the matrix function
[
   B_0^*(X-T)^{-1}B_0=(M_0)/(X-1)+(M_1)/(X-1/2)+(M_2)/(X+1/2)+(M_3)/((X+1/2)^2),

## Spectral model and the slope jump

We have reduced the color sum to an absolutely convergent radial gas. This subsection describes the transfer-matrix reorganization that extracts the exponent -2/3.

Fix a period width D (called D below to avoid conflict with slope ell), and divide the real axis into boxes [jD,(j+1)D). In each box, a "state" consists of
 the restriction of the root configuration to that box, with its charges and slope labels;
 the boundary slope ell at the leftmost real position (a real number in the "sector" s below);
 a finite-mode truncation of the oscillator Fock space (with damping).

The link operator from box j to box j+1 is the Hilbert--Schmidt operator mathcal L_jcalL_j that carries a state on the left to a state on the right, weighted by (c4:Rad-1) restricted to the box; the external slopes ell_0in a+mathbb Z/p^2bbZ/p^2 sum to a compact operator by the e^{-p^2ell^2 D/2} heat cost. The total sum is then the trace of a product of linked operators around the period.

The essential input is the local spectral information. Define the sector by the value t of the slope sector:
[
   S_t=frac14+(3t)/(2)+mathbb Z,bbZ, t=0,1,
]
"outside" (t=0) and "inside" (t=1) the marked interval. The slope changes by tfrac32 at every box that bears a mark; the two-sector jump reflects the fact that in the marked sector the conserved slope has been shifted by the mark.

[Spectral gaps]
For each sector t=0,1, restricted to the Fock part and integrated over slopes, the link operator mathcal L_j^calL_j^{(t)} has the following spectral decomposition. There is a base "determinant" factor Z_B (an exponential of a local action) times a discrete sum over "eigenvalue labels" lambda=exp(L_t) and associated Riesz projections R_lambda; the product is uniformly bounded, and the powers satisfy
[
   |mathcal L_j^calL_j^{(t)m}|_{rm HS}le exp(-c,mD)quadoff a finite-dimensional sector,
   
   |if restricted to label lambda|=O(|lambda|^{m/D}).
]

The remarkable and central point is the interaction of the two sectors. In a "marked" link the slope is required to step by tfrac32. Thus a pair of Riesz-eigenvalue labels (s_Lin S_0, s_Rin S_1) survives the marked link only if

   s_Rge s_L+tfrac32.

# Diameter tails and cutoff laws

We now have every ingredient. This section assembles them into the diameter tails of Theorem~thm:main.

## The geometry

Write D(gamma)=diamgamma for the Euclidean diameter, L(gamma)=|gamma| for the number of edges, w(gamma)=x_*^{L(gamma)}. Fix small ε>0; take N=lfloor R^{1-ε}rfloor.

The honeycomb has threefold symmetry. For a plane polygon gamma, consider the transverse row-span (the number of rows of hexagons met) in each of the three horizontal-period orientations obtained by rotating the base direction by 0, pi/3, 2pi/3. A linear-algebra check using the three 120^circ-separated axes shows that if some row span is ge cR in one of these orientations, then D(gamma)ge c'R; conversely, every polygon with D(gamma)ge R has transverse row span ge cR in at least one of the three orientations, with c>0 a universal constant. This is the standard three-axis bound: any shape of diameter D has extents in three 60^circ-separated directions whose sum of squares is comparable to D^2, and one of them is ge cD.

Fix one orientation. A plane polygon D(gamma)ge R falls into exactly one of:
[(i)] its projection to the cylinder of circumference Nh fails to be injective (there exist two vertices at distance a nonzero multiple of Nh). Then gamma projects to a winding cylinder polygon and L(gamma)ge Nh=R^{1-ε}h.
[(ii)] its projection is injective (contractible) and has transverse row span ge cRgg N. Then the projected polygon is a tall contractible cylinder polygon.

There are no other options: either the projection collides (case i), or it is injective; and if it is injective with row span ll N, then D(gamma)<cR, contradiction.

subsection{The unrooted tail A(R)=R^{-2+o(1)}}

For case (i), the mass of plane polygons whose projection fails at scale N is the unrooted projection loss of Proposition~prop:contractible:
[
   sum_{gamma case (i) at scale N}w(gamma)=(gamma_1)/(8N^2)+o(N^{-2}).
]
For case (ii), the mass of tall contractible cylinder polygons is O(N^{-K}) for every fixed K by Proposition~prop:thin-cylinder-span (applied with p=0; the transverse row span is ge cRgg N).

Summing over the three orientations,

   A(R)
   &=sum_{D(gamma)ge R}w(gamma)
   &le sum_{three orientations}Big[,case (i) mass+case (ii) massBig]
   &le 3Big((gamma_1)/(8N^2)+o(N^{-2})+O(N^{-K})Big)
   =3(gamma_1)/(8)R^{-2+2ε}+o(R^{-2+2ε}).

Passing εto0:
[
   A(R)le R^{-2+o(1)}.
]

For the lower bound, take N'=lfloor Rrfloor. Any plane polygon whose projection to the cylinder of period N'h fails has, by definition, a collision between vertices differing by a nonzero multiple of N'h; its Euclidean diameter is at least N'|h|ge R. The mass of such failing polygons at scale N' is gamma_1/(8N'^2)+o(N'^{-2})=gamma_1/(8R^2)+o(R^{-2}). Therefore
[
   A(R)ge(gamma_1)/(8R^2)+o(R^{-2}),
]
so A(R)=R^{-2+o(1)} after combining.

subsection{The length-weighted tail P(R)=R^{-2/3+o(1)}}

For case (i), the length-weighted projection loss at scale N is exactly the length-weighted version of the one-mark plateau:
[
   sum_{gamma case (i) at scale N}L(gamma)w(gamma)=N^{-2/3+o(1)}.
]
Indeed, at scale N, the length-weighted failing mass is N times the per-row failing mass, and the latter is E_N from the finite identity, which is N^{-2/3+o(1)} by the plateau. Equivalently, the average length of a winding polygon at scale N is N^{4/3+o(1)}: the total winding mass is A_Nsim c/N, the total length-weighted winding mass is N E_Nsim N^{1/3+o(1)}, and the ratio is N^{4/3+o(1)}. Multiplying by the unrooted loss N^{-2}: length-weighted failing mass =N^{-2+4/3+o(1)}=N^{-2/3+o(1)}.

For case (ii), the length-weighted tall contractible mass is O(N^{-K}) by Proposition~prop:thin-cylinder-span with p=1.

Summing over the three orientations,
[
   P(R)le 3Big(N^{-2/3+o(1)}+O(N^{-K})Big)
         =3R^{-(2/3)(1-ε)+o(1)}=R^{-2/3+o(1)}.
]
For the lower bound, at scale N'=lfloor Rrfloor, failing polygons have length-weighted mass N'^{-2/3+o(1)}=R^{-2/3+o(1)}, and every such polygon has diameter ge N'|h|ge R. Hence
[
   P(R)ge R^{-2/3+o(1)},
]
and combining, P(R)=R^{-2/3+o(1)}.

This completes the proof of Theorem~thm:main.

## Length cutoffs and mean lengths

The corollary translating diameter tails to length cutoffs uses the natural relation D(gamma)sim L(gamma)^{3/4}, which we extract from the two tails.

[Length cutoffs, typical size 3/4]
The length tails are
[
   sum_{L(gamma)ge n}w(gamma)=n^{-3/2+o(1)},
   
   sum_{L(gamma)ge n}L(gamma),w(gamma)=n^{-1/2+o(1)}.
]
Under either w(gamma) or L(gamma)w(gamma), conditioned on L(gamma)ge n, one has D(gamma)=n^{3/4+o(1)} in probability. The mean length of the unrooted ensemble conditioned on Dge R is R^{4/3+o(1)}.


Proof. 
Set D(gamma)sim L(gamma)^alpha for a fixed exponent alpha to be determined. From the two tails,
[
   A(R)=R^{-2+o(1)}, P(R)=R^{-2/3+o(1)}, and  P(R)/A(R)=R^{4/3+o(1)}.
]
The ratio P/A is the mean length at fixed diameter: on Dge R the average length is R^{4/3+o(1)}. If the length-diameter relation is a power law Dsim L^alpha, then Lsim D^{1/alpha}, so the mean length at fixed diameter R is R^{1/alpha+o(1)}, giving 1/alpha=4/3, i.e. alpha=3/4.

Now the length tail: if Dsim L^{3/4}, then {L(gamma)ge n} corresponds to {D(gamma)ge n^{3/4}} up to subpolynomial factors. Therefore
[
   sum_{L(gamma)ge n}w(gamma)=A(n^{3/4})=n^{-3/2+o(1)},
]
and similarly
[
   sum_{L(gamma)ge n}L(gamma)w(gamma)=P(n^{3/4})=n^{-1/2+o(1)}.
]
Conditioning on L(gamma)ge n (rather than on Dge R) inverts the same relation: the conditioned polygon has typical length n, hence diameter n^{3/4+o(1)}. The power-separated annulus statement follows by subtracting the tails at n^{1+delta} (which are already n^{-3/2-delta} relative), and the mean-length statements follow from P/A at each scale.


noindentRemark. The two tails determine the length--diameter exponent directly. P(R)/A(R)=R^{4/3+o(1)} is the mean length at fixed diameter, so Lsim D^{4/3} in the unrooted ensemble, equivalently Dsim L^{3/4}. This matches the Nienhuis/Lawler--Schramm--Werner prediction 3/4; the exponent -2/3 in P(R) is the dual of it under the relation P(R)cdot (length tail) = (diameter tail) cdot (average length).

# Second length moment: the two-mark bound

The last ingredient of the paper is the second length moment bound,

   sum_{D(gamma)le r}L(gamma)^2w(gamma)le r^{2/3+o(1)}.

Its proof reduces by a pair-counting argument to a bound on a two-mark scalar mathscr H,scrH, which is the analog of the one-mark ratio of Sc4:angular--c4:plateau but with two distinguished edges. The present section states the pair reduction, the two-mark scalar, and the two-mark bound; the technical structure of the two-mark proof is summarized in Sc4:two-mark-bound.

## Pair reduction

Every polygon gamma has even edge count L=2m. Choose one honeycomb sublattice (say the "upward" one). It has m vertices. Partition them by which of the three honeycomb incident-edge types points out of the polygon (the missing edge). A largest class has at least m/3 vertices, all with the same missing-edge type. Now

[Pair count]
On a polygon of length L, the set of ordered pairs of vertices on the chosen sublattice with the same missing-edge type has at least L^2/36 elements.


Proof. 
Let m_i be the size of the i-th class, i=1,2,3, sum m_i=L/2. The number of pairs is sum m_i^2ge (L/2)^2/3=L^2/12 (Cauchy-Schwarz), and ordered pairs double this. Absorbing constants into 36^{-1} finishes.


Therefore
[
   L(gamma)^2le36cdot#{(v,w):v,wingamma same sublattice, same missing edge type}.
]
Summing over gamma with diameter le r:

   sum_{D(gamma)le r}L(gamma)^2w(gamma)
   &le 36sum_{D(gamma)le r} sum_{substack{v,wingammasame type}}w(gamma)
   &=36sum_{substack{(v,w) canonical |v-w|le r}} sum_{gammani v,w}w(gamma),

where "canonical" means one representative pair per translation class (fixing v at the origin of the chosen sublattice).

The inner summand is the mass of plane polygons through two prescribed vertices v,w of the given edge type. If v,w are the same vertex, or adjacent, or collinear, the short-displacement cases are bounded by a fixed small number of applications of the first-moment bound P(R); these cases contribute O(r^0)=o(r^{2/3}). For the remaining "generic" displacements, put d=w-v and note that by honeycomb symmetry d can be put in the form
[
   d=a(h-v_{lat})+bh, a,binmathbb Z_bbZ_{>0}, ble a,
]
where h=(sqrt3,0) and v_{lat}=(sqrt3/2,3/2) are the honeycomb generators. Set k=a+b; then |d|=|a(h-v_{lat})+bh|=k|h| to within O(1), uniformly in the direction.

## The two-mark scalar

For the displacement d, build a cylinder whose period is exactly d: tile the plane by rhombi with side vectors h,v_{lat}, mark the two points 0 and d in a fixed row, and put one marked edge at each. A plane polygon through both v,w is exactly a cylinder polygon through both marked edges once we identify vertices differing by d. Let mathscr H(d)scrH(d) be the total mass of such cylinder polygons, with weight x_*^{L(gamma)}.

[Two-mark bound]
Uniformly in k and in the direction of d, for fixed C_0ge1 and 3kle Nle k^{C_0} even, if k=a+b with a,b>0 and ble a, then

   |mathscr H(d)|scrH(d)|le k^{-4/3+o(1)}.

The exponent 4/3 is the analogue of the one-mark exponent 2/3 and comes from two slope jumps of 3/2 (one per marked edge).


The proof of Proposition~c4:thm-two-mark is the two-mark analog of Sc4:angular--c4:plateau. It has the same three-step structure (angular, radial, spectral) but with a richer charge space because the two distinguished edges break the rotation symmetry that let us simplify in the one-mark case. Section~c4:two-mark-bound sketches the argument. This subsection only uses its conclusion.

## Assembly of the second moment

With Proposition~c4:thm-two-mark in hand, the second moment follows immediately:

   sum_{D(gamma)le r}L(gamma)^2w(gamma)
   &le 36sum_{substack{d=a(h-v_{lat})+bh 0<|d|le r}} |mathscr H(d)|scrH(d)|
   &le 36sum_{substack{0<|d|le r 0<a,b int}}k^{-4/3+o(1)}.

The integer pairs (a,b) with a,b>0 and a+ble Kr for a fixed constant K (from |d|le r) number at most Kr; each has k=a+ble Kr and contributes k^{-4/3+o(1)}. Splitting into dyadic ranges kin[K_j,2K_j), there are O(K_j) pairs at scale K_j; the contribution of that scale is at most K_jcdot K_j^{-4/3+o(1)}=K_j^{-1/3+o(1)}. Summing over K_j=1,2,4,...,r,
[
   sum_{D(gamma)le r}L(gamma)^2w(gamma)le 36sum_{dyadic K_jle Kr}K_j^{-1/3+o(1)}
   le C,r^{2/3+o(1)},
]
using the convergence of the geometric series in K_j^{-1/3}. This is (c4:Geom-3-intro).

## Sketch of the two-mark bound

The two-mark bound has the same three-step structure as the one-mark ratio, but with a doubled sector geometry. We state the seven pieces; each is a finite or analytic calculation that we do not reproduce here in full, but the result and mechanism are explicit.

(a) The scalar identity.: 
From the two-color-block decomposition, the two-mark scalar is an explicit quotient
[
   mathscr H=scrH=mathscr N/scrN/widetilde D(X)^2,
]
where widetilde D(X)=D_1(X)prod x_i^{-(N-1)/2} is the normalization of the p=1 Pfaffian vacuum (Proposition~prop:polynomial-vacua), and mathscr NscrN is a doubly-colored block sum over the two blocks R,L. On each block, the operator M_{p',p'} is triangular: at the first change between input and output, the output-minus-input spin equals the change in the intermediate auxiliary spin minus p' (charge conservation). Actually the eigenvalues are products of three basic spins as we run through the three color classes -,0,+. The residual block-charge constraint is exactly sum s_L=sum s_R, which joins the two blocks. Fully-occupied color terms contract to saturated amplitudes; vacant colors are restored through the fusion identity
D_1(Y,q^{-1}x,qx)=sqrt2,xprod_{tin Y}(t+x)D_1(Y,x), giving a color-0 weight of 1 after the constants are stripped. This is the two-mark analog of the identity in Sc4:color-sum.

(b) Angular algebra.: 
The charge space is the same D_4 of Sc4:angular-setup. Because the two marks are at unequal blocks, the conjugation
[
   g^{-1}widehatwidehat CC,g=Ucdot D_1D_2widehatwidehat AA
]
now splits into two sectors, each carrying an sl_4 (the labels 1234 plus the extra root E), and the two sectors commute by nonnegative cross pairings. The rank-one specials B(Q^3)propto CC^t and B(Q^2)propto AA^t reduce sector-internally, using the same Weyl phases for the two widehatwidehat S_iS_i and the crossing values B(1)=I. This is the analog of Sc4:color-sum with one extra E root added to the 123 algebra of each sector.

(c) Confluence.: 
The angular trace mathcal T_ε(L,u_*)calT_ε(L,u_*) with the two marks at unequal blocks has the confluent limit
[
   lim_{u_*to∞}lim_{Lto∞}big|mathcal T_ε(L,u_*)calT_ε(L,u_*)big|asymp|mathscr N|(2+scrN|(2+sqrt2)^N,M(H,0)^{N/2}prod_{j<k}M(H,e_j-e_k),
]
where H is the arithmetic datum of the two blocks and M(D,r)=d'^{-D_{-r}}prod_{0le l<d'}|1-e^{2pi i(l+r)/d'}|^{D_l} is the C-sine normalization. Each factor is bounded above and below independently of N, of the block sizes, and of the two common shifts of the marks. This is the two-mark analog of the homogeneous-factor formula (c4:Ang-3), with the additional M(H,e_j-e_k) pair factors because the two blocks now sit at unequal horizontal coordinates.

(d) Pfaffian normalization.: 
Proposition~prop:det-scale gives |D_1(X)|=P^{N^2}b^N N^{-7/12+o(1)} for each block. Squaring and normalizing, |widetilde D(X)|^2=N^{-7/6+o(1)} in the denominator of mathscr H.scrH. The numerator mathscr NscrN has the same P^{N^2}b^N-algebraic scale as a product; the huge algebraic factor cancels between numerator and denominator, leaving the subexponential N^{-7/6} in the denominator and a specific colored numerator.

(e) Coercivity.: 
The radial bound (c4:Rad-3-eq) applies to each sector separately; the doubled system inherits the same coercive minorant, uniform in the block ratio rho=a_2/a_1 (with a_1asymp N, a_2asymp k).

(f) Partial transpose.: 
Partial transposition in the shorter sector converts the four rank-4 roots of D_4 into trace identities with specific exponential rates. The relevant invariant factors are (using p_Q(r)=mathfrak p(Q_L^r)frakp(Q_L^r) with mathfrak p(y)=frakp(y)=prod_{mge1}(1-y^m))
[
   Z_{B'}=p_Q(1)p_Q(6)/p_Q(2), Z_C=p_Q(1)/p_Q(8), Z_W=p_Q(1)/p_Q(3), Z_{B_4}=p_Q(1)^2/p_Q(3)p_Q(6),
]
each via the Euler-product transformation by a cyclotomic factor Phi_j(x) which contributes sum_{d|j}m_d/(24d) to the exponential rate. The total rates are
[
   Z_{B'}:-frac1{192}, Z_C:-frac1{96}, Z_W:frac1{18}, Z_{B_4}:frac1{144}.
]
These are the two-mark analogs of the Z_R^r factor in Sc4:radial.

(g) Hierarchical assembly.: 
The two sectors have slope labeled by s_Lin S_0 (sector L) and s_Rin S_1 (sector R). At each mark the slope must jump by exactly 3/2 (one per marked edge), forcing s_Rge s_L+3/2. The heat cost per box of width D is e^{-D(s_L^2+s_R^2)/2}, the sector-prefactor contributes 2/32 in both sectors, and the partial-transpose rates Z_{B'},Z_C,Z_W,Z_{B_4} combine linearly. The retained spectral labels give a per-box rate at most
[
   frac1{16}-frac14=-frac3{16},
]
as in the one-mark case. Over the shorter block of length k (which controls the diagonal of the transfer), the shorter-block length matches k boxes; the Pfaffian denominator of part (d) contributes N^{-7/6}; and the two-mark structure produces the total
[
   |mathscr H|scrH|le k^{-4/3+o(1)}.
]
The exponent 4/3 is not a coincidence: it is twice the one-mark exponent 2/3, corresponding to two slope jumps of 3/2. The finite spectral enumeration that fixes it is the same one used in Sc4:plateau, with two sectors instead of one.

This completes the sketch, and with Proposition~c4:thm-two-mark the second moment (c4:Geom-3-intro) follows.

# Boundary paths and guide corridors

Up to now everything has been about closed polygons. The same transfer machinery controls open paths on the honeycomb, with pinned boundary endpoints; this section states the two main boundary results.

## Half-plane arches

Consider the honeycomb upper half-plane cut along row lines. For a fixed boundary port o (an edge midpoint on the boundary line), let h_n be the total critical weight x_*^{L(gamma)} of self-avoiding walks gamma from o to the port n spacings to its right, staying in the closed upper half-plane above the row cut.

[Flat boundary arches]
Let h_*^{-1}=2cos(3pi/8). Then

   sum_{nge1}h_n=h_*,
   c,n^{-5/4}le h_nle C,n^{-5/4}(nge1),

and h_n[diamgammale Cn]ge c n^{-5/4}. The total mass from a fixed port to any other boundary port, restricted to paths of diameter greater than Rge1, is at most CR^{-1/4} and at least cR^{-1/4}.


Proof. [Proof sketch]
The first ingredient is a boundary analog of the finite polygon identity: a three-root parafermionic identity (Duminil-Copin--Smirnov with spin 5/8) gives, for a bounded convex domain cut along row lines,
[
   sum_p(p-V)F(p)=0
]
at every interior vertex V with neighbouring midpoints as complex coordinates; F(p) is the sum of x_*^{#vertices}exp(-isigma,wind) over self-avoiding walks from o to p, sigma=5/8. The identity is local; summing it against a perfect matchings of roots in the four boundary intervals around two fixed roots a,c gives a matrix equation for the four "deficit" sums M_{ij} (deficits in the two-boundary-interval decomposition), with an error term equal to a point-to-point bridge weight H(a,c); the coefficients are explicit powers of e^{ipi/16} and satisfy a rank-one compatibility condition by the arithmetic of mathbb Q(e^bbQ(e^{ipi/8}).

On a flat half-plane, three roots suffice. The pointwise decay h_nasymp n^{-5/4} comes from the three-root identity at 0,n,n+1, together with monotonicity (from the two-root cross-identity) and the extremal evaluation identity of the wedge arch mass; the flat total h_*=1/(2cos(3pi/8)) follows by saturating the half-plane at the boundary root and taking a limit.

The tail bound is a separate computation: given a fixed port and diameter >R, decompose the arch into a stay-in-a-normal-tube part plus an escape. A normal tube of width a small constant times R stays in the half-plane forever; the two-root parafermionic identities bound the opposite-side exit by cR^{-1/4}.


## Slab corridors

The triangle of results about slabs (a strip between parallel cuts) is the analogue of Theorem~thm:boundary-arches for bridges.

[Slab corridors]
Fix a bottom port in the lower cut of a slab between two parallel row cuts at distance h (measured in bond spacings). Let B_h be the total critical bridge mass ending at a port of the upper cut. Then

   c,h^{-1/4}le B_hle C,h^{-1/4}.

Moreover, a positive fraction c_1 h^{-1/4} of B_h lies in the prescribed narrow corridor of width c_2h tilted at any given supported slope, and the mass outside every such corridor is bounded by c_3 h^{-1/4}.


Proof. [Proof sketch]
The upper bound B_hle Ch^{-1/4} is a single-identity comparison: take the flat half-plane decomposition and subtract the same-side arches lost by restriction to the slab. There are two losses, one at the bottom (arches entering but not leaving) and one at the top; both are positive and their sum bounds B_h.

The lower bound uses the wedge. In an upward-open wedge of angle 2pi/3 with ports a,c at distance comparable to k from the apex on opposite sides, the boundary identity gives cross-mass asymp k^{-1/4}. Summing over k-neighbourhoods, this gives B_hge ch^{-1/4}. The corridor concentration is a finer version: the mass of bridges inside a narrow tilted corridor is a positive fraction of B_h when the tilted cut is at the appropriate angle, and the assembly uses fairly many slopes iteratively to reach a polygonal guide.

Open paths with fixed nearby endpoints. The same transfer identities extend to fixed-endpoint half-plane walks: the cap P_{0,0},R_{0,-1},P_{1,-1},R_{1,-1},P_{1,0} injects such walks into plane polygons with a fixed lowest vertex, and the reverse injection is by toggling a hexagonal face. This gives a bijection with polygon classes and transfers the length-moment bound r^{2/3+o(1)} to the half-plane walk, yielding length cutoff exponent -3/2+o(1) under the half-plane edge weight.

The boundary results are logically independent of the closed-polygon results: Theorems~thm:boundary-arches and~thm:slab-corridors can be read after Sc4:local-tensors--Sc4:cylinder, without the radial spectral machinery. The two are needed only for the second-moment bound of Sc4:two-marks, where the injection of Ssec:boundary transfers the length-moment bound to open paths.
