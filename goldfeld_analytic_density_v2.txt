Let E be an elliptic curve over Q. Write L(E,s) for the complete
Hasse-Weil L-function, and set
$$
a(E)=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s),\qquad
r(E)=\operatorname{rank}_{\Z}E(\Q),\qquad
c_2(E)=\operatorname{corank}_{\Ztwo}\Sel_{2^\infty}(E/\Q).
$$
The 2-power Selmer group uses the local Kummer conditions at every
place. Its corank includes the Mordell-Weil rank and any divisible
2-primary part of the Tate-Shafarevich group $\Sha(E/\Q)$. Modularity
of elliptic curves over Q supplies the analytic continuation and
functional equation of $L(E,s)$.

Two theorems are proved. The first is a low-corank converse at a
single elliptic curve.

Theorem 1 (the 2-converse). For every elliptic curve $E/\Q$ with
$c_2(E)\in\{0,1\}$,
$$
a(E)=r(E)=c_2(E),\qquad\Sha(E/\Q)\text{ is finite}.
$$
No reduction type, complex multiplication, rational torsion, or
rational isogeny is excluded. The hypothesis is on the full 2-power
Selmer corank; a Mordell-Weil rank bound alone does not give it.

The second theorem is Goldfeld's analytic density conjecture. For a
nonzero squarefree integer $d$, let $E^{(d)}$ denote the quadratic
twist by $d$, and put
$$
\mathcal D(X)=\{d\in\Z:0<|d|\le X,\ d\text{ squarefree}\}.
$$
Positive and negative parameters are counted together and ordered by
absolute value. A single residue class can fix the sign of the
functional equation; the half-density statement below concerns the
full set $\mathcal D(X)$.

Theorem 2 (Goldfeld density). For every elliptic curve $E/\Q$ and
each $j\in\{0,1\}$,
$$
\lim_{X\to\infty}
\frac{\#\{d\in\mathcal D(X):a(E^{(d)})=j\}}{\#\mathcal D(X)}
=\frac12.
$$
Consequently the twists of analytic rank at least two have density
zero. For a density-one set of signed squarefree parameters $d$, the
analytic and Mordell-Weil ranks of $E^{(d)}$ agree and the whole
Tate-Shafarevich group $\Sha(E^{(d)}/\Q)$ is finite.

Theorem 2 combines Theorem 1 with Smith's distribution of full
2-power Selmer coranks over signed squarefree parameters. The pointwise
Theorem 1 is the main work.

A finite-descent corollary records what an honest finite 2-descent
already certifies. Put
$$
d_2(E)=\dim_{\F_2}\Sel_2(E/\Q)-\dim_{\F_2}E(\Q)[2].
$$

Corollary 3 (finite Selmer criterion). If $d_2(E)\in\{0,1\}$ then
$$
a(E)=r(E)=c_2(E)=d_2(E),\qquad
\Sha(E/\Q)\text{ is finite},\qquad
\Sha(E/\Q)[2^\infty]=0.
$$

Proof. The Kummer sequence at $n=1$ gives
$d_2(E)=r(E)+\dim_{\F_2}\Sha(E/\Q)[2]$. Meanwhile
$c_2(E)=r(E)+\operatorname{corank}_{\Ztwo}\Sha(E/\Q)[2^\infty]$,
the second summand being the 2-rank of the maximal divisible quotient
of $\Sha(E/\Q)[2^\infty]$. The Cassels-Tate pairing on that quotient
is alternating, so its 2-rank is even. If $d_2(E)\in\{0,1\}$ this
corank must vanish, hence $c_2(E)=d_2(E)\in\{0,1\}$ and Theorem 1
applies. The vanishing of $\Sha(E/\Q)[2^\infty]$ then follows from
$\Sha(E/\Q)$ finite together with $c_2(E)=r(E)$.

The arithmetic object in Theorem 1 first appears in the Birch and
Swinnerton-Dyer comparison of rational points with central L-values,
which led to the conjectural equality of algebraic and analytic rank
together with finiteness of $\Sha(E/\Q)$ and a formula for the leading
coefficient (Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer 1965; Tate 1966). Goldfeld's
1979 formulation asks for the mean analytic order in the family of
quadratic twists, parametrized by the quadratic-field discriminants
(Goldfeld 1979). The density form of that conjecture predicts that
analytic ranks zero and one each occur with frequency one-half among
signed squarefree twist parameters ordered by absolute value.

The analytic study of quadratic twists produced both nonzero central
values and simple central zeros. Bump, Friedberg, and Hoffstein,
together with Murty and Murty, supplied auxiliary twists with the
nonvanishing needed in Heegner-point arguments; Iwaniec and
Perelli-Pomykala developed quantitative estimates for central
derivatives (Bump, Friedberg, and Hoffstein 1990; Murty and Murty
1991; Iwaniec 1990; Perelli and Pomykala 1997). Ono and Skinner
obtained a lower bound of order $X/\log X$ for the number of
nonvanishing central values among twists by fundamental discriminants
$D$ with $0<|D|\le X$ (Ono and Skinner 1998). Kumar, Mallesham,
Sharma, and Singh sharpened derivative nonvanishing through second
moments, and Kriz and Nordentoft improved central-value lower bounds
for curves with no rational two-torsion (Kumar, Mallesham, Sharma,
and Singh 2024; Kriz and Nordentoft 2025). These quantitative results
yield many twists without giving, on their own, a positive proportion
for every fixed curve.

Positive proportions were obtained first in special families. James
proved analytic-rank-zero proportions for particular curves, and
Vatsal proved positive proportions of analytic ranks zero and one
for $X_0(19)$ (James 1998; Vatsal 1998). Kriz and Li proved both
positive proportions for every rational elliptic curve with a
rational $3$-isogeny (Kriz and Li 2019). In the congruent-number
family, Tian's Heegner-point work gives rank one and odd-order
Tate-Shafarevich group in specified subfamilies (Tian 2014).
Genus-period and genus-point congruences, followed by Selmer
statistics, give further analytic density results (Tian, Yuan, and
Zhang 2017). These arguments connect coefficient congruences and
arithmetic statistics with analytic nonvanishing, a connection that
also underlies the proof of Theorem 1.

The statistical input has its own progression. Smith first
established higher 2-power Selmer distributions for curves with full
rational two-torsion and no rational cyclic subgroup of order four
(Smith 2017). His later work treated the irreducible residual case
and further rational two-isogeny cases (Smith 2022, two papers).
The unrestricted theorem gives full Selmer coranks zero and one with
density one-half each, for every rational elliptic curve over signed
squarefree parameters (Smith 2025). Its analytic corollary assumes
Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer. Theorem 1 supplies the pointwise analytic
implication using only the Selmer theorem.

Gross and Zagier, followed by Kolyvagin, established the forward
implication: analytic rank at most one gives equality with the
Mordell-Weil rank and finiteness of the whole Tate-Shafarevich group
(Gross and Zagier 1986; Kolyvagin 1990). A converse must produce
analytic nonvanishing from Selmer data. Skinner and Urban proved
rank-zero converses at good ordinary odd primes under residual
irreducibility and ramification hypotheses (Skinner and Urban 2014).
Skinner and Wei Zhang proved rank-one converse criteria through
Heegner points and derived classes, under their respective
reduction, residual-image, and local hypotheses (Skinner 2020;
Zhang 2014). Burungale, Castella, and Skinner removed the auxiliary
ramification conditions in the good ordinary, residually irreducible
case for $p>3$ (Burungale, Castella, and Skinner 2025). At good
supersingular primes $p>3$, Castella and Wan obtained the rank-one
Selmer-corank implication for semistable curves (Castella and Wan
2024).

Reducible residual representations call for a different
Iwasawa-theoretic comparison. Castella, Grossi, Lee, and Skinner
proved the corank-zero and corank-one converses at good ordinary
Eisenstein primes $p>2$, with local isogeny character neither
trivial nor cyclotomic (Castella, Grossi, Lee, and Skinner 2022).
Keller and Yin treated potentially good ordinary reduction at odd
Eisenstein primes without that character exclusion (Keller and Yin
2024). These results enlarge the allowed residual representations
while retaining conditions on the prime and its reduction type.

The CM theory already includes substantial results at the prime
two. Burungale and Tian proved the rank-one converse at good
ordinary primes $p>3$ (Burungale and Tian 2020); Burungale,
Castella, Skinner, and Tian included the small ordinary primes
under an additional conductor hypothesis (Burungale, Castella,
Skinner, and Tian 2022). Ressler and Yu obtained the small-prime
good-ordinary result without that conductor condition (Yu 2021).
Burungale and Skinner's combined ordinary formulation includes
$p=2$, and their good supersingular CM theorem also permits $p=2$
under a surjectivity condition on Selmer localization at $p$
(Burungale and Skinner 2024). In corank zero, Burungale and Tian's
follow-up theorem covers every rational CM elliptic curve at every
prime, without a reduction restriction (Burungale and Tian 2026).
Kriz's preprint states a converse for curves with CM by the full
ring of integers at the unique ramified prime of the CM field, and
derives analytic densities for restricted CM families including
congruent-number curves (Kriz 2022). Theorem 1 treats the full
2-power Selmer corank for every rational elliptic curve, in ranks
zero and one simultaneously.

The proof strategy is the same for both ranks. Fix the curve $E$ under
study. Build finite binary families of twists
$$
h_x=\prod_{q\in\mathcal Q}(q^*)^{\lambda_q(x)},\qquad
q^*=(-1/q)q,\qquad x\in\F_2^b,
$$
where $\mathcal Q$ is a finite set of distinct good odd primes and the
$\lambda_q$ are linear forms on $\F_2^b$. The parameter at $x=0$ is
the untwisted curve. The families have the same local twisting classes
at every fixed bad place. Suppose that at every nonzero vertex $x$,
the corank of the relevant twist matches the rank of the fixed $E$.
Then a scalar determinant coordinate attached to the relevant
Beilinson-Kato or Heegner class in the Selmer complex has a nonzero
central specialization at every nonzero vertex, with a uniformly
bounded 2-adic valuation. Bounded integral interpolation recovers
nonvanishing at the missing vertex $x=0$.

At corank zero, the interpolated class is a Beilinson-Kato class, and
its central specialization detects $L(E,1)$ (Kato 2004). At corank
one, the class is a Heegner class over an imaginary quadratic field
$K=\Q(\sqrt{k})$, whose height detects
$$
\left[L(E,s)L(E^{(k)},s)\right]'_{s=1}.
$$
An analytically nonvanishing companion $E^{(k)}$ makes the Selmer
corank over $K$ equal to one. The construction retains integral
Kummer conditions at $2$ and at every bad prime. The rational Iwasawa
input is used in its stated scope; the uniform integral estimates at
$2$ are the content of the cyclotomic and Heegner chapters.

The finite families are built from modular coefficients. The
Waldspurger formula supplies the even-sign coefficients
(Waldspurger 1981). In odd sign, reductions of genus Heegner sums
give weighted ternary theta coefficients at increasing finite
precisions. A uniform lower bound allows a least normalized valuation
to be selected. In the even case this is a minimum among the
coefficients of one form. In the odd case it is a minimum over
bounded-weight sequences as the precision increases; each finite
collection of tests is then realized at a sufficiently high actual
precision. A test attaining the minimum detects the required analytic
order and gives a uniform upper bound. Weight-two Hecke traces turn
these coefficient tests into contraction and deletion rules for
finite configurations of primes.

Two exhaustive residual cases govern the arithmetic assembly. When
$E[2]$ is reducible over $\F_2$, its invariant line is a rational
point of order two, and graphs encoding prescribed quadratic residue
symbols between auxiliary primes make two separate coefficient
tests nonzero together at every nonzero binary vertex. When $E[2]$
is irreducible, local Selmer equations give matrices over $\F_2$
whose nullities bound the Selmer dimension from below. Subtracting a
correction determined by finitely many local types makes these
matrices symmetric without enlarging the kernel by more than a fixed
cost. Removing one selected prime or pair of primes, or retaining it
with either of two allowed local choices, gives three configurations
whose coefficient tests satisfy the same sum-zero relation as the
corresponding matrix determinants. This relation does not identify
the coefficient test with the determinant; it makes the two
functions obey one linear three-term recurrence across the three
configurations. Grouping configurations into blocks and arranging
zero matrix entries between distinct blocks, a nonzero coefficient
test bounds the matrix kernel and hence the number of singular blocks
in the finite configuration. Fixing a configuration with maximal
singular-block count lets the same construction realize nonzero tests
at every nonzero binary vertex. An additional alternating-matrix
construction supplies a companion with uniformly bounded prime count
and Selmer length.

Now the local conventions. All 2-adic valuations are normalized by
$v(2)=1$, with $v(0)=+\infty$. For a finite $\Ztwo$-module $M$, its
length is $\log_2\#M$. A fixed support $S$ is a finite set of primes
containing $2$ and every prime of bad reduction; it is enlarged
explicitly whenever extra primes are needed. Write $N$ for the
conductor of $E$. A local filter specifies a real sign and unit
squareclasses at the primes of $S$ for a variable odd fundamental
discriminant prime to $S$. The empty discriminant $h=1$ denotes the
untwisted curve. For an odd prime $q$, the signed prime discriminant
is $q^*=(-1/q)q$. Products of distinct $q^*$ are odd fundamental
discriminants; the empty product is $1$.

For an odd fundamental discriminant $h$ prime to $2N$, the
root-number formula for coprime quadratic twists is
$$
\varepsilon(E^{(h)})=\varepsilon(E)\chi_h(-N)
\qquad\text{(Radziwill and Soundararajan 2015).}
$$
Hence matching unit squareclasses at the primes dividing $2N$ and
opposite real signs give opposite functional signs: the values of
$\chi_h(N)$ agree, while $\chi_h(-1)=\operatorname{sgn}(h)$. This
observation is used repeatedly when selecting auxiliary partners.

Write $T=T_2(E)$ for the 2-adic Tate module and $V=E[2]$ for the
residual representation. Frobenius is arithmetic and
$a_p(E)=p+1-\#E(\F_p)$ at a good prime $p$. For $h$ prime to $S$,
put
$$
t_p=\dim_{\F_2}V^{\Fr_p},\qquad
w(h)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\mid |h|}t_p.
$$
The residual identity, an element of order two, and a rotation of
order three have $t_p=2,1,0$ respectively. These types are called
split, simple, and root; their weights are $1$, $1/2$, $0$. Quadratic
residue symbols are written additively in $\F_2$ and take values
$0,1$, corresponding to $+1,-1$.

Whenever the Tate-Shafarevich group of the relevant twist is finite,
set
$$
s(h)=\operatorname{length}_{\Ztwo}\Sha(E^{(h)}/\Q)[2^\infty].
$$
This covers every twist already proved to have analytic rank zero
or one, and every base of Selmer corank zero. Whole-Sha finiteness
is not asserted at such a base until analytic nonvanishing has been
established.

Let $\Omega_E^+$ be the positive real period and let $\Omega_E^-$ be
the positive magnitude of a nonzero anti-invariant period. Put
$$
\LL(h)=
\frac{\sqrt{|h|}L(E^{(h)},1)}{\Omega_E^{\operatorname{sgn}(h)}}.
$$
Valuations refer to this algebraic period-normalized central value
at a chosen place above $2$. Changes of the fixed period, modular
parametrization, or isogeny affect valuations by bounded constants.
Every parametrization sends the cusp at infinity to the origin. The
algebraic value $\LL(h)$ agrees with the central value divided by a
Neron real period of $E^{(h)}$ up to a bounded $2$-adic factor; the
differential changes by the usual twisting factor, and only finitely
many twisting classes occur at $2$.

The first lemma isolates the 2-power Selmer corank.

Lemma 4 (ranks and the forward implication). For an elliptic curve
$A/\Q$ there is an exact sequence
$$
0\longrightarrow A(\Q)\otimes\Qtwo/\Ztwo
\longrightarrow\Sel_{2^\infty}(A/\Q)
\longrightarrow\Sha(A/\Q)[2^\infty]\longrightarrow0.
$$
In particular
$$
c_2(A)=r(A)+\corank_{\Ztwo}\Sha(A/\Q)[2^\infty].
$$
If $a(A)\le 1$, then $r(A)=a(A)$, the whole group $\Sha(A/\Q)$ is
finite, and $c_2(A)=a(A)$.

Proof. The Kummer exact sequences
$$
0\longrightarrow A(\Q)/2^nA(\Q)
\longrightarrow\Sel_{2^n}(A/\Q)
\longrightarrow\Sha(A/\Q)[2^n]\longrightarrow0
$$
pass to the direct limit, giving the displayed sequence; taking
coranks gives the identity. The last assertion is the forward
theorem of Gross-Zagier and Kolyvagin (Gross and Zagier 1986;
Kolyvagin 1990); see also the unrestricted formulation in Skinner
2020, Section 1.

Lemma 5 (parity and change of curve). For every elliptic curve
$A/\Q$ with root number $\varepsilon(A)$,
$$
(-1)^{c_2(A)}=\varepsilon(A).
$$
A rational isogeny preserves $a(A)$, $r(A)$, and $c_2(A)$. For a
quadratic field $K=\Q(\sqrt{k})$,
$$
\corank_{\Ztwo}\Sel_{2^\infty}(A/K)
=c_2(A)+c_2(A^{(k)}).
$$

Proof. The parity statement is the $p=2$ case of Dokchitser and
Dokchitser 2010 (Theorem 1.4, equivalently Theorem 4.19); at $p=2$
this is Monsky's theorem (Monsky 1996). Isogenies and their duals
respect every local Kummer condition; their composites are
multiplication by the fixed degree, so after rationalization the
maps preserve ranks and coranks, and the L-functions agree by
isogeny invariance. For the corank identity over $K$, the
conjugation projections $1+c$ and $1-c$ give maps between
$\Sel_{2^\infty}(A/K)$ and the sum of the two rational Selmer groups,
with composites multiplication by $2$; they respect local Kummer
images, and rationalization gives the identity.

Lemma 6 (residual alternatives). The representation $E[2]$ is
reducible over $\F_2$ if and only if $E(\Q)[2]\ne 0$. In that case
it is an extension of two trivial $\F_2$-representations. Otherwise
its image in $\GL_2(\F_2)\simeq S_3$ is the cyclic group of order
three or the full symmetric group. Quadratic twisting leaves the
residual representation unchanged.

Proof. An invariant line over $\F_2$ contains a unique nonzero
vector, which must be fixed. Conversely a rational point of order
two spans an invariant line. For an invariant line, both that line
and the one-dimensional quotient are trivial, since $\F_2^\times$
has one element. The irreducible subgroups of $\GL_2(\F_2)$ are the
cyclic group $C_3$ and $S_3$. A quadratic character reduces to the
trivial character modulo $2$.

The finite families behind the interpolation argument require two
sorts of uniformity: analytic nonvanishing with an upper valuation
bound at every nonzero vertex, and a dimension threshold independent
of the number of new primes for a congruence at the base. The transfer
criteria below state the exact form of each; their proofs occupy the
later arithmetic chapters.

Fix $E/\Q$ of conductor $N$, and retain the weights $w(h)$, the
finite Sha lengths $s(h)$, and the period-normalized values $\LL(h)$
introduced earlier. A binary family has the form
$$
h_x=h_0\prod_{q\in Q}(q^*)^{\lambda_q(x)},\qquad
x\in\F_2^b,\qquad q^*=(-1/q)q,
$$
where the new primes are distinct and prime to $2Nh_0$, each
$\lambda_q$ is a linear form on $\F_2^b$, the old factors of $h_0$
remain at every vertex, and $h_0=1$ at the base used in the main
theorems. The set of primes may depend on $b$ and the primes may grow
with $b$, but the required local squareclasses at $2N$ are held fixed
as $b$ grows.

The arithmetic proofs attach, at each vertex of a family satisfying
the transfer hypotheses, a power series
$$
u_x(t)\in\Ztwo[[t]][1/2],
$$
using a common family of cochain models and compatible
trivializations of the determinant lines. The differences of these
power series therefore have a fixed meaning, and their constant terms
detect the relevant central value: the analytic central value at rank
zero, the Heegner class at rank one. The argument reduces to two
claims. First, failure of the sought nonvanishing at the base gives
$u_0(0)=0$. Second, the nonzero vertices satisfy
$v(u_x(0))\le B_1$ with $B_1$ independent of $b$. The denominator
analysis proves that, for every fixed integer $m$ and every
sufficiently large $b$, some $x\ne 0$ satisfies
$$
v\bigl(u_x(0)-u_0(0)\bigr)\ge m.
$$
Choosing $m>B_1$ contradicts the two claims. The burden is to obtain
this congruence with a dimension threshold independent of the number
of new primes. The binary algebra removes the new local blocks from
the Selmer complex and exhibits a clearing factor on the remaining
bounded complex; only that factor needs a uniform pole bound, while
the numerator may involve the whole prime set.

For a twist with nonzero central value, put
$$
D(h)=v(\LL(h))-2w(h)-s(h).
$$
The subtraction records the local ramified contributions and the
finite Selmer contribution. In the cyclotomic proof the determinant
coordinate $u_x$ is Kato's class together with the invariant line at
the real place; its central valuation differs from $D(h_x)$ by a
fixed bounded amount. An upper bound on $D$ is exactly the
arithmetic input to the contradiction above.

Theorem 7 (cyclotomic missing vertex). There is a constant $C_E$,
depending only on $E$, with the following property. Fix an odd
fundamental discriminant $h_0$ prime to $2N$ with
$c_2(E^{(h_0)})=0$. Suppose that for arbitrarily large $b$ there is a
family
$$
h_x=h_0\prod_{q\in Q}(q^*)^{\lambda_q(x)},\qquad
x\in\F_2^b,\qquad\lambda_q\in(\F_2^b)^*,
$$
where $Q$ consists of distinct primes outside $2Nh_0$, each
$\lambda_q$ is a nonzero linear form, and every $h_x$ has the same
sign and the same local squareclasses at $2N$. Assume that a
constant $B$ independent of $b$ satisfies
$$
L(E^{(h_x)},1)\ne 0,\qquad D(h_x)\le B\qquad(x\ne 0).
$$
If $E[2]$ is irreducible, assume also that some $L$ independent of
$b$ satisfies $v_2(q^2-1)\le L$ for every $q\in Q$. Then
$L(E^{(h_0)},1)\ne 0$ and
$$
D(h_0)\le B+C_E.
$$
The necessary dimension may depend on $h_0$, on its finite Selmer
group, and on the bound $v_2(q^2-1)\le L$ in the irreducible case;
$C_E$ does not. The nonzero vertices in applications are arranged so
that $v(\LL(h_x))\le 2w(h_x)+C$, and since $s(h_x)\ge 0$, this
supplies the hypothesis on $D(h_x)$.

At corank one the determinant coordinate comes from a Heegner point,
and the family must also carry a companion twist with nonzero central
value. Two constructions enter the estimates. Let $K=\Q(\sqrt{k})$
with $k<0$ odd fundamental and every prime dividing $2N$ split in
$K$. The two fields with extra units and, when $E$ has CM, its CM
field, are omitted. Fix a nonzero modular parametrization
$X_0(N)\to E$, translated so the cusp at infinity maps to the
origin, and compatible orientations of Heegner points $y_c\in E(H_c)$
for ring class fields $H_c$. The Manin-Drinfeld theorem makes cusp
differences torsion (Drinfeld 1973, Section 1); since the curve and
the parametrization are fixed, one integer kills all such
ambiguities.

If $\chi_e|_{G_K}$ is a primitive ring-class character of conductor
$c$, write $P_c(e)$ for the character sum of $y_c$. Equivalently,
twist over $H_c$ and trace to $E^{(e)}(K)$. When this latter group
has rank one, $j(P_c(e))$ is the valuation of its index in the free
rank-one lattice. Passing between this lattice and the rank-one
lattice of the contributing curve among $E^{(e)}(\Q)$ and
$E^{(ek)}(\Q)$ changes the index by a bounded amount: restriction
and addition or subtraction of conjugates have composites
multiplication by 2.

Two choices of data are used. Genus data: $k=h h_*$, $(h,h_*)=1$,
$e=h_*$, $c=1$, with $h_*$ fixed. Ring data: $(k,h)=1$, $e=h$,
$c=|h|$. The character in genus data is unramified. In ring data,
restriction at each $q\mid h$ has conductor exponent one, since the
quadratic character pulls back to a primitive character of conductor
one under the local norm.

Proposition 8 (comparison of the two Heegner constructions). For
either set of data,
$$
\widehat h(P_c(e))
=C_E c\sqrt{|k|}
\left[L(E^{(e)},s)L(E^{(ek)},s)\right]'_{s=1},
$$
with a fixed nonzero constant determined by the parametrization and
height conventions. In particular, a simple zero of the product
gives total Mordell-Weil rank one and finite Sha groups. If $h$ has
analytic rank one and genus data are used with a fixed partner $h_*$
of analytic rank zero, and ring data are used with a possibly varying
$k$ and $L(E^{(hk)},1)\ne 0$, then
$$
2j(P_{|h|}(h))-2j(P_1(h_*))=v(\LL(hk))+O_{E,h_*}(1).
$$
If instead $h_*$ has analytic rank one and $L(E^{(h)},1)\ne 0$,
genus data give
$$
2j(P_1(h_*))=v(\LL(h))+O_{E,h_*}(1).
$$

The genus construction varies $\Q(\sqrt{hh_*})$ while holding $h_*$
fixed; it is what the odd coefficients detect. Interpolation instead
uses the ring construction: within a binary family the field $K$ is
common, and it may change only when a new family is chosen.

Theorem 9 (Heegner interpolation). Fix $E$ and an initial
discriminant $h_0$. Consider families $h_x$ for $x\in\F_2^b$
obtained from $h_0$ by adjoining new prime factors with linear
activation functions on $\F_2^b$; no old factor is removed. Suppose
the local twisting classes at $2N$ are constant. For each family let
$k$ define an imaginary field satisfying the standing Heegner
conditions, with $(k,h_x)=1$ for every $x$, and every new prime
either splits in $K$ or has $t_q=0$. Assume fixed independently of
$b$: (i) a bound for $\omega(k)$; (ii) a bound for the length of the
finite quotient of $\Sel_{2^\infty}(E^{(h_0)}/K)$ by its divisible
subgroup; (iii) a constant $B$. Assume the base Selmer group has
corank one. Suppose for every $x\ne 0$ the product
$L(E^{(h_x)},s)L(E^{(h_xk)},s)$ has a simple zero and
$$
2j(P_{|h_x|}(h_x))-4w(h_x)-s(h_x)-s(h_xk)\le B.
$$
Then, for sufficiently large $b$, the base product has a simple zero.
The dimension threshold depends only on the fixed base and the
displayed bounds, not on the number or sizes of new primes; the
field $K$ may vary with the family.

The normalized quantity in ring data is
$2j(P_{|h|}(h))-4w(h)-s(h)-s(hk)$. Applications have the
genus-index and even central-value estimates giving
$2j(P_{|h_x|}(h_x))\le 4w(h_x)+C$ at every nonzero vertex; subtracting
nonnegative Sha lengths supplies the required upper bound. The
finite-quotient condition covers the two uses of Theorem 9.

Lemma 10 (base Selmer bound). The finite-quotient bound in Theorem 9
holds if $K$ belongs to a fixed finite collection. It also holds if
$E^{(h_0)}$ is fixed with Selmer corank one, the curves $E^{(h_0k)}$
have analytic rank zero with bounded $s(h_0k)$, and $\omega(k)$ is
bounded.

The following three lower bounds supply the coefficient
normalizations.

Theorem 11 (cyclotomic lower bound). Suppose that $E[2]$ is an
extension of two trivial $\F_2$-modules. There is a constant $C_E$
such that every odd fundamental discriminant $h$ prime to $2N$ with
$L(E^{(h)},1)\ne 0$ satisfies
$$
v(\LL(h))\ge 2w(h)+s(h)-C_E.
$$
The constant is independent of the number and sizes of the primes
dividing $h$.

Theorem 12 (genus bound). Suppose $E[2]$ is irreducible and the
product in Proposition 8 has a simple zero in genus data. Then,
uniformly in $h$,
$$
2j(P_1(h_*))\ge 2w(h)+s(h)-O_{E,h_*}(1).
$$
In proving the uniform estimate, the finitely many $h$ with $K$ meets
$\Q(E[2])$ nontrivially may be omitted.

Theorem 13 (ring bound). In ring data, suppose the product of
$L$-values has a simple zero, every prime $q\mid h$ splits in $K$, and
$\omega(k)\le a$. Then
$$
2j(P_{|h|}(h))\ge 4w(h)+s(h)+s(hk)-O_{E,a}(1).
$$
No irreducibility is assumed on $E[2]$.

The dependency between these lower bounds is as follows. When $E[2]$
is reducible, Theorem 11 supplies the even lower estimate directly.
When $E[2]$ is irreducible, Theorem 12 supplies the lower estimate
for either sign: an analytic-rank-zero fixed partner bounds the odd
genus index; an analytic-rank-one fixed partner, combined with the
reverse comparison in Proposition 8, gives the even central-value
bound. The case of the odd coefficient with rational two-torsion has
neither bound at once. The even coefficient construction first
supplies, for every already rank-one twist $h$ in the positive
filter, a companion parameter $k'$ satisfying the ring-bound
hypotheses: it is negative, odd fundamental, coprime to $2Nh$, a
local square at every prime of $2Nh$, denotes a nonexceptional
imaginary field, and obeys a bounded prime count together with
$L(E^{(hk')},1)\ne 0$ and
$$
v(\LL(hk'))\le 2w(hk')+s(hk')+C.
$$
Substituting this upper bound into the index comparison and the ring
lower bound gives
$$
2j(P_1(h_*))\ge 2w(h)+s(h)-C',
$$
because $w(hk')=w(h)+w(k')$ with $w(k')$ bounded. Only then is the
odd coefficient system normalized in the reducible branch.

Proposition 14 (auxiliary nonvanishing). Let $E/\Q$ have conductor
$N$, and let $S$ be a finite set of primes containing the prime
divisors of $2N$. Prescribe a real sign $\sigma\in\{1,-1\}$ and a
unit squareclass $c_p\in\Z_p^\times/\Z_p^{\times 2}$ for each
$p\in S$. Suppose these data are realized by an odd fundamental
discriminant, and that twists in the resulting filter have root
number $\varepsilon$. For every finite set $B$ of primes there are
odd fundamental discriminants $D$ of arbitrarily large absolute
value such that $\operatorname{sign}(D)=\sigma$,
$[D]_p=c_p$ for $p\in S$, and $p\nmid D$ for $p\in B$. Moreover:
(i) if $\varepsilon=1$, $L(E^{(D)},1)\ne 0$ and
$\omega(|D|)\le 5$; (ii) if $\varepsilon=-1$, $L'(E^{(D)},1)\ne 0$,
so the central zero is simple. The bound five is independent of $E$,
$S$, $B$, and the prescribed classes; every prime factor of $D$ can
be required to exceed any previously fixed bound. The proof is given
in the auxiliary-nonvanishing chapter.

Auxiliary nonvanishing (Proposition 14) is proved by sign. For the
even sign, one preliminary twist reduces the statement to
Hoffstein-Luo. For the odd sign, a derivative first moment preserves
the prescribed local conditions throughout.

Proof of Proposition 14, (i) even sign. An odd fundamental
discriminant is squarefree and congruent to $1$ modulo $4$, so its
2-adic value is $1$ or $5$ modulo $8$. For each odd $p\in S$ choose
a nonzero residue $u_p$ representing $c_p$. By the Chinese remainder
theorem and Dirichlet, there is a prime $q$, not in $S$ or $B$, such
that
$$
q\equiv\sigma u_2\pmod 8,\qquad
\sigma q\equiv u_p\pmod p\qquad(p\in S,p\ne 2).
$$
Then $D_0=\sigma q$ is an odd fundamental discriminant in the
prescribed filter, and the twist $A=E^{(D_0)}$ has root number $1$.

The theorem of Hoffstein and Luo (Hoffstein and Luo 1997,
pp.~435--436) gives infinitely many odd fundamental discriminants $h$
with $L(A^{(h)},1)\ne 0$, $\omega(|h|)\le 4$, and $\chi_h(p)=1$ at
every prime of the fixed finite set $S\cup B\cup\{q\}$ together with
the prime divisors of the conductor $N_A$ of $A$. The twisting formula
gives
$$
1=\varepsilon(A^{(h)})=\varepsilon(A)\chi_h(-N_A)
=\operatorname{sign}(h),
$$
so $h>0$. For odd $p$ the condition $\chi_h(p)=1$ says $h$ is a unit
square in $\Q_p$; at $2$, odd fundamentality and $\chi_h(2)=1$ give
$h\equiv 1\pmod 8$. Since $q$ does not divide $h$, the product
$D=D_0h$ is squarefree and congruent to $1$ modulo $4$, retains the
prescribed sign and squareclasses, avoids $B$, and satisfies
$$
E^{(D)}\simeq A^{(h)},\qquad
\omega(|D|)=1+\omega(h)\le 5.
$$
Distinct $h$ give distinct $D$. That completes the even case.

Proof of Proposition 14, (ii) odd sign. The analytic input is a
derivative first moment for the weight-two newform of $E$, adapted
to an arbitrary fixed admissible progression of squarefree conductors.
Central values in the odd case vanish identically, while their
derivatives admit a positive even moment at a fixed progression and
unit class.

Lemma 15 (odd first moment). Let $f$ be the weight-two newform of
$E/\Q$ with $L$-function normalized to center $1/2$. Fix $H$
divisible by $8$ and by $N$, a reduced class $a\pmod H$, and
$\sigma\in\{1,-1\}$ with $\sigma a\equiv 1\pmod 4$. Suppose the
twists by $n>0$ squarefree with $n\equiv a\pmod H$ all have root
number $-1$. For every nonnegative nonzero $\Phi\in C_c^\infty((1,2))$
there are $c>0$, $c_1\in\mathbb R$, and an absolute $\eta>0$ with
$$
\sum_{\substack{n>0\ \text{squarefree}\\n\equiv a\ (H)}}
L'(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{\sigma n})\Phi(n/X)
=cX\log X+c_1X+O_{f,H,a,\sigma,\Phi}(X^{1-\eta}).
$$
In particular this progression contains arbitrarily large $n$ whose
twists have a simple central zero.

Proof. Write $L(s,f)=\sum_m\lambda(m)m^{-s}$, with
$|\lambda(m)|\le\tau(m)$, and set $Q=\sqrt N/(2\pi)$. The progression
at modulus $H$ is kept fixed throughout. Three steps: introduce the
approximate functional equation; remove the squarefree restriction
using a large-sieve bound for the twists; evaluate the resulting
Poisson sum, retaining only the zero frequency.

Approximate functional equation. Define
$$
V(y)=(2\pi i)^{-1}\int_{(2)}e^{z^2}\Gamma(1+z)y^{-z}\,dz/z^2.
$$
The twist by $\chi_{\sigma n}$ has conductor $Nn^2$ and, by
assumption, odd functional equation. Then
$$
L'(1/2,f\otimes\chi_{\sigma n})
=2\sum_m\lambda(m)\chi_{\sigma n}(m)m^{-1/2}V(m/(Qn)).
$$
The right side also defines the analytic continuation of the
derivative for nonsquarefree $n\equiv a\pmod H$.

Squarefree reduction. Let $\mu$ be the Mobius function, expand
$\mu^2(n)=\sum_{d^2|n}\mu(d)$, and retain $d\le Y$ for $Y$ to be
chosen. The omitted part is $O(X^{1+\rho}/Y)$ for every $\rho>0$:
write $n=tv^2$ with $t$ squarefree and $v>Y$; the twist factor over
$p\mid v$ is $O_\epsilon(v^\epsilon)$, and the required $L$-moment
over $t\le 2X/v^2$ is $O((X/v^2)^{1+\epsilon})$ by Cauchy together
with the quadratic large sieve. The second moment
$$
\sum_{\substack{t\le Z\ \text{squarefree}\\(t,2N)=1}}
|L(1/2+\delta+iv,f\otimes\chi_{\sigma t})|^2
\ll_{f,\rho,\delta}Z^{1+\rho}(1+|v|)^{C_{\rho,\delta}}
$$
follows from the quadratic large sieve of Heath-Brown (Heath-Brown
1995, Theorem 1): writing $m=ur^2$ with $u$ squarefree and separating
dyadic ranges, the symbols reduce to a composite character, and the
Minkowski bound gives the stated power, with powers of $2$ in $2N$
contributing convergent geometric sums.

Poisson summation. For $d$ coprime to $H$ put $J=d^2$ and
$c_J\equiv aJ^{-1}\pmod H$. Let $T(J)$ be the contribution of
$n\equiv a\pmod H$ with $J\mid n$, without the removed squarefree
restriction. Writing $m=m_1q$ with $m_1\mid H^\infty$ and $(q,H)=1$,
and defining
$$
G_\nu(q)=\sum_{y\bmod q}(y/q)e(\nu y/q),\quad
W(z,R)=\int_0^\infty\Phi(x)V(z/(Qx))e(-Rx)\,dx,
$$
Poisson summation expresses $T(J)$ as $2X/(HJ)$ times the sum over
$m_1\mid H^\infty$, $(q,H)=1$, and $\nu\in\Z$ of
$\lambda(m_1)\kappa(m_1)m_1^{-1/2}\lambda(q)q^{-3/2}(\sigma JH/q)
G_\nu(q)e(\nu c_J\bar q/H)W(m_1q/X,\nu X/(HJq))$, with
$\kappa(m_1)=\chi_{\sigma a}(m_1)$ and $q\bar q\equiv 1\pmod H$.

Zero frequency. The Gauss sum $G_0(q)$ vanishes unless $q$ is a
square, in which case it equals $\varphi(q)$. Mellin inversion
together with the symmetric-square Euler product gives
$$
T_0(J)=\frac{2X}{HJ}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(2)}
e^{z^2}\Gamma(1+z)(QX)^z\widehat\Phi_0(z)L_H(z)F_J(z)\,dz/z^2,
$$
where $H_p(z)=1+(1-p^{-1})\sum_r\lambda(p^{2r})p^{-r(1+2z)}$,
$F_J(z)=\prod_{p\nmid HJ}H_p(z)$, and
$L_H(z)=\sum_{m_1\mid H^\infty}\lambda(m_1)\kappa(m_1)m_1^{-1/2-z}$.
At good primes, with $\alpha_p\beta_p=1$ and $|\alpha_p|=|\beta_p|=1$,
the identity $\sum_r\lambda(p^{2r})x^r=(1+x)/((1-\alpha_p^2x)
(1-\beta_p^2x))$ shows that $H_p(z)$ is the symmetric-square Euler
factor at $1+2z$ times a polynomial. Symmetric-square continuation
and its functional equation allow the shift to $\Re z=-1/16$ with
error $O(J^B X^{1-1/16})$. The residue sum is
$$
\frac{2X}{H}\bigl(M(0)\log X+M'(0)\bigr),\qquad c=2M(0)/H>0,
$$
because each $H_p(0)=p^{-1}+(1-p^{-1})F_p^{\rm ev}>p^{-1}$ and
$L(1,\operatorname{sym}^2 f)>0$ by the Rankin-Selberg residue formula.

Nonzero frequencies. For odd $q$ set $\epsilon_q=1$ or $i$ according
as $q\equiv 1$ or $3\pmod 4$ and write
$\widetilde G_\nu(q)=\epsilon_q^{-1}G_\nu(q)$. Quadratic reciprocity
makes $\widetilde G_\nu$ multiplicative. Expanding the phase
$\epsilon_qe(\nu c_J\bar q/H)$ in Dirichlet characters modulo $H$,
the twisted sum is the $L$-function of an entire $\operatorname{GL}_2$
twist; its functional equation and convexity bound give
$T_{\ne 0}(J)\ll J^B X^{7/8}$. The detail: Mellin inversion at a fixed
dyadic scale shifts to $\Re s=-1/4$, and the weight decay
$$
(z\partial_z)^r(R\partial_R)^sW(z,R)
\ll_{r,s,K}(1+z)^{-K}(1+|R|)^{-K}(1+|\log z|)
$$
supplies the convergence and the polynomial cost in $(J,|\nu|)$.
Retained frequencies satisfy $m_1q\ll X^{1+\rho}$,
$0<|\nu|\ll JX^{2\rho}$, $q\gg X^{1-\rho}/J$; choosing $\rho$
absolute small gives the eighth-power saving.

Conclusion. Summing the errors over $d\le Y$ gives
$O(Y^{2B+1}X^{1-\kappa}+X^{1+\rho}/Y)$ with $\kappa=1/16$. Choose
$\delta_0>0$ with $(2B+1)\delta_0<\kappa/2$, put $Y=X^{\delta_0}$,
$0<\rho<\delta_0/4$. Both error terms are $O(X^{1-\eta})$ for an
absolute $\eta>0$, and the positive main term dominates.

End of the odd-sign proof of Proposition 14. Choose $H$ divisible by
$8N$ and by every prime in $S\cup B$. The prescribed squareclasses
and sign determine a reduced class $a\pmod H$ with
$\sigma a\equiv 1\pmod 4$. Every positive squarefree $n\equiv a\pmod
H$ gives an odd fundamental discriminant $D=\sigma n$ in the
prescribed filter, prime to every prime of $B$. Lemma 15 supplies
arbitrarily large such $n$ with $L'(E^{(D)},1)\ne 0$.

Now the coefficient systems. The even sign uses weight 3/2 forms
attached to Waldspurger's correspondence. The odd sign uses
reduction of Heegner points on a definite quaternion order, in the
oriented form of Deuring. Both systems are set up before the graph
algebra because their detection interface constrains what a
coefficient must satisfy at auxiliary primes.

The ordinary theta multiplier cubed is used in weight 3/2. A finite
idele unit character and its Dirichlet character are related by the
reciprocal convention: if a finite Schwartz function obeys
$f(ux)=\alpha(u)f(x)$, its scalar classical character on the lower-right
entry is associated with $\alpha^{-1}$. All valuations satisfy
$v(2)=1$.

Proposition 16 (even coefficients). Fix a nonempty filter of even
functional sign. There are a holomorphic weight 3/2 cusp form
$g=\sum_{n\ge 1}a(n)q^n$ of level supported on $S$, after the
exponents at those primes are enlarged, and finite-order characters
$\psi,\eta$ of 2-power order with $\psi/\eta$ quadratic, such that:
(i) the coefficients vanish off the filter, and for $p\notin S$,
$$
a(p^2 n)
=\bigl(\eta(p)a_p(E)-\psi(p)(-n/p)\bigr)a(n)
-p\eta(p)^2 a(n/p^2);
$$
(ii) after normalization at one nonzero squarefree index, every
squarefree coefficient in the filter is a rational number times a root
of unity of 2-power order, and for a fixed nonzero squarefree index
$D_0$,
$$
2v\!\left(\frac{a(D)}{a(D_0)}\right)
=v\!\left(\frac{\LL(\sigma D)}{\LL(\sigma D_0)}\right)
\qquad(a(D)\ne0);
$$
equivalently, there is a constant $c_g$ independent of $D$ with
$2v(a(D))=v(\LL(h))+c_g$, and in particular $a(D)\ne0$ if and only if
$L(E^{(h)},1)\ne0$;
(iii) after the fixed auxiliary exclusions described below there is a
constant $C_g$ such that, at every nonzero squarefree coefficient,
$$
v(a(D))\ge w(D)+\tfrac{1}{2}s(h)-C_g.
$$
The constants depend on $E$, the filter, and the auxiliary choices,
and not on $D$ or its number of prime factors.

Proof. Choose the packet member and its local characters first. Then
establish rationality up to a 2-power phase, apply Waldspurger's
coefficient-square formula, and deduce the uniform lower bound from the
cyclotomic and genus lower bounds.

Packet and local characters. For $p\notin S$, the classes
split, simple, and root correspond to the Frobenius action on $V=E[2]$
by identity, transposition, or 3-cycle. Enlarge the exponents at $S$,
and choose $\eta$ restricting at $p\in S$ to a fixed even unit
character of the unramified quadratic extension when one is required.
Choose $\psi$ similarly, requiring $\psi/\eta$ to be quadratic and to
restrict to the real sign in the even case. The parity is compatible
with automorphy, the resulting character of the packet member equals
$\psi$, and after setting $\eta=\psi\chi_{-\sigma}$ the weight-3/2
Hecke formula gives the stated recurrence with eigenvalue
$\eta(p)a_p(E)-\psi(p)(-n/p)$.

Rationality up to 2-power phase. At the fixed level the weight-3/2
cusp space with character $\psi$ has a basis over the field of
character values (Shimura 1981, Section 2, Lemma 4(3)-(4)). The good
Hecke equations and the vanishing conditions off the filter are
linear equations over $\Q(\psi,\eta)$; finitely many cut out the full
kernel, and the constructed vector has a nonzero $D_0$-coefficient,
so some algebraic vector in the kernel has nonzero $D_0$-coefficient.
Such a vector remains orthogonal to unary theta series, whose
good-prime eigenvalues have absolute value $p+1>2\sqrt{p}$
(Purkait 2012, Section 2, Lemma 2.1 and Corollary 5.2).

Now let $u$ be an automorphism of $\overline\Q$ acting by an odd power
$e$ on 2-power characters, and put $\nu=\eta^{(e-1)/2}$. The
coefficient conjugate $g^u$ and the coefficient twist $g\otimes\nu$
have the same character and good Hecke system, since
$\psi\nu^2=\psi^e$ and $\eta\nu^2=\eta^e$. They can be compared at a
common level supported on $S$, in the same good Hecke eigenspace $W$,
and both have a nonzero $D_0$-coefficient. Waldspurger's corollary
(Waldspurger 1981, Corollary 2, pp. 483-484) applies to every
$f\in W$ and gives $\ell_D(f)^2=C_D\ell_{D_0}(f)^2$ with $C_D$
independent of $f$. If $C_D=0$ then $\ell_D=0$. If not, choose $c_D$
with $c_D^2=C_D$; then
$(\ell_D-c_D\ell_{D_0})(\ell_D+c_D\ell_{D_0})=0$, and the polynomial
ring on $W$ is an integral domain, so one factor vanishes
identically. Thus $\ell_D$ and $\ell_{D_0}$ are proportional on the
whole space, including oldvectors and linear combinations.

For $\xi=a(D)/a(D_0)$ this gives $u(\xi)=\nu(D/D_0)\xi$. The local
squareclass conditions imply $\eta(D/D_0)=z^2$ for a value $z$ in the
group of values of $\eta$, so $u(\xi/z)=\xi/z$; the quotient $\xi/z$
is rational, proving the phase assertion.

Coefficient squares. With the chosen characters and period
normalization the coefficient-square identity is
$$
\left(\frac{a(D)}{a(D_0)}\right)^2
=\psi(D/D_0)\frac{\LL(\sigma D)}{\LL(\sigma D_0)},
$$
where $\psi(D/D_0)$ has valuation zero (the classical central-value
comparison absorbs $\sqrt{D/D_0}$ in $\LL$). The local unit
squareclasses at $S$ agree, and at good primes the spherical
squarefree factor is one at both valuations zero and one
(Waldspurger 1981, Section I.3). There is no extra product over the
prime factors of $D$. Taking valuations gives the ratio, hence
the ratio of $\LL$-values, hence the vanishing equivalence.

Uniform lower bound. If $E[2]$ is an extension of trivial modules,
combine the coefficient-square formula with the cyclotomic lower bound
(Theorem 11). If $E[2]$ is irreducible, choose a fixed odd
fundamental discriminant $h_*$ of opposite infinite sign and analytic
rank one whose local characters agree with those of $h$ at $2N$:
Proposition 14 supplies such a choice. Exclude its primes from the
filter and, if needed, adjust its support to avoid the finitely many
exceptional genus fields. The reverse genus comparison in
Proposition 8 and Theorem 12 give the same lower bound. These are
fixed exclusions and constants, so the stated uniformity holds in
both cases.

Odd coefficients from genus Heegner sums. The odd construction
combines reduction of Heegner points with the ternary quadratic forms
attached to definite quaternion orders, using Deuring's reduction
correspondence in the oriented form of Cornut and Jetchev (Cornut and
Jetchev 2013) together with the Eichler order and ternary form
description of Jetchev and Kane (Jetchev and Kane 2011).

Fix a nonempty filter of odd functional sign. Choose an odd
fundamental discriminant $h_*$ of opposite infinite sign with
$L(E^{(h_*)},1)\ne0$ whose local characters agree with those of $h$
at $2N$; exclude its primes. Choose $|h_*|>3$ and include in the
support of $h_*$ a prime outside the exceptional imaginary quadratic
discriminants. Then all genus fields below have only units $\pm 1$
and avoid the CM field of $E$. Write
$$
k=hh_*<0,\qquad K_D=\Q(\sqrt{k}),\qquad P_D=P_1(h_*).
$$
The point $P_D$ is the character sum of the conductor-one Heegner
point for $K_D$. Fix a modular parametrization $\varphi$ with
$\varphi(\infty)=0$, so the constants in its Hecke and Atkin-Lehner
relations are torsion. At a simple zero the free line of $E^{(h)}$ is
that of $P_D$, up to the fixed-degree identifications of
Proposition 8.

Proposition 17 (odd coefficients under a genus-index bound). Suppose
a constant $C_*$ satisfies
$$
j(P_D)\ge w(D)+\tfrac{1}{2}s(h)-C_*
$$
for every $h$ in the filter with a simple zero. For every precision
$M$ there are good odd primes $\ell$ outside the fixed support and
holomorphic weight-3/2 forms $g_M=\sum a_M(n)q^n$ of level supported
on $S\cup\{\ell\}$ with four properties.
(i) The additional filter at $\ell$ is
$$
(h_*/\ell)=-\sigma,\qquad (h/\ell)=\sigma,\qquad
\rho_T(\Fr_\ell)\equiv C\pmod{2^M},
$$
where $C$ acts as complex conjugation on $T$; in particular
$\ell\equiv -1\pmod{2^M}$.
(ii) After a fixed harmless decrease in working precision, the forms
satisfy the Hecke recurrence modulo that precision, with characters
$\eta_M,\psi_M$ of 2-power order and quadratic ratio, and squarefree
coefficients that are a 2-power phase times an integer modulo $2^M$.
(iii) If a coefficient is visible, meaning $v(a_M(D))<M$, then
$E^{(h)}$ has a simple zero and $j(P_D)\le v(a_M(D))+C_1$; for every
admissible $D$,
$$
v(a_M(D))\ge\min\{M,\,w(D)+\tfrac{1}{2}s(h)-C_2\}
$$
when the product derivative is nonzero, and the coefficient vanishes
modulo $2^M$ when it is zero. Here $C_1,C_2$ are independent of
$\ell,M,D$.
(iv) A fixed squarefree witness $D_w$ in the original filter is
admissible at every chosen $\ell$ and has $v(a_M(D_w))$ bounded as
$M\to\infty$.

For irreducible $E[2]$ the hypothesis is Theorem 12. For rational
two-torsion the positive filters have this bound by Theorem 13 in
combination with Proposition 8 and Proposition 16.

Proof. There are three stages: a reduction map detects one fixed
nonzero genus point at arbitrarily high precision; weighted theta
series supply holomorphic forms with the required Hecke recurrence;
an exact comparison with genus sums transfers point divisibility and
the assumed lower bound to the squarefree coefficients.

Reduction map and detecting sequence. Choose a nonzero integral row
$b$ with $bC=\sigma b$. Under the additional $\ell$-filter,
finite-field Kummer theory identifies
$$
E(\F_{\ell^2})\to H^1(\F_{\ell^2},E[2^M])\simeq E[2^M],
$$
the last identification being evaluation at arithmetic Frobenius
squared. Put $\lambda_M=b\kappa_M$. This map has Frobenius sign
$\sigma$.

Choose $h_w=\sigma D_w$ in the original filter with a simple zero,
using Proposition 14. A fixed multiple of its genus point is a
nonzero point on the rational curve $E^{(h_w)}$. Its division
cocycle has open translation image on the kernel of the Tate action,
even after fixed finite auxiliary fields are adjoined. The
translation image is a Galois-stable $\Ztwo$-submodule of the Tate
module, its rational span is nonzero (otherwise Kummer injectivity
would fail, since the Tate image has a central nonidentity scalar
killing rational first cohomology), and the residual Tate
representation is irreducible (Serre 1968, Chapter IV, Section 2.1;
Bogomolov 1981, Section 2, Theorem 3). The nonzero stable span is the
whole Tate module, so the translation image contains a lattice.

Take an element acting as $C$ on $T$ and as complex conjugation on the
fixed quadratic character fields; modify it by such a translation. On
the twisted module for $h_w$ its action is $\sigma C$; at its square
the cocycle contains $1+\sigma C$; projection by $b$ is twice a
nonzero row. Nonzero detection with fixed finite valuation is
therefore achievable. Chebotarev, applied at successive finite
precisions to this compatible Galois element and its division data,
supplies arbitrarily large primes satisfying the additional filter.

Supersingular values and local weights. Let $B$ be the quaternion
algebra ramified at $\ell$ and infinity, and $R$ an Eichler order of
level $N$. Supersingular pairs on $X_0(N)$ are parametrized by
$B^\times\backslash\widehat B^\times/\widehat R^\times$ and defined
over $\F_{\ell^2}$ (Jetchev and Kane 2011, Sections 1.1, 1.2, 2.5).
For a representative $z$ write $R_z=B\cap z\widehat R z^{-1}$, and
let $P_M(z)$ be the reduced modular-parametrization value followed by
$\lambda_M$. Cusp differences have torsion image by Manin-Drinfeld;
multiply the parametrization once by a fixed integer $L$ killing the
cuspidal constants and the bounded-degree torsion in genus sums. The
resulting $P_M$ has good Brandt eigenvalues $a_p(E)$, level
Atkin-Lehner eigenvalues $w_{p^e}$ for $p^e\Vert N$, and eigenvalue
$\sigma$ for the right uniformizer at $\ell$; these hold modulo $2^M$.

On $B^0$, the trace-zero subspace, use the positive definite form
$Q(x)=-x^2=\Nrd(x)$. The aim is to make $a_M(D)$ equal, modulo $2^M$
and up to a 2-power phase, to a fixed integer multiple of
$\lambda_M(\operatorname{red}_\ell(LP_D))$. This identifies the terms
with optimal embeddings of $\mathcal O_{K_D}$; their local
orientations give copies of the genus sum, and the weights must make
those copies contribute equally with a unit-scaling law that yields
a scalar modular character.

Finite compactly supported locally constant weight. Define
$f=\prod_p f_p$ with $f_p$ zero at any residue failing one of the
following conditions.
(a) At $p\notin 2Nh_*\ell$, require order integrality; set $f_p=1$.
(b) At $2$, require $(1+x)/2$ integral and $x^2$ a split fundamental
unit squareclass; at $p^e\Vert N$ require the split fundamental unit
type and its order embedding, with the cyclic Eichler level line
selecting one of the two eigenroots $r$ and a 2-power unit character
$\eta_p(r)$ normalized by $\eta_p(-1)=w_{p^e}\chi_{h_*}(p)^e$. At
$p=2$ the selected summand of $(1+x)/2$ has eigenvalues
$(1\pm r)/2$ differing by the odd unit $r$; scaling by an odd $u$
sends the operator to $u(1+x)/2+(1-u)/2$, preserving that summand
with root $ur$. If $2\nmid N$ impose only the integrality restriction.
(c) At $p\mid h_*$ require $x$ integral with determinant of valuation
one; its reduction is nonzero nilpotent, and on that orbit use the
sign transforming by $(\det u/p)$ under conjugation by
$u\in\GL_2(\Z_p)$.
(d) At $\ell$ require integral unit norm and the unramified quadratic
type, and weight the nonzero residue on the trace-zero line by its
Legendre symbol relative to a fixed basis.

All conditions are imposed at finite depth and are stable under unit
scaling. Right order-unit conjugation changes $f$ by the inverse of
$\chi_{h_*}\circ\Nrd$, canceling the norm character. At $\ell$ order
units commute on the residue field while a uniformizer negates its
trace-zero line; since $\ell\equiv -1\pmod 4$, the latter weight
changes by $-1$. Write $\alpha_B=\prod_p\alpha_p$ for the finite
unit-scaling character, so $f(ux)=\alpha_B(u)f(x)$, and let $\eta_B$
denote the associated Dirichlet character on integers prime to the
support. Both characters are even (the product over $p^e\Vert N$
times $\chi_{h_*}(-1)(-1)$ equals $1$ by the even functional sign of
$E^{(h_*)}$) and both have 2-power order.

Weighted theta and transformation. Choose a fixed positive integer
$c_0$ divisible by the orders of all occurring unit groups (bounded
uniformly for a rational definite quaternion order). Form
$$
H_M(n)=c_0\sum_z\frac{P_M(z)\chi_{h_*}(\Nrd z)}{|R_z^\times|}
\sum_{\substack{x\in B^0\\-x^2=n}}f(z^{-1}xz),\qquad
H_M(\tau)=\sum_{n\ge 1}H_M(n)q^n.
$$
The norm character is trivial on rational $B^\times$, since the
reduced norms are positive, so $H_M$ is independent of the
representative. Apply the Weil representation of $(B^0,Q)$ to $f$
and the Gaussian at infinity (Weil 1964); at a finite prime with
additive character of conductor $\Z_p$ the local formulas are given
in Pal and De Vera Piquero 2019, Section 4.3. Integral $b$ fixes $f_p$
since $Q$ is integral on its support; choosing a translation
invariance lattice whose dual contains the support of $\widehat f_p$,
and taking $a_p$ large so that $p^{a_p}Q$ is integral there, makes
$n(p^{a_p}b)$ fix $\widehat f_p$, and conjugation by the Weyl element
gives lower-unipotent invariance at the required depth.

Compare with the threefold ordinary-theta reference line, using the
same additive character and lifts. In a presentation $B=(-a,-b)$ with
$Q=aX^2+bY^2+abZ^2$, the ratio of the diagonal Weil factor to the
reference factor is $(a,u)_p(b,u)_p(ab,u)_p=1$, even at $p=2$. The
remaining local scalar is $\alpha_p(u)$. Upper unipotents, diagonal
units, and lower unipotents at sufficient depth generate the required
local $\Gamma_0$ subgroup. At the chosen depth $a\equiv d^{-1}$ modulo
the character conductor, so the classical restriction gives character
$\eta_B(d)$. The metaplectic central ambiguity cancels in the
comparison, since both representations have the same odd central
action.

At good odd primes the lattice is spherical; the Gaussian gives
weight 3/2 and positive definiteness gives holomorphy at every cusp.
Thus $H_M$ is a classical form of level supported on $2Nh_*\ell$, and
at $p\nmid 2Nh_*\ell$ the neighbor relation has Hecke eigenvalue
$\eta_B(p)\chi_{h_*}(p)a_p(E)$.

Multiplying by the fixed integer $d=|h_*|$. The weights force $Q(x)$
divisible by $d$; the series with coefficients $H_M(dn)$ is the same
weighted theta sum for $Q/d$. Its ternary determinant changes by
$d^{-3}$, whose squareclass is $d$, so the scalar character acquires
$(d/\cdot)$; substitution of $dn$ in the good-prime recurrence leaves
$\eta_B\chi_{h_*}$ unchanged. Extraction of multiples changes the
character to $\psi_M=\eta_B(d/\cdot)$, while
$\eta_M=\eta_B\chi_{h_*}$ is the good eigenvalue character. Their
quotient is quadratic, and the neighbor relation becomes the stated
Hecke recurrence with level supported on $S\cup\{\ell\}$.

Weighted trace identity. For a squarefree $D$ in the filter put
$n=|h_*|D=-k$. A counted $x$ is exactly an optimal embedding of the
full order $\mathcal O_{K_D}$ into $R_z$ via $\sqrt{k}\mapsto x$. At
$2$ the generator is $(1+\sqrt{k})/2$, which is why the integrality
condition at 2 is necessary. At split level primes there are two
choices of cyclic level orientation, at $\ell$ two choices of
unramified quadratic orientation, and one local orbit at every other
prime. Stable lattices decompose into two idempotent level summands
at split primes; the fundamental quotient carries exactly one cyclic
summand. The division-prime choice is given by the two reduced-norm
parity cosets. At primes outside $2Nh_*\ell$, stable lattices are the
fractional ideals of the quadratic order, giving one local orbit.

Fix an orientation tuple and a rational embedding $x_0$. The global
optimal-embedding orbits are parametrized by $\Pic(\mathcal O_{K_D})$:
once one adelic representative $z_0$ is fixed, the representatives
are $z_{\mathfrak a}=az_0$ with $a\in\widehat K_D^\times$, and changing
$a$ by $K_D^\times$ or $\widehat O_{K_D}^\times$ gives the same class.
Because $a$ commutes with $x_0$, the Schwartz factor is constant
along the class-group orbit; its remaining weight is
$$
\chi_{h_*}(\Nrd a)=\chi_{h_*}(\operatorname{Norm}_{K_D/\Q}a),
$$
the unramified genus character. That proves the weighted identity,
not merely the unweighted correspondence. The stabilizer factor is
exact: each orbit in a given $R_z$ has
$|R_z^\times|/|\mathcal O_{K_D}^\times|=|R_z^\times|/2$ elements, so
its divided contribution is one half the class-group term. These are
oriented embeddings, not conjugate pairs: $x$ and $-x$ have opposite
orientations at $\ell$ and cannot be conjugate by an order unit. CM
reduction at the inert prime identifies these terms with reductions
of Heegner points and commutes with ideal action; the cyclic subgroup
carries the chosen level orientation (Cornut and Jetchev 2013,
Theorem 3.1, Corollary 3.2, Section 3.6 Remark 1; Jetchev and Kane
2011, Sections 2.4-2.6 and 4.1). Fixing the residue-field
identification at $\ell$ retains its orientation; both orientations
are kept in the theta sum.

Choose the initial orientation to agree with the Heegner point
defining $P_D$. The constant weight on its orbit is a 2-power phase
$\zeta_M(D)$, since all its local weights are units. Changing
orientation at $p^e\Vert N$ multiplies by $w_{p^e}\chi_{h_*}(p)^e$
and multiplies the norm-character-weighted parametrization value by
the same sign; at $\ell$ both signs are $-1$, since
$\sigma\chi_{h_*}(\ell)=-1$. All $2^{\omega(N)+1}$ orientation tuples
therefore contribute the same term. With $L$ already incorporated
into $P_M$, the comparison is
$$
a_M(D)=c_0 2^{\omega(N)}\zeta_M(D)\,
\lambda_M(\operatorname{red}_\ell(LP_D))\pmod{2^M}.
$$
The initial basis at $\ell$ and initial orientation affect only
$\zeta_M(D)$; the normalization uses a character sum without a
class-number denominator; all rational multipliers are fixed
independently of $D$ and $\ell$.

If the product derivative is zero, Gross-Zagier makes $P_D$ torsion;
the fixed multiplier kills it, because genus sums and their twist
comparisons are defined over biquadratic fields, and a fixed elliptic
curve has uniformly bounded torsion in degree at most four. If the
derivative is nonzero, division by $2^{j(P_D)}$ on the free twist
line implies the same divisibility after reduction and $\lambda_M$,
up to a fixed degree comparison and the fixed multiplier; a visible
image bounds $j(P_D)$ above by its visible valuation plus a fixed
constant. The weighted trace identity therefore proves detection and
the lower bound under the assumed genus-index lower bound. Fixed
denominators or multipliers reduce $M$ by a fixed amount; reindexing
the precisions gives the stated formulation.

The least normalized depth. Put the coefficient normalization before
any prime array is chosen. For the even source, first rescale $g$ so
that $a(D_0)=1$ at one nonzero squarefree index; its nonzero
squarefree valuations are integers by Proposition 16. Set
$$
\delta=\min_D\{v(a(D))-w(D)\},\qquad
A(D)=\begin{cases}1,&v(a(D))=\delta+w(D),\\0,&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}
$$
with $A(D)=0$ off the filter. The minimum exists: the lower bound is
uniform, visible valuations are integers after normalization, the
fractional part of the weight is fixed, and a nonzero witness exists.

For the odd source fix a nonprincipal ultrafilter on the sequence of
precisions. Consider all sequences $D_M$ of admissible indices whose
weight $w(D_M)$ is bounded and whose valuation $v(a_M(D_M))$ is
visible on an ultrafilter-large set. Each such sequence has a
normalized depth bounded below in a discrete set; let $\delta$ be the
least depth attainable on an ultrafilter-large set. For a
bounded-weight admissible sequence $\mathbf D=(D_M)$, set
$A(\mathbf D)=1$ if and only if
$\{M:v(a_M(D_M))=\delta+w(D_M)\}$ is ultrafilter-large, and
$A(\mathbf D)=0$ otherwise. A least weight supporting a unit symbol is
minimized among the same bounded-weight sequences that attain depth
$\delta$; after restricting to an ultrafilter-large set the weight is
constant on a bounded subset of $(1/2)\Z_{\ge0}$, and the set of such
weights is nonempty with a least element $m$.

Lemma 18 (uniformity at a fixed weight bound). For every fixed $W$,
on an ultrafilter-large set of sufficiently high precisions,
$$
v(a_M(D))\ge \delta+w(D)
$$
for every admissible index with $w(D)\le W$, with valuations truncated
at the working precision. Any finite list of stabilized symbol
assertions of bounded weight is realized simultaneously at such
precisions.

Proof. A failure on an ultrafilter-large set produces violating $D_M$
of bounded weight and bounded discrete depth, contradicting the
definition of $\delta$. Finite collections intersect their
ultrafilter-large realization sets. Only a bound on weight is used,
not on the number or size of primes.

Proposition 19 (detection by a unit symbol). For either coefficient
source $A(D)=1$ implies the prescribed analytic order, zero in the
even case and one in the odd case. At every such index,
$$
v(\LL(h))\le 2w(D)+C,\qquad j(P_D)\le w(D)+C,
$$
and
$$
s(h)\le C,\qquad \dim_{\F_2}\Sel_2(E^{(h)}/\Q)\le C.
$$
The constants depend on the original curve, filter, coefficient
normalization, and genus partner. For the odd source they are
independent of the detecting prime and working precision, and of the
number and size of active primes.

Proof. In the even case substitute $v(a(D))=\delta+w(D)$ into the
even coefficient identities. In the odd case use a precision greater
than the finitely many tested depths and apply detection and the
lower bound. At these indices the analytic order is established, so
Theorem 4 gives the same algebraic rank and finite Sha. The 2-Selmer
dimension is then bounded by that rank, the fixed bound on rational
2-torsion, and the 2-primary length of Sha.

The transfer machinery of Theorem 7 and Theorem 9 is purely algebraic.
Both analytic arguments produce one determinant coordinate at each
vertex of a binary cube, and the algebra below transfers a bounded
valuation at every nonzero vertex to the zero vertex. Its essential
feature is that the required dimension depends on a bounded number of
coefficients, even when the family involves arbitrarily many primes.

Put $\Lambda=\Ztwo[[t]]$ and $O=\widehat{\Lambda_{(2)}}$. Let
$G\simeq(\Z/2\Z)^b$ and $R=\Lambda[G]$. Index the sign homomorphisms
$G\to\{\pm1\}$ by $x\in\F_2^b$, with $x=0$ the augmentation. For an
element or matrix over $O[G]$, a subscript $x$ denotes evaluation at
this character. The proofs control Laurent coefficients, then clear
determinant denominators, and finally use the parity of the number of
solutions of low-degree equations on the cube.

Lemma 20 (Laurent expansions). For $M\ge1$ there is a ring
isomorphism $O/2^MO\simeq(\Z/2^M\Z)((t))$. Thus an element of $O$
has a unique expansion $\sum_{n\in\Z}a_nt^n$ with $a_n\in\Ztwo$ and
$v(a_n)\to\infty$ as $n\to-\infty$. Its $O$-valuation is
$\min_n v(a_n)$.

Proof. If $s\in\Lambda\setminus(2)$, write $s=t^hv_1+2w_1$ with
$v_1\in\Lambda^\times$ and $w_1\in\Lambda$. Modulo $2^M$ its inverse
is a geometric expansion of length $M$ in $2w_1/(t^hv_1)$, a Laurent
series with finite negative support. Conversely every Laurent series
with finite negative support lies in the localization of $\Lambda$
modulo $2^M$, since $t$ is invertible there. Inverse limits prove the
expansion; reduction modulo successive powers of $2$ gives the
valuation formula.

Lemma 21 (uniform pole bound). Let $A_1,\ldots,A_m$ be square
matrices over $\Lambda$ with nonzero residual determinants and
$\ord_t(\overline{\det A_j})\le h$. Put $A=\diag(A_1,\ldots,A_m)$.
If $B=A+2E_1$ with $E_1$ a matrix over $\Lambda$, then $B$ is
invertible over $O$. Modulo $2^M$, every entry of $B^{-1}$ has no
powers below $-Mh$, independently of $m$ and the matrix sizes. The
same bound holds for a Schur complement $D=P-QB^{-1}S$ when $P,Q,S$
have entries in $\Lambda$. If a block over $R$ augments to $A$, it is
invertible over $O[G]$, and the bound applies uniformly at all sign
evaluations.

Proof. Write $\det A_j=t^{h_j}v_j+2w_j$ with $h_j\le h$,
$v_j\in\Lambda^\times$, $w_j\in\Lambda$, and put
$C=\diag(\operatorname{adj}(A_j)/(t^{h_j}v_j))$. Every entry of $C$ has
pole at most $h$, and $BC=1+2F_1$ with every entry of $F_1$ pole at
most $h$. Thus
$$
B^{-1}\equiv C\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}(-2F_1)^j\pmod{2^M}.
$$
The $j$th summand has pole at most $(j+1)h\le Mh$; matrix products do
not increase a pole bound, and neither does multiplication by
power-series matrices. Since $O[G]$ is local with augmentation ideal
and residual determinant a unit, the block is invertible; each sign
evaluation differs from augmentation by a $2$-divisible matrix, to
which the same bound applies. The Schur formula closes the argument.

Lemma 22 (bounded clearing factor). Let $D$ be a based bounded
complex of finite free $O[G]$-modules, of total graded rank at most
$r$, whose augmentation $D_0$ is a complex over $\Lambda$. Suppose
the rational specialization of $D_0$ at $t=0$ has one of two
cohomology patterns: dimension one in degrees one and two and zero
elsewhere; or dimension two in degree one and zero elsewhere. Suppose
the total torsion length in the integral specialized cohomology is at
most $h_1$.

In the first pattern, take an integral degree-one cycle $z$ and a
closed functional $\lambda\in2^{-a_0}(D^2)^*$, and suppose each
character constituent over the fraction field of $O$ is either
acyclic or has the same pattern. Let $u_x$ be the determinant tensor
of the cohomology classes, with value zero in the acyclic case. In the
second pattern, take two cycles whose denominator exponents sum to at
most $a_0$, suppose each constituent has the second pattern, and let
$u_x$ be their wedge determinant coordinate. In either case the
coordinate may be multiplied by any one element
$\eta\in O[G]$.

Then there are $f,d\in O[G]$ with $d_x=f_xu_x$ at every character,
such that $f$ is $2^{a_0}$ times a polynomial of bounded degree in the
differential entries of $D$ (the degree depending only on $r$), and
$$
f_0\in\Lambda,\qquad f_0(0)\ne0,\qquad
v(f_0(0))\le C(r,h_1,a_0).
$$
If at every fixed precision the entries of $D_x$ have a uniform bound
on negative Laurent support, so do the $f_x$. No such bound is
required for the $d_x$, the cycles, or $\eta$.

Proof. Over $\Ztwo$, put the specialized complex into a sum of free
cohomology terms and one-dimensional two-term complexes with
differentials $2^{a_j}$, by elementary-divisor decomposition; the sum
of the positive $a_j$ is at most $h_1$. Lift the constant integral
basis changes to $D$ and cancel the same pivot positions formally,
retaining their pivot numerators as denominators; the number of
operations is at most $r$, and each pivot and basis change is rational
in the differential entries. Let $P_1$ be a product of sufficiently
high powers of the successive pivot numerators so that every
denominator in these changes and in the determinant comparison divides
$P_1$. Its degree is bounded in terms of $r$, and $P_{1,0}(0)$ is
nonzero with valuation bounded in terms of $r,h_1$.

Over $O[G][1/P_1]$, the first pattern leaves a complex
$[O[G]\xrightarrow{\delta}O[G]]$ in degrees one and two; letting
$\alpha,\beta$ be the projected cycle and closed functional, the
determinant tensor including the cancelled-pivot factor is a rational
expression in $D,z,2^{a_0}\lambda$. A field constituent with
$\delta\ne0$ gives $\alpha=0$ by the cycle identity
$\delta\alpha=0$, so the same expression is zero as stipulated. In
the second pattern the remaining complex is $O[G]^2[-1]$, and its
coordinate is the determinant of the two projected cycles times the
pivot factor. After denominator clearing, for every constituent with
$P_{1,x}\ne0$,
$$
u_x=2^{-a_0}\eta_xQ_{1,x}/P_{1,x},\qquad Q_1\in O[G].
$$
Set $f=2^{a_0}P_1^2$ and $d=\eta P_1Q_1$. This gives $d_x=f_xu_x$
where $P_{1,x}\ne0$; where a pivot numerator first vanishes both
sides are zero, so the identity holds on every character. Since $f$
involves only the bounded number of differential entries in the pivot
calculation, its degree, augmentation valuation, and pole bounds are
as asserted. The factor $\eta$ sits in $d$, never in $f$.

Lemma 23 (finite coefficient congruences). Let $A_1(x),\ldots,A_q(x)
\in\Ztwo$ be specified Laurent coefficients of the evaluations of
elements of $O[G]$. If $b>q(2^{M-1}-1)$, then some $x\ne0$ satisfies
$A_i(x)\equiv A_i(0)\pmod{2^M}$ for every $i$.

Proof. For one coefficient, write
$$
A(x)=\sum_{g\in G}a_g(-1)^{g\cdot x}
=\sum_{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,b\}}c_Sx_S,\qquad
x_S=\prod_{i\in S}x_i,
$$
with $2^{|S|}\mid c_S$, expanding each sign as a product of
$1-2x_i$. The $j$th binary digit of $z\in\Ztwo$ is
$\delta_j(z)=\binom{z}{2^j}\bmod2$; this holds on nonnegative integers
and extends to $\Ztwo$ by continuity. In the ring of functions on the
cube with coefficients in $\F_2[[Z]]$,
$$
(1+Z)^{A(x)}
=(1+Z)^{c_\varnothing}
\prod_{S\ne\varnothing}\left(1+x_S\big((1+Z)^{c_S}-1\big)\right).
$$
The nonconstant factor indexed by $S$ starts at degree at least
$2^{|S|}$ in $Z$. A term contributing to $Z^{2^j}$ for $j\ge1$ has
Boolean degree at most $\sum_S|S|\le\sum_S2^{|S|-1}\le2^{j-1}$. The
zeroth digit is constant. The congruences in the statement are
therefore equations over $\F_2$ of total degree at most
$q\sum_{j=1}^{M-1}2^{j-1}=q(2^{M-1}-1)$, vanishing at zero. Their
common zero count is even: modulo two it is the sum over the cube of
the product of $1+Q$ over all defining equations $Q$, and every
monomial has degree below $b$, so it omits a variable and sums to
zero. This is the Chevalley-Warning argument (Chevalley 1935; Warning
1935). The zero solution has a nonzero companion.

Lemma 24 (transfer through a bounded clearing factor). Let
$f,d\in O[G]$ and $u_x\in\Lambda[1/2]$ satisfy $d_x=f_xu_x$ at each
character. Suppose $f_0\in\Lambda$ and $c_0=v(f_0(0))<\infty$. Fix
$M>c_0$, and suppose every $f_x\bmod2^M$ has no powers below
$-L(M)$. If
$$
b>(L(M)+2)(2^{M-1}-1),
$$
some $x\ne0$ satisfies
$$
v\bigl(u_x(0)-u_0(0)\bigr)\ge M-2c_0.
$$
Consequently, when $c_0$ and each $L(M)$ have bounds independent of
the family, a uniform upper bound $v(u_x(0))\le B_1$ at all nonzero
vertices forces $v(u_0(0))\le B_1$ for all sufficiently large $b$.
The base value itself is allowed to vary with the family.

Proof. Apply Lemma 23 to the coefficients of $f_x$ in degrees
$-L(M),\ldots,0$ and to the constant coefficient of $d_x$. At the
supplied nonzero solution every negative coefficient of $f_x$ is
divisible by $2^M$, and its constant coefficient is congruent to
$f_0(0)$; its constant coefficient has valuation $c_0$, so
$v_O(f_x)\le c_0$. Since $d_x\in O$, the identity $d_x=f_xu_x$ gives
$v_O(u_x)\ge-c_0$; the same bound holds for $u_0$. By Lemma 20 all
coefficients are in $2^{-c_0}\Ztwo$, and both have zero negative
coefficients because they lie in $\Lambda[1/2]$. The constant-term
convolution gives
$$
[t^0]d_x\equiv([t^0]f_x)u_x(0)\pmod{2^{M-c_0}},\qquad
[t^0]d_0=f_0(0)u_0(0).
$$
Combining with the imposed coefficient congruences and dividing by
$f_0(0)$ yields the claimed valuation inequality. For the
consequence, choose $M>\max\{c_*,B_1+2c_*\}$ for a bound $c_*$ on
$c_0$ and take $b$ above the stated threshold; if the base valuation
exceeded $B_1$, the transferred nonzero valuation would also exceed
$B_1$, a contradiction. The pivot valuation affects the required
precision and dimension, not the transferred upper bound.

Lemma 25 (finite Artin models). Let $(A,\mathfrak m,k)$ be an Artin
local ring, and $D$ a complex of flat $A$-modules. Suppose
$H^*(D\otimes_A k)$ is finite dimensional and supported in $[a,b]$.
There is a bounded finite free complex $C$ with
$\operatorname{rank}_A C^j=\dim_k H^j(D\otimes_A k)$ and a strong
deformation retraction
$$
i:C\to D,\qquad p:D\to C,\qquad pi=1,\qquad 1-ip=dh+hd.
$$
Any fixed finite collection of chain maps, homotopies, triangles, and
perfect pairings transports to these models.

Proof. Flat modules over an Artin local ring are free, including
modules of infinite rank: lift a basis of $M/\mathfrak m M$ to a flat
module $M$ and map a free module onto $M$; the cokernel is zero
because $\mathfrak m$ is nilpotent, and flatness plus nilpotence force
the kernel to be zero. Split the residual complex as its cohomology
(with zero differential) plus contractible two-term disks, and lift
the graded bases; these lifts are graded isomorphisms, because an
inverse modulo $\mathfrak m$ lifts to an inverse by a finite geometric
series. Let $d_0$ be the lifted disk differential and $d=d_0+\epsilon$
with every coefficient of $\epsilon$ in $\mathfrak m$. For the disk
contraction $(i_0,p_0,h_0)$ arrange $h_0^2=h_0i_0=p_0h_0=0$ and
$1-i_0p_0=d_0h_0+h_0d_0$. The operators $1+h_0\epsilon$ and
$1+\epsilon h_0$ have finite geometric inverses, and the homological
perturbation lemma gives
$$
d_C=p_0\epsilon(1+h_0\epsilon)^{-1}i_0,\quad
i=(1+h_0\epsilon)^{-1}i_0,\quad
p=p_0(1+\epsilon h_0)^{-1},\quad
h=h_0(1+\epsilon h_0)^{-1};
$$
substitution using $(d_0+\epsilon)^2=0$ proves the contraction
identities (Crainic 2004, Sections 2.2-2.4 and 3.2). Because every
geometric series terminates at the nilpotence exponent of
$\mathfrak m$, the formulas are valid as module homomorphisms. A map
$f$ transports as $p'fi$, retaining contraction homotopies; a
quasi-isomorphism between bounded free complexes has a contractible
cone and a homotopy inverse, so finite diagrams including triangles
and perfect dualities transport.

The Heegner construction builds one determinant coordinate from a
Heegner point and its paired functional. Its central value measures
the index of the point after the Selmer and local contributions have
been subtracted. Local switches control its divisors, an outer limit
supplies the uniform ring bound when residual irreducibility is
unavailable, and a universal binary family supplies interpolation.

Proof of Proposition 8. The explicit Gross-Zagier formula for ring
classes (Cai, Shu, and Tian 2014, Theorem 1.1, pp. 2524-2525) applies.
Automorphic induction of the quadratic character gives the product
$L(E^{(e)},s)L(E^{(ek)},s)$ on the right. Discriminant and conductor
are prime to $N$, and the classical split Heegner hypothesis holds,
so the shared-prime corrections in the general formula are absent. The
unit-index convention is constant in the fields retained here. With
a character sum, solving the formula for the height gives
$C_Ec\sqrt{|k|}$ exactly. The forward Gross-Zagier-Kolyvagin theorem
gives the rank and finiteness assertion (Gross and Zagier 1986;
Kolyvagin 1990).

For the comparison of the two constructions, both free points are
compared to the free line on $E^{(h)}(\Q)$. Its regulator cancels
from the ratio of heights, since absolute canonical height is
unchanged by twist identification; this height invariance follows
from the canonical-height limit and the degree formula for heights
under morphisms (Milne 2021, Chapter IV, Lemmas 4.1 and 4.5-4.6,
Remark 4.12). The remaining variable factor is
$\sqrt{|hk|}L(E^{(hk)},1)$, whose infinity sign is that of $h_*$.
Period and lattice changes have bounded 2-valuation. If the rank-one
line is instead that of the fixed curve $E^{(h_*)}$, its regulator is
fixed and the same calculation gives the reversed comparison.

Proof of Lemma 10 (base Selmer). Restriction, twisting, and
addition or subtraction of conjugates give maps in both directions
between the Selmer group over $K$ and the direct sum of
$\Sel_{2^\infty}(E^{(h_0)}/\Q)$ and $\Sel_{2^\infty}(E^{(h_0k)}/\Q)$
whose composites are multiplication by 2. They induce isogenies on
divisible subgroups, and on the finite quotients the kernel of either
induced map is killed by 2: subtract a divisible-lift to lift a
divisible image, then use the composite. The generator dimensions
are uniformly bounded: the mod-2 Selmer group over $K$ is bounded by
Kummer cohomology over $K(E[2])$, a quadratic extension of a fixed
field with a bounded number of ramified primes, and Kummer theory
bounds the generator number of the corresponding pro-2 group over the
fixed field; the index-two subgroup bound preserves it. The fixed
local support includes that of $h_0$. The finite quotient on the
displayed product is bounded by hypothesis, so its counterpart over
$K$ has bounded length as well. For a finite collection of fields,
finiteness of each quotient is enough.

Finite cochain models with their maps. Write $T=T_2E$ and
$\Lambda=\Ztwo[[t]]$. For a quadratic squareclass $h$, put
$T^h=T\otimes\chi_h$. The imaginary quadratic field $K$ satisfies the
split Heegner hypothesis at $2N$; omit the two fields with extra units
and, when $E$ has complex multiplication, its CM field. A quadratic
character $\chi$ comes from a quadratic twist over $\Q$. Use the rings
$A_i=(\Z/2^i\Z)[t]/(t^i)$, and the same constructions over $A_i[G]$
and $\Lambda[G]$ for a fixed finite elementary abelian 2-group $G$.
Statements uniform in the number of generators of $G$ are identified
separately; finite-diagram compactness is a statement for fixed $G$.

Continuous inhomogeneous cochains into a finite free module over an
Artin local ring are flat, being the filtered union of finite free
modules of locally constant functions on finite clopen partitions.
The reduction is the cochain complex with reduced coefficients; the
same holds for restrictions, mapping cones, and local conditions
given by finite free complexes. In the applications the groups are
Galois groups of totally imaginary number fields or nonarchimedean
local fields, whose 2-cohomological dimension and the usual finiteness
theorems give the bounded residual cohomology of Lemma 25.

For an inner limit, the quadratic field $K$, the active conductor
support, the finite group $G$, and the number of derivative primes
are fixed. The residual representation is $E[2]$; all scalar
characters and group-like coefficients become trivial in the residue
field. Put $F=K(E[2])$. The number of places of $F$ in the permitted
set $S_i$ is bounded even when auxiliary primes move. Kummer theory
bounds $H^1(G_{F,S_i},\F_2)$ by the $S_i$-units modulo squares and
$\Cl(F)[2]$, whose dimensions are bounded by the unit rank, $\#S_i$,
and the fixed class-group dimension. Hochschild-Serre for the fixed
group $\Gal(F/K)$ then bounds the residual global $H^1$ for $E[2]$;
the global Euler characteristic bounds $H^2$, and $H^0$ is already
bounded. Local cohomology and the specified local conditions have
bounded residual dimensions, since the local degrees of $K$ are at most
two and the number of places is bounded. The target Selmer cones over
$K$ therefore have uniformly bounded finite-model ranks in this inner
limit. This bound may depend on the fixed active support; the
stronger outer bound is proved later.

Lemma 26 (limits of finite diagrams). Suppose diagrams of bounded
finite free complexes over $A_i$, or over $A_i[G]$, have a common
bounded cohomological degree interval and bounded ranks in every
degree, together with the chain homotopies and inverses for every
asserted identity or equivalence. Along a cofinal subsequence, or a
nonprincipal ultrafilter, their matrices converge to the same diagram
of perfect complexes over $\Lambda$ or $\Lambda[G]$. The contractions
into the original cochains are retained. Adding finitely many new
objects or maps does not require changing already chosen models for
the old objects. Finite-stage arithmetic operations with unbounded
source complexes are permitted when only finitely many of their
outputs and the witnesses to their identities are retained in the
bounded target complexes.

Proof. There are finitely many possible graded ranks; fix them on an
ultrafilter-large set. At a fixed precision $j$, all matrix entries
belong to the finite ring $A_j$ (or $A_j[G]$) and have unique
ultrafilter limits, compatible as $j$ varies. Matrix identities,
including the homotopy-inverse identities, are finite polynomial
identities and pass to the limit. New models and maps are mapped to
old models by their already fixed contractions; passing to a slower
cofinal precision gives the same old limits. Fix this ultrafilter once
for the all-sequence statements, keeping the original contraction at
every stage; later arithmetic diagrams must extend these same stages.

For the last assertion, let $D_i$ be an original target cochain
complex with contraction $(\iota_i,\pi_i,h_i)$ onto the bounded finite
model $C_i$. Transfer and scalar weighting are performed at stage $i$
before applying this contraction. A resulting cycle $z_i'\in D_i^1$
gives $z_i=\pi_i z_i'\in C_i^1$, and
$z_i'-\iota_i z_i=d(h_i z_i')$, so its relation to the original
arithmetic cocycle is retained. If an identity in the target is
$u_i'-v_i'=dw_i'$, retain the vectors $\pi_i u_i',\pi_i v_i',\pi_i w_i'$;
the corresponding identity is still exact. For identities involving
maps between target complexes, use the transported maps
$\pi_i'f_i\iota_i$ and their contraction homotopies. Local lifts,
closed degree-two functionals, and witnesses to specialization,
duality, and switch identities are treated the same way: they are
finitely many vectors or matrices of bounded size, whose entries and
differentials have simultaneous limits. Neither a free model of the
arithmetic source nor the entire transfer map from that source is
included in the limiting diagram.

Integral local conditions and duality. Let $F$ be a finite extension
of a nonarchimedean local field of characteristic zero, and $B/F$ any
elliptic curve. Put $M_F=B(F)^\wedge_2=\varprojlim_n B(F)/2^nB(F)$.

Lemma 27 (integral Kummer local condition). There is a perfect local
condition $U_F=M_F[-1]\to R\Gamma(F,T_2B)$ such that for every $n\ge1$,
$$
H^0(U_F\otimes^{\mathbf L}\Z/2^n)=B(F)[2^n],\qquad
H^1(U_F\otimes^{\mathbf L}\Z/2^n)=B(F)/2^nB(F).
$$
Its map in degree zero is the isomorphism onto $H^0(F,B[2^n])$, its
map in degree one is the finite Kummer injection, and it is its own
exact orthogonal complement under local Tate duality, for every
reduction type and with no hypothesis on $B[2]$.

Proof. The local analytic description of $B(F)$ shows that $M_F$ is a
finitely generated $\Ztwo$-module, with torsion the finite group
$B(F)[2^\infty]$; the free resolution has length at most one. Also
$H^0(F,T_2B)=0$, since $B(F)[2^\infty]$ has bounded exponent. The
integral Kummer injection into $H^1(F,T_2B)$ therefore defines the
morphism using the truncation in degrees at most one of
$R\Gamma(F,T_2B)$. Tensor the free resolution with $\Z/2^n$; its two
cohomology groups are $M_F[2^n]$ and $M_F/2^nM_F$, and inverse limits
of the ordinary Kummer sequences identify their maps as asserted.

For orthogonality, compose cup product and the Weil pairing with the
local invariant map: a morphism
$M_F[-1]\otimes^{\mathbf L}M_F[-1]\to\Ztwo[-2]$ is determined by its
homomorphism $M_F\otimes M_F\to\Ztwo$. The only possible
$\operatorname{Tor}_1$ lies in degree one and gives no extra
degree-zero morphism, and the indicated homomorphism vanishes because
the finite Kummer image annihilates itself at every level by local
Tate duality. Choose a nullhomotopy in free models. The induced
morphism
$$
R\Gamma(F,T_2B)/U_F\to
R\operatorname{Hom}_{\Ztwo}(U_F,\Ztwo)[-2]
$$
is a quasi-isomorphism; this can be checked after reduction modulo 2,
where degree one is the perfect pairing of the finite Kummer quotient
with the Kummer image and degree two is dual to $B(F)[2]$. Its perfect
cone is therefore zero by derived Nakayama. This proves exact
orthogonality and compatibility with derived reduction, and is
precisely the finite-module duality input of Nekovar 2006,
Proposition 5.2.4.

Where the scalar deformation is trivial, extend this local condition
by derived scalar extension to $A_i$ or $A_i[G]$. At an unramified
good prime use the inflated residue-field complex
$U_v=[M\xrightarrow{\Fr_v-1}M]$ in degrees 0 and 1, whose quotient in
full local cochains is the singular local complex. The full local
condition is the identity on local cochains; its orthogonal strict
condition is zero. At a derivative prime the used conditions are the
following specified truncations. In the split local model write
$$
L_v=D^0\oplus(F_v\oplus S_v)[-1]\oplus D^2[-2],
$$
with zero differential, $D^0$ dual to $D^2$, and the finite and
transverse planes $F_v,S_v$ isotropic. Put
$U_{\mathrm{str}}=D^0$,
$U_{\mathrm{rel}}=D^0\oplus(F_v\oplus S_v)[-1]$, $U_F=D^0\oplus F_v[-1]$,
$U_S=D^0\oplus S_v[-1]$. Then
$U_{\mathrm{str}}^\perp=U_{\mathrm{rel}}$, and each plane condition is
its own orthogonal complement. Every displayed condition maps
isomorphically onto local $H^0$ and injectively into local $H^1$,
satisfying the detection hypotheses below. Their finite arithmetic
realization and the isotropic-plane nullhomotopies are part of the
derivative construction; these truncations are not identified with
the full local condition.

For a finite permitted set $S$ the Selmer complex is the
cone
$$
C_S=\operatorname{Cone}\left(
R\Gamma(G_{K,S},M)\oplus\bigoplus_{v\in S}U_v
\to\bigoplus_{v\in S}R\Gamma(K_v,M)\right)[-1].
$$
At $2N$ take the Kummer conditions just constructed; restriction and
localization triangles are the triangles of this cone. The finite
rings $A_i$ and $A_i[G]$ are Frobenius coefficient rings, or the
duality maps may be checked after reduction to the residue field.
Exact local orthogonality and global duality therefore give
$$
C_S\simeq R\operatorname{Hom}(C_S^\perp,A_i)[-3],
$$
with the appropriate dual representation. This uses the orthogonality
hypotheses of Nekovar 2006, Theorem 6.3.4; the theorem is not used to
assert the existence of a varying-prime limit. Complex places
contribute zero Tate complexes. If the character is inverted by the
nontrivial automorphism of $K/\Q$, conjugate transport followed by the
Weil pairing identifies the two representations over the same scalar
ring. All maps and nullhomotopies in this paragraph are finite
diagrams to which Lemma 26 applies.

Moving conductor character. The conductor character must preserve the
local conditions at $2N$ while making the new local terms invertible
after reduction modulo 2 and inversion of $t$. The following
proposition supplies those local values; its bounds on Tate Frobenius
control the contribution of the moving prime when the family is
specialized to $t=0$.

Proposition 28 (ring-class characters with prescribed local values).
Fix finitely many split primes of $K$ above odd rational primes,
outside $2N$, and orient one prime in each conjugate pair. There are
distinct split rational primes $r_i$ avoiding all the fixed data and
characters
$$
\xi_i:\Gal(H_{r_i}/K)\to\Z/2^{n_i}\Z,\qquad n_i\to\infty,
$$
such that $\xi_i$ is surjective on the inertia quotient at $r_i$, is
zero at the primes above $2N$, and has odd value at each prescribed
oriented prime. One can require $r_i\to1$ in $\Ztwo$ and
$$
v_2(2+a_{r_i}(E)),\quad v_2(2-a_{r_i}(E)),\quad
v_2\det(1\pm\Fr_{r_i}\mid T)\le c_E,
$$
with $c_E$ independent of the list and of $K$.

Proof. First choose prime ideals $\mathfrak p_1,\ldots,\mathfrak p_s$
with pairwise disjoint conjugate pairs, including the oriented
prescribed primes and the primes above $2N$, and enough additional
split prime ideals to generate $\Cl(K)$, all avoiding any fixed
finite set. Put weight $w_j=1$ at a prescribed odd prime and $w_j=0$
at every additional prime and at every prime above $2N$; the
conjugate weight is $-w_j$. Let $R=\ker(\Z^s\to\Cl(K))$, choose a
basis $m^{(a)}$ of $R$, and choose $\beta_a\in K^\times$ with
$(\beta_a)=\prod_j\mathfrak p_j^{m_j^{(a)}}$.

Work first over $F_\infty=K(\mu_{2^\infty})$ and adjoin $2^n$-th roots
of the finitely many $\beta_a/\overline\beta_a$. Prescribe on these
roots the multipliers
$$
\zeta_{2^n}^{\,-\sum_j m_j^{(a)}w_j}.
$$
The minus sign fixes the choice of connecting map in the ring-class
sequence; reversing it throughout is harmless. These prescriptions
respect every Kummer relation: if
$\prod_a(\beta_a/\overline\beta_a)^{b_a}\in(F_\infty^\times)^{2^n}$,
take its valuation at a prime above each $\mathfrak p_j$ of nonzero
weight; those primes are unramified in $F_\infty/K$, so $2^n$ divides
$\sum_a b_am_j^{(a)}$ for each such $j$, proving compatibility.
Primes over 2 have weight zero and impose no division by a ramification
index; units impose no additional weighted relation. Kummer duality
supplies the prescribed automorphism of the radical extension over
$F_\infty$.

At a rational prime $r$ split in a sufficiently large finite
cyclotomic subfield, the power-residue character of $\F_r^\times$ is
surjective onto $\mu_{2^n}$. The split ring-class exact sequence
$$
1\to(\mathcal O_K/r\mathcal O_K)^\times/(\Z/r\Z)^\times
\to\operatorname{Pic}(\Z+r\mathcal O_K)\to\Cl(K)\to1
$$
has first group $\F_r^\times$ with principal relation measured by
$\beta/\overline\beta$ at the oriented prime over $r$. Consequently
the Kummer prescriptions say exactly that its primitive power-residue
character extends to the class group with the specified values $w_j$.
The inertia group and the chosen ideal classes present the ring-class
group, and the basis of $R$ lists its additional relations; these
relations are imposed on the inertia character itself, without
dividing by a class-group order to extend a previously fixed
character. This construction introduces no denominator, even when the
class number has a large 2-part. The class group and the number of
prescribed primes change the finite Chebotarev extension, but not the
integrality of the character. Since the ring-class extension is
unramified at $2N$, value zero on Frobenius means that the local
character is trivial there; conjugation acts by inversion.

The point index is not itself the quantity transferred across a growing
family. Each new conductor prime changes the local Selmer terms as well
as the point. These changes combine in a determinant coordinate, whose
central valuation equals twice the point index less the finite Selmer
length and the conductor contribution. The index estimates therefore
reduce to lower bounds for that coordinate.

Retain a primitive genus or ring instance, with point $P_c(e)$ over
$K$. Use the conductor characters just constructed, taking $n_i\ge2i$,
and the representations $T\chi_e\Psi_i$. Retain the same auxiliary
primes and the same fixed inner ultrafilter $\mathcal U_{\mathrm{in}}$.

The Selmer complex $C$ is a coefficientwise limit of the corresponding
finite free cochain models along $\mathcal U_{\mathrm{in}}$. It has the
integral Kummer condition at $2N$, the full condition at $r_i$ and at
the active good conductor primes, and the unramified condition at every
other permitted finite place; the full condition means that no
restriction is imposed on local cochains. Over
$\operatorname{Frac}(\Lambda)$ the modified full local complexes are
acyclic, so the full conditions agree with their orthogonal strict
conditions. Global duality, followed by conjugate transport and the
Weil pairing, therefore gives a perfect pairing
$$
H^1(C\otimes_\Lambda\operatorname{Frac}(\Lambda))
\times H^2(C\otimes_\Lambda\operatorname{Frac}(\Lambda))
\to\operatorname{Frac}(\Lambda),
$$
with conjugation in $K/\Q$ inverting the ring-class character.

Fix the single integer multiplier $L$ of Lemma 29. It depends on the
curve, parametrization, and fixed local types, and clears descent and
local Kummer ambiguities for the complete conductor; its valuation does
not grow with the number of primes. At each finite stage transfer the
Kummer class of $y_{cr_i}$ from $H_{cr_i}$ to $K$ with the specified
scalar coefficients, using $Ly_{cr_i}$. Complete the finite coset sum
before contracting its target cocycle and local Kummer lifts into the
models over $K$. The resulting cycles $P_i$ give $P\in H^1(C)$ by
Lemma 26. Local Kummer conditions at $2N$ are preserved because
$\Psi_i$ is trivial there. At the center of the $i$th finite model the
norm relation has the factor
$$
b_i=a_{r_i}-\chi_e(\Fr_{\mathfrak r_i})
-\chi_e(\Fr_{\bar{\mathfrak r}_i}),\qquad
P_i\big|_{t=0}=\varepsilon_i b_i\kappa(LP_c(e)),
$$
where $\kappa$ denotes the Kummer class in the central comparison and
$\varepsilon_i$ is a unit; the equality holds modulo the finite
coefficient precision. Since $\chi_e$ is defined over $\Q$, its values
at conjugate Frobenius elements agree, so $b_i=a_{r_i}-2\chi_e(r_i)$.
To retain the central identity at the cochain level, let $B_i$ be the
reduction of a fixed finite model of the ordinary Kummer complex over
$K$, and $\beta_i:B_i\to C_i/(t)$ its comparison map to the relaxed
central complex. Choose a cycle $q_i$ representing $\kappa(LP_c(e))$.
The finite norm relation gives a boundary witness $v_i$ in the target
model with
$$
P_i\bmod t-\varepsilon_i b_i\beta_i(q_i)=dv_i.
$$
Retain $\beta_i,q_i,v_i$ and the target contraction homotopies on the
same stages and at the same coefficient precision as $P_i$. Then
$b=\lim_{\mathcal U_{\mathrm{in}}}b_i$ has bounded valuation and
$\varepsilon=\lim\varepsilon_i$ is a unit, so
$P|_{t=0}=\varepsilon b\kappa(LP_c(e))$ with $b\ne0$. The special
complex differs from the ordinary Kummer complex only by the
active-prime relaxed quotients and the limit of the singular quotient
at the single moving prime $r_i$, of bounded length; inflation from the
complexes omitting $r_i$ gives this comparison, and all modified local
complexes are rationally acyclic at the center, so pairings agree there
as well.

If the product has a simple zero, its rational special fiber has
one-dimensional cohomology in degrees one and two, and $P$ specializes
nontrivially by Gross-Zagier. The generic dimensions are at most these
special dimensions. The long exact sequence for multiplication by $t$
shows $H^1(C[1/2])[t]=0$, since the special $H^0$ vanishes. A torsion
class that disappeared over the generic field would be killed by a
power of $t$ after localization at $(t)$, so the nonzero special class
cannot disappear. Thus the generic ranks are one in degrees one and two,
$P$ spans $H^1$, and the rational rank is constant at $t=0$. If only
base Selmer corank one is known, the same dimension bound gives generic
ranks at most one; a zero generic point is allowed.

Definition 30 (determinant coordinate). Choose an integral basis of
$\det^{-1}_{\Lambda}C$. In generic rank one the element formed by $P$
in $H^1$ and the functional $\langle P,\cdot\rangle$ on $H^2$
determines a scalar $u(C,P)\in\operatorname{Frac}(\Lambda)$ in that
basis. Put $u=0$ if $P=0$ or the generic rank is zero. Integral changes
of determinant basis multiply $u$ by a unit.

In the simple-zero case, constancy of the rational cohomology rank at
$t=0$ makes $u$ regular in the local ring $\Lambda_{(t)}$, so its value
$u(0)\in\Qtwo$ is defined. This specialization is at $(t)$; the stronger
assertion $u\in\Lambda[1/2]$ will follow from the divisor estimates.

The determinant has a simple integral interpretation. On a rank-one
free Selmer line, the point and its paired functional each contribute
the point's index. Torsion in degree two subtracts its length from the
coordinate in the inverse determinant lattice: the complex
$[\Ztwo\xrightarrow{2^a}\Ztwo]$ in degrees one and two has inverse
determinant lattice mapping to $2^a\Ztwo$ in the rational determinant,
so the coordinate of a fixed rational tensor loses $a$ in valuation.
The next lemma carries this out with the actual local Kummer
conditions, and compares the Selmer length over $K$ with those of the
two rational twists.

Lemma 31 (central determinant valuation). For a simple-zero instance,
$$
v(u(0))=2j(P_c(e))
-\length\Sha(E^{(e)}/K)[2^\infty]
-\sum_{q\mid c}\sum_{v\mid q}t_v+O_E(1),
$$
where $t_v=\dim_{\F_2}E[2]^{\Fr_v}$. Furthermore,
$$
\length\Sha(E^{(e)}/K)[2^\infty]
+\sum_{q\mid c}\sum_{v\mid q}t_v
=s(e)+s(ek)+\sum_{q\mid k}t_q+2\sum_{q\mid c}t_q+O_E(1).
$$
The constants include the one fixed class multiplier and the bounded
local contribution at the moving prime $r_i$.

Proof. The ordinary integral Kummer complex over $K$ is self-dual; its
$H^1$ is the compact Selmer module, and the torsion in $H^2$ has the
length of the finite quotient of the discrete Selmer group by its
divisible subgroup. This follows by derived base change to
$\Qtwo/\Ztwo$, since the local complexes give exactly the discrete
Kummer conditions. In the simple-zero case that finite quotient is
$\Sha(E^{(e)}/K)[2^\infty]$. Duality pairs the free parts in degrees
one and two perfectly; torsion in degrees one and three costs a bounded
amount, by global torsion and duality, and the uniform torsion bound
comes from a fixed nonidentity Tate scalar acting trivially on every
ring-class field in the construction, whose scalar minus one
annihilates that torsion.

At an active odd conductor place inertia acts by minus identity. All
integral $H^1$ is Kummer, and the relaxed quotient contributes only
$H^2(K_v,T^e)$ in degree two, of length $t_v$ by local duality and the
fixed 2-torsion. At the moving prime $r_i$ the contribution is bounded.
The generic pairings specialize to the ordinary Kummer pairing because
the modified local complexes are rationally acyclic at the center.
Thus the free determinant tensor has valuation twice the point index,
while torsion in degree two contributes negative length to the inverse
determinant; with the trace formula this gives the first identity.

For the second identity, apply the isogeny invariance of the BSD
quotient to quadratic Weil restriction and
$E^{(e)}\times E^{(ek)}$ (Tate 1966, Theorem 2.1 and the preceding
discussion). All groups are finite apart from the known rank-one
lattices, so no BSD equality is assumed. Forward and reverse isogenies
have composites multiplication by 2, so rank-one lattice indices,
height ratios, torsion ratios, and period ratios contribute bounded
2-powers: pullback indices of Neron differentials at 2 are bounded by
those composites, and real kernels and component ratios are bounded as
well.

The remaining terms are Tamagawa numbers. A ramified quadratic twist of
good reduction at an odd prime $q$ has type $I_0^*$ and component
number one plus the number of roots of its reduced auxiliary cubic
(Barrios et al. 2025, Theorem 4.1, Table 4 and proof, Case 3). Write a
good integral model as $y^2=f(x)$ retaining the quadratic term, and the
twisting parameter as $d=\pi u$; the auxiliary cubic is
$\bar u^3\bar f(T/\bar u)$, separable, with roots corresponding to the
nonzero rational two-torsion points. The component number is
$\#E[2]^{\Fr_q}$, whose 2-valuation is $t_q$; the argument also applies
in residue characteristic 3. The connected reduction is unipotent, some
inertia element acts by $-1$ on the twisted 2-adic representation, and
the local 2-primary torsion is precisely its Frobenius-fixed 2-torsion.
Over an unramified extension the same formula holds with the Frobenius
of the enlarged residue field. At unramified or split places, the
Weil-restriction component numbers are the products over $v\mid q$. At
a prime $q\mid k$ outside $2N$ the curve over $K$ has good reduction
and its Weil restriction has connected special fiber: Weil restriction
of its Neron model is the Neron model of the restriction of the curve
(Bosch, Lutkebohmert, and Raynaud 1990, Section 7.6, Proposition 6),
whose special fiber has the original connected elliptic fiber as
quotient, with connected vector-group layers from ramification. Exactly
one rational twist has the ramified component factor. At a conductor
prime $q\mid c$ the two rational twists give a combined valuation
$2t_q$, and the corresponding contribution over $K$ is
$\sum_{v\mid q}t_v$. All places above $2N$ have bounded local
contributions, completing the second identity.

In a simple-zero genus instance, the fixed partner absorbs the terms
involving $h_*$, while $c=1$ and $k=hh_*$, so
$$
v(u(0))=2j(P_1(h_*))-2w(h)-s(h)+O_{E,h_*}(1).
$$
In a simple-zero ring instance with $\omega(k)\le a$, the discriminant
term is bounded and the conductor term is $4w(h)$, so
$$
v(u(0))=2j(P_{|h|}(h))-4w(h)-s(h)-s(hk)+O_{E,a}(1).
$$
The ring identity also applies to the interpolation families in which
an added nonsplit prime has $t_q=0$; such a prime contributes no weight.
The all-split hypothesis is needed for the uniform ring lower bound.
These formulas explain both the lower-bound statements and the
normalization in Heegner interpolation. To prove the lower bounds, it
suffices to show that $u$ has no negative divisor away from $(2)$ and
has uniformly bounded negative divisor at $(2)$ in the stated cases.
Integral local switches preserve its determinant valuation, and inner
detection chooses switches lowering excess Selmer rank. The uniform
ring estimate requires an additional characteristic-zero limit; the
universal binary family requires a bounded clearing factor after the
new local blocks have been removed.

The main intermediate estimate is
$$
v_{\mathfrak p}(u)\ge0\quad(\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak p=1,\
\mathfrak p\ne(2)),
$$
and $v_{(2)}(u)\ge-O_{E,h_*}(1)$ in genus data with irreducible $E[2]$,
while $v_{(2)}(u)\ge-O_{E,a}(1)$ in ring data with $\omega(k)\le a$
and all active primes split.

Evaluation and the exact kernel. The determinant estimates require
switches that detect the excess Selmer dimension at a height-one test.
To choose switching primes, a nonzero limiting Selmer class must still
be detected by Galois evaluation. Two lemmas first retain that
detection under coefficient specialization, then restrict it to
sequences acting trivially on the full Tate module, all ring-class
fields, and the finitely many auxiliary fields.
