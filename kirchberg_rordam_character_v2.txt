----------------------------------------------------------------
PART 0.  A GUIDED TOUR
----------------------------------------------------------------

0.1  THREE EXAMPLES OF ONE SHAPE

You know three useful kinds of objects:

  (a) the complex numbers C;
  (b) the n-by-n complex matrices M_n;
  (c) the continuous complex functions C(X) on a compact space X,
      for example X = [0,1].

In each you can add, multiply, multiply by complex scalars, take
an adjoint (conjugate, conjugate transpose, complex conjugate),
and measure size with a norm.  Each has a unit: the number 1, the
identity matrix, the constant function 1.  A Cstar-algebra is
what these three have in common.

0.2  CSTAR-ALGEBRAS

A Cstar-algebra is a set A with operations +, ., scalar
multiplication by C, an adjoint a -> a*, a norm ||.||, and a
distinct element 1, such that

  (i)   A is a complex vector space, and 1 is a unit for . ;
  (ii)  multiplication is associative and complex-bilinear;
  (iii) (a*)* = a, (ab)* = b*a*, (a + lb)* = a* + conj(l)b*;
  (iv)  ||ab|| <= ||a|| ||b|| and ||1|| = 1;
  (v)   ||a*a|| = ||a||^2  (the Cstar identity);
  (vi)  A is complete for the norm.

Axiom (v) is the rigid one.  With (vi) it forces (Gelfand and
Naimark) every Cstar-algebra to be, up to isomorphism, a
norm-closed *-subalgebra of the bounded operators B(H) on some
Hilbert space.  We never use that theorem; the axioms are all we
need, and every construction below is an algebraic formula.

Two words that will recur.  An element a is self-adjoint if
a* = a, and positive if a = b*b for some b; positive elements are
exactly the self-adjoint ones with spectrum in [0,infinity), and
we write a >= 0 and a <= b for b - a >= 0.  A *-homomorphism is a
linear map preserving products and adjoints; if the algebras are
unital we require it to send 1 to 1.

0.3  CHARACTERS

A character of A is a nonzero *-homomorphism chi: A -> C.  Because
1 is a unit, chi(1) = 1 automatically: chi(1) = chi(1*1) =
chi(1)chi(1) and chi(1) is a nonzero complex number.

Examples.

  C has exactly one character, the identity.

  M_n has no characters once n >= 2.  Here is the whole proof.
  Let e_ij be the matrix unit with a single 1 in row i, column j.
  These obey e_ij e_kl = delta_jk e_il.  A character sends the
  self-adjoint projection e_ii to a real number c with c^2 = c,
  so c is 0 or 1; the sum of all chi(e_ii) is chi(1) = 1; so
  exactly one index j has chi(e_jj) = 1.  Pick k different from j.
  From e_jj = e_jk e_kj we get 1 = chi(e_jk)chi(e_kj), and from
  e_kk = e_kj e_jk we get 0 = chi(e_kj)chi(e_jk).  The two products
  are the same complex number.  Hence 1 = 0, a contradiction.

  C(X) with X compact Hausdorff: every character is evaluation at
  a point, chi(f) = f(x0).  So characters of C(X) are exactly the
  points of X.

The last example is the mental picture to keep.  Characters are
the one-dimensional points of an algebra; "A has no characters"
means "A has no one-dimensional points".  M_n is nonzero and has
none.

0.4  REPRESENTATIONS

A representation of A on a Hilbert space H is a *-homomorphism
pi: A -> B(H).  It is irreducible if the only closed subspaces of
H invariant under all pi(a) are 0 and H.

A one-dimensional irreducible representation is the same thing as
a character, because B(C) = C.  Hence

  A has no characters  <=>  A has no one-dimensional
                            irreducible representations.

Irreducible representations are the atoms of the theory: the
spectrum of A is the set of them modulo unitary equivalence, and
C(X) has spectrum X.  Most arguments below take the shape "it is
enough to test in every irreducible representation", and the
proof of fullness in 1.2 is the first instance.

0.5  TENSOR PRODUCTS

If A and B are Cstar-algebras, the algebraic tensor product A odot B
is the vector space spanned by symbols a odot b with
(a odot b)(a' odot b') = aa' odot bb' and (a odot b)* = a* odot b*.
It is a *-algebra but not complete; several norms complete it, and
two of them matter.

  The maximal tensor product A @max B completes A odot B in
  ||x||_max = sup ||(pi (x) sigma)(x)||, the supremum over all
  pairs of representations pi of A, sigma of B, acting on the
  Hilbert tensor product.  It is the largest completion, and it
  exists because the supremum is finite.

  The minimal (spatial) tensor product A @min B completes in the
  norm obtained from one faithful representation of each factor
  on H and H'.  It is the smallest completion and does not depend
  on the choices (this is Fell's theorem).

Always ||x||_min <= ||x||_max.  If the two agree for all x and all
B, A is nuclear.  Finite-dimensional algebras and commutative ones
are nuclear, and so is the matrix cone of 1.4; for those, max and
min agree and there is no ambiguity.  Nothing in this paper assumes
nuclearity of the algebras of interest.

The intuition: the maximal norm lets the two factors interact as
much as any pair of representations allows; the minimal norm lets
each factor see only its own Hilbert space.  For M_2 and M_3 the
result is M_6, because the algebraic tensor product of matrix
algebras is already complete for its unique norm.

0.6  CUNTZ COMPARISON

Let B be unital and let a, b be positive elements, allowed to sit
in finite matrix algebras over B.  Write a precsim b, "a is Cuntz
below b", if for every eps > 0 there is an element x (of the
matching finite matrix algebra over B) with

  || a - x* b x || <= eps.

The class of a is written [a]; the rules [a] <= [b] for a precsim b
and [a_1] + [a_2] = [diag(a_1,a_2)] make the classes of positive
elements of all finite matrix algebras over B into the Cuntz
semigroup Cu(B), with zero.

Only three facts are used in this whole document, and they are
elementary: they are stated here and proved in 1.3.

  (1)  [v*v] = [vv*] for every rectangular matrix v over B.

  (2)  [a_1 + ... + a_l] = [a_1] + ... + [a_l] whenever the a_i are
       positive and pairwise orthogonal.

  (3)  Comparison and addition survive *-homomorphisms, and survive
       tensoring with a fixed positive element c, in the sense that
       a precsim b gives a odot c precsim b odot c with the error
       multiplied by ||c||; this holds for maximal tensor products.

There is also the trivial but heavily used [a] <= [1_B] for every
a in B_+, with the exact witness x = a^(1/2).

No cancellation, no stable rank, and no comparison property of B
is ever invoked.  That is the point: everything below is a
first-order manipulation of these three facts.

0.7  FREE ULTRAFILTERS AND THE CENTRAL SEQUENCE ALGEBRA

Let N = {1,2,3,...}.  A filter on N is a family of subsets closed
under finite intersections and supersets, containing N and not the
empty set; it is an ultrafilter if it is maximal, and free if it
contains no finite set.  A free ultrafilter omega decides every
question, consistently: for every subset S, exactly one of S and
its complement lies in omega.  Think of "n in omega-large set" as
"for almost all n", with the twist that the notion of almost all is
chosen once and for all by omega.  Free ultrafilters exist by the
Axiom of Choice and cannot be written down; we only use the two
decision properties.

Now let A be a Cstar-algebra.

  bounded sequences:      l^inf(N,A), with pointwise operations
                          and the supremum norm
  null sequences:         J_omega = {(a_n) : lim_n->omega ||a_n|| = 0}
  norm ultrapower:        A_omega = l^inf(N,A) / J_omega
  central sequences:      F_omega(A) = A_omega intersected with A'

Here A embeds in A_omega as constant sequences, and A' is the
commutant of that copy inside A_omega.  Concretely, an element of
F_omega(A) is a bounded sequence (x_n) in A that asymptotically
commutes with every element of A, ||[x_n,a]|| -> 0 along omega for
every a in A, modulo sequences whose norms go to zero.

So F_omega(A) is the algebra of "almost central sequences" of A.
It is nonzero and unital whenever A is unital (the constant unit
sequence represents its unit).  The question of this paper is:
how much does this algebra remember about A?

0.8  THE JIANG-SU ALGEBRA Z

The Jiang-Su algebra Z, constructed by Jiang and Su in 1999, is a
nonzero unital separable simple Cstar-algebra with no characters,
K_0(Z) = Z, K_1(Z) = 0, and a tracial state.  Its striking property
is self-absorption: Z @min Z is isomorphic to Z, and likewise for
any finite number of factors.  It plays the role for Cstar-algebras
that the integers play for abelian groups, only subtler: it is what
you tensor with to remove regularity defects.

A Cstar-algebra A is Z-stable if A is isomorphic to A @min Z.
Z-stability is one of the standard regularity hypotheses in the
Elliott classification programme for simple separable unital
algebras.

The Kirchberg-Rordam question, Question 3.1 of their 2015 paper and
Problem LXXX of Schafhauser-Tikuisis-White, asks whether

  F_omega(A) has no characters

is equivalent to Z-stability of A.  The answer is yes, and this
document proves it.

0.9  THE DIMENSION-DROP ALGEBRA I(2,3)

Let M_2 @ M_3 = M_6.  Define

  I(2,3) = { f: [0,1] -> M_6 continuous :
             f(0) lies in M_2 @ 1_3,
             f(1) lies in 1_2 @ M_3 }.

This is a bundle of matrix algebras over the interval: the fibre
over t with 0 < t < 1 is M_6, but at the two endpoints the fibre
drops to M_2 and to M_3.  Hence the name.

Its irreducible representations are exactly evaluations at points
of [0,1], of dimension 2 at 0, 6 in the interior, and 3 at 1.  So
all irreducible dimensions are at most 6, and none is 1: I(2,3)
is subhomogeneous with bound 6 and has no characters.  That is the
sense in which it is a "one-dimensional-point-free" probe with a
concrete finite description; see 1.4 for the proof.

0.10  THE THREE THEOREMS

Every word in the following is now defined.

  MAIN THEOREM.  Let A be a nonzero unital separable complex
  Cstar-algebra and let omega be a free ultrafilter on N.  Then

    F_omega(A) has no characters

  if and only if

    A is isomorphic to A @min Z.

  FINITE THEOREM.  Let D be a nonzero unital complex Cstar-algebra
  without characters.  Then there is an integer m >= 1 and a unital
  *-homomorphism

    I(2,3) -> D^(x m).

  COROLLARY (infinite tensor powers).  Let D be a nonzero unital
  separable complex Cstar-algebra without characters and put
  B = D^(x_min infinity), the direct limit of the finite minimal
  tensor powers under x -> x @min 1_D.  Then B is isomorphic to
  B @min Z, and B contains a unital copy of Z.

The exponent m in the finite theorem depends on D; no uniform bound
is claimed, and none is needed.  None of the three statements
assumes nuclearity, simplicity, traces, finite nuclear dimension,
or any comparison property.

0.11  HOW THE PROOF GOES

The main theorem is a sandwich.  Kirchberg and Rordam proved that
Z-stability of A is equivalent to F_omega(A) containing, with the
same unit, a separable subhomogeneous subalgebra without characters
(Proposition 1.6 below).  So it suffices to produce such a
subalgebra from the bare hypothesis "no characters on F_omega(A)".

That is what the finite theorem does, with I(2,3) as the
subhomogeneous characterless algebra: a unital map I(2,3) -> D^(x m)
has an image that is a quotient of I(2,3), hence still
subhomogeneous and characterless; and the commuting-copies lemma
(Proposition 1.6(1)) pushes D^(x m) into F_omega(A).  The infinite
tensor power corollary then follows from Dadarlat-Toms.

The genuine work is therefore the finite theorem, and it runs in
two stages.

  STAGE A (PART 3).  Find a full element w with w^2 = 0 and
  ||w|| = 1 in a finite maximal tensor power P of D.  "Full" means
  w generates P as a closed two-sided ideal, equivalently (1.2)
  w survives every irreducible representation.  The tool is a
  family of selfadjoint "labels" in a tensor power of D that stays
  linearly independent under every irreducible representation of
  every further tensor product.  Linear independence replaces
  orthogonality, and lets finitely many commutator tests be welded
  into one element.

  STAGE B (PART 4).  Turn w into a unital map from the matrix cone
  C = {f in C([0,1],M_2) : f(0) in C 1_2}, with the first corner
  full; then use a continuous simplex of embeddings of M_2 into
  M_2^(x k), a cube, and a finite amplification, to produce an
  exactly equivalent orthogonal pair whose defect is Cuntz below a
  cutoff of one member.  The Rodam-Winter criterion (PART 2) then
  gives I(2,3) -> P^(x s).

The two stages are then assembled in PART 5, with the
Kirchberg-Rordam central-sequence inputs.

Nothing in this argument uses Gelfand-Naimark, the Elliott
classification, traces, or K-theory.

PART 1.  PRELIMINARIES
----------------------------------------------------------------

This part fixes conventions, proves the four elementary tools
(fullness, the Cuntz rules, the spectrum of I(2,3), the
commuting-copies lemma), and states the two inputs from the
literature.

1.1  CONVENTIONS

All algebras are complex and unital, with unit written 1 or 1_B.
All tensor products in the construction are maximal, and we write
D^(x m) for the maximal tensor power.  The algebraic tensor product
over C is odot; the Hilbert-space tensor product is (x).

A representation is a *-homomorphism into B(H).  It is irreducible
if 0 and H are the only closed invariant subspaces.  All
representations are taken nonzero and nondegenerate, so on a unital
algebra they are unital.  Schur's lemma is used twice: the
commutant of an irreducible image is C 1_H.

An element x of B is full if the closed two-sided ideal generated
by x is B.  A projection p is a self-adjoint idempotent.  For
b >= 0 we write Her(b) = closure(bBb), the closed two-sided ideal
generated by b.

1.2  FULLNESS, AND HOW TO TEST IT

Lemma.  Let B be unital and x in B.  Then x is full if and only if
pi(x) is nonzero for every irreducible representation pi of B.

Proof.  If the closed ideal J generated by x is proper, then B/J is
a nonzero Cstar-algebra, and every nonzero Cstar-algebra has a
nonzero irreducible representation; composing with the quotient
map B -> B/J gives an irreducible representation of B that
annihilates J and hence x.  Conversely, if an irreducible
representation annihilates x, then its kernel is a proper closed
two-sided ideal containing x, so the ideal generated by x is
proper.  QED

The moral, used twice below: to prove an element is full, test it
in every irreducible representation, where "nonzero" is the only
thing that can go wrong.

1.3  THE CUNTZ RULES

For positive elements a, b of finite matrix algebras over the unital
algebra B, write a precsim b when for each eps > 0 there is a
matching matrix x over B with ||a - x*bx|| <= eps.  Write [a] for
the class, [a] <= [b] for a precsim b, and [a_1] + [a_2] =
[diag(a_1,a_2)].

RULE (1).  [v*v] = [vv*] for every rectangular matrix v over B.

Proof.  Put b = v*v, c = vv*.  For gamma > 0 set
x_gamma = v (b + gamma*1)^(-1/2).  Then

  x_gamma* c x_gamma = (b+gamma)^(-1/2) v*v v*v (b+gamma)^(-1/2)
                     = b^2 (b + gamma)^(-1).

On the spectrum of b, the scalar inequality
|t^2/(t+gamma) - t| = gamma t/(t+gamma) <= gamma shows
||b^2(b+gamma)^(-1) - b|| <= gamma, so b precsim c.  Applying the
same computation with v* in place of v gives c precsim b.  QED

RULE (2).  If a_1,...,a_l are positive and pairwise orthogonal,
a_i a_j = 0 for i different from j, then
[a_1 + ... + a_l] = [a_1] + ... + [a_l].

Proof.  Let u be the l-by-1 column with entries a_i^(1/2).  Then
u*u = a_1 + ... + a_l and uu* = diag(a_1,...,a_l), so Rule (1)
gives [a_1 + ... + a_l] = [diag(a_1,...,a_l)], which is the sum by
definition.  QED

RULE (3).  Comparison and addition are preserved by *-homomorphisms
and by tensoring with a fixed positive element c, with the error
multiplied by ||c||.  This includes maximal tensor products.

Proof.  A *-homomorphism phi carries a witness x for a precsim b to
a witness phi(x) for phi(a) precsim phi(b).  Tensoring a witness
with c gives

  (x odot c^(1/2))*(b odot c)(x odot c^(1/2)) = (x*bx) odot c,

which differs from a odot c by at most eps||c||.  Direct sums are
untouched.  The use of the unit of the second factor is what makes
this work in the maximal tensor product: no embedding of either
factor into a common algebra is required.  QED

Also used repeatedly: [a] <= [1_B] for every a in B_+, exactly,
with the witness a^(1/2).

1.4  THE TWO PROBE ALGEBRAS

The matrix cone is

  C = { f in C([0,1], M_2) : f(0) in C 1_2 },

with the element iota(t) = t and the constant unit 1_C.  It is the
unitization of C_0((0,1], M_2), it is nuclear (unitization of a
nuclear algebra), and hence its tensor powers are unambiguous:
C^(x k) = C^(x_min k).

The dimension drop is

  I(2,3) = { f in C([0,1], M_2 @ M_3) :
             f(0) in M_2 @ 1_3, f(1) in 1_2 @ M_3 }.

Lemma.  Every irreducible representation of I(2,3) is evaluation at
some t in [0,1], of dimension 2 at t = 0, 6 for 0 < t < 1, and 3 at
t = 1.  In particular I(2,3) is separable, subhomogeneous with
bound 6, and has no characters.

Proof.  The scalar functions sit in I(2,3) as g 1_{M_6}, forming a
central copy of C([0,1]).  Let pi be an irreducible representation.
The image of that central copy is a commutative subalgebra of the
commutant of the irreducible image, hence is C 1_H; so its
restriction is a character of C([0,1]), that is, evaluation at some
t_0.  Let J be the closed ideal of I(2,3) generated by the elements
vanishing at t_0.  Each element of J is a norm limit of sums of
products g f with g vanishing at t_0; but pi(g 1) = g(t_0) 1 = 0,
so pi(J) = 0, and pi factors through I(2,3)/J.

The quotient I(2,3)/J is the fibre at t_0, namely M_2 when t_0 = 0,
M_6 when 0 < t_0 < 1, and M_3 when t_0 = 1.  An irreducible
representation of a full matrix algebra M_n is unique up to
equivalence and has dimension n.  Hence pi has the claimed
dimension.  QED

Consequently I(2,3) has irreducible dimensions 2, 3, 6, so no
one-dimensional ones, and every quotient I(2,3)/ker with nonzero
unit again has irreducible dimensions among 2, 3, 6 and no
characters: irreducible representations of the quotient are
irreducible representations of I(2,3).  This stability under
quotients is what makes I(2,3) usable in PART 5, where the map
I(2,3) -> F_omega(A) may well be non-injective.

1.5  THE RODAM-WINTER DIMENSION-DROP CRITERION

Proposition (Rodam-Winter).  Let B be a unital Cstar-algebra, let
a_1, a_2 in B be orthogonal positive contractions with

  a_1 = v*v,   a_2 = vv*

for a single element v in B, and suppose that for some eps > 0

  1_B - a_1 - a_2  precsim  (a_1 - eps)_+ .

Then there is a unital *-homomorphism I(2,3) -> B.

This is Proposition 5.1, (ii) => (iv), with n = 2 of Rodam-Winter.
We use it as an input.  PART 2 proves the three general tools the
criterion rests on, and says exactly what the citation supplies.

Two remarks.  First, orthogonality of a_1 and a_2 gives
a_1 + a_2 <= 1_B, so the defect in the display is positive and the
hypothesis says that the defect is dominated by a cutoff of a_1.
Second, the conclusion really is a unital *-homomorphism, not a
completely positive map: that is what the criterion buys, and it is
why the criterion is the right endpoint of the construction.

The stable rank one hypothesis that appears in other parts of the
cited proposition is needed there only for (i) => (ii).  It is not a
hypothesis of the implication used here, and nothing below assumes
it.

1.6  THE KIRCHBERG-RORDAM CENTRAL-SEQUENCE INPUTS

Fix a nonzero unital separable A and a free ultrafilter omega.

Proposition (Kirchberg-Rordam).  (1) If D is a unital separable
Cstar-subalgebra of F_omega(A) containing the ambient unit, then
for every m >= 1 there is a unital *-homomorphism

  D^(x m)  ->  F_omega(A).

(2) A is isomorphic to A @min Z if and only if F_omega(A) contains,
with the ambient unit, a separable subhomogeneous Cstar-subalgebra
without characters.

Statement (1) is the commuting-copies lemma, Lemma 9.3 of the
companion paper, whose primary source is Proposition 2.2 and
Corollary 2.3 of Kirchberg-Rordam; for unital A the annihilator
ideal used there vanishes, since an element that annihilates every
element of A annihilates its unit, so its F(A) is exactly the norm
central sequence algebra used here.  No nuclear dimension
hypothesis appears.  We prove (1) to make the quantifiers explicit.
Statement (2) is Theorem 2.5 of Kirchberg-Rordam; "subhomogeneous"
means a common finite bound on irreducible dimensions, and the
input imposes neither nuclearity of A nor a tracial ultrapower.

Proof of (1).  Fix a separable Cstar-subalgebra E of F_omega(A).
We construct a unital *-homomorphism D -> F_omega(A) intersected
with E', then iterate.

Choose a countable dense unital *-subalgebra D_0 of D over
Q + iQ.  For each x in D_0 choose a bounded lift (a_l^x)_l in A
with sup_l ||a_l^x|| <= 2||x||, taking the zero lift for x = 0 and
the constant unit lift for x = 1; such lifts exist by truncating
coordinate norms, which does not change the class in A_omega.

Along omega, each relation defect of these lifts tends to zero:
for every eps > 0 the set of l for which the additive, scalar,
multiplicative and adjoint relations hold within eps on a fixed
finite set of elements of D_0 belongs to omega.  So (a_l^x)_l is
asymptotically a unital *-homomorphism D_0 -> A along omega.

Choose a countable dense subset of A and, for a countable dense
subset of E, bounded lifts (b_s^j)_s indexed by the dense set j and
the coordinate s.  Enumerate all relation tests and all centrality
tests.  For each output index s choose a single old index l(s) so
that

  every relation of the first s tests holds at l(s) within 1/s, and
  ||[a_{l(s)}^x, b_s^j]|| <= 1/s for the first s pairs (x,j).

Each of these finitely many requirements holds on an omega-large set
of old indices l, so the finite intersection is nonempty and l(s)
exists.  For fixed s and x, each b_s^j is a fixed element of A, so
the centrality of the original class applies.

The new sequences (a_{l(s)}^x)_s are then central along omega, and
their classes satisfy all relations of D_0 in the limit.  They
therefore define a unital (Q+iQ)-linear *-homomorphism
D_0 -> F_omega(A) intersected with E', and the bound 2||x|| extends
it to a complex unital *-homomorphism D -> F_omega(A) intersected
with E'.

Iteration: starting from one copy D_1 of D, apply the result with
E = C*(D_1,1) to get a commuting copy D_2, then with
E = C*(D_1,D_2,1), and so on.  After m steps we have m mutually
commuting unital copies of D inside F_omega(A), and the universal
property of the maximal tensor product assembles them into a unital
map D^(x m) -> F_omega(A).  Injectivity of the individual copies is
irrelevant, and is not claimed: maximality only requires commuting
images.  QED

With PART 1 complete, PART 2 proves the criterion of 1.5, PART 3
builds the square-zero element, PART 4 converts a cone map into the
orthogonal pair the criterion needs, and PART 5 assembles.

PART 2.  THE DIMENSION-DROP CRITERION
----------------------------------------------------------------

This part proves the elementary tools that the dimension-drop
criterion rests on, states the criterion, and records exactly what
is taken from the literature.  Throughout, B is a unital
Cstar-algebra, B** is its double dual (the enveloping von Neumann
algebra, which contains B and is strong-operator closed), and
support projections and polar decompositions are taken in B**.
Whenever a constructed element is claimed to lie in B, that is said.

2.1  SUPPORT PROJECTIONS AND EXACT CUTS

For b >= 0 in B, supp(b) is the projection in B** onto the closure
of the range of b.  The facts used below: b = supp(b)b = b supp(b);
supp(b)f(b) = f(b) for continuous f with f(0) = 0; and supp(b) = 1
when b is invertible.

Lemma 2.1 (exact cut).  Let a, b in B_+ with a precsim b.  Then for
every delta > 0 there is z in B with

  z*z = (a - delta)_+     and     zz* in Her(b).

Proof.  A comparison witness can be taken in B itself: a witness x
in M_n(B) with ||a - x*(b (x) 1_n)x|| < eta has a column of entries,
and compressing to the (1,1) corner gives y in B with
||a - y*by|| < eta.  Fix eta with 0 < eta < delta and such an x, put

  e = (a - delta)_+,    y = b^(1/2) x e^(1/2),    T = y*y.

Since e is supported where a >= delta,

  T = e^(1/2) x*bx e^(1/2) >= e^(1/2)(a - eta)e^(1/2) >= (delta-eta)e,

while T <= ||x*bx|| e.  So e and T have the same support projection.

Let y = u T^(1/2) be the polar decomposition, so u*u = supp(T) =
supp(e), and put z = u e^(1/2).  Then

  z*z = e^(1/2) u*u e^(1/2) = e^(1/2) supp(e) e^(1/2) = e.

To see that z lies in B, approximate it by

  z_tau = y (T+tau)^(-1/2) e^(1/2)
        = b^(1/2) ( x e^(1/2) (T+tau)^(-1/2) e^(1/2) ),

which lies in b^(1/2)B.  With f_tau(t) = 1 - (t/(t+tau))^(1/2),
so that f_tau(0) = 0 and u*u = supp(T),

  ||z - z_tau||^2 = ||e^(1/2) f_tau(T)^2 e^(1/2)||
                  <= ||f_tau(T) T f_tau(T)||/(delta - eta)
                  <= sup_{t>=0} t f_tau(t)^2 / (delta - eta)
                  <= tau/(delta - eta),

because t f_tau(t)^2 = tau s(1-s^(1/2))/(1+s^(1/2)) <= tau for
s = t/(t+tau).  Hence z is in the norm closure of b^(1/2)B, that is,
in B.

Finally zz* = u e u* is the norm limit of z_tau z_tau*, and
z_tau z_tau* = b^(1/2) c c* b^(1/2) with c the bracket above, so it
lies in b^(1/2) B b^(1/2) and the limit lies in Her(b).  QED

2.2  ORDER-ZERO MAPS FROM GRAM DATA

A linear map alpha: M_q -> B is completely positive and contractive
(cpc) if all amplifications are positive and ||alpha|| <= 1, and
order zero if it maps orthogonal positive elements to orthogonal
positive elements; equivalently, with H = alpha(1_q),

  alpha(x)alpha(y) = H alpha(xy) = alpha(xy) H     for all x,y.

Lemma 2.2 (Gram construction).  Let x_1,...,x_q be contractions in B
with x_i x_j* = delta_ij h for a positive contraction h.  Then

  alpha(e_ij) = x_i* x_j

defines a cpc order-zero map alpha: M_q -> B with alpha(1_q) = H and
H alpha(x) = alpha(x)H = alpha(x) for all x, where H = sum_i x_i*x_i
is a sum of pairwise orthogonal positives.

Proof.  Complete positivity: for vectors xi_1,...,xi_q, the sum
sum_ij <alpha(e_ij)xi_j, xi_i> is the square norm of sum_i x_i xi_i,
hence nonnegative.  Contraction: the positives x_i*x_i are pairwise
orthogonal, because for i different from j we have
x_i*x_i x_j*x_j = x_i*(x_i x_j*)x_j = 0 (using that x_i*x_j = 0 when
i and j differ, which follows from x_jx_i* = 0 by taking adjoints);
each has norm at most ||h|| <= 1, so ||H|| <= 1.

Multiplicativity identity: using x_m x_i* = delta_mi h,

  alpha(e_ij)alpha(e_kl) = x_i*x_j x_k*x_l = delta_jk x_i* h x_l,
  H alpha(e_il) = sum_m x_m*x_m x_i*x_l = x_i* h x_l.

So alpha(x)alpha(y) = H alpha(xy) for all x,y, which is the
order-zero identity; for positive orthogonal x,y it gives
alpha(x)alpha(y) = H alpha(0) = 0.  QED

Remark.  There is a supporting *-homomorphism into B**: for tau > 0
put bar alpha_tau(e_ij) = alpha(e_ij)(H+tau)^(-1).  The identity above
and alpha(e_ij) = H alpha(e_ij) give matrix-unit relations up to an
error of order tau, so these elements converge strongly to matrix
units with support supp(H); the limit bar alpha satisfies
alpha(x) = H bar alpha(x) = bar alpha(x) H and bar alpha(1_q) =
supp(H).  This fact is used only inside the literature input of 2.4.

2.3  COMMUTING ORDER-ZERO MAPS GIVE A DIMENSION-DROP MAP

Lemma 2.3.  Let alpha: M_3 -> B and beta: M_2 -> B be cpc order-zero
maps with commuting ranges and alpha(1_3) + beta(1_2) = 1_B.  Then
there is a unital *-homomorphism I(2,3) -> B.

Proof.  Two cpc order-zero maps into I(2,3):

  alpha_0(y)(t) = t (1_2 (x) y),    beta_0(x)(t) = (1-t)(x (x) 1_3).

Their ranges commute and alpha_0(1_3) + beta_0(1_2) = 1.  The
Cstar-algebra they generate inside I(2,3) is all of I(2,3): it
contains t 1 and (1-t) 1, hence 1 and every scalar function; the
products alpha_0(y)beta_0(x) = t(1-t)(y (x) x) generate the ideal
C_0((0,1),M_6) together with the scalars; and the two maps supply
exactly the allowed endpoint fibres, 1_2 (x) M_3 at t = 1 and
M_2 (x) 1_3 at t = 0.

Let E = C*(alpha(M_3), beta(M_2), 1_B), and define a unital linear
map phi on the dense *-subalgebra generated by alpha_0, beta_0 by
substituting alpha and beta.  It suffices to bound ||pi(phi(p))||
for an irreducible representation pi of E and a *-polynomial p in
the generators, since the norm of an element of I(2,3) is the
supremum of the norms of its images under irreducible
representations.

The element alpha(1_3) is central in E and lies between 0 and 1, so
pi(alpha(1_3)) = r 1 with r in [0,1].

If r = 0 then pi(alpha(1_3)) = 0, and the order-zero identity
pi(alpha(x))pi(alpha(y)) = pi(alpha(1_3))pi(alpha(xy)) = 0 gives
pi(alpha) = 0 (take y = x*).  So pi(beta) is a unital order-zero
map M_2 -> B(H), and a unital order-zero map is multiplicative, hence
a *-homomorphism sigma; then pi(phi(p)) = sigma(p(0)) and its norm is
at most ||p(0)|| <= ||p||.  The case r = 1 is symmetric, with the
value at t = 1.

If 0 < r < 1, then r^(-1)pi(alpha) and (1-r)^(-1)pi(beta) are unital
cpc order-zero maps with commuting ranges, hence unital
*-homomorphisms, and together they give a unital *-homomorphism
rho: M_6 -> B(H).  Then pi(phi(p)) = rho(p(r)), of norm at most
||p(r)|| <= sup_t ||p(t)|| = ||p||.

So phi is well defined and contractive on the dense *-algebra, which
therefore is the Cstar-algebra I(2,3); extending by continuity gives
the required unital *-homomorphism.  QED

2.4  THE CRITERION OF RODAM-WINTER, AND WHAT IS TAKEN FROM IT

Proposition 2.4 (Rodam-Winter).  Let B be a unital Cstar-algebra and
let a_1, a_2 in B be orthogonal positive contractions with a_1 = v*v
and a_2 = vv* for a single element v in B.  Suppose that for some
eps > 0

  1_B - a_1 - a_2   precsim   (a_1 - eps)_+ .

Then there is a unital *-homomorphism I(2,3) -> B.

This is Proposition 5.1, (ii) => (iv), with n = 2, of
Rodam-Winter, and PART 4 uses it as an input.  Here is what the
citation supplies, and why the three tools above are exactly its
ingredients.

  (i)  Smoothing.  Functional calculus with f(t) = min{t/eps,1}
       replaces the given pair by elements that obey the Gram
       relations of Lemma 2.2, so that a cpc order-zero map
       rho: M_2 -> B with rho(1_2) = d = f(a_1) + f(a_2) and defect
       a = 1 - d exists.  Lemma 2.1 then produces a contraction v
       with v*v = a and vv* in the ideal generated by the cutoff
       (a_1-eps)_+, and the polar decomposition of v separates two
       orthogonal copies of the defect algebra.  The support
       projections of 2.1 are what makes this legible.

  (ii) Two commuting order-zero maps.  Sections over [0,1] with
       values in M_2, scalar at 0 and vanishing at 1, give a
       homomorphism lambda on a section algebra; a unitary path built
       from the flip of M_2 (x) M_2, weighted by a scalar function
       vanishing at both endpoints, then verifies that the resulting
       M_3-valued and M_2-valued order-zero maps have commuting
       ranges and complementary units.  Lemma 2.3 converts such a
       pair into the required unital map from I(2,3).

Both halves are long computations inside B**, and we do not
reproduce them; the reference is the place to check them.  What this
part supplies, and what the rest of this document uses, is the
precise statement together with Lemma 2.1, Lemma 2.2 and Lemma 2.3.
Nothing later in the document depends on how (i) and (ii) are
carried out.

Two remarks on the statement.  First, orthogonality of a_1 and a_2
gives a_1 + a_2 <= 1_B, so the defect is positive and the hypothesis
says it is dominated by a cutoff of a_1.  Second, the stable rank one
hypothesis that appears elsewhere in the cited proposition belongs to
the implication (i) => (ii) there; it is not a hypothesis of the
implication used here.

With the criterion in hand, PART 3 produces the cone map that PART 4
turns into the orthogonal pair the criterion needs.
