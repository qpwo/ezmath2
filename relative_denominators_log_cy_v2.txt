The concrete question concerns a very common kind of map in
algebraic geometry, and it answers it with one concrete computation. The map
takes a space X down to a smaller space Z; the fibres have trivial
canonical class in a suitable sense; and the question is how badly the base
piece of the canonical bundle formula can be written when you are allowed to
fiddle with the representative. The answer says: not badly at all, as long as
the total dimension is at most four and the coefficients at the boundary are
drawn from a fixed finite set. Every quantity that the formula produces then
has a denominator bounded by a constant that knows only the dimension and the
coefficient set, never the individual map.

There are three intended readers. The first is a graduate student in
algebraic geometry who has met the minimal model program and the canonical
bundle formula but who has not yet seen this uniformity argument. The second
is a strong undergraduate who wants the shape of the argument and is willing
to take some of the deep input theorems on faith. The third is a curious
high-school reader who wants to understand what is being claimed and why it is
hard. Sections~sec:question and~sec:vocab, and the opening words of
every later section, are written for the third audience: they say in plain
language what is about to be proved before any formula appears. Everything
after those opening words is written at the first audience's level.

Two registers run throughout. There is a plain form, one to
five sentences, that a reader can take in without stopping. There is a
technical form, which is the actual statement or proof. The two
describe the same object. If the technical form is out of reach, the plain
form still tells you what the object is and what it is for.

Two features shape the result: it is a uniformity (not existence) theorem, and
it uses that the total dimension is at most four. Sections~sec:question
through~sec:residue prove the main theorem; Sections~sec:effective
and~sec:torsion apply it.



medskipnoindentIn words. Take a compact space X that maps onto a smaller space
Z, and suppose the fibres have ``trivial canonical class''. Then a classical
theorem says that the canonical class of X is the pullback of a class on
Z plus a correction that measures how singular the fibres are. That
correction has two pieces: a boundary divisor that records which fibres are
badly singular, and a ``moduli'' part that records something finer, the
one-parameter family of fibres through each point of Z. The moduli part is
only defined up to adding a principal divisor from the base, and adding a
principal divisor can change its coefficients by nasty fractions. The question
of the argument is whether, for a fixed dimension bound and a fixed set of
allowed boundary coefficients, one can always choose the representative so
that those fractions have a common, bounded denominator. The answer is yes,
and the bound knows nothing about the map.

medskipnoindentTechnical setting. Let (X,B) be a projective log canonical pair
over C of dimension d, with B an effective boundary whose coefficients
lie in a fixed finite set Phisubset[0,1]capQ. Let
[
 f: X→ Z
]
be a contraction: a surjective projective morphism of normal projective
varieties with f_*OO_X=OO_Z. Assume dim Z>0 and that
[
 K_X+Bsim_{Q} f^*D
]
for a rational Cartier divisor D on Z. Then the adjoint of the generic
fibre is rationally linearly trivial, and (X,B) is a log Calabi--Yau
fibration over Z.

medskipnoindentThe canonical bundle formula. On a suitable smooth base model
q: Wto Z one writes
[
 K_X+Bsim_{Q} f^*(K_Z+B_Z+M_Z),
]
where B_Z is the discriminant: at a prime Psubset Z its coefficient
is 1-t_P, with t_P the log canonical threshold of f^*P over the generic
point of P. The remaining piece mathbf MbfM is the moduli b-divisor.
It is a b-divisor rather than a divisor because it only descends to a divisor
M_W on some high birational model Wto Z, and its pullbacks to higher
models are declared. When M_W is nef one says mathbf MbfM is b-nef;
when pM_W is Cartier one says pmathbf MbfM is b-Cartier.

The defect is the phrase ``on a suitable model''. Change D to a different
divisor D_Z in the same rational linear class, and M_W changes by a
principal divisor that comes from the base. That can change denominators
arbitrarily. The point of an exact presentation is to freeze the choice
of D_Z so that the moduli part has a denominator controlled from outside.

medskipnoindentPlain form of the main theorem. Fix the dimension dle 4 and the
coefficient set Phi. There is a single integer p, and a DCC set
B of allowed discriminant coefficients, both depending only on d
and Phi, with the following property. For every log Calabi--Yau fibration
of this dimension and coefficient set there is a corrected principal divisor
(1/p_0)Div(psi) and a representative D_Z of the base class such that
[
 K_X+B+frac1{p_0}Div(psi)=f^*D_Z
]
holds as an equality of actual rational divisors, with
D_Z=K_Z+B_Z+M_Z, with B_Z effective and coefficients in B, with
(Z,B_Z+M_Z) generalized log canonical, and with pmathbf MbfM b-Cartier. The
integer p_0 divides p and clears the denominators of Phi. The bound p
does not depend on the fibration, only on d and Phi.

medskipnoindentTechnical form.

[Uniform relative denominators]
Fix a finite set Phisubset[0,1]capQ and an integer 1le dle 4.
There are positive integers p_0mid p, with p_0 clearing Phi, and a
rational DCC set B[0,1], both depending only on d and
Phi, with the following property. Suppose (X,B) is a projective log
canonical pair of dimension d with coefficients in Phi, and
[
 f: X→ Z, dim Z>0, K_X+Bsim_{Q} f^*D,
]
where f is a contraction and D is a rational Cartier divisor. Then there
exist psiinC(X)^* and D_Zsim_{Q}D such that

 K_X+B+frac1{p_0}Div(psi)=f^*D_Z, D_Z=K_Z+B_Z+M_Z,

where B_Z is effective with coefficients in B, the pair
(Z,B_Z+M_Z) is generalized log canonical, and mathbf MbfM is b-nef with
pmathbf MbfM b-Cartier. If (X,B) is klt then the generalized pair is
generalized klt.


medskipnoindentWhy the two integers are separate. The integer p_0 does two
jobs: it lets the principal correction psi produce an exact pullback, and on
the generic fibre it is a trivializing degree for the log Calabi--Yau pair
there. The integer p is larger: it clears the moduli trace on a model that
determines mathbf M.bfM. The discriminant is controlled separately, by the DCC
set B.

medskipnoindentWhat is genuinely new. Qualitative versions of the formula put the
moduli part on a model and make it nef, but the index may depend on the
fibration (Ambro, Fujino--Gongyo). The curve case of Floris even shows that,
when the generic fibre is a rational curve and the fibre index is allowed to
vary, no polynomial bound on the clearing integer can exist. The theorem says
that when the total dimension is at most four and the boundary
coefficients are from a fixed finite set, the index can be chosen uniformly.
The source of the uniformity is the index theorem for the reduced special
fibre, whose dimension is one less than the total.

A short glossary follows. Each item is one or two sentences; a reader who
already knows the term can skip it. Everything later refers back by item
number. Where a term has a long standard definition only the shape is recorded,
not the full definition, and point the reader to Koll'ar--Mori or the Stacks
Project.
[(V1)] A variety is an integral separated scheme of finite type over
C. Unless stated otherwise all varieties here are normal and, in the global
statements, projective. Normal means the local rings are normal domains;
a normal variety is smooth outside a closed set of codimension at least two.
A resolution is a proper birational morphism from a smooth variety.
[(V2)] A contraction f: Xto Z is a surjective projective
morphism of normal projective varieties with f_*OO_X=OO_Z. Equivalently, the
function field C(Z) is algebraically closed in C(X), so the generic fibre
is geometrically integral and the fibres are connected.
[(V3)] A divisor on a normal variety is a formal finite Z-linear
combination of prime divisors. A Q-divisor allows rational
coefficients. A divisor is effective when all coefficients are
nonnegative. A boundary is an effective Q-divisor; the boundary
coefficients are its coefficients.
[(V4)] Two Q-divisors are linearly equivalent, written sim,
when their difference is the divisor of a rational function. They are
rationally linearly equivalent, written sim_{Q}, when a positive
integer multiple of the difference is. They are numerically equivalent,
written equiv, when they meet every complete curve the same way. A divisor
is big, nef, or pseudoeffective when its class has the
corresponding positivity: big means it grows like an ample class on high
multiples, nef means nonnegative degree on every curve, pseudoeffective means it
lies in the closure of the cone of effective classes.
[(V5)] The canonical divisor K_X of a normal variety is the
divisor of a rational top form; on a smooth variety it is the determinant of
the cotangent bundle. It is defined up to linear equivalence. A log pair
(X,B) is a normal variety together with a boundary.
[(V6)] For a prime divisor E over X (that is, on some resolution
g: Yto X with E exceptional or strict transform), the
discrepancy of E with respect to (X,B) is the coefficient of E in
K_Y-g^*(K_X+B) when g is chosen so that this pullback makes sense; it is the
discrepancy when K_Y and K_X are fixed compatibly. The pair is
log canonical (lc) when every discrepancy is at least -1, and
klt when every discrepancy is strictly bigger than -1. The pair is
dlt when it is lc and the coefficients are at most one and the crossings
are simple normal; dlt is a convenient regular representative of an lc pair.
[(V7)] The pair is slc, semi-log-canonical, when it is defined on a
possibly nonnormal variety with pure-dimension components, is S_2, is nodal in
codimension one, and its normalization with the conductor included is lc. This
is the right singular class on a reduced fibre that may be reducible and
nonnormal.
[(V8)] The log canonical threshold lct(X,B;P) of a prime P
over a point of Z is the largest tge0 such that the pair
(X,B+t,f^*P) is lc over that point. In the canonical bundle formula the
discriminant coefficient at P is 1-t_P. A threshold is a single rational
number, so it is the natural quantity to try to control uniformly.
[(V9)] A b-divisor mathbf MbfM on a normal variety Z is a rule that
assigns a Q-divisor M_{Z'} to every birational model Z'to Z, compatibly
with pushforward. It is b-nef when M_{Z'} is nef on some model and
stays nef under pullback, and b-Cartier when some integer pM_{Z'} is
Cartier on a determining model. Here mathbf MbfM denotes the moduli
b-divisor and pmathbf MbfM b-Cartier means pM_W is an honest Cartier divisor
on a model W that determines mathbf M.bfM.
[(V10)] A generalized pair (Z,B_Z+M_Z) consists of a boundary
B_Z and the trace M_Z of a b-divisor on a model. Its adjoint is
K_Z+B_Z+M_Z, and its discrepancies are read off the crepant boundaries on
higher models. It is generalized lc (or klt) when those discrepancies are
at least -1 (or bigger than -1). The crepant boundaries on higher models
may have negative coefficients; that is part of the definition, not an error.
[(V11)] A DCC set is a subset of R that contains no infinite
strictly decreasing sequence; an ACC set is the reverse. The relevance is
that a DCC set of coefficients cannot contain smaller and smaller rationals
approaching a limit from above, and an ACC theorem says that a collection of
coefficients produced by a geometric process must lie in some DCC set or even a
finite set. The argument uses the ascending chain condition for log canonical
thresholds, quoted from Hacon--McKernan--Xu.

{smallhttps://arxiv.org/pdf/1208.4150}
[(V12)] The index of a log Calabi--Yau pair (T,B_T) with
K_T+B_Tsim_{Q}0 is the smallest positive integer n with n(K_T+B_T)sim0.
An index theorem bounds n in terms of the dimension and a finite set of
allowed boundary coefficients. The argument imports the slc index theorem of
Jiang--Liu in dimension at most three.

{smallhttps://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.11928}
[(V13)] For an integral Weil divisor G on a normal variety, the
reflexive divisorial sheaf OO_X(G) has sections, on an open U,
the nonzero rational functions v with Div(v)|_U+G|_Uge0. For a rational
divisor D, the rounded system is the complete system of
OO_X(floor( D)): nonzero v with Div(v)+Dge0. This needs no
Cartier condition. The field generated by a system is the subfield of
C(X) generated by ratios of nonzero sections.
[(V14)] A cyclic cover of a smooth curve C at a point c is the
normalization of C in C(C)(w) with w^m a local uniformizer at c. It is
totally ramified of degree m over c and 'etale elsewhere. Pulling back a
fibration to a cyclic cover and comparing a rational generator with its Galois
translates is the mechanism by which a denominator is read off a
character.
[(V15)] The residue of a rational top form with a logarithmic pole
along a smooth divisor is the form obtained by contracting with a local
generator of the normal bundle. Iterated residues give a form on a reduced
normal crossing fibre, and the residues of two generators differ by a scalar
determined by the local node. This is the one analytic-flavoured operation the
paper uses.
[(V16)] Rational connectedness of a smooth projective variety Y
means that any two points are joined by a chain of rational curves. It implies
Pic^0(Y)=0 and, with it, that the N'eron--Severi group is finitely
generated. The divisor class group Cl(Z) of a normal variety with a
rationally connected resolution is then finitely generated, and its torsion
is bounded using Kummer theory.




medskipnoindentIn words. The proof of the main theorem has two halves. The first
half is conceptual: it produces the exact presentation and
turns the moduli bound into a statement about one prime at a time. The second
half, Sections~sec:curve and~sec:residue, is a single hard
computation for that one prime. The whole strategy is: reduce a global claim
to a local number, then compute that number once.

medskipnoindentHalf one, step by step. The argument starts with the generic fibre of f. It
is a log Calabi--Yau pair of dimension d-dim Zle d-1le 3, so the known
index theorem in low dimension applies to it. That theorem provides a degree
p_0 in which the adjoint of the generic fibre is principal, with an explicit
rational function. Because the function field extension is regular, a Galois
descent (Hilbert's theorem 90) moves that function down to the actual generic
fibre, not just its algebraic closure. The result is a function psi on X
whose (1/p_0)-divisor corrects K_X+B to be vertical over Z. A short
argument in the same spirit then chooses D_Z in the rational class of D so
that the corrected adjoint is literally f^*D_Z, with no vertical error left.
That is the exact presentation~(eq:relative-exact-presentation).

Once the presentation is exact, the qualitative lc-trivial fibration theorem
gives the structural facts for free: the discriminant B_Z is
effective and the moduli b-divisor mathbf MbfM is b-nef and descends on a
smooth model. What the qualitative theorem does not give for free is a
uniform Cartier index for mathbf M.bfM. That is the content of the argument, and
it is what remains to be reduced.

medskipnoindentTwo independent boundings. The discriminant coefficients 1-t_P
must fall into a fixed DCC set; this comes from ACC for log canonical
thresholds, applied prime by prime, and is purely qualitative and already
uniform. The moduli coefficients must have bounded denominators; this is the
hard part. On a smooth model Wto Z with M_W=D_W-K_W-B_W, a coefficient of
M_W at a prime Psubset W equals an integer (from K_W) plus
coeff_P D_W plus coeff_P(-B_W). If
alpha=coeff_P D_W and B_W has coefficient 1-t_P at P
over the generic point, that coefficient is alpha+t_P, up to an integer.
So it suffices to show that one fixed integer p, independent of the
fibration and of P, has
[
 p(alpha+t_P)inZ
  for every prime Psubset W.
]
This is the target~(eq:relative-coefficient-target) below. Everything in
Sections~sec:curve and~sec:residue is aimed at it.



medskipnoindentTechnical form. Put k=dim Z and let K=C(Z). The contraction
condition makes K algebraically closed in C(X), so the extension
C(X)/K is regular; in characteristic zero it is separably generated, and
the generic fibre X_eta is geometrically integral. It is normal by
localization from X, and normality is geometric for a finite-type variety
over the perfect field K.

Choose a rational top form on Z, and use it together with the form defining
K_X to fix a relative top form on X_eta. Its divisor is the canonical
divisor K_{X_eta}. At codimension-one points of X_eta, which are smooth
over K, this divisor is the coefficientwise localization of K_X, so
vertical primes disappear; when d=k, X_eta=Spec K and the
relative form is a nonzero 0-form. Restricting a log resolution and its
crepant sub-boundary to the geometric generic fibre exhibits the original
geometric generic pair as lc: generic smoothness makes the horizontal strata
simple normal crossing there. That pair is projective of dimension
d-kle 3, its boundary coefficients lie in Phi, and its adjoint is
rationally linearly trivial.

[generic-fibre trivialization]
There is a positive integer p_0, depending only on d and Phi and
clearing the denominators of Phi, and a rational function
psiinC(X)^* such that
[
 p_0(K_X+B)+Div(psi)
]
is vertical over Z.


Proof. [Plain form]
Apply the slc index theorem to the geometric generic fibre in dimension at most
three, getting a trivializing degree. Descend the trivialization across the
Galois action by taking ratios of conjugates, whose divisors are vertical and
hence constant, and clearing the resulting cocycle with Hilbert 90.


Proof. [Technical form]
The geometric generic fibre is a projective lc pair of dimension at most three
with boundary coefficients in Phi and rationally trivial adjoint. The slc
index theorem (Jiang--Liu, quoted as input) gives a common integer p_0,
depending only on d and the finite coefficient set, such that
p_0(K_{X_{overlineeta}}+B|_{X_{overlineeta}}) is principal on the
geometric generic fibre. Choosing p_0 also to clear Phi gives an
integral divisor.

Descend to K. Choose a finite Galois extension L/K over which a
principalizing rational function is defined. The quotient of two Galois
conjugates of it has zero divisor on the proper geometrically integral normal
fibre over L, hence is a nonzero constant. These quotients form a
multiplicative Galois cocycle, and Hilbert's theorem 90 rescales the function
to be Galois-invariant; being defined over K, it descends to C(X)^*.
Thus there is psiinC(X)^* making p_0(K_X+B)+Div(psi) vertical.




medskipnoindentTechnical form. Choose a divisible by p_0 and
phiinC(X)^* with
[
 a(K_X+B)+Div(phi)=f^*(aD).
]
Such a pair exists because K_X+Bsim_{Q}f^*D and a clears denominators.
The divisor of psi^{a/p_0}/phi is vertical. On the proper normal generic
fibre this function has neither zeros nor poles, so it belongs to K^*.
Writing it as h, set
[
 D_Z=D+frac1aDiv(h).
]
Substituting gives the first equality of~(eq:relative-exact-presentation)
as an equality of actual rational divisors. In particular D_Z is rational
Cartier and D_Zsim_{Q}D.



medskipnoindentTechnical form. Apply the lc-trivial-fibration theorem of
Fujino--Gongyo. Besides generic log canonicity and the rational pullback
relation it needs a rank-one condition: on a log resolution the modified
discrepancy divisor drops the discrepancy -1 terms, and because
0le Ble 1 rounding up leaves, over the generic point of the base, only an
effective exceptional divisor; its divisorial sheaf pushes forward to OO_X
by normality, and pushing on to Z has rank one. The theorem then makes
mathbf MbfM b-nef and gives its descent to a nef rational Cartier divisor on a
sufficiently high smooth projective model Wto Z. The statement is stable
under changing the rational trivialization, since that adds a rational
principal b-divisor from the base.

Write
[
 D_W=(Wto Z)^*D_Z, M_W=D_W-K_W-B_W.
]
For a prime Psubset W, set alpha=coeff_P D_W. Since
coeff_P B_W=1-t_P and the coefficient of K_W is an integer,
[
 coeff_P M_W
   =alpha+t_P-1-coeff_P K_W.
]
Hence it is enough to prove, uniformly in the choice of P and the fibration,

 p(alpha+t_P)inZ.

Once~(eq:relative-coefficient-target) holds for a single p, the divisor
pM_W is an integral divisor on the smooth variety W, hence Cartier, and
pmathbf MbfM is b-Cartier.



medskipnoindentTechnical form. For primes Psubset Z, shrink about the generic
point of P so that P is Cartier. In a log resolution computing the
threshold, only divisors with positive order along the pullback of P
matter; discard those whose images are proper subsets of P. Then t_P is an
ordinary log canonical threshold of an lc pair with coefficients in Phi
against an effective Cartier divisor with positive integral coefficients. The
threshold ACC theorem (Hacon--McKernan--Xu) applies, and since thresholds are
rational and lie in [0,1], their complements 1-t_P form the required
rational DCC set B[0,1]. The singularities of
(Z,B_Z+M_Z) are generalized lc (respectively generalized klt) because the
crepant sub-pair over Z is sub-lc (respectively sub-klt): t_Pge0 always,
and t_P>0 in the klt case. On the original base Z the boundary upstairs is
effective with positive integral multiplicity over P, which forces
t_Ple1 and hence B_Zge0.



After these considerations the main theorem is reduced to a single statement: there is
an integer p, depending only on d and Phi, such that the coarse identity

 p(alpha+t_P)inZ
  for every prime P of every determining model W

holds. The threshold t_P is disguised: it was computed as an lc threshold over
the generic point of P, but the required uniformity is insensitive to the
individual P. The next two sections compute~(eq:problem) by slicing to
a curve through the generic point of P, running a relative minimal model
program there, and reading the answer off a residue on the resulting reduced
fibre.



medskipnoindentIn words. The goal is to prove one congruence, p(alpha+t_P)inZ, for
one prime P of one determining model W. The trick is to make the situation
one-dimensional by cutting with general divisors through a general point of
P. A curve has no room for complications: the "fibre" over a point of the
curve is a divisor, the singularities are controlled, and a residue
computation can see the denominator. So slice and run the relative minimal
model program, and reduce the congruence to a statement about a single reduced
special fibre.

medskipnoindentThe plan. Fix Psubset W. Build a smooth model Vto X that maps
to W and is marked along everything relevant. Cut V by k-1=dim W-1
general very ample divisors to get a curve C and a total space V_Cto C.
On V_C write an exact relative identity whose base coefficient at the chosen
point cin C is exactly alpha. Construct a dlt pair by adding the reduced
fibre and a horizontal boundary. Run a minimal model program over C. The
program does not change the function field and does not change the coefficient
of interest; it makes the total space terminal enough that the reduced fibre
is well behaved. The output is a dlt model Nto C on which an exact divisor
relation holds:
[
 L+Div(psi_C)=p_0(alpha+t)f_N^*[c], L=p_0(K_N+T+H),
]
with T the reduced fibre. That relation is handed directly to the residue
lemma of Section~sec:residue.



medskipnoindentTechnical form. Fix a prime Psubset W. Take a smooth projective
model Vto X mapping to W by g: Vto W, with simple normal crossing
markings for the crepant boundary B_V^c, the exceptional divisors over X,
and the support of g^*P. Because the function field extension C(W)subset
C(V) is regular (the same argument as for f), the Stein factorisation of g
is W itself, so g has connected fibres. Let theta_V be the rational top
form compatible with the chosen canonical divisor on X, and put
K_V=Div(theta_V). Crepant pullback gives

 K_V+B_V^c+frac1{p_0}Div(psi)=g^*D_W.

Put t=t_P. At the generic point of P, the boundary B_V^c+t,g^*P has
coefficients at most one, with equality on at least one component dominating
P; this is the threshold computation.

Choose general very ample divisors A_1,...,A_{k-1} on W (taking
C=W when k=1) and let C=A_1cap...cap A_{k-1}. Bertini applied
successively to the pulled-back systems and the finitely many marked strata
makes C and V_C=g^{-1}C smooth, with the markings still SNC. The curve C
is connected; the restriction of g has connected fibres, so V_C is
connected and hence integral. Also required are CnotsubsetSupp D_W and
V_CnotsubsetSupp K_VcupSupp B_V^ccupSuppDiv(psi). The dimension of
V_C is d-k+1le4. Choose an intersection point cin Ccap P transverse and
general on P, avoiding at c all other components of Supp D_W, the bad
strata on P, and the images of all nondominating strata not containing P.



medskipnoindentTechnical form. Let h:V_Cto C, F=h^*[c], and B^c=B_V^c|_{V_C}.
To keep the coefficient of P the canonical divisor is specified using
complete-intersection adjunction. Choose general representatives A_j'sim A_j
avoiding c and rational functions chi_jinC(W)^* with
Div(chi_j)=A_j'-A_j. Define theta_C as the iterated Poincar'e residue,
in the order of the cuts, of
[
 theta_Vprod_{j=1}^{k-1}g^*chi_j
]
along g^{-1}A_1,...,g^{-1}A_{k-1}. Generality makes this a nonzero rational
top form on V_C; for k=1 the product is empty and the residue is the
identity. Each chi_j supplies a simple pole along its divisor and a zero along
its chosen representative, so, cancelling the cutting divisors before
restriction, the residue formula gives an equality of actual divisors
[

 K_{V_C}:=Div(theta_C)
     &=(Div(theta_Vprod_jg^*chi_j)+sum_jg^*A_j)|_{V_C}
     &=(K_V+sum_jg^*A_j')|_{V_C}.

]
Set psi_C=psi|_{V_C} and D_C'=(D_W+sum_jA_j')|_C. Restricting
(eq:relative-resolution-presentation) gives

 K_{V_C}+B^c+frac1{p_0}Div(psi_C)=h^*D'_C,
  coeff_c D'_C=alpha.

The coefficient assertion uses that C meets P transversely at c while the
A_j' and all other components of Supp D_W avoid c.



medskipnoindentTechnical form. Along F, the coefficients of B^c+tF are at most
one, with equality on a component of F: the divisors dominating P restrict
transversely and reducedly, and near c, g^*P|_{V_C}=F. Over the
generic point of C there is still a projective birational comparison with the
normal fibre of the original model X, and the restricted exceptional divisors
are exceptional there; this is because the generic point of C lies where
Wto Z is an isomorphism, and generic flatness and openness of geometric
normality give normal integral original fibres on an open base with an open
birational isomorphism locus whose exceptional images have fibre codimension at
least two.

On V_C define an effective boundary Gamma as F_{red}, plus the
horizontal strict transforms of the original boundary with their original
coefficients, plus all horizontal restricted exceptional divisors with
coefficient one. Then (V_C,Gamma) is log smooth, its horizontal coefficients
lie in the fixed set Phicup{1}, and

 E=Gamma-B^c-tF

is effective near c and has coefficient zero on a component of F. On the
generic fibre, E is effective: its horizontal nonexceptional terms cancel,
while every horizontal exceptional term has coefficient 1-coeff
B^cge0, and its support there is exceptional over the original normal fibre.
Equation~(eq:relative-curve-presentation) gives the exact identity
[
 K_{V_C}+Gamma+frac1{p_0}Div(psi_C)=h^*(D'_C+t[c])+E.
]

Run a minimal model program over C. All negative coefficients of E are
vertical and occur over finitely many points, so there is an effective rational

Apply the extraction corollary of BCHM to the klt pair (Z,Xi) with the
prescribed set {E} or the empty set. This gives a projective birational

Restore the generalized data by K_{Z^+}+B^++M_{Z^+}=h^*D with the same nef

Now apply global ACC. If the nef trace is not numerically trivial on a model
determining mathbf M,bfM, write M_W=frac1p(pM_W) with pM_W nef Cartier, and

weight adjoined to the DCC set, so the boundary coefficients lie in a finite
set. If instead M_Wequiv0, take a common model widetilde Wto Z^+; the

it zero, hence M_{Z^+}equiv0 and the generalized discrepancies are the ordinary
discrepancies of (Z^+,B^+); ordinary global ACC again gives finitely many
coefficients. Applying this first to the small modification with no marked
divisor shows the coefficients of B lie in a fixed finite set I_0subset I
and the pairs Z are uniformly generalized ε-lc for some
ε=ε(d,I,p)>0: otherwise a sequence of marked valuations
with discrepancies decreasing to zero would, after extraction, produce boundary
coefficients 1-a_i in a DCC set that global ACC would force to be finite, a

medskipnoindentTechnical form. Birkar's boundedness theorem applies to the
varieties Z: projectivity, fixed dimension, generalized ε-lc
singularities, effective boundary, rationally trivial adjoint, and rational
connectedness. It gives, for each Z, a projective V_Z, from one bounded set,
isomorphic to Z in codimension one; replacing by finite normalization keeps
this and stays bounded. Hence a projective morphism V S over a
finite-type base whose normal fibres are isomorphic to the Z in codimension
one.

Let U=Z_{reg}. First, H_1(U(C),Z) is invariant under isomorphism
in codimension one of normal varieties: the common open subset, intersected with
the regular loci, is each regular locus with a closed subset of complex
codimension at least two removed, and removing such a subset from a smooth
complex variety does not change the fundamental group (perturb paths and
homotopies, relative to boundary, transverse to a smooth stratification whose
strata have real codimension at least four). Second, the groups take only
finitely many types. Replace S by its reduction and cover by finitely many
flat affine strata with U=Sm( V/S)calV/S) the
relative smooth locus; embed V{C}times S and identify
PP^N(C) with Hermitian matrices H^2=H, tr H=1. The map

semialgebraic triviality theorem partitions S(C) into finitely many pieces
over which the whole inverse image is a product; each semialgebraic fibre has the
homotopy type of a finite complex. Thus the H_1 take only finitely many
finitely generated isomorphism types.

For each Z, Cl(Z) is finitely generated: a smooth projective rationally

medskipnoindentFinally, the corollary pulling the two theorems together.

[Uniform principal multiple from a fibration]
Fix a finite rational coefficient set Phisubset[0,1]capQ. There is a
positive integer r=r(Phi) such that the following holds. Let (X,B) be a
projective klt fourfold pair with effective boundary having coefficients in
Phi, and suppose K_X+Bsim_{Q}0. If there is a contraction f:Xto Z with
dim Z>0 and with a rationally connected smooth projective resolution of Z,
then r(K_X+B) is an integral principal divisor. This includes B=0, and every
positive multiple is also principal.


Proof. [Plain form]
Apply the uniform denominator theorem with zero base class. The base adjoint is
rationally trivial, so the rationally connected torsion proposition applies to
it, giving a principal multiple. Multiplying the exact presentation by that
multiple writes the total multiple as the divisor of an actual function.


Proof. [Technical form]
Apply Theorem~thm:relative-denominators to f with the zero divisor as
the rational pullback class. It gives uniformly bounded integers p_0mid p, a
function psiinC(X)^*, and an exact identity

 K_X+B+frac1{p_0}Div(psi)=f^*D_Z, D_Z=K_Z+B_Z+M_Z,

with (Z,B_Z+M_Z) generalized klt, B_Z effective with coefficients in a fixed
rational DCC set, and pmathbf MbfM b-Cartier with nef data. Since
f^*D_Zsim_{Q}0 and f_*OO_X=OO_Z, the projection formula makes
D_Zsim_{Q}0.

Proposition~prop:rc-torsion gives a uniform ell and vinC(Z)^* with
ell D_Z=Div(v). Choose r divisible by both ell and p, so p_0mid r.
Multiplying (eq:rc-exact-relative) by r gives
[
 r(K_X+B)=Div!((vcirc f)^{r/ell},psi^{-r/p_0}).
]
Both exponents are integers, so this is the divisor of an actual rational
function and includes the integrality of the adjoint multiple. All constants
depend only on Phi, since the possible dimensions of Z form a finite set and
the denominator theorem supplies uniform p_0,p and a fixed DCC set.

medskipnoindentConventions. The symbols sim, sim_{Q}, and equiv denote linear,
rational linear, and numerical equivalence. Tensor products of line bundles are
written additively: A+B means A ⊗  B and mA means A^{ ⊗  m}.
Equalities such as~(eq:relative-exact-presentation) are equalities of
actual rational divisors for compatible choices of canonical divisors and the
displayed rational function; the canonical divisors are fixed using rational top
forms and are not rescaled silently. An integral principal multiple rD means
rD=Div(v) for an actual rational function v, which includes integrality of
every coefficient of rD. The rounded system of a rational divisor D is the
complete system of OO_X(floor( D)); a nonzero v is a section exactly
when Div(v)+Dge0, and this needs no Cartier condition. The field generated by
a system is the subfield of C(X) generated by ratios of its nonzero sections.
The two integers p_0 and p satisfy p_0mid p; all bounds depend only on the
dimension dle4 and the coefficient set Phi, never on the individual
fibration.
