An unknown direction on the unit sphere of R^d can be pinned down by
noiseless Gaussian measurements only if enough of them accumulate. Each
observation is a fresh standard Gaussian vector X_t in R^d together with
its exact inner product Y_t = < X_t , s > with the unknown unit vector s.
A learner retains at most 2^M states. The current state, the current pair
(X_t, Y_t), the sample index, and a fresh random seed alone determine the
next state. The stopping decision obeys the same
restriction. The final output is a unit vector whose law depends only on
the terminal state and the stopping index, with a fresh random seed
allowed. A shared random seed may fix the rules before sampling.

The claim is that uniform-sphere average success at least 2/3 at angular
accuracy eps, for 0 < eps <= 1/10, forces T >= c d log(1/eps) samples,
for an absolute c > 0 and every sufficiently large d, whenever M = o(d^2).
The memory budget M is a nonnegative integer. No assumption on the rules
beyond the bit bound is used.

The bound has three independent proofs, all of which turn a projection
moment into a sample count. The first bounds an integrated density moment
after a Haar orthonormal projection, then iterates backward through
memory blocks. The second bounds each memory state's joint law by a
positive sum of spherical cap measures, then iterates forward. The third
builds a single good density version at every fixed affine offset and
controls the growth of its Riesz potential across one block. The three
proofs share one endpoint: with fewer than d/8 samples even a full-data
estimator fails with probability at least 3/8 over the average direction.

The geometric inputs belong to classical potential theory. The
projection theorems themselves go back to John Marstrand, whose 1954
study of plane sets established that positive-dimensional Hausdorff
measure and dimension survive almost every orthogonal projection.
Robert Kaufman gave a potential-theoretic proof that also characterizes
the exceptional set of projections. Pertti Mattila's 1975 study
generalizes both methods to arbitrary dimension and organizes the
inverse-distance energy method. It also compares positive-dimensional
Hausdorff measure to the existence of a measure with a ball-growth
bound, a Frostman-type criterion used below. The estimates require
successive distances to growing-dimensional affine spans, and moments
whose order grows with d.

The affine-heights change of variables is classical in integral geometry.

of a uniform direction projected onto a subspace appear in the study of

Throughout, sigma_d denotes uniform probability on the unit sphere S^{d-1}
inside R^d; the subscript is dropped once the dimension is fixed. Write
B(z,r) for the closed Euclidean ball with center z and radius r, ||.|| for
the Euclidean norm, and ||A||_op for the operator norm of a matrix.
Constants denoted by C are positive and absolute and may increase between
uses; a subscript records any permitted additional dependence. All
logarithms are natural unless a base is displayed. A spherical cap is
B(c,r) = { s in S^{d-1} : ||s - c|| <= r } with c in S^{d-1} and 0 < r <= 2,
and the normalized probability is mu_{c,r}( ds ) = 1_{B(c,r)}(s) sigma(ds)
/ sigma(B(c,r)). Let v_k be the Lebesgue volume of the unit ball in R^k.

Lemma (Coordinates of a uniform direction). Let D >= 2 and 1 <= k < D be
integers. If U is uniform on S^{D-1} and P is any k-by-D matrix with
P P^T = I_k, then P U has Lebesgue density

  c(D,k) (1 - ||u||^2)^((D-k-2)/2) 1_{||u|| < 1},
  c(D,k) = Gamma(D/2) / ( pi^(k/2) Gamma((D-k)/2) ).

The direction of the component of U in ker P is uniform on its unit sphere
and is independent of the pair (P U, ||(I - P^T P)U||). Conditional on
P U, that direction is uniform.

Proof. Rotation invariance reduces P to the first k coordinate
projections. Let G in R^k and H in R^{D-k} be independent standard
Gaussian vectors. Then (G,H) / sqrt(||G||^2 + ||H||^2) is uniform on
S^{D-1}. The positive variables ||G||^2/2 and ||H||^2/2 are independent
unit-scale gamma variables with shapes k/2 and (D-k)/2. Changing
variables from these two numbers to their sum and their fraction gives
the beta density

  (Gamma(D/2) / (Gamma(k/2) Gamma((D-k)/2))) t^(k/2-1) (1-t)^((D-k)/2-1)

for 0 < t < 1. The direction of G is uniform and independent of these
lengths. Polar coordinates in R^k, with vol(S^{k-1}) =
2 pi^(k/2) / Gamma(k/2), now give the displayed formula. The direction of
H is uniform and independent of both lengths and of G, which proves the
conditional assertion. The event H = 0 is null. []

Lemma (Unit-ball volume). For every integer k >= 1,

  v_k <= (2 pi e / k)^(k/2).

Proof. On the ball of radius sqrt(k) the function e^{-||z||^2/2} is at
least e^{-k/2}. Comparing its integral there with the full Gaussian
integral gives

  e^{-k/2} k^(k/2) v_k <= integral_{R^k} e^{-||z||^2/2} dz = (2 pi)^(k/2),

which is the claimed bound. []

Lemma (Spherical cap mass). Put n = d - 1 for d >= 2. There is an
absolute a > 0 such that, for every c in S^{d-1} and every 0 < r <= 2,

  (ar)^n <= sigma(B(c,r)) <= min{1, r^n}.

For every ambient center z in R^d and every r > 0,

  sigma(B(z,r)) <= min{1, (2r)^n}.

An output direction independent of the signal has probability at most
eps^n of angular error at most eps, averaged over sigma.

Proof. For r <= 1, rotate c to the north pole. A sphere point in B(c,r)
has last coordinate at least 1 - r^2/2 >= 1/2. Its surface element as a
graph over c^perp is at most twice Lebesgue measure, and its projection
lies in the n-ball of radius r. The full sphere has area at least twice
the volume of the unit n-ball, by projecting both hemispheres. This
gives the upper bound for r <= 1; total mass one extends it to r > 1.

For the lower bound when r <= 1, the angular radius 2 arcsin(r/2) is at
least r. In polar angle the cap mass is the integral of sin^(n-1) theta
over that interval divided by its integral over [0, pi]. Use
sin theta >= 2 theta / pi for 0 <= theta <= r and bound the denominator
by pi. Since n <= 2^(n-1), the result is at least (r/pi)^n. For
1 <= r <= 2, monotonicity gives the lower bound with a = 1/(2 pi).

If an ambient ball meets the sphere, choose c in the intersection. Its
sphere portion is contained in B(c,2r), proving the ambient bound; an
empty intersection is immediate. Finally, angular error at most eps
implies chordal distance 2 sin(theta/2) <= theta <= eps. Apply the
sphere-centered upper bound to each fixed output and integrate over any
independent output randomization. []

These estimates serve a concrete finite-state regression experiment.
An unknown direction s in S^{d-1} is observed through independent standard
Gaussian features and their exact labels:

  X_t ~ N(0, I_d),   Y_t = < X_t , s >,   t = 1, 2, ... .

At each sample index the learner retains a state from a set of at most
2^M values, with M a nonnegative integer. A transition may use the
current state, the entire current pair (X_t, Y_t), the sample index, and
fresh independent randomness. Computation within the transition is
unrestricted. Every piece of information depending on earlier samples
that remains available later must be encoded by the finite state. The
learner neither chooses a feature nor revisits an earlier one.

The learner stops after at most a prescribed integer T >= 0 samples, and
its stopping decisions obey the same restriction. For fixed rules, its
output is a unit vector, written S_hat below, whose law depends only on
the terminal state and stopping index, with fresh randomness allowed.
In particular, the last observed pair influences the output only
through the terminal state. The sample index is available without
being stored; the proofs account for the information in the stopping
index. A shared random seed may fix the rules before sampling. The
pair (seed, initial state) is jointly independent of the signal and
the entire sequence of feature rows. The seed and initial state may
depend on each other. Fresh transition and output randomness is
independent of these variables and of initialization. Nonuniform
rules may depend on d, eps, and the sample index. This is a bit-memory
model with a deterministic finite horizon.

The measurability convention: the original experiment is jointly
measurable in signal, pre-generated feature rows, shared seed, initial
state, and fresh randomness, including the resulting state path, stopping
index, and output. For almost every fixed shared seed, the finite vector
of transition and stopping probabilities at each fixed state and index is
measurable in the completion of gamma_d(dx) dy, where gamma_d = N(0,I_d).
A rule given instead as a function of the current pair and an independent
fresh seed, measurable in the completed product, is also allowed; the
kernel-version lemma below justifies this presentation. Each terminal
output law is a probability measure on the Borel sphere.

For the lower bounds, let S ~ sigma be independent of the feature rows.
The probability of arccos < S_hat , S > <= eps, including all learner
randomness, is the uniform-sphere average success probability.

Theorem (Finite-memory precision bound). Let M(d) = o(d^2) be a sequence
of nonnegative integers and let 0 < eps(d) <= 1/10. There is an absolute
c > 0 such that, for all sufficiently large d, every learner in the
experiment above whose uniform-sphere average success satisfies

  Prob{ arccos < S_hat , S > <= eps(d) } >= 2/3

must use

  T(d) >= c d log( 1 / eps(d) ).

The dimension threshold may depend on the memory sequence. In particular,
the conclusion holds when the same success probability is required
separately for every s in S^{d-1}.

An every-signal guarantee implies the average guarantee by integrating
against sigma. All three proofs use the average guarantee itself. The
first also yields a uniform dependence on the memory budget.

Corollary (Finite-dimensional memory dependence). There are absolute
constants c > 0 and d_0 such that, for every integer d >= d_0, every
nonnegative integer M, and every 0 < eps <= 1/10, a learner in the
experiment above with uniform-sphere average success at least 2/3 and
deterministic finite horizon T satisfies

  T >= c d log(1/eps) / (1 + M/d^2).

The proof appears at the end of the projection-moment discussion. In
particular, the same precision bound holds with absolute constants for
all M <= d^2 once d exceeds an absolute threshold. Ordinary least
squares on the retained pairs meets the d log(1/eps) rate while holding
O(d log d) bits of state, well inside the subquadratic memory allowed;
the lower bound is therefore tight up to the absolute constant.

The block arguments will start fresh executions from specified states,
including states the learner never reaches. A kernel-version lemma
supplies Borel rules for all such continuations while preserving the
actual uniform-prior experiment. Its proof uses the marginal law of each
observation pair, so it does not require a density conditional on a
selected state.

Lemma (Borel versions and counterfactual continuations). Fix d >= 2,
finite T, and finite state sets. Suppose each transition and stopping
probability, at each fixed state and index, is measurable in the
completion of the Borel sigma field of R^d x R under
lambda(dx,dy) = gamma_d(dx) dy. The terminal output law depends only on
the terminal state and index. Then there are everywhere Borel probability
kernels with the same state sets and output laws that induce the same
uniform-prior joint law of signal, state path, stopping index, and
output. For the Borel learner, starting from any state and index and
supplying fresh future features yields a measurable continuation
success function, whether or not that state is reachable in the original
execution. The same conclusion holds when a rule is given before seed
integration by a finite probability vector measurable in the completion
of lambda x kappa, with kappa the law of an independent fresh seed.

Proof. First, if a fresh seed of law kappa enters the rule, each
completed-product measurable coordinate has a product-measurable version,
by approximating with simple functions and replacing level sets modulo
null sets. Wherever the resulting finite vector fails to be a probability
vector, replace it by a fixed point mass; this repair lies in a product-
measurable null set. Integrating against kappa yields a Borel probability
vector, and Fubini gives agreement with the original integrated rule for
lambda-almost every pair. It therefore suffices to work with coordinate
probabilities.

For each fixed state and index, combine the possible transitions and
stops into a finite set of destinations tagged as continuing or
stopping. Choose Borel versions of their coordinate probabilities the
same way. On the Borel null set where the vector fails to be nonnegative
or to sum to one, replace it by a fixed point mass. Finitely many states
and indices involve one Borel lambda-null set Z.

For every nonzero x in R^d, the coordinate-density lemma gives a
Lebesgue density for <x,S>. A Gaussian feature is nonzero almost surely,
so every pre-generated pair (X_t, <X_t,S>) has law absolutely continuous
with respect to lambda. Each pair therefore avoids Z almost surely, even
after any fixed prehistory; conditioning on a particular current state
can only lower the probability of seeing a pair in Z below the marginal.

Almost surely, none of the finitely many pre-generated pairs lies in Z.
Couple original and modified finite destination kernels by the same
fresh uniform random numbers. Induction gives identical state paths and
stopping decisions. Couple the unchanged terminal output laws
identically. This gives equality of the specified joint laws. Finally,
finite composition and integration of the Borel kernels against
independent future features and coins defines a measurable continuation
from every specified state and index, including unreachable ones. []

Use the previous lemma as follows. First condition on a shared seed for
which the model assumptions hold. The joint independence of seed and
initial state from signal and feature rows ensures that the conditional
initial-state law still contains no signal or sample information. Then
apply the Borel learner supplied by the lemma. The lemma need only
preserve the uniform-prior law; preservation at every fixed signal is
unnecessary. All bounds below are uniform in the fixed rules and are
averaged over the shared seed at the end. The original conditional
success probabilities are measurable in that seed because the original
experiment is jointly measurable. Neither the replacement kernels nor any
subsequent proof decomposition need to be selected jointly across seeds.

All three moment arguments produce bounds involving T + d. The next
elementary obstruction supplies the linear lower bound on T needed to
remove that additive d, without using any memory restriction.

Lemma (Antipodal full-data bound). In the experiment above, if
eps <= 1/10 and T <= d/8, even an estimator given all T features and
labels has uniform-prior success probability at most 5/8.

Proof. Let F be the row span of the pre-generated feature matrix and put
u = P_F S. Its dimension is T almost surely, with the evident
interpretation for T=0. The labels determine u. For T=0 the residual is
the original uniform signal. For T > 0, the coordinate-density lemma,
applied after fixing the feature matrix, shows that conditional on all
the data the residual is sqrt(1 - ||u||^2) omega, with omega uniform on
the unit sphere of F^perp. Before conditioning on the labels, isotropy
gives E ||u||^2 = T/d. Hence Prob{||u||^2 >= 1/2} <= 2T/d.

When ||u||^2 < 1/2, opposite residual directions give two signals at
distance 2 sqrt(1 - ||u||^2) > sqrt 2 > 2 eps. The two signals have
the same conditional law, and at most one can be within chordal
distance eps of a
fixed output. Angular success implies this chordal condition, so
conditional success is at most 1/2, even after averaging independent
estimator randomness. Total success is at most

  1/2 + (1/2) Prob{||u||^2 >= 1/2} <= 1/2 + T/d <= 5/8.

Giving the estimator all pre-generated data also covers a learner that
stops at a prefix, with its independent shared and fresh randomness. []

The first proof turns an integrated density moment into a sample count.
For integers 1 <= k < d, let V_{d,k} be the space of k-by-d matrices P
with P P^T = I_k, and let vartheta_{d,k} be the law of the first k rows
of a uniform orthogonal matrix. One direct construction fixes the
normalization. For a square standard Gaussian matrix G, invertible
almost surely, put O = G (G^T G)^{-1/2}. Then O is orthogonal. For fixed
orthogonal U, V, the same formula on U G V gives U O V, and the Gaussian
law is unchanged by both multiplications. Thus O has a probability law
invariant on both sides, the Haar probability used below. Its columns
can equivalently be sampled successively uniformly in the orthogonal
complements of the preceding columns; averaging either invariant frame
law under an independent O makes the equivalence transitive.

Lemma (Projected frame density). Let k, m >= 1 and k+m+1 <= d. Fix a
d-by-m matrix E with orthonormal columns, and draw P ~ vartheta_{d,k}.
The k-by-m matrix P E has Lebesgue density

  (prod_{j=0}^{m-1} Gamma((d-j)/2) / (pi^(k/2) Gamma((d-j-k)/2)))
  x det(I_k - Z Z^T)^((d-k-m-1)/2) 1_{ I_k - Z Z^T > 0 },

where Z = P E and > 0 means positive definite. This density is bounded
by (C sqrt d)^{k m}. If x_1, ..., x_{m+1} in R^d are affinely independent
and delta_i = dist(x_i, aff(x_1,...,x_{i-1})) for 2 <= i <= m+1, then the
joint Lebesgue density of the columns P(x_i - x_1), 2 <= i <= m+1, is at
most

  (C sqrt d)^{k m} prod_{i=2}^{m+1} delta_i^{-k}.

Proof. Right rotational invariance lets the fixed columns of E be
replaced by an independent uniform orthonormal frame. Conditional on P,
its projection has the same law as the first k coordinates of that frame.
Sample the full columns successively and let Z_j be the matrix of the
first k coordinates of the first j columns. Set S_j = I_k - Z_j Z_j^T,
with S_0 = I_k. Given the preceding full columns, the next column is
uniform on the unit sphere in their (d-j)-dimensional orthogonal
complement. The coordinate map from that complement to the first k
coordinates has row Gram matrix S_j. When S_j > 0, multiplication by
S_j^{-1/2} makes the rows orthonormal. The coordinate-density lemma and
a linear change of variables give the next projected column z, conditional
on Z_j, density

  c(d-j,k) (det S_j)^{-1/2}
  x (1 - z^T S_j^{-1} z)^((d-j-k-2)/2)

on z^T S_j^{-1} z < 1. This expression depends only on Z_j, so it is
also the conditional density given the projected columns. The strict
inequality implies S_{j+1} = S_j - z z^T > 0 almost surely. The identity
det S_{j+1} = det S_j (1 - z^T S_j^{-1} z) makes the product of the m
conditional densities telescope to det S_m^((d-k-m-1)/2). Its exponent is
nonnegative and 0 < det S_m <= 1.

The normalizing constants have the displayed scale. For a unit-scale
gamma variable G of shape a = (d-j-k)/2 > 0, Cauchy-Schwarz and the gamma
recurrence give

  Gamma((d-j)/2)/Gamma((d-j-k)/2) = E G^(k/2) <= (E G^k)^(1/2)
  = (prod_{i=0}^{k-1} (a+i))^(1/2) <= d^(k/2).

Together with pi^(-k/2), this bounds each c(d-j,k) by (C sqrt d)^k.

For the last assertion, Gram-Schmidt writes
[x_2-x_1, ..., x_{m+1}-x_1] = E R with E having orthonormal columns and
R triangular with |det R| = prod_{i=2}^{m+1} delta_i. Right multiplication
by R on k-by-m matrices has Jacobian |det R|^k, one factor for each row.
The change of variables on the previous density gives the displayed
bound. []

Lemma (Affine inverse distances). Let 0 < beta <= d, K >= 0, and R > 0.
Let nu be a finite positive Borel measure supported in B(z,R) in R^d such
that nu(B(b,t)) <= K t^beta for every b in R^d and t > 0. If F is an
affine j-plane, k >= 1 is an integer, and beta - j - k >= 1, then
nu(F) = 0 and

  integral dist(x,F)^{-k} dnu(x) <= C^d K R^(beta - k),

provided j, k <= d.

Proof. For 0 < t <= R, the projection of B(z,R) onto F is contained in a
j-ball of radius R. A maximal t-separated subset is a t-net with at most
(3R/t)^j points, by packing disjoint j-balls of radius t/2. For j = 0 one
point suffices. The corresponding ambient balls of radius 2t cover the
points of B(z,R) within distance t of F. Thus

  nu{ dist(x,F) <= t } <= 3^j 2^beta K R^j t^(beta - j).

Because beta - j > 0, continuity from above as t decreases to zero gives
nu(F) = 0. Distances greater than R contribute at most R^{-k} nu(B(z,R))
<= K R^(beta - k). On the shell 2^{-h-1} R < dist(x,F) <= 2^{-h} R, the
previous display bounds the contribution by

  3^j 2^{beta+k} K R^(beta-k) 2^{-h (beta - j - k)}.

The sum over h >= 0 is at most twice its first term. Since beta, j, k
<= d, the resulting factor is bounded by C^d. []

Theorem (Integrated orthonormal-projection moment). Let d, k, q be
integers and beta a real number with k >= 1, q >= 2, and

  k + q <= d,   0 < beta <= d,   beta - k - (q - 2) >= 1.

Let nu be a finite positive Borel measure supported in B(z,R), R > 0,
with nu(B(b,t)) <= K t^beta for some K >= 0. Assume P_# nu is absolutely
continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure on R^k for vartheta_{d,k}-
almost every P. Then there is a jointly measurable version g(P,u) of its
density such that

  ( integral_{V_{d,k}} integral_{R^k} g(P,u)^q du dvartheta_{d,k}(P)
    )^(1/q)
    <= C^d (C sqrt d)^{k(1-1/q)} K R^{beta - k(1-1/q)}.

For every P, this version vanishes when ||u - P z|| > R.

Proof. If K = 0 the measure is zero and the assertion is immediate.
Otherwise, let v_k be the unit-ball volume and define

  g_delta(P,u) = nu{ x : ||P x - u|| <= delta } / (v_k delta^k).

These are jointly measurable nonnegative functions. Expanding their qth
powers and applying Tonelli expresses the integrated moment through q
points x_1, ..., x_q drawn from nu. The intersection of the q label balls
of radius delta has volume at most v_k delta^k, and is empty unless
||P(x_i - x_1)|| <= 2 delta for i >= 2.

Put delta_i = dist(x_i, aff(x_1, ..., x_{i-1})). The affine-inverse lemma
shows every affine plane encountered has zero nu-mass: its dimension
is at most q-2, and the last condition above applies. So the tuple is
affinely independent for nu^{x q}-almost every choice. For such a tuple,
the frame-density lemma applies with m = q-1; the condition k+q <= d is
exactly its determinant condition. The probability of all q-1 inequalities
is at most

  (C sqrt d)^{k(q-1)} prod_{i=2}^q delta_i^{-k} (v_k (2 delta)^k)^{q-1}.

The label-ball volumes cancel those in the definition of g_delta. The
resulting bound is

  integral integral g_delta^q du dvartheta_{d,k}
    <= (C sqrt d)^{k(q-1)}
       integral prod_{i=2}^q delta_i^{-k} dnu^{x q}.

Degenerate tuples form a null set and can be assigned an infinite upper
bound in this nonnegative integral.

Integrate first in x_q, then in x_{q-1}, ..., x_2. At each step the
affine-inverse lemma bounds the current factor by C^d K R^(beta - k).
The remaining first-point mass is at most K R^beta. Hence the last
integral above is at most

  C^{d(q-1)} K^q R^{q beta - k(q-1)}.

This proves the qth power of the displayed bound uniformly in delta.

Define g(P,u) = liminf_{j -> infinity} g_{1/j}(P,u). The assumed absolute
continuity and the Lebesgue differentiation theorem (Tao, An Introduction
to Measure Theory, Theorem 1.6.19) imply that, for almost every P, this
is a density of P_# nu for almost every u. Fatou's lemma on the product
space (Tao, Corollary 1.4.47) gives the qth-power bound. If ||u - P z|| > R,
sufficiently small label balls miss P(B(z,R)) because P is a contraction;

Lemma (Gaussian radial factor and frame). Let X be a k-by-d standard

Fix a parent cap B = B(c,r). Let W be a finite set and let
K_w(X,Y) in [0,1] for w in W be Borel functions with sum_w K_w <= 1.
Define the reference probabilities

  f_w(s) = E_X K_w(X, X s),

The variables z are independent of X and are used only to define a
reference law; the kernel still receives the whole matrix X and the
radial factor H through X c + r H z. In particular, sum_w q_w <= 1.

Proposition (Positive domination after one block). For each w in W
there are countably many caps B(c_i,rho_i) with 0 < rho_i <= r and
coefficients a_i >= 0 such that

  f_w mu_{c,r} <= sum_i a_i mu_{c_i,rho_i}
    and
  sum_i a_i (r/rho_i)^alpha <= exp(C d) q_w^theta.

The first inequality is an inequality of finite positive measures on
S^{d-1}: it holds after evaluating both sides on every measurable set.
The constant C is independent of the parent cap and of the kernels.

Proof. A bound for nonnegative tests is proved, then converted into a
countable positive domination.

Step 1: cap tests and their local mass. Put rho_j = r 2^{-j} and choose
a finite rho_j/2-net N_j of the sphere for every j >= 0. Take as a
dictionary the parent cap, indexed by *, together with all B(c',rho_j)
for c' in N_j. Repetitions may carry separate indices. For this
countable index set I, write B_i = B(c_i, rho_i) and

  phi_i = 1_{B(c_i,rho_i)}(.) / sigma(B(c_i,rho_i)),
  b_i = (r/rho_i)^alpha.

So phi_* dsigma = mu_{c,r}, every phi_i is a probability density, b_* = 1,
and the weights at level j are 2^{alpha j}. If h in L^infinity(sigma) is
nonnegative and satisfies

  integral h phi_i dsigma <= b_i for every i in I,

then its integral against any positive dictionary sum is at most that
sum's weighted cost. A bound on integral h f_w dmu_{c,r} for every such
h will finish.

Let nu be the image of h mu_{c,r} under s -> (s-c)/r. It has unit-ball
support, and mass at most one by the parent constraint. The claim is that
for an absolute D >= 1,

  nu(D(b,t)) <= D^d t^lambda for every b in R^d and t > 0.

For 0 < t <= 1/4, suppose the ball meets the rescaled cap and choose a
point s_0 in B whose image lies in it. The corresponding sphere points
are all within 2 r t of s_0. Set

  j = floor( log_2 (1/(4t)) ),
  rho = rho_j,

so 4 r t <= rho < 8 r t and rho <= r. A center c' in N_j within rho/2 of
s_0 gives a dictionary cap containing those points, since
2 r t + rho/2 <= rho. Its constraint and the two-sided cap estimate imply

  nu(D(b,t)) <= (sigma(B_i)/sigma(B)) integral h dmu_{c_i,rho_i}
    <= (A_0/a)^n (rho/r)^(n - alpha) <= (A_0/a)^n (8t)^lambda.

An empty intersection has zero mass. For t > 1/4, use the mass bound and
1 <= 4^lambda t^lambda. These estimates prove the displayed bound.

Step 2: the quantitative test estimate. All hypotheses of the normalized
moment theorem hold for nu. The test, the parent cap, and the measure are
fixed before the fresh X is drawn. For every orthonormal k-frame P, the
coordinate-density lemma makes P_# sigma absolutely continuous with
respect to Lebesgue measure, so the same holds for P_# nu. The normalized
theorem gives a jointly measurable density g_X with

  P_# nu = g_X m_k,
  g_X(z) = 0 for ||z|| > 1,
  E_X integral g_X^p dm_k <= exp(C_D d p).

Since s = c + r u gives X s = X c + r H P u, Tonelli's theorem and Hoelder
on the product law of X and z give

  integral h f_w dmu_{c,r}
    = E_X integral K_w(X, X c + r H z) g_X(z) dm_k(z)
    <= (E_X integral g_X^p dm_k)^{1/p}
       (E_X integral K_w(X, X c + r H z)^{p'} dm_k)^{1/p'}
    <= exp(Cd) q_w^theta,

with p' = p/(p-1). The last step uses 0 <= K_w <= 1. So every test
satisfying the dual constraints obeys the displayed bound.

Step 3: representation by positive cap sums. If q_w = 0, the feasible
test h = 1 shows f_w mu_{c,r} = 0, and the empty sum suffices. Otherwise
put K = exp(Cd) q_w^theta > 0 with the constant from Step 2, and define
in real L^1(sigma)

  C_K = { sum_i a_i phi_i : a_i >= 0, sum_i a_i b_i <= K }.

Each series converges in L^1 since b_i >= 1 and ||phi_i||_{L^1} = 1. The
set is convex and norm compact. A sequence may be represented by a
coefficient array for each term; a diagonal subsequence converges
coefficientwise, since 0 <= a_i <= K/b_i. The limiting array has cost at
most K by Fatou's lemma. There are finitely many indices through every
level J and, uniformly over admissible arrays,

  || sum_{j>J} sum_{i at level j} a_i phi_i ||_{L^1}
    = sum_{j>J} sum_{i at level j} a_i
    <= K 2^{-alpha (J+1)}.

The limiting array satisfies the same estimate. Finite-level convergence
followed by the tail bound gives L^1 convergence.

Its signed downward closure

  D_K = { u in L^1(sigma) : u <= v a.e. for some v in C_K }

is convex, contains every nonpositive L^1 function, and is norm closed.
If u_m -> u with u_m <= v_m in C_K, compactness gives a subsequence
v_m -> v in C_K; then

  ||(u - v)_+||_{L^1}
    <= ||u - u_m||_{L^1} + ||v_m - v||_{L^1} -> 0.

Put F = f_w phi_*. If F does not lie in D_K, strict separation of the
closed convex set D_K and the compact singleton {F} gives a continuous
real functional ell with

  ell(F) > sup_{u in D_K} ell(u).

Fremlin's measure-theory chapters supply the localization and duality
inputs. A probability measure is localizable (Fremlin, Chapter 21,
Theorem 211L), so real L^1-L^infinity duality (Fremlin, Chapter 24,
Theorem 243G(b)) represents ell(u) = integral h u dsigma for some real h
in L^infinity(sigma). The finite supremum forces h >= 0: if h <= -delta
on a set E of positive measure, the functions -t 1_E in D_K would send
the supremum to infinity.

  V(h) = sup_{z in R^d} integral h(s) / ||s - z||^alpha dsigma(s),

with alpha = d/2 fixed throughout. For a finite Borel measure mu, the raw
Riesz potential is U_alpha^mu(z) = integral ||s - z||^{-alpha} dmu(s), so
V(h) is its supremum over ambient centers for mu = h sigma. It is not the
Riesz energy. The kernel is understood as a nonnegative extended-valued
function at s = z. Write B_r(z) for { x in R^d : ||x - z|| <= r } and
intersect such balls with the sphere when integrating against sigma.

Throughout this third argument, fix an integer d >= 16 and put

  k = q = floor(d/8),   alpha = d/2.

These choices give q >= 2, d/16 <= k = q <= d/8,
alpha - k - q >= 1, and d - 1 - alpha >= 1. The matrix X always denotes a
k-by-d standard Gaussian matrix. Constants C >= 1 are absolute and may
increase between uses.

Lemma (Ambient potential bounds). For every z in R^d and 0 < r <= 1/10,

  sigma(B_r(z)) <= C^d r^{d-1}.

Every measurable h : S^{d-1} -> [0,1] has finite potential. Moreover, for
0 < eps <= 1/10,

  sup_{z in R^d} integral_{||s - z|| <= eps} ||s - z||^{-alpha} dsigma(s)
    <= C^d eps^{d - 1 - alpha}.

Proof. The ambient estimate follows from the cap-mass lemma, absorbing
2^{d-1} into C^d. For dyadic outer radii r_m = eps 2^{-m}, the shell
r_m/2 < ||s - z|| <= r_m contributes at most (2/r_m)^alpha C^d
r_m^{d-1}. The point s = z has zero sigma measure, and
sum_{m >= 0} 2^{-m (d - 1 - alpha)} <= 2. Summing gives the second bound.
The same argument at radius 1/10, together with the kernel bound 10^alpha
outside that ball, proves V(h) < infinity uniformly over 0 <= h <= 1. []

The potential bounds the measure of a tube around an affine subspace
through an ambient center. This fact integrates successive inverse
distances in the projection moment that controls a density at a fixed
offset.

Lemma (Inverse distance to an anchored affine space). Let
h : S^{d-1} -> [0,1] be measurable, z in R^d, rho > 0, and let F be an
affine subspace through z of dimension ell <= q - 1. Then F cap B_rho(z)
has zero h dsigma mass, and

  integral_{B_rho(z)} dist(s,F)^{-k} h(s) dsigma(s)
    <= C^d V(h) rho^{alpha - k}.

Proof. For every ambient center c and radius r > 0, the definition of V
gives

  integral_{B_r(c)} h dsigma <= V(h) r^alpha.

Fix 0 < r <= rho. A maximal r-separated subset of F cap B_rho(z) has at
most (1 + 2 rho/r)^ell <= (3 rho/r)^ell points, by comparing disjoint
radius-r/2 balls in F with the radius-(rho + r/2) ball. It is an r-net.
The orthogonal projection onto F of any point in B_rho(z) lies in
F cap B_rho(z), since z lies in F. Consequently the part of B_rho(z)
within distance r of F is covered by the corresponding ambient
radius-2r balls. The tube bound follows:

  integral_{B_rho(z) cap {dist(s,F) <= r}} h(s) dsigma(s)
    <= 3^ell 2^alpha V(h) rho^ell r^{alpha - ell}.

This includes ell = 0 with a one-point net. Since alpha - ell > 0, letting
r decrease to zero shows that F cap B_rho(z) has zero mass.

Every point of B_rho(z) is within distance rho of F. Apply the tube bound
on the shells with outer distance r_m = rho 2^{-m} and bound the inverse
power there by (2/r_m)^k. Their sum is at most

  3^ell 2^{alpha+k} V(h) rho^{alpha - k}
    sum_{m >= 0} 2^{-m (alpha - ell - k)}.

Since alpha - ell - k >= alpha - (q - 1) - k >= 2, the series is at
most 2. Since ell <= d/8, alpha = d/2, and k <= d/8, the remaining
constant is at most C^d. This gives the displayed inverse-distance
bound. []

An integrated density norm does not specify a density value on a
matrix-dependent graph. The next estimate builds one density version
whose moment is controlled at every fixed affine offset. Its cost is
the supremum of the ambient Riesz potential of the original measure.

Lemma (Projection density at deterministic offsets). Let z in R^d,
rho > 0, H subset S^{d-1} cap B_rho(z) measurable, and
h : S^{d-1} -> [0,1] measurable. Fix these before drawing a k-by-d
standard Gaussian matrix X. The pushforward of h 1_H dsigma by
s -> X s admits a single jointly Borel function

  R : R^{k x d} x R^k -> [0, infinity]

such that R(X, .) is its Lebesgue density for every full-row-rank X, and
for every fixed b in R^k,

  ( E_X R(X, X z + b)^q )^{1/q} <= C^d V(h) rho^{alpha - k}.

The same function R is used for all b. Moreover,

  R(X,y) = 0 whenever ||y - X z|| > ||X||_op rho.

Proof. Let mu be the finite Borel measure mu(E) = integral_E h 1_H dsigma
on Borel subsets of the sphere. For eta > 0, let B_eta^k be the
radius-eta ball in R^k and let vol_k be k-dimensional Lebesgue measure.
Define

  R_eta(X,y) = (1 / vol_k(B_eta^k))
               integral 1_{ ||X t - y|| <= eta } dmu(t).

The integrand is Borel in (X,y,t), so the parameter integral is Borel in
(X,y). Prove the moment bound with R_eta in place of R, uniformly in eta.

Expand the qth moment at X z + b by Tonelli using t_1, ..., t_q from
copies of mu. For a fixed tuple the columns X(t_j - z) form an array whose
k rows are independent centered Gaussian vectors with covariance

  G = (< t_i - z , t_j - z >)_{1 <= i, j <= q}.

If G is nonsingular, their joint density is bounded everywhere by
(2 pi)^(-k q / 2) (det G)^(-k/2). So the probability that all columns
belong to the radius-eta ball about b is at most this bound times
vol_k(B_eta^k)^q.

The singular tuples have zero mu^{x q} mass. At the first linearly
dependent vector t_j - z, the point t_j lies in
z + span(t_1 - z, ..., t_{j-1} - z), an affine space through z of
dimension at most q-1. The previous lemma assigns zero mu mass to it. The
degeneracy set is measurable, and Fubini at each j proves the claim.
For a nonsingular tuple, Gram-Schmidt triangularizes the matrix with
columns t_j - z, with diagonal entries equal to successive orthogonal
distances. Taking the determinant of its Gram matrix gives

  (det G)^(-k/2)
    = prod_{j=1}^q
        dist(t_j, z + span(t_1 - z, ..., t_{j-1} - z))^{-k}.

Integrate this product first in t_q, then in t_{q-1}, and so on. At every
nonsingular prefix the previous inverse-distance lemma bounds the next
integral by C^d V(h) rho^{alpha - k}. The exceptional prefixes have zero
product measure by the same degeneracy argument. It follows that the
integral of the displayed product is at most
(C^d V(h) rho^{alpha - k})^q. The ball volumes cancel in the moment
expansion, and the Gaussian prefactor is at most one. So

  E_X R_eta(X, X z + b)^q <= ( C^d V(h) rho^{alpha - k} )^q

for every eta > 0 and every fixed b.

Now choose the function once and for all:

  R(X,y) = liminf_{m -> infinity} R_{1/m}(X,y).

It is jointly Borel. To see that it is a density, fix a full-row-rank X.
Set D = (X X^T)^{1/2} and P = D^{-1} X, so D is invertible, P has
orthonormal rows, and X = D P. The coordinate-density lemma makes
P_# sigma absolutely continuous, and the invertible map D preserves
absolute continuity. Here D is a local matrix symbol, distinct from the
absolute constant of the same name. Since mu <= sigma, the pushforward X_# mu is also
absolutely continuous. The Lebesgue differentiation theorem (Tao,
Theorem 1.6.19) applied to its L^1 density shows that the limit equals
that density almost everywhere in y. A Gaussian X has full row rank
almost surely.

For each fixed b the identity R(X, X z + b) = liminf_m R_{1/m}(X, X z + b)
holds by the definition of the single function R. Fatou's lemma (Tao,
Corollary 1.4.47) over X gives the displayed moment bound. These are
separate moment statements for each deterministic b; no simultaneous
almost-sure bound in X over all offsets is asserted. Finally, if
||y - X z|| > ||X||_op rho, then every X t with t in H is at distance at
least ||y - X z|| - ||X||_op rho > 0 from y. The averages R_eta(X,y) are
eventually zero, proving the support statement. []

The explicit choice R(X,y) = liminf_m R_{1/m}(X,y) is the feature that
allows evaluation at X z + b. A density chosen only up to a Lebesgue-null
set in y for each X would not justify the moment bound at that point. An
integrated L^q(dX dy) bound controls a density only up to product-null
sets, while the graph y = X z + b has zero product measure. A positive
cap domination instead controls the measure of every Borel set by a
positive sum of cap measures. Neither of those conclusions supplies the
fixed-offset moment above, so the anchored determinant estimate and the
explicit limit stand as a separate argument.

Fix nonnegative integers M, T and accuracy 0 < eps <= 1/10, and set

  p_succ = Prob{ arccos < S_hat , S > <= eps },
  L = log(1/eps),   N = ceil(T/k),   W = (T+2) 2^M.

Complete unused rules and terminal laws arbitrarily and force a halt by
T. After a halt retain a tag consisting of the stopping index and
terminal state, and ignore further samples. At any time at most 2^M live
states and at most (T+1) 2^M such tags are available, including a
possible immediate halt; hence the displayed bound W. Add independent
unused Gaussian rows after T to complete the last block of length k. Let
V_i be the state set at the boundary after block i, 0 <= i <= N; thus
|V_i| <= W.

For every v in V_i and s in S^{d-1}, let h_i(v,s) be the success
probability of the remaining execution started at that boundary state.
This also applies to states not reached by the original run. At i = N
it is the success probability of the fixed output law associated with
the terminal state. These functions are Borel, take values in [0,1], and
satisfy, for a fresh k-by-d standard Gaussian matrix X,

  h_{i-1}(v,s) = E_X sum_{w in V_i} K^{(i)}_{v,w}(X, Xs) h_i(w,s),

1 <= i <= N, with K^{(i)}_{v,w}(X,y) the probability of the block
transition from v to w on the exact row pairs (X,y). These kernels are
Borel, nonnegative, and sum to one in w for every (X,y). Such kernels
incorporate internal randomization and the ignored rows after a halt.
The functions h_i(w,.) are fixed independently of X. The fresh
Gaussian block is independent of the signal, initialization, past rows,
and past coins.
Starting the continuation at the specified state therefore gives exactly
the recursion; the state and boundary index contain all retained
sample-dependent information.

Lemma (Terminal potential). Let Q be a signal-independent probability law
on unit-vector outputs, and let h(s) = Q{ u : arccos < u , s > <= eps }.
Then

  V(h) <= C^d eps^{d - 1 - alpha}.

Proof. The cap-mass lemma gives integral h dsigma <= eps^{d-1}, also for
a randomized output law. For any ambient center z, use h <= 1 and the
inner Riesz integral bound inside B_eps(z). Outside that ball bound the
kernel by eps^{-alpha} and use the total mass bound. The sum is at most
C^d eps^{d - 1 - alpha} uniformly in z. []

Set V_i = max_{v in V_i} V(h_i(v,.)). The maximum is finite by the
ambient potential bounds lemma. The terminal potential lemma gives
V_N <= C^d eps^{d - 1 - alpha}.

The block increase is estimated one distance shell at a time.

Lemma (One shell across a block). For 1 <= i <= N, v in V_{i-1}, z in
R^d, and rho > 0, let H_rho(z) = { s in S^{d-1} : rho/2 < ||s - z||
<= rho }. Then

  integral_{H_rho(z)} h_{i-1}(v,s) / ||s - z||^alpha dsigma(s)
    <= C^d W^{1/q} V_i.

Proof. For each successor w use the density R_w(X,y) of the
fixed-offset lemma for the measure h_i(w,.) 1_{H_rho(z)} dsigma.
Tonelli's theorem, the recursion, and pushforward integration give

  integral_{H_rho(z)} h_{i-1}(v,s) / ||s - z||^alpha dsigma(s)
    <= (2/rho)^alpha E_X integral_{R^k}
       sum_w K^{(i)}_{v,w}(X,y) R_w(X,y) dy
    <= (2/rho)^alpha E_X integral_{||y - X z|| <= ||X||_op rho}
       max_w R_w(X,y) dy.

The pushforward identity holds for every full-row-rank X, hence almost
surely. The second inequality uses the probability-vector property of K
and the support of R.

A deterministic integration region is needed after averaging over X.
Choose a sufficiently large absolute D_0 and partition the matrices into

  E_1 = { ||X||_op <= D_0 sqrt d },
  E_j = { D_0 (j-1) sqrt d < ||X||_op <= D_0 j sqrt d }  for j >= 2.

The bins obey

  P(E_j) <= exp( -4 (j-1)^2 d )  for j >= 2.

For completeness, take 1/4-nets on the unit spheres of R^d and R^k; a
maximal separated set has at most 9^d and 9^k points, by the disjoint-ball
volume argument. Approximating each unit vector by its net point incurs
at most ||X||_op/2 in a bilinear form. So ||X||_op is at most twice the
largest absolute bilinear form on the two nets. Each such form is
standard Gaussian, and its elementary tail bound combined with a union
bound gives

  P{ ||X||_op > t } <= 2 * 9^{d+k} exp( -t^2/8 ).

Putting t = D_0 (j-1) sqrt d proves the bin bound after increasing D_0,
since k <= d/8.

For every deterministic b in R^k, Hoelder's inequality and the
fixed-offset lemma give

  E_X [ 1_{E_j} max_w R_w(X, X z + b) ]
    <= P(E_j)^{1 - 1/q}
       (sum_w E_X R_w(X, X z + b)^q)^{1/q}
    <= P(E_j)^{1 - 1/q} W^{1/q} C^d V_i rho^{alpha - k}.

No conditional version of the density lemma is used: Hoelder's inequality
pays for the bin event. For a fixed matrix in E_j, translate y = X z + b
in the pushforward display; the remaining region is ||b|| <= D_0 j sqrt d
rho. Joint measurability permits Tonelli's theorem to integrate the
previous bound over this deterministic ball.

The volume of that ball is at most C^d j^k rho^k. The unit-ball volume
estimate gives v_k <= (2 pi e / k)^{k/2}, with d/k <= 16, hence
v_k (D_0 j sqrt d rho)^k <= C^d j^k rho^k.

Also, by q >= 2 and the bin bound,

  sum_{j>=1} j^k P(E_j)^{1 - 1/q}
    <= 1 + sum_{j>=2} exp( k log j - 2 (j-1)^2 d )
    <= 2,

since k log j <= (d/8)(j-1)^2 for j >= 2. Combining the volume and bin
estimates with the pushforward display proves the shell bound. The
powers of rho cancel exactly: rho^{-alpha} rho^{alpha-k} rho^k = 1. []

Proposition (Riesz success estimate). There is an absolute constant
C_0 >= 1 such that every learner considered satisfies

  p_succ <= B J^N,
  B = C_0^d eps^{d/2 - 1},
  J = 1 + C_0^d (1 + L) W^{1/q}.

This includes T = 0, and no asymptotic assumption on M is made.

Proof. Outside B_eps(z) use the shells 2^m eps < ||s - z|| <= 2^{m+1} eps
for m = 0, 1, 2, ... . For every ambient z, at most C(1+L) of these meet
the sphere. If ||z|| <= 3, all distances are at most 4, giving at most
ceil(log_2(4/eps)) nonempty shells. If ||z|| > 3, the distances lie
between ||z|| - 1 and ||z|| + 1, whose ratio is less than 2; only a
bounded number of dyadic shells can then meet that interval.

Inside B_eps(z) use h_{i-1} <= 1 and the inner Riesz integral bound. On
each remaining shell use the shell lemma. Taking the supremum over z and
the maximum over v gives

  V_{i-1} <= C^d eps^{d - 1 - alpha} + C^d (1 + L) W^{1/q} V_i.

Choose C_0 large enough that the first term and the terminal value V_N
are at most B, and the second coefficient is at most J - 1. With
U_i = max{V_i, B}, the displayed inequality gives U_{i-1} <= J U_i and
U_N = B, so V_0 <= B J^N, also when N = 0.

At z = 0 the kernel equals one on the unit sphere, so
integral h_0(v,s) dsigma(s) <= V_0 for each initial state. For almost
every fixed shared seed, averaging over its conditional initial-state law
preserves the bound, because that law is independent of the signal and
rows. Averaging the original conditional success probabilities over the
seed preserves it again. This proves the displayed bound. []

The final lower bound follows by adding a packing count of terminal tags
to the Riesz success estimate. The short-run case requires a separate
argument; in the long-run case the desired bound already holds.

Proof of the finite-memory precision bound by the Riesz method. For fixed
rules write p_{succ,rule} for the conditional success probability. For a
terminal tag a, let f_a(s) be the probability of reaching it given s,
and let g_a(s) be the success probability of its output law. This output
law depends only on the tag, and 0 <= f_a <= 1. The cap-mass lemma gives
integral g_a dsigma <= C^d eps^{d-1}, also for a randomized output law.
Summing over at most W tags gives the packing bound

  p_{succ,rule} = sum_a integral f_a(s) g_a(s) dsigma(s)
    <= W C^d eps^{d-1}.

The right side is independent of the chosen rules, so the same bound
holds for the averaged p_succ.

Write L = log(1/eps). If T >= dL, the conclusion already holds. In the
remaining case T < dL, assume p_succ >= 2/3. Taking logarithms in the
packing bound gives

  (d-1)L <= M log 2 + log(T+2) + d log C + log(3/2).

For the given memory sequence, eventually M <= d^2. Since L >= log 10 and
T < dL,

  log(T+2) <= log(2d) + log L <= log(2d) + L

for large d. So

  (d-2)L <= d^2 log 2 + d log C + log(2d) + log(3/2).

Consequently, only in this remaining case,

  L <= C d,   log W = M log 2 + log(T+2) <= C d^2.

Thus the multiplier in the Riesz success estimate obeys

  log J <= log 2 + d log C_0 + log(1 + L) + (log W)/q <= C d,

using q >= d/16. In particular the shell-count term is bounded rather
than removed.

Now the Riesz success bound and p_succ >= 2/3 imply

  (d/2 - 1)L <= d log C_0 + N log J + log(3/2)
    <= C d (1 + N) + log(3/2) <= C'(d + T),

because N = ceil(T/k) <= 16 T/d + 1. The full-data bound gives T > d/8,
so the additive d on the right is at most 8T. For d >= 4 the left
coefficient is at least d/4, so T >= c d L for an absolute c > 0. This
combines with the case T >= dL already separated. []

The third proof of the finite-memory precision bound is complete.

The three arguments share one target and one endpoint, differing in how
accuracy cost, stopping cost, and block structure trade off. The
integrated projection estimate gives a density moment integrated over the
frame and label space; its regression argument counts stopping indices
within each block and retains the full memory dependence. The positive
cap estimate gives an actual dominating sum of cap measures with a
weighted radius cost; its regression argument propagates joint
signal-state laws forward and sums once over stopping indices. The
specified-offset Riesz estimate gives one density version with a moment
bound at every fixed affine offset; its regression argument maintains
that version, bins by operator norm, and controls both the shell count
and the terminal clock. The common antipodal bound converts each T + d
estimate into a linear lower bound on T.

The estimates carry beyond the finite-memory question. Any finite-memory
estimator for a spherical signal observed through Gaussian features with
a local or Riesz mass condition inherits the projection moment bounds. Any
exact randomly projected measure with a Frostman-type ball bound inherits
the integrated L^q bound at every scale. Positive cap domination provides
a concrete sum of cap measures that dominates a fixed joint law, with an
explicitly bounded cost in the reference probabilities, and it holds as
an inequality of measures rather than as a test-function inequality.
