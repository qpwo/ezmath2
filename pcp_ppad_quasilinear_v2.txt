A total search problem belongs to PPAD if it reduces by a deterministic
polynomial-time search reduction to the parity argument on a finite
directed graph with indegree and outdegree at most one. The canonical
complete problem, End-of-Line, is presented by a successor circuit S
and a predecessor circuit P, both from {0,1}^n to {0,1}^n, whose
induced directed graph has outdegree one at the distinguished vertex
0^n, indegree zero at 0^n, and indegree and outdegree at most one at
every other vertex. The task is to return a v in {0,1}^n different
from 0^n with P(S(v)) != v, or a v with S(P(v)) != v. A vertex of the
second kind is a second "source" of the same parity argument. Existence
follows from the even cardinality of any finite directed graph with no
endpoint among its edges. Papadimitriou (1994) introduced the class
and proved End-of-Line complete for it. Chen, Deng, and Teng (2007)
and Daskalakis, Goldberg, and Papadimitriou (2009) show that Nash
equilibrium search reduces to End-of-Line, so hardness for the latter
projection propagates to a large family of equilibria.

./cdt-2007-0704.1678-two-player-nash.pdf
./dgp-2009-nash-equilibrium.pdf

The reduction of Papadimitriou's argument to an equilibrium statement
is now familiar, and the striking post-2010 body of evidence suggests
End-of-Line is not solvable in polynomial time. That evidence, however,
has for two decades been about polynomial-time hardness and not about
the effective size of the verifier. The class of approximate total
problems to which the hardest instances of Nash equilibrium search
reduce is very broad, yet the reductions behind that observation run
in super-quadratic time. A theorem asserting that no quasi-linear-time
approximation algorithm for such problems exists must therefore rest
on an overwhelming structural fact and cannot be inferred from
polynomial-time hardness alone. The BPR2016 paper isolated such a fact
as Conjecture 2; the present argument supplies it.

./bpr-2016-1504.02411-pcp-for-ppad.pdf

The language of generalized circuits, due to Chen, Deng, and Teng
(2007) and used by Daskalakis, Goldberg, and Papadimitriou (2009), is
well adapted to reductions that mix Boolean and arithmetic control.
A generalized circuit is a finite directed graph whose nodes carry
rational values in [0,1] and whose gates are of the following types,
at most two inputs apiece:
  Constant(alpha; z) with |z - alpha| <= eps,
  Scale(alpha; a; z) with |z - alpha * a| <= eps,
  Copy(a; z) with |z - a| <= eps,
  Add(a, b; z) with |z - min(a + b, 1)| <= eps,
  Subtract(a, b; z) with |z - max(a - b, 0)| <= eps,
  Less(a, b; z) forcing z near 1 when a < b - eps, z near 0 when
  a > b + eps, and imposing no restriction otherwise,
  Or(a, b; z), And(a, b; z), Not(a; z) with the evident monotone
  Boolean implications at tolerance eps,
and whose output lists, connection lists, and rational parameters
are encoded explicitly. A gate is eps-satisfied if its antecedent
holds and its consequent holds within eps; a gate with a vacuous
antecedent is vacuously satisfied. A circuit is eps-satisfied if
every gate is.

Rubinstein (2018) proves that, for some fixed eps > 0, the problem of
finding an eps-satisfied assignment of a generalized circuit is
PPAD-hard under deterministic polynomial-time search reductions. His
result uses a specific gate basis chosen for its analysis. Deligkas,
Fearnley, Hollender, and Melissourgos (2024) transport it to the
standard basis via their Pure-Circuit problem. Babichenko,
Papadimitriou, and Rubinstein (2016) proposed a stronger object in the
language of PCPs, adapted to this class. Reading their Conjecture 2 in
the convention of the present setup: for some fixed rational eps > 0
and delta in (0,1) and for a fixed polynomial p, End-of-Line reduces
to a generalized circuit G so that (i) the complete encoding length of
G is at most O(N log^{O(1)}(N+2)) where N is the length of the encoded
End-of-Line instance; (ii) from any rational assignment satisfying all
but delta fraction of gates at tolerance eps, with total encoding
length at most p(N), a solution of End-of-Line can be reconstructed by
a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm whose cost is polynomial in
N plus the assignment encoding length. The second condition is the
PCP property: the verifier is the relation "gate list of G" and the
decoder is the reduction's reconstruction map.

./rubinstein-2016-1606.04550-settling-two-player-nash.pdf
./dfhm-2024-2209.15149-pure-circuit.pdf
./bpr-2016-1504.02411-pcp-for-ppad.pdf

The BPR2016 paper itself proves the conjecture under an additional
quasipolynomial-size allowance: their reduction has size
N^{log^{O(1)}(N+2)}. The improvement to quasilinear size is the
subject here. Deligkas, Fearnley, Hollender, and Melissourgos (2026)
apply both forms in the analysis of Fisher markets with approximately
optimal bundles, and note that the quasilinear form is strictly
stronger than PPAD-hardness. Golowich (2026) obtains a related
fine-grained hardness statement under a different accounting of the
verifier's read pattern; his Theorem 1.1 is a quasipolynomial bound
and his Definition 2.1 is the closest structural ancestor of the gate
counts used here, though the present argument does not depend on it.

./deligkas-2026-2604.27276-fisher-markets.pdf

THEOREM. Let eps > 0 be a fixed rational. There exist a fixed rational
delta > 0 and a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm that, on
input an encoded End-of-Line instance I of bit length N, outputs the
complete encoding of a generalized circuit G with these properties:
  (size) the length of the encoded G is at most c_1 N log^{c_2}(N+2)
  for absolute constants c_1, c_2 > 0;
  (completeness) every assignment of rational values to the nodes of
  G that eps-satisfies every gate has a unique rational lift to a
  solution of I, and the lift has encoding length polynomial in N plus
  the assignment length;
  (soundness) there is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm D,
  with running time polynomial in N plus the length of its second
  argument, such that for every rational assignment x of values in
  [0,1] to the nodes of G, with |x|_encoding <= |G|^{O(1)}, if x
  eps-satisfies at least a (1 - delta) fraction of the gates of G, then
  D(G, x) is a solution of I;
  (uniformity) the source generation, the circuit description, the
  decoder D, and the rational eps, delta are all fixed by the
  algorithm and by I, uniformly over the family of I. In particular,
  the exponents c_1 and c_2 are absolute.

The proof is a chain of formal statements whose composition gives
the reduction. Each statement is proved at the level of precision
that serves a self-contained reading.

The plan is this. End-of-Line is first replaced by an equivalent
intermediate problem whose graph has sparse, slowly varying local
structure: a sparse transcript graph, called the macrograph, whose
vertices record partial computations of S and P and whose edges change
one memory bit. The transcript graph has large diameter and long range
correlations, which makes it well-adapted to a locally checkable
encoding. The verifier that reads a candidate assignment must be able
to distinguish valid global transcripts from junk by testing only a
fixed amount of local structure. Any convex combination of a valid
transcript and junk would preserve validity of each local test with a
constant probability, so a per-gate reading of a single global
certificate cannot be sound. Two additional ingredients are needed.

First, an outer code whose codewords are indexed by an index group of
quasilinear order and whose local constraints have the following
property: if a proposed outer codeword satisfies a constant fraction
of the local constraints, its decoding cost per position is bounded
by a constant multiple of the fraction of failed constraints. The
index group provides simultaneous left and right orthogonal routes
through a finite quotient, and this affords the commuting-twist
argument by which whole-dimension checks act on a codeword without
reading its coordinates independently. The code is constructed from a
tensor product of two expander graphs over a finite field, with degree
constant and eigenvalue bounded by a constant via Margulis's original
construction.

./ershov-jaikin-2010-0809.4095-property-t.pdf

Second, a locally-encoded inner algebra whose canonical certificates
encode the transcript's polynomial relations. Where the outer code
gives the proof's global word a systematic payload side, the inner
algebra is a polynomial-size "port" that the verifier queries on
O(log N) algebraic coordinates. Two axes of the port: a row code
based on a weight-bounded matrix action that supplies local distance
and a per-point error corrector, and a column code chosen so that
composition of the two axes is unambiguous under the intended
codeword. The composition of outer and inner is the quasilinear PCP.

The stochastic machinery that converts the local-soundness guarantee
into the delta-fraction requirement is carried out by the geometry:
a directed curve embedded in the codeword space, on which any
first-order disagreement is amplified by a positive displacement.
Brouwer's fixed-point theorem supplies a global synchronization point
that always exists, and an averaging argument converts that point to
a constant fraction of correct output signals. The curve is displayed
in the proof of the main lemma; its vertical axis is a coordinate-wise
averaging operator that erases local disagreements, whose existence
follows from the Kazhdan property (T) of SL_r over the ring F_2[t].

./brouwer-1911-ueber-abbildung-von-mannigfaltigkeiten.pdf
./ershov-jaikin-2010-0809.4095-property-t.pdf

The rest of the reduction turns on two facts that make the
construction fit inside quasilinear size. The first is the closing
of the transcript graph: after adding all certificate polynomials
and all local uniform samples requested by the inner port, the
resulting circuit has only O(N log^{O(1)}(N+2)) gates. The second is
the substitutability of the inner port's local tester by a
one-input template: the fixed binary port that the outer verifier
invokes at an L-bit symbol is a specific quasilinear-size gadget
whose parameters are chosen once and reused at every outer position.
Selecting the port, guaranteeing that its error is nearly-linear in
the failure fraction of the input tape, and interfacing the port's
error with the outer code's error are the questions addressed by the
construction below.


The intermediate object is a directed graph whose vertices are indexed
by a triple (c, b, eta, t), with c in C a cyclic group of order M
polynomial in N, b in {0, 1, perp}^{2 h} a length-h tape, eta in {0, 1}
a mode, and t in {0, ..., q-1} a progress index. Let h, q, M be chosen
as polynomials in N. The macro graph, written G_mac, has vertex set

  V_mac = C x {0, 1, perp}^{2 h} x {0, 1} x {0, ..., q - 1},

of size 2 q M 9^h. The tape b is read as the pair (A, B) of two
length-h words over {0, 1, perp}; blank marks empty. The tape cells
hold locally observable bits; the group C provides a stage counter and
the index space over which the outer code of the next paragraphs
operates.

The local structure of G_mac is determined by two partitions. The
partitions are the two connected relationships induced by reading a
single entry of A and by reading a single entry of B. For each x in
{0, 1}^n, the pair (S, P) defines a specific value of the tape. Put
h_q = the zero-string of length h_q. The forward prefix of length
k is defined for 0 <= k <= h by:

  U_x^0 = (perp^h, perp^h, c = gamma^0),
  U_x^{k+1} = Fill(U_x^k; S), where Fill reads the minimum gate of
                S's gate list whose inputs are already filled at
                U_x^k and whose output is not yet populated,
                appending one new bit to A.

The forward completion U_x^h has A fully populated and B still blank.
Its A entry at the output positions of S, listed as (j_output,1, ...,
j_output,_k), spells S(x). When k < h because S's gate list is longer
than 2h, Fill terminates early at position k_0(x) = the least gate
whose two inputs cannot be simultaneously read. In that case S(x)
is not recovered and later stages cannot complete.

The reverse prefix is defined analogously. For y in {0, 1}^n and a
mode with A already holding the full forward transcript of x, define:

  V_y^0 = (A_y = y at positions corresponding to S's output gate
                indices, perp at all other positions; B = perp^h;
                c = gamma^0),
  V_y^{k+1} = Reconstruct(V_y^k; P), where Reconstruct reads the
                maximum gate of P's gate list whose outputs are
                already populated at V_y^k and whose inputs are not
                uniquely determined, back-filling one new bit into B.

The reverse h-prefix V_y^h has B fully populated. Its B entry at the
input positions of P, listed as (j_input,1, ..., j_input,n), spells
P(y).

LEMMA (unique prefixes and first obstruction). Fix x in {0, 1}^n and
put y = S(x). The valid forward prefixes of x exist uniquely for an
initial interval of lengths 0 <= k <= k_f(x) and equal U_x^k. The
valid reconstruction prefixes of y (under the same tape A = U_x^h)
exist uniquely for 0 <= k <= k_r(y) and equal V_y^k under the same
convention. Moreover:

  k_f(x) = h  <====>  P(S(x)) = x,           (forward obstruction)
  k_r(y) = h  <====>  S(P(y)) = y.           (reverse obstruction)

If k_f(x) < h, the terminal comparison at gate k_f(x) is a designated
output gate whose value is not x_j for some j, and this is the first
failure. If k_r(y) < h, the failure is at gate k_r(y) and is also a
designated output.

Proof. Induct on k along the relevant order. Input copies, gates, and
dummy positions uniquely determine the next content bit from data
already determined. These are exactly the bits of U_x^k or V_y^k.
The only possible obstruction is an additional terminal comparison
against a specific already-determined bit. Shortening a valid prefix
removes requirements without changing retained bits, so the set of
valid lengths forms an initial segment. At length h all terminal
comparisons have been imposed, giving the two equivalences. When the
maximal length is < h, the failure is at a designated output and the
first such failure determines all content thereafter.

The macrograph proper extends each of these prefixes into a cyclic
schedule so that a partial computation can be read at multiple
stages. Given (c, b, eta, t), the enabled macro edit at the current
stage depends only on c and b. There are five edit types:

  Fill_A:  pre mode, low progress, A contains k entries with k < h
           and Fill(U; S) is applicable to b as tape A. Writes one
           bit at position k. This is a forward macro step of S.
  Erase_A: pre mode, low progress, A contains k >= 1 entries. Erases
           position k - 1. This is the inverse of Fill_A.
  Copy:    pre mode, low progress, A contains h entries and B is
           blank. Copies A into B. This is the transition from
           forward computation to reverse reconstruction.
  Fill_B:  pre mode, low progress, A contains h entries and B
           contains k < h entries. Extends B by one reverse step of
           P. This is a forward macro step of P.
  Erase_B: post mode (eta = 1), all progress, B contains k >= 1
           entries; erases position k - 1.
  Idle:    at the "hub" stages c = H_*, or A full and B full and
           P(S(x)) = x. The tape is unchanged.

The stage c advances as follows. At pre mode with t < q - 1, the
macro graph is deterministic: increment t. At pre mode with t = q - 1,
apply the enabled macro edit to b, dispatching on the cases above,
and set eta = 1 (moving into post mode) while keeping c fixed. At
post mode with t > 0, decrement t. At post mode with t = 0, set
eta = 0 and advance c to gamma c. When P(S(x)) = x, all four edit
cases are available at c = gamma^j for the appropriate j; when
P(S(x)) != x, one of the cases has no valid value at c = gamma^{j(x)},
which is exactly where the parity argument's second source lies.

LEMMA (reciprocity). For any x in {0, 1}^n and every stage c in C at
which the vertex Q_x = (c, b_x, eta_x, t_x) is defined, the enabled
macro edit at Q_x is the inverse of the enabled macro edit at the
designated successor of Q_x in pre-with-t=q-1 formation. Consequently
each Q_x has at most one incoming edge and at most one outgoing edge
in G_mac. Two distinct x, x' induce no shared edge unless x = x'.

Proof. A tape edit is one of the five types above. Fill_A and Erase_A
are exact inverses: the erased position is uniquely determined by the
state and by the direction of the operation, and Fill_A reads only the
retained positions. Copy and Idle are involutions on the appropriate
state. Fill_B and Erase_B are exact inverses by the same argument.
The commutativity of Fill_A with Copy, and of Fill_B with Erase_B,
follows from the fact that these operate on disjoint tape cells. The
ascending stage order gamma^j -> gamma^{j+1} is well-defined. Because
each edit is a bijection on the tape at its stage, and the tape
encodes 2h bits, distinct x, x' have distinct tape trajectories.

The structure of G_mac around x can be described explicitly. Put
y = S(x). Assume first that y = P(S(x)) as well. Then:

  The unique source of the parity argument relevant to x is
  (c = gamma^{-1}, b = Q_x, eta = 1, t = q - 1), with its immediate
  successor (c = gamma^{-1}, b = Q_x, eta = 1, t = q - 2).

  The other endpoint of the same directed path is the full-pre label
  of stage M - 1 for b = Q_x, i.e. (c = gamma^{M-1}, b = Q_x,
  eta = 0, t = q - 1).

This is the source/sink pair for a valid x. Both lie in G_mac and are
computable in polynomial time in N from x and (S, P).

Now suppose y = S(x) is a solution of End-of-Line, either because
P(y) != x or because some gate of S remains unfilled at position
k_f(x) < h. Then no edge from stage c = gamma^j at which the tape is
Q_x enters stage c = gamma^{j+1} at the same tape (there is a gap at
position k_f(x) in A, or at the terminal comparison at position h);
this gap is itself a designated source of the graph.

The "zero exception" arises at x = 0^n, since the promise of
End-of-Line gives both P(0^n) = 0^n and S(0^n) != 0^n. For this
specific x the tape Q_x = Q_{0^n} evolves through A fully to the value
S(0^n) and then B partially reconstructs to some V^k with k < h
(because the promise of End-of-Line gives P(0^n) = 0^n and
S(0^n) != 0^n, one exceptional vertex must be added to break the
otherwise closed path. Set v_* = (c = gamma^{2 h - k_*},
b = Q_{0^n}, eta, t) that has no predecessor and one successor,
thereby providing the required second source of the parity argument.
This v_* is computable in polynomial time in N from 0^n and (S, P).

LEMMA (exceptional vertex; size). Under the promise of End-of-Line,
G_mac has at most 2 q M 9^h vertices, is polynomially computable from
(S, P), and satisfies the degree bound of the reciprocity lemma.
Every directed cycle of G_mac has length divisible by 4 q, and G_mac
is acyclic as a graph because the stage counter c and progress
counter t strictly increase along directed paths, up to the wrapping
edge at gamma^{M-1} -> gamma^0 which is disabled by the promise.

The parameters h, q, M are chosen in the construction. Fix h = Theta(1)
and q = Theta(1) (both depending only on the gate list length of S and
P, which is at most O(N)); M is Theta(N log^{O(1)} N). This gives
|V_mac| = Theta(N log^{O(1)}(N + 2)). Each tape cell holds O(1) bits
of information, so the complete encoded graph has size O(N
log^{O(1)}(N + 2)).


An outer code placed on the index group V = Γ × {0,1}^2 provides the
PCP with a global word of quasilinear size whose local constraints
catch junk at a constant cost. The code is built in five layers: an
index group Γ of quasilinear order; a small local code on each row
and column; a tensor product; a simultaneous left-and-right twist;
and a systematic payload with a unique parity extension.

Index group. Let K_1 be a finite field of order h_q^ell with h_q a
small power of two and ell a fixed positive integer, and put
C = K_1^{mul} = <gamma>, the multiplicative group of K_1. Let
M = |C| = h_q^ell - 1 and identify C with Z/MZ. Set Γ = C, viewed
as a finite abelian group. Two commuting actions of Γ on itself act
by ak (left multiplication) and kb (right multiplication).

Expansion. Margulis (1973) constructed, for |C| = 2^a with a ≥ 2,
a generating set S ⊂ C of size d_0 = O(1) whose Cayley graph
Cay(C; S) has a second eigenvalue ≤ 1 - lambda_0 for an absolute
constant lambda_0 > 0, and whose expansion is independent of a.
Gabber and Galil (1981) simplify the proof at the cost of a more
restrictive choice of prime power. The proof of Margulis is a direct
sharpening of Kazhdan's property (T); the modern treatment via the
affine group F_2[t]^2 ⋊ SL_2(F_2[t]), together with the effective
Kazhdan constant of Ershov and Jaikin-Zapirain (2010), Theorem 1.1,
supplies the required bound in the form used below.

./ershov-jaikin-2010-0809.4095-property-t.pdf

Prime field. Linnik's theorem, in the effective form of Xylouris
(2011), Theorem 1.1, asserts that for every finite abelian group Γ
of exponent m there exists a prime p with p ≡ 1 (mod m) and
m < p ≤ C_L · m^L, for absolute constants C_L and L. The sharp
numerical value L ≤ 5.2 is more than enough. Fix J = F_{p^4} once
and for all, so |J| = p^4 ≤ (m log m)^{20.8 + o(1)}; identifying m
with |Γ| = Theta(N log^{O(1)} N) at the end, |J| = N^{21 + o(1)}.

./xylouris-2009-0906.2749-linnik.pdf

Four parts. Let V = Γ × {0,1}^2, with four "parts" sigma = (s,t) in
{0,1}^2. At each v = (k, sigma) in V store one d × d matrix
W_k^sigma with entries in J. The choice d ∈ Z^+ is bounded by a
fixed power of log N and does not vary with the outer program.

Local code. Fix a small integer c_0, for instance c_0 = 8. Choose
two c_0 × d scalar matrices H_A, H_B over J satisfying: (i) every
c_0 × c_0 minor is nonzero, and (ii) each row of H_A and H_B has at
most C_0 = O(1) nonzero entries. One concrete choice takes H_A,
H_B to be the first c_0 rows of a d × d Vandermonde matrix on d
distinct evaluation points of J, giving the Reed-Solomon local code
with distance c_0 + 1 in each axis.

Tensor code. The code C_out(V) ⊂ (J^{d × d})^V is the set of
assignments W = (W_k^sigma)_{k, sigma} satisfying, for each
sigma = (s,t) ∈ {0,1}^2, each k ∈ Γ, and each b < c_0:

   sum_{i=1}^{d}  (H_A)_{b, i} · W_{a_i · k}^{sigma ⊕ (1, 0)}  = 0
   sum_{j=1}^{d}  (H_B)_{b, j} · W_{k · b_j}^{sigma ⊕ (0, 1)}  = 0

Here ⊕ is coordinate-wise addition mod 2 on {0,1}^2, and a_1, ...,
a_d; b_1, ..., b_d are the generating elements of Γ supplied by the
expansion above.

The two systems interlock: the left check at sigma involves
W^{sigma ⊕ (1,0)}, so it couples parts (s, t) and (1 - s, t); the
right check couples (s, t) and (s, 1 - t). Together they determine W
from the systematic rectangle at part (0,0), at the four corners of
which the checks meet. The systematic fixing chooses W_k^{(0,0)} for
every k ∈ Γ, and this choice has a unique extension to the other
three parts. When d is large relative to c_0, the resulting code
has dimension exactly |Γ| (d - c_0)^2 in field coordinates and
relative distance exactly 2 (c_0 + 1) / (2 d^2) for the joint
distance.

Encoder. The systematic payload encoder E_{alpha, beta} : U^Γ →
C_out(V), for U = J or U = J^{d × d}, sends v ∈ U^Γ to the unique
code word W whose systematic part is diag(v(k)) for all k (using the
identity-scale choice of the local matrices). Uniqueness of the
extension is a Gaussian-elimination fact; the resulting code has rate
at most 1 / (4 d^2) in field coordinates, so 4 d^2 applications of
the local extension suffice to produce one payload word. In particular,
every symbol of the payload is encoded at exactly 4 d^2 positions of
V, and the outer code's contribution to the total size is
|Γ| · 4 d^2 · (bits per J entry).

Twists. In the matrix-valued parts of the code, the local checks
use two commuting automorphism tuples (alpha_i)_{i=1}^d and
(beta_j)_{j=1}^d of J, indexed by the same generators used in the
row/column systems. They are defined explicitly as follows. Let
H ⊂ Γ be a subgroup of order |H| = h with the property that the
action of Γ on the cosets Γ/H partitions Γ into orbits of size h.
For each distinguished bit e (there are three: e_1, e_2, e_3
indexing the three coordinates of the "spin" that appears in the
geometric part of the argument), set

  rho_e(g, u) e_{t'} = (-1)^{(u t' · e) · g} · e_{u t'}

for (g, u) ∈ Γ ⋊ Aut(H), t' ∈ H, and the character in the exponent
being the standard inner product in the prime field F_p. This is a
projective representation of Γ ⋊ Aut(H) with entries in F_p. In
matrix form put

  alpha_i W = M(rho_e(a_i)) · W,
  beta_j  W = W · M(rho_e(b_j)),

where M(rho_e(a_i)) is the h × h matrix of the representation. The
matrices M(rho_e(a_i)) and M(rho_e(b_j)) commute because right and
left multiplication on the index set Γ commute; consequently
alpha_i beta_j = beta_j alpha_i for all i, j. This "matrix twist"
gives the extended code its structure. There are at most 1 + 3·4
= 13 distinct local types (the untwisted case plus the three bits
times four parity combinations); the tensor product over an arbitrary
finite tuple of local types uses block-diagonal twists.

The distance and correction structure of C_out(V) rests on three
lemmas: a one-axis distance bound, a whole-code packing bound, and a
stability lemma that produces exact codewords at cost linear in the
normalized failure fraction. The three combine into a near-code
theorem that any local-check majority implies proximity to a genuine
codeword, with no slot-count factor.

One-axis distance. Let lambda_0 be the eigenvalue bound of the Cayley
graph Cay(C; S) fixed by the expansion construction, let
delta_1 = (c_0 + 1)/d be the local distance of the row-and-column
code in a single axis, and put Delta_A = (delta_1 - lambda_0)/4 > 0.
Call a word A-only if it is nonzero only in the (1, 0)-and-(1, 1)
parts of V, i.e. lies in one axis slice, so that the B-check is
trivially satisfied by absence of A-entries.

LEMMA (one-axis distance). Every nonzero A-only word W in C_out(V)
has relative vertex support at least Delta_A on V. The same bound
holds for B-only words, for differences of two words sharing an axis
constraint, and for finite joint tuples of such words.

Proof. Fix an A-slice: W supported at (k, s, 0) for one s. The row
code at each k couples d values of W along the A-axis via H_A.
Nonzero W implies at least one row of H_A times W is nonzero at each
k in the support. Let l, u be the vertex-support fractions of W at
the two A-axis endpoint groups. Let e be the fraction of nonzero
carried edges between the two groups; each nonzero k-entry is
carried one-to-one to a nonzero a_i · k-entry by the invertibility
of the twists. Then

   delta_1 * a  <=  e  <=  l * u + lambda_0 * sqrt(l u)  <=  a (a + lambda_0)

where a = max(l, u) and the middle inequality is the usual mixing
bound on Cay(C; S). Hence a >= delta_1 - lambda_0. On two parts,
this gives relative support at least (delta_1 - lambda_0)/2 in the
two-part normalization, which sits at Delta_A in the four-part
normalization of V. The other axis is identical.

Whole-code packing. Two distinct words W, W' in C_out(V) with the
same systematic part differ only in their parity extension, which is
linear over the systematic rectangle at (0, 0). The distance in each
transverse axis is at least c_0 + 1, so the joint Hamming distance
is at least K_* = 4 (1 + 3 d) positions of V. This is the packing
constant used below.

Stability. For W in (J^{d x d})^V, let

   A_fail(W) = { v in V : the A-check at v of W fails },
   B_fail(W) = { v in V : the B-check at v of W fails },

and z_A(W) = |A_fail(W)| / |V|, z_B(W) = |B_fail(W)| / |V|, z(W) =
z_A(W) + z_B(W). At v in V let p_A(v), p_B(v) be the fractions of
A- and B-check positions at v that lie in A_fail(W) and B_fail(W)
respectively, and put p(v) = p_A(v) + p_B(v). A vertex v is stable
for W if p(v) <= delta_1 / 16; W is stable if every v in V is
stable.

LEMMA (stability contraction). There is C_out = C_out(c_0, lambda_0)
> 0 with the following property. If W is stable and z(W) > 0, then

   p(v)  <=  C_out * (P_A p(v) + P_B p(v))

at every v in V, where P_A, P_B are the A-axis and B-axis neighbor
averaging operators on V induced by the left and right multiplication
of Gamma. Consequently z(W) is bounded away from 0 in a fixed
proportion to min_v p(v): if min_v p(v) < delta_1 / 16 and z(W) > 0,
then z(W) >= (delta_1 / 32) * min_v p(v).

Proof. At a stable v, the A-check failure fraction p_A(v) is
dominated by the neighbor disagreement: P_A p(v) is the average A-
neighbor p-value. The local checks at v fail only if at least one A
neighbor is in A_fail(W) or some B-check at v fails; both are
bounded by C_out times the corresponding averaged p value. Averaging
over v and using ||P_A||_2 <= lambda_0 on mean-zero vectors (and
similarly for P_B) gives

   ||p||_2  <=  C_1 (mu + lambda_0 ||p||_2)

where mu = E_v p(v) and C_1 = 4 C_out. Choosing lambda_0 <= 1/8 gives
||p||_2 <= 8 C_1 mu. Since some v has p(v) >= mu and ||p||_2 >=
||p||_1 / sqrt(|V|) actually improves to min_v p(v) >= mu / (1 +
16 C_1), the stated lower bound follows by choosing C_out large
enough that 32 / (1 + 16 C_1) * delta_1 >= (delta_1 / 32) / (2
C_out).

Near-code theorem. Let theta_out = 1 / (100 (1 + 13 d)) and
K_out = 4 * (1 + 6 d). For every finite tuple of full slots W_1,
..., W_R in C_out(V) with joint failure fraction z(W_1) + ... +
z(W_R) <= R theta_out, there exists a unique tuple W_1', ..., W_R'
in C_out(V) with systematically matching payloads such that the
normalized Hamming distance from (W_1, ..., W_R) to (W_1', ...,
W_R') is at most K_out (z(W_1) + ... + z(W_R)).

Proof. Combine the stability lemma with the packing distance K_*.
Iterating the one-step correction (replace A_fail or B_fail entries
by their local decoder values) strictly decreases z(W) by a factor
of at least (1 - Delta_A / 2) at each correction round, because each
failed v in A_fail∖B_fail is corrected in an A-slice whose support is
one-sided, while vertices in A_fail ∩ B_fail are corrected in both
axes. After K_out (z_A + z_B) rounds, z has collapsed to 0, so W'
is exact. Uniqueness of W' follows from the packing distance
K_* > 2K_out theta_out.
