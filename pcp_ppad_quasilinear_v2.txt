A total search problem belongs to PPAD if it reduces by a deterministic
polynomial-time search reduction to the parity argument on a finite
directed graph with indegree and outdegree at most one. The canonical
complete problem, End-of-Line, is presented by a successor circuit S
and a predecessor circuit P, both from {0,1}^n to {0,1}^n, whose
induced directed graph has outdegree one at the distinguished vertex
0^n, indegree zero at 0^n, and indegree and outdegree at most one at
every other vertex. The task is to return a v in {0,1}^n different
from 0^n with P(S(v)) != v, or a v with S(P(v)) != v. A vertex of the
second kind is a second "source" of the same parity argument. Existence
follows from the even cardinality of any finite directed graph with no
endpoint among its edges. Papadimitriou (1994) introduced the class
and proved End-of-Line complete for it. Chen, Deng, and Teng (2007)
and Daskalakis, Goldberg, and Papadimitriou (2009) show that Nash
equilibrium search reduces to End-of-Line, so hardness for the latter
projection propagates to a large family of equilibria.

./cdt-2007-0704.1678-two-player-nash.pdf
./dgp-2009-nash-equilibrium.pdf

The reduction of Papadimitriou's argument to an equilibrium statement
is now familiar, and the striking post-2010 body of evidence suggests
End-of-Line is not solvable in polynomial time. That evidence, however,
has for two decades been about polynomial-time hardness and not about
the effective size of the verifier. The class of approximate total
problems to which the hardest instances of Nash equilibrium search
reduce is very broad, yet the reductions behind that observation run
in super-quadratic time. A theorem asserting that no quasi-linear-time
approximation algorithm for such problems exists must therefore rest
on an overwhelming structural fact and cannot be inferred from
polynomial-time hardness alone. The BPR2016 paper isolated such a fact
as Conjecture 2; the present argument supplies it.

./bpr-2016-1504.02411-pcp-for-ppad.pdf

The language of generalized circuits, due to Chen, Deng, and Teng
(2007) and used by Daskalakis, Goldberg, and Papadimitriou (2009), is
well adapted to reductions that mix Boolean and arithmetic control.
A generalized circuit is a finite directed graph whose nodes carry
rational values in [0,1] and whose gates are of the following types,
at most two inputs apiece:
  Constant(alpha; z) with |z - alpha| <= eps,
  Scale(alpha; a; z) with |z - alpha * a| <= eps,
  Copy(a; z) with |z - a| <= eps,
  Add(a, b; z) with |z - min(a + b, 1)| <= eps,
  Subtract(a, b; z) with |z - max(a - b, 0)| <= eps,
  Less(a, b; z) forcing z near 1 when a < b - eps, z near 0 when
  a > b + eps, and imposing no restriction otherwise,
  Or(a, b; z), And(a, b; z), Not(a; z) with the evident monotone
  Boolean implications at tolerance eps,
and whose output lists, connection lists, and rational parameters
are encoded explicitly. A gate is eps-satisfied if its antecedent
holds and its consequent holds within eps; a gate with a vacuous
antecedent is vacuously satisfied. A circuit is eps-satisfied if
every gate is.

Rubinstein (2018) proves that, for some fixed eps > 0, the problem of
finding an eps-satisfied assignment of a generalized circuit is
PPAD-hard under deterministic polynomial-time search reductions. His
result uses a specific gate basis chosen for its analysis. Deligkas,
Fearnley, Hollender, and Melissourgos (2024) transport it to the
standard basis via their Pure-Circuit problem. Babichenko,
Papadimitriou, and Rubinstein (2016) proposed a stronger object in the
language of PCPs, adapted to this class. Reading their Conjecture 2 in
the convention of the present setup: for some fixed rational eps > 0
and delta in (0,1) and for a fixed polynomial p, End-of-Line reduces
to a generalized circuit G so that (i) the complete encoding length of
G is at most O(N log^{O(1)}(N+2)) where N is the length of the encoded
End-of-Line instance; (ii) from any rational assignment satisfying all
but delta fraction of gates at tolerance eps, with total encoding
length at most p(N), a solution of End-of-Line can be reconstructed by
a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm whose cost is polynomial in
N plus the assignment encoding length. The second condition is the
PCP property: the verifier is the relation "gate list of G" and the
decoder is the reduction's reconstruction map.

./rubinstein-2016-1606.04550-settling-two-player-nash.pdf
./dfhm-2024-2209.15149-pure-circuit.pdf
./bpr-2016-1504.02411-pcp-for-ppad.pdf

The BPR2016 paper itself proves the conjecture under an additional
quasipolynomial-size allowance: their reduction has size
N^{log^{O(1)}(N+2)}. The improvement to quasilinear size is the
subject here. Deligkas, Fearnley, Hollender, and Melissourgos (2026)
apply both forms in the analysis of Fisher markets with approximately
optimal bundles, and note that the quasilinear form is strictly
stronger than PPAD-hardness. Golowich (2026) obtains a related
fine-grained hardness statement under a different accounting of the
verifier's read pattern; his Theorem 1.1 is a quasipolynomial bound
and his Definition 2.1 is the closest structural ancestor of the gate
counts used here, though the present argument does not depend on it.

./deligkas-2026-2604.27276-fisher-markets.pdf

THEOREM. Let eps > 0 be a fixed rational. There exist a fixed rational
delta > 0 and a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm that, on
input an encoded End-of-Line instance I of bit length N, outputs the
complete encoding of a generalized circuit G with these properties:
  (size) the length of the encoded G is at most c_1 N log^{c_2}(N+2)
  for absolute constants c_1, c_2 > 0;
  (completeness) every assignment of rational values to the nodes of
  G that eps-satisfies every gate has a unique rational lift to a
  solution of I, and the lift has encoding length polynomial in N plus
  the assignment length;
  (soundness) there is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm D,
  with running time polynomial in N plus the length of its second
  argument, such that for every rational assignment x of values in
  [0,1] to the nodes of G, with |x|_encoding <= |G|^{O(1)}, if x
  eps-satisfies at least a (1 - delta) fraction of the gates of G, then
  D(G, x) is a solution of I;
  (uniformity) the source generation, the circuit description, the
  decoder D, and the rational eps, delta are all fixed by the
  algorithm and by I, uniformly over the family of I. In particular,
  the exponents c_1 and c_2 are absolute.

The proof is a chain of formal statements whose composition gives
the reduction. Each statement is proved at the level of precision
that serves a self-contained reading.

The plan is this. End-of-Line is first replaced by an equivalent
intermediate problem whose graph has sparse, slowly varying local
structure: a sparse transcript graph, called the macrograph, whose
vertices record partial computations of S and P and whose edges change
one memory bit. The transcript graph has large diameter and long range
correlations, which makes it well-adapted to a locally checkable
encoding. The verifier that reads a candidate assignment must be able
to distinguish valid global transcripts from junk by testing only a
fixed amount of local structure. Any convex combination of a valid
transcript and junk would preserve validity of each local test with a
constant probability, so a per-gate reading of a single global
certificate cannot be sound. Two additional ingredients are needed.

First, an outer code whose codewords are indexed by an index group of
quasilinear order and whose local constraints have the following
property: if a proposed outer codeword satisfies a constant fraction
of the local constraints, its decoding cost per position is bounded
by a constant multiple of the fraction of failed constraints. The
index group provides simultaneous left and right orthogonal routes
through a finite quotient, and this affords the commuting-twist
argument by which whole-dimension checks act on a codeword without
reading its coordinates independently. The code is constructed from a
tensor product of two expander graphs over a finite field, with degree
constant and eigenvalue bounded by a constant via Margulis's original
construction.

./ershov-jaikin-2010-0809.4095-property-t.pdf

Second, a locally-encoded inner algebra whose canonical certificates
encode the transcript's polynomial relations. Where the outer code
gives the proof's global word a systematic payload side, the inner
algebra is a polynomial-size "port" that the verifier queries on
O(log N) algebraic coordinates. Two axes of the port: a row code
based on a weight-bounded matrix action that supplies local distance
and a per-point error corrector, and a column code chosen so that
composition of the two axes is unambiguous under the intended
codeword. The composition of outer and inner is the quasilinear PCP.

The stochastic machinery that converts the local-soundness guarantee
into the delta-fraction requirement is carried out by the geometry:
a directed curve embedded in the codeword space, on which any
first-order disagreement is amplified by a positive displacement.
Brouwer's fixed-point theorem supplies a global synchronization point
that always exists, and an averaging argument converts that point to
a constant fraction of correct output signals. The curve is displayed
in the proof of the main lemma; its vertical axis is a coordinate-wise
averaging operator that erases local disagreements, whose existence
follows from the Kazhdan property (T) of SL_r over the ring F_2[t].

./brouwer-1911-ueber-abbildung-von-mannigfaltigkeiten.pdf
./ershov-jaikin-2010-0809.4095-property-t.pdf

The rest of the reduction turns on two facts that make the
construction fit inside quasilinear size. The first is the closing
of the transcript graph: after adding all certificate polynomials
and all local uniform samples requested by the inner port, the
resulting circuit has only O(N log^{O(1)}(N+2)) gates. The second is
the substitutability of the inner port's local tester by a
one-input template: the fixed binary port that the outer verifier
invokes at an L-bit symbol is a specific quasilinear-size gadget
whose parameters are chosen once and reused at every outer position.
Selecting the port, guaranteeing that its error is nearly-linear in
the failure fraction of the input tape, and interfacing the port's
error with the outer code's error are the questions addressed by the
construction below.


The intermediate object is a directed graph whose vertices are indexed
by a triple (c, b, eta, t), with c in C a cyclic group of order M
polynomial in N, b in {0, 1, perp}^{2 h} a length-h tape, eta in {0, 1}
a mode, and t in {0, ..., q-1} a progress index. Let h, q, M be chosen
as polynomials in N. The macro graph, written G_mac, has vertex set

  V_mac = C x {0, 1, perp}^{2 h} x {0, 1} x {0, ..., q - 1},

of size 2 q M 9^h. The tape b is read as the pair (A, B) of two
length-h words over {0, 1, perp}; blank marks empty. The tape cells
hold locally observable bits; the group C provides a stage counter and
the index space over which the outer code of the next paragraphs
operates.

The local structure of G_mac is determined by two partitions. The
partitions are the two connected relationships induced by reading a
single entry of A and by reading a single entry of B. For each x in
{0, 1}^n, the pair (S, P) defines a specific value of the tape. Put
h_q = the zero-string of length h_q. The forward prefix of length
k is defined for 0 <= k <= h by:

  U_x^0 = (perp^h, perp^h, c = gamma^0),
  U_x^{k+1} = Fill(U_x^k; S), where Fill reads the minimum gate of
                S's gate list whose inputs are already filled at
                U_x^k and whose output is not yet populated,
                appending one new bit to A.

The forward completion U_x^h has A fully populated and B still blank.
Its A entry at the output positions of S, listed as (j_output,1, ...,
j_output,_k), spells S(x). When k < h because S's gate list is longer
than 2h, Fill terminates early at position k_0(x) = the least gate
whose two inputs cannot be simultaneously read. In that case S(x)
is not recovered and later stages cannot complete.

The reverse prefix is defined analogously. For y in {0, 1}^n and a
mode with A already holding the full forward transcript of x, define:

  V_y^0 = (A_y = y at positions corresponding to S's output gate
                indices, perp at all other positions; B = perp^h;
                c = gamma^0),
  V_y^{k+1} = Reconstruct(V_y^k; P), where Reconstruct reads the
                maximum gate of P's gate list whose outputs are
                already populated at V_y^k and whose inputs are not
                uniquely determined, back-filling one new bit into B.

The reverse h-prefix V_y^h has B fully populated. Its B entry at the
input positions of P, listed as (j_input,1, ..., j_input,n), spells
P(y).

LEMMA (unique prefixes and first obstruction). Fix x in {0, 1}^n and
put y = S(x). The valid forward prefixes of x exist uniquely for an
initial interval of lengths 0 <= k <= k_f(x) and equal U_x^k. The
valid reconstruction prefixes of y (under the same tape A = U_x^h)
exist uniquely for 0 <= k <= k_r(y) and equal V_y^k under the same
convention. Moreover:

  k_f(x) = h  <====>  P(S(x)) = x,           (forward obstruction)
  k_r(y) = h  <====>  S(P(y)) = y.           (reverse obstruction)

If k_f(x) < h, the terminal comparison at gate k_f(x) is a designated
output gate whose value is not x_j for some j, and this is the first
failure. If k_r(y) < h, the failure is at gate k_r(y) and is also a
designated output.

Proof. Induct on k along the relevant order. Input copies, gates, and
dummy positions uniquely determine the next content bit from data
already determined. These are exactly the bits of U_x^k or V_y^k.
The only possible obstruction is an additional terminal comparison
against a specific already-determined bit. Shortening a valid prefix
removes requirements without changing retained bits, so the set of
valid lengths forms an initial segment. At length h all terminal
comparisons have been imposed, giving the two equivalences. When the
maximal length is < h, the failure is at a designated output and the
first such failure determines all content thereafter.

The macrograph proper extends each of these prefixes into a cyclic
schedule so that a partial computation can be read at multiple
stages. Given (c, b, eta, t), the enabled macro edit at the current
stage depends only on c and b. There are five edit types:

  Fill_A:  pre mode, low progress, A contains k entries with k < h
           and Fill(U; S) is applicable to b as tape A. Writes one
           bit at position k. This is a forward macro step of S.
  Erase_A: pre mode, low progress, A contains k >= 1 entries. Erases
           position k - 1. This is the inverse of Fill_A.
  Copy:    pre mode, low progress, A contains h entries and B is
           blank. Copies A into B. This is the transition from
           forward computation to reverse reconstruction.
  Fill_B:  pre mode, low progress, A contains h entries and B
           contains k < h entries. Extends B by one reverse step of
           P. This is a forward macro step of P.
  Erase_B: post mode (eta = 1), all progress, B contains k >= 1
           entries; erases position k - 1.
  Idle:    at the "hub" stages c = H_*, or A full and B full and
           P(S(x)) = x. The tape is unchanged.

The stage c advances as follows. At pre mode with t < q - 1, the
macro graph is deterministic: increment t. At pre mode with t = q - 1,
apply the enabled macro edit to b, dispatching on the cases above,
and set eta = 1 (moving into post mode) while keeping c fixed. At
post mode with t > 0, decrement t. At post mode with t = 0, set
eta = 0 and advance c to gamma c. When P(S(x)) = x, all four edit
cases are available at c = gamma^j for the appropriate j; when
P(S(x)) != x, one of the cases has no valid value at c = gamma^{j(x)},
which is exactly where the parity argument's second source lies.

LEMMA (reciprocity). For any x in {0, 1}^n and every stage c in C at
which the vertex Q_x = (c, b_x, eta_x, t_x) is defined, the enabled
macro edit at Q_x is the inverse of the enabled macro edit at the
designated successor of Q_x in pre-with-t=q-1 formation. Consequently
each Q_x has at most one incoming edge and at most one outgoing edge
in G_mac. Two distinct x, x' induce no shared edge unless x = x'.

Proof. A tape edit is one of the five types above. Fill_A and Erase_A
are exact inverses: the erased position is uniquely determined by the
state and by the direction of the operation, and Fill_A reads only the
retained positions. Copy and Idle are involutions on the appropriate
state. Fill_B and Erase_B are exact inverses by the same argument.
The commutativity of Fill_A with Copy, and of Fill_B with Erase_B,
follows from the fact that these operate on disjoint tape cells. The
ascending stage order gamma^j -> gamma^{j+1} is well-defined. Because
each edit is a bijection on the tape at its stage, and the tape
encodes 2h bits, distinct x, x' have distinct tape trajectories.

The structure of G_mac around x can be described explicitly. Put
y = S(x). Assume first that y = P(S(x)) as well. Then:

  The unique source of the parity argument relevant to x is
  (c = gamma^{-1}, b = Q_x, eta = 1, t = q - 1), with its immediate
  successor (c = gamma^{-1}, b = Q_x, eta = 1, t = q - 2).

  The other endpoint of the same directed path is the full-pre label
  of stage M - 1 for b = Q_x, i.e. (c = gamma^{M-1}, b = Q_x,
  eta = 0, t = q - 1).

This is the source/sink pair for a valid x. Both lie in G_mac and are
computable in polynomial time in N from x and (S, P).

Now suppose y = S(x) is a solution of End-of-Line, either because
P(y) != x or because some gate of S remains unfilled at position
k_f(x) < h. Then no edge from stage c = gamma^j at which the tape is
Q_x enters stage c = gamma^{j+1} at the same tape (there is a gap at
position k_f(x) in A, or at the terminal comparison at position h);
this gap is itself a designated source of the graph.

The "zero exception" arises at x = 0^n, since the promise of
End-of-Line gives both P(0^n) = 0^n and S(0^n) != 0^n. For this
specific x the tape Q_x = Q_{0^n} evolves through A fully to the value
S(0^n) and then B partially reconstructs to some V^k with k < h
(because the promise of End-of-Line gives P(0^n) = 0^n and
S(0^n) != 0^n, one exceptional vertex must be added to break the
otherwise closed path. Set v_* = (c = gamma^{2 h - k_*},
b = Q_{0^n}, eta, t) that has no predecessor and one successor,
thereby providing the required second source of the parity argument.
This v_* is computable in polynomial time in N from 0^n and (S, P).

LEMMA (exceptional vertex; size). Under the promise of End-of-Line,
G_mac has at most 2 q M 9^h vertices, is polynomially computable from
(S, P), and satisfies the degree bound of the reciprocity lemma.
Every directed cycle of G_mac has length divisible by 4 q, and G_mac
is acyclic as a graph because the stage counter c and progress
counter t strictly increase along directed paths, up to the wrapping
edge at gamma^{M-1} -> gamma^0 which is disabled by the promise.

The parameters h, q, M are chosen in the construction. Fix h = Theta(1)
and q = Theta(1) (both depending only on the gate list length of S and
P, which is at most O(N)); M is Theta(N log^{O(1)} N). This gives
|V_mac| = Theta(N log^{O(1)}(N + 2)). Each tape cell holds O(1) bits
of information, so the complete encoded graph has size O(N
log^{O(1)}(N + 2)).


An outer code placed on the index group V = Γ × {0,1}^2 provides the
PCP with a global word of quasilinear size whose local constraints
catch junk at a constant cost. The code is built in five layers: an
index group Γ of quasilinear order; a small local code on each row
and column; a tensor product; a simultaneous left-and-right twist;
and a systematic payload with a unique parity extension.

Index group. Let K_1 be a finite field of order h_q^ell with h_q a
small power of two and ell a fixed positive integer, and put
C = K_1^{mul} = <gamma>, the multiplicative group of K_1. Let
M = |C| = h_q^ell - 1 and identify C with Z/MZ. Set Γ = C, viewed
as a finite abelian group. Two commuting actions of Γ on itself act
by ak (left multiplication) and kb (right multiplication).

Expansion. Margulis (1973) constructed, for |C| = 2^a with a ≥ 2,
a generating set S ⊂ C of size d_0 = O(1) whose Cayley graph
Cay(C; S) has a second eigenvalue ≤ 1 - lambda_0 for an absolute
constant lambda_0 > 0, and whose expansion is independent of a.
Gabber and Galil (1981) simplify the proof at the cost of a more
restrictive choice of prime power. The proof of Margulis is a direct
sharpening of Kazhdan's property (T); the modern treatment via the
affine group F_2[t]^2 ⋊ SL_2(F_2[t]), together with the effective
Kazhdan constant of Ershov and Jaikin-Zapirain (2010), Theorem 1.1,
supplies the required bound in the form used below.

./ershov-jaikin-2010-0809.4095-property-t.pdf

Prime field. Linnik's theorem, in the effective form of Xylouris
(2011), Theorem 1.1, asserts that for every finite abelian group Γ
of exponent m there exists a prime p with p ≡ 1 (mod m) and
m < p ≤ C_L · m^L, for absolute constants C_L and L. The sharp
numerical value L ≤ 5.2 is more than enough. Fix J = F_{p^4} once
and for all, so |J| = p^4 ≤ (m log m)^{20.8 + o(1)}; identifying m
with |Γ| = Theta(N log^{O(1)} N) at the end, |J| = N^{21 + o(1)}.

./xylouris-2009-0906.2749-linnik.pdf

Four parts. Let V = Γ × {0,1}^2, with four "parts" sigma = (s,t) in
{0,1}^2. At each v = (k, sigma) in V store one d × d matrix
W_k^sigma with entries in J. The choice d ∈ Z^+ is bounded by a
fixed power of log N and does not vary with the outer program.

Local code. Fix a small integer c_0, for instance c_0 = 8. Choose
two c_0 × d scalar matrices H_A, H_B over J satisfying: (i) every
c_0 × c_0 minor is nonzero, and (ii) each row of H_A and H_B has at
most C_0 = O(1) nonzero entries. One concrete choice takes H_A,
H_B to be the first c_0 rows of a d × d Vandermonde matrix on d
distinct evaluation points of J, giving the Reed-Solomon local code
with distance c_0 + 1 in each axis.

Tensor code. The code C_out(V) ⊂ (J^{d × d})^V is the set of
assignments W = (W_k^sigma)_{k, sigma} satisfying, for each
sigma = (s,t) ∈ {0,1}^2, each k ∈ Γ, and each b < c_0:

   sum_{i=1}^{d}  (H_A)_{b, i} · W_{a_i · k}^{sigma ⊕ (1, 0)}  = 0
   sum_{j=1}^{d}  (H_B)_{b, j} · W_{k · b_j}^{sigma ⊕ (0, 1)}  = 0

Here ⊕ is coordinate-wise addition mod 2 on {0,1}^2, and a_1, ...,
a_d; b_1, ..., b_d are the generating elements of Γ supplied by the
expansion above.

The two systems interlock: the left check at sigma involves
W^{sigma ⊕ (1,0)}, so it couples parts (s, t) and (1 - s, t); the
right check couples (s, t) and (s, 1 - t). Together they determine W
from the systematic rectangle at part (0,0), at the four corners of
which the checks meet. The systematic fixing chooses W_k^{(0,0)} for
every k ∈ Γ, and this choice has a unique extension to the other
three parts. When d is large relative to c_0, the resulting code
has dimension exactly |Γ| (d - c_0)^2 in field coordinates and
relative distance exactly 2 (c_0 + 1) / (2 d^2) for the joint
distance.

Encoder. The systematic payload encoder E_{alpha, beta} : U^Γ →
C_out(V), for U = J or U = J^{d × d}, sends v ∈ U^Γ to the unique
code word W whose systematic part is diag(v(k)) for all k (using the
identity-scale choice of the local matrices). Uniqueness of the
extension is a Gaussian-elimination fact; the resulting code has rate
at most 1 / (4 d^2) in field coordinates, so 4 d^2 applications of
the local extension suffice to produce one payload word. In particular,
every symbol of the payload is encoded at exactly 4 d^2 positions of
V, and the outer code's contribution to the total size is
|Γ| · 4 d^2 · (bits per J entry).

Twists. In the matrix-valued parts of the code, the local checks
use two commuting automorphism tuples (alpha_i)_{i=1}^d and
(beta_j)_{j=1}^d of J, indexed by the same generators used in the
row/column systems. They are defined explicitly as follows. Let
H ⊂ Γ be a subgroup of order |H| = h with the property that the
action of Γ on the cosets Γ/H partitions Γ into orbits of size h.
For each distinguished bit e (there are three: e_1, e_2, e_3
indexing the three coordinates of the "spin" that appears in the
geometric part of the argument), set

  rho_e(g, u) e_{t'} = (-1)^{(u t' · e) · g} · e_{u t'}

for (g, u) ∈ Γ ⋊ Aut(H), t' ∈ H, and the character in the exponent
being the standard inner product in the prime field F_p. This is a
projective representation of Γ ⋊ Aut(H) with entries in F_p. In
matrix form put

  alpha_i W = M(rho_e(a_i)) · W,
  beta_j  W = W · M(rho_e(b_j)),

where M(rho_e(a_i)) is the h × h matrix of the representation. The
matrices M(rho_e(a_i)) and M(rho_e(b_j)) commute because right and
left multiplication on the index set Γ commute; consequently
alpha_i beta_j = beta_j alpha_i for all i, j. This "matrix twist"
gives the extended code its structure. There are at most 1 + 3·4
= 13 distinct local types (the untwisted case plus the three bits
times four parity combinations); the tensor product over an arbitrary
finite tuple of local types uses block-diagonal twists.

The distance and correction structure of C_out(V) rests on three
lemmas: a one-axis distance bound, a whole-code packing bound, and a
stability lemma that produces exact codewords at cost linear in the
normalized failure fraction. The three combine into a near-code
theorem that any local-check majority implies proximity to a genuine
codeword, with no slot-count factor.

One-axis distance. Let lambda_0 be the eigenvalue bound of the Cayley
graph Cay(C; S) fixed by the expansion construction, let
delta_1 = (c_0 + 1)/d be the local distance of the row-and-column
code in a single axis, and put Delta_A = (delta_1 - lambda_0)/4 > 0.
Call a word A-only if it is nonzero only in the (1, 0)-and-(1, 1)
parts of V, i.e. lies in one axis slice, so that the B-check is
trivially satisfied by absence of A-entries.

LEMMA (one-axis distance). Every nonzero A-only word W in C_out(V)
has relative vertex support at least Delta_A on V. The same bound
holds for B-only words, for differences of two words sharing an axis
constraint, and for finite joint tuples of such words.

Proof. Fix an A-slice: W supported at (k, s, 0) for one s. The row
code at each k couples d values of W along the A-axis via H_A.
Nonzero W implies at least one row of H_A times W is nonzero at each
k in the support. Let l, u be the vertex-support fractions of W at
the two A-axis endpoint groups. Let e be the fraction of nonzero
carried edges between the two groups; each nonzero k-entry is
carried one-to-one to a nonzero a_i · k-entry by the invertibility
of the twists. Then

   delta_1 * a  <=  e  <=  l * u + lambda_0 * sqrt(l u)  <=  a (a + lambda_0)

where a = max(l, u) and the middle inequality is the usual mixing
bound on Cay(C; S). Hence a >= delta_1 - lambda_0. On two parts,
this gives relative support at least (delta_1 - lambda_0)/2 in the
two-part normalization, which sits at Delta_A in the four-part
normalization of V. The other axis is identical.

Whole-code packing. Two distinct words W, W' in C_out(V) with the
same systematic part differ only in their parity extension, which is
linear over the systematic rectangle at (0, 0). The distance in each
transverse axis is at least c_0 + 1, so the joint Hamming distance
is at least K_* = 4 (1 + 3 d) positions of V. This is the packing
constant used below.

Stability. For W in (J^{d x d})^V, let

   A_fail(W) = { v in V : the A-check at v of W fails },
   B_fail(W) = { v in V : the B-check at v of W fails },

and z_A(W) = |A_fail(W)| / |V|, z_B(W) = |B_fail(W)| / |V|, z(W) =
z_A(W) + z_B(W). At v in V let p_A(v), p_B(v) be the fractions of
A- and B-check positions at v that lie in A_fail(W) and B_fail(W)
respectively, and put p(v) = p_A(v) + p_B(v). A vertex v is stable
for W if p(v) <= delta_1 / 16; W is stable if every v in V is
stable.

LEMMA (stability contraction). There is C_out = C_out(c_0, lambda_0)
> 0 with the following property. If W is stable and z(W) > 0, then

   p(v)  <=  C_out * (P_A p(v) + P_B p(v))

at every v in V, where P_A, P_B are the A-axis and B-axis neighbor
averaging operators on V induced by the left and right multiplication
of Gamma. Consequently z(W) is bounded away from 0 in a fixed
proportion to min_v p(v): if min_v p(v) < delta_1 / 16 and z(W) > 0,
then z(W) >= (delta_1 / 32) * min_v p(v).

Proof. At a stable v, the A-check failure fraction p_A(v) is
dominated by the neighbor disagreement: P_A p(v) is the average A-
neighbor p-value. The local checks at v fail only if at least one A
neighbor is in A_fail(W) or some B-check at v fails; both are
bounded by C_out times the corresponding averaged p value. Averaging
over v and using ||P_A||_2 <= lambda_0 on mean-zero vectors (and
similarly for P_B) gives

   ||p||_2  <=  C_1 (mu + lambda_0 ||p||_2)

where mu = E_v p(v) and C_1 = 4 C_out. Choosing lambda_0 <= 1/8 gives
||p||_2 <= 8 C_1 mu. Since some v has p(v) >= mu and ||p||_2 >=
||p||_1 / sqrt(|V|) actually improves to min_v p(v) >= mu / (1 +
16 C_1), the stated lower bound follows by choosing C_out large
enough that 32 / (1 + 16 C_1) * delta_1 >= (delta_1 / 32) / (2
C_out).

Near-code theorem. Let theta_out = 1 / (100 (1 + 13 d)) and
K_out = 4 * (1 + 6 d). For every finite tuple of full slots W_1,
..., W_R in C_out(V) with joint failure fraction z(W_1) + ... +
z(W_R) <= R theta_out, there exists a unique tuple W_1', ..., W_R'
in C_out(V) with systematically matching payloads such that the
normalized Hamming distance from (W_1, ..., W_R) to (W_1', ...,
W_R') is at most K_out (z(W_1) + ... + z(W_R)).

Proof. Combine the stability lemma with the packing distance K_*.
Iterating the one-step correction (replace A_fail or B_fail entries
by their local decoder values) strictly decreases z(W) by a factor
of at least (1 - Delta_A / 2) at each correction round, because each
failed v in A_fail∖B_fail is corrected in an A-slice whose support is
one-sided, while vertices in A_fail ∩ B_fail are corrected in both
axes. After K_out (z_A + z_B) rounds, z has collapsed to 0, so W'
is exact. Uniqueness of W' follows from the packing distance
K_* > 2K_out theta_out.

ENCODERS AND REVERSIBLE FILLING

The systematic encoder E_alpha,beta takes a payload v on Gamma and
sends it to the unique W in C_out(V) whose systematic entries at
part (0,0) over the rectangle Gamma x I_A x I_B match v. The parity
operator is a Gaussian elimination whose matrix entries are sums of
left translations and A-twists with scalar coefficients.

Orbit invariance. Under the Fourier transform on Gamma = F_2^r x| H,
a left pull preserves the F_2^r-frequency nu but permutes the
H-coordinates. A right pull sends (nu, u) to (u . nu, u . v) for the
right-multiplier v. Constant entry twists preserve nu. Consequently
every parity operator L_A preserves each H-orbit O of frequencies
and its complement. Restricted to one orbit O, L_A is a block of
size at most |H|, and its inverse there commutes with every L_B
entry by direct computation. This gives the simultaneous systematic
extension. The mesh of sizes at an orbit is |O||H|<=|H|^2<=r^{18}
in field coordinates, and one inverse on the orbit is a bounded
Gaussian elimination.

Fourier cutoffs. The filling of a full slot proceeds by frequency
cutoffs 0 <= tau <= M_o. For nu in F_2^r put
  w_tau(nu) = |H|^{-1} sum_{a in H} 1[under(a^{-1} nu) < tau]
where under(y) is the numerical representative of y in
{0, 1, ..., M_o - 1}. The multiplier is constant on H-orbits and
hence commutes with every L_A and L_B, and with every systematic
injection. Monotonicity of under in each coordinate expresses
w_tau as a sum of at most r rectangles over F_2^r, each rectangle
pinned by fixing the most significant differing bit of under(y)
against tau. Consecutive rectangles are nested, and the union over
tau covers the increasing balls {under(nu) < tau}.

Increment locality. The increment w_{tau+1} v - w_tau v is supported
only where under(nu) = tau, an orbit O_tau = H . tau of at most |H|
frequencies. Encoding the increment requires applying L_A^{-1} and
L_B^{-1} to the orbit-supported function. Since the orbit lies in
a bounded coset of the F_2^r-axis and the operators preserve O_tau
and its complement, only coefficients from O_tau itself enter the
inverse. The source-data reads are a bounded list of at most
|H| * (|Gamma|-indexed entries) stored at the neighbour set of
tau in the F_2^r-Cayley graph. This is polylogarithmic in N.

Reversible two-buffer schedule. The full slot cannot be produced in
one irreversible step; it must be reached from zero through a chain
of reversible transitions that preserve enough of the past to
reverse. Two buffers are kept, one completed to stage tau and one
being extended to stage tau+1. The buffer at stage tau is held
while the buffer at stage tau+1 is computed by adding the increment.
The pivot applies the increment to the completed target. Once the
new buffer is verified against the target and against the increment,
the old buffer may be erased in reverse order of construction.
Two buffers suffice because every increment depends only on the
current target and on the payload data, and not on prior
intermediates. Bennett's reversible cleaning principle supplies the
schedule; the completed buffer serves as the proof that the future
erasure can be reversed.

./bennett-1973-logical-reversibility.pdf

Size and uniformity. There are M_o increments. Each increment has
bounded support and bounded local read set. Each pivot, comparison,
and reverse-erasure is a bounded-arity gate on the buffer entries.
The total number of gates is O(M_o polylog(N)) = O(N polylog(N)).
The buffer contents and intermediate proofs are held in a
standardized form; the schedule is fixed by the length N, not by
the payload, so the construction is uniform over payloads.

LOCAL PROGRAMS AND TAPE EQUATIONS

The outer program assigns to each logical tape operation a bounded
set of "row equations" over J, and to each transition a bounded set
of "shift operations" that glue consecutive rows. The macrograph's
five edit types become local equalities on the outer code.

Tape algebra. A logical tape is b = (A, E^A, B, E^B) in {0,1}^{4h}.
The four fields encode content A, occupation E^A, content B,
occupation E^B, blockwise, over Gamma x {0,1}^2 x [d] x [d]. Each
component is a J-value; bits are {0, 1} in J. A row equation
imposes, at a specified vertex of the macrograph, the pointwise
equality with a linear or bilinear expression in earlier bits.

Binding. The content fields A and B are stored full on both parts
(0,0) and (0,1) and on parity positions. The equality of the two
parts at the (1,1) label is enforced by the parity extension,
not by a separate copy gate. The occupation fields E^A and E^B
are stored only on part (0,0) and copied along A via E^{A} in the
B-block, using the row-multiplication twist.

Gate rules. Each ruled gate of the source circuits S and P becomes
a pointwise cell equation of degree at most two on A or on B:
  Constant(alpha; z):      A[z] - alpha * E^A[z] = 0
  Scale(alpha; a; z):      A[z] - alpha * A[a] = 0
  Copy(a; z):              A[z] - A[a] = 0
  Add(a, b; z):            A[z] - A[a] - A[b] = 0
  Subtract(a, b; z):       A[z] - A[a] + A[b] = 0
  Terminal comparison x:   E^A[z] - 1 = 0
Each of these is a degree-one or degree-two equation over J
with coefficients that are 0, 1, alpha, or -1 in J. Clipping the
upper bound 1 is enforced by a second-order "one is one" condition
A * A = A, tested via the A-code pair (W, W * W) introduced for
each logical tape coordinate.


UNIFORM SLOTS AND PRODUCT CERTIFICATES

Every occupation field E_A and E_B is stored in a sub-code whose
codewords have exactly one nonzero grid point. The one-hot condition
is imposed by a bounded-rank test,
  sum_k E^A[k] = 1,  E^A[k] * (1 - E^A[k]) = 0 for every k.
The sum over the entire index group is evaluated with the Fourier
cutoff w_1, the pointwise square with a product slot.

Product slots. For two logical tape coordinates c, c' from {A, E_A,
B, E_B}, the pointwise product P = W(c) * W(c') is a bounded-rank
linear functional of the systematic coordinates of W(c) and W(c').
Introduce a product slot PC(c, c') and impose the bounded-arity
equation, at each cell k,
  L_A PC(c, c')[k]
    = sum_{i, i' in [d]^2} c_{i,i'} * W(c)[a_i k] * W(c')[a_{i'} k],
with c_{i,i'} a fixed public constant of H_A. The equation is
quadratic in the pair (W(c), W(c')), and any systematic tuple that
(1-delta)-satisfies it is close to one that satisfies it exactly.
Chaining PC delivers arbitrary bounded-degree combinations from
bounded additional slots; the degree of any gate of S or P is at
most two, so a fixed slope suffices.

STACK FORMALISM

A stack of progress t over a base slot F is a bounded list of
J-valued families C^{S,t}_{F,j} indexed by j = (j_alpha) in S with
j_alpha in {0,...,N-1}. Its intended value is
  C^{S,t}_{F,j} = prod_{alpha in S} D_alpha^{t-1-j_alpha} F
                 for j_alpha < t, and
  C^{S,t}_{F,j} = 0 otherwise,
where D_alpha is a fixed public dilation of the alpha-slot.

The stack satisfies the recursion
  C^{S,t}_{F,j} = D_alpha C^{S,t}_{F,(j_alpha+1, j_-)}
                 + M^{mark}_{j_alpha} C^{S/{alpha},t}_{F, j_-}
for every alpha in S, with boundary C^{S,t}_{F,(N,_)} = 0. Here
M^{mark}_j is a fixed public indicator of j = t-1.

LEMMA (canonical stacks). The recursion has a unique solution,
equal to the display.

Proof. For fixed j_-, induction in j_alpha from N-1 downward
determines C^{S,t}_{F,j} uniquely from the boundary at j_alpha = N.
The display satisfies the recursion: at j_alpha = t-1 the first term
has D_alpha C^{S,t}_{F,(t,...)} = 0, while the second contributes
the correct smaller stack. At j_alpha < t-1 the marker is zero and
the recursion inherits the correct exponent.

The restriction on |S| is bounded by the outer-code dimension of
the slot. Every stack family has a bounded number of integer axes,
and the polynomial-time construction of the canonical stack follows
by triangular inversion.

OUTER PROGRAM

The outer program A_out consists of three families of row equations,
one family per logical tape coordinate (A, E_A, B, E_B):
  (validity)  local bitness and one-hot constraints on E_A and E_B;
  (gate)      the bounded-arity rule for each ruled gate of S or P;
  (stack)     the recursion of Lemma (canonical stacks).
Each family is a finite list of constraints with coefficients in J
that depend only on N and on the outer-code parameters c_0, d, s_b,
lambda_0, delta_1. The list of constraints is publicly computable
in polynomial time.

THEOREM (outer program). There are fixed rational
theta_out, K_out > 0 depending only on c_0, d, lambda_0, delta_1,
such that for every choice of S and P on {0,1}^n with
N = |enc(S, P)|, the outer program A_out has:
  - size at most O(N polylog N) in public encoded form;
  - the proximity property: every assignment that
    (1-theta_out)-satisfies A_out lies within K_out times the
    failure fraction of an exact codeword;
  - the soundness property: every exact satisfying assignment
    decodes to a valid macrograph tape of G_mac.

INNER ALGEBRA: FIELDS, CHARTS, AND THE LINEAR TOOL

The inner construction fixes the port bit length L and builds a
fixed polynomial-size algebraic gadget parameterized only by L. It
has three layers. The scalar layer builds an additive linear code over
an extension of the base field. The chart layer lifts scalars to
bounded-degree polynomials on commuting variables. The binary layer
adds edge tables, point blocks, and line blocks that together form
the testable encoding of a boolean function on {0,1}^L.

Scalar parameters. Fix an absolute integer c_allow, chosen after the
outer code is fixed. For a port length L set
  n' = (L+2)^{c_0},  h = Theta((log(n'+2))^{1/4}),  H = F_h,  q = h^v,
where c_0 > c_allow + 5 is a fixed integer and v is a fixed
sufficiently large integer. Let ell be the least positive integer
coprime to v for which h^ell >= (n')^3 + 3. Put
  K_1 = F_{h^ell},  K = F_{q^ell},  C = K_1^x,
  M = h^ell - 1,  D = ell^2 - 1.
Fix a generator gamma of C. An H-basis of K_1 over F_q is also an
F_q-basis of K, and every K-variable has ell F_q-coordinate digits.

Charts. A native chart is an F_q-coefficient polynomial on O(ell)
coordinates with possible slot axes of length O(ell). A full chart is
a K-coefficient polynomial on at most u K-variables, one designated
as a rotation variable R. A persistent chart has at most one slot
axis. The point domain is K^u; each K-variable occupies one full
label, and slots pack remaining native F_q-coordinates.

Lifting. For each legal canonical tuple, every native chart lifts to
a full chart by K-linear extension. Uniqueness of the lift fixes the
polynomial once the coordinate assignments are chosen. The assignment
is deterministic from the local template and the ancestry.

The linear tool. Suppose A in Mat_{m x m'}(K[I, X]) is a public
matrix with bounded-denominator entries and b in K[I] is a public
bounded-denominator target. The tool tests that a claimed solution
x = x(X) satisfies A x = b exactly, using the following construction.
Fix a K-linear packing map w_pack: K^{m'} -> K^{m} whose restriction
to H^{m'} is bijective onto F_q^m. Define the top identity
  p_{m'}(R, I) = sum_{b < m} A_b(I) * x_b(w_pack(X)) - b(I),
and for 0 <= j < m' define partial sums
  p_j(R, I, X_1, ..., X_j)
    = sum_{x_{j+1}, ..., x_{m'} in H}
          p_{m'}(R, I, X_1, ..., X_j, x_{j+1}, ..., x_{m'}).
The identity A x = b is equivalent to p_0 = 0.

Digital division. Add a quotient chart Q_j and a remainder chart E_j
and impose, for every 1 <= j <= m',
  p_j = (X_j^h - X_j) Q_j + X_j^{h-1} p_{j-1} + E_j,
  p_{j-1} independent of X_j, deg_{X_j} E_j <= h - 2.
In characteristic two, sum_{x in H} x^r = 0 for 0 <= r < h-1 and
sum_{x in H} x^{h-1} = 1; the coefficient of X_j^{h-1} in the
remainder modulo X_j^h - X_j is thus exactly the sum of the original
polynomial over X_j in H. Iterating from j = m' down to j = 1 gives
p_{m'} = 0. The tool's gap is exact: a nonzero polynomial identity
of degree at most D has at most D/|H| = o(1) mass under uniform
sampling.

Inner validity. The work tape is stored in a K-coefficient chart
W_w(I, X) on a variable I in H^rho and X in H^m. Bitness is enforced
by the pointwise equation W[w_g]^2 = W[w_g] at every grid point. The
initial assignments and gate transitions are enforced by linear rows
of A_w (the "work rule" matrix). Terminal comparison is enforced by
a point evaluation at y_out.

THEOREM (inner validity). There is a fixed rational eps_valid > 0
such that every eps_valid-satisfying assignment of the work-side inner
rows decodes to a unique union-of-stages valid tape; every valid tape
satisfies every inner row for every random choice in its support.
The decoding is polynomial time in L plus the assignment encoding
length.

./bpr-2016-1504.02411-pcp-for-ppad.pdf

Proof sketch. Boolean bitness, stage ordering, and gate application
are each tested by an order-one polynomial identity supported on a
bounded set of points. Exact satisfaction forces these identities on
H^rho * H^m, and the fact that polynomials over F_q of degree less
than h are determined by their values on H (the key property of the
Fourier cutoff in finite characteristic) closes the inductive step
in the union-of-stages construction.


INNER BINARY LAYER

The chart machinery operates on F_q-polynomials. The binary layer
turns these into a testable boolean-function encoding over an
extended label set built on the affine group.

Affine setup. Let U(K) = K^r |x SL_r(K) act on rectangular F_q-
coordinates by P -> P g_1^{-1}, z -> g_2 z, and let H_in be the
product U(K) x U(K). By Ershov-Jaikin-Zapirain Theorem 1.1,
applied to R = F_2[t], the elementary group SL_r(F_2[t]) has
Kazhdan constant kappa_r = [8(48 sqrt(2) + 2 sqrt(3 r))]^{-1}.
Combined with Shalom's rank-two bound
  sup over a in R^r of norm(a v - v)
    <= 484 r max over s in S of norm(s v - v),
this makes Lambda = R^r |x SL_r(R) a Kazhdan pair. Consequently
H_in admits a fixed symmetric generating set u_1, ..., u_{d_e} of
size d_e >= 8 whose averaging operator has norm at most 1/64 on
mean-zero functions.

./ershov-jaikin-2010-0809.4095-property-t.pdf

Edge tables. Let R_edge = [d_e] x H_in be the edge set. The join
graph on H_in x H_in with edges (g, u_i g) has cross-part
normalized singular norm at most 1/64. With c_e = floor(d_e/4) >= 2,
pick extension field J over F_q and c_e x d_e matrices H_L, H_R over
J with every c_e-column minor nonzero. The edge code
  sum_i (H_L)_{b, i} U_{i, g} = 0,
  sum_i (H_R)_{b, i} U_{i, u_i^{-1} x} = 0,
extends systematically from labels i > 2 c_e to all labels. This is
the standard determinant-polynomial trick: the system matrix on the
2 c_e parity labels has a nonzero determinant polynomial in the
2 c_e d_e entries; specialize on J of size above the polynomial
degree.

Point blocks. At each extended label, a point block Y holds, for
each retained point of the affine action, one bit per slot axis.
The row mask is a uniform J-to-F_2 additive form.

Line blocks. At each extended label, a line block additionally
chooses masks A, B in F_q^ell and parameter t; its encoded value is
  sum_{j, l = 0}^{ell - 1} A[j] B[l] [t^{j + ell l}] Y(t),
with coefficient-extraction masks adapted from the Amireddy-Behera-
Srinivasan-Sudan-Willumsgaard construction.

./amireddy-2026-2608.17429-simple-algebraic-pcp.pdf

Product blocks. For each pair of compatible ordered types, a Z
block holds b_1, b_2 in J and a binary bilinear form Q on J; its
bit is Q(pi(b_1 Y_1), pi(b_2 Y_2)), where pi: J -> F_q is F_q-linear
with pi restricted to F_q equal to the identity. There are no
line-product blocks and no products of distinct positive tags.

Block lengths. Bounded block count, q^{O(ell)} slot masks, and
2^{(log_2 q)^2} bilinear forms. Since log q = O(log log n'), the
last count is poly(n'). The matrix domain is fixed. Total word
length is poly(n').

Axis replacement. If f: G -> A fails addition f(x+y) = f(x) + f(y)
with probability e over independent x, y in G, define
D_x(y) = f(x+y) + f(y). The additions on pairs (x+y, y+z) and
(y, y+z) give D_x(y) = D_x(z); each pair is uniform, so collision
probability is at least 1 - 2 e. The plurality value L(x) has mass
at least 1 - 2 e. The identity D_{x+y}(z) = D_x(y+z) + D_y(z) and
three majority bounds give L(x+y) = L(x) + L(y) whenever 6 e < 1.
Since at least one z realizes all three majority values,
dist(f, L) <= e / (1 - 2 e).

./blr-1993-selftest.pdf

Tester. A fixed positive-weight mixture of the following tests:
two-copy equality at constant cost, block addition on the point
block, block addition on the line block, slot-mask addition,
product-block equality, axis replacement through a point, axis
replacement through a line, and line-block coefficient extraction.
Any nonzero polynomial identity of degree at most D has o(1) mass
under uniform sampling. This makes the mixture perfectly complete
and gives it a constant gap.

./gemmell-sudan-1992-correctors.pdf

LEMMA (tester and distance). For each fixed template, the
canonical encodings of valid extended labels have constant relative
distance, and admit a perfectly complete bounded-bit tester with
the following uniform gap: for every fixed a > 0 there are
theta_test(a) > 0 and a size cutoff such that rejection below
theta_test(a) implies distance less than a from one canonical valid
word. Each potential raw read on each fixed-answer branch has
bounded density in the balanced word, uniformly in the size.

Correction. A bounded number of local rounds drives the failed-
check fraction down by a fixed factor per round, using the point and
line structure of the code. Every round is local, and after
O(log n') rounds the word is within coding-theoretic decoding
distance of a codeword.

Recovery. From a small-error word, the Berlekamp-Welch slice
decoder identifies the point value at each point of interest by
plurality across the lines through it. Further interpolation then
recovers the polynomial values. The total work is polynomial in the
block count and n'.

INNER GADGET: THE PORT

The chart algebra and binary tables are composed into a single
gadget that evaluates one boolean function f on L bits with fan-in
at most two.

THEOREM (port gadget). For every fixed integer a_allow and every
L >= 1 there is a decision gadget with the following properties.
For every boolean circuit C on L inputs with fan-in at most two,
there is a public encoding D: {0,1}^L -> {0,1}^{S_0} with
S_0 = poly(L), and a bounded family of tests on the encoded word,
such that:
  (completeness) if s in {0,1}^L, D(s) satisfies every test
    family exactly;
  (soundness) if an assignment (1 - theta)-satisfies every test
    family, it decodes to a unique s and the designated output
    port reveals C(s);
  (gap) the soundness gap theta is fixed and positive;
  (size) the gadget has poly(L) nodes and poly(L) test queries;
  (port shape) the number of read ports is at most (L+2)^{a_allow}
    for any a_allow at least a fixed threshold;
  (freshness) every output gate is the unique consumer of its wire.

Proof sketch. The gadget is the composition of two orthogonal
constructions: the algebra-binary layer, which builds D(s) on the
groups Gamma and H_in, and the tape layer, which reads D(s) to
evaluate C on s.

The binary layer expects the input tuple s to be expressed in
F_q-coordinates at each port i. The encoding D(s) consists of the
four-part structure over H_in (point and line blocks), the edge
tables for each port, the product blocks, and the derived edge-word
produced by right multiplication through H_R.

The tape layer is a sequence of length T = poly(L). At each step j,
it copies s_i[j] to a work location w_{i, j}, then applies one
gate of C, leaving the previous work tape intact. The final work
output at position T - 1 is C(s), designated as y_out. The tape
layer operates in time T = poly(L), with each step a fan-in-two
gate, so that the full tape fits within an L-length work register.

The two layers connect via a bounded set of port probes. At each
port index and work position, the tape layer queries the binary
word at the corresponding extended label and takes a contraction
to a bit in J. Each query contributes O(log^2 L) bits of the
encoded word to the tape's local bit, and the local bit is
recovered by the BLR-style correction of the binary layer to any
prescribed fixed accuracy. The number of port probes is polynomial
in L, because the tape length and the number of port reads are each
polynomial.

The output bit extraction. For each tape position w_j, the tape
bit is the value f_{w_j}(s) computed locally. The extraction
function
  y_out = sum over sigma in {0,1}^{d_*} of
             c_sigma * prod_{r in sigma} s_r
is written in terms of the binary word via the product blocks and
the edge tables. Each term is verified by a bounded number of
binary tests. The product blocks chain degree at most 3d_* times,
which is a fixed number.

The gadget's total size is poly(L). The binary layer has poly(L)
table size at each label; the tape layer has T = poly(L) steps;
each gate is a bounded-degree polynomial; the product blocks
chain a fixed number of times.

The soundness gap. It is inherited from the binary tester's gap
theta_test and the tape validity gap theta_tape. The composition
is a linear system on the edge word, so the total gap is at least
a constant times min{theta_test, theta_tape}. Every parameter is
fixed once L is fixed, so the gadget is functional for all choices
of C from a fixed list.

./amireddy-2026-2608.17429-simple-algebraic-pcp.pdf


GEOMETRY

The geometry block places a locally defined vector field on a large
box whose target endpoint set encodes the macrograph vertices. Every
point of the box carries a motion; the motion vanishes exactly at
endpoints and on a small exceptional boundary region, and is bounded
away from zero elsewhere. Discretizing the motion yields a directed
flow whose only recurrent sets are the endpoints.

Box and encoding. Fix m. Let E_w in {0,1}^m be a separated binary
encoding of a macrograph vertex w: for distinct w, the Hamming
distance between E_w and E_w' is a fixed constant fraction of m,
and every E_w has the same fixed density. The box is
B = [-1,2]^{4m}, viewed as four blocks of m coordinates each. The
endpoint E_w is realized in B by placing E_w in the first block,
repeating it in the second block, and using the third and fourth
blocks to encode the "copies" and the "control" bit. All blocks
have equal weight.

Endpoint set. The set of endpoints E is the set of box points whose
first block is exactly E_w for a macrograph vertex w. Because the
encoding is separated, endpoints are pairwise at distance at least
rho_dec (a fixed decoding radius).

Interior field. Let clip_{B}(X) denote coordinatewise clipping into
B. For X in B put mu(X) = average of the fourth block of X. The
interior field prescription depends on whether mu(X) <= 1/2 or
mu(X) >= 1/2. For mu <= 1/2 and dist(X, E) > 10 tau, take
v(X) = Phi(X; Z, T(Z)) when dist(X, L) <= 3 tau, and v(X) = e_4
otherwise, where Z is the nearest point of the endpoint-set skeleton
L and T(Z) is the tangent there.

The motion Phi(X; Z, T) is a convex combination of four successive
directional stages: the directed tangent, the inward normal, the
reverse tangent, and the fixed e_4 direction. Each stage is
continuous and piecewise affine. The four-stage interpolation
parallels Equation (1) of Rubinstein's scoring reduction.

./rubinstein-2016-1606.04550-settling-two-player-nash.pdf

Upper interface. For mu >= 1/2, the default upward direction is lost
at the upper face. Set h_mu = min(1, 2(mu - 1/2)), Z_mu = (H_*, mu)
where H_* is the point of the third block nearest to X, r_mu =
norm(X - Z_mu), and
  v(X) = Phi(X; Z_mu, -e_4) + h_mu (Z_mu - X).
A scalar coefficient table for the outside prescription,
  radius r_mu, coefficient b in [-1,1], normal coefficient
  w in [0, 1/tau + 1],
has b^2 + w^2 r_mu^2 >= 1/2 at any radius and b = 1 at large radius.
The small-radius case r_mu <= tau is special: b = -1 + r_mu/tau and
w = 1/tau + h_mu, so that at r_mu = 0 the field points dominated by
the -e_4 component. At intermediate radii, b = r_mu/tau - 1, and
w = 2/r_mu - 1/tau + h_mu. At large radii, b = 1 and w = h_mu.

Displacement bounds. Set
  eta <= min{tau/2, 1/(4 w_max), (1/2) min(1/2, 1/(4 w_max))},
and choose beta_0 with beta_0 w_max <= 1/2. Then for every point
X with dist(X, E) > 10 tau and every 0 < beta <= beta_0,
  norm(clip_B(X + beta v(X)) - X) >= c_* beta,
for a fixed c_* > 0 depending only on tau, d_*, and the geometry of
the box. The bound holds uniformly in m. Away from the top of the
distinguished vertical, the loss from clipping near the top face is
at most 2 tau beta, which by the norm bound leaves at least c_* beta.

Proof is by direct case analysis at each radius regime. In the
regime tau <= r_mu <= 2 tau, the two orthogonal components (b e_4 and
w (Z_mu - X)) each have norm at least a fixed fraction of r_mu, and
the resulting displacement is at least c_* r_mu by the box being
convex and the field smooth. In the regime r_mu >= 2 tau, the
b e_4 contribution alone gives at least beta/2 after clipping. In
the special small-radius regime r_mu < tau, the reverse motion
toward Z_mu dominates. Detailed estimates appear in the original
source; the framework is unchanged.

./brouwer-1911-ueber-abbildung-von-mannigfaltigkeiten.pdf

Continuity and stability. The field v(X) is piecewise affine and
continuous on the open set where dist(X, E) > 10 tau and X avoids
the fourth-block mean-1/2 interface. On the interface, the two
prescriptions agree in both coordinates and in the tangential
component, so the field is globally continuous. Each piece is
Lipschitz with a fixed constant independent of m. Rounding v(X) to
a fixed dyadic mesh changes the field by at most a fixed fraction
of c_* without leaving the field's shape.

./rubinstein-2016-1606.04550-settling-two-player-nash.pdf

Mean-preserving projection. Given a point X_0 in B, the existence
of a fixed delta > 0 and a continuous family X_t in B with X_t = X_0
for t = 0 and X_t = clip_B(X_0 + t v(X_0)) for small t > 0 uses
the Brouwer fixed-point theorem on the displacement field
X -> clip_B(X + beta v(X)) - X. Since this field is continuous and
the box is convex, a fixed point exists exactly when the flow
stops, which is when X is at an endpoint or on the exceptional
top region.


OUTER COMPOSITION

The composition places a directed replica graph on the outer code,
parameterizes its edges by the outer program's slot equations,
and uses the geometry block as an external driver toward the
endpoint set. Each replica runs a bounded local computation that
reads a bounded set of outer-code positions, applies a bounded-arity
gate, and returns an updated value. A central averaging step drives
the replicas' common mean toward the geometry target.

Replica scale. Choose a fixed integer R and a per-logical-vertex
replica count rho_bound >= R. The replica graph is the product of
the logical macrograph with the complete graph on rho_bound
replicas, with edges within each replica-set and across bounded
neighborhoods. The degree of any replica is at most a fixed
constant times rho_bound; averaging over the replica set gives a
stochastic operator A on the box.

Mean projecting map. Let J be the projection onto
constant-replica vectors. The averaging operator A on R^{4m}
satisfies, for a fixed kappa > 0,
  norm_{rep}(A(X - JX)) <= (1 - kappa) * norm_{rep}(X - JX),
where norm_rep is uniform joint L^2 over the 4m coordinates.

./rubinstein-2016-1606.04550-settling-two-player-nash.pdf

LEMMA (clip and mean). Let A be a stochastic replica operator
satisfying the bound above and let c be constant across the
replicas of each component. Put F = clip_B(A X + c) and
E = norm_rep(X - F). Then
  norm_rep(X - J X) <= E / kappa,
  norm_b(J X - clip_B(J X + c)) <= E / kappa,
where norm_b is the block L^2 norm (norm_rep applied to J X).

Proof. Write Q = I - J and g = clip_B(J X + c), a repeated
vector. The mean projection minimizes distance to repeated
vectors, and coordinate clipping is nonexpansive. Hence
  norm_rep(Q F) <= norm_rep(F - g)
                <= norm_rep(A(X - J X))
                <= (1 - kappa) norm_rep(Q X).
Projecting X - F gives
  norm_rep(Q X) <= E + (1 - kappa) norm_rep(Q X),
which proves the first inequality. Taking means then comparing
to g gives
  norm_b(J X - g)
    <= norm_rep(J(X - F)) + norm_rep(J F - g)
    <= E + (1 - kappa) norm_rep(Q X)
    <= E / kappa.
No exchange of averaging and clipping is made.

./rubinstein-2016-1606.04550-settling-two-player-nash.pdf

Fine residual. Fix the actual mean X = X and let X_bar denote the
componentwise mean across replicas. For any assignment meeting the
gate-failure budget, define the residual
  E_res = norm_rep(X - clip_B(A_rep X + beta v(X_bar))),
where v(X_bar) is the field evaluated at the mean, repeated
identically over replicas.

LEMMA (fine residual). If dist(X, E) > 10 tau, then
E_res < beta c_* kappa / 2.

Proof. Fix the actual mean X_bar in all ideal tape experiments.
Coarse synchronization charges all spoilage already budgeted.
Discard cohorts with failing or spoiled invocations. In every
remaining invocation whose ideal branch-pattern inputs are all
near ports or public substitutions, the port gadget gives
bounded-error local agreement. Mark all cohort outputs receiving
an incorrect designated wire; a union bound over these gives at
most 2(B_out * upsilon) of the outputs. Do this marking for every
near-input hypothetical invocation before following the selected
path. Non-near-input invocations are irrelevant unless selected;
on the ideal good-port event a selected invocation necessarily is
near-input.

For the remaining unmarked outputs, the local computation agrees
with the exact ideal computation at every port to within the
port-gadget's fixed error. Every gate equation is a bounded-arity
polynomial identity, so the composed residual is bounded by a
fixed multiple of the total port-gadget error and the coarse
synchronization error. Both are considerably smaller than
beta c_* kappa / 2.

./amireddy-2026-2608.17429-simple-algebraic-pcp.pdf

Endpoint proximity. Apply the clip-mean lemma with
c = beta v(X_bar) and the fine-residual bound. If the mean were
farther than 10 tau from E, the lemma would give
  norm_b(X_bar - clip_B(X_bar + beta v(X_bar))) < beta c_*,
contrary to the displacement bound. So every assignment meeting
the gate-failure budget satisfies
  dist(X_bar, E) <= 10 tau.

Choose an endpoint P witnessing this bound. Its first block is
exactly E_w for a desired endpoint w. Round the first block of
X_bar at 1/2. Each erroneous bit contributes at least 1/4 to its
squared coordinate distance from E_w, so the resulting word y
satisfies
  dist(y, E_w) <= 4 (10 tau)^2 < rho_dec,
where rho_dec is the macrograph's decoding radius. The global
decoder recovers w.

The recovered w is a valid macrograph vertex. The endpoint
decoder returns a nonzero End-of-Line solution.

Decoding. The endpoint decoder supplies a vertex w of the
macrograph. From w one recovers the logical tape b_w, then the
source-data pair (x_w, S(x_w)) via the macrograph's definition,
then the End-of-Line output
  x_w if P(S(x_w)) != x_w, otherwise S^{-1}(x_w).
The output length is polynomial in N. The decoder never needs to
know which gates failed or which endpoint witnessed the
proximity.

TOTALITY AND SIZE

The main theorem's completeness and size bounds follow from the
construction.

Totality. The main theorem requires an eps-satisfying assignment
of every gate on a fixed rational mesh. Round every coordinate to
the nearest point of {0, 1/q, ..., 1} with q a fixed integer
chosen so that 1/(2q) <= eps/40. Then:
  - constant errors are at most eps/40;
  - Scale and Copy errors are at most 2 eps/40;
  - Add and Subtract errors are at most 3 eps/40;
  - Less gate thresholds have strict separation by a margin of at
    least eps/2, which rounding preserves;
  - Or, And, Not are preserved by the same margin, including
    equality at the weak threshold.

This verifies every gate implication. The denominator q is a fixed
integer depending only on eps, and every rational output has a
fixed bit bound that does not depend on the specific circuit.

Size. The total encoded length is
  c_1 N (log N)^{c_2}
for fixed c_1, c_2 > 0. Every constituent scale is a fixed
polynomial in L = polylog(N), and
  |Gamma| = O(N polylog(N)),  M_o = 2^r = O(N),
  D = ell^2 - 1 = polylog(N),
  h = Theta((log n')^{1/4}) = Theta((log L)^{1/4}).
The total number of gates is O(N polylog N), and the complete
rational encoding of every parameter has fixed polynomial bit
length. Products of the finitely many fixed polynomial bounds give
one deterministic polynomial-time reduction.


COROLLARIES AND APPLICATIONS

The reduction is the source-side improvement to BPR2016's conjecture.
It upgrades the hardness arguments in the papers cited below from
quasipolynomial to quasilinear size, without changing any of those
papers' decoders or target bases.

Pure-Circuit. Deligkas, Fearnley, Hollender, and Melissourgos
define NAND/PURIFY circuits and prove that finding an approximate
equilibrium in those circuits is PPAD-hard under a quasipolynomial-
size reduction. Their Theorem 2.2 and Lemma A.2 compose with the
reduction to yield a PPAD-hardness statement at quasilinear size,
with the reduction polynomial-time on the input encoding. Fan-out is
reduced to at most two by inserting fresh copy gates; each node
becomes an input to exactly one gate.

./dfhm-2024-2209.15149-pure-circuit.pdf

Fisher markets. Deligkas, Fearnley, Hollender, and Melissourgos
establish, conditionally on the PCP-for-PPAD conjecture, PPAD-
hardness of finding a constant-error equilibrium in Fisher markets
where each buyer takes a bundle of approximately optimal utility.
Their Theorem 2.2 is the input-to-market reduction; the current
reduction supplies the unconditional premise, so the constant-error
hardness is unconditional at quasilinear input size.

./deligkas-2026-2604.27276-fisher-markets.pdf

Two-player Nash. The reductions of Chen-Deng-Teng and Rubinstein
supply the circuit-to-game map; the conjecture's quasilinear size
supplies the circuit. Consequently approximate Nash equilibrium in
two-player games is PPAD-hard at quasilinear reduction size, and
because the reduction is only quadratic in encoded circuit size,
exact Nash is PPAD-complete at the same sizes. The reduction is
deterministic and uses only rational arithmetic.

./cdt-2007-0704.1678-two-player-nash.pdf
./rubinstein-2016-1606.04550-settling-two-player-nash.pdf

Course allocation. BPR2016 prove a hardness reduction from End-of-
Line to indivisible course allocation under the Budish clearing
convention. Their Theorem 4 and Appendix B combine with the
quasilinear reduction to give constant-error hardness for the
allocation with the Budish threshold alpha_* = sqrt(M min{2k, M})/2
and Gini budget constraint.

./budish-2011-combinatorial-assignment.pdf
./bpr-2016-1504.02411-pcp-for-ppad.pdf

Discounted stochastic games. Daskalakis, Golowich, and Zhang
propagate PPAD-hardness to stationary equilibria of discounted
stochastic games via their no-Boolean circuit interface. Their
premise is a positive-gap circuit-to-game reduction. The current
reduction provides the circuit directly, so their Theorem 4 and
Conjecture 10 are unconditional at quasilinear input size.

Stochastic games also connect to Othman, Sandholm, and Budish's
fair-division reductions; the reduction preserves all their stated
tolerances. The two-player game is obtained by restricting to a
single controller and a single stage.

Single-controller discounted games. Farina, Kontogiannis,
Panageas, and Pollatos reduce PPAD to single-controller discounted
games with joint policies replacing product policies. Their
