DONOVAN'S CONJECTURE OVER ALGEBRAICALLY CLOSED FIELDS OF CHARACTERISTIC p

== PART 1: THE CONJECTURE ==

Let p be a prime, and let k be an algebraically closed field of
characteristic p. Let D be a finite p-group. A BLOCK of a finite
group algebra kG is an indecomposable direct summand kGb, where b is
a primitive central idempotent of kG. The DEFECT GROUP of the block
is a p-subgroup determined up to conjugacy, and its order measures
how far the block is from being a semisimple algebra. The block need
not be the principal block.

Two finite-dimensional k-algebras A and B are K-LINEARLY MORITA
EQUIVALENT if the categories of finite-dimensional left modules over
A and B are equivalent by a k-linear functor. On blocks of group
algebras, this is a very strong equivalence: it preserves the number
of simple modules, the Cartan matrix, the basic algebra, and most
other representation-theoretic invariants.

DONOVAN'S CONJECTURE (classical form).
Fix p, k, and a finite p-group D. Then there are only finitely many
k-linear Morita equivalence classes among all blocks kGb of all finite
groups G whose defect group is isomorphic to D.

DONOVAN'S CONJECTURE (bounded-order form).
Fix p, k, and a positive integer M. Then there are only finitely many
k-linear Morita equivalence classes among all blocks kGb of all finite
groups G whose defect group has order at most M.

The two forms are equivalent. Indeed, there are only finitely many
isomorphism types of groups of order at most M, and each fixed-D
statement is a special case. The theorem says nothing about the order
of G or the dimensions of its simple modules.

Note: it matters that k is algebraically closed and of characteristic
p. Over other fields, or over the p-adic integers, one must adjust the
notion of equivalence. In particular, an integral statement (Morita
equivalence over a complete discrete valuation ring such as W(k))
does not follow formally from the k-linear statement and is a
separate result.

== PART 2: WHAT IS ACTUALLY PROVED ==

Here is an honest, partial list. All results are over an algebraically
closed field k of characteristic p. "Fixed D" means the classical
form; "bounded |D|" is the uniform version.

(1) Symmetric groups. J. Scopes (1991). For each fixed weight w and
    prime p, only finitely many Morita classes of blocks of symmetric
    groups. The defect group of a weight-w block is (up to small
    prime-to-p corrections) a Sylow p-subgroup of the symmetric group
    on p*w letters. This gives Donovan for that family of defects.

(2) Double covers of symmetric and alternating groups. R. Kessar
    (1996). Analogous finiteness for the double covers.

(3) Unipotent blocks of GL_n and GU_n. T. Jost (1996). G. Hiss and R.
    Kessar (2000, 2005). A Scopes-type reduction of unipotent blocks
    to a finite list of ranks determined by the defect. Combined with
    the rationality results of N. Farrell and R. Kessar (2019), this
    gives finiteness for this family across all n and q.

(4) Abelian defect groups. C. Eaton and M. Livesey (2019, Proc. AMS).
    Donovan's conjecture holds for all blocks whose defect group is
    abelian. This is a landmark result.

(5) Integral versions for abelian defect. C. Eaton, F. Eisele, and
    M. Livesey (2020, Math. Z.). An integral/DVR version, and a
    reduction showing that a uniform Cartan bound for quasisimple
    blocks suffices for the integral conjecture in the abelian case.

(6) Extraspecial defect groups of order p^3 (exponent p, p >= 5).
    J. An and C. Eaton (2025). Donovan for that family, plus a
    reduction of the p = 3 case to a quasisimple Cartan bound.

(7) Suzuki 2-groups. C. Eaton (2025). Donovan for Suzuki 2-group
    defects.

(8) Tame defect groups (dihedral, semidihedral, quaternion). K.
    Erdmann's classification of tame block algebras gives finitely
    many Morita classes for each defect type. This is classical.

(9) Quaternion defect groups. Recently claimed (Eaton, Eisele,
    Kessar, Linckelmann, Schaeffer Fry, 2026). Treat as a claim,
    not as established.

(10) Defining-characteristic blocks of groups of Lie type. J.
     Humphreys (1971). Every positive-defect block has full defect,
     so bounded defect forces bounded Sylow r-order, which bounds
     both field size and rank. Combined with the finite list of
     small exceptional groups, this gives finiteness.

Beyond these, the full conjecture -- for arbitrary nonabelian defect
groups D, with unbounded structure, and all primes p including p = 2
-- remains OPEN.

== PART 3: WHAT IS OPEN ==

The central open piece is a UNIFORM CARTAN BOUND for quasisimple
groups with bounded defect group. Precisely:

   (CB(p,M)) There is a constant C = C(p,M) such that, for every
   quasisimple finite group S and every block of kS with defect
   group of order at most M, every entry of the Cartan matrix of
   the block is at most C.

Eaton, Eisele, and Livesey (2020) give a general reduction from
Donovan's conjecture to a Cartan bound of this shape. In the abelian
case, the required bound is what Eaton and Livesey proved. In the
nonabelian case, this bound -- with rank and field size unbounded --
is open. The specific difficulty is the interaction of a nonabelian
defect group with large-rank unipotent blocks of classical groups.

A closely related open problem is RATIONALITY: a bound on how many
times one must apply coefficient Frobenius before an integral Morita
class returns to itself. Farrell and Kessar provide rationality in
the abelian defect setting; the nonabelian case is open.

Another related problem is the Blockwise Alperin Weight Conjecture,
which is also open. It is not needed for Donovan in the k-linear
setting, and the original file's use of it is suspect.

== PART 4: WHAT THE ORIGINAL DOCUMENT CLAIMS AND WHY IT FAILS ==

The original file is structured as a complete proof of the full
conjecture. Here are the specific problems.

(a) Fabricated and self-referential citations.
The reference list contains entries such as:

  "OpenAI, The Blockwise Alperin Weight Conjecture, OpenAI Math
  Release preprint ..., 2026."

  "OpenAI, Integral Donovan Finiteness over Witt Vectors, OpenAI
  Math Release preprint ..., 2026."

Neither of these has any known existence. The Blockwise Alperin
Weight Conjecture is a major open problem (research by Spath and
collaborators and others has spanned decades); it has not been solved
by a 2026 "OpenAI Math Release" preprint. The main text of the
original file USES BAW as an established input. This is circular at
best, and fraudulent at worst.

(b) One document claims to solve several unrelated open problems.
The original file claims, among other things:

  * a fixed-root Cartan bound uniform in q (its Theorem 2.1);
  * a quasisimple Cartan bound for all p (its Theorem 3.1);
  * a runner reduction with a cuspidal-triangle endpoint (its
    Sections 5-6);
  * a Sylow-equivariant integral Bonnafe-Rouquier comparison with
    precise inner factors and bounded Frobenius period (its
    Section 8);
  * Donovan's conjecture in full generality (its Theorem 1.1).

Real progress in this area is incremental. Each of (4)-(9) in Part 2
above is a substantial paper. A single document claiming to solve all
of these at once, plus BAW, should be treated as an unverified stack
of claims, not as a proof.

(c) Key steps are not actually completed in the text.
Each of the four bulleted claims in (b) is, on its own, a big open
problem or a hard technical result that would take many pages of
careful case analysis. The original file gives a plausible-looking
outline but does not close the gaps.

  * The fixed-root Cartan bound of Section 2 is claimed via tilting
    objects on horocycle spaces. The dimensional estimate "uniform
    in q" at the level of dimensions of tilting objects (not just
    existence) is not justified.
  * The runner "cones" of Section 5 are claimed to preserve a Cartan
    bound by condition-number arguments; the determinant-based volume
    estimate is credible in outline, but the required uniformity at
    the cuspidal-triangle endpoint is a strong open-analogue.
  * The "Sylow comparison" of Section 8 requires an integral
    equivariant Bonnafe-Rouquier equivalence with precise control of
    the inner multiplication factors and a Frobenius period bounded
    in terms of p and M. That level of precision is not known for
    general nonabelian defect groups.

(d) Standard facts are cited as if they were the famous conjecture.
In the introduction, the original file invokes the Blockwise Alperin
Weight Conjecture to explain the coefficient descent "every block
over K descends uniquely to a block over k". That descent is a
standard consequence of Brauer's theory of blocks and scalar
extension, and does not need BAW. Citing BAW there is a red flag: it
suggests either a mis-application or a fabricated reference.

(e) Style itself is informative.
The original file is written in the register of a confident final
report, not of a research paper under review. It cites 2026 papers
without caveat, and self-cites "OpenAI Math Release" preprints as if
they were published. This is characteristic of AI-generated
mathematical prose. That does not by itself make every claim wrong,
but it means every claim needs independent verification before use.

== PART 5: WHAT A REAL PROOF WOULD LOOK LIKE ==

An honest path to Donovan's conjecture would combine at least:

  * a uniform Cartan bound for quasisimple blocks with bounded defect
    (this is the main open piece);
  * a rationality statement for primitive central idempotents and
    coefficient Frobenius orbits (Farrell-Kessar in the abelian case;
    open in general);
  * integral finiteness for base blocks (Eaton-Eisele-Livesey for
    abelian defect; open in general);
  * Puig's or Bonnafe-Rouquier's equivariant machinery for the
    crossed-extension step;
  * a Kulshammer-type reduction from arbitrary finite groups to
    crossed products over quasisimple blocks.

There are already very good reductions in the literature. In
particular, the Eaton-Eisele-Livesey framework isolates the Cartan
bound (CB(p,M)) as the key remaining input. So a proof of (CB(p,M))
for all p and M would yield Donovan. The target is clear.

== PART 6: READING GUIDE FOR STUDENTS ==

If you are a high-school student or undergraduate and want to learn
about this subject, do not start with the original file. Instead:

  * Learn the basics of group representations: Maschke's theorem,
    characters, primitive central idempotents, blocks.
  * Learn what a defect group is and why it controls the "p-part" of
    a block. Brauer's theory of blocks is the right reference.
  * Learn what Morita equivalence is: two rings A, B are Morita
    equivalent exactly when their module categories are equivalent.
    In the block setting, this says the blocks have "the same module
    categories".
  * Read Eaton-Livesey (2019) for the abelian defect case. It is a
    readable paper and a genuinely proven theorem.
  * Read Scopes (1991) for the symmetric-group case. It is a
    concrete and classical calculation.
  * Only after that, look at Eaton-Eisele-Livesey (2020) for the
    general reduction framework, and at specific papers (An-Eaton,
    Eaton on Suzuki groups) for families where the conjecture is
    known.

Do NOT present the original file to your children as a proof. It is
not one. Present this status report together with the papers above.

== PART 7: BOTTOM LINE ==

Donovan's conjecture in the generality claimed by the original file is
NOT known. The proven subcases are:

  abelian defect groups (Eaton-Livesey 2019),
  extraspecial p^3 defect groups for p >= 5 (An-Eaton 2025),
  Suzuki 2-group defect groups (Eaton 2025),
  tame defect groups (classical, via Erdmann),
  symmetric group blocks of fixed weight (Scopes 1991),
  unipotent blocks of GL_n and GU_n (Jost, Hiss-Kessar,
    Farrell-Kessar rationality).

The general case -- arbitrary nonabelian defect, unbounded, p = 2 --
remains open. The reductions in the literature (especially
Eaton-Eisele-Livesey 2020) reduce the conjecture to the uniform
Cartan bound (CB(p,M)) for quasisimple blocks. That is the main open
problem.

If you want a clean, honest, and pleasant introduction for your kids,
give them Eaton-Livesey (2019) and this status report. Do not give
them the original file.
