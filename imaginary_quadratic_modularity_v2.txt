# Modularity of elliptic curves over imaginary quadratic fields


maketitle

Abstract: 
We prove that every elliptic curve over an imaginary quadratic field is
modular: it is realized by an automorphic representation of GL_2 of the
field with matching local parameters at every place and matching
L-function. The method is a geometric form of Wiles's prime switch. A
single dihedral family of cyclic covers of a quadratic twist of the curve
produces four two-dimensional Galois representations, pairwise congruent
modulo the primes 3 and a large prime p, with an elliptic curve at one
end. Automorphy enters at that end through known results and is carried to
the original curve by three modularity-lifting steps, then descended from a
cyclotomic extension to the field itself. The complex-multiplication case is
treated separately by automorphic induction.

# Introduction

## The theorem

Let K be an imaginary quadratic number field and E/K an elliptic curve.
The modularity conjecture for E asks for an automorphic representation of
GL_2(A_K) whose attached Galois representations are the cohomology of
E. Concretely, at a place of good reduction this means that the Hecke
polynomial recovers the point count of the reduction; at a bad place it
means that the full local parameter, including inertia and monodromy, is
recovered; at the single complex place it means a fixed parameter dictated by
the Hodge decomposition. We prove that such a representation always exists.

Throughout, H^1(X)=H^1_{et}(X_{overlineoverline L}L,Q_l) for a variety over a
number field L, with coefficients made explicit when they matter, and
G_L=Gal(overlineoverline L/L).L/L). The cyclotomic character is eps_l, normalized
to have Hodge--Tate weight -1, so an elliptic curve has labeled
Hodge--Tate weights 0,1 on its H^1 and determinant eps_l^{-1}. For a
regular algebraic cuspidal representation pi of GL_2(A_L) and an
isomorphism iota:overlineQ_ltoC, we write r_iota(pi) for the
associated semisimple l-adic representation. The normalization of local
Langlands used on the cohomological side is
rec^T_{L_v}(pi_v)=rec_{L_v}(pi_v ⊗ |det|_v^{-1/2}); see
Section~sec:conv.


Let K be an imaginary quadratic field and E/K an elliptic curve. There is
an isobaric automorphic representation pi_E of GL_2(A_K) with trivial
central character and weight zero such that for every prime l and every
isomorphism iota:overlineQ_ltoC,
[
 r_iota(pi_E)simeq H^1_{et}(E_{overlineoverline K}K,overlineQ_l).
]
At every finite place vnmid l the Frobenius-semisimplified
Weil--Deligne parameter of the right side equals
rec_{K_v}(pi_{E,v} ⊗ |det|_v^{-1/2}), and the unitary parameter at
the complex place is zmapstodiag(z/|z|,overlineoverline z/|z|).z/|z|). In particular
every local factor agrees, so L(E/K,s)=L(pi_E,s-tfrac12). If E has no
complex multiplication then pi_E is cuspidal; if E has complex
multiplication it is an automorphic induction of a Hecke character, or an
isobaric sum when the CM field equals K.


The phrase ``with matching local parameters at every place'' together with
the equality of L-functions is exactly what modularity means: the Hasse
--Weil L-function of the curve, an arithmetic object counting points over
all finite fields, is the L-function of an automorphic form, an analytic
object with a functional equation and a spectral meaning.

## Why it is hard

Over Q, the modularity of every elliptic curve was completed by
Wiles--Taylor and Breuil--Conrad--Diamond--Taylor. A central tool is
modularity lifting. One knows automorphy of a residual Galois
representation overlineoverline r,r, and one proves that every lift r of
overlineoverline rr with the right local behavior is also automorphic; applied to
the Galois representation of E this gives modularity of E. Lifting
theorems require residual hypotheses, and Wiles's 3--5 switch handles
curves for which the residual representation of E itself is unsuitable, by
passing through a resolvent auxiliary curve whose own modularity is already
known.

Over an imaginary quadratic field three new difficulties appear.
[label=(arabic*)]
 The relevant cohomology of Bianchi modular forms sits in more than one
degree, so constructing Galois representations and running a patching or
deformation argument both require extra work. Harris--Soudry--Taylor,
Taylor, Harris--Lan--Taylor--Thorne, Scholze, and Calegari--Geraghty supply
these constructions, and potential modularity over CM fields is available
(Allen--Calegari--Caraiani--Gee--Helm--Le Hung--Newton--Scholze--Taylor
--Thorne). But potential modularity may enlarge the field, whereas we must
stay over K.
 The known lifting theorem in the small characteristics available for
starters --- Caraiani--Newton --- imposes residual conditions and local
conditions that E itself need not satisfy. Their theorem already gives
modularity for a positive proportion of curves, and for all curves when
X_0(15)(K) has rank zero, but not for all curves.
 There is no obvious resolvent auxiliary curve directly attached to
E in the way Wiles's auxiliary curve is attached over Q.


Our contribution is to remove the restriction on the curve and on the
field. We do not build a single auxiliary curve; we build a whole family of
covers, so that the four representations participating in the modularity
chain are simultaneous eigenspaces of one geometric object.

## The geometric prime switch

Fix a non-CM elliptic curve E/K and write it as y^2=h(x) with origin the
point at infinity O. Fix an odd integer n=3p, where p>100 is a prime
chosen (via Serre's open image theorem) so that the image of G_K on E[p]
contains SL_2(F_p). Put F=K(mu_n). For t in a nonempty Zariski-open
subset UsubsetA^1_K we build a genus-n cyclic cover
[
 C_n(t)→ B_t
]
of the quadratic twist B_t:h(t)y^2=h(x) of E, branched at two points,
carrying an involution w that inverts the deck rotations. For each
character theta of the rotation group mu_n, the theta-eigenspace
[
 V_theta=H^1(C_n(t))_theta
]
in integral cohomology is a rank-two compatible system with determinant
eps_l^{-1} and labeled Hodge--Tate weights 0,1. Choose characters
eta of order 3 and psi of order p, and put chi=etapsi of
order 3p. The point of the construction is the chain of congruences

 {V_eta}_{H^1(A)}
  equiv pmod{p} V_chi
  equiv pmod{3} V_psi
  equiv pmod{p} {V_1}_{H^1(B_t)},

each congruence being an equality of semisimplified residual Galois
representations. The order-p part of a character becomes trivial modulo
p, and the order-3 part becomes trivial modulo 3, so the primary
factors eta,psi,chi collapse onto their neighbours. The order-three
system V_eta is H^1 of an elliptic curve A/F, while V_1 is H^1 of
the twist B_t.

Automorphy enters at the left. The prime 5 is used only to certify that
the auxiliary elliptic curve A is modular: A is chosen (through the same
one-parameter family) with a large mod-5 image and non-CM, so the
elliptic-curve starting theorem of Caraiani--Newton applies. Automorphy then
propagates rightwards along (intro:chain) by three applications of the
Caraiani--Newton lifting theorem, at primes p,3,p respectively. At the end
we know H^1(B_t) is automorphic; because B_t is a quadratic twist of
E, twisting by the corresponding Hecke character gives automorphy of
H^1(E). Finally we descend the automorphic form from the cyclotomic field
F to the original field K, and handle the CM case separately.

Two geometric specializations of the cover family make this work.
 At one degeneration the Jacobian of the cover has compact-type limit
E^n. Because E^n is a product of copies of E, its cohomology --- and
hence the cohomology of every eigenspace --- has its reduction type (good
ordinary, good supersingular, or totally multiplicative) read off from E.
A local-constancy argument over the local field of coefficients transfers
this to all nearby fibers at once. This gives simultaneous local conditions
on all four systems for one choice of t.
 At the other degeneration explicit vanishing cycles cut out opposite
unipotent elements on each nontrivial eigenspace. These unipotents give
absolute irreducibility of the residual representations and produce
suitable Frobenius elements, without any assumption that E and its
conjugate are independent. They are also what forces the auxiliary curve to
be non-CM.

A Hilbert-irreducibility argument over the restriction of scalars
Res_{K/Q}U then finds a single rational point t satisfying prescribed
local conditions together with the residual hypotheses for both conjugates
of K at once. This gives decomposed genericity at every place above a
split rational prime.

We emphasize what is genuinely new here relative to Caraiani--Newton: the
character pieces appear in one integral cohomology lattice, an elliptic curve
occurs at each end of the chain, and the local-constancy and
two-conjugate-specialization arguments are what make each lifting step
applicable to a prescribed curve over a prescribed imaginary quadratic
field.

## Related work

The arithmetic groups of imaginary quadratic fields and their action on
hyperbolic three-space go back to Bianchi, with presentations and cohomology
developed by Swan and Harder. The link with elliptic curves is part of the
Langlands program; Langlands's problem list discusses automorphy of local
representations of elliptic curves over global fields. Computations
relating elliptic curves and automorphic forms over imaginary quadratic
fields are due to Grunewald--Helling--Mennicke, Cremona, and
Cremona--Whitley.

Over Q, Wiles and Taylor--Wiles proved modularity of semistable curves and
Breuil--Conrad--Diamond--Taylor of all curves. Wiles's 3--5 switch
introduced the idea that a residual representation failing the initial
hypotheses can be replaced by one coming from an auxiliary elliptic curve
with prescribed torsion and local behavior. For imaginary quadratic fields,
Harris--Soudry--Taylor and Taylor constructed Galois representations
attached to Bianchi forms in important cases; Berger--Harcos obtained
Frobenius compatibility for cyclotomic central character away from finitely
many primes; Harris--Lan--Taylor--Thorne and Scholze constructed the general
representations; Calegari--Geraghty developed patching beyond the
Taylor--Wiles method. Potential automorphy over CM fields is due to
Allen--Calegari--Caraiani--Gee--Helm--Le Hung--Newton--Scholze--Taylor--Thorne
and, for genus-one curves over quadratic extensions of totally real fields,
to Boxer--Calegari--Gee--Pilloni. Allen--Khare--Thorne gave residual
modularity results in small characteristic and deduced modularity for a
positive proportion of curves over each CM field not containing zeta_5.
Caraiani--Newton gave a lifting theorem allowing small coefficient primes and
ramified CM fields, and deduced modularity of all curves over imaginary
quadratic K with X_0(15)(K) of rank zero, of 100% of curves ordered by
height over each such K not containing zeta_5, and under residual-image
hypotheses in characteristic 3 or 5. Our Theorem~thm:main removes
the last restrictions; the Caraiani--Newton lifting theorem and modularity
criterion remain essential inputs.

The geometric ingredients have antecedents: cyclic covers of elliptic
curves with two branch points and dihedral symmetry occur in real
multiplication constructions of Tautz--Top--Verberkmoes, in Ellenberg's work
on endomorphism algebras of Jacobians, and in Mestre's constructions. Darmon
studied congruences removing prime-power parts of monodromy and a conditional
modularity argument by successive lifts for rigid local systems. The
difference operator 2-zeta-zeta^{-1} in the monodromy computation is the
same one appearing in rank-two monodromy studied by Darmon--Mestre. Taylor's
use of auxiliary abelian varieties with real multiplication is a
higher-dimensional form of the prime switch.

# Conventions

We write eps_l for the cyclotomic character with Hodge--Tate weight -1.
A bar over a characteristic-zero representation denotes the semisimplification
of the reduction of a chosen stable lattice; this semisimplification is
independent of the lattice. An elliptic curve thus has labeled Hodge--Tate
weights 0,1 on H^1 and determinant eps_l^{-1}.

For a regular algebraic cuspidal representation pi of GL_2(A_L) over a
CM field and iota:overlineQ_ltoC, the representation r_iota(pi) is
semisimple. We use the Tate-normalized local comparison

 rec^T_{L_v}(pi_v):=rec_{L_v}(pi_v ⊗ |det|_v^{-1/2}),

with geometric-Frobenius reciprocity. The algebraic weight coming from
trivial coefficients is called weight zero. At a complex place the generic
weight-zero parameter, in unitary normalization, is

 z⟼diag(z/|z|,overlineoverline z/|z|),z/|z|), |z|=(zoverlineoverline z)^z)^{1/2}.

Its Tate-normalized parameter has Hodge types (1,0) and (0,1). If the
finite parameters realize an elliptic curve E, then

 L(E/L,s)=L(pi,s-tfrac12).


For a local field, a Galois representation is potentially crystalline
if it becomes crystalline after a finite extension, and is potentially
ordinary of weight zero if after a finite extension it has an invariant line
on which inertia acts by an unramified character and a quotient on which
inertia acts by eps_l^{-1} times an unramified character; this is the
Hodge--Tate ordering (0,1). Local fields used to verify these properties may
be enlarged independently at different places.

A continuous representation overlineoverline r:G_Lr:G_LtoGL_2(overlineF_l) over a
number field L is decomposed generic if there is a rational prime
qne l splitting completely in L such that for every vmid q it is
unramified at v and the Frobenius eigenvalues alpha_v,beta_v satisfy
alpha_v/beta_vne q and beta_v/alpha_vne q in overlineF_l. Our
construction will take qequiv1pmod l, so it suffices to have
alpha_vnebeta_v at every vmid q.

# A cyclic-cover family and its two degenerations

Fix an imaginary quadratic field K, a non-CM elliptic curve E/K written
E:y^2=h(x) with h a separable cubic, and the origin O=[0:1:0] at
infinity. Fix an odd integer nge3 for the moment. We build a family of
cyclic covers of quadratic twists of E and identify two boundary fibers.
At the first the Jacobian becomes a product of copies of E; at the second
the cover acquires nodes whose vanishing cycles give explicit monodromy. All
degenerations here are over a characteristic-zero parameter curve.

## The family, its dihedral symmetry, and geometric connectedness

For t with h(t)ne0 put

 B_t: h(t)y^2=h(x), R_t=(t,1), S_t=[n]R_t,

the y-coordinate normalized so that R_t is the point (t,1) on the
twist. Let UsubsetA^1_k over ksupset K(mu_n) be the open set where
O,R_t,-R_t,S_t,-S_t are pairwise distinct. This is nonempty: over the double
cover t=x(R) parametrized by Rin E, the map
B_{x(R)}to E, (x,y)mapsto(x,y(R)y), identifies R_t with the moving
point R, and each excluded equality forces R to be torsion, hence excludes
only finitely many points. We may delete further finitely many points of U
whenever a statement requires it.


For each divisor m>1 of n there is a smooth proper family of
geometrically connected genus-m curves cC_mto U. It is the
normalization of the smooth proper elliptic family Bto U in the
function-field extension described fiberwise by the Kummer equation

 Y^m=f_t(Z),
 div(f_t)=(S_t)-(-S_t)-n(R_t)+n(-R_t), f_t(O)=1.

The family carries an involution

 w:(Z,Y)⟼(-Z,Y^{-1})

and, over a field containing mu_m, rotations
u_zeta:(Z,Y)mapsto(Z,zeta Y) with wu_zeta w=u_{zeta^{-1}}. For
dmid m the map Ymapsto Y^{m/d} exhibits C_{m,t}to C_{d,t}; for d=1
the quotient is B_t.


Proof. 
On an elliptic curve a degree-zero divisor is principal if and only if its
sum in the group law is O. The divisor in (geom:kummer) has sum
S_t-(-S_t)-nR_t+n(-R_t)=2S_t-2nR_t=O, so it cuts out a rational function,
pinned down by f_t(O)=1. Working over k(U) and spreading out gives such a
function on the family. Its divisor changes sign under Zmapsto-Z, so
normalizing at O yields

 f_t(-Z)=f_t(Z)^{-1}.

This also shows f_t has no vertical divisor: every fiber component is
preserved by inversion, so its valuation is its own negative. Hence the
prescribed disjoint sections are the whole divisor.

The valuation of f_t at S_t is 1, so the Kummer extension has degree
m even geometrically: it is totally ramified at S_t and -S_t and
unramified elsewhere, because the remaining nonzero valuations pm n are
divisible by m. Riemann--Hurwitz gives 2g(C_{m,t})-2=2(m-1), so g=m.
The same local computation shows the normalization is smooth over U: a
zero or pole of order divisible by m drops out of the Kummer equation, and
at a section of order pm1 one takes a root of an 'etale unit and obtains
the mth-root cover of a transverse parameter. Normalization is finite here,
so the resulting family is proper. Equation~(geom:inverse) gives w,
and the quotient statements follow from the Kummer equations.


We pull this family back to the open set of E where R moves and
O,pm R,pm[n]R are distinct; the base elliptic curve there is the constant
curve E. We write C_{m,R} for the fiber and f_R for its normalized
function. The normalization at O makes the pullback identification exact:
it introduces no scalar into Y^m=f_R.

## Separating colliding sections

The two boundary parameters we use are

 R=O,
 qquadandqquad
 R=R_*, [n]R_*in E[2]∖{O},
  O,pm R_*,[n]R_* distinct.

The second exists after extending scalars and will be used over C.


In the constant-E family, blow up the surface at the collision point over
either boundary parameter in (geom:boundary) and normalize the result in
Y^m=f_R. This is a regular semistable model near that parameter. Its
special fiber has m geometric nodes over the node of the blown-up elliptic
surface, each with smoothing parameter of order one in a base parameter. The
component over the exceptional projective line is a geometrically connected
rational curve.


Proof. 
At R=O use parameters r on the parameter curve and z on E, both with
the same tangent coordinate at O. The sections R,-R,[n]R,-[n]R have
first-order equations z=r,-r,nr,-nr; their strict transforms meet the
exceptional line at 1,-1,n,-n, and the node joining that line to the main
component is at ∞. At R_* only [n]R and -[n]R collide, with
distinct tangent slopes, so one blowup separates them from each other and from
the node.

The involution Zmapsto-Z preserves both components of each blown-up
fiber, so by (geom:inverse) the valuation of f_R on each component is
zero; no horizontal divisor section passes through the node. Since the
surface is regular, f_R is a unit there, and an mth root exists 'etale.
The node downstairs has local equation xy=b, so its m lifts have the same
equation and smoothing parameter b rather than a higher power. At the
separated sections the normalization is smooth as in Lemma~geom:family.
On the exceptional line the cover is ramified exactly at the two sections of
multiplicities pm1, is connected, and has genus zero by Riemann--Hurwitz.


[First degeneration]
Suppose k contains mu_m. At R=O the special fiber of
Lemma~geom:semistable is a rational curve meeting m copies of E,
each at one point, all defined over k. Rotations cyclically permute the
elliptic components. The Jacobian family of C_{m,R} extends to an abelian
scheme on a Zariski neighborhood of O in the parameter curve, with fiber
E^m at O.


Proof. 
Let v=z/r on the exceptional line. Its normalized function is

 f_{exc}(v)=(v-n)/(v+n)((v+1)/(v-1))^{n}.

The divisor gives this up to a scalar; the section Z=O meets the line at
v=0 where the value is 1, and since n is odd (geom:exc) is also
1 at 0, so the scalar is 1.

On the main elliptic component f_R has no divisor and is constant, equal to
f_{exc}(∞)=1 at the node. Thus the cover of the main
component is the disjoint union of the m copies of E indexed by
Yinmu_m. On the exceptional line the substitution
Y'=Y((v-1)/(v+1))^{n/m} gives Y'^m=(v-n)/(v+n); its m
points over v=∞ are defined over k and attach one to each elliptic
component.

The dual graph is a tree, so the fiber is of compact type: the
identity-component Picard variety is the product E^m of the component
Jacobians. The marked point (O,1) extends to a section through the smooth
locus, and the relative Picard theorem for nodal curves of compact type makes
the Jacobian family an abelian scheme across this fiber (see
cite[Section~9.4, Theorem~1, and Section~9.2, Proposition~10]{BLR}). Shrinking
the parameter neighborhood removes the other singular fibers.



Proposition~geom:compact concerns an abelian scheme over an open subset
of the characteristic-zero parameter curve E_k, and after completing k it
is a family over that local field. No integral model of the Kummer cover in
residue characteristic dividing m is needed; the arithmetic reduction types
of nearby Jacobians are established separately.


## The second degeneration and its nodes

Choose an oriented period basis a,b of E(C) and take R_*=a/(2n), so
[n]R_*=a/2 is a nonzero 2-torsion point. The first-order equation below
records why a loop about R_* contributes one twist at each node.


For R_*=a/(2n) the special fiber of Lemma~geom:semistable consists
of a connected unramified degree-m cover E'to E and a rational
component, joined at m distinct nodes. In analytic coordinates near each
node the family has equation XU=-2nt, where t=R-R_* is an additive local
parameter.


Proof. 
Write Q=a/2=[n]R_* and use additive coordinates t=R-R_* and z=Z-Q. The
colliding sections are z=nt and z=-nt, the other divisor sections avoid
this neighborhood, and
[
 f_R=g(z,t),(z-nt)/(z+nt)
]
with g a unit. Taking a local mth root of g and V=Y/g^{1/m}, the
Kummer equation becomes

 (z+nt)(V^m-1)=-2nt.

For each V=zetainmu_m, U=V^m-1 is a local coordinate; setting
X=z+nt gives the asserted node equation. This is the local chart of the
normalization of the blowup of Lemma~geom:semistable, with X=0 the
exceptional component and U=0 the main component.

On the main component the limiting divisor is n(-R_*)-n(R_*), whose
unramified Kummer cover is governed by the m-torsion class
[
 frac nm((-R_*)-(R_*))
 ↔
 -(2n)/(m)R_*=-(a)/(m)quadin E(C).
]
This class has exact order m, so the cover is connected; being unramified of
degree m over an elliptic curve, it is itself an elliptic curve E'. The
exceptional cover is rational by Lemma~geom:semistable, and its m
attachments are exactly these nodes.


The first degeneration gives a split product fiber E^m together with a
neighborhood over which nearby smooth fibers can be compared to it; the
second gives m nodes of smoothing order one whose vanishing cycles we count
next in a single integral basis.

# Integral homology and explicit monodromy

Fix the complex elliptic curve E and the constant-E family of
Section~sec:geom. Let nge3 be odd and m>1 divide n. Write
Gamma_m=mu_m and let u:(Z,Y)mapsto(Z,e^{2pi i/m}Y) generate the deck
group; throughout Z[Gamma_m] is the group ring, not a cyclotomic integer
ring. We build an integral basis permuted by u and compute two monodromy
operators in that one basis. Their reduction on character spaces gives the
residual irreducibility used later.

## A basis from cutting the torus

Choose an oriented period basis a,b and identify the underlying oriented
torus with (R/Z)^2, with a horizontal and b vertical. Consider the
path of parameters

 R_s=(s)/(n)a, 0<s<tfrac12,

whose branch points are mp sa. Let I_s be the horizontal arc from -sa
to sa through O.


For parameters (mon:path) the cover C_{m,R_s}to E is trivial over
E∖ I_s; it is obtained by cutting m copies of E along I_s and
joining adjacent banks cyclically. Crossing the slit changes the sheet index by
pm1 according to the crossing orientation and the initial indexing.


Proof. 
For small s enclose the colliding sections in a small disk about O. By
Proposition~geom:compact the model at R=O restricts outside that disk
to m disjoint copies of its complement. The inclusion
E∖int(D)into E∖ I_s is a homotopy
equivalence, so the unramified cover of E∖ I_s is trivial. As s
varies in (0,1/2) the arcs I_s move by isotopy and the cover's
topological type is constant. The monodromy around either branch point is a
generator of Gamma_m, since its valuation in f_R is pm1. This gives
the stated gluing.


On each sheet let a_i be the horizontal loop at height 1/2 and b_i the
vertical loop at position 1/2, iinZ/mZ. They avoid the slit, meet once
on their common sheet, and are oriented so that

 < a_i,b_j>=delta_{ij},
 < a_i,a_j>=< b_i,b_j>=0,

and u sends a_i,b_i to a_{i+1},b_{i+1}.


The cycles a_i,b_i form an integral symplectic basis of
H_1(C_{m,R_s},Z). Consequently, for every smooth fiber,

 H_1(C_m,Z)simeqZ[Gamma_m]^2,
  H^1(C_m,Z)simeqZ[Gamma_m]^2.

After comparison, H^1_{et}(C_m,Z_l) is free of rank two over
Z_l[Gamma_m] for every prime l, including lmid m.


Proof. 
The genus is m, so H_1 has rank 2m. By (mon:intersection) the
2m displayed classes are independent and span a sublattice with unimodular
pairing; if its index in H_1 were d, the intersection determinant would
differ by d^2, but both are 1, so d=1. The deck action gives the first
isomorphism.

The dual of a regular module is regular: the coefficient-of-identity pairing
Z[Gamma_m]timesZ[Gamma_m]toZ, (x,y)mapsto[1](xy), is perfect and,
with the pullback action on the dual, identifies
Hom_{Z}(Z[Gamma_m],Z) with Z[Gamma_m]. This proves the
cohomological assertion without dividing by m. Topological local
triviality transports the result through the smooth parameter open, and
comparison gives the 'etale statement.



The compact-type degeneration gives a second view: the Jacobian extends to an
abelian scheme with special fiber E^m on which Gamma_m permutes the
factors regularly, and the integral homology of that fiber is the regular
module twice. The slit construction makes this concrete by identifying cycles
that continue toward the other degeneration.


## Vanishing cycles and a common monodromy basis

Let sto1/2 in (mon:path). The branch points meet at a/2 while the
slit fills all but a shrinking gap in the horizontal circle. Take a small disk
about a/2 containing both branch points; its boundary has m closed lifts
delta_i, the neck circles at the m nodes of Lemma~geom:collision,
hence the vanishing cycles.


After a cyclic indexing of the lifts,

 [delta_i]=pm(a_i-a_{i+1}), iinZ/mZ,

or the same formula with i-1 in place of i+1.


Proof. 
Arrange the boundary circle to cross the slit transversely twice. A lifted
boundary circle has its upper arc on one sheet and its lower arc on an adjacent
sheet; index the upper sheet by i, so the lower is i+1 or i-1 by
Lemma~mon:slit. The circle is disjoint from every a_j, and crosses the
two corresponding vertical loops once each with opposite signs and no other
b_j. These intersections determine its class in the integral symplectic basis
of Proposition~mon:integral, giving (mon:delta).


[Monodromy transvections]
In one integral basis a_i,b_i as above, the geometric monodromy of the
constant-E family contains operators T_a,T_b with

 T_a(a_j)&=a_j,&

# Rank-two systems and the congruent chain

The cyclic covers now supply the Galois representations that transfer
automorphy. The involution pairs opposite rotation characters, making each
character space two-dimensional with the determinant and Hodge--Tate weights of
an elliptic curve. The integral regular-module computation then yields
congruences even at primes dividing the covering degree.

# Prescribing the local reduction type

The degeneration at the origin lets us impose the same local behavior on every
character summand of the covering Jacobian. We first explain why the reduction
type of an abelian variety is controlled, locally in a characteristic-zero
family, by a sufficiently large finite torsion module, and then apply this to
the Jacobian family and translate the reduction into conditions on the
two-dimensional systems.

## Finite torsion controls the reduction

Throughout, a local field is a finite extension of Q_ell; here ell is
the residue characteristic of the base field, and d is an auxiliary prime
different from ell. For an abelian variety A write
V_d(A)=T_d(A) ⊗ _{Z_d}Q_d.

unramified character on its line and eps_ell^{-1} times an unramified

the line is preserved already before the extension.

F of residue characteristic ell. Then simultaneously for every rotation

V_{theta,ell}|_{G_{F_v}} is potentially crystalline and potentially
ordinary of weight 0;

Proof.

potential ordinary filtration exists.

# A single parameter with all residual properties

We now choose one parameter for the whole congruence chain. The geometric
monodromy gives irreducibility and suitable Frobenius elements; Hilbert
irreducibility lets us keep them after imposing the local conditions of
Section~sec:local. To get decomposed genericity we must control every
place above a split rational prime, so we specialize the two conjugate
parameter families together.

Throughout, K,E,p,n,F,U are as in Section~sec:sys, so p>100, n=3p,
F=K(mu_n). The pairs of covering degree and residual characteristic needing

 (m,ell)=(3,p),(3,5),(p,3).

## Consequences of the two monodromy transformations

H^1(C_m,overlineF_ell) on every nontrivial rotation-character space is
absolutely irreducible, its image contains opposite nonidentity root

regular semisimply on all these nontrivial character spaces simultaneously.
These statements also hold for the conjugate family over K.


Proof. 
Work first with homology; duality transfers the conclusions. On the character
space with generator eigenvalue lambdainoverlineF_ell^times the
transformations of Theorem~mon:transvections are
[
 T_a=1&sigma c_lambda0&1,
 T_b=1&0tau c_lambda&1,
 c_lambda=2-lambda-lambda^{-1},
]
with sigma,tauin{1,-1}. Since c_lambda=-(lambda-1)^2/lambda, it is
nonzero when lambdane1. The two matrices have different unique invariant
lines, even over the algebraic closure, so their common representation is
absolutely irreducible.

The product T_aT_b^k has determinant one and trace

 2+sigmatau,k,c_lambda^2.

In odd characteristic a determinant-one matrix is regular semisimple exactly
when its trace is neither 2 nor -2. If m=3 then
lambda+lambda^{-1}=-1, so c_lambda=3 for both nontrivial characters; take
k=2, so the two possible traces in (res:trace) are 20 and -16.
Modulo 5 these are 0 and 4, and modulo p>100 they are 20 and -16;
in neither case do they equal pm2.

For m=p and ell=3 take k=1; failure of regular semisimplicity would
force c_lambda^2=4 or -4, since c_lambdane0 and the excluded traces are
pm2. Over F_3 the elements 4 and -4 are pm1, so
c_lambda^2=pm1; equivalently c_lambda=pm1 or pm i. Multiplying
c_lambda=2-lambda-lambda^{-1} through by lambda^2 gives, in each case, a
degree-four polynomial over F_3 satisfied by lambda. Hence
lambda has degree dle4 over F_3 and its order divides
3^d-1le80, contradicting the fact that its order is the prime p>100.
Thus the same product works in this case too.

The transformations come from loops in the constant-elliptic-curve parameter
space, which by Theorem~mon:transvections is a pullback of the family over
U; its monodromy image lies in the geometric monodromy over U. Duality
preserves absolute irreducibility, nonidentity unipotents, and regular
semisimplicity, giving the cohomological claims. The geometric argument works
for every complex elliptic curve, hence for the conjugate of E.


## The original elliptic curve and its conjugate

The trivial character needs a separate argument, because the geometric family
varies only a quadratic twist of E; its large residual image was built into

Proof.

(a&bc&d), trace and

# Propagation of automorphy, descent, and the CM case

Fix tin U(K) from Proposition~res:special, taking the local opens of
Proposition~loc:opens, and write B=B_t. The four systems
V_eta,V_chi,V_psi,V_1 have the congruences of
Corollary~sys:congruences, the local properties of
Corollary~loc:chars, and the residual properties of
Section~sec:res. We first explain how automorphy at one coefficient prime
gives what is needed at the next.

## Coefficient primes and local automorphic conditions


Suppose a weight-zero regular algebraic cuspidal representation pi of
GL_2(A_F) realizes one member of the compatible system V_theta. Then it
realizes every member, with the coefficient embeddings fixed in
Section~sec:sys.


Proof. 
Outside a finite set and away from the coefficient prime, the characteristic
polynomial of Frobenius on V_theta is independent of that prime, as an
algebraic polynomial with the fixed embeddings of character values; the
good-place characteristic polynomials of r_iota(pi) have the same
compatibility. Equality at the original prime identifies the polynomials at
every other prime. Both representations are semisimple: for V_theta this is
Faltings' theorem as in Proposition~sys:properties, and automorphic Galois
representations are semisimple. Chebotarev and Brauer--Nesbitt give the
isomorphism.


The next lift also needs the local automorphic component at the new prime. We
recover it by applying Varma's comparison at a different coefficient prime,
where it applies directly.


Suppose a weight-zero regular algebraic cuspidal representation pi of
PGL_2(A_F) realizes V_theta. Let lin{3,p} and vmid l, with
J_t=Jac(C_n(t)).
[label=(roman*)]

rec_{F_v}(pi_v) has zero monodromy.
 If J_t has semistable reduction of toric rank n at v, then pi_v
is a quadratic or trivial twist of Steinberg and pi is iota_l-ordinary of
weight zero at v.



Proof. 
Choose a coefficient prime a different from the residue characteristic l.
Lemma~auto:transport identifies r_{iota_a}(pi) with V_{theta,a}.
Varma's local-global compatibility identifies the underlying semisimplified
Weil representations of its local Weil--Deligne parameter and
rec^T_{F_v}(pi_v) cite[Theorem~1]{Varma}; we use this for the Weil
representations first, not assuming equality of monodromy operators.

In the potentially good case, pass to an extension where the Jacobian has good
reduction. All Frobenius eigenvalues on its H^1, hence on the character
summand, have complex absolute value the square root of the residue
cardinality; taking a positive power of Frobenius shows this also before the
extension. A rank-two Weil--Deligne representation with nonzero monodromy has
Frobenius eigenvalues differing by the norm, which cannot have equal absolute
value, so the automorphic parameter has zero monodromy. The Tate twist in
(eq:tate) does not change this.

In the totally toric case, Corollary~loc:chars gives nonzero monodromy on
V_{theta,a}, so its semisimplified Weil representation is a sum of two
characters differing by the norm. Under local Langlands for GL_2 the only
possibilities are the special parameter with nonzero monodromy and the
zero-monodromy parameter of a one-dimensional local representation; the latter is
not generic, and every local component of a cuspidal representation is generic.
So pi_v is a twist of Steinberg, and trivial central character makes the
twisting character have square one.

The local computation in the proof of cite[Lemma~6.1.3(4)]{CN}, invoking
cite[Lemma~5.6]{Geraghty}, shows a quadratic twist of Steinberg is
iota_l-ordinary of weight zero. It uses only the stated local representation
and trivial central character, so it applies also when the quadratic character is
ramified: the ordinary condition is imposed at a deep level, and a finite-order
twist multiplies normalized Hecke eigenvalues by units.

## Descending to the original field

The character adjustment in the next argument identifies the descended
representation with the prescribed one.

## Local parameters and the CM case

For non-CM E, Theorem~aut:compat shows pi_E has trivial central
character, realizes H^1(E) at every coefficient prime, and has the required
Frobenius-semisimplified Weil--Deligne parameter at each finite place (choosing
a coefficient prime different from the residue characteristic). At infinity a
cuspidal representation is generic, so the archimedean classification with
weight-zero infinitesimal character gives exactly (eq:inf); the
finite-dimensional alternative is not generic. Equations (eq:tate) and
(eq:Lshift) give the stated normalization.

Suppose now E has complex multiplication by an imaginary quadratic M. Its
endomorphisms are defined over KM (their faithful action on invariant
differentials has eigenvalues in M fixed by G_{KM}). The main theorem of
complex multiplication expresses the two one-dimensional constituents of
H^1(E)|_{G_{KM}} as realizations of algebraic Hecke characters with infinity
types given by the CM type [cite].

If Mnotsubset K, Galois exchanges the two characters and H^1(E) is induced
from either over KM/K; automorphic induction gives the required representation
of GL_2(A_K). If M=K, use the isobaric sum of the two characters. In either
case normalize by |det|^{1/2} so the Tate-normalized parameter realizes
H^1(E); complex multiplication and local class field theory, together with
local compatibility of quadratic automorphic induction in the first case, give
the finite parameters and infinity types. Their central character is trivial:
the determinant of H^1(E) is eps_l^{-1}, so undoing the Tate normalization
gives determinant one, hence trivial central character.

In both the CM and non-CM cases the finite factors also agree for the
residue-characteristic coefficient prime. For good reduction this is crystalline
comparison. For bad potentially good reduction, the Weil--Deligne inertia
action is finite and nontrivial with zero monodromy and determinant one; its
invariant space cannot be one-dimensional (a semisimple two-dimensional
determinant-one representation with a nonzero fixed vector is trivial), so it has
no invariants and local factor one, nontriviality of inertia following from the
crystalline good-reduction criterion [cite]. In the potentially
multiplicative case the Tate-curve description, with a quadratic twist, gives the
same special Weil--Deligne parameter at every coefficient prime. This completes
the proof of Theorem~thm:main.
