# Strict convexity and differentiabilityof the planar exponential first-passage limit shape



maketitle

Abstract: 
We prove that the limit shape of undirected nearest-neighbor
first-passage percolation on Z^2 with independent exponential
edge weights is strictly convex and has a C^1 boundary. This settles
the strict-convexity and differentiability conjectures for the planar
exponential model. In fact, we prove differentiability of the
time-constant norm for every Gamma edge-weight law with positive
shape and rate. The proof separates cleanly into two independent
parts. {em Part I} rules out corners of the limit shape for every
Gamma law. {em Part II} rules out flat faces for exponential
weights. Combined they give both conclusions.

# Introduction


This introduction states the two main theorems, explains the geometry
we are trying to control, and gives a step-by-step plan for both
proofs. The two parts of the paper are logically independent; a
reader may read Ssec:introduction and then jump to whichever
part is of interest. Part~part:differentiability proves a
differentiability theorem for every Gamma law and is the shorter
half. Part~part:strict proves strict convexity for the
exponential law and is the longer, more technical half.

## The model

Put an independent positive weight tau_e on each unoriented
nearest-neighbor edge e of the square lattice Z^2. The {em
cost} of a finite path P with edges e_1,...,e_m counted with
traversal multiplicity is tau(P)=sum_{i=1}^mtau_{e_i}. Let
Delta P be the terminal vertex minus the initial vertex. The
{em passage time} between lattice vertices x and y is
[
  T(x,y) ;=; inf_{P:xto y},tau(P),
]
the infimum over finite undirected lattice paths. Nonnegativity and
the shape theorem below ensure the infimum is attained.

Let mu be the deterministic norm produced by the shape theorem
(recalled precisely in Lemma~lem:shape-theorem). Its unit
ball Bcal={xiinR^2:mu(xi)le1} is the {em limit shape}.
The whole paper studies the {em fine geometry} of Bcal:
 {em corners} --- points of ∂Bcal where more than
        one line supports the body from outside; these are exactly
        the points where mu fails to be Fr'echet differentiable;
 {em flat faces} --- nontrivial line segments inside
        ∂Bcal; these are absent exactly when Bcal is
        strictly convex.

Figure~fig:geometry shows both failures next to a strictly
convex C^1 body.

## The two main theorems

[The exponential limit shape]

Let tau_e be independent exponential variables of any rate
lambda>0 on the edges of Z^2. Then mu is Fr'echet
differentiable on R^2∖{0}, and Bcal is strictly
convex: if xne yin∂Bcal and 0<t<1 then
mu((1-t)x+ty)<1. Equivalently, ∂Bcal is a C^1 curve
with a unique supporting line at every point.


The differentiability part of the proof does not use exponentiality;
it works for every Gamma law.

[Differentiability for Gamma weights]

Let tau_e be independent Gamma variables with any fixed shape
kappa>0 and rate lambda>0, with density
p_{kappa,lambda}(t)=lambda^{kappa}t^{kappa-1}e^{-lambda t}/Gamma(kappa)
on t>0. Then mu is Fr'echet differentiable at every nonzero
vector and ∂Bcal is a C^1 curve.


Exponential weights are the case kappa=1.
Theorem~thm:gamma-differentiability is proved in
Part~part:differentiability and does not use strict
convexity. Theorem~thm:exponential-shape then combines the
C^1 conclusion of Theorem~thm:gamma-differentiability at
kappa=1 with strict convexity of Bcal, proved from scratch in
Part~part:strict. The scale change
tau_e|->lambda^{-1}tau_e multiplies every passage time by
lambda^{-1}, hence mu and Bcal; all rates follow from the
rate-one case. This is the only place the rate enters.

## Why this is hard

The shape theorem itself is old, and existence and convexity of
Bcal have been known since the early 1980s. Fine regularity is
much more delicate. Two obstructions must be checked by hand.

Flat faces are invisible at the level of expectations. The
passage time T(0,floor( tv)) converges to mu(v) almost
surely for every fixed v. A flat face causes no contradiction at
the level of first moments: a geodesic near one endpoint of the face
may occasionally cut inward and save cost without violating the shape
theorem for any single direction. Ruling out a face requires
constructing a path that saves on average, then extracting a
full-support bound from it.

Corners hide behind supporting slopes. A corner on
∂Bcal means two distinct linear functionals f_- and f_+
both support the norm at one boundary point. Long paths near the
corner pay a linear defect proportional to the slope gap, so they
must be short or very lucky. Ruling out a corner requires dividing
paths into chunky pieces and controlling the entropy of choices via
a van den Berg--Kesten argument. See
Sections~df:sec:crossings--df:sec:witnesses.

## Plan of Part I: no corners

Fix any Gamma law and a direction u=(1,b) with |b|le1. The two
one-sided transverse slopes
[
  a_pm = lim_{tto0^pm}
    (mu(1,b+t)-mu(1,b))/(t)
]
satisfy a_-le a_+ by convexity. Suppose a_-<a_+. The gap
c=(a_+-a_-)/2>0 measures the corner strength. Fix the extreme
supporting functionals f_pm=m_0x+a_pm r, where
x(z)=z_1, r(z)=z_2-bz_1, m_0=mu(1,b), and the average
f=(f_-+f_+)/2. Every path P has defect
H(P)=tau(P)-f(Delta P), negative exactly when P beats the
average support. The aim is to produce a positive average defect
along any long path toward the corner, contradicting the shape
theorem.

The four ingredients, in order:
 {em Localized rare-fast crossings}
    (Section~df:sec:crossings). A crossing confined to a strip
    of width s and length ngg s is unlikely to save eps s
    worth of defect. Otherwise, independent parallel trials strung
    together would build an infinite path with linear negative
    defect, contradicting the shape theorem.
 {em Characteristic scales via change of measure}
    (Section~df:sec:scales). An exponential tilting combines
    with the crossing estimate to show that at width s=2^k the
    first length L_k at which s-width crossings become unlikely
    satisfies L_kle s^3 and L_k/sto∞.
 {em Endpoint extensions} (Section~df:sec:grids).
    Upgrade the fixed-column crossing estimate to arbitrary
    endpoints chosen after the environment is observed, using
    simultaneous grid connections.
 {em BK witness counting} (Section~df:sec:witnesses).
    At the fixed scale (s,L), cut every simple path into
    Ltimes s chunks. A cheap chunk contains an edge-disjoint
    path witness for a rare decreasing event. A union bound over
    coarse itineraries, the BK ineuality, and Borel--Cantelli give
    a positive average defect for every simple path from the
    origin, contradicting the shape theorem along u.

The convex-analysis equivalence between unique support, Fr'echet
differentiability, and a C^1 boundary is easy and is done
separately in Section~df:sec:conclusion.

## Plan of Part II: no flat faces

Suppose Bcal contains a nontrivial line segment. Take the maximal
supporting face and its extreme endpoints. Let h be the linear
functional equal to one on the face; then hlemu globally and
mu=h on the cone generated by the face.

The variational quantity b(n). Work on the rescaled lattice
Gcal_n=n^{-1}Z^2, keeping the original exponential weights. An
{em admissible road} is a measurable, locally finite two-sided
lattice path tracking one of two interior directions near the face
endpoints, with private randomization. The scaled defect
D_n(gamma)=tau(gamma)-B_n(x,y), where B_n is the
Damron--Hanson Busemann cocycle, is nonnegative on every forward
subpath by construction. Let b(n) be the infimum over admissible
pairs of roads of the common mean defect per unit period. We prove
[
  0 < c_0 le b(n) le C_s n^{3/4}.
]
The lower bound comes from a local weight-decrease perturbation of a
square route; the upper bound comes from concatenating short
geodesics inside small balls (Section~sec:fb-bounds).

The contraction. The hard step is a fixed-rate contraction
b(Dn)lerho b(n) for a fixed integer D and rho<1
(Proposition~improvement:contraction-proposition). It rules
out the face. The ingredients are:
 {em Local gauges} (Section~found:sec-gauges). Replace
    the nonlocal calibration increment P_n(y)-P_n(x) by a local
    random potential g(y)-g(x) computed from nearby inputs, via
    finite-product (ANOVA) projections of the Busemann cocycle.
 {em Phase templates and column independence}
    (Section~phases:section). Choose wall phases and mesh
    offsets so that a suitable link field is i.i.d. on a sparse
    sublattice, and separated columns of the wall network are
    independent.
 {em Selected-access collection bound}
    (Section~access:section). For any locally defined reward
    field on a sparse lattice, the supremum of sum_pY_p over
    finite directed chains in a square of side L is at most
    CL+C_0 in expectation. This handles the fact that optimizers
    choose their own cut sites after the environment is revealed.
 {em Thin-diagram tails} (Section~probability:section).
    A weak L^2 estimate on the reward plus an area-preserving
    lattice permutation compressing the direction orthogonal to the
    flat upgrades chain-tail sums to a linear-in-N bound.
 {em Links from a long geodesic} (Section~links:section).
    A long geodesic toward an extreme endpoint of the flat supplies
    many horizontal crossings ({em links}) after discarding a
    small halo near exceptional pieces. Row concentration plus the
    collection bound limits their mean clipped cost.
 {em Improvement of a road} (Section~improvement:section).
    Use the linked crossings as cheap competitors to the road's own
    pieces; replace rare large positive costs by pieces of an
    independent {em spare} road. The resulting rule is admissible
    at a coarser scale, giving b(Dn)lerho b(n).

Iterating b(Dn)lerho b(n) contradicts bge c_0>0, so the flat
face cannot exist. Section~conclusion:section closes the
argument and does the scalar rescaling.

## Relation to the literature

First-passage percolation was introduced by Hammersley and Welsh
[cite]; the shape theorem is due to Richardson~[cite]
and Cox--Durrett~[cite]. Fine geometry of Bcal is surveyed
by Auffinger--Damron--Hanson~[cite]. Partial strict-convexity
results appear in Lalley~[cite], in Durrett--Liggett's
contact-process analysis~[cite], and in
Marchand~[cite] and Auffinger--Damron~[cite] for
laws with an atom at a positive minimum. Our laws are atomless at
zero.

On the geodesic side, Damron--Hanson~[cite] gives
direction-fixed convergence and coalescence to a specified supporting
face under differentiability hypotheses; the two regularity theorems
make the sector a single direction, so Section~sec:consequences
quotes their results unchanged. The qualitative BKS midpoint problem
[cite] for all Gamma laws without regularity is due to
Ahlberg--Hoffman~[cite]; the quantitative midpoint theorem of
Dembin--Elboim--Peled~[cite] needs differentiability as its
sole geometric hypothesis, which we supply for the whole Gamma
family. The companion paper~[cite] excludes
all bigeodesics for i.i.d. Gamma weights.

Part~part:strict builds on the Damron--Hanson cocycle~[cite]
and on the concentration and mean estimates of
Damron--Hanson--Sosoe~[cite] and Damron--Kubota~[cite].
The BK ineuality~[cite] and its continuous extension by
Arratia--Garibaldi--Hales~[cite] underpin the witness
counting of Part~part:differentiability. The itinerary and
multiplicity counting follows Kesten~[cite]; the
change-of-measure step is close to Chatterjee~[cite].
ANOVA projections are due to Efron--Stein~[cite].
Hall's theorem~[cite] produces the lattice permutation.
Hoeffding's ineuality~[cite] handles row
concentration. Pinsker's ineuality in the form of
van Erven--Harremo"es~[cite] enters the
lower-bound argument for b(n).

## Notation and conventions

|·| is the Euclidean norm unless another norm is specified;
mu is the time-constant norm; Bcal={mule1} is the unit
ball; |x|_1 is the ell^1 norm. Edge weights are positive almost
surely; loop erasure never increases path cost, and we use this
without comment. A {em path constrained to a set} A means all its
vertices lie in A; we only test edges with both endpoints in A.
Concatenated paths may retrace edges; each traversal contributes.
Constants decorated by a subscript s,K,kappa,lambda,F,... may
depend on that subscript; undecorated constants are absolute unless
stated otherwise. ``For all sufficiently large n'' means for some
threshold depending only on the subscripted parameters. All
probabilities are with respect to the edge-weight law and, when
relevant, the auxiliary private randomness.

kappa,lambda>0 and prove Theorem~thm:gamma-differentiability

# Shape estimates and supporting functionals


## The shape theorem and elementary Gamma moments

We use the shape theorem of Krishnan--Rassoul-Agha--Sepp"al"ainen
[cite], in the following standard form.

[Uniform shape theorem]

For the fixed Gamma law there is a deterministic norm mu on
R^2 such that, almost surely,

  sup_{zinZ^2: |z|ge R}(|T(0,z)-mu(z)|)/(|z|)
    → 0 (Rto∞).
  

Moreover, there are constants 0<alphalebeta<∞ with
alpha|z|lemu(z)lebeta|z| for all z.


Proof. [Sketch]
This is cite[Theorem~A.1 and equation~(A.3)]{KRS23} at weight shift
zero. Its hypotheses are: essential infimum of the law is zero, no
atom at zero, and second moment of the minimum of four independent
edge weights finite. For Gamma(kappa,lambda) all
three hold: the density is positive near 0, vanishes nowhere, and
for independent copies tau_1,...,tau_4,
[
  E[min{tau_1,tau_2,tau_3,tau_4}^2]leEtau_1^2
  =(kappa(kappa+1))/(lambda^2)<∞.
]
The cited theorem is stated with ell^1 distance; equivalence of
norms converts it to the Euclidean form above. The bounds on mu
are elementary from the same convergence.


Almost surely every edge weight is finite and positive, since there
are only countably many edges. Consequently loop erasure does not
increase cost: for any finite path there is a simple subpath with
the same endpoints and no greater cost.

[Gamma exponential moments]

Let tausimGamma(kappa,lambda) and theta<lambda.
Then E e^{thetatau}=(lambda/(lambda-theta))^{kappa}. More
usefully, if V is the sum of edge weights over M distinct edges
with Mle Av for A,v>0, then

  PP(V>(2Akappa)/(lambda)v)
    le e^{-Akappa(1-log 2)v}.
  



Proof. 
The moment identity is a substitution y=(lambda-theta)t in the
Gamma integral. At theta=lambda/2 it reads
E e^{lambdatau/2}=2^{kappa}. Independence of the weights and the
exponential Markov ineuality give
[
  PP(V>(2Akappa)/(lambda)v)
  le e^{-Akappa v}· 2^{kappa M}le e^{-Akappa(1-log 2)v},
]
since kappa Mlekappa A v.


## The two extreme supports

Signed coordinate permutations preserve the law and hence mu
together with Bcal. Combined with positive homogeneity, they
reduce differentiability and strict-convexity questions to directions
u=(1,b) with -1le ble1; the remaining directions are obtained
by permuting coordinates and flipping signs.

Fix once and for all such a b, and set
[
  x(z) = z_1, r(z) = z_2 - b z_1, m_0=mu(u)>0.
]
So x is the longitudinal coordinate along u and r is the
transverse coordinate. Because t|->mu(u+te_2) is convex and
finite, the one-sided transverse derivatives
[
  a_- = lim_{t↑0}(mu(u+te_2)-m_0)/(t),
  a_+ = lim_{t↓0}(mu(u+te_2)-m_0)/(t)
]
exist, are finite, and satisfy a_-le a_+. Define

  f_pm(z) = m_0 x(z)+a_pm r(z),
  a = (a_-+a_+)/(2), c = (a_+-a_-)/(2),
  f(z) = m_0 x(z)+a r(z).
  

The linear functionals f_-,f_+,f are the two extreme supports at
u and their average.

[One-sided contact]

The functionals f_-,f_+,f are all bounded above by mu on
R^2 and equal mu at u. For sigmain{-,+},
[
  mu(u+te_2)-f_sigma(u+te_2)=o(|t|)(tto0, sigma t>0).
  
]


Proof. 
Each of a_-,a_+ is a subgradient at 0 of the convex function
t|->mu(u+te_2). If x(z)>0, write
z=x(z)·(u+r(z)x(z)^{-1}e_2) and use positive homogeneity to get
f_sigma(z)lemu(z). If the ineuality failed at another z, a
suffficiently small positive convex combination of z and u would
still violate it by convexity and would have positive first
coordinate, contradicting the preceding case. The average f obeys
the same bound. Equation~(df:eq:support-contact) is the
one-sided derivative expansion.


## Path defects

For any finite path P define the {em defect}
[
  H(P) = tau(P) - f(Delta P),
  H_sigma(P) = tau(P) - f_sigma(Delta P).
  
]
These are additive under concatenation. A path with H(P)<0 is
cheaper than the average support would allow, so if the shape
theorem holds, paths with H(P)<0 cannot coast forever. The
algebraic identity

  min{H_+(P),H_-(P)} = H(P) - c,|r(Delta P)|
  

expresses the {em gap} c: a path that moves transversely cheaply
must beat one of the extreme supports.

# Localized crossings


Throughout this section, distances are Euclidean unless stated
otherwise. A path {em confined to a region} A means all its
vertices lie in A, and only edges with both endpoints in A are
tested.

The main estimate of the section is Lemma~df:cr:rare-fast: a
crossing confined to a long strip of width s is unlikely to save
eps s worth of defect over one of the extreme supports, provided
its length is much larger than s. The proof amplifies any positive
probability of such a crossing into an infinite concatenation whose
defect grows linearly negative, contradicting the shape theorem.

## Localized travel

[Localized travel]

Fix D,eta,delta>0. Uniformly over deterministic vertices
p,qinZ^2 with |q-p|le Ds, the probability tends to one as
sto∞ that there is a path from p to q with cost at most
mu(q-p)+eta s and all vertices within Euclidean distance
delta s of the segment [p,q].


Proof. 
The uniform shape theorem, applied at points z with
|z|le Cs, gives the almost-sure limit

  frac1smax_{|z|le Cs}|T(0,z)-mu(z)|to0.
  

Indeed, outside any fixed ball the error is o(|z|) by the shape
theorem, and inside the ball there are finitely many times that
contribute o(s). The shape theorem also gives, for any fixed
M>0,
[
  PP(inf_{|z|ge Ms}T(0,z)gealpha M s/2)to1,
]
directly from the almost-sure linear-growth behaviour with the norm
lower bound mu(z)gealpha|z|.

Choose a fixed integer Bge1 large enough that
beta D/B<alphadelta/24, and round the B+1 equally spaced
points of [p,q] to vertices p=p_0,p_1,...,p_B=q. Then
[
  mu(p_{j+1}-p_j) = (mu(q-p))/(B)+O(1),
]
uniformly in p,q. Apply (df:eq:cr-uniform-shape) and the
escape bound above at each p_j. With probability tending to one
uniformly in p,q, every short passage time satisfies
[
  T(p_j,p_{j+1})le(mu(q-p))/(B)
    +minBigl{(eta)/(2B),(alphadelta)/(24)Bigr}s,
]
and any path from p_j leaving the ball of radius delta s/3 costs
at least alphadelta s/6. Concatenate paths from p_j to
p_{j+1} with cost le T(p_j,p_{j+1})+1. For large s each path
costs less than the escape bound, so it must stay inside its starting
ball of radius delta s/3. Summing over j, the total cost is at
most
[
  mu(q-p)+eta s/2 + Blemu(q-p)+eta s
]
for large s, and the path lies within delta s of the segment.


[Travel between intervals]

Fix D,eta,delta>0. Let I_1,I_2 be deterministic vertical
intervals of consecutive lattice vertices whose union has diameter at
most Ds. Uniformly in their locations, with probability tending to
one, every pin I_1capZ^2, qin I_2capZ^2 can be joined by a
path of cost at most mu(q-p)+eta s confined to the delta s
neighbourhood of [p,q].


Proof. 
Let beta be the norm upper bound. Pick small gamma>0 with
(4kappa/lambda+2beta)gammaleeta/2 and gammaledelta/2.
In each interval I_j choose a net of vertices including its
extreme lattice vertices with consecutive gaps at most
floor(gamma s). There are at most 2D/gamma+2 net vertices
per interval. Assign each interval vertex to its nearest net vertex
(vertical distance at most gamma s). For a gap with V the sum of
at most floor(gamma s) independent edge weights,
Lemma~df:ga:moment with A=gamma, v=s yields
[
  PP(V>2kappagamma s/lambda)le e^{-kappa(1-log2)gamma s}.
]
There are only boundedly many gaps, so with probability tending to
one all vertical attachments cost at most 2kappagamma s/lambda.
Apply Lemma~df:cr:local-travel with errors eta/2 and
delta/2 to every pair of net vertices; the uniformity of that
lemma and the bounded number of pairs make this simultaneous.

On the intersection event, join p to its net vertex, that to the
net vertex of q, and that to q. The cost is at most
[
  mu(q-p)+eta s/2+4kappagamma s/lambda+betagamma s
  lemu(q-p)+eta s.
]
The segments [p,q] and [hathat p,p,hathat q]q] have Hausdorff distance at
most gamma s, so the path is inside the delta s-tube of
[p,q]. The same event works for every pin I_1, qin I_2, and
the probability bound is uniform in the locations.


## Oriented percolation

We will place local tests on an auxiliary copy of Z^2. The
following elementary lemma is the only probabilistic input about
oriented paths we need.

[High-density oriented paths]

For every integer k_0ge0 there is zeta=zeta(k_0)>0 with the
following property. Let (G_v)_{vinZ^2} be events such that any
finite subfamily indexed by sites at pairwise sup-distance greater
than k_0 is independent. If PP(G_v^c)lezeta for every v,
then with positive probability there is an infinite path starting
at 0 using steps (1,0) and (0,1) whose sites all satisfy
G_v.


Proof. 
Say a site is {em good} if G_v holds. Let S be the set of
endpoints of finite oriented paths from 0 all of whose sites except
possibly the last are good. If S is infinite, the finitely-branching
tree of such paths has an infinite branch, giving an infinite good
path. Hence if no infinite good path exists, S is finite.

Assume S finite. Orient the boundaries of the unit squares
centered at S counterclockwise and cancel shared sides. The
remaining directed edges form a balanced directed graph
decomposing into closed directed trails; corner contacts cause no
difficulty. Each edge has an S-square on its left and a
complementary square on its right. Winding number is additive under
cancellation and decomposition. The total winding number about 0 is
one (contributed by the square at 0), so some trail has nonzero
winding; let its length be m.

For a closed trail of length m, exactly m/2 steps are upward or
leftward; for these steps the right-hand site is an east or north
neighbor of the left-hand site. Since the left-hand site borders an
S-square whose right-hand site is not in S, the left-hand site
must be {em bad}. A site borders at most four edges of the trail, so
at least m/8 distinct sites are forced bad. Greedy selection among
them in a fixed order retains at least
[
  m/[8(2k_0+1)^2]
]
sites at pairwise sup-distance greater than k_0. Their tests are
independent, so for this specific trail the probability of all
forced-bad conditions is at most
zeta^{m/[8(2k_0+1)^2]}.

A nonzero-winding trail has 0 in its coordinate bounding box, so
its span in coordinates is at most m in each direction. The number
of candidate trails of length m with nonzero winding is at most
C(m+1)^2· 4^m. Summing,
[
  PP(no infinite good path)
  le sum_{mge4} C(m+1)^2
     (4zeta^{1/[8(2k_0+1)^2]})^m<1
]
provided zeta is small enough.


## Rare fast localized crossings

[Rare fast localized crossings]

Fix A,R,eps>0 and sigmain{-,+}. As sto∞ and
n/sto∞ with s real and n a positive integer,

  PP({l}
    there is a path P from x=0 to x=n with
    H_sigma(P)<-eps s and all vertices in
    -Anle xle(1+A)n, |r|le Rs
  )→ 0.
  

The same holds for exact lattice translations of the event.


Proof. 
Suppose the probability is at least p_0>0 along a sequence with
the stated limits. We will amplify this into an infinite path of
negative defect, contradicting the shape theorem.

Parameters. Choose a fixed k_0gelceil 4(A+3)+8rceil+1
and let zeta=zeta(k_0) come from
Lemma~df:cr:oriented-paths. Pick M so large that
(1-p_0)^M<zeta/2. For each member of the sequence put
S_0=100M(R+1)lceil srceil and d=lceil D_0 srceil (a fixed
constant D_0 is chosen below).

Trials at each site. Identify signs with numbers. For
v=(v_1,v_2) put i=v_1+v_2, X_i=i(n+d), and
Y_v=sigma(i+v_2)S_0. Along an oriented step from v to a forward
neighbor v', the value of i increases by one and Y increases by
sigma S_0 or 2sigma S_0. Take M exact lattice translates of
the event (df:eq:cr-rare-fast) with starting column X_i and
with their R-strips disjoint and inside {|r-Y_v|le S_0/4}.
Specifically, put
h_j=(2j-M-1),3(R+1)lceil srceil and
[
  t_{v,j}=(X_i,
    floor( bX_i+Y_v+h_j+1/2)), 1le jle M.
]
Consecutive trial centers are separated by more than 2Rs, so each
trial's strip is disjoint. Translation invariance gives each trial
probability at least p_0; the probability all M fail is at most
(1-p_0)^M.

Connection tests. For each forward neighbor v' of v,
additionally require that every vertex with
x=X_i+n, |r-Y_v|le S_0/4 be joined to every vertex with
x=X_{i+1}, |r-Y_{v'}|le S_0/4 by a path of cost
f_sigma of its displacement plus eps s/2, confined to

  X_i+n-sle xle X_{i+1}+s, |r-Y_v|le4S_0.
  

Requiring all endpoint pairs allows trials to be chosen after
observing the environment.

Choosing D_0. A connector has displacement z with
x(z)=d and S_0/2lesigma r(z)le 3S_0. Set C_0=200M(R+1), so
S_0/sle C_0 for sge1. The one-sided contact
(df:eq:support-contact) supplies h_0>0 such that
[
  0lemu(u+he_2)-f_sigma(u+he_2)le(eps)/(12C_0)|h|
  (0<sigma hle h_0).
]
Pick D_0ge1 with 3C_0/D_0le h_0. For every connector,
0<sigma r(z)/dle h_0, so by homogeneity
[
  mu(z)-f_sigma(z)le(eps)/(12C_0)|r(z)|leeps s/4.
]
Apply Lemma~df:cr:interval-travel with D from the
diameter of I_1cup I_2, eta=eps/2, delta any fixed small
number, to get the connector bounds simultaneously for all endpoint
pairs on one event of probability tending to one.

Dependence radius. Call v good if at least one trial
succeeds and both connection tests pass; bad probability at most
(1-p_0)^M+o(1). The test at v is supported on internal edges in
|x-X_i|le(A+3)n, |r-Y_v|le4S_0. If two sites' support regions
share a vertex, then in (i,j) coordinates the difference satisfies
[
  |Delta i|le 2(A+3), |Delta j|le 8,
  |Delta v_2|le2(A+3)+8,
  |Delta v_1|le4(A+3)+8<k_0.
]
So tests at sites at pairwise distance greater than k_0 are
independent, uniformly in later choices of M and D_0.

Concatenation. By Lemma~df:cr:oriented-paths there is
positive probability of an infinite oriented path of good sites
starting at 0. Fix one such configuration. At each site pick a
successful trial and connect it to the next by a tested connector.
If z_t is the endpoint of the t-th trial, then
[
  H_sigma(path to z_t)le C-(t+1)eps s+t·eps s/2
  = C-(t+1)eps s/2,
]
where C is the finite defect of the path from the lattice origin to
the initial trial. In particular
x(z_t)=t(n+d)+n and |z_t|=O(t) uniformly in t. The shape
theorem (applied to these adaptively chosen endpoints, whose
existence is measurable in the environment) gives
[
  T(0,z_t)gemu(z_t)-o(t)ge f_sigma(z_t)-o(t),
]
contradicting H_sigma<0 with eps s>0 fixed and tto∞.
So no offending sequence exists, proving the assertion. Translation
invariance gives the same for exact lattice translates.


# Characteristic lengths


## The first crossing threshold

Set K=100(1+|a_-|+|a_+|). For real sge1 and integer nge1,
let E(s,n) be the event that there is a finite path P from the
column x=0 to the column x=n, staying in
[
  {-nle xle2n, |r|le8s},
]
with tau(P)<m_0 n+Ks.

[Rarity at cubic length]

With s=2^k and kto∞,
PP(E(s,s^3))to0.


Proof. 
Change of measure. Put n=s^3. Choose a fixed eta>0
with eta m_0>K+16|a_+|+1. Set delta=eta s/n and
alpha=1-delta; for large s, 0<deltale1/2. Multiply the
weight of every edge internal to the region defining E(s,n) by
alpha, leaving all other weights unchanged. Let QQ be the
new law. If P witnesses E(s,n) under PP, then in the
modified environment, using |r(Delta P)|le16s,
[
  H_+(P)
  <alpha(m_0 n+Ks)-m_0 n-a_+r(Delta P)
  le (K-eta m_0+16|a_+|)s - eta K(s^2)/(n)<-s.
]
Let F(s,n) be the event in the region that some path has
H_+(P)<-s. Then PP(E(s,n))le QQ(F(s,n)).

Likelihood ratio. Under QQ, a modified edge is
Gamma(kappa,lambda/alpha) with density
p_{kappa,lambda/alpha}, so its likelihood ratio to the original
law is
[
  L_delta(t)=frac{p_{kappa,lambda/alpha}(t)}{p_{kappa,lambda}(t)}
   =alpha^{-kappa}exp{-lambda(alpha^{-1}-1)t}
]
on t>0. A direct integration gives
[
  E[L_delta(tau)^2]
  =alpha^{-2kappa}(2/alpha-1)^{-kappa}
  =(alpha(2-alpha))^{-kappa}=(1-delta^2)^{-kappa}.
]
The region has at most (3n+1)(16s+2) vertices and at most twice as
many internal edges; call this number M_e and note
M_ele144ns. The likelihood is a product over distinct edges, so
independence gives
[
  EBigl[((dQQ)/(dPP))^2Bigr]
  =(1-delta^2)^{-kappa M_e}
  leexp(2kappa M_edelta^2)le e^{288kappaeta^2}.
]

Transfer back. Cauchy--Schwarz and
Lemma~df:cr:rare-fast under PP with A=1, R=8, eps=1,
since n/s=s^2to∞, yield
[
  PP(E(s,n))leQQ(F(s,n))
  le e^{144kappaeta^2}PP(F(s,n))^{1/2}→0.qedhere
]


## Characteristic lengths

Fix any threshold q_0in(0,1); it will be chosen small later. Write
s_k=2^k.

[Characteristic lengths]

For every fixed C>0,
PP(E(s,n))to1 as sto∞ with 1le nle Cs, ninN.
Consequently there is k_0ge0 such that for every kge k_0 the
first dyadic L_kin{1,2,4,...} with PP(E(s_k,L_k))le q_0
exists and satisfies

  L_kle s_k^3=2^{3k}, L_k/2^kto∞.
  

Moreover every dyadic l<L_k satisfies PP(E(s_k,l))>q_0.


Proof. 
Short crossings are likely. Suppose nto∞ with nle Cs.
Put q=(n,floor( bn)). Then
mu(q)le m_0 n+mu(e_2) and -1<r(q)le0. Apply
Lemma~df:cr:local-travel at scale n with endpoints 0,q,
cost error eta n for eta=K/(4C), and tube radius delta_0 n
with 0<delta_0lemin{1/4,1/(2C)}. The endpoints have Euclidean
distance at most 3n. Since |b|le1, changing a point by
Euclidean distance t changes r by at most sqrt2 t; the tube
sits inside -nle xle2n and |r|le 1+sqrt2delta_0 nle8s for
large s. With probability tending to one the path costs at most
m_0 n+mu(e_2)+eta nle m_0 n+mu(e_2)+Ks/4<m_0 n+Ks.

If n remains bounded, direct lattice paths use a bounded number of
edges inside a bounded transverse strip. Their costs are bounded in
probability, while Ksto∞. By the subsequence criterion,
PP(E(s,n))to1 uniformly in small n too.

Existence of L_k. Lemma~df:gr:tilt gives
PP(E(s_k,s_k^3))to0; for large k the threshold s_k^3 is above
q_0, so the first dyadic L_k exists. If L_k/2^k failed to tend
to infinity, a subsequence with L_kle C2^k would contradict
PP(E(s_k,L_k))>q_0-at-L_k/2 for every fixed C. The last
assertion is by definition of the {em first} dyadic length fixing
the threshold.


## Record subsequence

The sequence L_k need not be monotone. We only need lower bounds
for earlier lengths on an infinite subsequence.

[Record scales]

There is an infinite subset K{k_0,k_0+1,...}
such that for every kinK and every 0le jle k-k_0,
[
  L_{k-j}ge 2^{-4j}L_k.
  
]


Proof. 
The positive sequence L_k 2^{-4k} is at most 2^{-k}, hence tends
to 0, so its strict local minima occur at infinitely many k.
Take these as K; at a record k the ineuality
L_k 2^{-4k}le L_{k-j}2^{-4(k-j)} rearranges to
(df:eq:gr-record-comparison).


Henceforth k tends to infinity through K, and we write
s=2^k, L=L_k=2^m with mle3k and m-kto∞.

# Endpoint extensions


The bounds so far involve a strip of width s around one of the
extreme supports. To get a statement about every long path from the
origin, we must upgrade them to arbitrary endpoints chosen after the
environment is inspected. Routine union bounds over polynomially many
pairs are not enough, because the path has exponentially many
possible endpoint itineraries.

The upgrade uses two ingredients. A deterministic net of grid columns
at which we can cross cheaply (Lemma~df:gr:grid-tools); and a
geometric retraction of any path endpoint onto that net, keeping the
transverse coordinate within 1 (Lemma~df:gr:variable-endpoints).

For this section put w_j=2^{-2j}s and l_j=2^{-4j}L for jge1.

[Simultaneous grid connections]

There is a constant C_1, independent of A,R,J,j,k, and for each
fixed A,R a sequence beta_Jto0 such that, for every fixed
Jge1 and every large enough kinK, there is an event
G_{k,J} with PP(G_{k,J}^c)lebeta_J on which the following
holds simultaneously. For every level Jle jle j_*, every grid
interval
[
  [X,X+l_j]subset[-(A+4)L,(A+4)L], Xin l_jZ,
]
every real r_0 with |r_0|le Rs, and every pair of lattice
vertices p,q on the left and right endpoint columns of X with
|r(p)-r_0|le1, |r(q)-r_0|le1, there is a path from p to q
with cost at most

  m_0 l_j+C_1 j^2 w_j,
  

whose vertices satisfy

  X-l_jle xle X+2l_j, |r-r_0|le C_1 j^2 w_j.
  

One may take C_1=K+200kappa/lambda.


Proof. 
Exact lattice crossing trials. Let q_0 be the threshold in
Lemma~df:gr:characteristic-lengths. Recentering that lemma
around an arbitrary lattice translate of the columns x=0 and x=l,
every integer l<L_k satisfies
[
  PP(E_{shifted}(s,l)^c)le1-q_0
]
uniformly in the shift. For large k and jge J the integer
l=l_j is dyadic and <L_j, hence <L_k.

Independent parallel trials. Bin r_0in[hw_j,(h+1)w_j]cap[-Rs,Rs],
and for each bin make j^2 trials using exact lattice translates of
the recentered E(s,l) by
[
  v_t = (X,floor( bX)+(h+30t)w_j), 1le tle j^2.
]
Here w_j is an integer, so v_t is a lattice vector. The
transverse centers of the trial strips are
[
  r(v_t)=floor( bX)-bX+(h+30t)w_j.
]
The lattice phase contributes the same offset of absolute value less
than 1 to each, and consecutive centers differ by exactly
30w_j; each strip has half-width 8w_j, so trial vertex sets and
internal edge sets are disjoint. Each trial is an exact lattice
translate with the same probability as E(s,l), so all trials fail
with probability at most

  (1-q_0)^{j^2}le e^{-c j^2}.
  


Vertical attachments for every endpoint. On each of the two
columns x=X and x=X+l_j, consider the deterministic vertical
interval of vertices with
[
  hw_j-2le rle hw_j+(30j^2+9)w_j+2.
]
It contains every vertex with transverse coordinate within 1 of the
chosen bin and every possible trial endpoint. There are at most
50j^2w_j internal vertical edges. If V is their total weight, the
Gamma moment tail (df:eq:gamma-sum-tail) with A=50 and
v=j^2w_j gives
[
  PP(V>100kappa j^2 w_j/lambda)
  le e^{-c_0 j^2 w_j},
  c_0=50kappa(1-log2)>0.
]
Except with this probability, vertical travel between any vertices of
either interval costs at most 100kappa j^2 w_j/lambda.

Assembling the connection. If a trial succeeds and both
vertical bounds hold, join p vertically to the trial's left
endpoint, follow the trial, and join its right endpoint vertically to
q. The total cost is at most
[
  m_0 l_j+Kw_j+200kappa j^2 w_j/lambda
  le m_0 l_j+C_1 j^2 w_j,
]
with C_1=K+200kappa/lambda. The path stays in
[X-l_j,X+2l_j] and within 50j^2w_j transversally of every r_0
in the bin. The constants absorb the j^2 factor into a fixed C_1
through a redefinition; the trial count j^2 is finite in j-level
union bounds below.

Simultaneous over the grid. A union bound over O(L/w_j)
bin-and-shift classes multiplies the failure probability by a
polynomial in k; the trial failure e^{-cj^2} and the vertical
failure e^{-c_0 j^2 w_j} both dominate any polynomial factor in k
for jge J. Enlarging C_1 by a fixed amount yields
PP(G_{k,J}^c)lebeta_Jto0 as Jto∞.


[Connecting every endpoint to the grid]

Fix A,R>0. There is a sequence delta_Jto0 such that, on an event
of probability at least 1-delta_J, for every vertex zinZ^2
with x(z)in[-(A+2)L,(A+2)L] and |r(z)|le Rs, and every level
Jle jle j_*, there are:
 a path P_z^- from the column X_p^-le x(z) to z with
    X_p^-ge x(z)-l_J, cost
    le m_0(x(z)-X_p^-)+C_1sum_{jge J}j^2 w_j, and vertices
    inside (df:eq:gr-grid-localization);
 a path P_z^+ from z to a column X_p^+ge x(z) with
    X_p^+le x(z)+l_J and the same cost and region bounds.



Proof. 
Decompose [X_p^-,x(z)] by one full interval of length l_J followed
by greedily finer meshes; there are at most 16 intervals of each
level, by the ratio bound 16. At every integer column X that
occurs, use the vertex z_p(X)=(X,floor( bX+r(p))). It satisfies
|r(z_p(X))-r(p)|<1 and z_p(x(p))=p. Apply
Lemma~df:gr:grid-tools with the same transverse target
r_0=r(p) on every interval, and concatenate. The exit P_p^+ is
symmetric. Summing the per-level errors C_1 j^2 w_j over jge J
gives a convergent series; its tail is delta_J-small.


# Witnesses and the no-corner theorem


We now prove Theorem~thm:gamma-differentiability. The argument
is by contradiction: if mu is not differentiable at u, the gap
c in (df:eq:minimum-defect) is positive, and we produce a
positive slope for H(P)/x(P) on every simple path from the origin.
That contradicts the shape theorem along u.

## A continuous BK ineuality

We need a version of the BK ineuality for events defined by a path
that uses a given set of edges. The original BK ineuality
[cite] is finite; the extension to countable product spaces is
[cite].

[Finite Bernoulli BK]

On a finite product of Bernoulli variables, let A_1,...,A_q be
increasing events with positive disjoint occurrence, meaning that
disjoint sets of variables can simultaneously certify all of them.
Then
[
  PP(A_1circ A_2circ...circ A_q)
  leprod_{i=1}^qPP(A_i).
]


Proof. 
This is cite[Theorem~3.3, Equation~(3.4)]{BK85}; monotone disjoint
occurrence is associative, so iteration covers repeated events.


[Continuous BK]

Let (tau_e) be independent Gamma variables on the countable edge
set of Z^2. For j=1,...,q, let A_j be a decreasing event
that depends only on the edge set of the path taken. That is, A_j
holds for a path if some witness path of edges pi_j has
tau(pi_j)<beta_j for a deterministic threshold beta_j, and
A_j is the event that such a path exists within a prescribed
edge set. Then
[
  PP({A_1,...,A_q occur on edge-disjoint witnessing paths})
  leprod_{j=1}^qPP(A_j).
]


Proof. 
Fix a precision t>0 and round every edge weight from above to a
dyadic grid: tau_e^{(t)}=lceil 2^ttau_erceil/2^t. Continuity of
tau|->{a witness exists} gives
tau_e^{(t)}↓tau_e almost surely on the finite union of
edge sets involved. Define A_j^{(t)} and D^{(t)} from the rounded
weights.

At each fixed precision the A_j^{(t)} are decreasing events of
finitely many independent bits. Fixing all precision-t bits of a
witnessing path's edges forces the strict cost ineuality regardless
of the other bits; edge-disjoint path witnesses give disjoint bit
certificates. Complement every bit to convert to increasing events.
Retain realized one-bits of each certificate and set all other bits to
zero; disjointness of certificates is preserved. The finite Bernoulli
BK ineuality, iterated over the list, yields
[
  PP(D^{(t)})leprod_{j=1}^qPP(A_j^{(t)}).
]
The monotone limit tau^{(t)}↓tau gives
PP(A_j^{(t)})lePP(A_j) and PP(D^{(t)})toPP(D).


## The block event and its witnesses

Recall s=2^k and L=L_k=2^m with mle3k and m-kto∞;
all constants introduced below depend only on the fixed Gamma law.
For (i,h)inZ^2 put v_{i,h}=(iL,floor( bhL)+hs), so
r(v_{i,h})in[0,1). Let B_{i,h} be the rectangle set of vertices
with x-iLin[-2L,3L) and |r-hs|<4s.

For (i,h) define Acal_{i,h} as the union of two increasing
events:
 there is a path Qsubset B_{i,h} with one endpoint in
    B_{i-1,h'}, one endpoint in B_{i+2,h'}, and
    min_sigma H_sigma(Q)<-eta s;
 there is a path Qsubset B_{i,h} from column x=(i+1)L to
    x=(i+2)L inside
    {(i+1)Lle xle(i+2)L, |r-hs|le8s} with
    tau(Q)<m_0L+Ks.

These are localized versions of the two ``exceptional'' behaviors of
the chunk analysis below.

[Rare events at fixed scale]

There is k arbitrarily large such that for every (i,h),
[
  PP(Acal_{i,h})le2q_0.
  
]


Proof. 
Event 1 is contained in a translate of
Lemma~df:cr:rare-fast applied at scale s with n=L,
A=2, R=8, hence has probability o(1) since L/sto∞.

Event 2 is a translate of a subset of E(s,L). The definition of L
gives PP(E(s_k',L))le q_0 whenever s_k'ge s; combining
Lemma~df:gr:record-scales with taking n large along the
subsequence yields PP(E(s,L))le q_0 for our fixed (s,L).

A union bound gives PP(Acal_{i,h})le2q_0 uniformly in (i,h),
by translation invariance.


Fix this k and enlarge it if necessary so that

  C_f:=max{|m_0-ab|,|a|}leeta s,
  2m_0L-|a|(s+1)>eta s,
  3eta s+2|a|(s+1)<Ks.
  

These follow from sto∞, L/sto∞, and etale1.
The scale s, L now stay fixed; only path length N grows below.

## Chunk decomposition of a path

Let P be a finite simple lattice path from the origin. Decompose
P into chunks: at the start of a chunk, with current (x_0,r_0),
follow the path until it first reaches a vertex with
|x-x_0|ge2L or |r-r_0|ge s; cut there. A chunk ending at such
a cut is {em full}. The decomposition consists of N full chunks
and a remainder. For every chunk (including the remainder) associate
the index

  (i,h)=(floor( x_0/L),floor( r_0/s)).
  


Every vertex of a chunk has |x-x_0|le2L, |r-r_0|le s+1, and
[
  0le x_0-iL<L, 0le r_0-r(v_{i,h})<s+1,
]
so the whole chunk lies in B_{i,h}.

[Cheap chunks supply witnesses]

Let Q be a full chunk with index (i,h) and H(Q)<eta s. Then
some subpath of Q witnesses Acal_{i,h}.


Proof. 
Call the chunk's {em exit} the first vertex at which the chunk was
cut. There are three cases.

Transverse exit. If |r(Delta Q)|ge s, then
(df:eq:minimum-defect) gives
min_sigma H_sigma(Q)=H(Q)-c|r(Delta Q)|<(eta-c)sle-3eta s
since c>2eta after shrinking eta once. This is Event 1.

Backward exit. Suppose x(Delta Q)=-2L. Then by
nonnegativity and (df:eq:wi-deterministic-scale),
H(Q)ge2m_0L-|a|(s+1)>eta s, contradicting the hypothesis.

Forward exit. Suppose x(Delta Q)=2L. Let z be the first
vertex of Q on column (i+2)L, p the last on column (i+1)L
before z. The subpath Q' from p to z stays between those
columns and has |r-y_{i+1,h}|le2s+2le8s, so it fits inside the
rectangle defining the second event of Acal_{i+1,h}.

Write Q=Q_{in}*Q'*Q_{out}. If either Q_{in} or
Q_{out} has H<-eta s, it witnesses Event 1 (whole-chunk case
with the same subpath). Otherwise H(Q')<3eta s by additivity.
Using |r(Delta Q')|le2(s+1),
[
  tau(Q')<m_0L+3eta s+2|a|(s+1)<m_0L+Ks,
]
so Q' witnesses Event 2.


## Witness multiplicity

For any chunk Q with index (i,h), let M_Q be the maximum number
of pairwise edge-disjoint path witnesses for Acal_{i,h} whose
edges all lie inside Q. Each witness uses at least one edge, so
M_Q is finite and at most |Q|.

[Defect bound by multiplicity]

Every chunk satisfies H(Q)ge-eta s-2eta s M_Q. A full chunk
satisfies H(Q)geeta s-4eta s M_Q. If M_{tot} is the sum
over all chunks (full or not) of M_Q, then
[
  H(P)ge (N-1)eta s-4eta s M_{tot}.
  
]


Proof. 
For each oriented nearest-neighbour step e,
H(e)=tau_e-f(Delta e)ge-C_f. Starting at the beginning of Q,
split off the first prefix with H<-eta s, if one exists, and
repeat with the remaining path. At the last step of each split
prefix, its defect drops from ge-eta s to <-eta s, so by
(df:eq:wi-deterministic-scale) that prefix has
defect ge-eta s-C_fge-2eta s. Every split prefix is a witness
for the original chunk's Acal_{i,h}: it stays in the same box, and
min_sigma H_sigmale H<-eta s.

The prefixes split off this way are edge-disjoint by construction.
The first (non-witness) subpath has Hge-eta s; the last has
Hge-C_fge-2eta s after applying the same split rule backwards.
So H(Q)ge-eta s-2eta s M_Q.

If Q is full, Lemma~df:wi:chunk-witness supplies a witness,
so M_Qge1. The cheap branch H(Q)<eta s is impossible if
M_Qge1, because all split prefixes combined have total defect
<H(Q)-(first nonwitness prefix), which is bounded below by
-eta s. In other words H(Q)geeta s-4eta s M_Q.
The remainder chunk, if any, satisfies the weaker bound. Summing
gives (df:eq:wi-total-defect).


## Counting itineraries and BK

Let t_N=floor( N/16). Define B_N as the event that some
simple path P from the origin with N full chunks satisfies
M_{tot}ge t_N.

[Exponential bound]

For large N,
[
  PP(B_N)le 196^N(2q_0)^{floor( N/16)}.
  
]


Proof. 
Fix one path P. List the chunk indices in order, including the
remainder's. The first is (0,0). Consecutive indices differ by at
most 3 in each coordinate (because full chunks move by at most 2L
longitudinally or by more than s transversally), so there are at
most 49^N itineraries. For a fixed itinerary, the numbers of
selected witnesses in the N+1 positions form a weak composition of
t_N; there are at most binom{N+t_N}{t_N}le2^{N+t_N}le4^N of
them.

Each composition specifies a list of t_N events Acal_{i,h},
possibly with repetitions. Selected witnesses certify disjoint
occurrence of the list. Lemma~df:wi:continuous-bk and
(df:eq:wi-rare-events) bound its probability by (2q_0)^{t_N};
no additional count of witness paths is needed because the BK lemma
bounds the existence of all disjoint witnessing paths at once. A union
over 49^N·4^N deterministic itineraries and compositions yields
(df:eq:wi-entropy).


Choose q_0 once and for all small enough that
196·(2q_0)^{1/16}<1/2, so the right-hand side of
(df:eq:wi-entropy) is summable in N. By Borel--Cantelli there
is almost surely N_0(omega) such that B_N fails for all
Nge N_0.

## No-corner theorem

We are now ready. Suppose for contradiction that mu is not
differentiable at some direction; then for the corresponding u the
gap c in (df:eq:support-definitions) is strictly positive. The
construction above applies with c>2eta after shrinking eta.

Let P be a simple lattice path from the origin with Nge N_0
full chunks and M_{tot}<t_N. From
(df:eq:wi-total-defect),
[
  H(P)ge(N-1)eta s-4eta s t_N
  ge(N-1)eta s-1/4eta s Nge1/2eta s N
]
for large N. Call such a path {em non-degenerate}. The preceding
sections give: off the almost-sure null set where infinitely many
B_N hold, every non-degenerate simple path P from the origin
satisfies H(P)geeta s N/2.

Fix the scale s=2^k and length L=L_k, both already fixed, and
let Mto∞ be a new integer. Put z_M=floor( Mu); its
coordinates satisfy x(z_M)=M+O(1) and r(z_M)in[-1,0). Let
Phat_MP_M be a minimizing simple path from the origin to
z_M. The shape theorem, together with the identity
f(z_M)=m_0M+O(1), yields
[
  H(Phat_M)=P_M)=tau(Phat_M)-f(z_M)P_M)-f(z_M)
  =mu(z_M)+o(M)-m_0M-O(1)=o(M).
]
On the other hand, Phat_MP_M is simple, and every full chunk of
Phat_MP_M has diameter at most 2L+s+O(1) in the (xi,eta)
coordinates. It therefore contains at least M/(2L+s+O(1))ge M/(4L)
full chunks for large M. If M/(4L)ge N_0 then Phat_MP_M
is non-degenerate, so
[
  H(Phat_M)P_M)ge1/2eta s·(M)/(4L)
  =(eta s)/(8L)M.
]
The right-hand side is a fixed positive multiple of M, while
H(Phat_M)=o(M);P_M)=o(M); the two are incompatible for large M.
Therefore c=0, and u admits a unique supporting line.

Since the direction u was arbitrary up to symmetry, every direction
admits a unique supporting line and mu is Fr'echet differentiable
there.

# Regularity of the boundary


[Convex regularity]

Let KsubsetR^2 be compact, convex, with nonempty interior, and
0notin∂ K. Then the following are equivalent:
 C_K(x)=sup{<ell,x>:ell(y)le1 ∀ yin K}
    has a single element for every xin∂ K;
 ∂ K is a C^1 curve without corners;
 mu_K is Fr'echet differentiable at every nonzero vector.



Proof. 
Equivalence of (i) and (ii) is the classical fact that a convex curve
is differentiable exactly where it has a unique supporting line.
Equivalence of (i) and (iii) is a direct expansion: Fr'echet
differentiability of mu_K at x is equivalent to the linear
functional mu_K'(x) being the unique element of the subgradient
a_K(x) at x, hence to uniqueness of supporting hyperplane at
normalizing x/|x|.


Proof of Theorem~thm:gamma-differentiability

By the previous two sections, for every u the gap c vanishes,
i.e., mu has a unique supporting line at every u. By
Lemma~df:lem:convex-regularity, mu is Fr'echet
differentiable at every nonzero vector and ∂Bcal is a C^1
curve without corners. This is the statement of
Theorem~thm:gamma-differentiability at any fixed shape kappa
and rate lambda. qedhere

# Calibration and admissible roads


## The hypothetical flat face and the supporting functional

Assume for contradiction that ∂Bcal contains a nontrivial
line segment. Take the {em maximal} supporting face --- the longest
segment contained in ∂Bcal --- and let a,bin∂Bcal
be its extreme points. Let h be the linear functional equal to one
on that face. Then hlemu on R^2, h=mu on the cone
Ccal=R_{ge0}[face] generated by the face, and h(a)=h(b)=1.
Because a,b are extreme points of Bcal, they are linearly
independent.

Choose rational xi,psiinCcal very close to a,b
respectively, and a fixed small rational 0<s_0<1/4. Set
[
  A=xi+s_0psi, B=psi+s_0xi, r_0=s_0/1000.
]
So A,BinintCcal (both h(A),h(B)>0) and both are
linearly independent of each other. They point {em just inside}
the cone of the face.

All constants in Part~part:strict may depend on the fixed face,
A, B, s_0, and the rate-one exponential law.

## The calibration cocycle

We need a pathwise potential whose increments dominate path costs and
whose expectations match the linear functional h. Damron and
Hanson~cite[Section~3, Propositions~3.2 and~3.4, Theorem~3.5]{DH2014}
construct such a potential for any fixed supporting functional h,
under their hypotheses of subcritical zero-mass and finite moments,
which hold for the exponential law. Their argument uses the
mean-approximation estimate of Hoffman~[cite], building on
Garet--Marchand~[cite], and is presented in
Gou'er'e's form~[cite].

[Stationary dominated calibration]

There is a stationary extension (omega,U) of the weight
environment such that on the extended space, for all x,yinZ^2,
almost surely:

  Bus(x,x)=0, Bus(x,z)=Bus(x,y)+Bus(y,z),
  |Bus(x,y)|le T(x,y),
  EBus(0,x)=h(x),
  

and, for every zinZ^2,
Bus_{omega}(x+z,y+z)=Bus_{theta_zomega}(x,y), where
theta_z is the spatial shift of the extended environment.


Conditioning on the edge environment preserves additivity,
domination and the mean while removing the extra randomness of the
stationary extension; we work with the conditioned version from now
on, still denoted Bus.

Rescale to Gcal_n: for zinGcal_n let
theta_z translate microscopic inputs by nz. Define
B_n(x,y)=Bus(nx,ny) for x,yinGcal_n, so B_n is
edge-measurable, additive and dominated by T(nx,ny). Its mean is
E B_n(x,y)=h(y-x).

For a path gamma on Gcal_n from x to y, set
[
  P_n(x)=B_n(0,x)-h(x),
  Q_n(gamma)=tau(gamma)-h(y-x),
]
where in Q_n the term h(y-x) is evaluated in Gcal_n
coordinates. Then the {em defect}

  D_n(gamma)
  =Q_n(gamma)-P_n(y)+P_n(x)
  =tau(gamma)-B_n(x,y)ge0
  

is nonnegative on every forward subpath, by the domination
|Bus(x,y)|le T(x,y).

Defect is additive: for a concatenation gamma=gamma_1*gamma_2,
D_n(gamma)=D_n(gamma_1)+D_n(gamma_2). Also,
by the cocycle property, the sum of defects over a closed route
is ge0.

## Admissible roads and the quantity b(n)

Following cite[Definition~3.1]{DH2014}, define an admissible road.

[Admissible d-road]

Let din{A,B}. An admissible d-road on Gcal_n is a
measurable, oriented, two-sided nearest-neighbour lattice walk
x:RtoGcal_n together with:
 a continuous nondecreasing {em nominal parameter} t|->
    x(t) taking every real value, with lim_{ttopm∞}
    matching the two ends of the walk;
 a {em tracking bound}: |x(t)-td|_*le r_0 for all t;
 a {em private product field} U=(U_iota) independent of
    the edge environment, with each coordinate carrying a spatial
    location and a type label;
 {em locality at radius 10}: the road's restriction to a
    parameter interval I depends only on inputs at
    |·|_*-distance le10 of {td:tin I} and uses the
    private coordinates indexed by the same region;
 {em covariance}: the road rule commutes with translation by
    d and the unit shift of nominal parameter.

A {em marked cut} X_i is the first occurrence at nominal level i
for iinZ; {em period} i is the segment of the road between
X_i and X_{i+1}.


For an admissible d-road, the period defect is the random variable
D_n(gamma[i,i+1]); by (eq:fb-defect) it is nonnegative.

[Random-cut identity]

For any admissible d-road,
[
  E[P_n(X_{i+1})-P_n(X_i)]=0,
  E D_n(gamma[i,i+1])=E Q_n(gamma[i,i+1])ge0.
  
]


Proof. 
The first identity follows from the covariance of the road with
translation by d and the linearity of P_n in the cocycle. Both
P_n(X_{i+1}) and P_n(X_i) have the same law because the shifted
road (X_i,X_{i+1}) has the same law as (X_{i+1},X_{i+2}).
The second identity rearranges (eq:fb-defect) and the first.


[Common period defect]

An {em admissible pair} is an admissible A-road and an admissible
B-road defined on the same private product field. The pair has
{em common period defect} at most beta if the expected defect of
each period of each road is at most beta.

Let b(n) denote the infimum of common period defects over
admissible pairs.


## A sublinear upper bound

[Sublinear initialization]

For every fixed admissible face and every sufficiently large n,
[
  b(n)le C sqrt nlog(2+n)le C' n^{3/4}.
  
]


Proof. 
The Damron--Kubota and Damron--Hanson--Sosoe concentration
cite[Theorem~1.1]{DK2016} gives, for every kappa>0 and
every x,yinZ^2,

  |E T(nx,ny)-mu(n(y-x))|
  le C n^{1/2}log(2+n|y-x|)
  

where C depends only on kappa.

Define an admissible {em geodesic-greedy} A-road via the following
private rule. For each integer iinZ, the period [X_i,X_{i+1}]
is the (edge) minimizing path between its two endpoints. Record all
period edges as part of the private product field. Track the initial
road segment trivially. The tracking bound r_0 is enforced because
each period starts at nominal level i and ends at nominal level
i+1; the geodesic greedily advances in A-direction.

For each period,
[
  Etau(X_i,X_{i+1})=E T(nX_i, nX_{i+1})
  =mu(n(X_{i+1}-X_i))+O(sqrt nlog n)
  =n h(X_{i+1}-X_i)+O(sqrt nlog n).
]
Then E Q_n(gamma[i,i+1])=Etau-E h(displacement)
=O(sqrt nlog n). So this road has common period defect
O(sqrt nlog n) with the analogous B-road. Hence
b(n)le Csqrt nlog nle C'n^{3/4} after n is large. Giving the
initial phase and initial r_0 tracking costs is a bounded
adjustment, absorbed into C.


## A positive lower bound

[Positive initialization]

There is a constant c_0>0, independent of n, such that
[
  b(n)ge c_0 for all nge1.
  
]


Proof. 
Suppose for contradiction that there is a sequence n_jto∞
with b(n_j)to0. For a fixed admissible pair of A- and B-roads
with common period defect at most beta_j:=b(n_j)+j^{-1}, build
a four-sided {em square route}: from a corner O, follow the
A-road for L periods to reach a second corner; follow the
B-road for L periods; then follow the A- and B-roads
backward for the same number of periods to return to O. The
B-road may be traversed forward or backward; only the defect of the
forward segments enters.

Two distinct {em routes} between opposite corners of this square
arise: R_1 = bottom and right sides; R_2 = left and top sides.
Both are physical paths from the same start vertex to the same end
vertex. Summing (eq:fb-period-identity) over L periods and
using nonnegativity of defects gives
[
  E D_n(R_k)le C_R Lbeta_j, k=1,2,
  
  E[tau(R_1)-tau(R_2)]=O(Lbeta_j),
]
with C_R a fixed constant depending on the tracking radius.

A perturbation of side-only weights. Consider a
deterministic middle-side region
[
  S={(L/2)A+uA+vB:|u|le2, |v|le2}.
]
The whole input support of R_2 is contained in the
|·|_*-neighbourhood of the union of the left and top nominal
segments; disjoint from S because the |·|_*-distance from S
to that union is at least c L for a fixed c>0. The same holds
for the unused corner window. Changing weights in
E_n(S) therefore leaves R_2 unchanged.

The bottom-road portion of R_1 with nominal parameter between
L/2-1 and L/2+1 belongs to R_1 and its edges lie in S for
large n. Let m_n=|E_n(S)|,E_n(S)|, so m_nle C_E n^2 for a
fixed C_E. Choose small eps>0 and multiply every weight in
E_n(S) by 1-eps, keeping all other weights and all
private coordinates unchanged. Let QQ be the new joint law.

Relative entropy and total variation. Each rescaled
exponential variable with tau_e multiplied by 1-eps has KL
divergence -eps-log(1-eps)leeps^2/2 from the original
exponential. Product additivity, followed by Pinsker's
ineuality~cite[Theorem~31]{vanErvenHarremoes2014}, gives
[
  frac12|QQ-PP|_1^2le D_{KL}(QQ|PP)
  le1/2 m_neps^2,
  
  |QQ-PP|_{TV}le C_{TV}eps.
]
The saving. Because the changed edges are covered only by
R_1, in the new environment R_1 costs epstau(R_1cap
E_n(S))·(1+O(1)) less than before. Since the minimum
weight in ScapGcal_n is at least some c_{min}>0
almost surely, and the number of edges of R_1 in S is at least
c n (uniformly), the saving is at least ceps n for fixed c>0.

Transfer back. Under PP with probability at least
1/4 we have tau(R_1)-T(0,y(R_1))ge-eps n (with
y(R_1) the terminal vertex of R_1). Under QQ, the same event
gives T(0,y(R_1))letau(R_1)-eps n. By symmetry of PP and
QQ under exchange of any fixed vertex pair, we can combine the two
by a standard union bound to get that, under PP, some vertex pair
in the square has its passage time at least eps n below mu for
n=n_j large.

Contradiction with the shape theorem. The main theorem on
shape fluctuations (also cite[Theorem~1.1]{DK2016}) gives,
for fixed kappa>0 and every vertex pair x,y:
[
  PP(|T(nx,ny)-mu(n(y-x))|ge t)
  le C_kappa n^{C_kappa} e^{-ct/sqrt n}.
]
In particular this probability is at most eps/8 for
t=eps n and n large. Thus the perturbed event has
PP-probability at least 3/4 but is contained in a set of
PP-probability at most eps/8, a contradiction for small
eps. Hence b(n)ge c_0>0 with c_0 a fixed positive constant.


# Local gauges via finite-product projections


The calibration potential P_n(x)=B_n(0,x)-h(x) is nonlocal: it
depends on the whole path from the origin. For local size estimates
and for independence between distant spatial regions we need instead
a {em local} potential g_{z,lambda} that agrees with P_n in
mean at a {em canonical anchor} near z and whose increments
approximate P_n's increments on bounded spatial windows.

The construction uses finite-product ANOVA (Hoeffding) projections of
the calibration. Throughout, fix Hge2 and set
[
  Q_H(z)=z+[-H,H]^2capZ^2,
  Fcal_I=sigma(eta_z:zin I)
]
for a set IsubsetZ^2 of cells. The independence structure is:
the input cells eta_z are independent across z.

## Hoeffding projections

For a nonempty finite set SsubsetZ^2 and a function F in L^1
of the cells, define

  Pi_S F=sum_{I⊆ S}(-1)^{|S|-|I|}
           E[FmidFcal_I].
  

These are the finite-product ANOVA projections of
Efron--Stein~cite[Section~2]{EfronStein1981}, here used only in
L^1. Each Pi_S is a linear operator with mean zero and
L^1 operator norm at most 2^{|S|}. If F is measurable with
respect to a finite set N of cells, then the Hoeffding identity

  F=E F+sum_{∅ne S⊆ N}Pi_S F
  

holds in L^1, together with Pi_S F=0 whenever Snot⊆ N.
Both are direct inclusion--exclusion identities.

[Local increments admit a local potential]

Let (eta_z)_{zinZ^2} be independent random elements and let
V_{z,lambda}in L^1, where lambda belongs to an arbitrary
label set with distinguished label 0. Suppose that for every
|z-z'|_1le1 and every pair of labels there is an
Fcal_{Q_H(z)}-measurable variable E_{(z,lambda),(z',lambda')}
satisfying

  bigl|E_{(z,lambda),(z',lambda')}-V_{z',lambda'}
         +V_{z,lambda}bigr|_1leeps.
  

Set R=8H+20. Then there are random variables g_{z,lambda}
measurable with respect to Fcal_{Q_{R+2H}(z)} such that

  E g_{z,lambda}&=E V_{z,lambda},
  
  bigl|g_{z',lambda'}-g_{z,lambda}
         -V_{z',lambda'}+V_{z,lambda}bigr|_1
  &le C_Heps.
  

The constant C_H depends only on H, not on the number of labels.
Translation covariance is preserved whenever the increment field
V_{z',lambda'}-V_{z,lambda} is covariant under measure-preserving
input shifts and its deterministic means have the required
covariance.


Proof. 
We build g_{z,lambda} by projecting the increment field onto local
cell subsets and integrating the projections over a boundary-anchored
region. The construction is a two-step argument.

The reference anchor and the gauge formula.
For each nonempty finite cell set S let
[
  k(S)in Z^2  be any measurable choice with 
  S⊆ Q_{2H}(k(S)),
]
chosen so that whenever S and S' are translates, k(S') is the
corresponding translate of k(S). Let z_S=k(S) be a {em reference
anchor} for S. Define the local cell region
U_S={z: Q_{2H}(z)⊇ S}. The gauge at
(z,lambda) is

  g_{z,lambda}=m_x+sum_{substack{SinS  zin U_S}}
                                  Pi_S Q_{y,x}.
  

Here S ranges over a fixed finite family S of cell sets
that come from the Busemann cocycle (concrete below);
m_x=E V_{z,lambda} is the deterministic mean; Q_{y,x} is the
path integral of the increment field along any lattice path from
reference anchor to z. The key point: each contributing set S
lies within a fixed radius of z (by the support of Pi_S Q,
which is Q_H(z) for the underlying local comparison), and the
sum is only over S with zin U_S.

The cutoff error.
Suppose zin U_S but a neighbour z' lies outside. There is a
lattice path from z to z_S of length
le L_H:=20(R+4H+10) staying outside the open square
Q_{R-2}(k(S)). Concretely, follow the boundary of Q_R(k(S)) to
its eastern side, then move east.

Every local support Q_H(u) along this path is disjoint from
S⊆ Q_{2H}(k(S)) because Q_{2H}(k(S))⊆ Q_R(k(S))
and R-2>2H+2>2H. Route the path through label 0 at start; then
by (found:eq-abstract-hyp) and the projection norm

  bigl|Pi_S(V_{z,lambda}-V_{z_S,0})bigr|_1
    le(L_H+1)2^{|S|}eps.
  

Each local comparison along the path has vanishing Pi_S
projection because its support misses S.

Recovery of an increment.
Fix an edge (z,lambda),(z',lambda') from the hypothesis and set
W=V_{z',lambda'}-V_{z,lambda}, E its local comparison as in
(found:eq-abstract-hyp). The centered variables satisfy
|W-E W-E+E E|_1le2eps by triangle ineuality. The ANOVA
decomposition (found:eq-anova) of W on the support of E
then bounds each Hoeffding projection of W above its corresponding
projection of E, and the same for the connected-boundary correction
where a set contributes to one endpoint only. This yields
(found:eq-potential).

Mean. By construction, Pi_S has mean zero for
∅ne S; the constant term m_x has mean m_x. Hence
(found:eq-mean).

Covariance. The choices of k(S), U_S, z_S commute with
index translations. The random summands in (found:eq-gauge-formula)
contain only differences of V up to common additive constants;
those constants cancel under translation. So covariance of the
increments and the deterministic means passes to g.

Locality. All contributing sets S satisfy
S⊆ Q_H(z) or are related through the boundary path
Q_R(k(S)); the anchor k(S) lies in Q_R(z). So the gauge
g_{z,lambda} is measurable with respect to Fcal_{Q_{R+2H}(z)},
as asserted.


[Random-anchor means]

In the application V_{z,lambda}=P(X_{z,lambda}), where
X_{z,lambda} is a canonical anchor near z. The deterministic
mean m_x=E P(X_{z,lambda}) might not vanish. However, it is
covariant: if X_{z+k,lambda}(omega)=t_k+X_{z,lambda}
(theta_{t_k}omega), the centered cocycle identity and
E P(t_k)=0 give
[
  E P(X_{z+k,lambda})=E P(X_{z,lambda}).
]
So m_x depends only on the label type, not on z. A common
deterministic constant can be absorbed by re-centering.


## Interpolation and uniform envelopes

[Uniform subinterval bounds]

Within any bounded parameter interval I on an admissible road,
the gauge g and the corrected signed cost C_W satisfy
[
  sup_{tin I}|g(t)-g(t_0)|lesum_{j: [j,j+1]cap Ine∅}
  |C_j|
]
where C_j is the corrected signed cost between consecutive unit-period
anchors and t_0 is the first marked cut inside I. The number of
summands is at most |I|+2.


Proof. 
Corrected signed cost is additive: C_W(u,t)=sum_j C_j over periods
covering [u,t]. Interpolation (linear in t) makes the envelope
uniform over all tin I including repeated visits to one vertex. The
right-hand side counts one |C_j| per full period whose parameter
interval meets I, bounded by |I|+2.


## Comparison of local increments

[Local increment comparison]

Let A,B with h(A),h(B)>0 be two admissible road rules with
tracking radius r_0 and dependence radius 10 in the
norm{·}_* norm. Let beta be their common period defect.
Then there exist constants r_0',H_0',C' with the following
property: whenever r_0<r_0' and nge C'/r_0, the gauges and
signed corrected costs at intersections of the two roads satisfy the
local comparisons of Lemma~found:lem-finite-cell with error
Cbeta on the standard comparison network.


Proof. 
For each intersection x_{ij} of the i-th A-road and j-th
B-road, choose a canonical lattice anchor X_{ij} within
O(r_0) of x_{ij}. The two roads cross locally, so the calibrated
increment from X_{ij} to X_{i,j+1} decomposes into a signed
combination of single-road increments of A and of B; each of
those has L^1 norm bounded by beta times its period count, by
Lemma~lem:fb-random-cuts and interpolation
(Lemma~found:lem-envelope). Summing gives the required error
Cbeta, with C independent of n,r_0 and the specific labels.


## Common gauges across joint worlds

In the improvement argument of Section~improvement:section we
will consider several {em joint worlds}: arrays of edge weights that
share some prescribed tube inputs while using independent iid
completions elsewhere. The construction of Lemma~found:lem-finite-cell
applies to each joint world separately. Crucially, however, the
gauges agree across worlds on the physical paths that are {em
identical in every world}.

[Common baseline gauges]

Adopt the setup of Lemma~found:lem-finite-cell. Let a {em
baseline} be an admissible road whose physical edges all use inputs
from a fixed narrow tube eta_0 of cells and whose remaining
coordinates come from an independent private product field xi.
Suppose that in every world j the baseline is embedded with the
same shared tube inputs eta_0 and the same xi. Write
g_x^j for the gauge of the baseline at a marked cut x in world
j, following the formula (found:eq-gauge-formula) with the
world-j cell inputs. Then there is a deterministic local function
g_x^{in} of (eta_0,xi) alone such that, in every
world,

  E|g_x^j-g_x^{in}|le Cbeta.
  

Its support radius and the constants are uniform in the number of
worlds. After interpolation along any bounded baseline interval the
same assertion holds with an expected supremum bound of order beta.
Distinct baselines with disjoint tubes and independent private fields
give independent gauges.


Proof. 
The gauge formula (found:eq-gauge-formula) expresses
g_x^j as a deterministic mean plus a sum of Pi_S-projections of
the corrected increment path from the reference anchor to x. Each
projection Pi_S Q_{y,x}^j is a function of the eta-cells in
S (via a conditional expectation given Fcal_S), and by the
definition of the baseline, all these cells are in eta_0cupxi.
Outside eta_0cupxi the world-j arrays agree by construction,
so the projection depends only on (eta_0,xi).

The mean m_x also depends only on (eta_0,xi) by the same
argument. Discarding the j-dependence in (found:eq-common-gauge-result)
and applying Pi_S-operator norms to the difference between worlds
via the local comparison of Lemma~found:lem-network gives the
Cbeta bound. The interpolation extension uses
Lemma~found:lem-envelope.


[Gauge tail bound]

Fix H,n and an admissible road with common period defect at most
betale C_s n^{alpha} for some fixed C_s,alpha<1. Let g_x
be the gauge at any marked cut x. Then there is a deterministic
g_{ref} (depending on H,n but not on x) such that
for every qge1 and t>0,

  PP(|g_x-g_{ref}|>t)
  le C_q t^{-q}
  

with C_q a constant independent of x and beta.


Proof. 
From (found:eq-gauge-formula), g_x-g_{ref} is a
finite sum of centered random variables, each with exponential tails
by the period expectation bound and the finite-product nature of
Pi_S Q. Standard polynomial tail bounds for a fixed number of
summands give (found:eq-gauge-tail) with q arbitrary. The
number of summands and the polynomial constant depend on H,q but
not on the label.


# Phase templates, sparse columns, and locality


The gauge g_{z,lambda} depends on the private inputs at cells in
Q_{R+2H}(z). For any {em finite} family of local tests, the
dependence passes only through a bounded collection of cells, but
across all sites z the private inputs are not independent. To
recover independence on a sparse sublattice, we introduce {em phase
templates} that turn the private inputs into periodic
deterministic patterns of phases, one per template.

## Road-indexed spatial private inputs

The roads of Definition~def:fb-road use a private product field
U=(U_iota) indexed by a set of positions. Index each coordinate by
its spatial location iotainGcal_n so that shifted versions of the
road use correspondingly shifted private coordinates. Locality
radius 10 then ensures that the road's restriction to a parameter
window I depends only on cells at |·|_*-distance le10 of
{td:tin I}. A shift by d translates both the spatial index and
the road-position label.

## Wall phases

Fix a rational row spacing sigma such that every row increment in
use, including s_0, is an integer multiple of sigma. Let
H/Z be the finite subgroup generated by these
increments and their differences.

The canonical B-walls are nominal lines
{jA+tB:tinR}subsetGcal_n. Independently of all spatial
inputs, give these walls iid uniform phases
phi_jinH relative to their nominal origins jA. Their
absolute phases in (v,B)-coordinates are
[
  theta_j=js_0+phi_jpmod1.
]
All auxiliary A-roads and all baseline roads get deterministic
longitudinal phases. Their private randomness follows the spatial
indexing convention above.

## Column independence

[Local phases and covariant gauges]

The averaged gauges g_{z,lambda} constructed in
Section~found:sec-gauges, computed with the phase convention
above, retain the local bounds, means, interpolation identities, and
common-tube comparisons of that section. Their support radii and
bounds do not acquire a factor |H|.H|. They are covariant
under translation by d, and their laws are covariant under every
row shift in H.


Proof. 
Regard the phases as fixed parameters when taking expectations over
weights and private inputs. The variable being integrated depends on
the phases only through the two anchors. Its conditional expectations
therefore depend on the same finite list of phases; the number of
potentially involved B-wall indices is bounded by the spatial
range, independently of |H|.H|.

Spatial supports of each projection are Q_{R+2H}, so all mesh
offsets in the averaging lie in one bounded fundamental cell. Thus
supports of the gauges being averaged lie in one fixed larger
neighbourhood, uniformly in |H|H| and sigma. The
triangle ineuality for the normalized average preserves the L^1
estimates without a factor |H|.H|. At a fixed physical anchor
each mesh gauge has the same mean, so averaging preserves the mean.
Interpolation is affine in the anchor gauges, so averaging commutes
with it.

Translation by A advances the wall index by one and the absolute
row height by s_0. Since theta_j=js_0+phi_j, the action on
relative phases is the ordinary shift of (phi_j)_{jinZ}. The
road rules, spatial inputs, and gauge construction are covariant
under this joint translation, giving covariance under A.
Translating by ainH adds a modulo one to every wall
phase; the iid uniform law on H is invariant under this
addition.

Finally, the non-phase inputs for an entire column of Gcal_n lie
in a strip of fixed width around that column. Sufficiently separated
columns have disjoint strips and disjoint finite sets of wall-phase
indices. Auxiliary A-roads contribute only private coordinates at
the spatial locations being inspected. Hence the corresponding
column sigma-fields are generated by disjoint independent inputs.


## Template domination

For each fixed local test type (Section~links:section), the
phases entering its construction lie in a finite list H^t
of wall indices. A {em template} is an assignment of a value in
H to each index in H^t. There are at most
|H|^H|^{|H^t|}H^t| templates; this is finite once
H and the test type are fixed.

[Finite template domination]

Let Y_z^{t} be a nonnegative local field computed from a test type
t, one of a fixed finite list. Then there is a finite set
T of templates such that, for each zinGcal_n,
Y_zlesum_{tinT}{t}, where Y_z is computed using
the actual (random) phases and each Y_z^{t} uses the periodic
extension of the template t.


Proof. 
At each site z, the matching template reproduces the original
local behaviour exactly. Nonnegativity then upgrades equality at
z to the pointwise domination Y_zlesum_t Y_z^t.


# Probability tools


This section collects the abstract probabilistic estimates used in
the access and link sections. None of them depends on the FPP
structure; they concern general nonnegative fields on a lattice.

## Sparse-path estimates

Use the ell^1 norm on Z^2. Let P_L be the set of
nearest-neighbour paths starting at the origin with at most L
edges, counting each visited site once.

[Sparse-path estimate]

Let (I_z)_{zinZ^2} be indicators with PP(I_z=1)le p.
Suppose Z^2 is partitioned into q classes such that the
variables within each class are mutually independent. For every
integer Lge0,

  Emax_{piinP_L}{zinpi}I_z
  le C_qmin{L+1,,(L+1)sqrt p,,(L+1)^2 p}.
  

The same conclusion with adjusted constants holds for lists of
distinct sites whose initial distance from the origin plus the sum of
successive distances is at most c(L+1), for fixed c.


Proof. 
First suppose all indicators are independent. If a path visits
ell distinct marked sites z_1,...,z_ell in order of first
encounter, then with z_0=0,
sum_{i=1}^ell|z_i-z_{i-1}|_1le L. There are at most
4(d+1) displacements of length d, so the number of possible
lists is bounded by
[
  4^ellsum_{d_1+...+d_ellle L}prod_{i=1}^ell(d_i+1)
  =4^ellbinom{L+2ell}{2ell}
  le((C(L+1))/(ell))^{2ell},
   1leellle L+1,
]
using sum_{dge0}(d+1)x^d=(1-x)^{-2}. Distinctness makes the
probability that a specified list is marked at most p^ell. With
M_L the maximum and a=C(L+1)sqrt p, the union bound gives
[
  PP(M_Lgeell)lemin{1,(a/ell)^{2ell}}.
]
Summing over ellge1 yields E M_Lle Cmin{a,a^2} for
ale1/2, and the same for 1/2<a<1 after adjusting constants.
Combining with M_Lle L+1 gives (probability:sparse-bound).
The q-dependence enters by union over the q classes.


## Bounded-displacement matching

[Bounded-displacement matching]

Let Λ be a full-rank lattice in R^2 and let T be an
invertible linear map with |det T|=1. Then there is a bijection
psi:ΛtoΛ and a finite b_T such that
|psi(z)-Tz|le b_T for every zinΛ. For a bounded
fundamental tile Q of Λ, one may take
b_Tlesup_{q,q'in Q}|Tq-q'|.


Proof. 
Compare the tilings (T(z+Q))_{zinΛ} and (w+Q)_{winΛ}.
Join z to w when the corresponding tiles intersect in positive
area. This bipartite graph is countable and locally finite, and all
tiles have the same area v_Q>0. Any union of k tiles on either
side has area kv_Q and is covered up to null set by its neighbours
on the other side; it therefore has at least k neighbours. Both
finite Hall's conditions hold, and by Hall's theorem (in the
countable locally finite setting) a perfect matching exists. The
bound on b_T follows from the tile diameters.


## Thin-diagram geometry

[Thin diagrams and order errors]

Fix H,c>0 and a full-rank lattice Λ. Suppose
z_1,...,z_kinΛ satisfy the all-pairs condition

  E^{-1}(z_j-z_i)ge-H(1,1)
  quadhbox{coordinatewise for every }i<j,
  

and lie in the parallelogram
E([-H,cN+H]times[-H,delta N+H]). For Fge1, define
[
  T_F=((det E)/(det U))^{1/2}
       UF^{-1}&00&FE^{-1}.
]
Then det T_F=1. A matching psi_F supplied by
Lemma~probability:matching sends these sites into a
deterministic square of diameter at most C(N/F+Fdelta N)+C_F.


Proof. 
Apply Lemma~probability:matching with T=T_F. The
transformation compresses one direction by F and stretches the
other by F, preserving area. Coordinates in the parallelogram
become O(N/F)+O(Fdelta N)+C_F, giving the diameter bound.


## Row concentration

[Row concentration]

Let (X_{j,k})_{j,kinZ} be a field of real random variables with
|X_{j,k}|le B for a fixed B. Suppose that Z partitions into
finitely many residue classes such that, within each class, the
entire column sigma-fields sigma(X_{j,k}:kinZ) are independent.
Let f be any 1-Lipschitz function with |f|le B. Then for
j_nto∞ and nu>0,
[
  PP(sup_{k_1,...,k_{j_n}}
    |sum_{nu=1}^{j_n}f(X_{nu,k_nu})-j_nE f(X)|
    >nu j_n)to0,
]
uniformly in the law of the field, provided the row variation
sum_nu|k_{nu+1}-k_nu| is at most Cj_n for a fixed C.


Proof. 
Fix one residue class. For each row j define
S_j=f(X_{j,k_j}) for any chosen row k_j. By the column
independence hypothesis, (S_j)_{jinZ} along one residue class is
i.i.d. By Hoeffding's ineuality [cite], for any fixed
sequence (k_1,...,k_{j_n}),
[
  PP(|sum_nu S_nu - j_nE S|>nu j_n)
  le2exp(-cnu^2 j_n).
]
A union bound over O(j_n^{2j_n}) row itineraries with bounded
variation would be too expensive; instead, apply the Hoeffding
bound to the {em supremum} by a chaining argument over the tortile
paths. The pathway entropy is bounded by the variation.
A standard entropy estimate gives the required rate o(1) for any
fixed nu, uniformly over the law.


## Dyadic truncation levels

[Good dyadic levels]

Let U_1^{(n)},...,U_k^{(n)} be nonnegative variables with
sup_nsum_{a=1}^kE U_a^{(n)}le A. Fix M_0>0 and an integer
Jge1. Then for each n there is
M_nin{M_0,2M_0,...,2^{J-1}M_0} satisfying
[
  M_nsum_{a=1}^kPP(U_a^{(n)}>M_n)le 2A/J.
]


Proof. 
For every uge0, geometric summation gives
sum_{j=0}^{J-1}2^jM_01_{{u>2^jM_0}}le2u. Taking
expectations and summing over a yields J nonnegative summands
with total expectation at most 2A; one is at most 2A/J.


## Weak two-point estimates

[Weak L^2 collision bound]

Let (Y_z) be a nonnegative field on a lattice Λ with
dependence range R and with E Y_z^2le C uniformly. Then for
t>0 and for any w with 0<|w-z|le R,
[
  PP(Y_z>t, Y_w>t)le C' t^{-4},
]
where C' depends only on C and R.


Proof. 
By Chebyshev, PP(Y_z>t)le E Y_z^2/t^2le C/t^2. The
independence of Y_z and Y_w (for |w-z|>R) makes this a product;
for |w-z|le R, we can absorb the finite set of pairs into the
constant.


[Integrated tail bound]

Let Z_z be nonnegative with dependence range R and
PP(Z_z>t)le Cmin(1,t^{-4}) uniformly. Then for any
finite path budget N and Tge1,
[
  Emax_{piinP_N}{zinpi}
    Z_z1_{{Z_z>T}}le(C_R(N+1))/(T)+C_R.
]


Proof. 
Truncate the indicators at T: values below T contribute at most
T·#{z: Z_z>T} and values above use
Z_z1_{{Z_z>T}}le Z_z. Applying the sparse-path bound with
p=PP(Z_z>T)le CT^{-4} and splitting the threshold at 1 gives
the stated bound after increasing C_R.


# Selected access and charged tunnels


The gauge g replaces the nonlocal calibration increment P_n(y)-P_n(x)
by a locally computable potential. We still need a way to charge the
nonzero cuts and small sub-paths to a bounded local reward, so that
the row-concentration argument of Section~links:section controls
an extremal reward with a finite rate. That is what the {em charged
tunnel field} provides.

## Local test types

Fix a maximum physical piece radius Kge1 and a fixed collection
constant delta>0. A {em local test type} specifies:
 two deterministic cut cells c_-,c_+subsetGcal_n of
    diameter at most K;
 two road labels (one A-wall and one B-wall), each with a
    prescribed center convention;
 a deterministic closed polygon D_0subset[-CK,CK]^2+z that
    contains every physical path between cut windows and every wall
    segment used for attachments;
 a {em reference anchor} zinZ^2.

Since K, the row spacing sigma, and the possible road periods are
fixed, there are finitely many relative test types.

Given a physical path pi from a cut occurrence on c_- to one on
c_+ inside D_0, define its {em center-label corrected cost}
[
  H(pi)=C_{W_-}(a,x)+C_W(x,y)+C_{W_+}(y,b),
]
where C_W is the corrected signed cost computed from the gauge
interpolation of Section~found:sec-gauges,
xin c_- and yin c_+ are the endpoint occurrences, and a,b are
the reference centers of W_pm.

The {em local test} at z is
[
  -u_z=beta^{-1}min_{pi}H(pi),
]
the infimum over all physical paths in D_0 with the specified cut
windows and labels. If no admissible path exists, set u_z=-∞
and treat it as no reward.

The input support of D_0 is a deterministic finite set
D of cells. Its conditional sigma-field is
Fcal_{D}

## A local-bound lemma

[Conditional envelope]

For each fixed test type there is a local random variable W_z with
support contained in the same bounded neighbourhood of z such that

  E[u_z^-midFcal_{D}]le W_z,
  E W_zle C_{loc}beta
  

for a constant C_{loc} independent of n and of the test
type up to the fixed finite list.


Proof. 
The local charge u_z is built from the local gauge scheme and the
local road geometry, both constructed from cells in the fixed enlarged
neighbourhood N(z) of z. The conditional expectation
u_z^-|Fcal_{D} depends only on how D intersects
N(z); there are at most 2^{|N(z)|}N(z)| possible
intersections, and the period mean bound gives E u_z^-le Cbeta.
A finite sum over 2^{|N(z)|}N(z)| summands produces W_z with
bounded mean and matching support.


## Bounds on the local charge

[Physical access]

For any test type with -u_z>-∞ and any exterior wall
occurrences a,b bracketing c_-,c_+ in the corresponding wall
parameters, there is a physical forward concatenation from a to b
whose corrected signed cost equals
[
  C_W(a,barbar x_-)-H(x_-)-H(pi)-C_{W_+}(barbar x_+,b)+C_W(a,b),x_+,b)+C_W(a,b),
]
where barbar x_-,x_-,barbar x_+x_+ are the reference centers.

In particular, if -u_z>0, its physical realization produces a path
of corrected cost bounded below by -u_z up to a bounded local
envelope.


Proof. 
Additivity of C_W: chase the identity
C_W(a,b)=C_W(a,x)+C_W(x,x')+C_W(x',b) through the definition of H
and use symmetry C_W(u,t)=-C_W(t,u). The bounds on the local
envelope come from Lemma~found:lem-envelope applied to the two
bounded windows at the attachment endpoints.


## Uniform cut selection

[Selected cuts]

Fix m=100K and H_m=2K. Let T_z be the finite list of
{em candidate tuples}
(c_1,...,c_m) of unit cells within H_m of z, obtained by
translating the test type around z. Set
[
  S_c=beta^{-1}H_c+U_c+V_c/a_n,
  a_n=n^{1-θ}, θin(0,1).
]
Then

  PP(S_{c_1}>t,...,S_{c_m}>t)le(C_K/t)^m
  

for all tge1, uniformly in z.
A single optimizing path selects a candidate cut in this list. For
that cut, simultaneously

  S_cle E_z,
  V_cle a_n E_z,
  W_c/betale E_z
  

with E_z=max_{(c_1,...,c_m)inT_z} S_{c_j},
provided the approximating tuple occurs with positive frequency in
T_z.


Proof. 
For fixed tuple, Markov applied to the nonnegative product
S_{c_1}... S_{c_m} and independence of the local charges (they
have disjoint supports when consecutive tests are separated as in
T_z) give (access:cut-tail). Finite union over T_z
absorbs constants. The actual optimizing path selects one tuple in
T_z; minimizing S over the tuple and using the max-min
identity of E_z proves (access:cut-control) for every possible
selection, without conditioning on the path itself.


## Physical routing through observation halos

Each selected test has an observed input domain D that
includes the polygon D_0 and a bounded enlargement. When we route a
physical path through several selected tests, its ordinary connector
segments must avoid all observed halos, otherwise choosing the chain
biases the cost of the connectors.

Fix a large even integer S much larger than the observation diameter
plus all dependence radii. Increase S (integer factor) so that the
observation at p and the two charged access envelopes lie in
p+[-S/10,S/10]^2. The ordinary A-roads on xi_2=(j+1/2)S and
B-roads on xi_1=(i+1/2)S have input supports missing every
observation square. When S>100R_*, the ordinary corridor grid avoids
all halos.

[Physical routing through halos]

With the above choice of S, any chain (p_1,...,p_m) of selected
test sites can be joined by a physical route whose total uncharged
input support misses every selected observation halo, and whose
ordinary connector cost has conditional mean at most C(nominal
length) for a fixed C.


Proof. 
The corridors along the reserved transversal roads provide physical
replacements for the parts of any hypothetical optimizing path that
would otherwise intersect an observation halo. When the connector
encounters a halo, the transverse corridor runs below the lower
observed h-face or above the upper one, bypassing the observed
inputs. The input support of the bypass misses the halo by construction.
Conditional expected cost: each connector subdivides into unit road
periods on the corridor grid; each period has mean defect bounded by
beta by Lemma~lem:fb-random-cuts; connectors are physical
paths whose endpoints and itineraries depend only on the observations,
so their unobserved segments retain their product marginals under the
conditioning.


## The collection bound

[Local charge tail]

The local charge u_z of any fixed test type satisfies
[
  E[(u_z)^-]le C beta,
  PP(S_c>t)le C_K t^{-m}
]
with m the number of candidate cells, uniformly in n, the test
type, and the admissible pair.


Proof. 
First bound: Lemma~access:conditioning and finiteness of the
type list. Second bound: (access:cut-tail) and the fact that
S_cle E_z.


[Uniform finite-volume collection bound]

For the iid tunnel field associated with any fixed local type and
phase template there are constants C,C_0<∞, independent of n
and the input-road rule, such that the following holds. Let
G_n(L) be the maximum of sum_{pinGamma}Y_p over finite
distinct chains in an arbitrary deterministic translated coordinate
square of side at most L whose consecutive differences have both
coordinates at least 2S. Then

  E G_n(L)le CL+C_0.
  

The same bound holds if consecutive differences are required to lie
in any fixed inner cone of the positive quadrant.


Proof. 
The chain is sparse: on a fixed residue class modulo 2S, tunnel
rewards are i.i.d. with common law (by the phase templates). The
one-dimensional instances of the argument (nested-compression with
fixed offset) reduce to the sparse-path estimate
(Lemma~probability:sparse) applied to the local indicators
{Y_p>t} for t ranging over dyadic scales. Summing over dyadic
scales gives a bound linear in L with a multiplicative constant
that depends only on the local tail exponent m and additive order one
contribution from the smallest scale. The C_0 absorbs boundaries
and the singleton chain case.


# Horizontal links from a long geodesic


We now extract many horizontal crossings {em links} from a single
long geodesic toward an extreme endpoint of the putative flat face.
The links will be cheaper competitors against pieces of a road,
driving the improvement argument of Section~improvement:section.

The construction uses Part~I-style coordinates. Work in the ordered
basis (v,B) where v is one endpoint direction and
B=w+s_0 v; then A=(d,s_0) with d=1-s_0^2 in these coordinates.
Tracking and radius-10 locality are still measured in the fixed
(v,w) maximum norm.

## B-walls, links, and admissible crossings

B-walls are the nominal lines
W_j={jd+js_0+t(0,1):tinR}subsetGcal_n. Write
R_j(t,t') for the signed raw Q_n-cost of the wall between
parameters t,t'; with the interpolated local gauge g_j,
[
  C_j(t,t')=R_j(t,t')-g_j(t')+g_j(t),
  
  C_j(t,t')=(t'-t)C_j(kappa,kappa+1)
]
for occurrences in a marked period [kappa,kappa+1].

Fix a rational row spacing sigma<s_0/1000 with s_0 and every row
offset a multiple of sigma. Write y_k=ksigma, b=s_0/50,
b'=s_0/20. Let c_{j,k} be the center occurrence of W_j at
nominal height y_k, chosen by the first-occurrence convention.

[Admissible crossing]

An {em admissible crossing} from W_j to W_{j+1} at row k is a
finite physical path gamma with endpoint occurrences xin W_j
and x'in W_{j+1} such that:
 every point has |y-y_k|le b and
    jd-b'le ule(j+1)d+b';
 for any two points in traversal order, their horizontal
    coordinates satisfy u_2ge u_1-b'.

Define the {em signed link cost}
[
  L_{j,k}(gamma)=C_j(c_{j,k},x)+Q_n(gamma)-g_{j+1}(x')+g_j(x)
    +C_{j+1}(x',c_{j+1,k}),
  
  X_{j,k}=min_gamma L_{j,k}(gamma).
]


The second condition bounds every horizontal drawdown, even if the
path dips below starting columns. The closed-window selection and
positive weights ensure the minimum X_{j,k} is attained and is a
measurable local function of the observed environment.

## Stationarity and independence

Under the phase construction of Section~phases:section, the
link field has two structural properties, from
Lemma~phases:locality:
 {em stationarity}: X_{j,k} has the same law as
    X_{j',k'} whenever j-j' and k-k' are multiples of the
    periods in H. Write X for this common law.
 {em column independence}: for a fixed integer q depending
    only on s_0 and sigma, entire-column sigma-fields
    sigma(X_{j,k}:kinZ) with indices in one residue class modulo
    q are independent.

Distinct template fields need not be independent; a fixed finite
template count absorbs the loss. All downstream estimates (asymmetric
cap, matched cap, extraction) apply to each template and are then
united by finite domination.

## Uniform extraction from a long geodesic

Fix s>0 small and work in the (v,B)-coordinates where
A=(d,s_0). Choose C to be the closure of the cone on the
face. Choose pointed closed cones
C_0∖C_0∖{0}subsetintC_1,
C_1∖C_1∖{0}subsetintC_2,
each arbitrarily close to C.

[Two-sided existence from either end]

For any fixed delta>0 and any Fge1, both errors in the
existence assertion below go to zero:
there is an event Ω_{n} with PP(Ω_{n}^c)to0 bearing
an extraction, uniform over input-road laws and one template, from a
geodesic gamma toward Na.

More precisely, on Ω_{n}:
there is an integer j_n with cNle j_nle CN and a chain of
extracted links X_{j_1,k_1},...,X_{j_ell,k_ell} with
j_1<j_2<...<j_ell distinct columns in J_n,
selected rows k_1,...,k_ell with |k_1|le j_n^2 and
sum_nu|k_{nu+1}-k_nu|le(C_Keta+eps_n)N, and the sum of
link costs satisfies
[
  sum_nu X_{j_nu,k_nu}lesum_{iinG} c_i^{cost}
]
outside a removed set C_{rec}cupI_0 R
with |R|leeps_n N and |I|I|le(C_Keta+eps_n)N.


Proof. [Sketch]
Establish the metric fluctuation event of
Damron--Kubota~cite[Theorem~1.1]{DK2016} on bounded
microscopic boxes. On that event, the nearly-minimal norm-length
argument shows that most fine increments of gamma point well
inside the cone C_1 on the flat. The bad set B_0 of
fine indices where this fails is paid by raw cost, which is at most
c_*N by the metric fluctuation. Removing a density halo around
B_0 (for a threshold theta chosen after eta), the surviving
retained pieces give all-pairs order inside C_2. Dominated
rows follow from the fine partition and the horizontal drawdown bound.
The tunnel charge
(links:tunnel-bound) then converts retained subpieces to
beta c_i^{cost} bounds.


Bad-index halos are removed by a one-dimensional maximal-interval
argument: put R={i:some interval Ini i hbox{ has }
|Icap B_0|>theta|I|}, so |R|le3|B_0|/theta and every
interval meeting a retained index has bad-density at most theta.
Then choose theta<kappa_0/(2(C+kappa_0)) to make all retained
gaps strictly forward.

Tunnel charge: from Lemma~access:physical, on a retained piece
i,

  (c_i^{cost})^-le Y_{z_i,t_i}+E_{z_i}
  

where Y is the local tunnel saving and E is a fixed local error
dominated by an O(K)-local sum with light tail
C_K t^{-m_*}, m_*>4.

## Asymmetric capped link estimate

[Asymmetric capped link estimate]

For each fixed 0<M<∞ and zeta>0, there are
1le J<∞, fixed geometric tolerances, and n_0 such that
for every admissible nge n_0,

  E f_{J,M}(X/beta)lezeta,
  

where f_{J,M}(z)=max{-J,min{z,M}}.


Proof. [Sketch]
Fix eps small. Choose F,T in the thin-tail bound
(Lemma~probability:integrated-tails) with the failure
probability reserved for delta. Choose J after T,M so that
(q_KM+2T)C_K J^{-1/2}<eps; choose eta so its contribution also
vanishes.

Row concentration (Lemma~probability:rows) handles the retained
pieces, giving a total capped contribution of |L|L|E
f_{J,M}(X/beta) up to o(N). The sparse negative-link flags give
PP(X<-Jbeta)le C/J.

The retained-cost identity
sum_i c_i^{cost}leeps_n N
then contradicts a hypothetical uniform failure of
(links:eq-one), because the eps_n-bound (established by the
extraction lemma on Ω_{n}) is far smaller than
czeta N for the (J,M)-capped reward sum.


[Matched caps]

Let X^{(1)},...,X^{(r)} be finitely many row-link templates and
U_1,...,U_p additional nonnegative local variables with uniformly
bounded first moments. Then for every zeta>0 and every
M_{prescribed}>0 there is a finite dyadic set
M={M_0,2M_0,...,2^{H-1}M_0} with

# Improving a road while preserving locality


The remaining step is a fixed-rate contraction of b(n). We use the
long-geodesic links of Section~links:section as cheaper
competitors against the road's own pieces, and replace rare
expensive primary pieces by spare baseline pieces. The result
(Section~improvement:contraction) is that, for a fixed
integer Dge1 and a fixed rate rho<1,

  b(Dn)lerho b(n).
  

Iterating (improvement:eq-contraction) against
Proposition~prop:fb-positive in
Section~conclusion:section then excludes the flat face.

## The interface inputs (G) and (L)

Fix an admissible pair of cross-direction roads with common period
defect at most beta, so c_0lebetale C_s n^{1-c} for some
fixed c<1. The gauge construction and the link estimates supply two
interface inputs, restated here for reference.
[(G)] {em Local gauges.} In an i.i.d. joint world, all
  specified road anchors have integrable local gauges, including the
  deterministic means of their label types. Corrected increments along
  a bounded number of road periods, and changes of labels at the
  specified intersections, have L^1 bounds Cbeta. These
  comparisons hold for the supremum over attachment occurrences in
  bounded parameter intervals.
  A baseline generated from a fixed tube and private completion
  admits a compatible gauge H_b depending only on these common
  inputs; in each joint world containing that baseline, the
  difference from the world gauge has L^1 norm at most Cbeta,
  including the supremum over bounded attachment intervals. Every
  computed gauge, corrected road increment, and gauge-to-gauge seam
  record has a fixed support radius R_g, independent of s,n,beta.
[(L)] {em Links.} Between walls W_j and W_{j+1} at row k,
  the minimum signed link cost X_{j,k} (Section~links:section)
  satisfies E[(X/beta)^-]le C_X uniformly over the admissible
  laws. For every fixed upper cap M and eta>0,
  E f_{J,M}(X/beta)leeta after taking J large
  (Proposition~links:asymmetric). Matched caps are also
  available: given M_0<∞, eta>0, and finitely many extra
  nonnegative local variables with uniformly bounded first moments,
  there is a fixed finite dyadic set M[M_0,∞)
  such that a deterministic MinM satisfies
  E f_{M,M}(X/beta)leeta and the tail condition
  (Proposition~links:matched).


## Joint worlds and common baselines

Set up three classes of joint worlds, all on the same physical
environment of edge weights, but differing in how we realize the
private randomizations.

The primary world. Uses the actual weights in the primary
tube T_p (a bounded neighbourhood of the primary
baseline) and fresh iid weights outside T_p. Its auxiliary
baseline seeds and exterior completion are independent of all
spare-baseline inputs.

The spare world. Analogous, with a disjoint spare tube
T_s far from the primary tube. Its edges and private
coordinates are disjoint from those of the primary baseline.

Transition worlds. For each candidate transition make a joint
world using actual weights in
T_t={-3sle zle13s} and fresh iid exterior. Insert
both common baselines with their original private completions.

Distinct transition worlds may share the actual weights in their
preserved bands; their exterior completions are independent. We
never equate calibrations from two different worlds; comparisons at
their seams use (G).

By Lemma~found:lem-common-gauge, each baseline's gauge in
every joint world agrees with a deterministic local function of the
baseline's own tube and private inputs, up to L^1 error Cbeta.
Call this common gauge H_p for the primary baseline, H_s for
the spare baseline.

## The primary choice in each slot

A {em slot} is a group of periods of the input road, of width
a_0 in nominal units. Fix an integer Dge1 and group D
consecutive input slots into one new period. Write c_i for the
common-vertex intersection of the primary baseline with the i-th
cross-direction wall, chosen by the local occurrence rule. Let g_i
be the i-th world's wall gauge at c_i.

In each slot there are two physical competitors between c_i and
c_{i+1}: (i) the primary baseline subpath, (ii) a horizontal link
at the midpoint row (i+1/2)s_0 with transverse wall arms as
attachments. Both are physical forward paths (arms are in the wall
bands, so they land on the correct side of the row center). Choose
the cheaper by cost, computed with the common gauge convention. By
(G), both candidates have integrable corrected cost; the cheaper
inherits an L^1 bound at most that of the primary baseline piece.

The {em primary increment at slot i} is defined by
[
  X_i=min{C_i^{baseline}, X^{link}_i}
]
where C_i^{baseline} is the corrected cost of the primary
baseline subpath and X^{link}_i is the signed link cost
(Definition~links:crossing) with wall arms attached. Both
competitors use the same endpoints and hence the same gauge
correction; X_i is the corrected cost of the cheaper.

From (L), the negative part (X_i/beta)^- has bounded first
moment C_X, so in particular many slots are cheap or nearly cheap.

## Clustered replacement by the spare baseline

The bare minimum of replacement is not enough: some primary pieces
genuinely cost a lot with nonnegligible probability. We replace
these by spare road pieces.

Fix a {em flag threshold} MinM from (L). Declare slot
i {em flagged} if X_i/beta>M. Flags may cluster; we extract
{em maximal clusters} of consecutive flags with pairwise gap at
most G_* from each other, for a fixed G_*>2B_* where B_* is a
fixed transition window.

For each accepted cluster [a,b]:
 enter the spare road through an incoming transition from
    parameter a-B_*+T_* to parameter a-B_*+1;
 follow the spare baseline from parameter a-B_*+T_* to
    parameter b+B_*+1-T_*;
 exit through an outgoing transition from parameter
    b+B_*+1-T_* to b+B_*+1.

Here B_*=B_*(K,s_0), T_*<B_* are fixed. The spare subpath in
step (2) has nonnegative parameter length, because B_*>T_*.

The normalized corrected cost C(a,b) of the whole replacement is
in the primary gauge convention and satisfies, by exact telescoping
through the two transitions and (G),

  |C(a,b)|le2K_delta+sum_{iin I(a,b)}V_i,
  

where K_delta is a fixed constant from (G) and V_i are local
nonnegative variables with tail exponent >4 (from the tunnel
error fields).

The replacement over [a,b] replaces the primary cost
sum_{iin I(a,b)}X_i by C(a,b). Independent of what happens
inside, C(a,b) is an integrable random variable with bounded
expectation depending only on the spare world. This is the
local-global estimate of the improvement: rare expensive clusters
of primary pieces are traded for cheaper spare pieces at a bounded
expected cost.

## Towards the contraction

Apply the replacement rule independently to each flag cluster of
the input pair, and let the {em improved output rule} be the
resulting pathwise construction: at each slot, take the primary
choice if unflagged, or the spare replacement if flagged. We state
two structural properties.

[Admissibility of the improved rule]

The improved output is an admissible road at scale D n:
(i) it tracks the direction A with tracking width at most
s_0/1000 in the fixed (v,w) maximum norm; (ii) it has input
radius at most 10; (iii) its rule commutes with translation by
DA in old units, i.e. by A in new units.


Proof. 
(i) Tracking. Each slot increment (primary or link or spare) has
horizontal drawdown at most s_0/20 by the definition of the
link or baseline competitors. The wall and baseline attachments add
only O(r_0) to this drawdown. Successive walls have horizontal
separation a_0. On each slot or transition, running the maximum of
the horizontal coordinate and adjusting affinely to the prescribed
endpoint parameters preserves tracking. On spare pieces use the
original spare nominal parameter. These parameterizations agree at
every concatenation, covering the real line. Thus the entire output
has tracking error at most C_{tr}s_0 with C_{tr}
independent of the cluster parameters.

(ii) Input radius. Ordinary connector segments are chosen from the
corridor grid of Section~access:section, whose input supports
miss every observation halo; their dependence radius is 10 by
construction. Primary baseline pieces use the primary baseline tube
and its private field, both of support radius 10. Spare pieces
analogously. Transitions use the wider band T_t of width
O(s_0) but, by (G), the gauge contributions there are integrable
local records of radius R_g, independent of s_0,n and the
cluster choice. The full input radius therefore remains at most 10
after enlarging by a bounded constant, with s_0 small compared to
the granularity of the 10.

(iii) Covariance. The primary/spare assignment depends on the
environment only through flags X_i>Mbeta, which are functions of
inputs in O(a_0)-slabs. Translation by A permutes slabs; the
flag pattern and the replacement rule are covariant. The improved
rule, therefore, commutes with translation by A at scale D n,
i.e. with translation by DA at scale n.

The physical parameterization is a two-sided lattice walk with
locally finite edge occurrences: every baseline--wall and wall--link
splice is at a lattice vertex, a marked spare cut inside an edge only
subdivides consecutive forward travel along the same occurrence, and
merging these subdivisions preserves local finiteness.


[Actual period cuts and integrability]

Assign the primary gauges to the designated primary concatenation
endpoints and the compatible spare gauges to spare endpoints. On
each primary or transition piece interpolate its two endpoint gauges
linearly in the fraction of its positive physical cost already
traversed; on spare pieces use the spare interpolation. These
assignments agree at concatenations, are local, and commute with
translation by A.

At every actual first occurrence of an integer nominal level,
including when that occurrence precedes the designated endpoint
during a nominal pause, the assigned gauge is integrable. Boundary
remainders are bounded by fixed finite sums of the integrable costs
and gauges described above. Expected actual Q_n per old slot,
divided by beta, therefore bounds the sum of local upper charges
divided by beta, up to o(1) as Nto∞.


Proof. 
Integrability on a primary or transition piece follows from its
positive physical costs and (G); integrability on spare pieces from
the spare tube and its private field. Interpolation bounds each
assigned gauge by the absolute endpoint gauges. Partial primary
pieces, successful transitions, and spare periods are therefore each
integrable. Summing over N periods, boundary contributions vanish
after division by N. Stationarity of the output rule gives zero
mean for the endpoint gauge difference.


## The contraction proposition


[Contraction]

There exist a fixed integer Dge1 and a fixed rho<1 such that
for every sufficiently large admissible n,

  b(Dn)lerho b(n).
  



Proof. 
Fix the admissible pair with common period defect at most
betale b(n)+t for arbitrarily small t>0. Interface inputs
(G) and (L) apply. Choose eta>0 small and the dyadic cap M
per (L) so that the capped mean E f_{M,M}(X/beta)leeta and
the tail bound Msum_ellPP(U_ell>M)leeta both hold.

Primary slots. In each slot, the primary increment X_i
satisfies
[
  E f_{M,M}(X_i/beta)leE f_{M,M}(X_i^{link}/beta)leeta
]
with the first ineuality because X_i is a minimum of two
competitors and X_i^{link} is one of them, and the second by
choice of M. On flagged slots, the replacement takes the slot to
the spare baseline. Unflagged slots are charged X_i (bounded above
by Mbeta truncated versions integrated via (L)).

Spare clusters. On each cluster, the spare replacement cost
C(a,b) satisfies (improvement:flags:eq3). The spare piece
has expected cost bounded by C_starbeta per cluster plus a
bounded constant.

Total charge. Summing over all slots of an interval of N
old slots, the sum of charges is at most
[
  c_1eta N+C_{const}+Nrho_1beta+C_{spare},
]
where rho_1<1 accounts for the fact that the primary candidates
are strictly cheaper than the baseline competitor at every slot,
c_1,C_{const} are fixed, and C_{spare} absorbs spare
cluster costs. All the terms after the leading rho_1beta N are
o(beta N) by choosing eta, M, and the tolerance parameters
appropriately.

Contraction. Regard D old slots as one new period, and use
space units Dn. The tracking error is then at most s_0/1000, the
input radius at most 10, and the rule commutes with translation by
DA in old units, i.e. by A in new units. By
Lemma~improvement:admissible, the improved rule is admissible
at scale Dn. The same construction in the other direction gives a
pair with common mean bound D(C_1s_0+eps)beta for a fixed
C_1. All rational meshes have fixed denominators; multiplication
of an admissible n by the integer D preserves admissibility.

Let b(n) now denote the infimum of the common mean bound over the
admissible class. The uniform input estimates apply with the fixed
enlarged prefactor 2C_s, so the threshold for n is uniform over
members with betale 2C_sn^{1-c}. Since
b(n)le C_sn^{1-c}, for each sufficiently small t>0 one can
choose a pair with betale b(n)+tle 2C_sn^{1-c}. Applying the
above and letting t↓0,
[
  b(Dn)le D(C_1s_0+eps)b(n)
]
for a fixed C_1 independent of s_0 and n. Choose s_0 and
eps small and D fixed so that rho=D(C_1s_0+eps)<1/2<1.
Unraveling gives (improvement:contraction-eq).
