Let L0 >= 4 be an even integer and write Lambda = (Z/L0Z)^2 for the
periodic square lattice with one bond for each unordered pair of
nearest neighbours.  A spin configuration assigns to each site x a unit
vector sigma_x in S^3 subset R^4, where S^3 is the unit sphere in R^4
and omega_3 is its normalized surface measure.  At inverse temperature
beta > 0 the Gibbs measure on Lambda is

    d mu_beta(sigma)
        = Z_beta^{-1} exp( beta * sum_{<xy>} sigma_x . sigma_y )
              * prod_{x in Lambda} d omega_3(sigma_x),

the sum running over nearest-neighbour bonds <xy> exactly once and
sigma_x . sigma_y the Euclidean inner product in R^4.  The symbol Z_beta
denotes the partition function that makes mu_beta a probability measure.
The periodic infinite-volume limit mu_beta exists and is unique as a
periodic local weak limit; this is the uniqueness statement proved in
the sharp lattice bounds for this model, obtained from the Fortuin-
Kasteleyn-Ginibre (FKG) inequalities of Fortuin, Kasteleyn and Ginibre
(1971) together with the Edwards-Sokal coupling (Edwards and Sokal,
1988).

Write x = (t, x1), so the first coordinate is time, and let Theta be
the reflection t -> -t.  For bounded local functions F and G whose
supports lie in t >= 0, reflection positivity yields the semidefinite
form

    <F, G>_OS = E_{mu_beta}[ (Theta conj(F)) G ],

and the quotient by its null space, completed, is the Osterwalder-
Schrader Hilbert space H_beta (Osterwalder and Schrader, 1973).  The
constant function represents a unit vector Omega_beta, and translation
by one time step induces on H_beta a positive self-adjoint contraction
T_beta with T_beta Omega_beta = Omega_beta.  Define the lattice mass

    m_lat(beta) = - log || T_beta restricted to Omega_beta^perp ||,

the operator norm being on the orthogonal complement of the vacuum.
One unit of mass is therefore one original lattice time step.  The
orthogonal complement contains every sector created by local
observables, including the rotation-invariant sector, so the norm above
is the full transfer gap and not just the gap in a single spin channel.

The main statement is the following exact asymptotic.

    THEOREM.  For the measures mu_beta,

        lim_{beta -> infinity} ( exp(pi beta) / sqrt(beta) )
              * m_lat(beta)  =  32 * exp( pi/4 - 1/2 ).

The constant 32 exp(pi/4 - 1/2) equals 32 e^{-1/2} e^{pi/4}.  It is the
same constant that arises in the continuum exact-mass prediction of
Hasenfratz, Maggiore and Niedermayer (1990) for the O(4) nonlinear
sigma model, whose physical mass gap obeys m / Lambda_MSbar =
sqrt(32/(pi e)); the additional factor e^{pi/4} relative to that
prediction is the lattice-to-continuum conversion of the
renormalization scale, fixed below by matching a rotational response
coefficient.

For a two-dimensional system with continuous spin symmetry, the absence
of spontaneous magnetization and the exponential decay of correlations
are logically separate.  Mermin and Wagner (1966) proved the first for
finite-range isotropic Heisenberg systems at positive temperature.
Polyakov (1975), and independently Brezin and Zinn-Justin (1976),
identified the nonlinear-sigma-model renormalization that produces an
exponentially large correlation length for O(n) spins with n > 2.  The
leading renormalization flow alone does not determine the prefactor of
the exponential: the exact prefactor requires a full spectral or
integrable computation.  The integrable continuum theory supplied that
computation.  Hasenfratz, Maggiore and Niedermayer (1990) obtained
exact ratios of the physical mass gap to the renormalization-group
scale Lambda_MSbar for the O(3) and O(4) sigma models, matching Bethe-
ansatz scattering data to perturbation theory; Hasenfratz and
Niedermayer (1990) extended the computation to O(n) for n >= 3.  The
rigorous large-component expansion of Kupiainen (1980) reaches the same
regime by a different method and does not by itself give the fixed-O(4)
lattice constant.  Rather than recapitulate the integrable continuum calculation, the
constant is obtained directly from the lattice, using an auxiliary
exactly solvable regulator and a rigorous comparison of regulators.

The proof is routed through partition functions, because a partition
function counts every transfer eigenvalue at once and therefore carries
the full gap, while an auxiliary exactly solvable model can be evaluated
in closed form.  Three ingredients are combined.

The first ingredient is an observable-sector comparison, showing that
the spin two-point function already sees the full transfer gap rather
than only the spin channel.  On a finite ferromagnetic graph, the
covariance of two even spin monomials is bounded by a constant, fixed by
the monomial degrees alone, times the square of the largest spin
correlation between the two supports; two odd monomials are bounded by
the first power; and a monomial of odd total degree paired with an even
one has zero covariance.  Positivity of the transfer spectral measures
together with polynomial density then controls every local observable
sector, and a bounded odd detector on finite cylinders gives the
converse comparison needed to pass between cylinder eigenvalues and the
plane gap.  The outcome expresses m_lat(beta) as the decay rate of the
ratio of odd to even traces on growing rectangles.

The second ingredient is an auxiliary Brownian regulator.  Its spins are
loops in U(2) = (U(1) x SU(2)) / {+-1}, its space is a circle of
circumference W, and its time is discrete.  Expanding the time
interaction gives positive transfer operators T_{n_p} indexed by a
particle number n_p >= 0; a minus-center time twist multiplies the
n_p-sector by (-1)^{n_p}.  For l in {0,1} set tau_l = max over
n_p congruent to l mod 2 of ||T_{n_p}||.  A spectral calculation gives

    log tau_0 - log tau_1 ~ 32 sqrt(2) sqrt(b) exp(-pi b)

on an explicitly specified scale of large circles, provided the
maximizing particle numbers have density n_p / W = b^2 + O(b); here b is
the auxiliary coupling.  The calculation constructs the required
finite-circle eigenstates, identifies them with positive ground states
of a contact Hamiltonian, and controls the remaining rotor winding
terms.  A nearby-fugacity estimate then verifies the particle-density
condition.

The third ingredient matches this Brownian regulator to the Wilson model
augmented by a circle field built from a real Gaussian lift and a sum
over its two winding numbers, each winding parity imposing the
corresponding sign twist on the Wilson spins.  The matching has two
outputs.  On growing rectangles it controls absolute differences of
partition functions after a bulk volume factor is removed; although the
Brownian density may be complex, the estimate is obtained by integrating
against the positive augmented Wilson measure, and its error is small
enough to resolve the exponentially small odd trace.  Separately, the
same construction matches finite-order response coefficients in
fixed-volume expansions.  The response to a small rotational boundary
twist, a helicity, pins the finite shift

    beta - b = 1/4 - (1 + log 2)/(2 pi) + o(1).

To extract the mass, periodic time boundary conditions are combined with
a boundary condition that flips the sign of every spin on crossing the
time seam; the half-sum and half-difference of the two partition
functions isolate the two parities.  In the augmented Wilson model the
added circle field contributes a zero-winding Gaussian factor and
winding corrections; the winding corrections are negligible at the
precision of the odd trace, and the Gaussian factor cancels in the
parity ratio while contributing to time doubling only at an order
smaller than the mass term.  Comparing time lengths t and 2t then passes
from the Brownian traces to the norms tau_0 and tau_1.  The partition
comparison transfers the mass, and the response comparison fixes its
normalization; the two together give the theorem.

The first ingredient rests on a finite-graph estimate.  Let G = (V,E)
be a finite graph and assign to q_x in S^3, x in V, the probability law

    d nu_J(q) = Z_J^{-1} exp( sum_{xy in E} J_{xy} q_x . q_y )
                    prod_{x in V} d omega_3(q_x),   J_{xy} >= 0,

with no external field and no pinned boundary; E_J denotes expectation
under nu_J.  A monomial is a product of individual spin components with
coefficient one; its site list has one entry per factor, repetitions
included.

Two classical finite-system inequalities are used.  For a zero-field
ferromagnetic plane rotator with angles theta_x, every pair of integer
vectors k, l indexed by V satisfies

    E cos(k . theta) >= 0,   Cov( cos(k . theta), cos(l . theta) ) >= 0.

These are the plane-rotator inequalities of Ginibre (1970).  They have a
short replica proof: Fourier expansion gives the first from the
nonnegative coefficients, and for the second one takes independent
copies theta, theta' and substitutes theta = phi + psi, theta' = phi -
psi, a surjective Haar-preserving map of product tori, after which the
replicated weight is a Gibbs weight

    exp( 2 sum_{xy in E} J_{xy} cos(phi_x - phi_y) cos(psi_x - psi_y) ),

whose expansion writes twice the covariance as a sum of squares of real
integrals with nonnegative coefficients.  Absolute convergence holds on
a finite graph.  Differentiating a cosine mean in a bond coupling shows
the mean is nondecreasing in that coupling.  The zero-field Ising GKS
inequalities of Kelly and Sherman (1968) are also used: sign-monomial
means and their covariances are nonnegative.

At each vertex decompose

    q_x = ( cos(a_x) cos(t_x),  cos(a_x) sin(t_x),
            sin(a_x) cos(p_x),  sin(a_x) sin(p_x) ),   0 <= a_x <= pi/2.

The sphere prior is the product of the two uniform circle laws and
sin(2 a_x) d a_x; conditional on a, the two circle systems are
independent, with couplings J^L_{xy} = J_{xy} cos(a_x) cos(a_y) and
J^R_{xy} = J_{xy} sin(a_x) sin(a_y).

A law of real variables is associated if any two bounded coordinatewise
increasing functions have nonnegative covariance.  The marginal of a is
associated, and single-component monomials have definite correlation
signs:

    Lemma (association and single-component signs).  The a-marginal is
    associated.  For two even-degree monomials each using one spin
    component, the covariance is nonnegative when the components agree
    and nonpositive when they differ.  The same signs hold for the two
    components of a zero-field ferromagnetic plane rotator.

For the plane rotator write the components as (eps cos g, eps' sin g),
0 <= g <= pi/2; conditional on g the signs are independent
ferromagnetic Ising systems, the same mixed-derivative computation with
the Ising GKS inequalities proves association of g, and the same-plane
monomial means are monotone in g.  For the three-sphere case the density
of a relative to its product one-site prior is proportional to
Z_L(a) Z_R(a), where Z_L, Z_R are the two circle partition functions with
couplings J^L and J^R.  At two distinct vertices,

    d_x d_y log Z = sum_e E(O_e) d_x d_y J_e
                  + sum_{e,f} Cov(O_e, O_f) d_x J_e d_y J_f,

with O_e the bond observables.  All first coupling derivatives of J^L
are nonpositive and all of J^R nonnegative; a nonzero mixed derivative
at distinct vertices occurs only at a bond's endpoints and is
nonnegative.  The bond means and covariances are nonnegative by the
Ginibre inequalities, so the logarithmic density has nonnegative mixed
derivatives, satisfies the FKG lattice condition, and is associated by
the Fortuin-Kasteleyn-Ginibre theorem (1971); continuous arrays follow by
restriction to compact subintervals of (0,pi/2) and passage to the limit.
In the two-plane decomposition a component-one monomial has conditional
mean equal to its cosine amplitudes times a nonnegative combination of
cosine-character means, hence nonnegative and decreasing in a; a
component-four monomial has a nonnegative increasing conditional mean by
a fixed rotation of the right circle.  Conditional independence gives the
nonpositive covariance of unlike components, and the like-component case
combines the conditional Ginibre inequality with the association of a.
This proves the lemma.

    Proposition (finite-graph sector comparison).  For the law nu_J let
    P, Q be spin monomials of positive degrees r, s with site lists X, Y,
    and put G = max over x in X, y in Y of E_J(q_x^1 q_y^1).  Then G >= 0,
    and a constant C_{r,s} depending only on the degrees satisfies

        |Cov_J(P,Q)| <= C_{r,s} * G^2   if r, s are even,
        |Cov_J(P,Q)| <= C_{r,s} * G     if r, s are odd,
        Cov_J(P,Q)   = 0                if r + s is odd.

    If either monomial is constant its covariance is zero.  The estimate
    is independent of the finite graph and its nonnegative couplings.

The proof controls the coefficients in a positive pairing expansion; the
odd case and several even cases follow at once, and the remaining even
case needs control of the covariance of conditional means.

The uniform sphere moment tensor vanishes in odd degree and in degree 2h
equals

    int_{S^3} q^{i_1} ... q^{i_{2h}} d omega_3(q)
        = ( 1 / (4 * 6 * ... * (4 + 2h - 2)) )
              * sum_pi prod_{{b,c} in pi} delta_{i_b = i_c},

the sum running over pairings of labeled factors.  Expanding each bond
exponential in powers of the dot product and applying the tensor identity
at each site, contracted bond lines pair the external factors and every
closed line contributes four, so with positive coefficients d_pi,

    E_J prod_{b=1}^{r+s} q_{x_b}^{i_b}
        = sum_pi d_pi prod_{{b,c} in pi} delta_{i_b = i_c},
        d_pi >= 0,

independently of the component assignment; repeated sites cause no
difficulty because the factors remain labeled, and the component
two-point functions are in particular nonnegative.  Call a pair crossing
if it joins X to Y.

When r, s are odd every pairing crosses.  Fix one crossing pair, assign
component four to its endpoints and component one to all remaining
endpoints.  The resulting moment contains d_pi as a nonnegative summand;
conditionally on a, the remaining left-plane monomial has mean in [0,1]
including amplitudes, so removing it bounds the moment by the chosen
component-four pair mean, giving d_pi <= G.  Separate means vanish in odd
total degree, so summing coefficients proves the odd case.

When r, s are even every crossing pairing has at least two crossing pairs.
Assign component four to one, component two to another, component one to
the remaining endpoints.  In the left plane the joint mean of the
component-two pair and the component-one monomial is at most the product
of their nonnegative means, the latter at most one; the component-two pair
mean including amplitudes is decreasing in a because

    E( sin(t_x) sin(t_y) | a ) = (1/2) E( cos(t_x - t_y) | a ),

which holds also for x = y, while the component-four pair mean is
increasing in a.  Association bounds their product expectation by the
product of the full pair means, each at most G, so d_pi <= G^2 for every
crossing pairing of two even lists.

For two single-component even monomials, compare equal and different
choices of components: the products of their means agree by symmetry,
their joint moments differ by exactly the crossing contributions, and the
two covariances have opposite weak signs, so each absolute covariance is
at most this difference and is bounded by C G^2.  The same reasoning
applies to every pair of even sublists with the original G.  If P or Q
has odd degree in some component, its separate mean is zero and every
compatible joint pairing crosses.

It remains to consider monomials even in every component.  Write
P = P_L P_R by planes, put b_{P,L} = E(P_L | a), b_{P,R} = E(P_R | a);
let P_L^* replace all components by component one and P_R^* replace all by
component three, and denote the conditional means of the starred
monomials by B_{P,L}, B_{P,R}, with analogous notation for Q.  Then
|b_{P,L}|, |b_{P,R}| <= 1 and 0 <= B_{P,L}, B_{P,R} <= 1, with B_{P,L}
decreasing and B_{P,R} increasing in a.  Factoring the common amplitude
from the left plane and Fourier-expanding gives coefficients c_k of P_L
and d_k of P_L^* with |c_k| <= d_k after replacement, because each sine or
cosine supplies two exponentials of modulus 1/2 and the even total number
of sines makes the original coefficients real and symmetric under
k -> -k; collecting equal frequencies preserves the domination.  The
Ginibre inequality then gives

    |Cov(P_L, Q_L | a)| <= Cov(P_L^*, Q_L^* | a),

and likewise in the right plane.  Conditional independence gives

    Cov(P, Q | a) = Cov(P_L, Q_L | a) E(P_R Q_R | a)
                  + b_{P,L} b_{Q,L} Cov(P_R, Q_R | a),

whose averaged absolute value is at most
E Cov(P_L^*, Q_L^* | a) + E Cov(P_R^*, Q_R^* | a); each term is at most
the full single-component covariance, since the omitted covariance of
conditional means is nonnegative by association, and both are bounded by
C G^2.

For the covariance of the conditional means, the Fourier comparison shows
B_{P,L} +- b_{P,L} are nonnegative combinations of cosine-character means
multiplied by their common amplitude, hence decreasing, and
B_{P,R} +- b_{P,R} are increasing.  For a <= a' this gives

    |b_{P,L}(a') - b_{P,L}(a)| <= B_{P,L}(a) - B_{P,L}(a'),

and the corresponding increasing bound on the right.  The factors
b_{P,R}, b_{P,L} in the conditional mean need not be monotone, but their
changes are bounded by those of the increasing B_{P,R} and decreasing
B_{P,L}.  Put Y_P = b_{P,R} b_{P,L}, D_P = B_{P,R} - B_{P,L}; the modulus
bounds give |Y_P(a') - Y_P(a)| <= D_P(a') - D_P(a), so D_P + Y_P and
D_P - Y_P are increasing, and association applied to these and to
D_Q +- Y_Q, together with the two matching-sign and two opposite-sign
inequalities, proves

    |Cov(Y_P, Y_Q)| <= Cov(D_P, D_Q).

Expanding the right side, the two same-plane terms are bounded by the
already proved full single-component covariances, and the two
opposite-plane terms are exactly full single-component covariances by
conditional independence, entering with minus signs; each is bounded in
absolute value by the estimate on even sublists, and empty sublists give
constants and zero covariances.  Global spin inversion makes the joint
moment zero when r + s is odd.  All cases follow.

An observable is even or odd according to its parity under simultaneous
inversion q_x -> -q_x at every site, and a component reflection reverses
one fixed component at every site and leaves the others unchanged; these
definitions apply equally on a row.  Total inversion is the product of
the four commuting component reflections, so being even under total
inversion does not require being even under each of them.  For the Wilson
law write q = sigma and m(beta) = m_lat(beta), and let r_*(beta) be the
supremum of the support of the T_beta-spectral measure of [q_0^1] in the
OS Hilbert space.

    Proposition (the spin field sees the full gap).  For every beta > 0,
    in the periodic plane state,

        m(beta) = - log r_*(beta)
                = - log lim_{n -> infinity}
                      ( E_{mu_beta} q_0^1 q_{(n,0)}^1 )^{1/n}.

    The transfer norm on the even subspace orthogonal to the vacuum is
    at most r_*(beta)^2, so its gap is at least 2 m(beta).  The logarithms
    are read in the extended sense; the imported upper bound below keeps
    them finite throughout the large-beta regime.

The finite-graph estimate passes to the periodic plane limit for fixed
monomials.  Spatial translations are unitary in the OS form and commute
with T_beta, so translated one-spin vectors have the same spectral
support and OS Cauchy-Schwarz gives, for arbitrary spatial sites x, y,

    0 <= E_{mu_beta}( q_{(0,x)}^1 q_{(n,y)}^1 ) <= (1/4) r_*^n,

the factor 1/4 being the squared one-site reflection norm of q^1.  A
reflected polynomial transfer autocorrelation is a finite sum of monomial
covariances at temporal separation at least n; for an odd polynomial the
sector comparison bounds it by C_P r_*^n, and for a centered even
polynomial by C_P r_*^{2n}.  The spectral measures are positive, so their
supports lie in [0, r_*] and [0, r_*^2] respectively.  Polynomials on a
fixed finite spin product are dense in the L^2 space of its marginal law,
and reflection invariance gives ||[F]||_OS^2 <= E|F|^2, so the
approximation also holds in the OS seminorm; centering and parity
symmetrization preserve density, and bounded spectral projections extend
the spectral bounds to the full subspaces.  Since [q_0^1] is centered, the
full nonvacuum norm is exactly r_*, and the n-th root of a moment of a
positive spectral measure tends to the supremum of its support.  This
proves the proposition.

For even integers n, l >= 4 let Z_beta(n,l) be the Wilson partition
function on the rectangular torus with n time sites and l spatial sites,
with the same singly counted bonds and probability spin measure as
before.  For even spatial circumference l >= 4 let X_l = (S^3)^l with
product probability measure, put V_l(s) = sum_{i=1}^{l} s_i . s_{i+1}
periodically, and define the row transfer kernel

    K_{beta,l}(s,s') = exp[ (beta/2) V_l(s)
                             + beta sum_i s_i . s'_i
                             + (beta/2) V_l(s') ].

The tensor-power expansion of exp(beta s . s') makes K_{beta,l} positive
semidefinite and trace class, with a continuous strictly positive kernel
and a simple top eigenvalue lambda_1.  Set U_{beta,l} = K_{beta,l} /
lambda_1, let Omega_{beta,l} be the normalized positive top eigenvector,
and let tau_{beta,l} = lambda_2 / lambda_1.  For beta > 0 the kernel is
not rank one, since its cross ratios vary with the row configuration,
hence 0 < tau_{beta,l} < 1.  Closing time gives Z_beta(n,l) = Tr
K_{beta,l}^{n}; omega_{beta,l} denotes the infinite-time cylinder
expectation of the corresponding row-transfer chain.

    Lemma (an odd detector for the first cylinder excitation).  The
    restriction of U_{beta,l} to the centered even row subspace has norm
    at most tau_{beta,l}^2.  There is a real row function F with |F| <= 1,
    odd under reflection of one spin component, such that

        omega_{beta,l}( F * F circ vartheta_n )
            >= ( tau_{beta,l} / (1 + tau_{beta,l}) )
                   * tau_{beta,l}^{n},   n >= 0,

    where vartheta_n translates by n rows.

The finite-graph estimate passes to the infinite-time cylinder through
the row transfer representation.  Component vectors q_x^i Omega are
centered, so their correlations across n rows are at most tau^n, the
subscript beta,l suppressed.  The even polynomial estimate therefore
places the spectral support of every centered even polynomial vector in
[0, tau^2].  Polynomials are uniformly dense in continuous row functions,
and the continuous positive Omega has a positive minimum, so polynomials
times Omega are dense in row space; this proves the even-sector norm
bound.

Choose a real unit tau-eigenfunction v.  Since tau > tau^2 its eigenspace
is odd under simultaneous inversion.  The four component reflections
commute with one another and with U, so v has definite parity under each,
and at least one is odd; relabel it as component one.  The modulus |v| is
even, and with b_0 = <Omega, |v|>^2, positivity of the kernel and the
even-sector bound give

    tau <= <|v|, U|v|> <= b_0 + (1 - b_0) tau^2,   b_0 >= tau/(1+tau).

Take F = sgn v, equal to zero on its zero set.  Its overlap with v is
<v, F Omega> = <|v|, Omega>, and keeping this contribution in the
positive spectral expansion of its correlation proves the displayed
overlap bound.

The detector may involve the entire row.  A component correlation
recoverable with a cost polynomial in the circumference next shows that
visibility is not lost.

    Lemma (visibility through component correlations).  For fixed
    beta > 0 there is a finite constant C_beta, independent of even
    l >= 4, such that bounded row functions F, G of modulus at most one,
    both odd under reflection of component one, satisfy

        | omega_{beta,l}( F * G circ vartheta_n ) |
            <= C_beta sum_{x in R_0, y in R_n}
                    omega_{beta,l}( q_x^1 q_y^1 ),   n >= 1,

    with R_0, R_n the two rows of l sites.

On a finite torus write q_i = ( eps_i u_i, sqrt(1 - u_i^2) l_i ) with
eps_i in {-1, 1}, u_i in [0,1], l_i in S^2.  The prior is a product:
eps_i is fair, l_i uniform, and u_i has density proportional to
sqrt(1 - u_i^2).  Conditionally on u and l the signs form a
ferromagnetic Ising model with coupling beta u_i u_j.  In its
Edwards-Sokal representation (Edwards and Sokal, 1988) an edge between
equal signs is open with probability 1 - exp(-2 beta u_i u_j), an edge
between different signs is closed, and given the edges the cluster signs
are independent and fair.  If no cluster joins the detector rows, flipping
all clusters touching the first row reverses F while leaving G unchanged,
so the conditional product mean is zero; the absolute detector
correlation is therefore bounded by the row-connection probability, and
then by the sum of pair-connection probabilities.

For a pair x, y the joint spin-edge representation gives

    E(q_x^1 q_y^1) = E[ u_x u_y * 1_{x <-> y} ].

Condition on all edges, the directions l, and the amplitudes except u_i.
After summing the signs, each closed incident edge carries a factor
exp(-J) and each open edge a factor exp(J) - exp(-J), where J = beta u_i
u_j, and the cluster multiplicity depends only on the fixed edges.
Raising u_i in [0, 1/4] to u_i + 1/4 in [1/4, 1/2] increases every open
factor, while closed-edge factors and perpendicular factors
exp(beta sqrt(1 - u_i^2) sqrt(1 - u_j^2) l_i . l_j) change by bounded
ratios depending only on beta and the degree four.  The amplitude prior
is bounded above and below on these intervals, so comparing the integrals
gives P( u_i >= 1/4 | all other data ) >= c_beta > 0 uniformly in the
volume; applying this twice by the tower property gives
E[ u_x u_y | edges, l ] >= c_beta^2 / 16.  The connection event is
edge-measurable, so the pair representation bounds its probability by
16 c_beta^{-2} E(q_x^1 q_y^1), and summing proves the finite-torus
estimate.  At fixed circumference the transfer representation converges
to the cylinder for all bounded Borel row insertions, by separating the
ground projection, using their bounded multiplication norms, and letting
the excited trace tail vanish; the estimate therefore passes to the
cylinder, including the sign detector.

The plane mass is now expressed as the decay rate of a
volume-cancelling combination of squared partition functions.  Trace
estimates are uniform over the admitted cutoff trajectories, whereas the
visibility argument is applied only after one trajectory is fixed and the
circumference sent to infinity.

The following numerical input is imported from the sharp lattice bounds.
Fix a sufficiently large dyadic blocking factor L and auxiliary
parameters, and then a terminal reference coupling H large enough for the
construction and mass bounds.  Put

    H_j = H + (log L / pi) j,    I_H = [0.9 H, 1.1 H].

An admitted nearest-neighbour trajectory of depth N is the exact N-step
block integration of the nearest-neighbour Wilson law, so that at every
remaining depth j = N, ..., 0 its retained density satisfies the
controlled-class bounds and its kinetic coupling lies in [H_j/2, 2H_j];
the kinetic coupling is the coefficient extracted from the quadratic
energy of a slowly varying retained spin field, with initial value the
bare coupling beta and final value denoted h.  Uniqueness of an admitted
bare coupling with given N, h is not assumed.

    Proposition (Wilson trajectory input).  For each N >= 1 and h in I_H
    there is an admitted nearest-neighbour trajectory of depth N ending
    at h, and every sufficiently large bare coupling occurs in at least
    one such trajectory.  There are fixed integers N_0, M_0 > 0, with M_0
    dyadic, such that every admitted trajectory with N >= N_0, terminal
    coupling h in I_H, bare coupling beta, and spacing a = L^{-N} obeys

        4 log Z_beta(M_0/a, M_0/a) - log Z_beta(2M_0/a, 2M_0/a) <= 2^{-10},
        m(beta)/a <= log 8,
        N log L = pi beta - (1/2) log beta + O_{L,H}(1).

    Hence a is comparable to sqrt(beta) exp(-pi beta), and the error bound
    and the two inequalities are uniform over these trajectories.  The
    periodic plane limit is unique and has a positive full OS gap for
    every fixed beta > 0.

Admission and coverage, calibration, the seed inequality, the upper
bound, and the periodic-limit statements are the corresponding
propositions, calibration estimate, concluding seed estimate, upper
bound, and uniqueness/gap theorems of those sharp lattice bounds; the
sphere probability convention, single counting of bonds, even periods,
and site-reflection one-step transfer all coincide.  In particular the
cited gaps concern the full local OS space, including rotation-invariant
observables.

Fix one admitted trajectory with N >= N_0 and h in I_H, and suppress its
parameters.  Physical lengths t, w always have t/a, w/a even integers at
least four.  Write

    Z(t,w) = Z_beta(t/a, w/a),   U(w) = U_{beta, w/a},
    E(w) = -a^{-1} log tau_{beta, w/a},

and define

    p(t,w)   = 2 log Z(t,w) - log Z(2t,w),
    Delta(w) = 4 log Z(w,w) - log Z(2w,2w),
    s(t,w)   = Tr U(w)^{t/a} - 1.

The top-eigenvalue scalar cancels in p, and every area-proportional
free-energy term cancels in Delta.

    Proposition (uniform cylinder and partition rates).  For all admitted
    trajectories with N >= N_0 and terminal coupling h in I_H there are
    constants C_H, w_H < infinity, independent of the trajectory, such
    that every dyadic w >= w_H satisfies

        | E(w) - m(beta)/a | <= C_H w^{-1/4},

        | - (log Delta(w))/w - m(beta)/a | <= C_H w^{-1/4}.

Uniform convergence of a truncated cylinder rate is obtained first from
exact rectangle identities; its limit is then identified with the plane
gap, and the truncation removed.

The transfer eigenvalues give

    p(t,w) = 2 log(1 + s(t,w)) - log(1 + s(2t,w)).

If 1 = u_1 >= u_2 >= ... >= 0 are the eigenvalues of U(w), the
normalized trace weights at time t are eta_i = u_i^{t/a} / (1 + s(t,w)),
so exp(-p(t,w)) = sum_i eta_i^2 <= eta_1 = (1 + s(t,w))^{-1}; in
particular p >= 0.  Rotation interchanges the two periods, and
substitution in the definitions proves

    Delta(w) = 2 p(w,w) + p(w,2w),
    p(t,2w)  = 2 p(t,w) - 2 p(w,t) + p(w,2t),
    0 <= p(t,w) <= 2 s(t,w),
    s(t,w) <= exp(p(t,w)) - 1,

and also

    exp(-t E(w)) <= s(t,w) <= s(t_0,w) exp(-(t - t_0) E(w))  for t >= t_0.

If p(t,w) <= 1/4 then s <= p exp(p) gives

    exp(-t E(w)) <= 2 p(t,w),    p(2t,w) <= 4 p(t,w)^2,

the second by p(2t,w) <= 2 s(2t,w) <= 2 s(t,w)^2 <= 2 p^2 exp(2p).

A dyadic argument drives the seed forward.  If Delta(r) <= 2^{-10}, then
p(r,2r) <= Delta(r) and p(2r,2r) <= 4 Delta(r)^2, and the rectangle
identity gives

    p(r,4r) <= 2 p(r,2r) + p(2r,2r) <= 3 Delta(r),

whose squaring yields p(2r,4r) <= 36 Delta(r)^2 and hence
Delta(2r) <= 44 Delta(r)^2 <= 64 Delta(r)^2.  All squaring steps are
justified by 3 * 2^{-10} < 1/4.  Starting from the seed, on dyadic
w = 2^k M_0 with uniform c, C > 0,

    Delta(w) <= C exp(-cw),   E(w) >= c,   s(w/2,w) <= C exp(-cw)
        for w >= 2 M_0,

because exp(-w E(w)) <= 2 p(w,w) <= Delta(w) and p(w/2,w) <= Delta(w/2)
controls the half-time trace.

    Lemma (long rectangles).  Uniformly over admitted trajectories with
    N >= N_0 and h in I_H, for all sufficiently large dyadic t and dyadic
    w >= 2t,

        p(t,w) <= C (w/t) exp(-c t).

At w = 2t, p(t,2t) <= Delta(t).  For widths z = t, 2t, advancing the
half-circumference estimate to time w using exp(-z E(z)) >= c gives
p(w,t) + p(w,2t) <= C exp(-cw) after decreasing c, so the rectangle
identity gives p(t,2w) <= 2 p(t,w) + C exp(-cw), and iteration over
dyadic widths proves the lemma.

Choose dyadic t comparable to w^{1/2} and t_0 comparable to w^{1/4},
with fixed implicit constants.  At widths W = w, 2w the long-rectangle
lemma makes s(t_0,W) <= 1 uniformly for large w, so

    exp(-t E(W)) <= s(t,W) <= exp(-(t - t_0) E(W)),

    (1/2) s(t,W) <= p(t,W) <= 2 s(t,W),

the lower bound using p >= log(1 + s) >= s/2 when s <= 1.  No uniform
upper bound for E(W) is yet available.  For fixed B > 0 put

    f_B(t,W) = - (1/t) log( p(t,W) + exp(-B t) ).

The sandwich gives f_B(t,W) = min{E(W), B} + O_B(w^{-1/4}), whose upper
and lower exponential rates differ by at most B t_0 / t, even when
E(W) > B.  The swapped terms in the rectangle identity are O(exp(-cw)),
so p(t,2w) = 2 p(t,w) + O(exp(-cw)); the added exp(-B t) keeps both
logarithm arguments bounded, and t comparable to sqrt(w) makes the
additive error negligible.  The factor two alters their logarithms by at
most a constant, so |f_B(t,2w) - f_B(t,w)| <= C_B/t and

    | min{E(2w), B} - min{E(w), B} | <= C_B w^{-1/4}.

This is summable along dyadic doublings, so the truncated rate converges
for each trajectory, with convergence error O_B(w^{-1/4}) uniform over all
trajectories.

The limit is next identified with the plane gap.  For each fixed
trajectory, m(beta)/a >= limsup over dyadic w of E(w).  For a bounded
continuous observable F in a time slab of r lattice steps, its normalized
transfer insertion A_F has norm at most ||F||_inf; for r = 0 it is
multiplication by F, and for r >= 1 its kernel is pointwise dominated in
modulus by ||F||_inf U(w)^r.  For two such insertions, with d the number
of lattice steps from the last row of the first slab to the first row of
the second,

    | Cov_{omega_{beta,w/a}}(F,G) | <= ||F||_inf ||G||_inf exp(-a d E(w)).

The square-torus expectation of F equals Tr(A_F U(w)^{w/a - r}) /
Tr(U(w)^{w/a}); when a r <= w/2, separating the ground projection in
numerator and denominator bounds its difference from the cylinder
expectation by 2 ||F||_inf s(w/2,w).  By the basic tails, cylinders and
square tori therefore have the same local limits, the unique periodic
plane state.  Taking a subsequence realizing the limsup of E(w) and
passing the slab inequality to the plane, applied to reflected local
autocorrelations, positivity of their spectral measures and density in
the OS space prove m(beta)/a >= limsup E(w); an infinite limsup would
give an infinite gap, contradicting m(beta)/a <= log 8.

Take B = 10 in the truncated-rate convergence.  Its limit is at most
log 8 < 10, by the preceding inequality and m(beta)/a <= log 8, and
uniform convergence makes the truncation inactive for all large widths,
uniformly over the trajectories.  Thus

    E_inf = lim_{w -> infinity} E(w),
    | E(w) - E_inf | <= C_H w^{-1/4},
    E_inf <= m(beta)/a <= log 8.

For the reverse inequality fix beta, a and keep t comparable to sqrt(w).
Apply the detector and visibility lemmas at n = t/a.  Since E(w) is
bounded, tau_{beta,w/a} = exp(-a E(w)) and the detector overlap are
bounded below by positive constants for the fixed trajectory, so some
pair on the two rows has correlation

    omega_{beta,w/a}( q_x^1 q_y^1 )
        >= c_beta' (w/a)^{-2} exp(-t E(w)),

with c_beta' independent of w.  Lift the spatial pair along a shortest
circle arc: its spatial span is at most w/2, and its temporal span t is
also at most w/2 for large w.  The cylinder expectation and its plane
counterpart satisfy

    | omega_{beta,w/a}( q_x^1 q_y^1 )
        - E_{mu_beta}( q_{x~}^1 q_{y~}^1 ) | <= C exp(-cw).

Indeed, first compare the cylinder to the w by w torus by the slab
estimate with error at most 2 s(w/2,w); compare the w by w and 2w by w
tori through their common width-w cylinder; then rotate and compare the
2w by w and 2w by 2w tori through the width-2w cylinder, the error being
bounded by a constant times s(w/2,2w) = O(exp(-cw)) by the long-rectangle
lemma; translations fit the product into the required arcs at every
comparison, and repeating the two-direction doublings sums the errors to
C exp(-cw).  For each initial w the lifted observable is fixed throughout
the larger-torus limit, so periodic local convergence proves the pair
comparison.

Because t is comparable to sqrt(w) and E(w) is bounded, this error is
negligible relative to the visible-pair lower bound as w -> infinity at
the fixed trajectory.  The plane component pair is at most
exp(-m(beta) t/a) by the plane-gap proposition, regardless of spatial
separation, so taking logarithms gives

    m(beta)/a <= E(w) + (2 log(w/a) - log(c_beta'/2)) / t.

The extra term tends to zero, hence m(beta)/a <= E_inf.  Together with
E_inf <= m(beta)/a this gives equality and |E(w) - m(beta)/a| <=
C_H w^{-1/4}.  The fixed-trajectory argument removed the nonuniform
visibility constants, and the convergence rate was already uniform.

For the partition rate apply the trace advance at time w, with t_0
comparable to w^{1/4} and widths W = w, 2w.  The long-rectangle bound
gives s(t_0,W) <= 1, hence exp(-w E(W)) <= s(w,W) <= exp(-(w - t_0)
E(W)).  Since E(W) is uniformly bounded and p(w,W) lies between s(w,W)/2
and 2 s(w,W),

    - w^{-1} log p(w,W) = E(W) + O_H(w^{-3/4})
                        = m(beta)/a + O_H(w^{-1/4}),

and the positive sum Delta(w) = 2 p(w,w) + p(w,2w) has the same
logarithmic rate within this error, proving |- (log Delta(w))/w -
m(beta)/a| <= C_H w^{-1/4}.

The partition comparison introduces a model with continuous space and
discrete time.  Its transfer operators are constructed first, and the
state on which their norms can be computed is identified.  The
circumference stays finite throughout, which is essential because the
comparison with the lattice model needs a quantitative statement on a
specified growing family of circles.

Let space be the circle R/W Z with W > 0, and let one unit of discrete
time be a vertical lattice step.  The spin manifold is

    G = ( (R/2pi Z) x SU(2) ) / ( (v,Q) ~ (v + pi, -Q) ),
    G = exp(i v) Q,

with SU(2) identified with the unit sphere S^3.  On a row use the
unnormalized Brownian loop measure with Hamiltonian

    E = (1/2) ( - d_v^2 - Delta_{S^3} ),

Haar probability being the reference measure, so the mass of the row
measure is Tr exp(-W E); integration against this measure is written E_W.
For b > 0 set lambda = b^2 / 2 and assign successive rows the oriented
interaction

    exp( lambda * int_0^W Tr( G_t(x) G_{t+1}(x)^* ) dx ).

Reversing orientation complex-conjugates the interaction and reproduces
the same construction after relabelling successive rows.

Expanding each vertical exponential gives a particle expansion.  A
constant circle rotation on one row multiplies its incoming and outgoing
terms by opposite circle characters, so integration over that rotation
forces the same particle number on every time-link.  A colour A_i =
(a_i^L, a_i^R) in {1,2}^2 records the two matrix indices of the i-th
insertion.  The particle Hilbert space is the bosonic subspace of

    L^2( (R/W Z)^{n_p} ; (C^2 (x) C^2)^{(x) n_p} ),

equivalently positions integrated over the ordered chamber
0 < X_1 < ... < X_{n_p} < W with the colour identifications induced by
simultaneous permutation of particles; the chamber integral absorbs the
factorial in the exponential series.  For position and colour lists
(X,A) and (Y,B) define

    T_{n_p}(Y,B;X,A)
        = lambda^{n_p} E_W[ prod_{i=1}^{n_p} G_{B_i}(Y_i)
              conj( G_{A_i}(X_i) ) ],

and for time period n the periodic partition function is

    Z^B_+(n,W) = sum_{n_p >= 0} Tr T_{n_p}^{n}.

A minus-centre seam in time multiplies each term by (-1)^{n_p}.  The
expansion converges absolutely at every finite period, and every T_{n_p}
is positive because its kernel is the Gram kernel of the insertion
functions in the row loop measure.  Moreover

    sum_{n_p >= 0} Tr T_{n_p} = exp(2 lambda W) Tr exp(-W E) < infinity,

since sum_{a,b} |G_{ab}|^2 = 2.  Hence for each parity l in {0,1} the
maximum tau_l = max over n_p congruent to l mod 2 of ||T_{n_p}|| is
attained.

    Proposition (the parity norm difference).  Let b -> infinity and
    suppose that for a fixed D > 0

        W ~ exp(pi b) b^{-1/2} (log b)^D,

    and that for some fixed C_* > 0 every particle number attaining either
    parity maximum satisfies | n_p / W - b^2 | <= C_* b.  Then

        log tau_0 - log tau_1 ~ 32 sqrt(2) b^{1/2} exp(-pi b).

The scalar giving this difference is determined by a set of integral
equations, and the finite-circle spectral calculation completes the
identification.  The particle-number hypothesis is verified when the
Brownian and Wilson partition functions are compared.

The two copies of SU(2) act on the left and right binary indices.  A
fixed-weight sector fixes the number of occurrences of the second basis
vector in each index; the balanced sector has floor(n_p / 2) such
occurrences in each index.  Every irreducible representation carried by
n_p binary indices contains this weight, so rotation invariance puts the
norm of T_{n_p} in the balanced sector.

For c > 0 let P^L_{ij} interchange the left indices of particles i and j,
and on the symmetric Sobolev form domain define

    H_c = - sum_{i=1}^{n_p} d_i^2
          + 2 c sum_{i<j} delta(X_i - X_j) P^L_{ij},

the delta interactions being defined by traces on the contact
hyperplanes.  Their infinitesimal form bound with respect to the kinetic
energy gives a closed bounded-below form, and on a fixed circle H_c has
compact resolvent.

    Lemma (the state attaining the transfer norm).  For fixed W > 0 and
    n_p >= 0 the ground state of H_c in every nonempty fixed-weight sector
    is simple for each c > 0, and in a fixed colour sign convention it is
    strictly positive.  The operator H_2 commutes with T_{n_p}, and the
    norm of T_{n_p} is its eigenvalue on the balanced ground state of
    H_2.  The commutation statement persists with the corresponding
    diagonal boundary twists for the particle sections and the Brownian
    heat loop.

The proof uses a cone of componentwise nonnegative wavefunctions.  Write
the rows of Q as Q = ( c0 exp(i phi), s0 exp(i theta) ; -s0 exp(-i theta),
c0 exp(-i phi) ) with c0, s0 >= 0 and c0^2 + s0^2 = 1.  Changing the sign
of the colour (2,1) makes every matrix-entry insertion a nonnegative
radial amplitude times a single angular monomial in (v, phi, theta).  In a
fixed-weight transfer kernel the total angular charge vanishes, and
separation of variables expresses the intervening heat operators, at each
angular frequency, as radial heat operators with nonnegative killing
potential, whose kernels are strictly positive in the radial interior.
Imposing Dirichlet boundaries on compact subintervals of 0 < c0 < 1 and
letting them increase, with the two codimension-two coordinate
degeneracies polar, gives the same separated heat kernel at every angular
frequency.  This proves almost-everywhere strict positivity of the
fixed-weight kernel of T_{n_p} in the stated sign convention.

The same convention makes the contact Hamiltonian cooperative.  If either
binary index of a contacting pair agrees, the interaction is diagonal on
the symmetric contact trace; if both indices differ, the left exchange
changes the number of colours (2,1) by one and its off-diagonal entry is
negative after the sign change.  Regard a wavefunction as a list of
species components, each symmetric among particles of the same colour;
the diagonal operator in each component is a scalar Laplacian with scalar
contact terms, whose heat kernel is positivity improving on the full
periodic position torus.  Mollifying each scalar delta in its normal
difference coordinate, the expected time-integral of the mollified
interactions over bridge times in [1,2] is bounded uniformly in the bridge
endpoints and the mollifier scale, by splitting the time interval and
using the free heat-kernel bounds with the normal-coordinate bound
C(1 + t^{-1/2}) near either endpoint; Feynman-Kac and Jensen give a
strictly positive lower bound for the mollified scalar heat kernel.  The
hyperplane trace estimate passes this domination to the contact operator,
since translating a trace through normal distance eps changes its L^2 norm
by at most C sqrt(eps) ||u||_{H^1} and the associated resolvent forms are
uniformly coercive at a sufficiently negative spectral parameter.
Integrating the heat-kernel bound gives a positive lower bound for the
scalar resolvent applied to any nonzero nonnegative component, including
its contact trace, and a nonnegative source supported only on contact
hyperplanes has nonnegative resolvent by the form maximum principle.

The absolute-value form inequality permits a ground vector to be chosen
nonnegative.  In its component equations the off-diagonal contacts give
nonnegative sources, and moving a sufficiently large positive multiple of
the component to the right produces a nonzero interior source whenever
that component is nonzero, which the scalar resolvent argument makes
positive, with positive contact trace.  Positivity reaches every species
component: writing the four colour counts as n_{11}, n_{12}, n_{21},
n_{22}, the row sums and column sums are fixed by the two weights, so
n_{11} determines all four counts and ranges over an interval of
integers, while the exchange (11),(22) <-> (12),(21) changes n_{11} by one
and connects successive admissible counts; permutations and the positive
multiplicities from identical-species symmetry do not affect this
argument.  If a real ground vector changed sign, the absolute-value
inequality and the variational principle would make its positive and
negative parts nonnegative ground vectors with disjoint supports,
contradicting strict positivity.  This proves ground-state simplicity.

The positive state is connected to the Brownian transfer as follows.
Rotor representations are indexed by charge q in Z and spin j in
(1/2) Z_{>=0} with 2j congruent to q mod 2.  With d = 2j + 1, each
representation contributes its d^2 matrix elements and has energy

    E_{qj} = ( q^2 + d^2 - 1 ) / 2.

Multiplication by a matrix entry of G raises q by one and changes d by
one in either allowed direction, so in the variables x = (q + d)/2,
y = (q - d)/2 it raises exactly one variable by one.  With P(z) =
(4 z^3 - z)/3 and J = P(x) + P(y), the finite-difference identity
P(z + 1) - P(z) = (2z + 1)^2 gives

    [E, [E, G_{ab}]] = [J, G_{ab}],
    [E, [E, conj G_{ab}]] = - [J, conj G_{ab}].

In any ordering of insertion positions, derivatives of the rotor trace
insert these commutators, and cyclicity makes the sum of the J
commutators zero, so the sums of second derivatives in output and input
positions agree away from coincidences.  At a coincidence of two output
insertions the derivative jump in either coordinate is

    [[E, G_{ab}], G_{cd}] = 2 G_{cb} G_{ad},

which follows from the Pauli identity, including the scalar generator;
equivalently, for E = -Delta/2 the double commutator is the negative
scalar product of the two gradients.  The two kinetic derivatives hence
give the contact term cancelled by 2c P^L delta at c = 2.  The conjugate
computation handles two inputs, and at a mixed input-output coincidence
the two delta terms cancel.  This is the distributional intertwining
identity for H_2 and the kernel of T_{n_p}.

All differentiations are justified before distributions are taken.  In
each ordering some heat interval has length at least W/(2 n_p), and
multiplication by an entry shifts each angular label only a bounded
amount, so the rotor series converges with its one-sided derivatives and
first derivatives are uniformly bounded on each closed ordering chamber.
Continuity across its faces makes the kernel globally Sobolev H^1 in
either set of positions, intersections of several faces adding no further
boundary terms.  The derivative bounds and the finite volume make T_{n_p}
a bounded map L^2 -> H^1.  With h_2 the form of H_2, integration by parts
in the distributional identity gives h_2(T_{n_p} u, v) = h_2(u, T_{n_p} v)
first for smooth symmetric functions and then, by the trace bound and
form-domain density, for all u, v in H^1.  For u in D(H_2) the right side
equals <T_{n_p} H_2 u, v>, by self-adjointness of the transfer, so
T_{n_p} u lies in D(H_2) and H_2 T_{n_p} u = T_{n_p} H_2 u, which also
gives resolvent commutation.

At zero twist commutation maps the simple balanced ground space of H_2 to
itself, so its positive ground vector is an eigenvector of T_{n_p}, and
strict positivity of the latter kernel identifies that eigenvalue with
its spectral radius in the sector, hence with its norm.  The preceding
weight argument gives the full norm.  Commutation extends to the matching
boundary twists used later.  For real zeta, chi put g = diag(exp(i chi),
exp(-i chi)) and eps_2 = (0, 1; -1, 0).  After the fixed exterior colour
change Q~ = eps_2 Q eps_2^T, use the rotor pullback (R_{zeta,chi} F)(v,Q)
= F(v + zeta, g^{-1} Q g^{-1}).  Since eps_2 g^{-1} = g eps_2, it
transforms Q~ into exp(i zeta) g Q~ g, so moving an output insertion
through the seam gives the particle colour holonomy A = exp(i zeta)
g (x) g, and an input insertion has the conjugate covariance.  The
transfer maps sections with psi(..., X_i + W, ...) = A_i psi(...) to
sections with the same boundary condition.  On rotor harmonics of charge
q and spin j the pullback acts by exp(i zeta q) and g_j^{-1} on both
matrix indices; it commutes with E and J, so cyclicity and the contact
calculation remain valid.  For real twists the loop measure is positive
and the boundary holonomy unitary, and the same Gram and weak-form
arguments apply on the twisted Sobolev domain.  Multiplying by
prod_i exp( (i X_i / W)( zeta I + chi (sigma_3^L + sigma_3^R)_i ) ) with
sigma_3 = diag(1, -1) identifies this domain with the periodic one, and
in that fixed gauge the operator identities extend holomorphically to
small complex twists, self-adjointness and positivity being used only for
real twists.

The scalar quantities needed to construct and evaluate the ground state of
H_2 are determined by a pair of convolution equations.  On the finite
circle the n_p = 2M + l particles organize into M pairs with momenta close
to u_j - i and u_j + i for real u_j, together with one unpaired particle of
momentum close to zero when l = 1.  The density of pair centres per unit
circumference is described by a function rho_B on an interval (-B,B), so
M/W is approximated by the integral of rho_B over (-B,B).  The same
calculation writes the logarithm of the transfer norm as a sum of one
contribution per pair and one for the unpaired particle, with controlled
error; the equations below reorganize that sum into a common bulk term plus
a positive cost m_* for odd particle number.  Their endpoint condition
selects the interval on which the maximization over particle numbers is
attained.  The leading exponential of m_* alone does not fix the exact
mass: a correction of order one in B changes its multiplicative constant,
so the interior density must be matched to its endpoint profile keeping
that correction.

All momenta here use the normalization c = 2 of the contact Hamiltonian.
Define

    K_a(x) = a / ( pi (a^2 + x^2) ),   K = K_4,
    S(x)   = (1/4) sech(pi x / 2),

convolution being on R, and extend a density defined only on (-B,B) by
zero.  Consider

    rho_B = 1/pi + K * rho_B                             (|x| < B),

    epsilon = p + K * epsilon_in,
    p(x) = 4 log b - log( (x^2 + 1)(x^2 + 9) ),   epsilon(B) = 0.

The second line defines epsilon on the whole line with epsilon_in =
epsilon on (-B,B) and zero outside, and the boundary condition selects
B = B_*(b).  The function p is the leading logarithmic transfer
contribution of the momenta x - i and x + i, since

    b^4 / ( ((x-i)^2 + 4)((x+i)^2 + 4) )
        = b^4 / ((x^2 + 1)(x^2 + 9)) = exp(p(x)).

The function epsilon is the dressed energy.  Its equation is chosen to
cancel the interaction kernel when a sum of pair weights is replaced by a
density integral: symmetry of K and the two equations give

    int_{-B}^{B} p(x) rho_B(x) dx
        = int_{-B}^{B} epsilon(x)( rho_B - K * rho_B )(x) dx
        = (1/pi) int_{-B}^{B} epsilon(x) dx.

The dressed energy is positive exactly on the selected interval, which is
what lets the finite-circle maximization fill that interval and bound all
other choices by the same bulk term.

    Lemma (parametrization by the endpoint).  For every B > 0 the first
    equation has a unique positive solution.  There is a unique value
    b = b(B) > sqrt(3) for which the second equation has an even solution
    with epsilon(B) = 0, and this solution is positive inside (-B,B) and
    negative outside.  The function b(B) is continuous and strictly
    increasing.  If eta_B = - epsilon' on (-B,B), then

        (1 - K*) eta_B = 2x/(x^2 + 1) + 2x/(x^2 + 9)   (|x| < B),

        4 log b = log 9 + int_0^B a(x) eta_B(x) dx,
        a(x) = (2/pi) arctan(4/x).

    The inverse of 1 - K* on (-B,B) has supremum norm O(1 + B).

The kernel is positive and its mass on (-B,B) is bounded above by
1 - c/(1 + B) uniformly in its centre in the interval, so a Neumann series
gives the inverse, the norm bound, and positivity of rho_B.  On odd
functions restriction to (0,B) replaces the kernel by K(x - y) - K(x + y),
strictly positive for x, y > 0, and iteration gives a unique odd solution
eta_B, positive for x > 0; increasing B increases this solution on the
old positive half-interval.  Define the even function
epsilon(x) = int_{|x|}^B eta_B(y) dy inside the interval, so its boundary
values vanish; differentiating K * epsilon_in introduces no endpoint terms,
so the equation for eta_B shows (1 - K*)epsilon has the required
logarithmic form up to an additive constant.  At zero, changing the order
of integration gives

    epsilon(0) - (K * epsilon_in)(0)
        = int_0^B ( 1 - 2 int_0^x K(y) dy ) eta_B(x) dx
        = int_0^B a(x) eta_B(x) dx,

which is the displayed logarithmic formula; it proves strict monotonicity
and continuity of b(B), with b(0) = sqrt(3) by continuation.  Extending
epsilon by the second equation, to the right of B,

    - epsilon'(x) = 2x/(x^2 + 1) + 2x/(x^2 + 9) + (K * eta_B)(x) > 0,

positivity following by pairing the odd integrand at y and -y, so the
extension is negative outside.  This proves the lemma.

With Fourier transform u^(k) = int exp(-i k x) u(x) dx, one has
K_a^(k) = exp(-a|k|) and S^(k) = (2 cosh k)^{-1}, hence

    S * (delta_0 - K) = K_1 - K_3,
    f(x) := log( b^2 / (x^2 + 4) ) = (S * p)(x),

the second identity by differentiating first and then fixing the constant
from growth at infinity.  Define epsilon_out = epsilon - epsilon_in and

    m_* = - { f(0) - ((K_1 - K_3) * epsilon_in)(0) }
        = - (S * epsilon_out)(0) > 0.

Here f(0) is the leading logarithmic transfer contribution of an unpaired
particle at zero momentum.  That particle also adds the source
- W^{-1}(K_1 - K_3) to the pair counting equation, and on a fixed interval
the density approximation gives a logarithmic correction equal to W times
the pairing of this density change with p; transferring the symmetric
resolvent to p gives the correction -((K_1 - K_3) * epsilon_in)(0), so the
expression in braces is the net odd contribution.  The second expression
follows by convolving the epsilon equation with S and using the kernel
identity; it both proves positivity, by the exterior sign of epsilon, and
isolates the part to be evaluated, the exponentially weighted tail beyond
the selected endpoint.

    Proposition (endpoint, density, and mass asymptotics).  As
    B -> infinity, with b = b(B) and m_* as above,

        b(B) = B/2 + ( log(4 pi B) + 1 ) / pi + o(1),

        m_* ~ ( 8 / (pi e sqrt(B)) ) exp(- pi B / 2),

        2 int_{-B}^{B} rho_B(x) dx = B^2/4 + (B/pi) log B + O(B).

    There are positive constants c_1, C_1 such that, for all sufficiently
    large B, rho_B >= c_1 sqrt(B) within distance one of either endpoint
    and rho_B <= C_1 B throughout the interval, and for every B > 0 the
    solution satisfies sup over y of |epsilon'(y)| <= pi/2.

The proposition is proved in three stages: an outer solution on the scaled
interval, a half-line solution resolving the endpoints, and the final
integrations; the uniform estimate matching the first two stages is what
preserves the multiplicative constant in m_*.

    Lemma (half-line solution).  There are functions U, V from [0,
    infinity) to R, continuous from the right at zero and continuous on
    (0, infinity), with the following properties.  Extend U by zero on
    (-infinity, 0).  Then

        U(X) = (K_1 * U)(X)  (X > 0),   V(Y) = (K_1 * U)(-Y)  (Y >= 0),

    and U(X) > 0, V(Y) > 0 for X, Y >= 0.  For Re s > 0,

        C(s) = sqrt(s) exp{ (s/(2 pi)) ( log(s/(2 pi)) - 1 ) }
               / Gamma( 1 + s/(2 pi) ),

        int_0^inf exp(-sX) U(X) dX = sqrt(pi) / (2 s C(s)),

        int_0^inf exp(-sY) V(Y) dY = sqrt(pi) / (2 s) C(s),

    the logarithm and square root being principal branches.  Moreover

        U(X) = sqrt(X) + ( log X + C' ) / ( 4 pi sqrt X )
               + O( X^{-3/2} (1 + log^2 X) ),

        C' = log(8 pi) + 1,   X -> infinity,

    while V(Y) = O((1 + Y)^{-1/2}) and U(0) = V(0) = sqrt(pi)/2.

Both transforms are analytic in Re s > 0, and Stirling's formula gives,
uniformly at infinity in the closed right half-plane,

    C(s) = 1 + O(s^{-1}),
    sqrt(pi)/(2 s C(s)) = sqrt(pi)/(2s) + O(s^{-2}),
    sqrt(pi) C(s)/(2 s) = sqrt(pi)/(2s) + O(s^{-2}),

so the Bromwich inverses define locally bounded functions supported on
[0, infinity).  Subtracting sqrt(pi)/(2(s+1)) from either transform leaves
an absolutely integrable remainder on each vertical line in the right
half-plane, whose inverse is continuous and vanishes for negative
arguments by closing the contour to the right; the subtracted transform
inverts to (sqrt(pi)/2) exp(-X) on X > 0, and the O(s^{-2}) remainder has
inverse tending to zero as X decreases to zero.  Both transforms commute
with complex conjugation, so the inverses are real.

For the large-X behaviour expand at the origin.  With gamma_E the Euler
constant,

    C(s) = sqrt(s) [ 1 + (s/(2 pi)) ( log s - log(2 pi) - 1 + gamma_E )
                    + O( s^2 (1 + |log s|^2) ) ],

so the transform of U is

    (sqrt(pi)/2) s^{-3/2}
      - (sqrt(pi)/(4 pi)) s^{-1/2} ( log s - log(2 pi) - 1 + gamma_E )
      + O( |s|^{1/2} (1 + |log s|^2) ).

The first term inverts to sqrt(X).  Differentiating
L(X^{a-1})(s) = Gamma(a) s^{-a} at a = 1/2, and using
Gamma'(1/2)/Gamma(1/2) = -gamma_E - 2 log 2, gives the second term with
C' = log(8 pi) + 1.  For the remainder deform the inversion contour to the
rays arg s = +-3 pi/4 joined by a small arc of radius 1/X: the principal
branches are analytic in the intervening region, Stirling's formula is
uniform in that sector, the small arc contributes
O(X^{-3/2}(1 + log^2 X)) after s = t/X, and the exponential exp(sX)
supplies the decay on the rays.  Applying the same contour argument to
the transform of V, whose leading term at zero is (sqrt(pi)/2) s^{-1/2},
gives V(Y) = O(Y^{-1/2}) for large Y, which with local boundedness is the
stated bound.

To prove the half-line equation, use the gamma-function identity

    | Gamma(1 + i k/(2 pi)) |^2 = (|k|/2) / sinh(|k|/2),

which for real k not zero gives

    C(i k) C(-i k) = 1 - exp(-|k|).

With Fourier convention f^(k) = int exp(-i k X) f(X) dX, the polynomial
growth permits distributional transforms whose boundary values the
Laplace transforms give away from k = 0.  Since K_1^(k) = exp(-|k|), the
factorization implies

    (1 - exp(-|k|)) sqrt(pi)/(2 i k C(i k))
        = - sqrt(pi) C(-i k)/(2 (-i k))   (k not zero).

Thus the Fourier transform of U - K_1 * U + V~ is supported at zero, where
V~(X) = V(-X) for X < 0 and zero for X > 0; a distribution with transform
supported at one point is a polynomial, so U - K_1 * U + V~ is a
polynomial.  That polynomial vanishes: writing U(X) = sqrt(X_+) + R(X)
with X_+ = max(X, 0), the asymptotic makes R bounded and vanishing at both
infinities, hence K_1 * R also tends to zero there; the Poisson extension
identity (K_1 * sqrt(._+))(X) = Re sqrt(X + i) shows sqrt(X_+) - K_1 *
sqrt(._+) tends to zero at both infinities; and V~ tends to zero, so the
polynomial has limit zero and is identically zero.  Restricting to X > 0
and X < 0 proves the two convolution identities, whose values at zero
follow by continuity of K_1 * U and the established one-sided limits.

For positivity, U is positive for all large X by the asymptotic and has a
positive right limit at zero.  If its minimum on the positive half-line
were negative it would be attained at some X > 0, and averaging against
K_1 with zero exterior value would give a value strictly larger than that
minimum, contradicting U = K_1 * U.  Thus U >= 0, and the averaging
equation with positivity of the kernel makes the inequality strict; the
formula for V gives V > 0.  This proves the lemma.

The normalization U(X) ~ sqrt(X) matches the leading outer terms.
Matching the next term of the asymptotic to the outer expansion at the
right endpoint requires log 2 + 2 pi C_0 = log(delta) - C', so

    C_0 = ( log(delta) - log(16 pi) - 1 ) / ( 2 pi ),

the left endpoint giving the same value by symmetry.  Replace the two
singular outer expansions by this exact profile, subtracting their common
terms so they are counted once.  With X_+(x) = (1 - x)/delta and
X_-(x) = (1 + x)/delta, define

    D_0(X) = U(X) - [ sqrt(X) + ( log X + C' )/(4 pi sqrt X) ]  (X > 0),
    D_0(X) = 0                                                   (X < 0).

The singularity of D_0 at zero is integrable, and its decay at infinity
follows from the asymptotic, so D_0 lies in L^1(R); in formulas with an
endpoint the limit from the relevant side is used.

    Lemma (uniform endpoint matching).  Let h, w solve the scaled interval
    equations with B = 4/delta, and let F_{j,h}, F_{j,w} be the two rows
    of the outer table.  On the real line away from the endpoints define

        h_app(x) = Re( F_{0,h}(x) + delta F_{1,h}(x) )
                   + sqrt(2 delta) [ - D_0(X_+(x)) + D_0(X_-(x)) ],

        w_app(x) = Re( F_{0,w}(x) + delta F_{1,w}(x) )
                   + sqrt(2 delta) [   D_0(X_+(x)) + D_0(X_-(x)) ].

    Then there are constants C < infinity and delta_0 > 0 such that, for
    0 < delta < delta_0,

        || h - h_app ||_{Linf(-1,1)} + || w - w_app ||_{Linf(-1,1)}
            <= C delta^{3/2} |log delta|,

    and both approximants have finite one-sided limits at the endpoints.

The cancellation that makes the approximants bounded is first verified
at the right endpoint, where d = 1 - z.  The singular terms of the h row
are F_{0,h}(z) = pi/2 - sqrt(2d) + O(d^{3/2}) and

    delta F_{1,h}(z) = - ( delta / (2 pi sqrt(2d)) )
                         ( log(d/delta) + C' + i pi )
                       + O( delta sqrt(|d|)
                            (1 + |log delta| + |log |d||) ),

the constant in the leading correction being exactly C' because
2 pi C_0 = log(delta) - log(16 pi) - 1.  The w row has the opposite
square-root and inverse-square-root terms plus its analytic terms; at the
left endpoint the formulas follow by reflection and complex conjugation.
They give precisely the signs used in the definitions of h_app and w_app.
The analytic expression subtracted from U is

    A(X) = sqrt(X) + ( log X + C' + i pi ) / ( 4 pi sqrt(X) ),
        Im X < 0,

whose real boundary value is the bracket defining D_0 for X > 0 and is
zero for X < 0; since X = (1 - z)/delta lies in the lower half-plane when
z lies in the upper half-plane, this is the appropriate continuation at
the right edge, its conjugate being used at the left.  Adding the D_0
terms thus replaces the singular terms by multiples of the locally
bounded U, which gives the assertion about endpoint limits.

The residual is estimated before the interval comparison principle is
applied.  Each U(X_+(x)) satisfies (1 - K_delta*)U(X_+) = 0 on -1 < x <
1, by scaling the half-line equation.  After separating these exact terms
from either approximant, write the remaining part as Re(G_0 + delta G_1),
where G_0, G_1 come from the corresponding outer row by subtracting the
analytic edge expression, with the verified signs.  The subtraction
preserves the identities

    Im G_0' = q,   Im G_1' = - (1/2) Re G_0'',
    q = 0 for h,   q = 1 for w,

and in particular Im G_0''' = 0 on the interval.  The removed terms leave
endpoint remainders of order d^{3/2} in G_0 and sqrt(d)(1 + |log delta| +
|log d|) in G_1.

The remaining analytic functions are bounded at the endpoints and have
sublinear growth at infinity, so convolution with K_delta is their
Poisson extension: (K_delta * Re G)(x) = Re G(x + i delta).  Put d_0 = 1 -
|x|.  If d_0 >= 2 delta, expand G_0 to third order and G_1 to first order
on the vertical segment from x to x + i delta.  The derivative identities
cancel the term of order delta^2, and the cubic term from G_0 has zero
real part, so the derivative bounds of the endpoint remainders give, for
either approximant f_app,

    | (1 - K_delta*) f_app(x) - q delta |
        <= C [ delta^4 d_0^{-5/2}
             + delta^3 (1 + |log delta| + |log d_0|) d_0^{-3/2} ].

If d_0 <= 2 delta, use the endpoint remainders directly at x and at
x + i delta; their contributions are bounded by C delta^{3/2}|log delta|.
The derivative of the nonanalytic O(d^{3/2}) part of G_0 tends to zero at
the endpoint, so Im G_0' = q identifies the imaginary part of the
analytic linear coefficient there as q, also at the left endpoint by
reflection, and the analytic terms contribute q delta + O(delta^2 (1 +
|log delta|)).  The residual in this region is therefore also at most
C delta^{3/2}|log delta|.

On the exterior the correction from the nearest endpoint vanishes, and
the argument of D_0 at the opposite endpoint is at least 2/delta, so the
asymptotic bounds that correction by C delta^2 (1 + log^2 delta).  The
uncorrected outer functions have exactly the step and zero exterior data,
and the step differs from the exterior data of h by an exponentially
small quantity, so the exterior discrepancy of either approximant is
bounded by e_delta = C delta^2 (1 + log^2 delta).

Finally the mass of the kernel that leaves the interval is

    lambda_delta(x) = int_{R minus (-1,1)} K_delta(x - y) dy
                    >= c delta / ( d_0 + delta ).

Dividing the residual bound by this lower bound, and using the bound in
the region d_0 <= 2 delta, gives

    | (1 - K_delta*) f_app(x) - q delta |
        <= C delta^{3/2} |log delta| lambda_delta(x)   (-1 < x < 1);

for d_0 >= 2 delta the two ratios are bounded by C delta^3 d_0^{-3/2} and
C delta^2 (1 + |log delta| + |log d_0|) d_0^{-1/2}, both of the asserted
size.  The nonnegative function

    e_delta + M delta^{3/2} |log delta| 1_{(-1,1)}

is consequently a comparison bound for both signs of the difference
between the exact solution and its approximant once M is large enough:
the constant has zero Poisson difference, while the interval indicator
has Poisson difference lambda_delta inside, and positivity of K_delta with
the strict loss of mass on the interval gives the comparison principle.
Since e_delta = o(delta^{3/2}|log delta|), the uniform estimate follows.

The endpoint, density, and mass asymptotics now follow by integration of
the approximants.  Integrating w, since int_{-1}^1 r = pi/2,
int_{-1}^1 r^{-1} = pi, and int_{-1}^1 x L(x)/r(x) dx is finite, the outer
part gives

    int_{-1}^1 w(x) dx = pi/2 + delta ( - pi C_0 + O(1) ).

The matching terms are O(delta^{3/2}) because D_0 is integrable, and the
uniform error contributes O(delta^{3/2}|log delta|) = o(delta).

Returning to the unscaled density, the outer integral gives

    2 int_{-B}^{B} rho_B = (2B/(pi delta)) int_{-1}^{1} w
        = B^2/4 - 2 B C_0 + O(B)
        = B^2/4 + (B/pi) log B + O(B).

In a physical distance one from either edge the argument of U ranges over
[0, 1/4]; its positive minimum there, with the uniform matching error,
gives w >= c sqrt(delta) and hence rho_B >= c_1 sqrt(B), and the same
composite approximation is bounded throughout the interval, giving
rho_B <= C_1 B.

To recover b(B), use the logarithmic formula and write eta_B(y) =
eta_inf(y) - h(y/B), where eta_inf(x) = (pi/2) tanh(pi x / 2).  The
whole-line contribution is

    int_0^B a(y) eta_inf(y) dy = 4 log B - log 9 + o(B^{-1}).

Indeed a = (1 - K*) sgn on the positive half-line, so transferring 1 - K*
from the sign function to eta_inf in the symmetrized integral over (-B,B),
writing eta_inf = (pi/2) sgn + r_inf with r_inf odd and exponentially
decreasing, the sign part cancels in the crossing-end error while oddness
of r_inf and the difference of the two Cauchy kernels make the remaining
error O(B^{-2}).  The remaining integral is

    int_0^B ( 2y/(y^2 + 1) + 2y/(y^2 + 9) ) dy
        = log( (B^2 + 1)(B^2 + 9) / 9 ),

which proves the displayed identity.

For the leading outer term H_0(x) = Re arcsin x,

    int_0^B a(y) H_0(y/B) dy = 4 log 2 - 8/B + o(B^{-1}).

The constant and first correction follow respectively from

    (8/pi) int_0^1 H_0(x)/x dx = 4 log 2,
    int_0^infinity y ( a(y) - 8/(pi y) ) dy = -8,

the second by integrating with respect to the scale in the Cauchy kernel;
justifying its use, B H_0(y/B) -> y and B H_0(y/B) <= C y for 0 < y < B,
so the displayed correction is absolutely integrable.

For the real part of delta F_{1,h} one similarly obtains

    int_0^B a(y) delta Re F_{1,h}(y/B) dy = (8 + 16 C_0)/B + o(B^{-1}),

the required elementary identity being

    int_0^1 L(x)/(x r(x)) dx = pi^2/2,

obtained by replacing L(x) with log((1 + ell x)/(1 - ell x)),
differentiating in ell, and integrating 0 <= ell <= 1.  After replacing
a(Bx) by 8/(pi B x), the two terms in F_{1,h} contribute 8/B and
16 C_0/B, the replacement error being o(B^{-1}) since at zero the
numerator is linear in x with coefficient growing only as
1 + |C_0| = O(log B).  The edge compensation in the h approximation
contributes o(B^{-1}) to this integral: near the right edge it is bounded
by C B^{-3/2} int_0^infinity |D_0(X)| dX, the opposite-edge term is smaller
by the decay of D_0, and the uniform approximation error contributes at
most C B^{-3/2} log B int_0^B a(y) dy = O(B^{-3/2} log^2 B) = o(B^{-1}).
Combining the four integrals in the logarithmic formula therefore gives

    4 log b = 4 log(B/2) - 16 C_0/B + o(B^{-1}).

Since 2 pi C_0 = - log(4 pi B) - 1, exponentiation proves

    b(B) = B/2 + ( log(4 pi B) + 1 )/pi + o(1),

and in particular b(B) -> infinity, so the endpoint B_*(b) is defined for
every sufficiently large b.

It remains to evaluate the exponentially small quantity m_*.  This uses
the endpoint profile on the exterior rather than an integral of the
interior approximation.  The whole-line equation for eta_inf gives, also
for y > B,

    - epsilon'(y) = eta_inf(y) - (K_delta * h)(y/B).

At y = B + 4Y the uniform endpoint approximation and Poisson convolution
imply

    - epsilon'(B + 4Y) / sqrt(2 delta)  ->  V(Y),   Y >= 0,

with a bound independent of Y >= 0 and of sufficiently large B.  To see
both assertions write (pi/2 - h(1 - delta X))/sqrt(2 delta): on X >= 0
this converges to U(X) and is bounded by C sqrt(1 + X); for
0 <= X <= (2 delta)^{-1} the bound follows from the local composite
approximation; for X >= (2 delta)^{-1}, including the opposite endpoint
and its exterior, |h| <= pi/2 bounds the quotient by
C delta^{-1/2} <= C' sqrt(X); and on X < 0 its value is exponentially
small.  Convolution at the exterior point is against K_1(X + Y), and the
bound is integrable against that kernel uniformly in Y >= 0, giving the
limit and the stated domination.

Since epsilon is even and vanishes at the endpoints,

    m_* = 2 int_B^infinity S(y) int_B^y [ - epsilon'(z) ] dz dy.

Use S(y) ~ (1/2) exp(- pi y / 2), interchange the integrals, and put
z = B + 4Y.  The domination just proved permits passage to the limit and
gives

    m_* ~ 16 exp(- pi B / 2) sqrt(2 delta)
            (1/(2 pi)) int_0^infinity exp(- 2 pi Y) V(Y) dY.

The half-line transform formula yields

    C(2 pi) = sqrt(2 pi)/e,
    int_0^infinity exp(- 2 pi Y) V(Y) dY = 1/(2 sqrt(2) e).

Substituting delta = 4/B gives

    m_* ~ ( 8 / (pi e sqrt(B)) ) exp(- pi B / 2),

which completes the endpoint, density, and mass asymptotics.

For later use the conversion between the two large parameters is

    exp(- pi B / 2) = 4 pi B e exp(- pi b) (1 + o(1)),
    m_* ~ 32 sqrt(2) b^{1/2} exp(- pi b).

It therefore suffices to identify the parity difference with m_* to error
O(W^{-1}): under the width assumption of the parity-difference
proposition, W^{-1} = o(m_*).  The density estimate also gives
2 int rho_{B_*} = b^2 + O(b), matching the particle-number scale in that
proposition.

The spectral calculation left open by the parity-difference proposition is
now completed.  The commuting contact Hamiltonian reduces the norm of
T_{n_p} to its eigenvalue on the balanced ground state of H_2.  Three
tasks remain: construct that state on the actual circle, identify it
without asserting completeness of periodic Bethe states, and evaluate the
transfer eigenvalue including its winding terms.  The construction uses
two scales.  At each fixed particle number a line spectral resolution
identifies the possible strong-coupling limits on a circle; finite-circle
roots are then constructed, their wavefunction is shown to have a strictly
positive limiting shape, and the identification is continued to the
required circumference.  The root and transfer estimates, unlike this
identification argument, must be uniform as the particle number grows
with the circumference.

Write n_p = 2M + l with M >= 0 and l in {0,1}.  Fix D > 0, a constant
C_band < infinity, and positive lower and upper constants in

    W ~ exp(pi b) b^{-1/2} (log b)^D,   | n_p / W - b^2 | <= C_band b.

Then b is sufficiently large in terms of these data.  Uniform constants
may depend on them but not on n_p, W, or the larger circumference W' >= W
used in the continuation; poly(b) denotes a fixed polynomial bound whose
degree may increase after finitely many differentiations, and bounds with
subscript n_p are used only at fixed n_p.

The coordinate and nested Bethe-ansatz methods for one-dimensional contact
interactions originate with Lieb and Liniger (1963), Gaudin (1967), and
Yang (1967); the scattering and auxiliary-spin calculations are given in
the present normalization because their signs and fusion factors enter
the finite-circle proof.  In the ordered chamber X_1 < ... < X_{n_p} an
eigenfunction with momenta k_1, ..., k_{n_p} is a sum of plane waves, one
for each assignment of momenta to slots, with coefficients in
(C^2 (x) C^2)^{(x) n_p}.  Let P^L and P^R exchange the left and right
binary indices of two neighbouring slots.  If their assigned momenta have
difference x, exchanging them changes the coefficient by

    check R(x) = P^L P^R (x - i c P^L)(x - i c P^R) / (x^2 + c^2).

Indeed, in the colour subspace symmetric under P^L P^R the contact
condition is (d_{a+1} - d_a) psi = c P^L psi at X_{a+1} = X_a, while the
complementary subspace has Dirichlet boundary condition; substituting the
two plane waves at this face gives the displayed R-matrix.  The inverse
and braid relations follow from the two single-index relations for
x - i c P, each of which follows by expanding and using the permutation
relations, so the coefficient obtained from an initial one does not
depend on the sequence of neighbouring exchanges.

Momentum units set c = 2, and the circumference in those units is W.  A
boundary twist removes apparent singularities in intermediate algebra:

    exp(i zeta) g (x) g,   g = diag( exp(i chi), exp(-i chi) ),

with zeta, chi near zero.  Introduce M auxiliary variables a_r and the
polynomial Q_0(u) = prod_{r=1}^{M} (u - a_r), the same variables for the
two binary indices.

    Lemma (algebraic eigenvectors).  Suppose the momenta and auxiliary
    variables are nonsingular solutions of

        exp(i k_j W) = exp(i zeta + 2 i chi)
            prod_{t != j} (k_j - k_t + 2i)/(k_j - k_t - 2i)
            ( Q_0(k_j - i)/Q_0(k_j + i) )^2,   1 <= j <= n_p,

        exp(-2 i chi) prod_{j=1}^{n_p} (a_r - k_j - i)/(a_r - k_j + i)
            = prod_{s != r} (a_r - a_s - 2i)/(a_r - a_s + 2i),
            1 <= r <= M,

    nonsingular meaning that the displayed rational functions and the
    coefficient formulas below are defined by ordinary substitution.  For
    each binary index apply the upper-right entries B(a_1), ..., B(a_M) of
    the spin-1/2 monodromy defined below to the all-up vector; the tensor
    product of these two vectors, propagated by check R, gives a periodic
    twisted eigenfunction of H_2 whenever it is nonzero, of energy
    sum_j k_j^2.

    More generally, let S_J be the spin-J angular momentum matrices, g_J
    the spin-J representation of g, sigma the Pauli matrices, and

        L_J(x) = x - 2 i S_J . sigma,   F(u) = prod_{j=1}^{n_p} (u - k_j).

    In the product of L_J(u - k_j), later physical sites are put on the
    left.  Its auxiliary trace with twist g_J^{-1} has eigenvalue

        t_J(u) = sum_{m=-J}^{J} exp(-2 i m chi) F(u - 2 i m)
            Q_0(u - i(2J+1)) Q_0(u + i(2J+1))
            / [ Q_0(u - i(2m+1)) Q_0(u - i(2m-1)) ].

    All identities continue meromorphically, and a nonzero continuation
    limit of the plane eigenfunction is again an eigenfunction at the
    limiting parameters.

For J = 1/2 the local matrix is L_{1/2}(x) = (x + i) - 2 i P.  Writing
its monodromy entries as A, B, C, D, the relation with intertwiner
u - v - 2 i P shows first that the B entries commute, and also gives the
following coefficients when a diagonal entry is moved to the right past
B(a): the terms retaining its spectral variable u have factors
(u - a + 2i)/(u - a) for A(u) and (u - a - 2i)/(u - a) for D(u), while the
terms replacing this variable by a have the opposite pole coefficients and
contain B(u) A(a) or B(u) D(a); the all-up eigenvalues of A(u) and D(u)
are F(u - i) and F(u + i).  For several B entries commute a selected
B(a_r) past the others: the coefficient of the term in which B(a_r) is
replaced by B(u) is its pole coefficient times the keep factors for every
other auxiliary variable evaluated at a_r, which also follows by induction
and partial fractions.  The two such coefficients, from A and D in the
twisted trace, cancel precisely by the second equation above, and the
terms with no replacement give the J = 1/2 case of t_J(u).

For the higher auxiliary spins multiply L_{1/2}(x + 2 i J) L_J(x - i).
Relative to the total auxiliary spins J + 1/2 and J - 1/2 this product is
triangular with diagonal blocks

    (x + i(2J - 1)) L_{J+1/2}(x),   (x + i(2J + 1)) L_{J-1/2}(x - 2i).

Writing the two auxiliary angular momenta as A, B, the Pauli identity and
[A . B, A] = i A x B reduce the expansion to the two total-spin
projections, the projection of A on these blocks being
+-(A + B)/(2J + 1); the same calculation proves invariance of the plus
block, and taking twisted traces yields a recurrence in J which t_J(u)
satisfies with initial values J = 0, 1/2, proving the formula for all J.

It remains to impose the boundary condition.  Move a momentum initially
in the first slot once around the circle.  The spin-1/2 trace is tested at
k_j - i, where the local factor at that slot is a permutation times -2i;
in each index the resulting transport is the twisted trace divided by
-2i, with joint scalar denominator prod_{t != j} ((k_j - k_t)^2 + 4)
from the other slots.  With the plane-wave phase this is exactly the
first displayed equation.  Braid consistency lets any momentum be moved
into the first slot, so every boundary condition follows; the contact
conditions were already imposed by check R.  Finally the coefficients are
rational functions times entire plane factors, which gives the meromorphic
continuation, and at a nonzero limit the contact and periodic boundary
conditions persist, as does the eigenvalue equation.

The algebra constructs candidate eigenstates but does not place them in
the spectrum.  The spectral information needed for that placement is
obtained next on the line, where contour integration also explains why
only single particles and singlet pairs appear.

The ground-state identification starts at strong coupling, where each
singlet pair has binding energy - c^2/2, and the goal is to know that
every state of energy - M c^2/2 + O(1) is concentrated on M such pairs
with at most one single particle.  This is proved first on the line by a
spectral resolution and then carried to a fixed circle by localization.
Throughout the argument n_p = 2M + l is fixed, with l in {0,1}, and the
coupling c > 0 is not yet set to 2.

Write C_{n_p} = { X in R^{n_p} : X_1 < ... < X_{n_p} } and V_{n_p} =
(C^2 (x) C^2)^{(x) n_p}, and realize the line Hamiltonian H_c on
L^2(C_{n_p}; V_{n_p}) with the contact conditions already derived from
its definition, using Lebesgue measure on the ordered chamber.  For two
slots a, b let s_{ab} = 2^{-1/2}(e_1 (x) e_2 - e_2 (x) e_1) be the
normalized singlet in one binary index and s_{ab}^L (x) s_{ab}^R the
double singlet.

For integers l', m' >= 0 with l' + 2m' = n_p choose l' real elementary
momenta k_1, ..., k_{l'} and m' real pair centres u_1, ..., u_{m'}; the
two momenta belonging to centre u_a are u_a - i c/2 and u_a + i c/2.
Put each pair initially in adjacent slots with its lower momentum first
and insert its double singlet, the elementary slots retaining arbitrary
colours.  The resulting plane-wave sum E_X^{l'm'}(k,u) is an operator
V_{l'} -> V_{n_p} whose coefficients are obtained with the interchange
matrix check R and whose sum runs over all shuffles preserving the order
of the two members of every pair.  Explicitly, with kappa_1, ...,
kappa_{n_p} the initial list of momenta and iota : V_{l'} -> V_{n_p} the
insertion of the normalized double singlets into the pair slots, let
S_{l'm'} be the set of permutations pi of labels into slots satisfying
pi(A_a) < pi(B_a) for every labelled low-high pair A_a B_a; then

    E_X^{l'm'}(k,u) = sum_{pi in S_{l'm'}}
        exp( i sum_j kappa_j X_{pi(j)} ) A_pi(k,u) iota,

where A_pi is the product of adjacent interchange matrices along a
reduced sequence producing pi and A_id = I.  Coefficients reversing a
pair are zero, the inverse and braid relations make the prescription
independent of the chosen sequence, and at a removable singularity the
continuous value is used.  The initial order of the whole blocks may be
chosen arbitrarily, the block-interchange formulas below describing the
change of that convention.

    Lemma (line spectral resolution).  For every fixed integer
    n_p = 2M + l >= 0, every c > 0, and every s > 0, the heat kernel of
    H_c on the ordered line chamber is

        exp(-s H_c)(X,Y) =
          sum_{l' + 2m' = n_p} (2c)^{m'}/(l'! m'!)
          int_{R^{l'+m'}} E_X^{l'm'}(k,u) E_Y^{l'm'}(k,u)^dagger
          exp{ -s ( sum_{a=1}^{l'} k_a^2 + 2 sum_{a=1}^{m'} u_a^2
                      - m' c^2/2 ) }
          prod_{a=1}^{l'} dk_a/(2 pi)  prod_{a=1}^{m'} du_a/(2 pi),

    empty products and the vacuum term being 1.  The transform defined by
    these kernels, with the square roots of the displayed weights, is an
    isometry.  Moreover H_c + M c^2/2 >= 0, and its quadratic form is the
    integral of

        sum_{a=1}^{l'} k_a^2 + 2 sum_{a=1}^{m'} u_a^2
            + (M - m') c^2/2

    against the squared modulus of this transform.

The proof has three parts: the scattering identities show that taking a
pair residue creates no further bound cluster; a heat-kernel formula on
separated contours supplies the initial condition; lowering those
contours produces the resolution, its normalization fixed by the particle
occupying the first slot at each step.

Let Pi_{eps,eta}, with eps, eta in {+1,-1}, be the joint spectral
projections of P^L, P^R on two slots.  The interchange matrix has the
decomposition

    check R(x) = ((x - i c)/(x + i c)) Pi_{++}
               + ((x + i c)/(x - i c)) Pi_{--}
               - Pi_{+-} - Pi_{-+},

so Res_{x = i c} check R(x) = 2 i c Pi_{--} and check R(0) = -I.  Here
Pi_{--} is exactly the double-singlet projection, and taking this residue
produces a low-high pair in the specified order.  Multiplying two
interchanges transports a pair past a single and multiplies its colour
vector by

    S_{21}(x) = (x + i c/2)(x - 3 i c/2) / ((x - i c/2)(x + 3 i c/2)),

where x is the centre of the left block minus the momentum of the right
block, the same formula holding with the single on the left and x equal
to its momentum minus the pair centre.  Moving a pair past another pair
gives S_{22}(x) = (x - 2 i c)/(x + 2 i c), with x again the left centre
minus the right centre; both scalars have modulus one for real x.

Explicit checks cover the partial exchanges that arise in contour
motion.  In one binary index, for a pair AB followed by a single C set
E(q) = s_{12} (x) q_3, H(q) = q_1 (x) s_{23}, F(q) = s_{13} (x) q_2 =
E(q) + H(q); for an operator or vector V in one index write V^[2] =
V^L (x) V^R and put

    D(x) = (x - i c/2)(x + 3 i c/2),
    T(x) = (x - i c/2) E + (x + i c/2) H.

With x = u - k the coefficients of the three shuffles ABC, ACB, CAB are

    E^[2],   T(x)^[2]/D(x),   S_{21}(x) H^[2].

These follow by applying the two permutation factors in check R to the
singlet; for a single initially on the left, interchange E and H and take
x = k - u.  Hence neither partial nor complete exchanges have a pole at
x = 3 i c/2.  At x = i c/2 the last two residues in the pair-first order
are (i c/2) H^[2] and -(i c/2) H^[2]; the elementary momentum then equals
the lower member of the pair, so their plane phases agree and the residues
cancel, and in the single-first order the equality is with the upper
member.  This is the cancellation expressed by check R(0) = -I.

For two pairs AB, CD both in low-high order, put x = u - v and, in one
binary index, e = s_{12} s_{34}, f = s_{14} s_{23}, h = s_{13} s_{24} =
e + f.  The coefficients of the six allowed shuffles are

    ABCD : e^[2]
    ACBD : (x h + i c f)^[2] / ( x (x + 2 i c) )
    ACDB : (x^2 + c^2) f^[2] / ( x (x + 2 i c) )
    CABD : (x^2 + c^2) f^[2] / ( x (x + 2 i c) )
    CADB : (x h - i c f)^[2] / ( x (x + 2 i c) )
    CDAB : (x - 2 i c) e^[2] / (x + 2 i c).

This table follows equally from P_{12} = -1 on a singlet and
1 - P_{12} - P_{13} - P_{23} + P_{12} P_{23} + P_{23} P_{12} = 0 on three
binary slots; it proves the pair-pair scalar and shows that the apparent
poles at x = i c and x = 2 i c are absent.  At x = 0 the four middle
residues are, in their listed order, (i c/2) f^[2], -(i c/2) f^[2],
-(i c/2) f^[2], (i c/2) f^[2]; the phases agree in the pairs ACBD, ACDB
and CABD, CADB because B and D have the same momentum, so the cancellation
holds both in the whole wave and in either part in which the first slot
is required to contain the low member of a specified pair, the part
starting with A consisting of the first three shuffles and the part
starting with C of the last three.

Now consider

    K_s(X,Y) = int_{Gamma_1 x ... x Gamma_{n_p}}
        E_X^{n_p,0}(k) exp( - i sum_j k_j Y_j - s sum_j k_j^2 )
        prod_j dk_j/(2 pi),

where Gamma_j = R + i alpha_j, alpha_{n_p} = 0 and alpha_j - alpha_{j+1}
> c, and where the wave E^{n_p,0}(k) = E(k) is defined freely by its
meromorphic coefficients, for complex k with distinct real parts.  The
largest analyticity strip, truncated by a Gaussian factor of width bounded
below by c/4, keeps the contour differences contained in the complement of
the pole hyperplanes.  Gaussian decay makes this integral and its spatial
derivatives convergent on compact sets, and each plane wave solves the
free heat equation in the chamber, while the interchange relation imposes
the contact conditions on each face, so K_s satisfies the homogeneous
contact equation.

To verify the initial condition one needs the support of the Fourier
transforms of the rational coefficients.  For Im x > c the projector
decomposition gives

    check R(x) = P^L P^R
        + 2 c int_0^infinity exp(i x t)
            ( exp(c t) Pi_{--} - exp(-c t) Pi_{++} ) dt.

Hence an inversion of labels i < j contributes a nonnegative variable
t_{ij}, possibly with an atom at zero, and a phase exp(i (k_i - k_j)
t_{ij}).  If a permutation puts label i at position p(i), its zero-time
contribution can be supported only at

    Y_i = X_{p(i)} + sum_{j > i, p(j) < p(i)} t_{ij}
                    - sum_{h < i, p(h) > p(i)} t_{hi}.

For a nonidentity permutation let r be the first nonfixed position and
j > r the label occupying it.  Label r occurs at some p(r) > r.  No
smaller label contributes a negative shift to label r, and no larger
label contributes a positive shift to label j, so the formula would imply
Y_r >= X_{p(r)} > X_r >= Y_j, incompatible with Y in the ordered
chamber.  Only the identity permutation remains, and its coefficient is
the identity colour operator; it supplies the delta initial condition.

The support argument also justifies the limiting calculation and
heat-kernel uniqueness.  Let d be the shift vector on the right side, so
z_i = X_{p(i)} + d_i.  The shifts belong to the cone generated by
e_i - e_j for i < j.  This cone is pointed, and

    sum_{i<j} t_{ij} <= sum_{i<j} (j - i) t_{ij}
        = - sum_i i d_i <= C_{n_p} |d|,

while

    sum_i X_{p(i)} d_i = sum_{i<j, p(j)<p(i)} t_{ij}
        ( X_{p(i)} - X_{p(j)} ) >= 0,   |z|^2 >= |X|^2 + |d|^2.

The measures in the transform formula have at most exponential growth in
sum t_{ij}, so Gaussian convolution dominates them and gives spatial
decay for compactly supported initial data; it also justifies convergence
to the stated initial condition, first for data supported strictly inside
the chamber and then by density.  The contact form is bounded below by
the hyperplane trace inequality, so uniqueness of its L^2 heat evolution
identifies K_s with exp(-s H_c).

For the contour lowering process the momenta from last to first.  After
the last n_p - h slots are processed, sum over l' + 2m' = n_p - h with
weight (2c)^{m'}/(l'! m'!); the first h elementary momenta xi_1, ..., xi_h
stay on their original high contours, while the suffix momenta k and pair
centres u are real.  The wave factor in the integrand is

    E_X^{h+l',m'}(xi,k,u)
        [ I_{V_h} (x) E_{(Y_{h+1},...,Y_{n_p})}^{l'm'}(k,u)^dagger ]
        exp( - i sum_{a=1}^{h} xi_a Y_a ),

with Gaussian energy sum xi_a^2 + sum k_a^2 + 2 sum u_a^2 - m' c^2/2, and
the X-wave uses the first h elementary slots followed by the suffix
blocks as its initial order, its finite meromorphic formula defining it
for complex xi.  Thus h = n_p is K_s and h = 0 is the resolution.

To pass from h to h - 1, decompose the new suffix wave by its first
occupied slot.  For each elementary carrier or labelled pair a let
E_Y^[a] be the partial sum in which that slot contains, respectively, the
elementary momentum or the pair's low member; these possibilities are
disjoint and exhaust the allowed shuffles, so E_Y = sum_a E_Y^[a].  The
ordinary contour integral supplies the elementary-first partial adjoints
and its residues supply the pair-first partial adjoints.  When block
parameters z leave the real contours an adjoint wave is continued as the
meromorphic function E_Y(conj z)^dagger, agreeing with E_Y(z)^dagger for
real z, and the same convention applies to each partial adjoint.

Let k = xi_h be the next momentum.  Lowering its contour to the real line
provides the adjoint-wave terms whose first suffix slot is an elementary
momentum: with a fixed elementary carrier put that carrier first among
the suffix blocks, the unitary elementary and whole-block interchanges
move the corresponding coefficient from the X-wave to the Y-wave, and
symmetrizing the elementary variables supplies all elementary choices for
the first slot.  The residues occur at k = p + i c, where p is an
elementary momentum in the old suffix; put p first in that suffix before
computing, a downward contour shift contributes -i times the residue, so
the residue 2 i c Pi_{--} contributes the factor 2c and inserts a
normalized double singlet.  The momenta at X are p, k in low-high order,
with centre u = p + i c/2; lower u from this contour to the real line.  On
the Y side the new first slot uses the adjointed phase of the low member,
namely the phase with the high momentum k, while the other member is the
old suffix variable p, so the old suffix exchanges continued with this p
give exactly the part of E_Y^dagger with that pair's low member first.

The pole checks justifying both contour motions are as follows.  When k
descends past an existing pair, the three-slot formulas have no pole at
centre difference 3 i c/2, and the possible pole at i c/2 cancels between
the partial and complete exchanges whose phases agree, so there is no
three-particle residue.  When the newly formed centre u is lowered, the
same cancellation applies to the X-wave.  In the restricted adjoint
Y-expression the low member of the new pair has already been put first,
and the crossing that remains uses only p = u - i c/2, whose imaginary
part moves from 0 to -c/2 without crossing any elementary interchange
pole at difference +-i c, including an elementary momentum on the old
real contour by continuation with an indentation if necessary.  The
adjoint of the pair-low-first three-slot sum has denominator
(x + i c/2)(x - 3 i c/2) with x = u - k_single, which has no zero in
0 <= Im x <= c/2, and for two pairs the four-slot table proves regularity
at coincident centres even in the restricted first-slot expression.  The
only other possible centre poles are at -2 i c for the X-coefficients and
at 2 i c for the continued adjoint coefficients, both outside the strips
being traversed.

These checks remain valid with other variables inserted.  At a generic
point of a candidate pole hyperplane use the braid and whole-block
identities to bring the two relevant blocks together; for fixed occupied
slots, inserting the other momenta multiplies the two cancelling residues
by the same nonsingular interchange operators, and if the first slot is
restricted put its block first before the operation, the earlier
unprocessed contours staying separated from all these motions.  Writing
the finite sums over a common rational denominator, removal of the poles
at generic points of every denominator hyperplane removes the
corresponding factors, so the removals extend across their intersections.
Distinct real parts or indented contours may therefore be used for the
residue computations and subsequently removed, and on bounded X, Y small
contour circles about removable singularities give uniform bounds for the
finite wave sums which, with the Gaussian, justify the integrations and
deformations.

It remains to give every partial adjoint the same weight.  Changing the
initial block order multiplies both waves by the same unitary
block-interchange operator, so the product E_X (E_Y^[a])^dagger is
independent of that choice, and each first carrier may be put in front
before symmetrizing the labels.

For a final sector (l', m') the elementary-first contribution comes from
(l' - 1, m') in the old suffix and has weight

    (2c)^{m'}/((l' - 1)! m'!) = l' (2c)^{m'}/(l'! m'!),

accounting for the l' choices of elementary first carrier.  The pair-first
contribution comes from (l' + 1, m' - 1): there are l' + 1 elementary
choices for p and the residue supplies 2c, so its weight is

    2c (l' + 1) (2c)^{m'-1}/((l' + 1)! (m' - 1)!)
        = m' (2c)^{m'}/(l'! m'!),

accounting for the m' choices of pair first carrier.  After
symmetrization each of the l' + m' partial adjoints has weight
(2c)^{m'}/(l'! m'!), and their sum is the full adjoint wave.  This closes
the induction and proves the resolution.

Define the transform by

    (F_c f)_{l'm'}(k,u) = ((2c)^{m'}/(l'! m'!))^{1/2}
        int_{C_{n_p}} E_Y^{l'm'}(k,u)^dagger f(Y) dY,

initially for compactly supported f, with target the direct sum of the
L^2 spaces with the product measures dk du/(2 pi) and colour space
V_{l'}.  Writing

    lambda_{l'm'}(k,u) = sum k_a^2 + 2 sum u_a^2
        + (M - m') c^2/2 >= 0,

the quadratic identity from the resolution is

    <f, exp(-s (H_c + M c^2/2)) f>
        = sum_{l' + 2m' = n_p} int exp(-s lambda_{l'm'})
            | (F_c f)_{l'm'} |^2.

As s decreases to zero, strong continuity on the left and monotone
convergence on the right give ||F_c f|| = ||f||, so the transform
extends isometrically to the full Hilbert space.  The identity then
implies ||exp(-s(H_c + M c^2/2))|| <= 1, hence H_c + M c^2/2 >= 0, and
taking the increasing limit of (1 - exp(-s lambda))/s gives, by the
spectral theorem, the stated quadratic-form identity and its domain.
Only the reconstruction F_c^* F_c = I is used; surjectivity onto the
direct sum is unnecessary.

The spectral resolution contains only single particles and singlet pairs;
the residue cancellations have excluded additional binding channels.  Its
form identity gives a penalty c^2/2 for every missing pair.  To use this
on a circle, control of the spatial shape of the pair waves is also
needed, uniformly in their real centre momenta.

For a shuffle contributing to E_X^{l'm'}, draw the closed interval
between the two slots occupied by each pair, combine overlapping
intervals into connected components, and include every single whose slot
lies inside such a component; a single outside all pair intervals forms
a component of one slot.  Group the shuffles for which every component
has the same set of slots and the same set of carriers.  Let
E_{X,g}^{l'm'} denote the sum for one such group.

    Lemma (decay across pair spans).  At fixed n_p there are constants
    C_{n_p} < infinity and a_{n_p} > 0, independent of c > 0 and the real
    momenta, such that every group just defined satisfies

        || E_{X,g}^{l'm'}(k,u) ||
            <= C_{n_p} exp( - a_{n_p} c
                 sum_{components I} ( X_{max I} - X_{min I} ) ),

    the norm being the operator norm after summing the shuffles in the
    group.

Divide all momenta by c.  A shuffle's plane phase has modulus

    exp( - (c/2) sum_{pairs a} ( X_{high slot of a}
                                 - X_{low slot of a} ) ),

and the sum of the pair spans is at least the sum of the component
spans.  Rational interchange coefficients are uniformly bounded away
from their pole hyperplanes.  Near real centres their only apparent
singularities are coincident pair centres, and the four-slot
cancellations are internal to the present groups: the cancelling
shuffles are ACBD, ACDB and CABD, CADB, and within either pair the union
of the spans belonging to AB and CD is the same interval, from their
earliest low member to their latest high member, an equality unchanged
by inserting further slots, so the connected components, their carrier
sets, and their included singles are unchanged and every group sum is
holomorphic through these real hyperplanes.

A uniform Cauchy estimate applies to this holomorphic sum.  Choose a
sufficiently small number depending only on n_p; among finitely many
successively much smaller scales choose one with no distance between
real divided momenta in the intervening annulus, and cluster the
arguments at the smaller scale, so different clusters are separated by
much more than the chosen scale.  Within each cluster put the variables
on small circles about their original real values, using distinct radii
separated by fixed multiples of that scale and all kept below the initial
small number.  On the product of these circles, equal-centre
denominators are bounded below by a positive n_p-dependent constant,
denominators with nonzero imaginary offsets are also separated from
zero, and every rational coefficient is bounded there by a constant
depending only on n_p, so Cauchy's formula recovers the holomorphic group
sum at the original real arguments.  To control the plane phases on those
circles, factor from each component the common phase evaluated at its
leftmost position; the carrier set of that component is fixed throughout
the group, so the factored phase is the same in every term and has
modulus one at real momenta, and perturbing the remaining divided momenta
by the small circle radii increases their modulus by at most
exp(eps C_{n_p} c sum_I span(I)).  Choosing the initial upper bound on the
radii small enough that this uses less than half the original pair-gap
decay gives the displayed estimate.

Fix a circle length W_0 > 0 and choose finitely many seams and smooth
nonnegative permutation-invariant cutoffs vartheta_alpha with
sum_alpha vartheta_alpha^2 = 1, such that all positions in the support of
vartheta_alpha stay a positive distance from the corresponding seam: at
fixed particle number, among sufficiently many candidate seams at least
one stays a fixed distance from every position, and cutting at that seam
identifies each localized function with a compactly supported function on
a line chamber.  In each chamber let p be a partition of its ordered
slots into M adjacent pairs and, when l = 1, one single.  For a pair
occupying slots a, a + 1 use coordinates

    q = (X_a + X_{a+1})/2,   z = c (X_{a+1} - X_a) > 0,

keeping the single coordinate unchanged.  The coordinate Jacobian is
c^{-M}, so the corresponding isometric change of L^2 coordinates
multiplies a wavefunction by c^{-M/2}.  Pair centres and the single
coordinate are separated when all their mutual distances are positive,
the sets of nonseparated centre coordinates have measure zero, and for
delta > 0 and Z < infinity a retained pair chart is the part of these
coordinates on which all centre distances exceed delta and
0 < z_a < Z for every pair.  The regions for distinct pair assignments
are disjoint once c is sufficiently large in terms of delta, Z; convergence
in these charts is followed by their joint exhaustion as delta decreases
to zero and Z increases to infinity, and does not mean that the whole
wavefunction has an L^2 limit under every pairing coordinate map.

    Lemma (compactness in pair coordinates).  Fix W_0 > 0,
    n_p = 2M + l, and C < infinity.  Let c_j -> infinity and let psi_j
    be normalized vectors in the form domain of the circle Hamiltonian
    such that

        < psi_j, ( H_{c_j} + M c_j^2/2 ) psi_j > <= C.

    After passage to a subsequence the localized functions in the pair
    coordinates just defined converge in L^2 on every fixed retained
    chart.  For each seam and pair assignment these limits are compatible
    under enlargement of the chart and have the form

        f_{alpha,p}(q,v) exp( - (1/2) sum_{a=1}^{M} z_a )
            (x)_{a=1}^{M} ( s_a^L (x) s_a^R ),

    with v and its colour present only if l = 1 and the coefficient
    f_{alpha,p} absorbing that colour.  As delta decreases to zero and Z
    increases to infinity the sum of the squared norms of these chart
    limits tends to 1, counted with the fixed square partition of unity.
    No norm is lost away from the pair coincidence manifolds or at their
    intersections, and inner products of two sequences converging in this
    sense converge to the corresponding sums of inner products of their
    limits.

The form localization identity gives

    sum_alpha < vartheta_alpha psi_j,
            ( H_{c_j} + M c_j^2/2 ) vartheta_alpha psi_j >
        = < psi_j, ( H_{c_j} + M c_j^2/2 ) psi_j >
          + sum_alpha || |grad vartheta_alpha| psi_j ||^2.

The error is bounded independently of j and every localized term is
nonnegative by the line resolution, so it suffices to prove the assertion
for one localized function on the line with bounded norm and bounded
shifted form energy.  Fix a bounded chamber box containing the supports
of all these localized functions; restricting the output of every
reconstruction kernel to this box is an L^2 contraction that changes
none of the localized functions, and is needed because a
momentum-truncated reconstruction need not have compact support.

Apply the isometric transform and restrict all elementary momenta and
pair centres to a fixed bounded set |.| <= R.  The form identity bounds
the squared norm discarded by this restriction by C'/R^2, and the
reconstruction has at most this norm because F_c^* is a contraction.
Likewise every sector with fewer than M pairs has lambda_{l'm'} >=
c^2/2, so its total squared transform norm is at most 2 C'/c^2.  Thus
only the sector with M pairs and l singles can survive, and its momenta
may first be kept in a fixed bounded set.

For that sector the reconstruction kernel contains the factor
(2c)^{M/2}/sqrt(l! M!).  In a group with overlapping pair spans or with
a single inside a pair span, a component with p pairs and s_0 singles has
2p + s_0 slots and 2p + s_0 - 1 internal gaps, one gap for a component
that is one adjacent pair and at least p + 1 gaps for an overlapping
component or one containing an inserted single.  Squaring the span-decay
estimate and integrating the ordered coordinates therefore gives one
factor O(c^{-1}) for every such gap, and there are at least M + 1 decaying
gaps over all components in these groups.  The squared kernel
normalization is O(c^M), so their squared Hilbert-Schmidt norms on the
momentum cutoff are O(c^{-1}); the remaining component positions range
over a fixed bounded set, and the bounded momentum volume with
Cauchy-Schwarz makes all these groups have vanishing reconstructed L^2
norm, uniformly for bounded transform coefficients.

Every surviving shuffle is a permutation of whole adjacent blocks.  In
the coordinates of its adjacent-pair assignment its normal plane factor
is exactly exp(- sum z_a/2) and its colour factor is the product of
double singlets, while the coefficients between whole blocks are the
explicit scalars S_{21}, S_{22}, whose limits on a fixed momentum cutoff
exist as c -> infinity.  The rescaled Jacobian cancels the factor
c^{M/2} in the kernel normalization, and the fixed centre-coordinate
volume with the finite momentum cutoff and the exponential normal factor
give an integrable dominating square.  The reconstruction kernels for
this assignment consequently converge in Hilbert-Schmidt norm on every
retained chart.  On a chart belonging to another assignment their
contribution tends to zero: bounded rescaled gaps for both assignments
would force two distinct block centres to approach each other, whereas
the retained chart separates those centres by delta, and the exponential
gap bound controls the remaining contribution.

This kernel convergence implies compactness even though the transform
coefficients depend on j.  On the fixed cutoff their L^2 norms are
bounded, a weakly convergent subsequence can be extracted, the limiting
Hilbert-Schmidt operator is compact, and convergence to it in operator
norm gives strong convergence of the reconstructed functions; the limit
has the asserted normal and colour factors.

The argument retains the whole norm.  Restricting first to centres
separated by at least delta > 0 and to 0 < z_a < Z, different
adjacent-pair assignments occupy disjoint configuration regions for all
sufficiently large c; the retained region is the union over those
assignments, each whole-block kernel being estimated in the coordinates
of its own assignment.  For the bounded-momentum kernels the squared
norm excluded by centre distances below delta is uniformly small as
delta decreases to zero, since the kernels are uniformly bounded in the
centre coordinates and these collision neighbourhoods have vanishing
volume.  The norm excluded by z_a > Z tends uniformly to zero as Z
increases, by the exponential normal bound, and the groups already
discarded cover the overlaps and inserted singles with vanishing norm.
Thus neither intersections of pair coincidence manifolds nor their
complement retain additional norm.  Finally remove the momentum cutoff
using the uniform C'/R^2 bound and take a diagonal subsequence over the
finitely many seam charts and pair assignments and a countable
exhaustion of the separation and gap cutoffs; the identity
sum_alpha vartheta_alpha^2 = 1 gives the asserted total norm, and
applying the same cutoffs to two sequences, strong convergence on the
retained charts gives convergence of their inner products with
Cauchy-Schwarz controlling all discarded parts.

    Corollary (ground-state test by positive overlap).  Fix a circle
    length W_0 > 0 and a particle number n_p = 2M + l, l in {0,1}, and
    work in the fixed-weight sector balanced in each binary index.
    Suppose a normalized eigenbranch has energy - M c^2/2 + O(1) as
    c -> infinity, and suppose that after one common phase choice for all
    charts every subsequential limit in the pair charts has the displayed
    form, with scalar centre coefficient strictly positive almost
    everywhere that the limiting chart cutoff is positive, in the
    static-sign convention, and with the remaining single, when l = 1,
    having the majority colour in each index.  Then the branch is the
    ground state for all sufficiently large c.

Trial states consisting of separated, exponentially bound pairs and
smooth centre wavefunctions give a circle ground-state energy at most
- M c^2/2 + O(1): one can cut off the pair gaps inside fixed disjoint
intervals, the cutoff error being exponentially small in c and the
centre kinetic energy bounded.  The true normalized ground state
therefore satisfies the compactness lemma.  It is the simple ground
state in this sector, is nonnegative in the static-sign convention, and
its limits retain total norm 1, with normal factors as displayed and,
in a sector balanced in each binary index, the remaining single, if
present, having the majority colour in each index.  A nonzero
nonnegative limit consequently has strictly positive inner product with
the strictly positive limiting centre coefficient of the eigenbranch.
If the two eigenstates were different for an unbounded sequence of
couplings, self-adjointness and simplicity of the ground state would
make their inner products zero on that sequence; passing to a
simultaneous compactness subsequence, the inner-product convergence just
proved gives a strictly positive limit, a contradiction.  This
identifies the strong-coupling branch from its wavefunction and energy,
without assuming a string description of the ground state on a finite
circle.

The construction of the finite-circle states now proceeds.  Each pair has
a large gap and real centre u_j plus an auxiliary variable near u_j, and
when l = 1 there is also a single momentum v.  Before solving for the
exponentially small changes in the pair spacings, the real equations for
the pair centres are solved, along the entire ray W' >= W with n_p fixed;
its large-W' end corresponds, after rescaling the circle, to the
strong-coupling limit of the ground-state test.

At exact pair spacings, multiply the two momentum equations and eliminate
their internal auxiliary factors by the auxiliary equation before passing
to the limit.  With consecutive pair quantum numbers centred at zero this
gives

    2 W' u_j = sum_{t != j} [ pi sgn(j - t)
                 - 2 arctan( (u_j - u_t)/4 ) ] + l phi(u_j - v),

    W' v = sum_{j=1}^{M} phi(v - u_j),
    phi(x) = 2 ( arctan x - arctan(x/3) ),

the second equation being present only when l = 1.  These equations are
obtained from the full Bethe equations, not by omitting a vanishing
internal factor.

    Lemma (counting density and the single-particle Jacobian).  Under the
    parameter assumptions, for every W' >= W the equations have a
    reflection-symmetric solution with v = 0 and u_1 < ... < u_M, and
    with v fixed the pair solution is unique.  Define

        Z_c(x) = x/pi + (1/(pi W')) sum_{j=1}^{M} arctan((x - u_j)/4)
                 - (l/(2 pi W')) phi(x),   d(x) = Z_c'(x).

    Then Z_c is odd and

        d(x) = 1/pi + (1/W') sum_j K(x - u_j)
               - (l/W') (K_1 - K_3)(x),
        Z_c(u_j) = ( j - (M + 1)/2 ) / W'.

    Let B_c > 0 be determined by Z_c(B_c) = M/(2 W').  Uniformly:

    (i) d is bounded below by a positive constant; B_c = O((1 + b)^2);
        d and any fixed number of its derivatives are bounded by poly(b);
        neighbouring centres are separated by at least
        1/(W' poly(b));

    (ii) on (-B_c, B_c),

        d = rho_{B_c} - (l/W') (1 - K*)_{B_c}^{-1} (K_1 - K_3)
            + O( poly(b)/W'^2 ),

        the inverse in the middle term having bounded supremum and L^1 norm
        independently of B_c;

    (iii) B_c <= B_* + O(1), and at W' = W also B_c >= B_* - O(1);

    (iv) the full Jacobian of the centre equations, including the single
        equation when present, is invertible, with inverse row sums
        bounded by a fixed power of W'.

Move the increasing pair terms in the first equation to the left.  For
fixed v these equations are the gradient of a strictly convex function:
the quadratic part has Hessian 2 W' I, the pair interactions give a
nonnegative weighted graph Laplacian, and the term involving phi has
uniformly bounded diagonal curvature, so the Hessian is strictly
positive and coercivity gives existence and uniqueness.  The increasing
right-hand sides pi(2j - M - 1) imply the ordering of the u_j, which
follows also by interchanging two variables in the convex minimization
problem.  Reflection reverses the index order and changes every sign, so
uniqueness gives u_j = -u_{M+1-j} when v = 0, and oddness of phi proves
the single equation.

The formulas for Z_c and d follow by division and differentiation.
Since K >= 0 its only possibly negative term is O(1/W'), giving the lower
bound for d.  The total quantile interval has length M/W' = O(b^2), giving
the first bound for B_c; derivatives of each Cauchy kernel are bounded and
M/W' = O(b^2), giving the derivative bounds; the quantile increments are
1/W', so the upper bound for d gives the stated separation.

For a smooth function of u, summation over the centres is midpoint
summation after the change of variable s = Z_c(u), and the inverse change
of variable and its fixed derivatives have polynomial bounds because d is
bounded below.  Applied to K(x - u) uniformly in x this gives

    (1/W') sum_j K(x - u_j) = int_{-B_c}^{B_c} K(x - y) d(y) dy
        + O( poly(b)/W'^2 ).

The inverse of 1 - K* on this interval has norm O(1 + B_c), and solving
the resulting equation for d proves the second bound.  To bound its
middle term use S*(delta_0 - K) = K_1 - K_3 and write

    (1 - K*)_{B_c}^{-1} (K_1 - K_3)
        = S_in - (1 - K*)_{B_c}^{-1} (K * S_out),

the inverse costing a polynomial in 1 + B_c while S_out has an
exponential tail, which gives uniform supremum and L^1 bounds; bounded
values of B_c are covered by the same inverse estimate.

Integrating the bound shows int rho_{B_c} <= b^2/2 + O(b), and the
total-density asymptotic together with its relation between b and B_*
yields B_c <= B_* + O(1); at W' = W the two-sided particle band yields the
reverse estimate.

It remains to check the Jacobian when the single is present.  The pair
block is a diagonally dominant matrix with nonpositive off-diagonal
entries, and its inverse has absolute row sums O(1/W').  At the symmetric
solution the single diagonal entry is

    W' - sum_j phi'(-u_j)
        = 2 pi W' ( S * ( rho_ext 1_{|x| > B_c}) )(0) + O(1),
    rho_ext = 1/pi + K * rho_{B_c},

with rho_ext = rho_{B_c} inside the interval.  Indeed phi' = 2 pi (K_1 -
K_3), and applying midpoint summation and the bound, then convolving

    rho_{B_c} - K * rho_{B_c} = 1/pi - rho_ext 1_{|x| > B_c}

with S, gives the identity; the impurity correction has total mass
O(1/W'), so its contribution is O(1).  The edge lower bound and
positivity of S bound the main term below by
c W' sqrt(1 + B_c) exp(- pi B_c/2), and the same conclusion for bounded
B_c follows directly from positivity.  The absolute sum of the cross
entries is sum_j |phi'(u_j)| = O((1 + b) W'): sum a smooth integrable
envelope of |phi'| by the counting rule and use
rho_{B_c} <= C(1 + B_c) <= C(1 + b).  Combined with the pair inverse
bound, this makes the correction in the single Schur complement
O(1 + b).  Since the parameter assumptions give

    W sqrt(1 + B_*) exp(- pi B_*/2) ~ b (log b)^D,

which dominates the Schur correction and the bounded remainder by a factor
tending to infinity, and the bound improves for W' >= W because
B_c <= B_* + O(1), the Schur complement is invertible and block inversion
gives the claimed polynomial bound for the full inverse.

The centre equations are well conditioned except for the explicitly
controlled single-particle direction.  The imaginary pair-spacing
corrections are exponentially small but are divided by products over all
other pairs, and the next bound controls precisely those products.

    Lemma (suppression of pair-spacing corrections).  With the centres of
    the preceding lemma, set

        A(x) = log( |x| sqrt(x^2 + 16)/(x^2 + 4) ),
        kappa(x) = 1 + (A * rho_{B_c})(x).

    Uniformly for W' >= W,

        inf_{|x| <= B_c} W' kappa(x) >= c_2 W' sqrt(1 + B_c)
            exp(- pi B_c/2) >> log W',

    the last ratio tending to infinity as b -> infinity, and

        W' + sum_{t != j} A(u_j - u_t)
            = W' kappa(u_j) + O(log W')

    uniformly in j.

The edge lower bound for rho_{B_c} also bounds rho_ext below by a
constant times sqrt(1 + B_c) in a fixed interval just outside each
endpoint, and convolution over these intervals proves the first
inequality; its final comparison follows from the scale estimate at the
end of the counting-density lemma, for larger W' the left side only
gaining the needed margin.

For the sum, apply midpoint summation in the coordinate s = Z_c(u).
Away from the cell containing u_j and its two neighbours, monotone or
bounded-variation summation for the logarithmic singularity costs
O(log W'); the spacings show that the log integral over each of the
excluded quantile cells is at most C log W'/W', giving the same total
cost for those cells.  Replacing d by rho_{B_c} uses the counting-density
bound: the inverse there is bounded, A is integrable, and the remaining
error is polynomial in b times 1/W'.

    Lemma (finite-circle roots).  In the parameter range of the section
    the centre solution has a locally unique continuation to solutions
    of the Bethe equations, with the singular solutions understood as
    analytic continuation limits.  At zero twist write these solutions as

        k_{j+} = a_j + i + X_j,   k_{j-} = a_j - i + Y_j,
        u_j^true = (k_{j+} + k_{j-})/2.

    For every fixed A_0 > 0 the differences between the true variables
    and their formal centre values are O_{A_0}(W'^{-A_0}), uniformly for
    W' >= W, once b is sufficiently large.  The solutions are locally
    analytic in W' and in twists near zero, and these local solutions link
    along [W, infinity).  At fixed n_p their zero-twist centres satisfy

        u_j = pi ( j - (M + 1)/2 )/W' + O_{n_p}(W'^{-2}),   W' -> infinity.

    Ordinary nonsingular parameters form a generic set on this continued
    branch, and a common twist translates every rapidity by zeta/W'.

The equations are first rewritten at the formal pair spacings without
dividing by a vanishing internal factor; the resulting system has a
polynomially bounded inverse linearization, while its error is smaller
than every inverse power of W', which gives the true roots by
contraction.

The j-th auxiliary equation can be written

    X_j/(2i + X_j) * (Y_j - 2i)/Y_j = A_j,

with A_j analytic and containing only external factors and the twist.
Set X_j = D_j Y_j and impose D_j = A_j (2i + X_j)/(Y_j - 2i).  At the
formal solution |A_j| = 1 and D_j = -A_j, so D_j stays away from zero.
Denote by F_j the product in the upper-member momentum equation after
removing its internal pair factors; solving for Y_j gives the regular
relation

    Y_j = (D_j - 1) exp(i k_{j+} W') (2i + X_j)^2
          / ( F_j (4i + X_j - Y_j) D_j^2 ).

In addition retain the product of the two momentum equations, replacing
the internal auxiliary ratio by A_j, and retain the single equation when
present; the logarithms of these last equations are taken near the
centre equations and are analytic there.  Distinct pair centres are
separated, and the single need not be separated from a centre because
its potentially dangerous factors have nonzero imaginary shifts.

On a neighbourhood of radius W'^{-C}, with a fixed sufficiently large C,
the logarithmic derivatives through two orders of the nonzero external
factors, and the row sums of the logarithmic derivatives of their
products, are bounded by fixed powers of W'.  This follows from the
centre-spacing bound and the polynomial number of factors, so increasing
C makes the variation of each product logarithm arbitrarily small.  A
polynomial bound for the raw product F_j or its derivatives is not
required.  For A_j, whose formal modulus is one, these logarithmic
bounds give polynomial bounds for the ordinary derivatives.  For the
upper-member equation the bounded quantity is instead the normalized
ratio exp(i k_{j+} W')/F_j: at the formal centres the logarithm of its
modulus is

    - W' - sum_{t != j} A(u_j - u_t) + O(log W')
        = - W' kappa(u_j) + O(log W'),

the possible single-particle factor accounting for the first remainder,
and by the suppression lemma the negative term dominates every fixed
multiple of log W'.  The preceding logarithmic derivative bounds
preserve this estimate on a slightly smaller neighbourhood, so the
fraction after D_j - 1, together with the derivatives needed for
contraction, is smaller than every inverse power of W'.

If the right side is set to zero then Y = 0, and after eliminating
X = D Y the linearization is triangular: first the Y equations, then
the centre equations, then the equations determining D.  Its inverse is
bounded by a power of W' by the counting-density lemma.  Applying this
inverse to the regular system and using its second-derivative row-sum
bounds, the resulting Newton correction map is a contraction on a
smaller polynomial-radius ball: its forcing term is superpolynomially
small and its Lipschitz constant less than one.  This proves local
existence, uniqueness, and the claimed error, and the same construction
for complex parameters proves local analyticity.  Its balls can be
chosen to overlap as W' varies, because the displacement from the
formal solution is much smaller than their radii, so the local solutions
link along the whole ray.

For fixed n_p, expansion of the centre equations at large W' gives the
asymptotic for u_j, and the fraction in the regular relation is then
exp(-W') times a fixed-n_p polynomial.  The only remaining issue is
whether an internal singularity could persist identically; at formal
spacings the external pair factors cancel in A_j, whose explicit twist
factor is exp(-2i chi), the remaining factor being independent of the
other pair centres with bounded derivatives including the single factor
if present.  At this fixed-n_p end the centre and single responses to
the twists are O_{n_p}(1/W'), since the eliminated pair equations have
the fused phases of the centre equations, so an infinitesimal spin twist
makes D_j - 1 nonzero generically even if it vanishes at zero twist, and
the exponentially small correction in the regular relation does not
change this.  Analytic continuation gives generic ordinary points along
the branch, direct substitution proves the common translation by
zeta/W', and at real zero twist conjugation symmetry, or continuity from
the ordinary roots, gives real eigenenergies whenever the vectors are
nonzero; no global uniqueness assertion about Bethe roots is needed.

Suppose the upper member k_p = a + i precedes the lower member k_q = a - i,
so p < q.  If r < q the first product vanishes at s = q; if r >= q the
second product vanishes at s = p.  Thus B(a) annihilates the vacuum for
any separation of these sites and any values of the other momenta, and
since the B entries commute this B(a) may be applied first in the full
spin vector, proving the claimed vanishing as a polynomial identity
without a potentially singular conjugation.

Apply this vanishing separately to each reversed pair.  Taylor's formula
in the corresponding variables bounds each single-index vector by a
product of factors |X_j| + |Y_j|: for each reversed pair subtract the
evaluation at X_j = Y_j = 0, these evaluation operators commute because
their variable pairs are disjoint, each subtracted evaluation is zero by
the preceding identity with all other variables unrestricted, and the
product of the resulting difference operators is the original vector, so
the integral form of Taylor's formula gives the product bound with
fixed-n_p polynomial constants at the large-circle spacings.  The two
indices supply the square of this product, whereas the vacuum scalar
costs only one factor |X_j - Y_j|^{-1} per reversal.  Since

    X_j - Y_j = (D_j - 1) Y_j,
    Y_j = (D_j - 1) * ( the fraction in the regular relation ),

the quotient is removable even at D_j = 1, and at fixed n_p its bound is
poly_{n_p}(W') exp(-r W') when r pairs are reversed.  All remaining
permutation factors cost only fixed-n_p powers, because distinct centres
are separated.  The same argument works with infinitesimal twists and
then passes to zero twist; in particular the initial ordered amplitude
remains nonzero at the large-circle end, and singular roots there yield
ordinary continuation-limit eigenfunctions.

To apply the ground-state test, rescale the circle of length W' at
coupling 2 to a fixed length W_0.  The new coupling is c = 2 W'/W_0,
momenta are multiplied by W'/W_0, and energies by (W'/W_0)^2, so the
eigenenergy is - M c^2/2 + O_{n_p}(1).  Consider a coordinate chart with
the seam avoided, distinct pair centres separated by a fixed positive
distance, and within-pair gaps rescaled by c.  Reversed-pair amplitudes
are negligible there, because the spans remain short of a full
circumference, and a permutation that pairs positions near different
separated centres is negligible for the same exponential reason.  The
remaining permutations exchange entire blocks, and their coefficients are
given, to leading order, by the two fusion scalars of the line
calculation: as c increases to infinity the pair-pair scalar tends to -1
and the pair-single scalar to 1.

The momentum conjugate to a pair centre is twice its individual momentum,
so the centre wave is

    det[ exp( (2 pi i / W_0) ( j - (M + 1)/2 ) q_a ) ]_{j,a=1}^{M},

together with the normal profile and a constant single wave if present.
For ordered distinct centres the Vandermonde formula is a constant phase
times prod_{a<t} sin( pi (q_t - q_a)/W_0 ), which is strictly positive;
after the static sign change each double singlet has nonnegative
components, as does the possible majority-colour single.

The same phase choice applies to charts crossing the seam, by periodicity
and translation.  Summing the centre equations gives
2 sum_j u_j + l v = 0, and at zero twist periodicity quantizes the total
momentum of a nonzero true wave in (2 pi/W') Z.  Its difference from the
formal value is o(1/W') at the fixed-n_p large-circle end, so that integer
is zero there; it is constant along the continuous root branch, including
continuation limits, and hence the total momentum is zero throughout.

The convergence obtained is uniform on compact separated-centre charts
with bounded rescaled gaps, before the final L^2 normalization.  After
normalization the wave on each such chart is a common scalar multiple of
this limiting shape, up to a vanishing relative error.  The scalar is
bounded above because the shape has positive norm on any one nonempty
chart, and it cannot tend to zero along a subsequence: the pair
compactness lemma would then give zero limit on every retained chart,
although those charts retain the full norm.  Every normalized limit
consequently has the same strictly positive centre shape, with the chart
cutoffs included, and the ground-state test identifies the plane branch
with the ground state for sufficiently large c.

Finally decrease W' along the root branch.  The energy is locally
analytic and is an eigenvalue at generic parameters.  The plane
coefficients and B vectors are meromorphic, and their nonvanishing at the
large-circle end shows that ordinary nonzero states occur on an open dense
set.  At complementary parameters take the twist continuation first and
then pass to zero twist.  Continuity of the compact-resolvent spectrum on
a finite circle keeps the limiting energy in the spectrum, and the
balanced ground energy remains simple, so a continuous eigenvalue branch
already equal to it cannot leave it without a forbidden degeneracy.  The
identification therefore holds all the way to W' = W, and commutation
together with positivity now identify the corresponding transfer
eigenvalue with ||T_{n_p}||.  The same simple eigenvalue admits the
infinitesimal twist continuation used below.

The transfer eigenvalue on a finite circle is now computed.  Setting
c = 2 and W' = W, the root branch identifies the solution on which the
transfer norm is attained, and the eigenvalue of the heat kernel on the
circle must still be calculated.  The rotor representation carried
across the seam contributes a sum absent from a calculation with
infinite-line plane waves; that sum is first obtained exactly and then
its nontrivial terms are bounded uniformly when n_p grows with W.

Write n_p = 2M + l with l in {0,1} and retain the twists zeta, chi.
Conjugating each exterior binary index by the antisymmetric tensor is a
fixed change of basis that reverses the diagonal matrix-index twists, so
the seam acts in the rotor sector (q,j) by the scalar exp(i zeta q) and
by g_j^{-1} on each of its two matrix indices, with g_j the spin-j
representation of g = diag(exp(i chi), exp(-i chi)).  Throughout,

    q in Z,  j in (1/2) Z_{>=0},  2j = q mod 2,
    d_j = 2j + 1,   E_{qj} = (1/2)( q^2 + d_j^2 - 1 ),

and the normalized Haar harmonics are sqrt(d_j) D^j_{ab}(Q) exp(i q v).
By the ground-state lemma the simple balanced ground space is invariant
under the transfer operator, and near zero twist its analytic
continuation is still one-dimensional.  Denote the transfer scalar on
that space by Lambda_{n_p}(zeta, chi); at zero twist it is ||T_{n_p}||.

    Lemma (finite-circle transfer formula).  For the root branch, with
    fixed finite n_p and W, let k_t be its momenta and t_j its auxiliary
    trace eigenvalue.  Near zero twist

        Lambda_{n_p}(zeta, chi) = lambda^{n_p} sum_{q,j}
            exp( - W E_{qj} + i zeta q )
            ( - 2 )^{n_p} i^{-2 n_p} t_j(-i q)^2
            / prod_{t=1}^{n_p}
                [ (d_j + 1)^2 - (i k_t - q)^2 ]
                [ (d_j - 1)^2 - (i k_t - q)^2 ].

    The sum runs over the admissible rotor sectors, and apparent
    singularities are interpreted by analytic continuation from
    parameters at which coefficient extraction and the Bethe vectors are
    nonsingular.

At an ordinary point of the root branch choose a momentum ordering whose
colour coefficient A in the Bethe wave is nonzero.  The transfer
preserves its eigenline, so the coefficient of exp(i sum_a k_a Y_a) in
the transferred wave is Lambda_{n_p} A, and this coefficient is computed
first at nonresonant parameters.

Near one output coordinate Y let X be the integration coordinate
assigned to it, k the momentum carried by X, and fix the rotor sector
(q,j) on the intervals outside these two insertions.  Put d = 2j + 1
and z = i k - q.  With S_j the spin-j angular momentum, the operators
eta_L = S_j . sigma and eta_R = S_j . sigma act on the left and right
rotor and binary indices respectively and commute.  Extracting the
coefficient of exp(i k Y) obtained by taking Y as the integration
endpoint for X, and summing over the two orders X < Y and X > Y, gives

    - 2 ( z - 2 eta_L )( z - 2 eta_R )
        / ( ((d + 1)^2 - z^2) ((d - 1)^2 - z^2) ).

Both the normalization and the endpoint denominators are checked as
follows.  Suppose first X < Y with no other insertion between them.
Multiplication by the conjugate entry at X changes the intermediate
sector to (q - 1, j +- 1/2), whose energy minus E_{qj} is 1 +- d - q.
On one matrix index the Clebsch-Gordan projections in j (x) 1/2 are

    P_+(eta) = (j + 1 + eta)/d,   P_-(eta) = (j - eta)/d,

and the two matrix indices supply P_+-(eta_L) P_+-(eta_R) with the same
choice of sign, because they belong to the same intermediate rotor
representation.  The normalization of the Haar harmonics supplies the
factor d/(d +- 1): multiplication from a normalized spin-j matrix element
to a normalized spin-(j +- 1/2) matrix element has coefficient
sqrt(d/(d +- 1)) times the two Clebsch-Gordan coefficients, and the
return insertion, being its adjoint, contributes the same factor.  The
dimension factor therefore occurs once in the product of the two
insertions, not once for each matrix index.

For X < Y the input plane times the heat factor is
exp(i k X) exp( -(1 +- d - q)(Y - X) ), whose upper-endpoint
contribution is exp(i k Y)/(1 +- d + z); for X > Y the lower-endpoint
calculation gives denominator 1 +- d - z, and the exterior transpose and
conjugation by the antisymmetric tensor replace eta_L, eta_R by
-eta_L, -eta_R in that contribution.  The full coefficient is therefore

    sum_{s = +-1} d/(d + s)
        { P_s(eta_L) P_s(eta_R)/(1 + s d + z)
          + P_s(-eta_L) P_s(-eta_R)/(1 + s d - z) }.

Substituting the projection formulas and taking a common denominator
gives the displayed coefficient.  If d = 1 the minus representation is
absent and is omitted before simplifying; the result is the cancelled
value, in particular 2/(4 + k^2) when (q,j) = (0,0).

The coefficient of a full output plane exp(i sum_a k_a Y_a) in the
actual heat-kernel integral is extracted next.  Initially choose a
nonsingular Bethe vector and parameters for which all integrated
partial-sum rates are nonzero, no i k_a is a pure rotor-energy
difference, and no i(k_a - k_t) for a != t is such a difference; the
relevant energy differences are even integers, and these conditions can
be met on the branch, since a generic common twist zeta removes
pure-rotor coincidences and zero partial-sum rates, while near the
large-circle end the separated centres and an infinitesimal spin twist
chi resolve the remaining pair-difference resonances.  Here n_p and c = 2
are fixed while the circumference varies, and the passage to all other
parameters is by analytic continuation.

Fix a total ordering of X and Y and expand in rotor harmonics.  An
ordered block x_1 < ... < x_r of input coordinates in a gap (a,b)
between consecutive output coordinates has exponential rates alpha_1,
..., alpha_r, and repeated integration produces terms with endpoint
phases

    exp( a sum_{h=1}^{s} alpha_h + b sum_{h=s+1}^{r} alpha_h ),
    0 <= s <= r,

so a prefix is assigned to the left endpoint and the remaining suffix to
the right, a description that includes the nested integrations whose
intermediate endpoints coincide.  Over all gaps this gives a weakly
order-preserving assignment of inputs to outputs or to the seam
endpoints 0, W.

At an output Y_a the exponent is the sum of the assigned input momenta
multiplied by i plus the adjacent rotor-energy difference; if no input
is assigned there the exponent is purely a rotor-energy difference and
cannot equal i k_a.  Every output therefore receives at least one input,
and since their numbers agree exactly one input is assigned to each
output and none to the seam.  The difference nonresonance then forces
that input to carry k_a and forces its remaining rotor-energy difference
to vanish.

In particular a contributing gap contains at most two inputs, one
assigned to each endpoint.  When two integration variables lie in the
same Y gap and are assigned to its opposite ends, the coefficient is the
product of their two endpoint factors: for exponential rates alpha,
beta, the term assigning x_1 to a and x_2 to b in

    int_a^b exp(beta x_2) int_a^{x_2} exp(alpha x_1) dx_1 dx_2

has coefficient -1/(alpha beta), precisely the lower and upper endpoint
coefficients multiplied together, while the other terms assign the two
variables to a common endpoint and do not contribute to the required
plane.  After every X has been paired with a Y, the vanishing energy
differences show that all intervening intervals carry the same rotor
energy; the charge has already returned to the same q, so equality of
energies forces the same j.  Thus one fixed through sector (q,j)
contributes the ordered product of the local coefficients over the
momenta, followed by its seam trace.

Coefficient extraction also for the infinite rotor sum is justified in
the ordered complex tube 0 < Re Y_1 < ... < Re Y_{n_p} < W.  At fixed
finite n_p intermediate labels differ from the through labels by bounded
steps, so their energies differ from E_{qj} by O_{n_p}(1 + |q| + d_j),
and on each compact subset of this tube the endpoint terms are bounded
by a fixed polynomial in the labels times

    exp( - W E_{qj} + C (1 + |q| + d_j) ).

The quadratic energy gives normal convergence of the analytic exponential
sums, including after any finite imaginary translation.  Modulo
even-integer shifts their exponent vectors have only finitely many types,
and the commuting translations Y_a -> Y_a + i pi multiply an exponential
of rate gamma_a by exp(i pi gamma_a), these multipliers distinguishing the
types, so polynomial interpolation in the translations projects onto each
type.  In the type containing the required plane divide by
exp(i sum_a k_a Y_a); the resulting locally convergent Laurent series in
w_a = exp(2 Y_a), on 1 < |w_1| < ... < |w_{n_p}| < exp(2W), has the
desired plane coefficient as its constant coefficient, and uniqueness of
Laurent coefficients proves that no other term of the infinite sum
changes the extraction.

Finally

    i ( z - 2 eta ) = ( - i q - k ) - 2 i S_j . sigma = L_j(-i q - k),

so the ordered product in each matrix index is the monodromy whose
twisted trace has eigenvalue t_j(-i q), the two indices giving its
square.  Multiplying by lambda^{n_p} and summing the seam weights yields
the transfer formula.  At zero twist this is the top transfer eigenvalue
by the ground branch and the ground-state lemma, and exceptional ring
lengths follow by continuity.

The nontrivial rotor sectors must now be estimated uniformly in the band
of the parity proposition.  The (q,j) = (0,0) term is

    Z_{n_p}(zeta, chi) = prod_{t=1}^{n_p} 2 lambda / ( 4 + k_t(zeta, chi)^2 ),

and the issue is to compare every other term with this product without
accumulating a fixed loss for each of the O(W b^2) particles.

    Lemma (uniform transfer asymptotics).  Fix A, D > 0 and positive
    constants c_-, c_+.  Suppose b -> infinity,

        c_- exp(pi b) b^{-1/2} (log b)^D <= W
            <= c_+ exp(pi b) b^{-1/2} (log b)^D,
        | n_p/W - b^2 | <= A b.

    There is c' > 0, depending only on these fixed constants, such that
    at zero twist the sum over (q,j) != (0,0), divided by Z_{n_p}(0,0),
    is O(exp(-c' W)), uniformly over these particle numbers.  If u_r are
    the real centres of the centre equations at W' = W, then

        log ||T_{n_p}|| = sum_{r=1}^{M} p(u_r) + l f(0) + o(W^{-1}),

    with the error uniform over the same band.

Set the spin twist to zero, initially retaining continuation from
arbitrarily small nonsingular spin twists where needed.  In the auxiliary
trace evaluated at -i q every shifted argument of Q_0 has an odd integral
imaginary part relative to a real formal root: since 2m = 2j = q mod 2,
q + 2m +- 1 is odd, and the same is true of q +- (2j + 1), so these
factors are uniformly separated from zero.

At the formal pair spacing k_{r+-} = u_r +- i the contribution of this
pair to F and its accompanying Q_0 factors in each summand cancels to
(-i q - u_r)^2 + d^2 with d = 2j + 1, independently of m.  In each of the
d^2 summands obtained by squaring t_j the absolute contribution of this
pair relative to its (0,0) contribution is therefore

    (u_r^2 + 1)(u_r^2 + 9)
        / | ( (i u_r - q)^2 - (d + 2)^2 )
            ( (i u_r - q)^2 - (d - 2)^2 ) | <= 1.

To see the inequality factor the denominator into four linear factors,
whose absolute values are sqrt(u_r^2 + w^2) with widths
|q +- (d + 2)| and |q +- (d - 2)|; all are positive odd integers and at
least two are at least three, so their product is at least
(u_r^2 + 1)(u_r^2 + 9).

This comparison remains uniform for the true roots.  The linear factors
just used, and the individual factors before cancellation for the two
pair constituents, stay a fixed distance from zero; the root estimates
make the corrections smaller than every power of W^{-1}; and there are
only polynomially many factors in W in the particle band, so the total
multiplicative change is bounded and in fact differs from one by less
than every prescribed power of W^{-1}.  This is where an estimate per
particle would have been insufficient without the sign-free bound of one
at the formal spacing.

If l = 1 the denominators contributed by the single momentum can vanish
at zero common twist before cancellation, and they are controlled on one
complex zeta circle instead.  Choose a sufficiently large fixed C and
put |zeta| = W^{-C}.  The common twist translates every momentum and
auxiliary root by zeta/W; at zeta = 0 the single momentum is zero up to
an error smaller than every power of W^{-1}, so on this circle every
possibly vanishing single factor has modulus bounded below by a fixed
multiple of W^{-C-1}, and all single-root contributions are bounded by a
fixed power of 1 + W + d + |q|.  The change in all pair factors is bounded
as well, since each argument moves by W^{-C-1} and the number of factors
is polynomial in W, and choosing C once sufficiently large controls
their product.

The maximum-modulus principle is applied on this circle; this requires
analyticity on the whole interior, with no appeal to a lower bound for
the gap between different total-momentum sectors.  Let psi_0 be the
normalized untwisted ground vector, with total momentum zero, and define
the change of gauge

    (U_zeta psi)(X) = exp( (i zeta / W) sum_{t=1}^{n_p} X_t ) psi(X).

After this change of gauge the Hamiltonian on periodic functions is
H_2 + 2 zeta P / W + n_p zeta^2 / W^2, with P = -i sum_t d/dX_t, and in
the P = 0 subspace its ground line is therefore the same simple line for
every zeta.  The twisted transfer T_{n_p}(zeta, 0) has an analytic rotor
expansion, and so does the fixed-gauge scalar

    < psi_0, U_zeta^{-1} T_{n_p}(zeta, 0) U_zeta psi_0 >.

Near zero this scalar equals Lambda_{n_p}(zeta, 0) by commutation, so it
continues Lambda_{n_p} across any crossings with other momentum sectors.
The coefficient formula may first be used with arbitrarily small spin
twists and then continued to zero spin twist, equivalently taking the
removable analytic limits in the transfer formula.

On the zeta circle the ratio of the nontrivial-sector sum to Z_{n_p} is
therefore bounded by

    C sum_{(q,j) != (0,0)} d^2 (1 + W + d + |q|)^K
        exp( - W E_{qj} + W^{-C} |q| ) = O( exp(-c' W) )

for fixed K and some c' > 0.  Every nontrivial admissible sector has
E_{qj} >= 2 and the energies grow quadratically in q, d, so the
polynomial factors and the number of summands are absorbed by the
exponential.  The ratio is analytic on the enclosed disk, since Z_{n_p}
is analytic and nonzero there and the whole nontrivial-sector sum is
Lambda_{n_p} - Z_{n_p}, so the maximum-modulus principle gives the same
bound at zero twist; for l = 0 the separated pair factors already give
the bound directly and the same circle argument also applies.

It remains to take the logarithm of the leading product.  Since
2 lambda = b^2 a formal pair contributes

    b^4 / ( (4 + (u_r + i)^2)(4 + (u_r - i)^2) )
        = b^4 / ( (u_r^2 + 1)(u_r^2 + 9) ) = exp( p(u_r) ),

and the possible zero single contributes b^2/4 = exp(f(0)).  The
cumulative error from replacing the true roots by the centres is smaller
than every power of W^{-1}, and the nontrivial-sector error is
exponentially smaller, so the positive zero-twist eigenvalue has the
stated logarithm.

The transfer asymptotics have now reduced the problem to a sum over real
pair centres.  The dressed energy of the integral equations, with
endpoint B = B_*, expresses this sum as a common bulk term, an explicit
single-particle cost, and a quadrature error.

Recall that epsilon_in = epsilon * 1_{(-B_*, B_*)} is also the positive
part of epsilon.  Let d(x) now denote the globally defined counting
derivative at W' = W, not truncated to its counting interval
(-B_c, B_c).  Summing p = epsilon - K * epsilon_in at the centres and
using the definition of d gives the exact identity

    sum_{r=1}^{M} p(u_r) + l f(0)
        = (W/pi) int_R epsilon_in(x) dx - l m_*
          + [ sum_{r=1}^{M} epsilon(u_r)
              - W int_R epsilon_in(x) d(x) dx ].

Indeed

    W int epsilon_in d = (W/pi) int epsilon_in
        + sum_{r=1}^{M} (K * epsilon_in)(u_r)
        - l ( (K_1 - K_3) * epsilon_in )(0),

and the remaining single term is - l m_* by its definition.

    Lemma (the parity variational error).  Under the parameter
    assumptions of the uniform transfer asymptotics, the bracket is at
    most C/W, uniformly in the particle band.  In each parity there is a
    particle number in a band of the same form for which the bracket is
    at least -C/W; specifically one may take M a nearest integer to
    W int rho_{B_*} and set n_p = 2M + l.

For the upper bound replace each epsilon(u_r) by its positive part and
use the counting coordinate

    s(x) = int_0^x d(y) dy,
    s(u_r) = ( r - (M + 1)/2 )/W,
    s(+- B_c) = +- M/(2W).

The centres are exactly the midpoints of cells of length 1/W in this
coordinate.  The counting-density estimates give d >= c > 0 and
polynomial bounds in b for its fixed-order derivatives on the needed
interval, the integral equation gives such bounds for the fixed-order
derivatives of epsilon, and |epsilon'| <= pi/2 on the whole line, so for
the inverse function x = x(s)

    | d/ds epsilon(x(s)) | = | epsilon'(x(s))/d(x(s)) | <= C,

while each further fixed-order derivative is polynomially bounded in b.

Set g(s) = epsilon_+(x(s)).  On any consecutive collection of full cells
in a smooth portion, with outer endpoints alpha, beta, the composite
midpoint expansion gives

    sum_{these cells} g(s_r) - W int_alpha^beta g(s) ds
        = - ( g'(beta) - g'(alpha) )/(24 W)
          + O( poly(b)/W^2 ).

The first derivatives are uniformly bounded, and the higher derivatives
and interval length cost only polynomial factors in the remainder, so
this error is O(1/W) with a constant independent of b.  At most two cells
meet the cutoffs x = +- B_*; since epsilon vanishes there and its
derivative in s is bounded, those cells contribute O(1/W) as well.
Consequently

    sum_r epsilon(u_r) <= W int_{-B_c}^{B_c} epsilon_+(x) d(x) dx + C/W
        <= W int_R epsilon_in(x) d(x) dx + C/W,

the last inequality by positivity, since any positive portion outside
the counting interval is only omitted on the left-hand side.

For the lower bound choose M as in the statement.  The endpoint
asymptotics and the relation between b and B_* imply
2 int rho_{B_*} = b^2 + O(b), so these particle numbers satisfy the band
condition after enlarging its fixed constant.  Since
int_{-B_c}^{B_c} d = M/W, the counting-density bound, including its
uniform L^1 bound for the impurity correction, yields

    | int rho_{B_c} - int rho_{B_*} | = O(1/W).

The densities increase with their interval, by positive iteration of
their defining equation, so their difference in total mass bounds from
below the mass on the excess or missing interval.  First rho_B >= 1/pi
gives |B_c - B_*| = O(1/W); those intervals then lie within distance one
of the relevant larger endpoint, and the edge bound rho_B >= c sqrt(B)
improves this to |B_c - B_*| <= C/(W sqrt(B_*)).  On this excess or
missing interval the counting density obeys d <= C B_*, by the
counting-density bound and its defining convolution, and
|epsilon(x)| <= C | |x| - B_* |, so replacing the counting interval by
(-B_*, B_*) in the dressed-energy integral costs at most

    C W B_* |B_c - B_*|^2 <= C/W.

The same midpoint estimate, now for the smooth function epsilon on the
counting interval, compares its sum and integral with error O(1/W), and
combining the two bounds proves the lower estimate for the bracket.

The parity proposition now follows.  By its particle-band hypothesis
every maximizing particle number is covered by the uniform upper bound;
after one fixed enlargement of the band constant the choices of the
lemma are covered as well and give the matching lower bounds.  Thus

    log tau_l = (W/pi) int epsilon_in - l m_* + O(1/W),   l = 0, 1.

By the endpoint asymptotics,

    exp(- pi B_*/2) = 4 pi e B_* exp(- pi b) (1 + o(1)),

    m_* ~ 32 sqrt(B_*) exp(- pi b) ~ 32 sqrt(2) b^{1/2} exp(- pi b).

The prescribed size of W gives W m_* ~ (log b)^D -> infinity, and
subtracting the two parity formulas gives

    log tau_0 - log tau_1 = m_* + O(1/W),

hence the parity norm difference log tau_0 - log tau_1 ~ 32 sqrt(2)
b^{1/2} exp(- pi b).

The four matching parameters are chosen only after the exact blocking
estimates are in place, and the present section builds those estimates.

Set

    gamma = (log L)/pi,   H_j = H + gamma j,   h = N - j,
    M_j = ceil( (log H_j)^2 ),   p_j = (log H_j)^{P_0},
    t_j = H_j^{-1/2} p_j,   T_j = H_j^{-0.49}.

The running layers are j = N, N-1, ..., 0; the fixed power P_0 exceeds 20
and finitely many powers arising below, and H is chosen last.  A layer has
periods m_x L^j, m_t L^j with min(m_x, m_t) >= C_adm M_0.  All cutoffs use
the reference value H_j, not a running kinetic coefficient, so that the
cutoffs are common to the two trajectories being compared.

At layer j put R_j = sqrt(beta/H_j), P_j = pi R_j and w = R_j v.  At a
site integrate (q, w) in S^3 x [0, P_j) with measure d omega_3(q) dw.
Assign an integer k_e to each oriented positive lattice edge and set

    u_e = d_e w + P_j k_e,
    nabla_e q = q_y - (-1)^{k_e} eps_e q_x,
    r_e^2 = | nabla_e q |^2 + u_e^2,

with eps_e = -1 on the specified central seam and 1 elsewhere.  Replacing
a site representative by w_x + P_j n_x, q_x -> (-1)^{n_x} q_x and changing
k to keep u fixed is a change of coordinates, and everything below is
invariant under it.  The flat-lift reference is exactly the restriction
dk = 0 on every plaquette; the Brownian density permits dk != 0.  The
lower bound on R_j is uniform although its upper bound need not be.

There are four displayed kinetic coefficients B_x^q, B_t^q, B_x^w, B_t^w,
and the combinations

    B = ( B_x^q B_t^q )^{1/2},   chi = ( B_x^w B_t^w )^{1/2} / B,

with determinant-one shapes (l_q^{-1}, l_q) and (l_w^{-1}, l_w).  The
shapes lie in a fixed small neighbourhood of one, chi in [1/2, 2], and B
in [H_j/2, 2 H_j], slightly enlarged bands being allowed during a single
step.  The symmetries are left and right quaternion multiplication,
common w translation, spatial reflection, time reflection accompanied by
w -> -w, and w -> -w accompanied by complex conjugation; they force an
affine quadratic form to have exactly these four coefficients, a mixed
q-w bilinear being excluded by quaternion conjugation and mixed
spatial-temporal derivatives by spatial reflection.

Let Q be the block average with weights obtained by sampling and
normalizing the product of an even smooth bump supported in the middle of
an L-by-L block, so Q1 = 1 and its first moment is the block centre;
observation precision is one, and the conditional mean operator P_h is
distinguished from the lift period.

For the flat reference the initial scalar precision of shape l is the
nearest-neighbour precision l^{-1} D_x + l D_t.  For the Brownian
regulator it is the Schur precision obtained by minimizing, in each
horizontal strip,

    (1/2) l^{-1} int_0^1 sum_t dot f_t^2 ds
        + (1/2) l int_0^1 sum_t | f_{t+1} - f_t |^2 ds

with prescribed endpoint columns.

    Lemma (free operators for the two initial precisions).  For either
    initial precision and either admissible shape the free block iteration
    has uniformly elliptic, exponentially localized precisions K_h,
    conditional means P_h, conditional covariances C_h, and positive edge
    stacks.  Their required fixed derivatives, including shape
    derivatives, obey bounds on one common complex momentum neighbourhood,
    and for two initial precisions with the same shape their histories
    differ by C L^{-h} in those bounds.  The constants needed to choose L
    are independent of sufficiently large L.

    For a Brownian strip the edge lift consists of the horizontal term
    l^{-1} and a positive matrix on the two endpoint values of each
    vertical edge.  With z the vertical edge Laplacian symbol this matrix
    is

        B_l(z) = l sum_{m>=1} J_m^* ( 1 + l^2 z/(pi m)^2 )^{-1} J_m,
        J_s(c, c') = (1 - s) c + s c',

    where J_m is the m-th normalized sine coefficient of J_s.  It is
    analytic near [0,4] with uniform positive lower and upper bounds
    there, the formula holds also for cut vertical arrays using their
    existing bonds, and the affine precision after strips are summed is
    diag(l^{-1}, l).

Subtract J_s from a strip field and expand its zero-endpoint part in sine
modes; completing the square at eigenvalue (pi m)^2 gives the edge
matrix.  At z = 0 its quadratic form is l int_0^1 |J_s(c,c')|^2 ds, which
is positive for a nonzero endpoint pair.  The resolvent is positive on
[0,4], compactness gives uniform bounds, and the poles stay away from a
common neighbourhood of that interval; summing strips gives the affine
form.

The quantitative free input is the symbol recursion

    K_{h+1}(p)^{-1} = 1 + L^{-2} sum_{l mod L}
        b_Q(k_l) b_Q(k_l)^* K_h(k_l)^{-1},
    k_l = (p + 2 pi l)/L,

with stars continued analytically from real momentum.  The explicit depth
formula has principal numerator m^2 K_0(p/m) with m = L^h, and the
product of bump symbols has nonprincipal shell sums bounded by C A_0^h.
The only estimates required on the initial numerator are even analyticity
on a common strip, bounded derivatives, real ellipticity, and the
specified quadratic jet with a fourth-order remainder, all of which both
precisions have.  In particular, on every nonprincipal alias
p_l = p + 2 pi l,

    | m^2 K_0(p_l/m) | >= c (1 + |l|)^2,
    | m^2 (K_{0,1} - K_{0,2})(p_l/m) | <= C |p_l|^4 / m^2.

Real ellipticity proves the first estimate on the real cube and reducing
the imaginary width preserves it; even Taylor expansion on a larger
analytic cube proves the second.  The reciprocal difference is therefore
O(m^{-2}), summation with the product-bump weights costs C A_0^h, and
L > A_0 gives C L^{-h}.  The principal alias has its direct Taylor
estimate, and the principal denominator stays bounded away from zero on a
common smaller strip, so Cauchy estimates give every fixed derivative,
including the shape derivatives.

The other free conclusions used below are

    C_h = ( K_h + Q^* Q )^{-1},
    K_h P_h = Q^* K_{h+1},   I - Q P_h = K_{h+1},
    P_h 1 = 1,
    P_h(centered affine field) = its affine extension,
    sup_x sum_y exp( c |x/L - y| ) | nabla_f^s P_h(x,y) |
        <= C_s L^{-s},   s = 0, 1, 2,

the covariance having exponentially decaying kernels in block units with
constants allowed to depend on fixed L.

The edge form of the positive completion is as follows.  Choose a common
c_0 > 0 and write A_h = ( sqrt(c_0) I, ell_h )^t, so that
K_h = d^* A_h^* A_h d.  There are operators M_h, N_h with

    M_h^* M_h + N_h^* N_h = I,
    M_h^* ( A_h d f, V - Q f ) = A_{h+1} d' V,
    M_h A_{h+1} d' = ( A_h d P_h, I - Q P_h ),
    N_h A_{h+1} d' = 0.

The part of M_h taking coarse data to fine edge rows has weighted row
bound C/L and column bound C L, its first two fine differences have
bounds C/L^2 and C/L^3, and the coarse parts have bounded row and column
sums, all retaining the history gain.

For our shapes the raw edge lift starts at the shape diagonal rather than
the identity; division of its fourth-order-zero remainder and the curl
addition remain uniformly bounded, the two principal linear jets in the
edge intertwiner carry the same shape factor, and both alias divisions
and their gluing apply, the common real bounds giving positivity.  At
depth zero the specified strip lift is retained without the fine
inverse-curl correction, whose deletion improves the positive-defect
inequality, and its second-stack rows are the square root after removing
c_0/2 on each side of the edge matrix.  Zero rows are added to give common
index sets, and the direct sum of the q and w constructions multiplies all
w edge, observation, and propagated output entries by sqrt(chi).

What is controlled after a block integration is now specified.  In a
region where all increments are small, finitely many polynomial
interactions are displayed and the remaining function has a derivative
norm.

A region containing large increments is represented by a bounded weight
with an exact inventory of those increments, and recording every
argument read by such a weight is essential to the later product
estimates.

The actual energy uses numerical second-stack rows, with stencils
truncated at radius c_L M_j, c_L small enough for all compositions; for
the Brownian edge stack a Chebyshev expansion of the square root is
truncated.  Analyticity gives error C_L exp(-c_L M_j) in operator norm,
also for cut arrays, and bandwidth per polynomial degree gives weighted
row and column errors.  The same-centre coefficient is corrected by the
same exponential amount so that the affine normalization is exact, and
coefficient calculations always use full kernels.  Numerical rows are
needed because a finite-read mask cannot bound an infinite row acting on
unbounded w.

A q stencil is transported along specified coordinate paths and averaged
over the reflection-related choices, its complete read set including
those paths.  A second-stack row centred at e is enabled only if

    r_f <= t_j exp( dist(e,f)/R_* )

for every edge on its numerical stencil and its transport paths, and if
there is no edge with r_f > t_j within a fixed distance D_0 of its
centre; R_* is larger than the localization length, and D_0 is chosen
before L during initialization.  Enabled rows contribute half their
squared norm to E.  Direct rows contribute

    S_t(e) = (c_0/2)( |nabla_e q|^2 + chi u_e^2 )        if r_e <= t,
           = d_0 t ( |nabla_e q|^2 + chi u_e^2 ) / r_e  if r_e > t,

with 0 < d_0 < c_0/16 and t = t_j, the tail being linear in r_e and
integrable in the lifted variable.

Every label has an anchor, its complete read support, and a connected
record of cubes of radius M_j and joining paths.  Its integer load s >= 1
bounds the number of sites by C M_j^2 s and the record length by
C M_j s.  Every spin argument, path, mask, eligibility test, negative
status query, and covariance window is recorded.  A finite-period label
retains its lifted record before folding, intersecting records can be
joined at a fixed multiple of total load, and projection to the next
lattice has load

    4 s / L <= s' <= D_* (1 + s/L).

Unused load may be retained to obtain the lower bound.  A record is
nonwinding if its load is less than a fixed small constant times the
short period divided by M_j; its lift is then injective and its formula
is the bulk formula.  Every other record is called winding, even when its
function is constant or has short support.  The short period is the one
used for rectangles.

For a connected graph X let 1_X = 1{ r_e <= t_j for e in X }.  Local
continuations are taken in fixed sheets on r_e < 4 t_j, and across a
boundary of the chosen fundamental interval the real w coordinate is
continued rather than differentiating its discontinuous reduction.  On a
small-field stencil all local transports agree, since a nonflat plaquette
has an edge with r_e >= P_j/4 while P_j/t_j -> infinity uniformly, and
long paths keep their specified transports.

At an anchor o the relative variables are

    y_o(x) = Im( q_x^{(o)} q_o^{-1} ),
    z_o(x) = sum_{e in specified path o->x} u_e,

with q_x^{(o)} transported to the anchor sheet; a fixed basis of
invariant tensors is used in these variables.  A degree-n polynomial label
has coefficient a and path length u = 1 + sum_{nu=1}^{n} |x_nu - o|_1,
counting multiplicity, or the comparable length of its declared paths,
and coefficient price |a| exp(sigma u)(1 + u)^2 with fixed small sigma >
0.  The norm is the supremum over anchors of the sum of these prices.
The ordered slots are

    BP_3;   BP_4, I_2;   BP_5, I_3;   BP_6, I_4, B^{-1} J_2,

a subscript denoting homogeneous field degree.  A degree-n term with
prefactor B^a has normalized order n - 2a, each relative field counting
as one power of B^{-1/2}.  Every quadratic orbit is compensated by
directional nearest-edge squares, separately in q and w and averaging
opposite directions, so that its Hessian vanishes on every affine field;
compensators retain their provenance and complete read sets and price
their own short path.

A regular error f_X is C^8 on the indicated graph domain, its local norm
[f_X]_{8,j} being the sum of supremum norms of derivatives through order
eight with independent tangent directions satisfying

    |v_x^q|, |v_x^w| <= t_j ( 1 + dist(x,o) )^8,

quaternion variations using left exponential coordinates and w variations
ordinary real variations in the fixed sheet.  Its norm and cap are

    ||f||_{8,j,A} = sup_o sum_{X anchored at o} exp(A s_X) [f_X]_{8,j}
        <= delta_j := H_j^{-2.05},   A = 64.

Every nonwinding error has zero constant term, zero entire first jet, and
zero Hessian on pairs of affine fields; winding errors are exempt, and a
linear w term must be removed explicitly, quaternion symmetry alone not
removing it.

For the nonsmooth part put D(q,w,k) = { e : r_e > t_j }.  A covering
label l has complete support P_l, load s_l, a nonempty inventory J_l of
edges, and a bounded measurable weight k_l vanishing unless J_l is a
subset of D.  Define

    Xi = sum over forests Gamma with the P_l pairwise disjoint and
         the disjoint union of J_l equal to D, of prod_{l in Gamma} k_l,

    sup_x sum_{l : x in P_l} exp(A s_l) ||k_l||_inf
        <= w_j^cap := exp( - p_j^{1/4} ).

Thus every flagged edge is covered exactly once and Xi = 1 when there are
no flags.  The weights may be complex; this is not an optional polymer
gas and need not be positive term by term.

    Definition (retained density).  A retained density is an expression

        exp( i theta sum_{e_t} u_e )
        exp( V |Lambda| - B E - X_can - X_err ) Xi,

    with the four kinetic coefficients in the stated bands and the
    numerical energy E formed from the direct terms and the enabled
    second-stack squares.  Writing P_a for a canonical label including
    its displayed prefactor,

        X_can = sum_a 1_{X_a} P_a,   X_err = sum_X 1_X f_X,

    where X_a is the declared graph of the complete polynomial label
    including compensators and paths.  Each masked term is zero outside
    its graph domain, so no local continuation is evaluated there.  The
    errors and covering sum have the stated norms, each polynomial slot
    has a fixed coefficient cap chosen successively in its displayed
    order, the real number theta is unrestricted, and bulk scalars and
    coefficient extractions are independent of the periods and seam.

All terms obey the listed symmetries with orbit assignments made on
complete labels.  Reference covering weights vanish whenever a plaquette
containing an edge of their inventory is nonflat; Brownian weights
restricted to the condition that some such plaquette is nonflat obey
their covering cap with the additional factor exp(-F_h), F_0 = 0, and
these plaquette tests belong to the complete support.

For the reference the covering convention imposes flatness exactly, since
every nonflat plaquette has an edge with r_e >= P_j/4 > t_j whose
mandatory label vanishes, and elsewhere it makes no change.  The
construction commutes with lattice translation before a seam is fixed; on
a seam rectangle its short formulas use the same seam transports, and
moving the seam is a change of coordinates.  Gaussian chart integration
is equivariant under quaternion actions, and the orbit assignments
preserve both the density and its norms.

When passing from layer j to j - 1 write

    T = R_j / R_{j-1},   g = B^{-1/2},   t = t_j,   t' = t_{j-1},

this scale conversion T being distinct from T_j.  The propagated output
lift in input units is Tw', while output masks and the threshold still
use r'_e, t_{j-1}; the propagated w square therefore has coefficient
chi T^2 even though its denominator and cutoff are in output units.

On a flat block transport the inputs into a common lift.  The w
coordinate is observed by a Gaussian of precision B chi centred at its
Q-mean.  For q put m_Y = Qq(Y) and use

    ( exp( - B |q'_Y - mhat_Y|^2 / 2 ) / z(B) ) d omega_3(q'_Y),

    z(B) = int_{S^3} exp( - B |q - 1|^2 / 2 ) d omega_3(q),

taking mhat_Y = m_Y/|m_Y| if |m_Y| >= 1/2 and otherwise sampling a
constituent with the Q weights as the target; the denominator is

The target of the block observation is a medoid: a minimizer m_Y of
sum_x Q_Y(x) d(m_Y, s_x) over the quotient of S^3 x R by
(q,w) ~ (-q, w + P_j), with product quotient metric

    d_{P_j}((q,w),(q',w'))
        = min_{n in Z} ( |q - (-1)^n q'|^2
                        + |w - w' + P_j n|^2 )^{1/2}

and symmetric randomized ties; both coordinates are then observed with
the preceding noises.  Reducing the observed coordinates to the output
representative convention retains the shifts: two adjacent blocks with
flat union use the coarse-edge integer induced by their common lift, and
otherwise a coarse integer giving least quotient distance between the
output sites is chosen, with symmetric ties, the seam sign included in
both rules.  The construction is a probability kernel, and on a flat
input it decouples exactly into the quaternion observation and a Gaussian
observation on the cover and preserves both winding integers.

    Lemma (observation displacement and phase).  For neighbouring blocks
    Y, Y' and their coarse edge E,

        r'_E <= (C/L) sum_{e subset Y union Y'} r_e
                + C ( |eta_Y| + |eta_Y'| ),

    eta being the observation displacement with its real coordinate
    measured before reduction, with a constant independent of large L and
    of R_j.  In conditional integration at fixed outputs and output
    integers the old phase propagates to theta' = theta L T, and its
    remaining ratio is a product of local modulus-one factors depending
    only on sheet integers, every nonidentity factor requiring curvature
    in a block or an adjacent block pair.

Put A_Y = sum_{x,z in Y} Q_Y(x) Q_Y(z) d(s_x, s_z) with s_x = (q_x, w_x).
The medoid obeys sum_x Q_Y(x) d(m_Y, s_x) <= A_Y, by averaging the same
expression over candidate constituents, and the triangle inequality gives

    d(m_Y, m_Y') <= A_Y + A_Y'
        + sum_{x in Y, z in Y'} Q_Y(x) Q_Y'(z) d(s_x, s_z).

Bounding each distance by a coordinate-path sum of the r_e, and using
smooth bump weights whose total path congestion on an edge is at most
C/L, proves the first term; the argument works before quotienting on a
flat patch.  Normalizing a quaternion mean costs only a fixed factor when
|m_Y| >= 1/2, and on the fallback branch the weighted path bound is
already bounded below by a positive constant.  Adding the two noise
distances finishes the estimate.

For the phase, fix a tree in each fine block.  Site shifts n_x set all
its tree-edge integers to zero, and the remaining integers lie off the
tree.  Align the observation in this lift, using the lifted medoid on a
curved block.  The tree equations fix the differences of the n_x and
leave one root shift, and summation over observation sheets supplies
that shift.  Equivalently, choose the root representative so that the
observed real coordinate lies in the basic interval; conditioning this
coordinate to be T w'_Y unfolds the root, and then all tree coordinates,
over R.  Reducing the fine coordinates reconstructs the original
integers and observation sheet, so this is a bijection whose only output
scaling Jacobian is T per block.

On a flat adjacent pair the residual integers inside both blocks vanish
and all fitted seam integers equal the prescribed coarse integer.  On a
curved pair the least-distance condition reads only the quotient output
and its specified integer, and the tree used inside a curved block cannot
enter a flat-pair rule at that block, so tree choices do not alter the
identity and may be symmetrized identically in both inputs.  Summing
temporal real differences in these tree coordinates telescopes, and with
L crossing fine edges per coarse temporal edge and P_j = T P_{j-1},

    sum_{e_t} u_e - L T sum_{E_t} u'_E
        = P_j { sum_{internal e_t} k_e
                + sum_{E_t} sum_{e crossing E} ( k_e - k'_E ) }.

Internal residual integers vanish on a flat block and every crossing
difference vanishes on a flat pair, so multiplication by i theta gives
the claimed local phase factors, independent of continuous field
variations in fixed sheets.

    Theorem (exact step and comparison).  Choose the constants in the

        alpha + kappa/B + O_L( H_j^{-1.05} ),

    the direction and field indices being understood; the bounded

    For a Brownian input and a flat reference input on common lists their

        U' <= q_* U + C_L L^{-h} + C_L H_j^{-c} |lambda|,
        |lambda' - lambda| <= C_L U + C_L L^{-h}
                              + C_L H_j^{-c} |lambda|.

    The coefficients in lambda' use output units, so the second estimate

        F_{h+1} = F_h + c_L sqrt(beta) p_j,

        4 ( L^2 - 1 ) log g + V_L(l_q, l_w, chi, T, h)

The subsections prove the theorem.  The old phase is already isolated in
curvature regions; a positive energy reserve is first obtained for those
regions and for all large increments, and controls the unbounded real
coordinates and integer sums in a localized Gaussian expansion.  The
joint product estimate for that expansion is stated and then verified for
the retained class.  Connected summation gives the exact output density,
its finite Taylor part gives the canonical map, and a separate argument
contracts the remaining regular errors; the low-jet normalizations,
winding records, and scalar are restored at the end.

For the energy reserve, insert a smooth edge profile equal to one for
r_e <= t/4 and zero for r_e >= t/2, and an analogous noise profile at a
sufficiently large fixed multiple of t.  A selected complement is an
input seed, every true output flag is a mandatory seed, and the list of
selected complements is a fixed primary pattern during each integral; a
configuration produced by subsequently opening other profiles does not
change that pattern.  Seeds whose 50 M_j neighbourhoods meet are joined,
and for each connected component all fine blocks meeting its 3 M_j
neighbourhood are frozen.  The remaining coordinates are the exterior
Gaussian coordinates, and a core is the factor assigned to one such
component together with its frozen integrations and retained tests.

Numerical energy rows within 5 M_j of the seeds are assigned to the core,
stationary squares within 8 M_j, and retained default profiles within
14 M_j, the stationary squares being the residual squares from the
Gaussian completion.  Gaussian affine rows farther than 2.5 M_j from the
seeds are selected, every positive and negative test is included in the
factor that uses it, covariance-window reads include the entire window
and the tests deciding which sites in it are exterior, and determinant
compensations include the corresponding hole neighbourhoods.  A noncore
factor retains a 20 M_j enlargement of its arguments and joining record,
long old paths and determinant chains retain all their endpoints and
windows, and local composition radii are chosen so their sum is less than
M_j/100, the fixed outer margins containing every read.  Freezing whole
blocks adds only O(L) to these radii; hole tests and determinant
neighbourhoods are enlarged by that same margin, H is taken large after
L, and this is a finite change of the recorded support dilation D_*.

The energy estimate behind the seed prescription is algebraic.  Let X be
the input stack applied to (nabla q, u) before masking, X' the output
stack applied to (nabla q', T u'), and Y the observation displacement
with its observation-square weights; all w entries carry sqrt(chi).  Let
zeta have direct output rows clipped by min(1, t'/r'_E) and the enabled
second-stack output rows, and for the numerically truncated operators
put (nu, omega) = M_s zeta.  Then

    |zeta| + |omega| <= C t,    |nu| <= C t / L,

row by row uniformly in the volume, the subscript s denoting numerical
stencils.  With a row's direct and second entries understood, the input
energy plus observation energy minus output energy is the sum of

    F_e = S_t(e) + (1/2) m_e |X_{2,e}|^2 - nu_e . X_e + (1/2)|nu_e|^2,
    B_Y = (1/2) |Y_Y - omega_Y|^2,
    C_E = zeta_E . X'_E - (1/2)|zeta_E|^2 - S_{t'}(E)
              - (1/2) m'_E |X'_{2,E}|^2,
    U_E = zeta_E . [ M_s^*(X,Y) - X' ]_E,
    N_i^row = (1/2) | (N_s zeta)_i |^2,
    Z_E = (1/2) zeta_E . [ ( I - M_s^* M_s - N_s^* N_s ) zeta ]_E,

where m_e, m'_E are the second-stack masks.  Cancelling the cross terms
separates the direct, observation, and output reserves in F, B, C from
the defects in U, Z; the N^row term is nonnegative; and the identity uses
the actual transposes and products of the chosen matrices, so it holds
even when the transport has holonomy.  Observation rows are transported
through their block-tree lift and other rows through their declared
paths, signs leaving the absolute exponential envelopes unchanged, and
free differential identities are used only when the entire stencil,
including intervening blocks and pair constraints, has a common flat
lift.

    Lemma (reserve for flagged and curved regions).  After the affine
    Gaussian squares have been selected and subtracted, each core with
    s_c seeds retains energy at least c_L t^2 s_c, and, in addition, an
    unspent fixed fraction of

        c t sum_{input flags} r_e
            + c t' sum_{output flags} r'_E
            + c sum_{noise seeds} |eta_Y|^2,

    the energy being multiplied by B in the density.  The estimate holds
    on the retained tests and on the closed supports of the profile
    derivatives used below, and a core containing curvature retains at
    least c t P_j before multiplication by B.

The direct F row pays c t^2 at an edge seed and c t r_e at a true input
flag, and its cross term has coefficient C t/L.  A failed second-stack
mask is charged to one witness r_e > t; a witness hits at most
C(1 + log(r_e/t))^2 <= C' r_e/t rows, the extra D_0 exclusion only
changing this constant, so exponential column sums bound the total loss
by (C t/L) sum_{r_e > t} r_e.  Large observation displacements have
their square reserve once the fixed noise threshold is increased, and the
direct C row pays c t' r'_E on an output flag and is zero otherwise, its
second-stack part cancelling.

On a flat stencil the free identities give the quaternion defect bound

    C_L t exp(-c_L M_j)
        + C t sum_Y exp(-c dist(E,Y)) | m_Y - mhat_Y |.

The real-coordinate identity is linear and exact before truncation.
After truncation, subtract a common real constant on the enlarged
stencil; differences of coordinate values cost at most its length times
the largest increment or noise there, so the error is

    C_L M_j^D t exp(-c_L M_j)
        ( 1 + max_{enlarged stencil} ( r_e, |eta_Y| ) ).

This estimates coefficients of finite stencils, not remote unbounded
values: a maximum above the default threshold lies in the same core and
is paid by its direct or noise reserve.  For the quaternion mean the
block path estimate gives (C/L) sum r_e, improving to C_L T_j^2 on blocks
with chords below T_j, and every other block has a witness above T_j to
which the C t/L charge is assigned.

A stencil that sees curvature, where a free differential identity is
unavailable, has a Z row costing C_L t^2.  For U, use the absolute
coefficient envelopes of the ledger.  The fine part of M_s has row sum
C/L and the input stack has bounded column sums, so summing curved rows
gives a fine-input charge

    (C t / L) sum_e a_e r_e,

where the nonnegative incidence weights have bounded column
multiplicity.  The observation and coarse-stack column sums similarly
give C t sum_E a'_E r'_E + C t sum_Y a_Y |eta_Y|, and substituting the
displacement bound, each neighbouring block pair uses an input edge only
a bounded number of times, so the coarse charge again gives
(C t/L) sum r_e plus noise terms with the L^{-1} constants independent
of L.  Large increments are paid by the direct rows and large noises by
their squares, and all remaining increments, noises, and Z rows cost at
most C_L M_j^D t^2 per curvature witness.  Such a witness has
r_e >= P_j/4, and the ratio of this cost to t P_j is C_L M_j^D t/P_j,
which tends to zero; output curvature also forces fine curvature in the
enlarged stencil, since the pair-lift rules carry a flat neighbourhood to
a flat neighbourhood, including across a four-block corner.

Choose L to spend less than a fixed fraction of the direct reserve on

    t M_j^D << 1,    M_j^D ( T_j^2/t + exp(-c_L M_j)/t ) << 1.

The abstract Gaussian estimate is imported.  If |Q_alpha| <= e_alpha
U_alpha with U_alpha >= 1 then

    | exp(Q_alpha) - 1 | <= sqrt(e_alpha) exp(2 sqrt(e_alpha) U_alpha).

summable and small for large H.  Exact factorization for disjoint

an unspecified Gaussian law.

At the fine scale, cut the flat reference into two half cylinders and
choose lifts in each half; their transverse integer windings must agree.
Along either separating seam fix an origin and express each boundary
site's sheet by cumulative tangential integers.  Flatness then says every
cross-link integer is the difference of these boundary sheet
displacements plus one common integer, and the common integers on the two
seams can be summed independently.  In the lifted variables the kernel
for one seam is consequently a product
Gaussian kernel and Wilson q-kernel, summed over a common deck shift.
Each unsummed kernel is positive semidefinite: the Gaussian is positive
definite, and exp(beta q . Q) is positive definite by its power series,
and the common-shift sum preserves this property.  Indeed let g be
simultaneous translation by one sheet, including its sign on q, and let
K be the unsummed kernel.  For a finite set of boundary configurations
z_i the matrices

    (1/(2M+1)) sum_{a,b=-M}^{M} K( g^a z_i, g^b z_j )

are Gram matrices, and simultaneous-shift invariance and Gaussian decay
show that their limit is sum_{n in Z} K(z_i, g^n z_j).  The restrictions
to the flat halves and their equal transverse winding are diagonal
restrictions on these positive forms, tangential directions are chosen
the same in the two halves, normal integers transform with their
orientation, and this proves seam reflection positivity of the untwisted
flat reference.

A minus-center twist inserts the isometry q -> -q in the seam kernel, so
Cauchy-Schwarz bounds a twisted pairing by the geometric mean of its two
untwisted doubled-half pairings, and the twist can be moved to any
desired seam by a change of representatives.  These statements pass
through the exact observations: a half-observable uses only internal
output sites and integers, its fine data and observation noises lie in
that same half, the observation kernels are reflection equivariant on
the lifts, and cross-link output integers are not read by such an
observable and sum out by normalization, so the fine reflections can be
taken at the block seams corresponding to the terminal reflections.

Selecting a compatible macrolabel family A, with combined support S_A and

remaining label avoids all changed sites and incident integer data, so its

macrolabel product difference marks one of its original labels, at cost
d_l, while all other label factors cost b_l.  The deterministic forest
encoding has original labels and exact region site sets as vertices:
internal edges describe each macrolabel, and a second edge type joins a
later macrolabel to an intersecting one in the preceding generation.
Root each component at its marked label.  No region is repeated, because
it is consumed at first use, and no original label is repeated, because
its fixed nonempty inventory becomes ineligible once consumed.  The
forest retains the generations, the marked choices, and every evaluation
configuration, so the same counting applies with the present
incident-integer convention.  The common label list includes labels whose
evaluated weight is zero, all spin-dependent eligibility tests staying in
the evaluated weights, and for a fixed encoded term the set Q in the
integrated-gain lemma is consequently defined from the original geometry
and inventories before the fillings are sampled.

That lemma assigns a region of n sites weight exp(-c' p_0^2 n/d^2).  On
the rectangular torus the number of connected site animals of size n
through a given site is at most C^n, with a constant independent of
either period.  Let R = C_L M_0^2 be a fixed support-to-load bound, so
that |P_l| <= R s_l.  For H large,

    sup_x sum_{E containing x} exp(-c' p_0^2 |E|/d^2) exp(2|E|) < 1/(16R),
    sup_x sum_{l : x in P_l} b_l exp(2 s_l) < 1/(16R).

Summing over an intersection site and the two edge types bounds the
one-child sum at a vertex of size s by s/4, including an exponential
allowance for descendants, and induction on tree depth bounds all
descendants by e^s.  A marked root consequently costs at most
d_l exp(2 s_l).  Dropping disjointness between the resulting rooted trees
and summing unordered nonempty root families gives

    sum_{j >= 1} (1/j!) ( sum_l d_l exp(2 s_l) )^j,

which is the claimed bound.  The support-hit bounds justify passing from
finite to countable label lists.  Positivity has been used only for
complete flat-reference sums at the filled configurations, never for a
restricted signed sum, and a common multiplier of modulus at most one can
be removed in this absolute estimate, proving the last assertion as well.

The partition comparison now follows.  Apply the integrated flat
comparison with the Brownian covering sum on F.  By the terminal-distance
estimate the sum in the comparison bound is at most C_H V exp(-c_H beta),
and the scalar-stripped exponents have the same supremum discrepancy.
The direct unbounded rows agree exactly at the matched terminal
couplings, and all remaining exponent differences are bounded, so the
integrated estimate also permits replacing the Brownian exponent by the
reference exponent.  Since V is polynomial in beta this gives an error
O(exp(-c beta)) Z_{F,+}^end after decreasing c, and adding the nonflat
estimate finishes the partition comparison.

Simultaneously insert the Brownian terminal phase in both flat integrals.
Its modulus is one, with no bound required on its coefficient.  On the
reference side exact transport of winding through the observations
identifies it with the initial multiplier exp(i phi sum d_t v) for the
corresponding real phi.  Reassemble the exact observations and remove each
regulator's accumulated bulk scalar.  The two scalar coefficients need not
agree, but each is independent of the periods and the time seam, so the
same coefficient for each regulator is used for both seams and for time
lengths m_t and 2m_t.  The resulting scalar-stripped partition functions
satisfy the partition comparison needed downstream.

The Brownian spectral calculation requires control of the particle

the change in the untwisted pressure when the logarithm of the fugacity is

    Lemma (pressure comparison at nearby fugacities).  Fix the blocking
    factor L and the admission constants as above.  Let (b, r) be the
    matched Brownian parameters, with b = beta + O_L(1) and
    r = 1 + O_L(1/b).  Consider the periodic rectangle with initial side
    lengths

        X = L^N m_x,   n = L^N m_t,
        m_x ~ beta (log beta)^2,   m_t ~ sqrt(beta) (log beta)^4,

    the terminal lengths dyadic and satisfying the admission conditions.
    Write V = m_x m_t and V_site = X n = L^{2N} V.  For sigma in {-1,1}
    set

        b' = b exp( sigma / (2b) ),   r' = r b' / b,

    so the fugacity b^2/2 changes by log(b'^2/b^2) = sigma/b while
    r'/b' = r/b; in particular the horizontal heat time per initial
    interval, the width W = X r/b, and the rectangle volume are unchanged.
    Then for all sufficiently large beta the actual untwisted Brownian
    partition functions satisfy

        log Z^B(b', r') - log Z^B(b, r)
            <= V_site [ b' r' - b r + O_L(1/b) ],

    the error uniform in sigma and in the indicated rectangle lengths,
    with fixed constants in the two asymptotic conditions, and the
    assertion also holding with m_t replaced by 2 m_t.

First obtain the lower bound at the matched parameters, including the
phase.  In the initial variables the flat reference is the product of a
Wilson integral and a Gaussian lift, summed over their compatible
windings.  If (k_x, k_t) in Z^2 is the winding pair, the harmonic part of
the Gaussian energy is

    (pi^2/2) ( c_x (m_t/m_x) k_x^2 + c_t (m_x/m_t) k_t^2 ),
        c_x, c_t = beta + O_L(1),

and the Gaussian fluctuation integral is the same in every winding
sector.  The corresponding Wilson integral has the centre twists
prescribed by (k_x, k_t) and is at most its untwisted value: expanding the
Wilson bonds in powers, after spin integration the coefficients with no
seam signs are nonnegative, and adding a centre seam only inserts signs.

Let A_0 denote the positive zero-winding contribution to the untwisted
reference partition function.  Since the phase has modulus one and is
identically one at zero winding, the total absolute contribution of all
other windings is at most eps_beta A_0, where

    eps_beta <= sum_{(k_x,k_t) not (0,0)}
        exp{ -c beta ( (m_t/m_x) k_x^2 + (m_x/m_t) k_t^2 ) } = o(1),

because beta times the smaller of m_t/m_x and m_x/m_t tends to infinity
for the chosen rectangles.  Pairing opposite windings makes the
phase-weighted reference integral real, so it is at least
(1 - eps_beta) A_0, whereas the reference integral with phase zero is at
most (1 + eps_beta) A_0, and these comparisons are unchanged when the
common reference volume scalar is removed.  In particular the
phase-weighted reference retains, up to a fixed factor, the aligned-cap
lower bound; the integrated comparison transfers that lower bound to the
matched Brownian integral after its volume scalars are removed, so at the
terminal layer that integral is at least

    c P exp( -C_L V log H ),

with P = pi R_0 the terminal lift period.

For the upper bound at (b', r') only a short initial part of the
renormalization is needed.  Initialize both runs with the same fixed R_N
and perform s = ceil(log b / log L) = O_L(log b) steps, so s is much less
than N.  At forward step h <= s the depth index is j = N - h and H_j is
comparable to b.  The initial displacement in the displayed couplings is
bounded and each coefficient step, with the prescribed coordinate
rescalings, costs at most C_L, so the displacement and the accumulated
corrections are at most C_L(1 + h) and the admission bands have room for
this initial segment at both parameter values.

After s steps the layer contains L^{-2s} V_site sites.  The spanning-tree
integration bound and the class caps, with the phase replaced by its
absolute value, bound the remaining integral by

    P_{N-s} exp( C_L L^{-2s} V_site log b ),

and the choice of s gives L^{-2s} log b <= C_L/b.  The logarithm of this
integral therefore costs O_L(1/b) per initial site, and the single factor
P_{N-s} has the same property: |log P_j| = O_L(log b) throughout the
trajectory while V_site = L^{2N} V grows exponentially in beta.

It remains to compare the extracted scalars on this short segment.  Let
S_s(b, r) denote their sum per initial site, including initialization and
the first s steps; a scalar extracted per output site at forward step h
carries weight L^{-2(h+1)} in this sum.  By the initial scalar formula the
initial scalar difference is b' r' - b r + O_L(1/b).  Here the changes in
log g and in the bounded Lipschitz initialization term are O_L(1/b), and
the two scalar remainders have that size as well.

and the two scalar remainders have that size as well.  On the subsequent
short segment the bounds H_j comparable to b and C_L(1 + h) on
coefficient drift show that the shape, log g, and chi arguments in the
scalar formula differ by at most C_L(1 + h)/b, while the rescaling
schedule and the type of free history are the same in the two Brownian
runs.  The Lipschitz bound for the displayed terms, together with the
absolute O_L(1/H_j) bounds on its two remainders, consequently gives

    S_s(b',r') - S_s(b,r)
        = b'r' - br + O_L(1/b) ( 1 + sum_{h=0}^{s-1} (1+h) L^{-2(h+1)} )
        = b'r' - br + O_L(1/b).

For the matched run the scalars extracted after these s steps also have
total absolute size O_L(1/b) per initial site, since the scalar formula
bounds each by C_L log b per output site and their remaining volume
weights sum to O_L(L^{-2s}).  The terminal lower bound has logarithm
bounded below by log(cP) - C_L V log H, whose negative part divided by
V_site is again O_L(1/b).  Therefore

    log Z^B(b',r') / V_site <= S_s(b',r') + O_L(1/b),
    log Z^B(b,r)  / V_site >= S_s(b,r)  - O_L(1/b),

and subtracting the scalar comparison proves the pressure bound.  The
argument uses the full matched trajectory only for the lower bound; the
displaced parameters need just the admitted short segment.

The comparison supplies parameters b = beta + O(1) and
r = 1 + O(beta^{-1}).  An additive constant in beta - b changes the mass
prefactor, so boundedness of that difference is not enough; its limit is
determined by measuring the response to a small quaternion twist.  The
finite Taylor maps show that the responses of the matched models agree
through their constant terms, and a direct Gaussian calculation evaluates
their difference in the microscopic variables.

Let e be a unit imaginary quaternion.  On a square with n initial cells
in each direction impose the boundary condition

    q_{x + n e_i} = q_x exp( zeta e ),   i in {x, t},

with periodic boundary conditions in the other direction, the twist
acting only on the quaternion field.  In the Brownian model the physical
horizontal circumference is n r/b; the square terminology refers to the
initial cell coordinates.  Write Z_i^M(n; zeta) for the resulting
partition function in model M in {F, B} and define its helicity by

    h_i^M(n) = - (d^2 / d zeta^2) log Z_i^M(n; zeta) at zeta = 0.

For F use the flat-lift model without its phase.  Left and right
quaternion twists have the same helicity, since inversion of the
quaternion field interchanges the two conventions and preserves the
microscopic weights.

    Proposition (helicity matching).  Fix the blocking factor L and the
    admission constants.  For every sufficiently large beta, choose any
    matched trajectory satisfying the shooting conclusion, with its
    parameters b, r, c_x, c_t.  Then for i = x, t

        lim_{m to infinity} limsup_{s to infinity}
            limsup_{beta to infinity}
            | h_i^B(m L^s) - h_i^F(m L^s) | = 0,

    where m tends to infinity through sufficiently large dyadic integers
    and the inner limit is taken with m and s fixed.

Fix m, s and put n = m L^s.  For sufficiently large beta the trajectory
has at least s layers.  After s observation steps the period is m and the
depth index of the original trajectory is N - s.  We prove

    h_i^M(m L^s) = B_{i,N-s}^{q,M} + C_i^M(m,s; beta)
        + O( C_L m^D exp(-c m) ) + o_{beta;m,s}(1),   M in {F, B}.

Here C_i^M(m,s; beta) is the Gaussian correction from the normalized
order-two logarithmic expansion at period m, computed from the current
canonical coefficients, shapes, and full free kernel; it includes the
contribution of two order-one vertices.  Its beta dependence is through
those current data, and no convergence as beta tends to infinity is
assumed.  The leading coupling must be identified exactly; the
constant-order correction and the displayed winding error are then
controlled uniformly in s, whereas the last remainder need only tend to
zero for fixed m and s.

Insert s normalized observation kernels at the actual parameters of the
matched trajectories, choosing the observation blocks inside the
fundamental cell.  Near an aligned configuration use the same equivariant
formulas as in the renormalization construction; away from this
neighbourhood complete them by any local equivariant probability kernels,
which need not depend on zeta.  For B observe in the block lift near
alignment and then reduce to the quotient; for F first omit nonzero
circle winding and work in its real lift.  At fixed n these omitted
sectors and their first two quaternion-twist derivatives have relative
size O_n( beta^C exp(-c_n beta) ): harmonic circle winding has a
positive cost of order beta, and differentiating the finitely many
twisted bonds costs only a polynomial in beta.  Their contribution to the
helicity is thus o(1), and the normalized observation insertions leave
the retained partition functions unchanged.

Right twisting preserves the common left action on the quaternion field,
so a last-layer quaternion spin may be pinned, and the common circle
translation may also be pinned.  More explicitly, subtract one output
circle coordinate from every lifted coordinate: integration of the
remaining common translation over one period contributes exactly that
period, independently of the other coordinates, and this remains true if
it was originally one microscopic circle value required to lie in a
fundamental domain.  The extracted phase is identically one on
zero-winding configurations, since sum d_t v = 0, and therefore
contributes no helicity term.

The fixed-volume expansion and its identification with the bulk Taylor
maps come next.  After the two symmetries have been removed the aligned
saddle at zeta = 0 is unique and its transverse quadratic form is strictly
positive.  Count the angle zeta at the same scale as the chart fields,
namely g of order beta^{-1/2}.  At fixed n and s the Wilson expansion is
an ordinary Laplace expansion, and for the Brownian expansion the
endpoint and path estimates used in the initialization give the same
conclusion: restrict endpoint bond distances to g times a fixed power of
log(1/g) and the path-size variables of the initialization to a
corresponding logarithmic bound, and the endpoint and bridge tail
estimates, together with the vertical endpoint-bond energy bound, make the
complement smaller than any prescribed power of g.  They remain valid
after two derivatives in the scaled twist, by the endpoint-translation
estimate.  Integer lifts in the horizontal heat kernel are summed using
its Gaussian bounds, and observation kernels likewise localize their
variables in the aligned charts.  The relevant Taylor coefficients are
therefore Gaussian polynomial moments.

Writing eta = zeta/g, expand the logarithm through normalized order two.
At fixed n and s its remainder and its first two eta derivatives at zero
are o(g^2), uniformly for the actual shape and coordinate-scale parameters
in their compact bands.  Since

    h_i^M(n) = - g^{-2} (d^2 / d eta^2) log Z_i^M(n; g eta) at eta = 0,

this remainder contributes o(1) to the unscaled helicity.  For fixed s all
observation widths are comparable to g.

To identify these coefficients, complete the Gaussians using full kernels
rather than their numerical truncations.  Extend each background field
across the boundary with the prescribed right twist.  Ambient linear
kernels commute with this continuation, because right multiplication is
orthogonal on R^4, and their compositions and adjoints fold with the same
transport.  If T_x^q is the coarse reference field and
q_x = exp(xi_x) T_x^q, then xi_x is ordinary periodic: both q_x and T_x^q
acquire the same right multiplier on crossing a period, which cancels in
q_x (T_x^q)^{-1}.  The affine row in the chart expansion therefore has the
usual folded matrix on xi and a background term that is also ordinary
periodic, so the leading conditional Gaussian precision in the joint
expansion, with both the twist and chart fields counted at scale g, is the
ordinary folded precision; higher-order background and twist dependence
remains among the Taylor vertices.  The same argument applies to
horizontal seam strips in the initialization, where endpoint transport
leaves the left tangent logarithms and bridge variables periodic.

Consequently every Wick contraction without a winding obstruction has the
bulk formula, evaluated on the fields lifted along its contraction paths.
Removing the anchored quaternion rotation cancels the right transports,
and the higher-order determinant and Jacobian terms remain among the
Taylor vertices.  The logarithmic determinant coefficients are sums over
closed contraction paths and obey the same identification.  Repeated
transports on a path contribute at most a fixed power of its length to
the twist jets under consideration, so the exponential path norms still
sum absolutely, and linear circle terms telescope in this saddle
calculation.

There is one leading-order point for which an exponentially small
estimate would be insufficient.  At harmonic order the first jet of a
twisted field is an affine slope plus an ordinary periodic field, and the
full free kernels preserve affine fields.  For every folded free edge
matrix, translation invariance makes the cross term between a constant
slope and periodic gradients exactly zero.  One can see this also at the
last layer by using a linear ramp that vanishes at the pinned site: its
sum with an ordinary
periodic field describes every first jet with the given seam increment.
The affine Hessian normalization therefore gives, exactly, the current
directional coupling B_i^q as the leading unscaled helicity on the
square.  The leading determinant is independent of the angle, and the
strip kernels have the same property, so no term of the form beta times
an exponentially small winding error is discarded.  The coupling resets,
including those at initialization, allow the actual running couplings to
be used: at fixed s the remaining coefficient error is o(1) and
multiplies a chart quadratic.

The constant-order terms must now be controlled uniformly as the number
of formal steps grows.  At an output period m' = m L^j, a discrepancy
between a finite-period Wick coefficient and its folded bulk value
requires contraction paths to travel a distance at least c m'.  The path
estimates proved for both initializations give a bound C_L exp(-c m') per
anchor, twist derivatives introducing only fixed path powers, and a
previously generated discrepancy grows by at most C_L in one subsequent
formal step.  This is estimated in the total absolute polynomial
coefficient norm, including the number of anchors at the layer where the
discrepancy was generated: prediction and Wick substitutions have bounded
row moments, and a new vertex attached to an old discrepancy is summed
with the same intervertex covariance bound.  The total angle is another
field variable here, its occurrences in predictions costing path moments
rather than the period, and products of discrepancies satisfy the same
polynomial estimates.

Thus all winding contributions through normalized order two, including
changes of coupling convention, are bounded by a fixed polynomial times

    sum_{j >= 0} C_L^{j+1} exp( -c m L^j ).

At the final period the pinned Gaussian moments grow at most polynomially
in m, by uniform ellipticity, so enlarging a fixed exponent D gives the
bound C_L m^D exp(-c m) for their contribution to the constant-order
helicity, uniformly in s after the inner limit.  Normalized order-one
polynomials have odd total degree in the scaled angle and Gaussian
fields, hence zero direct second derivative after Gaussian integration,
and their propagation into order two is already included in the bound,
which also covers winding paths whose contractions leave a function of
only a few sites.

This proves the helicity decomposition: earlier bulk scalar terms have no
angle dependence, and the remaining final-period Gaussian correction is
C_i^M(m,s; beta).  After separating the leading coupling the normalized
coefficient slots have fixed caps and the shapes lie in compact bands, and
at fixed m pinning removes the kernel of the uniformly elliptic free
precision.  The Gaussian moments and convergent path sums defining the
order-two logarithmic correction are therefore Lipschitz in these data,
with a constant depending on m but independent of s.  By the shooting
bound the directional coupling discrepancies and canonical coefficient
discrepancies at this layer are O(rho^s), and the free-history estimates
add a term tending to zero with s.  Subtracting the decomposition for F
and B, first letting beta tend to infinity at fixed m and s and then
letting s tend to infinity, leaves a bound C_L m^D exp(-c m) that tends to
zero with m.  This proves the helicity matching.  The fixed-volume
expansions are legitimate even when n is smaller than a numerical cutoff
required for the exact retained-density representation, since only the
full-kernel formal coefficients are used here.

The constant terms compared above are now computed.  Put

    D_j(k) = 4 sin^2(k_j/2),   D(k) = D_x(k) + D_t(k),
    D_*(k) = k_x^2 + D_t(k).

For the square of side n, write int_{W,n} for the normalized momentum sum
n^{-2} sum_k with both momenta in one period of (2 pi/n) Z, and int_{B,n}
for the corresponding sum with k_t in one period and k_x in (2 pi/n) Z
unrestricted; in both cases the single zero mode (0,0) is omitted.

    Lemma (Gaussian helicity formulas).  For each fixed even n, along the
    matched parameters as beta tends to infinity,

        h_i^F(n) = beta - (3/2) int_{W,n} D_i/D
                   - 4 int_{W,n} sin^2 k_i / D^2 + o(1),   i = x, t,

    and

        h_x^B(n) = b/r - 4 int_{B,n} k_x^2 / D_*^2 + o(1),
        h_t^B(n) = b r - 2 int_{B,n} D_t/D_*
                   - 4 int_{B,n} ( sin^2 k_t - D_t^2/4 ) / D_*^2 + o(1).

    The errors tend to zero with n fixed, and in the correction terms the
    shape parameter has already been replaced by its limit r = 1.

Distribute the twist uniformly over the links in its direction, using
left multiplication by the inversion symmetry.  In the Wilson model write
the imaginary part of a spin, relative to its aligned value, as
beta^{-1/2} pi with pi having three real components; the leading Gaussian
covariance of each component is D^{-1}.  The second derivative of the bond
factor contributes its scalar product, whose expectation gives

    beta - (3/2) int_{W,n} D_i/D.

The first derivative contains the chosen component of
Im( q_{x+e_i} q_x^{-1} ).  Its linear term telescopes on summing over
links, and its quadratic term, up to an irrelevant sign, is
pi_x cross pi_{x+e_i}.  Choosing the first current component, put

    S_i = sum_x ( pi_x^2 pi_{x+e_i}^3 - pi_x^3 pi_{x+e_i}^2 )
        = sum_x pi_x^2 ( pi_{x+e_i}^3 - pi_{x-e_i}^3 ).

Wick contraction yields

    n^{-2} E S_i^2 = 4 int_{W,n} sin^2 k_i / D^2,

and subtracting this current variance proves the Wilson formula.  Every
quantity in the calculation is unchanged by adding a common field
constant, so pinning one spin or using the mean-zero propagator gives the
same answer, and nonzero circle windings in F are exponentially small at
fixed n and do not alter the formula.

For B, the Brownian quadratic energy gives four independent tangent
fields at scale b^{-1/2}: three quaternion components pi and the circle
component nu.  At r = 1 their covariance is D_*^{-1}.  The vertical scalar
product is multiplied by exp(i Delta_t v), so all four fields contribute
to its quadratic correction, giving

    b r - 2 int_{B,n} D_t/D_*.

The linear circle term telescopes.  The quadratic vertical current now has
the form S_t + i T_t, with the horizontal coordinate continuous,

    S_t = sum_{u=0}^{n-1} int_0^n ( pi^2(x,u) pi^3(x,u+1)
                - pi^3(x,u) pi^2(x,u+1) ) dx,
    T_t = sum_{u=0}^{n-1} int_0^n (Delta_t nu)(x,u) (Delta_t pi^1)(x,u) dx.

The components occurring in S_t and T_t are independent, so

    n^{-2} E S_t T_t = 0,
    n^{-2} E T_t^2 = int_{B,n} D_t^2 / D_*^2.

The derivative of the logarithm of a complex weight uses the ordinary
bilinear second moment, with no complex conjugation, so the i T_t term
contributes the negative of this last quantity to the current variance;
together with the Wilson current calculation this is exactly the vertical
Brownian formula.

For the horizontal response, rotate each Brownian path steadily on the
left.  The deterministic-rotation identity for the heat measure, in
Stratonovich convention, gives the constant quadratic stiffness b/r.  Its
first derivative is the integrated current int Im(dq q^{-1}) in direction
e, multiplied by b/r and by the angle-per-period factor, and the linear
tangent term telescopes.  The remaining Gaussian current is

    sum_t int_0^n ( pi^2 d pi^3 - pi^3 d pi^2 ),

whose variance divided by n^2 is 4 int_{B,n} k_x^2 / D_*^2.

For completeness this last identity is obtained without treating a
Brownian derivative as a pointwise field.  Interpolate the paths on a
dyadic polygonal mesh of spacing ell = n/N', including all strip
endpoints, and let N' tend to infinity.  The leading fields consist of
independent Gaussian bridges with the deterministic interpolation and
Gaussian C^1 additions from the strip completion, so polygonal
Stratonovich approximation converges with every fixed moment and there are
no jumps at the finitely many joins.  The point covariance has an
absolutely summable horizontal Fourier series, and sampling replaces its
coefficient at -N'/2 < j <= N'/2 by the sum of coefficients at j + u N'.
The antisymmetric polygonal area has Wick multiplier

    4 sin^2( 2 pi j / N' ) / ell^2.

At fixed n the added covariance aliases are O_n(N'^{-2}) uniformly in j,
the multiplier is O_n(j^2), and the original covariance is
O_n((1 + |j|)^{-2}), so summing the alias errors gives a quantity tending
to zero while the remaining sums converge to the displayed Fourier
variance.  Subtracting a constant for pinning does not change the area.
Finally, since r tends to 1, continuity of each fixed-n correction permits
replacing r by 1 there while retaining b/r and b r in the leading terms.

    Proposition (the finite coupling shift).  For every choice of matched
    parameters satisfying the shooting conclusion,

    beta - b = 1/4 - (1 + log 2)/(2 pi) + o(1)   as beta tends to infinity.

Average the two directional formulas of the microscopic helicity lemma and
compare them by the helicity matching.  The leading Brownian average is

    (b/2)( r + 1/r ) = b + o(1),

since r - 1 = O(beta^{-1}).  The identities sin^2 k_i = D_i - D_i^2/4
express the difference of the constant terms as paired momentum sums, and
these must be paired before passing to integrals, since each propagator
sum diverges as n tends to infinity.  On their common momentum region

    1/D_* - 1/D = ( D_x - k_x^2 )/( D_* D ) = O(1)   as k tends to 0,

the other resulting integrands are locally bounded, and the Brownian tails
in k_x are absolutely and uniformly summable, so Riemann convergence
applies to the paired expression.

To respect the order of the limits, start with any subsequence along which
the bounded difference beta - b converges, first take its beta limit at
fixed m and s, and then let s and m tend to infinity.  This determines that
limit point from the paired integrals.  Equivalently,

    beta - b = 3/4 - int_B D_t/D_* + int_B D_t^2/D_*^2
        - (1/2) int_W ( D_x^2 + D_t^2 )/D^2
        - 2 ( int_B 1/D_* - int_W 1/D ) + o(1),

where int_W means (2 pi)^{-2} times integration over [-pi,pi]^2 and int_B
the same normalization over R times [-pi,pi], the propagator difference
being taken with a common neighbourhood of the origin deleted before its
size tends to zero.  In the finite sums the constant 3/4 is replaced by
3(1 - n^{-2})/4, which has the same limit.  It remains to evaluate the
constants.

Set d = 2|sin(k_t/2)| and let < . > denote a vertical angular average.
Horizontal integration gives

    int_R dk_x / ( 2 pi ( k_x^2 + d^2 ) ) = 1/(2d),
    int_R dk_x / ( 2 pi ( k_x^2 + d^2 )^2 ) = 1/(4 d^3),

so that

    int_B D_t/D_* = < d/2 > = 2/pi,
    int_B D_t^2/D_*^2 = < d/4 > = 1/pi.

By symmetry the lattice term with coefficient 1/2 equals
int_W D_t^2/D^2, and the one-dimensional lattice propagator and its
derivative yield

    int_W D_t^2/D^2 = < d(d^2 + 2)/(d^2 + 4)^{3/2} >
        = (1/pi) int_0^1 ( 3 - 2u^2 )/( 2 - u^2 )^{3/2} du
        = 1/2 - 1/(2 pi),

an antiderivative for the last equality being
2 arcsin(u/sqrt2) - u/(2 sqrt(2 - u^2)).

The propagator difference is

    < 1/(2d) - 1/( d sqrt(d^2 + 4) ) >.

To evaluate it define

    I(z) = int_0^{pi/2} ( 1 - ( 1 + z sin^2 y )^{-1/2} )/sin y dy,  z >= 0.

Differentiation under the integral gives I'(z) = 1/(2(1+z)) and I(0) = 0,
so the required difference is I(1)/(2 pi) = log 2/(4 pi).  Substitution
gives

    3/4 - 2/pi + 1/pi - ( 1/2 - 1/(2 pi) ) - log 2/(2 pi)
        = 1/4 - (1 + log 2)/(2 pi),

and every limit point is this number.

The comparison is now applied to rectangles whose odd traces are small but
still much larger than the comparison error.  The additional circle field
has a massless Gaussian contribution; time doubling removes its extensive
part and leaves an error small enough to recover the Brownian transfer
norms, and a nearby-fugacity estimate then verifies the particle-number
hypothesis.  Fix the admitted depth N chosen for the Wilson coupling beta
and put a = L^{-N}.  Choose dyadic terminal lengths

    w ~ beta (log beta)^2,   t ~ sqrt(beta) (log beta)^4,
    X = w/a,   n = t/a,   W = r X / b,

all fine periods being even integers, and also use the time length 2t.
The short terminal period tends to infinity and hence eventually exceeds
the fixed admission, stencil, and tile sizes.  The comparisons needed are
summarized by

    t = o(beta),   t = o(w),   beta w/t >> t,
    beta t/w -> infinity,   w/t = o(t);

for example (beta w/t)/t is comparable to beta/(log beta)^6 and w/t^2 to
(log beta)^{-6}.

Write Z^q_{eps,eta}(t,w) for the Wilson partition function, where eps and
eta specify the time and space centre seams, + being periodic and - the
minus-centre twist.  With periodic space define

    Z_l^q(t) = (1/2)( Z^q_{+,+}(t,w) + (-1)^l Z^q_{-,+}(t,w) ),
        l = 0, 1.

These are the even and odd row-transfer traces, because the time-centre
seam inserts total spin inversion, and Z_l^{F(phi)} and Z_l^B are defined
by the same time-seam half-sum and half-difference.

    Lemma (Wilson trace asymptotics).  For these rectangles, uniformly
    along the matched trajectories,

        log( Z_1^q(t)/Z_0^q(t) ) = -(t/a) m_lat(beta) + o(t),
        2 log Z_0^q(t) - log Z_0^q(2t) = o(1),

    the first formula also holding with t replaced by 2t.

Let U(w) be the row transfer normalized by its top eigenvalue and let
E(w) = -a^{-1} log || U(w) restricted to Omega^perp ||.  The cylinder-rate
proposition gives

    E(w) = m_lat(beta)/a + O(w^{-1/4}),

with m_lat(beta)/a comparable to 1, and by the odd-detector lemma the
largest nonvacuum eigenvalue is attained in odd parity.  Write
s(u,w) = Tr U(w)^{u/a} - 1.  The long-rectangle estimate, together with
the rectangle identities, gives exponentially small trace tails at u = t
and 2t, and also at a dyadic t_0 comparable to t^eps for any fixed
sufficiently small eps > 0, their prefactors growing at most polynomially
in the present w and t.

After dividing by the top eigenvalue to the power n, the odd trace is at
least exp(-t E(w)) and at most

    s(t_0,w) exp( -(t - t_0) E(w) ) <= exp( -(t - t_0) E(w) ),

so its logarithm is -t E(w) + o(t).  The normalized even trace is 1 + o(1),
since it contains the vacuum and its remaining terms are bounded by
s(t,w).  These estimates, also at 2t, prove the trace asymptotics, and the
vacuum eigenvalue cancels in the time-doubling expression.

Let Z_G(t) be the zero-winding Gaussian circle integral in F on the
n by X rectangle.  Its value is independent of the quaternion field, and
the phase exp(i phi sum d_t v) is one in this sector.

    Lemma (the circle contribution).  For l = 0, 1, at both time lengths
    t and 2t,

        Z_l^{F(phi)}(t) = Z_G(t) Z_l^q(t) (1 + o(1)),

    and moreover

        2 log Z_G(t) - log Z_G(2t) = o(t).

The circle integral is the product of its zero-winding Gaussian integral
and a sum of harmonic winding weights.  A temporal winding has Gaussian
cost at least c beta (w/t) times its winding number squared, and a spatial
winding at least c beta (t/w) times its winding number squared.  Each
winding also inserts the corresponding centre signs in the quaternion
boundary conditions, and the temporal winding may carry the phase of
modulus one.

First estimate the effect of a spatial quaternion twist.  Expanding all
Wilson bonds in powers gives nonnegative integrated coefficients before
seam signs are inserted, so for either time seam

    | Z^q_{eps,+} - Z^q_{eps,-} | <= Z^q_{+,+} - Z^q_{+,-}.

Applying the row-trace estimate along the other lattice axis bounds the
right-hand side by C exp(-cw) Z^q_{+,+}, which also bounds the change in
either time-parity combination.

By the Wilson trace asymptotics, Z_1^q/Z_0^q >= exp(-Ct) for a fixed C and
all large beta, so the total contribution of nonzero temporal winding is
negligible even relative to the odd trace, since

    exp( Ct - c beta w/t ) -> 0.

For spatial winding use the preceding twist estimate instead of requiring
its Gaussian cost to dominate t; the sum of its nonzero relative Gaussian
weights tends to zero because beta t/w tends to infinity.  Replacing each
spatially twisted quaternion partition by the untwisted one costs,
relative to either parity trace, at most a constant times
exp(Ct - cw), which also tends to zero.  Summing the Gaussian winding
tails proves the circle-contribution factorization, the same comparisons
holding at 2t.  This argument controls the odd half-difference itself,
rather than subtracting two partition estimates whose errors might exceed
that difference.

For the time-doubling identity use the initial circle variable of period
ell_v = pi and its kinetic coefficients c_x, c_t comparable to beta, whose
ratio is bounded above and below.  Up to a scalar exponential proportional
to nX, the zero-winding Gaussian integral is

    Z_G(t) = ell_v sqrt(nX) (2 pi/c_t)^{(nX-1)/2}
                / sqrt( det'( D_t + (c_x/c_t) D_x ) ),

the prime omitting the constant mode.  Factor the determinant over
horizontal momenta p_x in (2 pi/X) Z in one period.  With

    2 cosh gamma(p_x) = 2 + (c_x/c_t) D_x(p_x),

the elementary product over time momenta gives

    sqrt( det'( D_t + (c_x/c_t) D_x ) )
        = n prod_{p_x not 0} 2 sinh( n gamma(p_x)/2 ).

In 2 log Z_G(t) - log Z_G(2t) all terms linear in n cancel.  The product
terms log( 1 - exp(-n gamma(p_x)) ) have total absolute size O(1 + X/n):
use gamma(p_x) >= c|p_x| for centred momenta and the integrability of
|log(1 - exp(-u))| on (0, infinity).  The remaining n, X prefactor is
sqrt(X/n), so it contributes only a logarithm of the aspect ratio,
together with O(log beta).  Since X/n = w/t = o(t), these terms prove the
time-doubling identity.

The integrated comparison has an absolute error exp(-c beta) relative to
the untwisted flat reference.  Because t = o(beta) this is negligible
relative to the odd expression, and the volume scalars are independent of
the centre seam and proportional to the rectangle area, so they cancel
both in parity ratios and in time doubling.  Combining the three
estimates therefore gives

    log( Z_1^B(t)/Z_0^B(t) ) = -(t/a) m_lat(beta) + o(t),
    2 log Z_l^B(t) - log Z_l^B(2t) = o(t),   l = 0, 1.

For l = 1, add twice the log-ratio relation at t minus the same relation
at 2t to the even-parity doubling relation: the terms linear in the mass
cancel, giving the asserted odd-parity relation.

The elementary spectral fact that makes these estimates useful allows the
number of appreciable eigenvalues to depend on the parameters.

    Lemma (trace doubling and the norm).  Let A be a nonzero positive
    compact operator, suppose A^n is trace class for a positive integer n,
    and write tau = ||A||.  Then

        0 <= log( Tr A^n / tau^n )
            <= 2 log Tr A^n - log Tr A^{2n}.

    The same conclusion holds for a direct sum of positive operators
    whenever the indicated total trace is finite.

Let x_j be the eigenvalues of A/tau, with multiplicity.  They lie in [0,1]
and at least one equals 1, so 1 <= sum_j x_j^{2n} <= sum_j x_j^n, and
taking logarithms gives the claim.  For a direct sum, finiteness of the
positive total trace makes it compact and ensures that its nonzero norm is
attained, and the same argument applies.

The Brownian operators are positive and have finite total trace in each
parity.  Let tau_l be the maximum of their norms over particle numbers of
parity l; both parities have nonzero trace.  The trace-doubling lemma and
the trace estimates give

    log Z_l^B(t) = n log tau_l + o(t),   l = 0, 1,

and subtracting, with n = t/a,

    log tau_0 - log tau_1 = m_lat(beta) + o(a).

The error is o(a) because the preceding errors were o(t) before division
by n, which is the precision required for the exponentially small lattice
mass.

    Lemma (location of maximizing particle numbers).  For either parity,
    every particle number j attaining tau_l on the stated width satisfies

        j/W = b^2 + O(b),

    with a constant uniform as beta tends to infinity.

Change the fugacity by exp(+-1/b), choosing b', r' so that

    (b'/b)^2 = exp(+-1/b),   r'/b' = r/b.

The heat time and the width remain fixed.  Homogeneity in fugacity
multiplies the sector-j transfer by exp(+-j/b), so the numerator untwisted
trace in the pressure estimate is at least tau_l^n exp(+-nj/b).  On the
other hand the total trace at the matched parameter is at most
tau_l^n exp(Ct) for either l.  Hence

    +- nj/b <= Xn ( b'r' - b r + O(1/b) ) + O(t),

and since b'r' = br exp(+-1/b), division by n gives, for the two signs,

    j/b <= X( r + O(1/b) ) + O(a),
    -j/b <= X( -r + O(1/b) ) + O(a).

Using X = Wb/r and r comparable to 1, these are the required two-sided
bounds j/W = b^2 + O(b).  The argument uses only the pressure estimate at
the displaced fugacity; it requires no matched trajectory at that
parameter.

The depth calibration gives a comparable to sqrt(beta) exp(-pi beta).
Since b = beta + O(1) and r = 1 + O(1/beta), the width satisfies

    W = r w/(a b) ~ exp(pi b) b^{-1/2} (log b)^2.

Thus the width hypothesis of the Brownian mass formula holds, with
logarithmic exponent 2, and the particle-density lemma verifies its
particle-number hypothesis for every maximizing sector in both parities.
Applying that formula to the norm comparison gives

    m_lat(beta) = 32 sqrt2 b^{1/2} exp(-pi b) (1 + o(1)) + o(a).

Multiplication by exp(pi beta)/sqrt(beta) turns the last error into o(1),
by the depth calibration, and the finite coupling shift supplies the
remaining factor:

    lim_{beta to infinity} exp(pi beta)/sqrt(beta) m_lat(beta)
        = 32 sqrt2 exp( pi/4 - (1 + log 2)/2 )
        = 32 exp( pi/4 - 1/2 ).

The quantity entering the trace asymptotics and the norm comparison is the
full Osterwalder-Schrader transfer gap identified earlier, including the
rotation-invariant sectors.  The asserted asymptotic therefore has the
full-gap meaning of the main theorem.  This completes the proof.
