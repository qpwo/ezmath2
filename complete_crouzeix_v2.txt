It is compact, being the continuous image of the unit sphere, and convex: the two-dimensional case is due to Toeplitz (1918), the general case follows by compressing to the span of two unit vectors, and Hausdorff (1919) completed the argument. Crouzeix's 2004 conjecture states that every polynomial p and every A satisfy |p(A)|le2max_{W(A)}|p|; his 2007 paper proves the universal bound 11.08. Von Neumann's (1929) polynomial inequality for contractions, |p(T)|lemax_{|z|le1}|p(z)| for every contraction T and every polynomial p, is the prototype: the closed unit disk is a complete 1-spectral set for the shift on the disk. Crouzeix's conjecture replaces the unit disk by the numerical range. Delyon and Delyon (1999) introduced a positive double-layer integral representation, expressing a matrix function as a boundary average against a positive density on the boundary of an enclosing domain. Crouzeix and Palencia (2017) obtained the bound 1+√(2) through a conjugate-data Cauchy transform, and Ransford and Schwenninger (2018) simplified the final step. Scalar constant-two proofs are due to Jin (2026), to Lorist and Schwenninger (2026), and to Luo (2026); AA hag, Czy{.{z}}, and Virtanen (2026) prove the constant-two estimate for base matrices of order at most three at arbitrary coefficient size. The matrix-valued case at every finite base dimension, and the resulting operator case on arbitrary complex Hilbert spaces, are proved below.
