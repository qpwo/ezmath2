STRONG DIFFEOMORPHIC APPROXIMATION IN THREE DIMENSIONS FOR p > 2
=================================================================




PREFACE
-------

What this document claims
.........................

Take any homeomorphism f from one bounded three-dimensional region
(Omega) to another (Lambda), and suppose f has finite p-energy for
some p > 2. The paper proves that smooth invertible maps
(diffeomorphisms) from Omega onto exactly the same target Lambda can
be chosen arbitrarily close to f in the strong Sobolev sense W^{1,p}.

A homeomorphism is a continuous bijection with continuous inverse.
A diffeomorphism is a homeomorphism that is infinitely differentiable
with an infinitely differentiable inverse. The Sobolev norm W^{1,p}
measures both the map and its weak derivative, and "strong
approximation" means convergence in that norm: not just the points
f_j(x) approach f(x), but the derivatives Df_j(x) approach Df(x) as
well, in an averaged L^p sense.

The problem goes back to conversations of John Ball (nonlinear
elasticity) and Craig Evans. Iwaniec, Kovalev, and Onninen solved the
planar case (every 1 < p < infinity) in 2010-2011. In three
dimensions the range 1 <= p <= 2 was solved in companion work. The
present document treats every p > 2 in three dimensions.








1. THE PROBLEM
---------------------------------

Imagine a piece of rubber filling a region Omega of ordinary
three-dimensional space. You deform it -- twist, stretch, bend --
into a different region Lambda. Each point x of Omega moves to a new
position f(x) in Lambda. The map f is continuous, one-to-one, and
onto; its inverse is continuous too. Such an f is called a
homeomorphism.

Continuity forbids tearing. Injectivity forbids splitting or
collapsing distinct points together. Surjectivity says every target
point is filled exactly once.

Homeomorphisms can be very rough. The example

                f(x, y, z) = (x, y, z + eps*sin(1/z))

is a homeomorphism near the origin (the z-direction is monotone) but
is not differentiable there. That is a "folding", and many of the
homeomorphisms we care about have similar folds.

To attach a number to a deformation, look at its derivative. At an
interior point x where f is differentiable, Df(x) is a 3x3 matrix
describing how an infinitesimal cube around x stretches and rotates.
Write |Df(x)| for the Frobenius norm (square root of the sum of
squared entries). Define the p-energy

                E_p(f) = integral_Omega |Df(x)|^p dx.

When this is finite, we say f belongs to the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(Omega; R^3). On a bounded domain a continuous f is
automatically p-integrable, so the only real content is
integrability of the derivative.

The question answered here:

        Suppose f : Omega -> Lambda is a homeomorphism of finite
        p-energy, with p > 2. Can smooth diffeomorphisms
        f_j : Omega -> Lambda be chosen so that f_j -> f in the
        W^{1,p} sense?

The short answer is yes (the Main Theorem below).


2. THE MAIN THEOREM
-------------------

Throughout, Omega and Lambda are bounded domains (connected open
nonempty subsets) of R^3. For a 3x3 matrix A,

                |A| = ( sum_{i,j} A_{ij}^2 )^{1/2}.

For a Sobolev map f in W^{1,p}(Omega; R^3), Df denotes the
distributional derivative: a 3x3 matrix of L^p functions on Omega,
defined almost everywhere.

Main Theorem. Let Omega and Lambda be bounded domains in R^3, let
2 < p < infinity, and let f : Omega -> Lambda be a homeomorphism in
W^{1,p}(Omega; R^3). Then there exist C-infinity diffeomorphisms
f_j : Omega -> Lambda, each in W^{1,p}(Omega; R^3), such that

        integral_Omega ( |f_j - f|^p + |Df_j - Df|^p ) dx  ->  0

as j -> infinity. Equivalently: f_j -> f strongly in W^{1,p}, and
every f_j maps Omega onto exactly the fixed target Lambda.

Comments:

  * No boundary regularity of f is assumed.
  * No Sobolev regularity of the inverse f^{-1} is assumed. Each
    f_j has a smooth inverse (that is qualitative); convergence of
    (f_j)^{-1} to f^{-1} in W^{1,p} is NOT claimed.
  * The Jacobian det Df may vanish or change sign on sets of
    positive measure.
  * The complementary exponent range 1 <= p <= 2, and everything in
    two dimensions, is treated elsewhere. No theorem from that range
    is used here.
  * The proof is entirely in the forward Sobolev norm; nothing in
    the argument needs the inverse to be in W^{1,p}.



3. WHY THE PROBLEM IS HARD
--------------------------

Smoothing a single function in W^{1,p} is routine: convolve with a
mollifier, pass to a limit. Smoothing a map while keeping it both
locally invertible and surjective onto a fixed target is a different
matter. Three distinct obstructions arise, and the full paper is
really a bookkeeping device for controlling all three at once.

3.1. Local injectivity, broken at creases and vertices
.......................................................

Take a piecewise-affine (PL) homeomorphism h. Let h_eps = h * m_eps
be the mollified version. For very small eps, h_eps is close to h
uniformly with small Lipschitz error, but it need not be injective.

The obstruction appears at a vertex where several affine tetrahedra
meet. Suppose v is a common vertex and h restricts to affine maps
A_i on the tetrahedra T_i touching v. For h to be a homeomorphism
near v, the local gradients A_i must all be invertible and their
images A_i(T_i - v) must tile the target cone around h(v) without
overlap. That is a global constraint on the A_i's, not implied by
each being invertible.

Smoothing averages. At a point very near v, the derivative Dh_eps
is approximately a weighted average of the A_i's. Averages of
invertible matrices need not be invertible: for instance

        A = diag(1, 1, 1),  B = diag(-1, -1, 1)

both have determinant +1, but (A + B)/2 = diag(0, 0, 1) is singular.
So naively smoothed maps can develop a degenerate derivative at a
collapsed vertex and therefore fail to be local diffeomorphisms
there.

The fix in the paper (Section 6, "Strong smoothing") is careful
gluing: preimages of the folded image spheres are repaired by
Schoenflies (in dimension 3) and each vertex is smoothed by a
homogeneous model whose family of derivatives stays inside GL^+.

3.2. Global surjectivity: local injectivity does not give onto
.............................................................

Even a locally injective smooth map Omega -> R^3 has an image that
might not fill the target Lambda. A locally injective map that misses
a point of Lambda is still locally a homeomorphism onto its image;
degree theory is what rules this out.

The "degree argument" is exactly this: a proper, locally injective
map of constant positive orientation has topological degree
determined by the image near infinity. For f itself, if f is a
homeomorphism and Lambda is connected, then f has degree 1 (with
suitable orientation), and any f_j sufficiently close to f has the
same degree 1. Any proper, orientation-preserving, locally injective
map with degree 1 is a homeomorphism onto its image. As long as f_j
cannot escape Lambda (that is, we can ensure |f_j(x) - f(x)| is
bounded by half the distance from f(x) to the complement of Lambda),
the image of f_j is forced to be exactly Lambda.

The tricky part: the corresponding bound (1/2) dist(f(x), Lambda^c)
goes to zero as f(x) approaches the boundary of Lambda. So we need
POINTWISE closeness to f, with a boundary-aware envelope, not just
uniform closeness. This is part of the fine-tolerance machinery
(Lemma 6.4, "Fine control, properness, and the target").

3.3. Rank deficiency: the deepest obstruction
.............................................

Away from a null set, Df(x) is a 3x3 matrix. At every such point
its rank is 1, 2, or 3. Write R_k for the set of points where
rank Df = k.

  * Rank 3 (regular): the local affine approximation is invertible,
    so a small cube can be replaced exactly by its affine
    approximation. This is the "easy" region.

  * Rank 1 and 2 (deficient): Df collapses at least one direction.
    At these points f locally maps a small ball to a lower-
    dimensional image. Its image has zero volume. Two bad things:

        (i) Ordinary smoothing cannot raise the rank. If Dh has
            rank 2 on a set, the mollified Dh_eps has derivative
            whose dominant term is rank 2, so h_eps is still not
            a local diffeomorphism near the set. The convergence
            of the mollified map h_eps -> h in W^{1,p} is fine,
            but a smoothed function with rank 2 somewhere is not a
            diffeomorphism.

        (ii) The Sobolev trace on rank 1 and rank 2 points can
             carry non-null p-energy, even though the target image
             carries null volume. So we cannot discard these
             points by declaring them "exceptional"; we have to
             retain them and change the map in a way that preserves
             their energy.

The construction in the paper retains rank 1 and 2 data, flattens
it onto separated target plates for a controlled change, and only
later uses topological tricks (the carrier, quantizer, walls) to
realize the correct derivatives along the retained data.

3.4. The strategy, in one paragraph
....................................

Because all three obstructions must be handled simultaneously, the
proof is a multistage surgery on f. The plan, in plain language:

  (a) Reduce to locally bi-Lipschitz. At points of full rank,
      replace f by an affine approximation on a small cube. This
      preserves the strong energy, and it ensures the approximant
      has a genuine derivative wherever rank is 3.

  (b) Handle rank 1 and rank 2 by flattening. At deficient-rank
      points, the corresponding target image is a plate (a surface)
      or a curve (an arc). Smooth maps cannot realize these as
      homeomorphisms, so the paper flattens the data onto
      separated target plates, keeping the source energy close to
      the original via a scalar estimate.

  (c) Reduce the target to a two-complex. A convex quantizer
      T : Lambda -> Lambda collapses neighborhoods of retained
      plates onto specific two-faces of a polyhedral diagram,
      leaving the rest of the target structure manageable.

  (d) Route realization via walls. Families of annular walls and
      disk walls are placed in the target exterior to route the
      retained coordinates back through the carrier. Guards pin
      the routed labels. This is the "wall argument".

  (e) Recover the exact gradient and smooth. Sharp estimates on
      small source patches recover the derivative of q_0 = T f_b
      up to the required Sobolev accuracy. Uniform-convexity
      (the Clarkson inequality) then turns near-sharp energy
      control into strong gradient accuracy. Finally, use the
      topology-plus-smoothing theorem (Sections 6 and 12) to
      produce C-infinity approximants onto the fixed target.

Section 4 gives a more detailed look at each stage. Sections 6-12
carry out the construction.


4. THE PROOF, IN FIVE STAGES
----------------------------

The proof produces the approximants in five layers of construction.
Each layer is a real piece of work (a full section in the original
paper), and each has an input and an output. The numbered list
below is a guided tour; the actual constructions are in
Sections 6 through 11.

The build-up is cumulative. Stage k produces an object (a map,
a mesh, a family of walls) that Stage k+1 needs. The final stage
puts all the pieces together.


4.1. Stage A: prepare retained data and build a carrier (Section 7)
..................................................................

Input. The homeomorphism f itself, together with its p-energy
E = integral |Df|^p, and the small error parameter eps.

Problem. f might be wild (rank-deficient on sets of positive
measure, non-Lipschitz, etc.). We cannot manipulate f directly
without losing the strong W^{1,p} convergence.

Output. A continuous, proper, onto carrier map

        F_b : Omega -> Lambda

with the following properties:

  (i)  F_b belongs to W^{1,p}_loc(Omega; R^3).
  (ii) F_b equals f on a compact set K_c of retained rank-1 and
       rank-2 data (up to small change in f already made by
       flattening). The retained set carries almost all the
       rank-deficient energy: E over the discarded part < eps.
  (iii) Away from K_c, F_b is locally affine or piecewise-affine
       (PL) on the interior of an open target set O subset Lambda.
       The set O is a "big and well-shaped" subset: every compact
       Q subset Lambda satisfies volume(Q minus O) < a_Q where a_Q is
       any prescribed positive number.
  (iv) The complement of O in Lambda is allowed to contain some
       "exceptional" balls. Inside each such ball, F_b may collapse
       a small source ball to a single point. The set of collapsed
       balls is countable, they have null asymptotic diameter, and
       they can be "opened" (turned into homeomorphisms) at the
       end of the construction, at negligible energy cost.

Key idea. The trick is to build up F_b in two rounds.

  Round 1. Enclose the retained rank-1 and rank-2 data of f, and its
  exceptional set, in small cubes. Inside each cube, cut off f by a
  PL homeomorphism from the cube boundary onto the cube image, with
  only finitely many "tame ball" fibers. The carrier now has a
  countable family of collapsed balls, but it is a proper W^{1,p}
  map onto Lambda.

  Round 2. On the remaining bulk (the complement of the round-1
  cubes), implant exact affine or PL cores: on small cubes
  of Lambda, replace the input map by a fixed affine or PL map,
  matching the boundary. Any energy excess is controlled by choosing
  the cubes small. After all implants, the "exact region" O of
  Lambda is a locally finite union of affine/PL core images with
  volume(Q minus O) < a_Q for every compact Q, and the map is locally
  exactly that affine/PL map there.

The flattening construction is what controls rank-1 and rank-2.
At those points, the image ∂-measure is null but the source energy
is not: so we cannot simply cut the source out. Instead:

  (a) Pass a small Lipschitz map S_0 : Lambda -> Lambda over the
      target. On retained rank-1 and rank-2 data, this map flattens
      the image onto a family of nearly-flat plates, in the sense
      that |D(S_0 o f) - Df| is small on the retained set. The
      flattening only drastically changes the map where the source
      differential collapses a direction, and only within small
      boxes so that the plate images stay disjoint.
  (b) Place "protected endpoint rectangles" on both sides of each
      plate. These become exact regions for the carrier, on which
      the map will later be replaced by the specific affine/PL map
      of the quantizer.

At the end of Stage A we have a proper, onto, W^{1,p} carrier F_b
that equals a Lipschitz flattening of f on almost all of the
retained data, is affine/PL on a big open target set O, and has
only countably many tame ball fibers to be opened later.


4.2. Stage B: reduce the target to a two-complex (Section 8)
...........................................................

Input. The carrier F_b of Stage A.

Problem. F_b is not yet a homeomorphism (it has folded balls), and
even if we open those balls, its differential on the retained data
is not the same as Df. We need a machine that turns "F_b restricted
to retained data" back into a map with the right derivative.

Output. A Lipschitz map T : Lambda -> Lambda, defined by

        T(y) = (∂G)^{-1}(y)

where G : R^3 -> R is a specific convex function built from a finite
family of "support" affine functions, and (∂G)^{-1} denotes the
inverse of the subdifferential map.

Properties:

  (i)  T is a proper onto map of Lambda to itself, is 1/c-Lipschitz
       for a universal c > 0, and is C^infinity outside a locally
       finite union of closed convex polyhedral sets.
  (ii) The image T(F_b(retained data)) consists of points in a
       specific family of "true two-faces" of the diagram of G.
       Each such face has a natural "radial-perimeter" coordinate
       system derived from a strongly convex potential.
  (iii) The derivative D(T o F_b) on retained data satisfies a
       quantitative relation to Df. In particular, the singular
       values of D(T o F_b) at rank-1 and rank-2 points are close
       to those of Df (up to the small smoothing error of Stage A).
  (iv) T can be perturbed into a strict quantizer T_eta, whose
       derivative converges to that of T in W^{1,p}_loc, and where
       the pushforward of volume under T_eta o h is absolutely
       continuous with respect to dimensional measure on strata.

Key idea. The convex potential G is built from a lower convex
envelope of a specific psi = (|x|^2)/2 + small perturbing bumps.
Its Legendre transform F(q) = sup_x (q.x - psi(x)) is the
Fenchel-Legendre conjugate. The key fact is that every point of
Lambda admits a unique "maximizing contact" from pi_1, and this
contact structure produces a polyhedral diagram in the target.

The "protected endpoint rectangles" from Stage A become faces of
this diagram, and the retained data of F_b are pushed by T onto
those faces. The two scalar coordinates of the retained data --
log-radius and arc-length perimeter on the face -- become the
dominant degrees of freedom. The derivative of D(TF_b) on retained
data is essentially the two parameters times a controlled scalar
factor, so if we can control the two parameters, we control the
gradient.

At the end of Stage B, the retained data has been "quasi-realized"
as points on specific 2-faces of T o F_b, but the two coordinates
of those points are not yet pinned. Pinning is Stage C.


4.3. Stage C: wall barriers realize the parameters (Section 9)
..............................................................

Input. The image T(F_b(retained data)) in the target.

Problem. The image sits on the correct 2-faces but its two
coordinates are only controlled up to the small smoothing error of
Stage A. We need a homeomorphism h : Omega -> Lambda, close to F_b,
that realizes specific parameter values at specific preimages.

Output. A locally bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism

        h : M_x -> M_y

from an "exterior" M_x subset Omega to an "exterior" M_y subset
Lambda (both with boundary), which:

  (i)  sends every "occupied" routed layer at parameter lambda
       to the standard layer at the correct label p_j(lambda);
  (ii) is as finely close on physical labels to the carrier q_* =
       T h_* as prescribed.

Key idea. The exterior target M_y carries two families of proper
walls:

  * A(D, t): annuli, one for each 2-face D and each logarithmic
    radial level t;
  * B(e, s): meridians, one for each 1-face e and each perimeter
    level s.

The two families are "standard" (they come from the polyhedral
diagram geometry), and the intersection theory of a general
transverse A and B wall is completely understood: each incident
pair has exactly one spanning intersection arc, and otherwise only
circles. This is proved by handle-body theory (a small
combinatorial/topological lemma).

Routing. For an arbitrary "pre-stack" (a continuous thickening of
A and B walls with parameter coordinate), we route the walls
through each other by a "diagonal substitution":

        (u, v) -> (u, v + (w_j - u))

in a circle intersection. The key facts are that (a) every
non-root face of the routed surface has exactly one incoming
predecessor, so it is a tree; (b) each parameter interval is
conserved exactly, because every jump is a partial isometry of
length; and (c) the routed surface has the same boundary, homology,
and intersection behavior as the standard.

The route-control step ("wall barrier") is the quantitative
heart. Guards: a locally finite family of paths (each from a net
point in Omega to a target ancore) that are guaranteed, for
structural reasons, to lie at a fixed positive distance from any
good routed wall. If a guard's target label is not realized
correctly, we find a path from it to a distant wall crossing many
spheres (Lemma 9.8, "Spherical path estimate"), each of which
costs fixed G-energy. This forces a lower bound on integral of G^p
over a small chart, independent of routing decisions. If we have
already fixed an upper bound (a capacity) on that integral
(Section 10's Lemma 10.11), we have a contradiction.

So: (fixed capacities) + (wall barrier) => (label pinning).

At the end of Stage C, we have a locally bi-Lipschitz h that is
close to q_* in the label sense, whose differential we do not
directly estimate but whose parameters we have pinned.


4.4. Stage D: calibrated meshes and sharp gradient recovery (Section 10)
........................................................................

Input. The routed homeomorphism h of Stage C, and its retained
data.

Problem. Label pinning gets us close on the mean, but uniform
convexity needs a near-sharp pointwise gradient bound. In
particular, on retained rank-1 and rank-2 data, we need
|Dq_0| <= sqrt(2) + small error at rank 2 and <= 1 + small error
at rank 1 (this is the sharp affine value from the source data).

Output. On a collection of small source patches (the "saturation
cubes"), the map q_h = T h has:

  (i)  pointwise gradient bound |D q_h| <= sqrt(2) + C eta at
       rank 2 and <= 1 + C eta at rank 1;
  (ii) mean gradient equal, up to small error, to the source
       differential Df;
  (iii) proportional volume in the source taken by "dirty" points
       (from which the sharp "clean" estimate fails) is small.

Key idea. The "wall realization" from Stage C is geometrically
flexible: it lets us realize parameter labels, but the collar
widths (i.e. the derivative of the map between source and its
realized version) can be huge, because we could have chosen
arbitrarily thin corridors. The trick is to use a calibrated
mesh that forces the widths to be at least half the available
"gap", giving the sharp scalar gradient estimate.

The construction is subtle. It has two competing requirements:
(a) enough collision between opposite-colour walls to realize
parameters; (b) not too much collision, because that would make
the derivative large. So the mesh is chosen so that just enough
walls meet, and the widths are exactly optimized.

Then uniform convexity (Lemma 11.x, "Saturation and physical
gradient accuracy") converts the (near-sharp energy) + (near mean)
into (pointwise gradient closeness). This is a classical argument
using |A|^p for p > 2 being 2-uniformly convex with modulus
proportional to p - 1 (Clarkson's inequality).

At the end of Stage D, the map q_h on each saturation cube has
gradient close to the source gradient of f, with only a small
proportional error. Cap the map at the sharp affine gradient. The
remaining Sobolev discrepancy is controlled.


4.5. Stage E: assemble, correct, and smooth (Section 11)
........................................................

Input. The locally bi-Lipschitz h of Stage C with sharp gradient
data from Stage D on saturated cubes.

Problem. The map q_h not yet global W^{1,p} bounded. Exceptional
interiors (from Stage A) are not yet bounded. Rank-3 data must be
made exactly affine. The result must be C-infinity.

Output. A C-infinity diffeomorphism f_j : Omega -> Lambda with

        ||f_j - f||_{W^{1,p}} < eps.

Key idea. Four substeps.

  (1) Strict substitutions. Replace the non-strict target maps
      T, P_0, and the quantizer's piecewise-linear charts by
      strict bi-Lipschitz ones that converge to them (Lemma 8.x,
      9.x). The substitutions preserve label pinning but smooth
      out the non-smooth boundaries. This gives a W^{1,p}-local
      map on Omega -> Lambda.

  (2) Marked-cube correction. When 2 < p < 3, exceptional source
      cubes from Stage A carry potentially unbounded energy under
      the strict substitutions. A "cube-hiding" construction
      (Lemma 5.x, "hide-cubes") replaces the map on each such
      cube by a self-homeomorphism that is small-energy on its
      interior. The result is a full W^{1,p} map with only the
      full-rank cubes remaining to be corrected.

  (3) Full-rank correction. On the finitely many full-rank
      correction cubes (from Stage A), replace the map by its
      affine approximation. The change is small by the choice of
      cubes and by the sharp estimates from Stage D.

  (4) Smooth. The final map is locally bi-Lipschitz and belongs
      to W^{1,p}. Apply the smoothing theorem from Stage 0
      (Section 6, Theorem 12.5) with any tiny tolerance to get
      a C-infinity diffeomorphism f_j with the same target and
      arbitrarily small W^{1,p} error.

Combining stages A-E with the smoothing foundation, we get the
Main Theorem.

Section 5 gives the "constant order" and explains why the whole
procedure converges.


5. THE ORDER OF CHOICES
-----------------------

The proof has many approximations. Read straight through, one
natural worry is: an early small error might get multiplied by a
later large constant. If so, the whole scheme only converges when
the late constant is small -- something we cannot guarantee.

The paper resolves this with a disciplined ORDER of choices and
two supporting principles.

  Principle 1: free small errors.
  Every "free small error" is prescribed only after every factor
  that will multiply it is fixed. Concretely: we choose things in
  stages, and each stage's constants are already in place before
  the corresponding errors are prescribed.

  Principle 2: locally finite bookkeeping.
  On noncompact parts of Omega or Lambda we work with locally
  finite covers. Each local error is prescribed small relative to
  its own local factor, and the errors over a countable exhaustion
  sum to less than the global error budget (2^{-j} on the jth
  local piece). A locally finite partition of unity glues the
  local data.

The explicit order (this is the abstract version of Remark 11.5
in the original, "ha:scale-order"):

   1. Fix p and the desired global error eps > 0.
   2. Fix a small aspect gamma > 0 and slot-loss eta > 0.
   3. Fix zeta, c_* > 0 small compared to eta.
   4. Fix the ordinary weak constant C_w = C_w(p, gamma, eta, zeta, c_*).
   5. Choose affordable rank tails in the source measure
      mu = (1 + |Df|^p) dx, for the rank-1, rank-2, and
      rank-3 sets.
   6. Choose finite jet truncations (bounds on |Df|^{-1}, on
      singular values) on the retained sets.
   7. Choose full-rank correction shells: finitely many cubes
      B_i around retained rank-3 points with affordable power-mean
      of |Df - b_i|, where b_i is the affine jet at a representative
      point.
   8. Construct the carrier F_b (Stage A). Fix regional energy
      budgets for:
         - the exterior residual (small |Df|^p),
         - the full-rank correction shells,
         - the marked collars (weight ell_U/d_U).
   9. Fix polar and localization data (activation radii,
      protected arrays, full-cell boundary layers).
  10. Fix all compact sharp-template and basis bounds.
  11. Fix the count threshold and level-edge threshold delta',
      both small compared with gamma, eta.
  12. Choose chart and jitter error fractions, small compared with
      the bounds from step 10.
  13. Choose power-mean data errors on the sharp patches, small
      compared with the inverse jitter-density factor from step 12.
  14. Select the finite sharp patches (the "saturation cubes").
  15. Choose the paid transition regions and predetermined
      capacities A_j for integral_{U_j} G^p.
  16. Choose guards and their forbidden label gaps.
  17. Choose boundary widths d_0 and meshes.
  18. Select a permissible jitter.
  19. Sample with sufficiently small weights.
  20. Apply wall realization (Stage C) with these data.
  21. Apply strict substitutions, marked-cube corrections, and
      final smoothing.

Each error is prescribed small relative to its (already fixed)
multipliers so that the sum total is < eps.

Why the order is what it is
...........................

Two of the subtler aspects of the order.

(a) Why capacities come before guards.
    The wall barrier argument (Section 4.3) uses a fixed upper
    bound integral_{U_j} G^p <= A_j to derive a contradiction if
    a guard is misplaced. This requires A_j to be fixed
    INDEPENDENTLY of the guard, but it also requires the
    eventual pre-stack to satisfy the capacity bound for its OWN
    slope majorant G. The resolution:

      - construct a preliminary slope majorant from the mesh
        and template data,
      - show its integral has finite expectation, uniformly
        over mesh and sample choices,
      - fix A_j from that expectation,
      - choose guards,
      - then choose the actual mesh and samples to make the
        realized majorant obey the capacity.

    This is Lemma 10.11 (Prior capacities and simultaneous
    choice).

(b) Why polar data precedes sharp patches.
    Sizes of sharp patches depend on the target's polar
    structure (aspect bounds, activation radii). If we chose
    patches first and polar data later, we could get squeezed
    into shrinking patches (destroying fine-tolerance bounds)
    or widening polar bands (destroying the sharp estimate).
    So we fix polar data first, then patches.

Both are instances of Principle 1: never introduce a multiplier
after the error it will amplify.

What the order gives
....................

Once the whole order is complete:

   * ||f_j - f||_{W^{1,p}} < eps, where
     eps = C_p (gamma + eta) (1 + E) + sum of free small errors,
     where C_p depends only on p.

   * Every free small error is arbitrarily small (by the
     sequencing).

   * The dominant term C_p (gamma + eta) (1 + E) can be made
     smaller than any prescribed threshold by taking gamma, eta
     sufficiently small.

So we can arrange ||f_j - f||_{W^{1,p}} < eps for any prescribed
eps > 0. Taking eps = 2^{-j} for j = 1, 2, 3, ... gives the
sequence f_j in the Main Theorem.


6. FOUNDATIONS: TOPOLOGY AND SMOOTHING
--------------------------------------

This section develops the PL-topology and smoothing machinery that
every later stage relies on. It is largely independent of the
Ball-Evans construction: the goal is to reduce "need a C-infinity
diffeomorphism close to a general W^{1,p} homeomorphism" to "need
a C-infinity diffeomorphism close to a locally bi-Lipschitz one".

The whole of Section 6 is a self-contained restart: if you are
reading this document top to bottom, you could jump to the
statement of Theorem 6.1 now and come back later for Sections
7-12 if you want the Ball-Evans details first. Strictly speaking
Sections 7-12 build on Theorem 6.1, so we put it here.

6.1. The smoothing theorem
..........................

Theorem 6.1 (Strong smoothing of locally bi-Lipschitz maps).
Let Omega, Omega' be bounded domains in R^3, let 1 <= p < infinity,
and let H : Omega -> Omega' be a locally bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism
belonging to W^{1,p}(Omega; R^3). For every eps > 0 and every
continuous function xi : Omega -> (0, infinity) there exists a
C-infinity diffeomorphism g : Omega -> Omega' with

        ||g - H||_{W^{1,p}(Omega)} < eps,

        |g(x) - H(x)| < xi(x)   for every x in Omega.

In particular g belongs to W^{1,p}(Omega; R^3), and every g admits
exactly the fixed target Omega'.

The proof of Theorem 6.1 has three simple steps.

  Step 1. PL approximation of a locally bi-Lipschitz map. Given
  the hypothesis of Theorem 6.1, produce a locally finite PL
  homeomorphism h : Omega -> Omega' with

        ||h - H||_{W^{1,p}} < eps/2,  |h - H| < xi/2.

  (Proposition 6.6, proved in Subsection 6.6.)

  Step 2. Smoothing of a locally finite PL homeomorphism. Given
  a locally finite PL homeomorphism h : Omega -> Omega' in
  W^{1,p}, produce a C-infinity diffeomorphism g : Omega -> Omega'
  with

        ||g - h||_{W^{1,p}} < eps/2,  |g - h| < xi/2.

  (Proposition 6.5, proved in Subsection 6.5.)

  Step 3. Triangle inequality. Combine the two steps.

The bulk of the section is Step 2 and Step 1 in that order (Step
2 is technically more transparent, so we do it first).

The main structural observation is:

   Once H is a locally finite PL homeomorphism, its singular
   set is a locally finite 2-dimensional skeleton (edges and
   vertices of an affine triangulation). Smoothing only needs to
   change h near this skeleton, and there the local structure of
   h is extremely constrained (affine or nicely homogeneous).

Before proving either step, we record three supporting lemmas
about PL topology and about degree/properness. These are the
"qualitative inputs" that the quantitative estimates later
require, but do not themselves provide any derivative bound.

6.2. Properness and degree (Lemma 6.2)
......................................

Lemma 6.2 (Fine control, properness, and the target).
Let h : Omega -> Omega' be an orientation-preserving homeomorphism
between bounded domains, and let g : Omega -> R^3 be a smooth
map with det Dg > 0 everywhere. If

        |g(x) - h(x)| < (1/2) dist(h(x), R^3 minus Omega')
                        for every x in Omega,

then g is a diffeomorphism of Omega onto Omega'.

Here det Dg > 0 is assumed; if the sign is negative, we reverse
orientation by composing with a reflection on the target, hence
the reduction to the orientation-preserving case.

Proof. Put d(y) = dist(y, R^3 minus Omega') and g_t = (1-t) h + t g.
The displayed inequality puts the whole segment g_t(x) inside
Omega'.

The straight homotopy is proper. Indeed, if K is a compact subset
of Omega', and m = min_K d > 0, then

        g_t^{-1}(K) subset h^{-1}(y in Omega' : d(y) >= 2m/3)
                     x [0, 1].

The set inside braces is compact because Omega' is bounded. So
g_t is a proper homotopy.

A proper homotopy preserves degree. In particular, deg(g) = deg(h).
For h a homeomorphism with positive orientation, deg(h) = 1. So
deg(g) = 1.

A proper local diffeomorphism has finitely many preimages of each
point, each with positive local degree. Total degree 1 and each
local degree positive force exactly one preimage per target point.
So g is a bijection Omega -> Omega'. Being a local diffeomorphism
and bijective, g is a diffeomorphism (its local inverses patch to
a global smooth inverse). QED

6.3. Fine relative PL approximation (Lemma 6.3)
...............................................

Lemma 6.3 (Hamilton, relative PL approximation).
Let M, N be PL three-manifolds without boundary, N metrizable,
and let f : M -> N be a homeomorphism. Suppose K subset M is
closed and f is PL on an open neighborhood of K. Given a
continuous function eta : M -> (0, infinity), there is a PL
homeomorphism f_1 : M -> N with

        f_1 = f on a neighborhood of K,
        d_N(f_1(x), f(x)) < eta(x)   for all x in M.

Noncompact manifolds are allowed. For manifolds with boundary
the same statement holds if K contains dM and f is PL near K.

Proof. Choose a closed neighborhood K^+ of K contained in the
open PL neighborhood of f. Transport the PL structure of N to M
via f. Then id : M_orig -> M_transported is PL near K^+.

Hamilton's relative approximation theorem [Hamilton 1976,
Section 3, Theorem 2.2] says we can find a homeomorphism a that
is PL between these two structures, agrees with the identity on
K^+, and satisfies d_f(a(x), x) < eta(x) for all x.

Set f_1 = f o a. Then f_1 is the required PL homeomorphism. QED

6.4. Small ambient extension (Lemma 6.4)
........................................

Lemma 6.4 (Small local ambient extension).
Fix a closed Euclidean cube B subset R^3, compact sets
C, D subset B with C subset int B, and an open neighborhood O of
C union D union dB. For every lambda > 0 there exists delta > 0,
depending only on the source configuration and lambda, with the
following property. If e : O -> R^3 is an embedding with

        sup_{x in O} |e(x) - x| < delta,
        e = id on D union dB,

then there is a homeomorphism A : R^3 -> R^3 with

        A = e on C,
        A = id on D union (R^3 minus int B),
        sup_{R^3} |A - id| < lambda.

The number delta is uniform over a finite list of fixed normalized
configurations (B, C, D, O). No derivative bound on e is required.

Proof. Put E = C union D union dB.

The deformation theorem of Edwards-Kirby [EdwardsKirby 1971,
Theorem 5.1], applied to the inclusion of O in R^3, deforms every
sufficiently close embedding e through embeddings e_t, starting
at e_0 = e, so that e_1 = id on E.

The deformation is unchanged outside a fixed compact neighborhood
L of E in O, and fixes the inclusion. Its continuity at the
inclusion permits us to require |e_t(x) - x| < lambda/2 and
|e(x) - x| < lambda/2 for x in L, t in [0, 1].

Now all the images e_t(O) coincide. Recall the argument: enclose
L in a finite union of relatively compact subdomains of O whose
boundary collars are outside the region where the deformation
changes the map. The embeddings agree on a shared collar.
Invariance of domain plus degree relative to the common boundary
show that the images of the enclosed subdomain are the same.
Outside it the maps already agree.

On this common open image, define

        A_0 = e o e_1^{-1}    on the image of O,
        A_0 = id              elsewhere.

This is a homeomorphism of R^3 (A_0 is already the identity off
a compact set), it agrees with e on E, and it has displacement
< lambda. Since A_0 fixes dB pointwise, it preserves the bounded
complementary component int B. Restricting A_0 to B and extending
by the identity outside B gives the required A. QED

The construction works for a finite list of source configurations
by taking the minimum of the deltas. QED

The next lemma is the lynchpin for gluing local insertions.

Lemma 6.5 (Patched-embedding condition).
Let O_0, U_0 be open sets with closure(O_0) subset U_0, and let
V be an open set with eta = id on U_0 intersect V, where eta is
the local embedding defined on U_0. If eta is sufficiently close
to the identity that eta(O_0) subset U_0, then the patched map

        e = eta  on O_0,
        e = id   on V,

is an embedding of O_0 union V into R^3.

Proof. A collision eta(x) = y with x in O_0 and y in V has
y in U_0 intersect V, whence eta(y) = y, so injectivity of eta
gives x = y. The open embedding property then follows from
invariance of domain. QED

These three lemmas are the qualitative topology inputs. They are
used all through Step 2 and Step 1.

Step 2 (PL smoothing) is proved in Subsection 6.5, Step 1
(bilip to PL) in Subsection 6.6, and the two are combined in
Subsection 6.7.


6.5. Smoothing a locally finite PL homeomorphism
...............................................

Proposition 6.5 (Strong PL smoothing). Let h : Omega -> Omega' be
a locally finite PL homeomorphism between bounded domains in R^3,
belonging to W^{1,p}(Omega; R^3) for some 1 <= p < infinity. Then
the conclusion of Theorem 6.1 holds with H replaced by h. That
is: for every eps > 0 and continuous xi : Omega -> (0, infinity),
there is a C-infinity diffeomorphism g : Omega -> Omega' with

        ||g - h||_{W^{1,p}} < eps,  |g - h| < xi  pointwise.

The rest of this subsection proves Proposition 6.5.

Fix an affine triangulation of h, locally finite. The idea is
to modify h only inside thin tubes around each part of the
skeleton (edges, faces away from edges, punctured vertex balls,
vertices), each of which we replace by a smooth map on the same
boundary. Because the tubes have small volume, the W^{1,p}
change is small. Because each replacement agrees with h on the
tube boundary, the operations glue.

6.5.1. The open edges.
......................

Let e be an open edge, with axial coordinate t in (0, l). Place
around it a small tube of radius d(t), where d : (0, l) -> (0, l/2)
is smooth and equal to a linear function near both ends. Choose
the tubes of distinct open edges to be disjoint. This is possible
by shrinking the tubes (each edge has a finite numerical ratio
of tube radius to distance from adjacent edges).

In positively oriented orthogonal frames, and after translating,
the PL map on the tube takes the form

        h(t, r, theta) = (a t + r b(theta),
                          r s(theta) e_{phi(theta)}),

where a > 0, s > 0, b, s, phi : [0, 2 pi) -> R / circle are
smooth on the closed angular sectors (they are the PL boundary
values extended). Here e_alpha = (cos alpha, sin alpha).

The assumption that h is injective at the edge implies that the
"homogeneous" transverse map r theta -> r s(theta) e_{phi(theta)}
is injective as a map on a small disk. Consequently its angular
map theta -> phi(theta) is an orientation-preserving circle
homeomorphism. Choose a lift phi with phi(theta + 2 pi) =
phi(theta) + 2 pi.

Positive determinant on the finitely many sectors gives upper and
positive lower bounds for s and for the one-sided derivative
phi'.

Interpolation. For 0 <= tau <= 1 put

        b_tau = (1 - tau) b,
        s_tau = (1 - tau) s + tau c,
        phi_tau = (1 - tau) phi + tau (theta + c'),

with c > 0, c' chosen constants. At tau = 1 the map is the
smooth rigid map (t, r, theta) -> (a t, c r e_{theta + c'}); at
tau = 0 it is h. The determinant of the interpolated map in the
frame (d/dt, d/dr, r^{-1} d/dtheta) is

        a s_tau^2 phi_tau' > 0

on all the closed sectors and all tau in [0, 1]. There is a
positive minimum of this quantity.

The blend. Let chi be a smooth cutoff equal to 1 on (-infinity, -1]
and to 0 on [-1/2, +infinity). Put tau(t, r) = chi(log(r/d(t))/N).
Then the new map is

        H_eps(t, r, theta) = h_{tau(t, r)}(t, r, theta),

something intermediate between h and the rigid map. Near the tube
axis r ~ 0 we recover the "tau = 1" rigid map; near the tube
boundary r ~ d(t) we recover the "tau = 0" map h.

The derivative error. The additional derivative columns due to
differentiating tau contribute a size at most

        C/N * (1 + |d'|_infinity),

because |r d_tau/dr| <= C/N and |r d_tau/dt| <= C(r/d) |d'|/N,
while r/d <= 1 on the support of the modification. The other
factors are the fixed angular derivatives. So for N large enough
positive determinants persist on every sector.

The tube width. Because d is linear near the tube ends, the map
H_eps near each tube end is exactly homogeneous: H_eps(t, r,
theta) - H_eps(t, 0, theta) is affine in (r, 1) with homogeneous
piecewise-linear data.

Energy cost per tube. All first derivatives of H_eps are bounded
by an edge-dependent constant independent of a common decrease of
d. The volume of the tube goes to zero under that decrease.
So both the value error and the derivative error can be made
arbitrarily small in W^{1,p}. Choose the tube widths so that
the total over all edges is bounded by a prescribed small
fraction (summable over the locally finite edge set, e.g. 2^{-j}).

Call the result h_1. It agrees with h outside the tubes, is
continuous and locally Lipschitz, and equals h on the leftover
vertices. Near each vertex v, h_1 - h_1(v) is homogeneous of
degree 1 on a sufficiently small ball about v - this is the
consequence of the linear tube-end choice.

6.5.2. Faces and punctured vertex balls.
........................................

Away from vertices, the gradient of h_1 belongs to GL^+ (3) on
each piece, and on each point the local "convexification" of the
gradient set is still inside GL^+ (3): at a smooth point by
continuity of h_1 there, and at a codimension-1 interface by the
elementary computation below.

Elementary lemma (face interface). Let A_- and A_+ be the two
gradient traces at a face interface (they agree on the face
tangent plane by continuity of h_1). Then A_+ - A_- has rank at
most one, with normal covector to the face, so the segment
t -> (1 - t) A_- + t A_+ has determinant

        det((1 - t) A_- + t A_+) = (1 - t) det A_- + t det A_+ > 0

for 0 <= t <= 1. Consequently the convex hull of nearby gradients
lies in a small neighborhood of this compact positive-determinant
segment, still in GL^+(3).

Homogeneity. At a relative scale about a vertex v, the map h_1
satisfies h_1(r theta) - h_1(v) = r * d/dr[h_1]_v(theta). Cover
the unit sphere by finitely many small neighborhoods, each small
enough that the gradient set at relative scale is contained in a
fixed compact neighborhood of GL^+. This is uniform in r and v
near the vertex.

Variable mollifier. Choose a smooth probability kernel rho
supported in the unit ball. Away from vertices define

        h_2(x) = integral h_1(x + w(x) z) rho(z) dz.

Its derivative is

        D h_2(x) = integral D h_1(x + w(x) z) rho(z) dz
                 + integral (D h_1(x + w(x) z) z) (x) D w(x) rho(z) dz.

The first integral is the average of nearby gradients, so it lies
in the positive-determinant convex hull of the previous lemma.
The second integral, made small by choosing w(x) and |D w(x)| small
locally, is a perturbation. Hence det D h_2 > 0.

Formula for punctured vertex balls. On a small ball B(v, r_0) minus
v, choose w(x) = c_v |x - v| with c_v > 0 sufficiently small. Then
H_2 - h_1(v) is homogeneous on a smaller ball, so the invariance
under positive scaling of "positive determinant" persists.

The chosen radius function w : punctured neighborhood -> (0,
infinity) is smooth and positive, with small values and small
derivative, supplied by a locally finite partition of unity with
sufficiently small positive coefficients.

6.5.3. Vertices.
................

Let v be a vertex, translate to origin, subtract h_1(v). The
resulting map F : B_r(0) -> R^3 is homogeneous of degree 1 on
small r. Euler's identity is D F(x) x = F(x). Since D F is
invertible on the punctured ball, F(x) is nonzero there. Thus we
can write

        F(r theta) = r R(theta) Phi(theta),

with R > 0 smooth and Phi : S^2 -> S^2 smooth. Positive
determinant of D F means Phi is an orientation-preserving smooth
local diffeomorphism of S^2. Since S^2 is compact and simply
connected, Phi is a global homeomorphism, hence a diffeomorphism.

Smale's theorem gives a smooth isotopy Phi_tau : S^2 -> S^2 from
Phi to a rotation, smooth jointly in (tau, theta). Interpolate
the positive function R through positive functions R_tau to a
positive constant 1. For fixed tau the homogeneous map

        F_tau(r theta) = r R_tau(theta) Phi_tau(theta)

has determinant

        R_tau(theta)^3 J_{S^2} Phi_tau(theta) > 0.

The family (F_tau)_{0 <= tau <= 1} has bounded first derivatives
and a positive determinant minimum, by compactness.

The blend. Replace F with F_{tau(r)}, where tau : [0, r_0] ->
[0, 1] is 0 near r_0 and 1 near 0, and the small quantity
sup_r |r tau'(r)| is sufficiently small. Meaning: spread a fixed
smooth cutoff over a long interval of log r so that its
derivative |d tau/d log r| = r |d tau/d r| is small. Then the
extra derivative term is bounded by C * sup_r |r tau'(r)|, so
positive determinant persists. At r = 0 the map is a dilation
followed by a rotation, hence smooth and nonsingular.

Derivative bound. The extra derivative term in F_{tau(r)} does
not grow as r_0 decreases. Shrinking r_0 makes the W^{1,p}
integral over the vertex ball arbitrarily small on each vertex;
summing over the locally finite vertex set with summable error
budgets gives arbitrarily small total.

Result. The smoothly-modified map g : Omega -> Omega' is smooth,
has det Dg > 0 everywhere, and equals h off the tube-and-ball
neighborhoods.

6.5.4. Target preservation.
...........................

We have chosen local value tolerances on the replacements in
6.5.1-6.5.3 so that they total less than min(xi(x),
(1/2) dist(h(x), R^3 minus Omega')). Lemma 6.2 (properness/degree) now
applies with H := h and the smooth map g. Consequently g is a
diffeomorphism of Omega onto the original target Omega'. The
first display of Proposition 6.5 also follows from the
construction: each replacement has W^{1,p} change < the prescribed
share, and the locally finite summability gives total < eps.

This completes the proof of Proposition 6.5. QED


6.6. Approximating a locally bi-Lipschitz map by PL
..................................................

Proposition 6.6 (Strong PL approximation of a locally bi-Lipschitz
map). Let Omega, Omega' be bounded domains, let 1 <= p < infinity,
and let H : Omega -> Omega' be a locally bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism
in W^{1,p}(Omega; R^3). Given eps > 0 and continuous
xi : Omega -> (0, infinity), there exists a locally finite PL
homeomorphism G : Omega -> Omega' with

        ||G - H||_{W^{1,p}(Omega)} < eps,  |G - H| < xi  pointwise.

The rest of this subsection proves Proposition 6.6.

Broadly: build a dyadic mesh; on each cube insert a local PL
model that interpolates H at the fine vertices; the resulting
map is PL by construction, has value close to H (because
interpolation is close), and has derivative close to DH on
most cubes. The "bad" cubes - where H is far from affine -
are absorbed by the fact that their total volume can be made
arbitrarily small.

The tricky part is that a naive PL interpolation at all fine
vertices need not be injective. We insert patches one at a time
and use Lemma 6.4 (small local ambient extension) to keep the
old map's images intact.

6.6.1. Adapted dyadic meshes (Lemma 6.7).
........................................

Lemma 6.7 (Adapted dyadic meshes). There is an absolute integer
n_0 with the following property. Given arbitrary positive local
upper bounds on a "fine scale function", there is a locally
finite dyadic cube decomposition Q of Omega with centers c_Q and
side lengths l_Q such that, writing B_Q(s) = c_Q + [-s l_Q, s l_Q]^3,

  (i)   The box B_Q(100) is compactly contained in Omega. Cubes
        meeting B_Q(50) have side lengths comparable to l_Q by
        absolute constants.

  (ii)  The normalized configurations of the mesh inside B_Q(6)
        belong to a finite list. There is a conforming affine
        triangulation of each cube with finitely many normalized
        nondegenerate shapes on these configurations.

  (iii) The cubes have n_0 colours so that the closed boxes B_Q(6)
        of a single colour are pairwise disjoint.

  (iv)  Each cube Q is attached to a coarse index P(Q) so that
        interactions through any fixed number of neighboring
        patch layers stay in a fixed locally finite graph of
        coarse indices, independent of mesh refinement.

The upper bounds can be arbitrary positive continuous functions.

Proof. Choose a positive Lipschitz size function s : Omega ->
(0, 1) with small absolute Lipschitz constant, below the
prescribed local bounds and below 10^{-3} dist(x, R^3 minus Omega).
Lemma 6.5.5 (local tolerances) supplies such a minorant.

Take the maximal dyadic cubes Q such that l_Q <= inf_Q s.
Failure of the condition for the dyadic parent gives
s(x) <= (2 + C * Lip(s)) l_Q on Q. Choosing Lip(s) small enough
makes every dyadic ratio in a neighborhood of Q belong to
{1/2, 1, 2}. So local comparability on B_Q(50). The distance
bound puts B_Q(100) inside Omega.

Maximality gives a decomposition; positive lower bound of s
near any compact gives local finiteness.

The cube vertices have dyadic coordinates. Bounded local scale
ratios give finitely many normalized configurations.

For the conforming triangulation, decompose each cube face into
its square contact facets, put every contact vertex and hanging
point on the perimeter of each such square, cone from the
square center, and then cone the triangulation of each cube
boundary from the cube center. Nondegenerate simplices and finite
shape list follow from scale comparability.

Local comparability bounds the degree of the graph joining cubes
whose B_Q(6) boxes meet. So n_0 = degree + 1 colours suffice.
Finally, choosing the size function sufficiently small relative
to the coarse Whitney sizes confines the interaction to fixed
coarse neighborhoods. QED

6.6.2. Insertion with protected cores (Lemma 6.8).
..................................................

Lemma 6.8 (Insertion with protected cores). Consider a patch Q
and a finite collection of "older protected boxes" satisfying
the relative sizes and positions provided by Lemma 6.7. For
every older box prescribe a larger half-shrunk box and a smaller
fully-shrunk box, separated by a fixed positive normalized
margin. Let G : Omega -> Omega' be a homeomorphism, and let h_Q
be a PL embedding on a neighborhood of B_Q(3). Assume that
h_Q = G on the overlaps with the half-shrunk boxes.

For every lambda > 0 there is an a > 0 depending only on
lambda, the normalized source configuration, and a local
constant K_P from the local bi-Lipschitz bounds, such that: if

        sup_{B_Q(5)} |G - H| < a l_Q,
        sup_{B_Q(3)} |h_Q - H| < a l_Q,

then there is a domain homeomorphism A supported in B_Q(5), with
sup |A - id| < lambda l_Q, such that (G o A) = h_Q on a
neighborhood of B_Q(2) and (G o A) = G on the fully-shrunk older
boxes. The tolerances are uniform over a finite list of source
configurations. They do not depend on derivatives of G or of h_Q.

Proof. Scale the patch to l_Q = 1. Reduce a using K_P so that
h_Q(B_Q(3)) subset G(int B_Q(4)). Explanation: for x in B_Q(3)
and z in dB_Q(4), K_P^{-1} |x - z| <= |H(x) - H(z)|, so H keeps
them separated; small errors in the display preserve this.
Degree on int B_Q(4) comparing G with H on the boundary shows
that h_Q(B_Q(3)) lies in the image. The same statement holds on a
small margin around B_Q(3).

The embedding eta = G^{-1} o h_Q is consequently defined there.
If z = eta(x), then

        |z - x| <= K_P |H(z) - H(x)|
                <= K_P (|H(z) - G(z)| + |h_Q(x) - H(x)|)
                < 2 K_P a.

Thus its closeness to the inclusion uses no inverse-Lipschitz
bound for G.

Take a closed neighborhood C of B_Q(2) inside B_Q(3); let D be the
union of the fully-shrunk older boxes intersected with B_Q(5).
Half/full shrink margins give neighborhoods on which eta near C
agrees with the identity near D. Include also an identity
neighborhood of dB_Q(5), separated from C. Choose these
neighborhoods once for each normalized source configuration. By
the inequality above, sufficiently small a makes the union of
eta and these identity maps an embedding (patched-embedding
Lemma 6.4b). Apply Lemma 6.4 (local ambient extension) with
B = B_Q(5) and displacement lambda. Precomposition by the
resulting A installs h_Q on C and preserves the fully-shrunk
boxes. Rescaling proves the Lemma. QED


6.6.3. Two rounds, finite PL libraries.
.......................................

We use two rounds of n_0 insertion stages each, for 2 n_0 total
stages. At each stage, we install patch models in disjoint
boxes of one colour. We let h_Q denote a PL model inserted on
cube Q; at the end of both rounds h_Q will equal the final map
on an entire neighborhood of Q.

The colouring gives that the boxes B_Q(5) for a single colour
are pairwise disjoint. So all cells of one colour can be
installed simultaneously without conflict.

Shrinking older cores. Let

        d_* = (4 n_0 + 4)^{-1}.

At each subsequent stage decrease the radius (in the current
B_Q coordinates) of every previously-installed patch by d_*. Its
final radius is greater than 3/2. Since the "shrunken" range is
inscribed in the original cube, the closed original cube Q
remains in the interior of its protected core throughout.

Backward recursion for tolerances. Fix any target final
value-error schedule (say, sum of stage tolerances < xi/2). Work
backwards from stage 2 n_0 to stage 1: at stage s+1, the current
map's distance from H is bounded by the stage s+1 map's own
distance from H plus the accumulated displacement introduced by
the insertion at stage s+1. So we can require the current map's
value error before stage s+1 to be a small fraction of the
tolerance allowed at stage s+1. This gives a positive sequence
a_1, a_2, ..., a_{2 n_0} of insertion thresholds. Record them now.

First round: good cubes. For each cube Q, define its "affine
comparison" A_Q (as below). A cube Q is called GOOD if there is
an affine map A_Q of bi-Lipschitz constant <= 2 K_P such that

        sup_{B_Q(20)} |H - A_Q| <= delta_P l_Q,

        ( (1/|Q|) integral_Q |DH - DA_Q|^p )^{1/p} <= delta_P.

Here K_P is the local bi-Lipschitz constant from the coarse
neighborhood of Q, and delta_P is a small positive introduced
according to the backward schedule. Changing the "20" to a
larger absolute constant would do no harm.

Quantize the H-value at every fine vertex to a target dyadic
grid of spacing proportional to the smallest incident cube size.
The dyadic factor is fixed from the coarse indices of the
incident cubes, small enough that the normalized rounding error
is at most delta_P. Interpolate the rounded values affinely on
the finite triangulation. Call the result L. It is piecewise
affine, though NOT globally injective.

On a good cube, the following estimates hold (this is a direct
computation):

        |DL - DA_Q| <= C delta_P near B_Q(3),

        sup_{B_Q(3)} |L - H| <= C delta_P l_Q.

Specifically, subtract A_Q at the vertices of any tetrahedron of
the finite triangulation. Vertex errors are at most C delta_P
l_Q; the inverse matrix of its three edge vectors has norm at
most C/l_Q (by the finite shape list); multiplication gives the
first estimate, and affine interpolation gives the second.

The first estimate implies that L is an embedding on a
neighborhood of B_Q(3). Indeed L - A_Q is Lipschitz with
constant at most C delta_P (integrate the piecewise constant
derivatives on segments). So

        |L(x) - L(y)| >= ((2 K_P)^{-1} - C delta_P) |x - y| > 0.

Invariance of domain gives the open embedding assertion.

First round. Set G_0 = H. For stages s = 1, ..., n_0 process the
colours in order; at colour c insert a patch on every good cube
of colour c with model h_Q = L. On every overlap with an older
protected core this model agrees with the already-installed map
(both equal the single global L). The good-cube estimates and
the chosen thresholds supply the input accuracy for Lemma 6.8
(insertion). Supports B_Q(5) of one colour are disjoint, so the
insertions of one stage are simultaneous. Local finiteness makes
the union a domain homeomorphism. The value invariants follow
from the backward recursion.

At the end of this round, the map agrees with L on every good
cube, and on a protected neighborhood of every good cube.

Second round: finite PL libraries. This is the technical trunk.
Fix once and for all, before the final mesh resolution is
chosen, a FINITE LIBRARY of candidate PL models for every
possible coarse index, stage, and "combination" of data. Then,
in the second round, install library models directly.

Data finiteness. For each cube Q choose m_Q in Z^3 nearest to
H(c_Q) / l_Q; use normalized coordinates

        z = (x - c_Q) / l_Q,
        Hhat_Q(z) = ( H(c_Q + l_Q z) - l_Q m_Q ) / l_Q.

The value at z = 0 is at most an absolute constant; equation
(sm:coarse-bilip) in the source gives a uniform local Lipschitz
bound. So the normalized vertex values of L on B_Q(4) lie in a
bounded set on finitely many dyadic grids. They therefore take
only finitely many values for each coarse index.

If R interacts with Q, then

        ( l_R m_R - l_Q m_Q ) / l_Q

is a bounded dyadic vector with a denominator from a finite
list. Boundedness follows by comparing H(c_R) with H(c_Q) via
the local bi-Lipschitz bounds; the denominators are fixed by
bounded dyadic scale ratios. Consequently the first-round
exact PL data, as viewed in any interacting normalized patch,
have only finitely many possibilities.

The finite library. For stages n_0 + 1, ..., 2 n_0, construct
the models as follows. For each coarse index P, the normalized
maps Hhat_Q on a fixed larger patch form a uniformly bounded
equicontinuous family (compact P, Lipschitz bound from the local
bi-Lipschitzness). Equicontinuity gives finitely many sup-norm
"bins" of any prescribed positive diameter. Pick bin diameter
< a_{s, P} / 8, where a_{s, P} is the insertion threshold from
the backward schedule (taken locally smaller over the coarse
neighbors of P).

Inductively, assume the already-installed normalized PL models
have finite lists. Let hhat_R be an older model in its own
R-normalization. In the normalized coordinates of an interacting
Q, this same map becomes

        z |-> (l_R / l_Q) hhat_R( (c_Q - c_R)/l_R + (l_Q/l_R) z )
               + (l_R m_R - l_Q m_Q)/l_Q.

The source similarities and protected-core positions have finite
lists (Lemma 6.7 and d_*). The target translations do too
(the relative-origins computation above). Only boundedly many
older cores meet B_Q(5), and their coarse indices lie in a
fixed finite neighborhood. Record each older model's library
label, its normalized source placement, its target translation,
and its current protected radius. These records determine the
model on the whole protected core and have finitely many
possibilities. In particular their restrictions to the
half-shrunk overlaps give a finite list of exact PL data.

So there are only finitely many possible

        "combination" = (source configuration, Hhat_Q bin,
                          exact protected-model records).

For each combination which CAN occur, pick one representative
normalized embedding G^* close to a map H^* in that bin by the
prescribed current tolerance, and realizing the specified older
data. Take the representative on the fixed open patch (-4, 4)^3.
If Khat_i are the normalized half-shrunk older boxes, the set

        K = [-3, 3]^3 intersect (union_i Khat_i)

is compact in that open patch. It lies strictly inside the
currently protected cores, so G^* is PL on a neighborhood of K.
Apply Lemma 6.3 (Hamilton relative PL) to G^* on the open patch,
keeping K fixed. Obtain a PL embedding h^* with uniform error
less than a_{s, P}/8 on the required compact patch.

The full protected-model records also ensure agreement on a
neighborhood of K with every actual tuple having the same
combination: both its current map and G^* equal the recorded
models on the protected cores, which contain the half-shrunk
boxes in their interiors. Hamilton makes h^* = G^* on a
neighborhood of K. Since there are only finitely many closed
half-shrunk boxes, we can choose a neighborhood N of [-3, 3]^3
inside (-4, 4)^3 whose intersection with each such box lies in
this common agreement neighborhood.

Store h^*|_N as the library entry indexed by (coarse region,
stage, combination). Its entire overlap with each half-shrunk
box now has the equality required by Lemma 6.8.

Reuse estimate. If a different actual tuple has the same
combination, this single model agrees with all of its prescribed
PL overlap data. Moreover

        ||h^* - Hhat_Q||_infinity
          <= ||h^* - G^*||_infinity + ||G^* - H^*||_infinity
             + ||H^* - Hhat_Q||_infinity
          < (3/8) a_{s, P},

where the three sup norms are on the fixed patch needed for
insertion. The actual current map is already within
a_{s, P}/8 of its H (by the backward schedule), so Lemma 6.8
applies after undoing the normalization. This installs h_Q while
preserving the fully-shrunk older cores.

Note that this selection does NOT require compactness of the
family of possible intermediate maps G_j. Only the H-family is
binned; the intermediate map is used just as an existence
witness. All possible normalized meshes and inputs obeying the
fixed coarse bounds may be included when deciding which
combinations occur. At any stage the choices for a coarse index
depend only on libraries from strictly earlier stages at its
finitely many interacting indices; so they can be made
simultaneously over all coarse indices. Hence the choices are
independent of the eventual mesh resolution.

Each stored map is PL on a neighborhood of a fixed compact
patch, so has finitely many affine pieces there. Finite upper
derivative bounds follow. Only finitely many stored maps per
coarse combination. After the second round every cube retains
its installed model on a protected neighborhood (radius
greater than 3/2), so the final derivative on that cube comes
from a stored PL model, not from an ambient transition.
Consequently there are constants M_P < infinity such that

        |DG| <= M_P   almost everywhere on Q,   P = P(Q).

The M_P are fixed before reducing the fine scale. They may be
arbitrarily large. No efficient dependence on K_P is claimed.

Assertion. If a single triangulation is desired, take common
refinements of the finitely many affine subdivisions meeting
each compact set and triangulate the resulting polyhedral cells.
Local finiteness gives a locally finite affine triangulation on
Omega.

Funequal trianguability: G is still onto Omega' because every
stage was a precomposition by a domain homeomorphism. On every
good cube G still equals L, since its first-round protected
neighborhood was preserved throughout.

6.6.4. Good versus bad cubes and the final mesh.
................................................

Select the final local upper size bounds of Lemma 6.7 only
NOW. On a fixed coarse compact region, almost every x is both a
differentiability point of H and a Lebesgue point of DH. The
derivative at such a point is invertible, with upper and lower
bounds inherited from local bi-Lipschitzness.

At such a point x, the affine map A_x(y) = H(x) + DH(x) (y - x)
passes both tests in the good-cube definition on any sufficiently
small cube containing x. (Its enlargement is contained in a ball
of radius C l_Q about x, giving the sup test by differentiability;
the volume of that ball is at most a fixed multiple of |Q|,
giving the derivative mean test by the Lebesgue-point property.)
Only finitely many coarse thresholds occur near a fixed compact
set. So the measure of the union of bad cubes with a fixed coarse
index P tends to zero as their local upper side length tends to
zero. This is uniform over the admissible meshes: take the union
of all bad dyadic cubes with that index and side at most r; these
decrease as r down to zero, and their intersection is null.

Choose positive numbers b_P with sum b_P < eps^p/8, and reduce
the local mesh bounds until

        (M_P + K_P)^p |bad cubes with coarse index P| < b_P.

All bounds can be imposed simultaneously by Lemma 6.7.

The stage value tolerances also bound |G - H|/l_Q by fixed local
constants on each cube. Further reduce the local size bounds so
that the actual value error is below xi and has p-th integral
less than the remaining part of eps^p.

Now choose a mesh satisfying all these bounds and carry out both
rounds with the already selected libraries, obtaining the final
G.

6.6.5. Final W^{1,p} estimate.
..............................

On good cubes:

        integral_Q |DG - DH|^p <= (C delta_P)^p |Q|.

Sum over good cubes in each coarse index P and add:

        total good <= (C delta_P)^p |Omega| <= eps^p/8,

choosing delta_P small.

On bad cubes:

        |DG - DH|^p <= (M_P + K_P)^p  almost everywhere,
        total bad <= sum_P b_P < eps^p/8.

Combine. The derivative error is < eps. The value error is < xi.
The map G is locally Lipschitz, its derivative is globally
p-integrable by the estimate, its values lie in the bounded
target Omega'. So G belongs to W^{1,p}(Omega; R^3) and has the
required strong error.

Local PL structure: after two rounds the map equals a stored PL
model on each cube's protected neighborhood. Shared cube faces
have the same map on both sides (protected cores are interiors
of the closed cubes, but the map is locally PL and the
restriction to the face is consistent). A common refinement of
finitely many affine subdivisions per compact gives the locally
finite triangulation. QED

6.7. Proof of Theorem 6.1
.........................

Apply Proposition 6.6 with Sobolev tolerance eps/2 and value
tolerance xi/2 to get PL homeomorphism h. Apply Proposition 6.5
to h with the same tolerances to get C-infinity diffeomorphism
g. The triangle inequality gives

        ||g - H||_{W^{1,p}} < eps,   |g - H| < xi  pointwise.

Both approximations have exactly the target Omega'. QED

Appendices to Section 6
.......................

Three useful consequences we will cite but not fully reprove:

Fact 6.A ("Local tolerances"). Let U subset R^n be open and let
a : U -> (0, infinity) be continuous. For every L > 0 there is
a positive L-Lipschitz function d on U with d(x) <= min(a(x),
1, L dist(x, R^n minus U)). For any positive continuous upper bounds
on d and |Dd|, a smooth minorant exists with d <= v, |Dd| <= w.
Finite collections of such bounds combine. Locally finite
families with summable cost combine, via partition of unity.

Proof. Extend e(x) = min(a(x), 1, L dist(x, R^n minus U)) by zero
outside U; put d(x) = inf_y { e(y) + L |x - y| }. For the smooth
version use a locally finite partition of unity with coefficients
chosen small relative to v and w/|D chi_i|. QED

Fact 6.B ("PL approximation of a locally bi-Lipschitz map with
fine derivative" - used in Stage A). Let H : Omega -> Omega' be
locally bi-Lipschitz and in W^{1,1}_{loc}. Then for every
eps-locally-integrable "derivative budget" there is a PL
approximation with matching strong gradient. This is technically
a variant of Proposition 6.6 with the additional constraint that
the fine-scale mesh approximates the differential; the proof is
identical to 6.6, using the fact that density of PL approximations
follows from the fact that the target error is already small on
the bad cubes and can be sharpened by finer mesh on the good
cubes.

Fact 6.C ("Volume Lusin N"). A map u in W^{1,3}_{loc} from an
open set Omega subset R^3 to R^3 has the volume Lusin property
N locally: it maps Lebesgue-null sets to Lebesgue-null sets.
Consequently for p >= 3, Lebesgue null subsets E subset Omega
have u(E) null. (Section 7, Lemma 7.2 gives the interior
estimate that makes this a black box for our purposes.)

These facts are cited as needed in later sections.


7. RETAINED RANK-DEFICIENT DATA AND THE CARRIER
-----------------------------------------------

This is one of the two heaviest sections. The goal is to construct
a continuous proper onto map F_b : Omega -> Lambda such that:

  - F_b agrees with the (flattened image of) f on a large
    retained set of rank-deficient data;
  - F_b is locally affine or PL almost everywhere else;
  - F_b is a homeomorphism off a locally finite set of isolated
    compact balls (opened at the very end of the proof).

We call F_b the carrier.

7.1. Why rank-deficient data needs special treatment
...................................................

Set

        D = { x in Omega : Df(x) exists and 1 <= rank Df(x) <= 2 }.

By Sard-type reasoning for Sobolev homeomorphisms, f(D) has
zero three-dimensional Lebesgue measure. But by contrast with
the volume, the p-energy

        mu(E) = integral_{E cap D} (1 + |Df|^p) dx

need not be small on D. Discarding D - which would be the
naive approach - would lose the approximation theorem.
Section 7.4 therefore flattens the retained data onto plates
without removing it from the source.

The construction has three stages:

  (i)   Flatten the image of D onto separated target plates.
  (ii)  Choose "protected endpoint" rectangles on both sides of
        each plate (these carry the retained data).
  (iii) Build F_b by exact affine/PL implants on top of the
        endpoint rectangles.

The flattening is possible because of the geometric structure of
the pushforward measure f_*(L^3 | Omega), and the flattening is
compatible with injectivity because it is "vertical" - it collapses
only directions that were already collapsed by f.

7.2. The decomposability bundle and a forbidden cone
....................................................

We use the measure nu = f_#(L^3 | Omega). Its decomposability
bundle V(nu, y) at a point y in Lambda records all direction
vectors tangent (in an averaged sense) to rectifiable curves
whose pushforward length measures are absolutely continuous with
respect to nu. This is the Alberti-Marchese (2016) construction.

Lemma 7.1 (Forbidden cone).
Let D be as in 7.1. Then:

  (a) im Df(x) subset V(nu, f(x)) for almost every x in Omega.

  (b) For nu-almost every y in f(D), V(nu, y) is a proper
      subspace of R^3.

Consequently, for every 0 < lambda < 1, the set f(D) can be
partitioned, up to a nu-null set, into finitely many measurable
pieces {P_a}, each with an associated unit vector e_a, such that

        |proj_{V(nu, y)} e_a| < lambda / 10 on P_a.

In local coordinates (h, v) in e_a^perp x R, the bundle on P_a
contains no nonzero vector in the double cone

        { (a', b) : |a'| <= |b| / lambda }.

Point (a) is standard: the range of a Sobolev homeomorphism at
almost every point is contained in the decomposability bundle of
the pushforward of the Lebesgue measure (the "transport formula"
for Sobolev maps with the volume Lusin property N and the
weighted area formula). Point (b) uses the fact that D has zero
pushforward volume - if V(nu, y) were R^3 for nu-positive measure
of y in f(D), the area formula would imply positive pushforward
of measure on D.

The covering by e_a-directions is a measurable selection
argument.

7.3. A simultaneous null set for all Lipschitz graphs
.....................................................

The next ingredient is that the "vertical" structure can be
arranged once and for all for all admissible curves, not just a
countable family:

Lemma 7.2 (Graph-null set). Fix coordinates (h, v) in R^2 x R
and 0 < lambda < 1. Let sigma be a finite measure concentrated
on a Borel set A in a compact rectangle X = H x I (where H is a
closed horizontal box and I is a compact vertical interval), and
suppose sigma << nu. Assume V(nu, y) contains no nonzero vector
in the forbidden cone of Lemma 7.1 at sigma-almost every y in A.

Then there is a Borel set A_0 subset A of full sigma-measure such
that, for every Lipschitz graph h : I -> H with Lipschitz
constant <= 1/lambda:

        |{ v in I : (h(v), v) in A_0 }| = 0.

Proof sketch. Put

        rho_h = (v :-> (h(v), v))_#(L^1 | I),
        C = { integral_{Gamma} rho_h dP(h) : P probability on Gamma },

where Gamma is the compact family of all 1/lambda-Lipschitz
graphs I -> H. Weak continuity makes C convex and weakly
compact.

The key step: no rho in C can be close to sigma, because sigma is
concentrated on A where V(nu, .) is transverse to every graph
direction, but each rho_h is equivalent to length measure on the
graph, tangent to the graph direction. This uses the definition
of V(nu, .) - if sigma << rho with positive density, then sigma-
almost every point has a direction of the graph in V(nu, .),
contradicting the forbidden-cone property.

The Sion minimax theorem (Sion 1958, applied to the continuous
affine functional (phi, rho) :-> integral phi d rho + integral
(1 - phi) d sigma on C(X) x C) then produces functionals phi_n
in C(X) with 0 <= phi_n <= 1 such that:

        integral (1 - phi_n) d sigma + sup_{h in Gamma}
            integral_I phi_n(h(v), v) dv < 2^{-n}.

Tonelli shows phi_n -> 1 at sigma-almost every point, while
phi_n(h(v), .) -> 0 for L^1-almost every v, for each fixed graph
h. The set { y : phi_n(y) -> 1 } works as A_0. QED

7.4. Flattening
...............

Given Lemma 7.2, we can now construct the flattening map S_0.

Proposition 7.3 (Flattening). There is a Lipschitz self-map
S_0 : Lambda -> Lambda, equal to the identity near dLambda, with:

  (a) sup |S_0 - id| < delta (given arbitrarily).

  (b) S_0 is a locally bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism of Lambda minus
      S_0^{-1}(Z) onto Lambda minus Z, where Z subset Lambda is
      closed with three-dimensional volume zero. Its only
      non-singleton fibres are vertical intervals over points of Z.

  (c) |D(S_0 o f)(x)| <= (1 + C lambda) |Df(x)| for almost
      every x.

  (d) ||D(S_0 o f) - Df||_{L^p(D)} < (flattening tolerance) *
      ||Df||_{L^p(D)} on the retained rank-deficient data.

Sketch. Partition f(D) by Lemma 7.1 into finitely many pieces
with assigned vertical directions. Group the pieces inside
finitely many disjoint coordinate-parallel boxes; on each box
we perform a vertical collapse.

Fix one such box. Sort the retained and sigma-full data pointwise
by v-coordinate, writing the starts of the "bands" as s_j(h)
(the smallest retained v-height, then s_{j-1} + L, then the
smallest retained v above that, etc.). Since the s_j are order
statistics of finitely many Lipschitz piecewise affine functions,
they are Lipschitz piecewise affine after finite subdivision.

Choose a horizontal cutoff 0 <= chi <= 1, compactly supported in
the horizontal interior of the box and equal to one near the
horizontal projection of the retained data. Choose a nondecreasing
Lipschitz function eta(v), zero below a level above every band,
equal to one near the top of the box.

Define, with [t]_{[0, L]} = max(0, min(t, L)),

        q_0(h, v) = v + chi(h) ( - sum_{j=1}^N [v - s_j(h)]_{[0, L]}
                                 + N L eta(v) ),
        S_0(h, v) = (h, q_0(h, v)).

Then:

  - Each vertical column h = const is mapped to itself
    S_0({h} x I) = {h} x I.
  - The vector field q_0(h, v) - v has vertical slope in the
    interior of each band of at most (1 + C lambda) after
    controlling chi's derivative.
  - The image of the band [s_j, s_j + L] is the single height
    b_j(h) = s_j(h) - (j-1) L. When chi(h) = 1, the union over
    j of these collapsed heights is the set Z_{box}.
  - The image of Z (over all boxes) has volume zero because each
    b_j is piecewise affine with slope O(lambda).
  - For v not in any band, q_0 is strictly increasing in v.

Then S_0 is one-to-one off the union of bands, and collapses each
band [s_j, s_j + L] to a single height. Consequently the only
non-singleton fibres of S_0 are intervals over Z. Interpolating
with the identity S_kappa = (1 - kappa) S_0 + kappa id gives
local bi-Lipschitz (with bounds depending on kappa, not
uniformly in kappa -> 0).

The anisotropic derivative bound: |q_0(h, v) - q_0(h', v')| <=
C lambda |h - h'| + C |v - v'|, so S_0 is locally Lipschitz.
Same for the inverse on the homeomorphic complement.

7.5. Protected endpoint rectangles
..................................

After flattening, the retained data sits on a family of plates
(images S_0(f(D))). At each plate, choose:

Lemma 7.4 (Paired endpoint rectangles). Let finitely many
separated compact plates in Lambda be given, with relatively
compact disjoint ambient chart neighborhoods. Let a positive
continuous motion tolerance delta on Lambda be prescribed. After
arbitrarily small mu-loss, one can choose, on each plate:

  - an origin z;
  - a unit vector n close to the plate normal, as close to desired;
  - a scalar d > 0;
  - nested protected horizontal rectangles in n^perp;

together with a nonnegative smooth compactly supported function
b_1 : Lambda -> R, extended by zero to R^3, such that:

  (i)   Delta b_1 <= C (bounded Laplacian), b_1 = o(dist(., dLambda)^2)
        at the boundary.

  (ii)  For h in the protected horizontal rectangle, put q = z + h
        and define L_h(x) = q . x - (1/2)|q|^2 + d^2. Then L_h
        supports psi_1(x) = (1/2)|x|^2 + b_1(x) globally, and its
        contact set consists exactly of the two points

                q + d n,    q - d n.

        Equivalently, sup_x { q . x - psi_1(x) } = (1/2)|q|^2 - d^2.

  (iii) For almost every retained x under mu, with X = S_0(f(x)),
        both endpoint values

                Y_+(X) = X + n ( d - <X - z, n> ),
                Y_-(X) = X + n ( -d - <X - z, n> )

        have genuine homeomorphic neighborhoods of S_0 o f and
        carry either a full-rank inverse point or an inverse point
        in a fixed source null set. (The "exceptional" alternative
        is needed only for 2 < p < 3.)

All assertions persist after shrinking the protected horizontal
rectangles. The endpoint assignments can be restricted to compact
sets on which their inverse points and their "regular or
exceptional" types have fixed separated compact ranges.

Idea of proof. Consider the small parameter space
[d_-, d_+] x (open cap about n in S^2). For a fixed X, the map
(n, d) :-> X + n(d - <X - z, n>) has volume Jacobian |r|^2 * dn,
where r = d - <X - z, n>. Since d_- > 0, |r| is bounded below,
so the endpoint law X + r n is absolutely continuous for
three-dimensional volume.

Fubini for the finite assignment measure mu therefore gives a
parameter (n, d) for which both endpoints have genuine
homeomorphic inverse types for mu-almost every retained x.

The point (ii) is algebraic. The support condition L_h <= psi_1
with equality at q + dn and q - dn is a direct computation using
psi_1(q + r n) = (1/2)|q|^2 + q.rn + r^2/2 + b_1(q + rn) and
b_1 having strict min at q (after choosing b_1 to vanish near
the protected plate). QED

The geometry: on each plate, above and below the retained
retained data, we have chosen nice target points Y_+, Y_-.
These are the "endpoints" whose inverse image under S_0 o f is
well-behaved. In the carrier construction, these will become
exact regions of F_b.


7.6. Two topological tools: cubical cutoff and opening
......................................................

Both lemmas appear inside the carrier and again, later, in
Sections 10 and 11. They are purely topological with one energy
estimate in the cutoff lemma; no reference to the Ball-Evans
construction appears in their proofs.

Put

        t(x) = max(|x_1|, |x_2|, |x_3|) - 1,
        B_r  = { x : t(x) < r },

so that the "inner cube" is B_0 = interior([-1, 1]^3) and
B_r is the outer shell for r > 0.

Lemma 7.5 (Cubical cutoff). There are universal constants
C > 2 and c > 0 with the following property. Let 1 <= p <
infinity, let s > 0 be dyadic and sufficiently small, and let
h be a homeomorphism on an open neighborhood of closure(B_{Cs})
onto its image, with h in W^{1,p}_{loc}. Suppose

        |h(x) - x| <= c s  whenever |t(x)| <= C s.

Then one can replace h inside B_{2s} by a continuous Sobolev
map F, keeping a neighborhood of dB_{2s} unchanged, so that

        F = id on B,
        F(B_{2s}) = h(B_{2s}),
        integral_{0 < t < 2s} |DF|^p <= C_p integral_{|t| <= Cs} |Dh|^p.

The only nonsingleton fibres of F are tame closed balls over a
countable set of target points. Their diameters tend to zero at
every accumulation, and the target points can accumulate only on
dB. These fibres can be opened simultaneously to give a
homeomorphism, altering F only over arbitrarily small mutually
disjoint target neighborhoods of their values. If h has volume
Lusin property N, so does F.

Sketch. Three parts.

(a) Build a smooth immersion i of a neighborhood of the two-
skeleton of {0 < t < 2s} into a small ball such that
i = id near dB_{2s} and |i - id| <= C_0 s everywhere.

(b) Sample Dh near the two-skeleton using i as a normalized
local chart, and extend to the shell. The derivative of F on the
shell is bounded by a fixed multiple of the sampled Dh on the
slightly larger shell. Multiplicities of samples are summable by
the immersion's kernel structure; this gives the energy
estimate.

(c) Remove the "uncontrolled" interior balls by a topological
push. On the source, we create a whole collection of balls, one
over each "hole" in the shell. Replace F by a homeomorphism from
the (non-hole) region to the target image and collapse each hole
to its image point. The result is continuous, injective where
the original was, and has tame ball fibres.

Upshot: we can surgically insert an exact identity or an exact
PL core inside a homeomorphism at the price of creating a null
family of tame ball fibres, and those fibres are then removable
by Lemma 7.6.

Lemma 7.6 (Relative opening). Let F : Omega -> Lambda be a
continuous proper onto map obtained by locally finite,
image-preserving applications of Lemma 7.5 in genuine
homeomorphic neighborhoods, with subsequent applications
avoiding earlier fibre values and their accumulation sets. Let
P be the countable set of non-singleton fibre values of F. Then:

  (a) The fibres of F over P are tame closed balls and form a
      null family on every compact localization.

  (b) Their value accumulations have singleton fibres.

  (c) If C subset Lambda is relatively closed with C cap P = empty,
      there is a homeomorphism H : Omega -> Lambda, finely close in
      values to F, agreeing with F on F^{-1}(C).

Sketch. Enumerate P = {p_1, p_2, ...}. For each k, F^{-1}(p_k)
is a tame closed topological ball (the construction in Lemma
7.5 creates only topological ball holes; Schoenflies in R^3
covers them). The inverse image of a small sphere in the target
around p_k is a locally flat standard sphere in the source.
Replace F on the interior by a ball homeomorphism matching the
boundary. Since the target neighbourhoods are disjoint across k,
the resulting map is a homeomorphism.

At an accumulation point of P, the diameters of the replaced
balls tend to zero, so the map equals F in the limit. QED

7.7. Exceptional boxes for 2 < p < 3
....................................

The carrier for 2 < p < 3 requires handling potentially
"exceptional" inverse data at rank-deficient points that is
NOT at a full-rank inverse image (the alternative type from
Lemma 7.4(iii)). We enclose these in marked cubes.

Lemma 7.7 (Exceptional boxes). Let g : Omega -> [0, infinity)
be locally integrable. Let K subset Omega be compact, contained
in the exceptional retained data of Lemma 7.4(iii). For every
eps > 0 there is a finite set of dyadic cubes {U_i}, and a
subset E_0 subset K with mu(K minus E_0) < eps, such that:

        integral_{U_i} g < eps for every i,
        E_0 subset union_i interior(U_i),
        the U_i have pairwise disjoint interiors,

and each U_i has a fixed inner margin U_i^- subset interior(U_i)
whose diameter is comparable to that of U_i.

Proof. g is in L^1_loc, so for almost every point x of K there
is a sufficiently small dyadic cube containing x with integral
< eps. Vitali covering plus finite subcover. QED

The volumes and mu-masses of the U_i are later charged against
the total energy budget (Stage E of Section 11).

7.8. The carrier
................

Proposition 7.8 (Carrier). Let f : Omega -> Lambda be a
homeomorphism in W^{1,p} for p > 2, orientation positive. Fix:

  - a fine target value tolerance q_* : Lambda -> (0, infinity);
  - an eps > 0 for the retained Sobolev error;
  - a positive a_Q for every compact Q subset Lambda (residual
    volume);
  - when 2 < p < 3, the exceptional cubes U_i of Lemma 7.7.

Then there exists a continuous proper onto map F_b : Omega ->
Lambda with:

  (a) Retention. On the retained rank-deficient data
      D_eps subset D, F_b equals S_0 o f in value to precision
      q_*, and its differential agrees with that of S_0 o f in
      W^{1,p} up to prescribed smallness there.

  (b) Exactness. On any compact Q subset Lambda there is a
      locally finite union of affine/PL core images
      O subset Lambda with volume(Q minus O) < a_Q, and
      F_b|F_b^{-1}(O) is locally exactly that affine/PL map.

  (c) Errors. On the retained source set, W^{1,p} error of F_b
      against f is < eps. Inside the exceptional cubes U_i
      (2 < p < 3), F_b may have large local Lipschitz constant,
      but the source measure of union_i U_i is arbitrarily small.

  (d) Isolated collapses. The non-singleton fibres of F_b are
      exactly the tame isolated balls of Lemma 7.6, openable
      at the end.

Construction. Two rounds.

ROUND 1: exact endpoint neighborhoods.
For each protected plate (Section 7.5) we have endpoints Y_+
and Y_-, with inverse types either full-rank or in the fixed
source null set. For each:

  - Choose a small target ball U_+ around Y_+ (or, for the
    exceptional type, inside a marked cube U_i of Lemma 7.7)
    with a prescribed affine or PL map phi_+ on U_+.
  - Similarly U_-, phi_-.
  - Replace S_0 o f on the preimage (S_0 o f)^{-1}(U_+) by
    phi_+ o (S_0 o f)' where the "prime" is the local inverse
    of S_0 o f restricted to that preimage. This makes the
    composite exactly phi_+ on (S_0 o f)^{-1}(U_+). Apply the
    same on U_-.
  - The replacement is a small locally bi-Lipschitz change, and
    it preserves the multiplicity structure of S_0 o f outside
    the endpoint neighborhoods. Rounds of different plates have
    disjoint endpoint neighborhoods (plates are separated), so
    all Round-1 implants are simultaneous.

ROUND 2: exact affine/PL cores on the complement.
After Round 1, Lambda is "prepared" on a locally finite family
of small target balls. On the complement, choose a dyadic
partition into cubes Q_k with side lengths s_k small compared to
q_*.

Do NOT apply the cubical cutoff directly in the target. Apply
it in the source preimage:

        t_k = max_{i} |(F_b^{-1}(Q_k)^c)_i| ... etc.

More concretely: take a dyadic cube Q_k' in the source that
contains (F_b)^{-1}(Q_k) with margin, and apply Lemma 7.5 with
h := F_b|(Q_k') and the smallness supplied by the fact that
F_b is locally bi-Lipschitz on the source region between the
"inner" and "outer" boundaries of Q_k'.

This inserts an exact identity in a small inner source cube
sitting over Q_k, gluing back to F_b outside. Composing with a
target-side affine adjustment makes F_b exactly affine or PL
on F_b^{-1}(O_k). The tame ball fibres created by the cutoff
are countable and removable by Lemma 7.6.

Energy accounting.
The cutoff lemma's estimate:

        integral |DF|^p <= C_p integral_{outer shell} |Dh|^p

controls the total energy of each insertion by the outer-shell
energy. Summing over cubes and using the shell-measure summability
gives total energy < eps.

Injectivity.
Round-2 cubes are disjoint from Round-1 neighborhoods and from
each other. The cutoffs preserve injectivity where it existed
and add only isolated tame ball fibres. Lemma 7.6 then opens all
fibres at the end.

The upshot is that after two rounds the map F_b is:

  - locally exactly affine-or-PL on a "fat" target region
    O subset Lambda (volume deficit < a_Q on each compact);
  - close to f and matching its retained rank-deficient data
    up to eps;
  - collapses only isolated tame balls, all removable.

QED

The two rounds must be done IN THIS ORDER. The residual volume
a_Q can only be prescribed AFTER the endpoint neighborhoods are
fixed. If we fixed a_Q first, we might not have enough target
"slack" left for the exact cores near the endpoint
neighborhoods.

Remark. Everything in Proposition 7.8 is controlled except the
energy inside the marked cubes U_i (2 < p < 3). Those are
charged against the total budget in Stage E of Section 11.


7.9. Full-rank correction
..........................

At full-rank points, f is locally invertible and can be replaced
by an exact affine map on a small cube near each point.

Proposition 7.9 (Full-rank correction). Let R_3 subset Omega be
the open set of full-rank points of f. For every eps > 0 and
every mu-retained neighborhood K of R_3, there exist finitely
many open cubes B_i subset R_3 with:

  - the union of B_i covers K up to mu-mass < eps;
  - the B_i have pairwise disjoint closures;
  - on each B_i, f is within eps (in the mu-normalized sense)
    of its affine comparison b_i = the best affine approximation
    of f at the center x_i.

The map obtained by replacing f by b_i on each B_i is a
homeomorphism that agrees with f outside the B_i; the W^{1,p}
mismatch is bounded by eps on K.

Idea. Because f is a Lebesgue-differentiable bijection at almost
every point of R_3, we can cover most of K by small cubes
centered at such points, on which |f - b_i| and |Df - Db_i| are
small in the appropriate mu-normalized sense. The cubical cutoff
(Lemma 7.5) then implants the exact affine b_i on the inner
part of B_i, matching f on the outer shell.

The upshot: everywhere on the full-rank portion of Omega, we can
take f to be exactly affine on a fat open region with arbitrarily
small measure deficit.


8. THE QUANTIZER: REDUCING THE TARGET TO A 2-COMPLEX
----------------------------------------------------

The carrier F_b produced in Section 7 is close to f on retained
rank-deficient data and is affine/PL on a large class of small
target cubes. But those cores are scattered; to organize them
for the strong gradient estimates of Section 10, we introduce a
specific self-map of Lambda that reduces the target to a
2-dimensional combinatorial structure.

The quantizer has three properties:

  (i)   It is a proper onto Lipschitz self-map of Lambda, with
        Lipschitz constant 1/c for a universal c > 0.

  (ii)  The image T(F_b(D_eps)) of the retained rank-deficient
        data consists of points on specific "true 2-faces" of a
        polyhedral diagram associated to a strongly convex
        potential G.

  (iii) Each such face has two natural scalar coordinates,
        radial r and perimeter s, with quantitative bounds on
        the differential d(r, s) in terms of the retained
        derivatives of F_b.

These two scalars are the ones we will later "realize" via
source walls (Section 9), then sharpen (Section 10), then
assemble (Section 11).

8.1. The convex potential
..........................

Let

        psi(x) = (1/2) |x|^2 + b_1(x),

where b_1 : Lambda -> R is the small smooth compactly supported
function of Lemma 7.4, extended by zero outside Lambda. Take b_1
small in C^2 so that psi is 2-uniformly convex:

        D^2 psi >= (1 - C ||b_1||_{C^2}) Id  >=  (1/2) Id.

Write G := psi.

The Legendre-Fenchel conjugate

        F_G(q) = sup_x { q . x - G(x) }                for q in R^3

is finite, convex, and equal to

        F_G(q) = q . (DG)^{-1}(q) - G((DG)^{-1}(q)).

The gradient map DG : R^3 -> R^3 is a smooth bi-Lipschitz
homeomorphism onto its image (which is all of R^3), and so is
its inverse (DG)^{-1}.

The "diagram" of G is the closure of

        D(G) = { (x, DG(x)) : x in R^3 }   subset R^3 x R^3.

Because DG differs from the identity by a compactly supported
perturbation (from Db_1), D(G) is a finite polyhedral complex
in the region away from the support of Db_1, and it deforms
the identity on the rest.

8.2. Faces of the diagram and two scalars
..........................................

The projection of D(G) to the q-factor (target Lambda) is a
covering map, but at "special" points x, the fibre over DG(x)
has positive dimension because corresponding piece of the
diagram has many adjacent pieces.

A face F of D(G) is a "true 2-face" if its projection to the
q-factor (target Lambda) is two-dimensional. Each such D comes
with:

  - A "cell vertex" d_D in Lambda, typically the projection of
    a preferred vertex of the 2-face.

  - Two scalar coordinates on the spatial image of D:
      * radial r(D) : Lambda -> (0, infinity), singular
        near d_D (r -> 0 at d_D, log-growth along D away
        from d_D);
      * perimeter s(D) : Lambda -> R, bounded Lipschitz.

  - A "coefficient" c(D) > 0 controlling the ratio between the
    radial and perimeter rates.

At a point y in the spatial image of a true 2-face D:
  T(y) = (DG)^{-1}(y) lies in the preimage, and the two scalars
  (r(y), s(y)) parametrize the position.

The structural relation that makes the quantizer useful: for a
point x in the retained source data D_eps, the image T(F_b(x))
sits on a true 2-face D(x) whose radial and perimeter coordinates
are close to (r(x), s(x)) determined by the retained differential
Df(x). Because the radial coordinate is log-singular at d_D,
"close in r" translates to "close in log |x - d_D|" - which
under later deformation gives strong control of the derivative.

8.3. The quantizer
..................

The quantizer

        T = (DG)^{-1}       (as a self-map of Lambda)

is proper, onto, locally Lipschitz, and in fact 1/c-Lipschitz
for a universal c > 0. It is C^infinity on the complement of the
closed union of true 1-faces and true 0-faces of the diagram (a
locally finite closed polyhedral set). At the boundary of a
true 2-face D, the one-sided derivatives of T exist and are
bounded in terms of the coefficients c(D), c(D') of adjacent
faces.

The following properties are what Section 8.4 will attach to
T:

  Retention. T maps the image F_b(D_eps) onto the true 2-faces
  of the diagram, and the two scalars (r, s) along each face
  encode the retained differential of F_b up to a specific
  scalar multiple.

  Spatial locality. T affects only a small neighborhood of
  each true 2-face: outside a fixed neighborhood of the (locally
  finite) set of true faces, T is the identity.

  Lipschitz, proper, onto. These facts follow directly from
  the structure of DG^{-1} as a Lipschitz bijection, and from
  the compact-perturbation control of the diagram.

  Quantitative derivative. The "sharp" calibrated expression
  |D(TF_b)| on retained data has the form

        |D(TF_b)(x)| <= C * r(q(x)) * |(Dr, Ds)(x)|,

  where q(x) = T(F_b(x)) is the retained target point. This is
  the key bridge from retained data to the two scalars.

Sections 8.4 through 8.6 develop these three properties in
detail, ending with the strict quantizer T_eta of T and the
derivative comparison to the carrier F_b.


8.4. Retention on true 2-faces
...............................

Lemma 8.4 (Retention). There is a specific combinatorial
assignment x |-> D(x) sending almost every retained source
point x in D_eps to a true 2-face D(x) of the diagram, such
that the image

        y(x) := T(F_b(x))

lies on D(x), and the two scalars

        r(x) := r(D(x))(y(x)),    s(x) := s(D(x))(y(x))

coincide with the two scalars determined by the retained
differential Df(x) up to smoothing error.

More precisely, write Df(x) = sigma_1 u_1 tensor v_1 +
sigma_2 u_2 tensor v_2 in its SVD (sigma_1 >= sigma_2 >= 0,
with sigma_3 = 0 for rank-2 points). Then:

    log r(x) = -log sigma_1 + O(eps),
    s(x)     = phi(sigma_1, sigma_2) + O(eps),

where phi is a fixed smooth function determined by the diagram
geometry, and eps is the smoothing error from Section 7.

The proof uses the retention structure from Lemma 7.1 (the
retained source sits on a decomposed target manifold) and the
diagrammatic structure of the quantizer (each true 2-face D
has an exact contact set with two distinguished directions).

This is the key step that allows the two scalars to encode the
retained derivative. It uses none of the later machinery and is
proved by a finite construction (Section 6 of the original).

8.5. The strict quantizer T_eta
................................

The quantizer T itself is only a Lipschitz map; we could not
directly take its Sobolev derivative in the final estimates. It
is replaced by a "strict" version:

        T_eta(y) = q + eta(q)(y - q),   q = T(y),

where eta : Lambda -> (0, infinity) is a smooth bounded function
with small global Lipschitz constant. T_eta maps Lambda to itself;
on the true faces (where y = q), it equals the identity.

Lemma 8.5 (Strict quantizer). For every prescribed smallness
tolerance epsilon there exists eta such that:

        ||T_eta - T||_{L^inf} < epsilon,
        ||D T_eta - DT||_{L^p_loc} < epsilon.

Moreover, T_eta is strictly "coercive": for every pair y_1, y_2
in Lambda, writing q_i = T(y_i),

        (T_eta(y_1) - T_eta(y_2)) . (q_1 - q_2) >= (1/2)|q_1 - q_2|^2.

Consequently T_eta is injective, locally bi-Lipschitz, and (by
the properness of T, invariance of domain, and connectedness of
Lambda) surjective onto Lambda.

Proof sketch of the inequality. Put M = sup_Lambda |y - T(y)|
< infinity (finite because T is proper and Lambda is bounded).
For any pair y_1, y_2,

        (T_eta(y_1) - T_eta(y_2)) . (q_1 - q_2)
        = |q_1 - q_2|^2
            + eta(q_1) (y_1 - q_1).(q_1 - q_2)
            - eta(q_2) (y_2 - q_2).(q_1 - q_2).

Because T is the inverse of DG and G is strongly convex, T is
monotone: (T(q) - T(q')).(q - q') >= c |q - q'|^2. This monotonicity
controls how the y_i - q_i terms behave relative to q_1 - q_2:

        |eta(q_i) (y_i - q_i).(q_1 - q_2)|
        <= ||eta||_infinity * M * |q_1 - q_2|.

Both perturbation terms are bounded this way. Hence

        (T_eta(y_1) - T_eta(y_2)) . (q_1 - q_2)
        >= (1 - 2 M ||eta||_infinity) |q_1 - q_2|^2
        >= (1/2) |q_1 - q_2|^2

by taking ||eta||_infinity < 1/(4M). The value and derivative
convergence claims follow from a standard locally finite partition-
of-unity argument, choosing the coefficient of each basis function
small relative to the local overlap count and the local derivative
bounds of the basis function. QED

We will write T_eta in place of T where appropriate. The strict
version has stronger properties than T (injectivity, quantitative
inverse control), at the cost of a small approximation error and
a slight loss of exact retention on the true faces (which T_eta
recovers by perturbing only in the interior of true faces).

8.6. What the quantizer buys
.............................

The remaining stages work only with two scalar quantities on the
source: the radial coordinate r and the perimeter coordinate s.
Concretely:

    - Section 9 realizes each retained source point as sitting on
      a specific "wall" of the target, with parameters (r, s).

    - Section 10 sharpens the parameters to (1 + O(eta))-accuracy
      on a calibrated mesh.

    - Section 11 recovers the gradient from (D r, D s) via the
      calibrated sharp estimate, then assembles with the
      scattered full-rank corrections from Section 7.

    - Section 12 smooths the result.

The figure below summarizes the logical flow.

    [F_b] --> [T] --> [T_eta] --> [realized h] --> [sharp q_h] --> [smooth f_j]

9. REALIZATION BY WALLS AND GUARDS
----------------------------------

At this point we have a carrier F_b: Omega -> Lambda close to f on
retained data, and a quantizer T that reduces retained differentials
to two scalars. The next task is to construct a locally
bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism

        h : M_x -> M_y

from an "exterior" source M_x subset Omega to an "exterior"
target M_y subset Lambda, such that:

  (a) h maps each retained source point x to a target point h(x)
      whose two scalars under T_eta are close to (r(x), s(x)).

  (b) h is close to F_b in the fine-tolerance sense.

  (c) h equals F_b_h_* on the exact boundary data from Section 7.

The tools are: standard walls (a specific family in the target),
and guards (a specific source-side net that controls how much the
realization can deviate).

9.1. Standard walls
....................

The target Lambda contains a standard family of "walls":

    - For each true 2-face D of the diagram and each level
      t in (0, infinity), the radial wall

            A(D, t)  = { y in Lambda : r(D)(y) = t },

      an annular submanifold of codimension 1.

    - For each true 1-face e of the diagram (any perimeter-type
      edge) and each level s in R, the perimeter wall

            B(e, s)  = { y in Lambda : s(e)(y) = s },

      a meridian-disk-shaped submanifold of codimension 1.

These two families are defined globally on Lambda (up to the
combinatorial edges of the diagram) and are locally finite: each
point of Lambda meets finitely many walls.

The key topological fact: for a transverse intersection of an A
wall and a B wall that are "compatible" (same face D_2 and its
incident 1-face e), the local intersection is:

    - an arc spanning the two ends of the annulus (the "central
      meridian"), and
    - possible additional circles, each of which is null-homotopic
      in the A wall.

This is proved by elementary handle-body considerations: the A
wall is an annulus, the B wall is a meridian disk, and the
meridian cuts the annulus exactly once. Any additional
intersections are circles that bound disks on B and hence lie in
trivial homotopy classes of A.

Consequently, in the source, we can "route" each retained point
by crossing walls at prescribed levels, in a controlled way, and
the routing can be made locally finite.

9.2. The realization theorem
............................

Theorem 9.1 (Realization). Fix the carrier F_b, the strict
quantizer T_eta, and a locally bounded source-to-target anchor
map h_* on an "exact" boundary portion of Omega. Given a guard
net {kappa_g}, prescribed local tolerances, and any positive
Sobolev error eps > 0, there exists a locally bi-Lipschitz
homeomorphism

        h : M_x -> M_y

satisfying:

  (a) Retention. For almost every retained x in D_eps,
      T_eta(h(x)) is on the associated true 2-face D(x), with
      the radial and perimeter scalars within (prescribed)
      accuracy of (r(x), s(x)).

  (b) Fine motion. |h(x) - F_b(x)| < q_*(x) for x in
      D_eps and for every other point where q_* (the carrier
      target tolerance) has been prescribed.

  (c) Boundary. h coincides with h_* = F_b_h_* on the exact
      boundary region.

  (d) Guard avoidance. h(x) avoids the target images of every
      guard {kappa_g} that has been excluded by the capacity
      schedule of Section 10.

The realization is not proven by direct construction of h.
Instead, one:

  1. Picks a locally finite source net {g}.
  2. For each net point g, picks a "guard" kappa_g: a compact
     path from g to a boundary anchor, whose image under the
     base map h_* ends at two distinct extremal target points.
  3. Verifies that, with high probability over a finite random
     choice of routing options, h avoids all guards and
     realizes all retained parameters.
  4. Uses a barrier argument (Section 9.3) to show that
     non-realization is impossible: if any retained parameter
     were not realized, the source would support too much
     Sobolev energy to be approximated.

We defer the barrier argument and the detailed routing to
Section 9.4.


9.3. The barrier: guards force realization
...........................................

Realization is proved indirectly, using a Sobolev energy barrier.

Lemma 9.2 (Guard barrier). Fix a chart ball U_a in Lambda, a
positive local capacity A_a, and any prescribed guard spacing
h_0 > 0. If a guard-free locally bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism h
satisfies

        integral_{U_a} G_h^p >= A_a,

where G_h is the pre-slope density of h (defined in Section
10.5), then some retained parameter is NOT realized by more
than a bounded deficit.

Proof outline. Assume some retained parameter is off by a
definite delta from its prescribed value. Take any source sphere
Sigma_R inside U_a around the preimage of the offending
parameter, with radius R <= h_0.

Each source path from the wall-stratum to the closest guard must
cross a level set of the offending parameter. If the parameter
misses its target value by delta, this level set carries at least
a fixed cost c_delta of "crossing energy" on every such path (the
"crossing bound"). The spherical cost

        integral_{Sigma_R} G_h^p >= c R^{2-p}

is a lower bound on the local Sobolev energy of h at radius R.

Integrating over R in [h, 2h] gives a per-annulus lower bound of
c h^{3-p}. Packing a fixed number a of disjoint chart balls of
radius h inside U_a gives

        integral_{U_a} G_h^p >= c a h^{3-p}.

Now p > 2 implies 2 - p < 0, so as h -> 0 the quantity
c a h^{3-p} = c a h * h^{2-p} blows up. For any fixed a >= 1,
taking h small enough forces the integral above any prescribed
capacity A_a. Contradiction.

Consequently the situation "guard-free but parameter not
realized" is impossible when the guard spacing is chosen small
enough relative to the capacity. Transcribing back, every
solution h that respects the guard net must realize all retained
parameters up to the prescribed tolerance.

The barrier uses only the fact that p > 2 (so the exponent
2 - p < 0) and that the guard crossings have definite energy
cost. The full bookkeeping (Lemma 7.x of the original) is
routine but lengthy.

9.4. Diagonal routing (summary)
................................

The proof of Theorem 9.1 combines:

    - the barrier lemma (which says "realization is forced"),

    - a partial construction of h that realizes ALL parameters
      EXCEPT possibly a set of measure zero (which we handle by
      the barrier's "off by delta" clause),

    - the diagonal routing, which produces that partial
      construction.

The partial construction is:

    (1) Choose a routing tree: a locally finite collection of
        "walls" (preimages of the target walls) in the source,
        together with "occupied intervals" of parameters.

    (2) On each wall and each occupied interval, place a
        specific scalar coordinate (a "stair") that maps the
        source parameter to the target parameter.

    (3) Assemble the walls and stairs into a PL structure.

The support of the routing is a locally finite 2-complex Sigma in
the source (a union of walls and stair-flaps). Away from Sigma
the map h is the local identity or the carrier value. On Sigma,
h maps each stratum to the target stratum at the prescribed
parameter.

The barrier then rules out any parameter that missed its target:
because the barrier integral would blow up if any parameter were
off by delta.

The full construction (Sections 7-8 of the original) is quite
involved; we have summarized only the essential structure.


10. CALIBRATED MESHES AND SHARP GRADIENT ESTIMATES
--------------------------------------------------

After realization we have h close to F_b with the parameters
correct on retained data. The next step: recover the actual
gradient Df from the parameters, and prove it converges.

Three subgoals:

    (1)  A sharp derivative estimate
             |D q_h(x)| <= C_1 + O(eta)  on retained data,
         where q_h = T_eta o h,
             C_1 = sqrt(2) for rank-2 (Frobenius norm),
             C_1 = 1 for rank-1.

    (2)  Equality is nearly achieved on a calibrated mesh, so
         the p-mean energy on each saturation cube comes close
         to its theoretical minimum.

    (3)  Uniform convexity (Clarkson's inequality) then shows
         that closeness to the minimum means closeness to the
         flat gradient. This is Stage E.

10.1. Slots and slabs
.....................

The mesh construction. On each "sharp" tetrahedron sigma (a
piece of the master lattice at correct scale), place "slots" of
width W at prescribed positions along each axis. Between slots
are "slabs" of the two colours, which carry the walls A and B.

Properties:

    - Central hits (of the source walls with tetrahedron faces)
      can be placed in consecutive slots of axial spacing at
      least (1 - C eta) W, starting at offset of order eta H
      from the lower endpoint.

    - Fractional positions stay in the fixed interval
      [gamma(eta), 1 - gamma(eta)], gamma(eta) > 0.

    - All but C delta' H / W of the disks of a family are
      slabs separating lower and upper vertex layers of the
      tetrahedron.

    - The main triangles of these slabs have slopes
      O_eta(delta') from the coordinate planes.

    - Successive slabs are separated along that axis by at
      least (1 - C eta) W.

    - In "parallel mode" the same holds between the two families,
      arranged in consecutive separate bands.

These are engineering choices on a fixed combinatorial structure
(the tetrahedron). All quantities depend only on the fixed
combinatorial data and on the tolerances (eta, delta', W/H).

10.2. The master system
.......................

Assembly: on each sharp tetrahedron, the slot/slab pattern
extends affinely across faces. The resulting "master system" is
locally finite per compact, has bounded complexity per coarse
index, and preserves:
    - the slot positions,
    - the wall-crossing structure,
    - the parameter separations.

The key "cluster condition" is that at each slot, the two walls
are strictly separated by a fixed slack, and the opposite-band
distance is controlled by W and H in a specific way. This
allows the two scalars to remain "near-independent" inside a
slot, so that the sharp gradient estimate can be applied
locally.

Finally, we add transition cells along the boundary of the
"sharp" region. These carry the parameters into the "weak
region" (where the mesh is coarser and the gradient is not
required to be sharp). The transition cells have bounded
slope but no requirement of sharpness.

The master system is described by a finite list of normalized
configurations (Lemma 10.2 in the original). Everything
downstream depends only on that finite list and on the
tolerances.


10.3. Broadening from a_0 to a_1
................................

The initial strips in the master system have half-width a_0 = c_0 W
(microscopic). We extend them to a_1 = (1 - C eta) W/2, which is
half the standard slot spacing minus a small slack. The extension
is achieved by a common ambient flow.

Put a(x) = smooth cutoff that equals a_0 in a fixed neighborhood of
each wall and a_1 outside a larger excluded-set buffer. Choose the
cutoff so that |nabla a| <= C/N, where N is a fixed large integer
that controls the buffer width.

For a supporting main triangle P (of the master lattice), define
the parameter by the signed axial height l_P(x):

        u_P(x) = (W/2) (1 + l_P(x) / a(x)).

Near a_0 (deep in the wall support), u_P agrees with the original
sharp value. Near a_1 (transition region), u_P is written in terms
of the wider strip.

Claim: the gradient of u_P satisfies

        |nabla u_P| <= 1 + C eta

on the wide-strip portion of the saturation cube. This uses the
smallness of nabla a and the fact that the supporting main triangle
is near-horizontal (slope O_eta(delta') from 10.1).

All the extensions are compatible: the ambient flow is supported in
a fixed neighborhood of the walls, and the transition cells
(between a_0 and a_1) are chosen once and for all.

10.4. Sharp gradient contract
..............................

Proposition 10.4 (Sharp gradient). Fix tolerances (eta, delta', c_*,
zeta) as in the schedule of Section 10.5. The calibrated islands
(master-system tetrahedra with slot placements) admit pre-stacks
with the following quantitative properties:

  (a) Throughout an enlarged equal-weight island, the pre-slope
      density is at most C M_sigma in aligned units, where M_sigma
      is a coarse majorant independent of microscopic master widths,
      central crossing angles, final guard gaps, H, W, and delta'.

  (b) In deep regular low-leakage portions, each scalar gradient
      satisfies

            nabla u_j = epsilon_j e_i + O(eta),
            |nabla u_j| <= 1 + C eta,

      with a fixed sign epsilon_j in {-1, +1} and a fixed coordinate
      e_i.

  (c) At concurrent independent strips (two active scalars), the
      two-coordinate squared Frobenius cost is at most 2 + C eta.

  (d) At an occupied diagonal transition after routing, the active
      coordinate has gradient +/- nabla u_j +/- nabla u_k, and its
      squared Frobenius cost has the same bound as (b) or (c).

  (e) In "parallel mode" (opposite bands disjoint in the deep
      portion), the squared Frobenius cost is at most 1 + C eta.

  (f) In a regular tetrahedron, the excluded CNW neighborhoods of
      its faces, all its main slab creases, and its non-slab disks
      occupy relative volume at most C_N (W/H + delta').

  (g) Occupied-stair speeds multiply all bounds by at most
      (1 + C zeta)^2.

Here "occupied-stair speed" is the fractional slope of the
realized scalar vs. the source coordinate, controlled by Lemma 10.1
(slots).

Consequences. For the derivative tilde-Y of the calibrated
scalars along the surface of a saturation cube,

        |D tilde-Y|_F^2 <= 2 + C eta on rank-2 data,
        |D tilde-Y|_F^2 <= 1 + C eta on rank-1 data.

This is the sharp upper bound for what the retained derivative can
contribute. The complementary equality--the fact that the bound is
nearly achieved--will be used in Section 11.1 to recover the
gradient from the energy.

The proofs of (a)-(g) are a combination of:
    - the master-system composition (10.1, 10.2);
    - geometry of the pure slot/slab configuration;
    - broadening (10.3);
    - finite template enumeration for the combinatorial data
      (Section 10.3 of the original, Lemma ms:sharp-contract);
    - averaging of jittered displacements (Section 10.4 of the
      original).

They are intricate but entirely finite; nothing in them depends on
the mesh or on the carrier beyond the "coarse majorant" M_sigma.

10.5. Prior capacities and simultaneous choice
...............................................

The pieces we have constructed so far depend on several parameters:
the mesh resolution H, the "resolution weight" W/H, the jitter on
mesh vertices, and the guards. The order of choice matters because
some bounds blow up with the "pre-slope density"; we cannot afford
to fix that density before its multipliers (e.g., bad-cube measures)
are known.

The schedule.

  1. Fix tolerances eta, delta', zeta (as in Section 10.1). Fix the
     finite sharp-template data (from Proposition 10.4) and the
     strong-convexity bounds.

  2. Fix the carrier F_b and its regional budgets (from
     Proposition 7.8 plus 7.9).

  3. Fix the polar data (full-cell layers, activation radii).

  4. Select the finite set of calibrated islands (saturation cubes).

  5. For each buffered barrier chart U_j, fix a "prior capacity"
     A_j from a rough majorant: A_j = 2^{j+6} (1 + B_j), where B_j
     is any deterministic bound on integral_{U_j} G^p from the
     master system and the carrier.

  6. Choose guards (net spacings kappa_g) small enough that the
     barrier lemma 9.2 is consistent with the capacities:
     each guard contributes at most A_j / (some local count).

  7. Choose the boundary widths d_0.

  8. Choose the master system step H and slot width W: W/H << 1
     small enough that dirty volume C_N(W/H + delta') fits
     under pre-set energy budget.

  9. Choose a "permissible jitter" (a random jitter of mesh
     vertices) such that:

            P(integral_{U_j} G^p (with jitter) >= A_j) < 2^{-j-6}

     for every j, and similar bounds for the sharp errors.

 10. Sample with sufficiently small weights.

 11. Impose strict substitutions and final smoothing.

The simultaneous probability in step 9 is handled by the Lovasz
Local Lemma (Moser-Tardos 2010): the "bad events" (integral_{U_j}
G^p too large) have small probabilities and are only locally
dependent on the jitter choices, so there exists a jitter that
avoids all of them simultaneously.

This gives the construction of A_j (Section 10.5 conclusion):
Lemma 10.11 (Prior capacities and simultaneous choice).

The comparison with the naive order (fix A_j after guards) is:
the naive order gives too large a bound for A_j, which then makes
some later quantity (e.g., the boundary width, or the final W^{1,p}
error) too large to fit. The new order gives small A_j, which
works.

That's the "constant bookkeeping" of the original Section 8.


11. ASSEMBLY AND STRICT APPROXIMATION
-------------------------------------

The final section combines everything: the carrier, the
realization, the sharp estimates, and the smoothing from Section
6. The main work is:

    11.1  Recover the gradient from the sharp energy bound
          (uniform convexity).

    11.2  Handle exact boundary data and mesh-edge compatibility

    11.3  Correct marked cubes for 2 < p < 3

    11.4  Apply strict substitutions and final smoothing

11.1. Uniform convexity recovery
................................

The key inequality is due to Clarkson (1936). For p >= 2 and
any matrices A, B (of the same shape),

        |(A+B)/2|_F^p + |(A-B)/2|_F^p
            <= (|A|_F^p + |B|_F^p)/2 - c_p |A-B|_F^p,

with a positive constant c_p. Equivalently,

        |A - B|_F^p <= C_p ( (|A|_F^p + |B|_F^p)/2 - |(A+B)/2|_F^p ).

Structural setup. Let q_h = T_eta o h be the calibrated map. We
have shown:

    (a) |D q_h|_F^p <= 2 + C eta on rank-2 saturation cubes,
        <= 1 + C eta on rank-1 cubes (from Proposition 10.4).

    (b) integral |D q_h|_F^p >= (near minimal) * |saturation|.

    (c) The mean gradient of q_h on each saturation cube is close
        to the mean gradient of Df (from the retention property
        of Lemma 8.4 plus the "fine motion" q_*).

Then Clarkson's inequality gives:

        |D q_h - A_0|_F^p <= C_p ( (2 + C eta) - (2 - eps) )
                          <= C p (eps + eta)

on each saturation cube where A_0 is the mean gradient. Hence

        integral_{saturation} |D q_h - Df|_F^p <= C_p (eps + eta) |saturation|,

uniformly in the mesh.

Interpolating with the "dirty" fraction of each saturation cube
(from Proposition 10.4(f)), and summing over the calibrated
islands, gives the final sharp energy estimate.

The final result: the map q_h = T_eta o h has

        integral_{Omega} |D q_h - Df|_F^p  <  eps_1,

for a prescribed eps_1 > 0. This is the "sharp recovery" step.

The recovery uses only:
    - p > 2 (for Clarkson),
    - the sharp upper bound (from 10.4),
    - the near-minimal energy (from the mesh construction),
    - the mean-gradient closeness (from the retention in 8.4).

It does not use any other property of the carrier.


11.2. Exact boundary data and mesh-edge compatibility
.....................................................

The realization theorem 9.1 requires h to agree exactly with
F_b =: h_* on the "exact boundary" of the target. That is: on
the images of the carrier's exact neighborhoods near dLambda,
h must be exactly the carrier value, not just close.

Achieving this uses two considerations.

First, the wall structure of Section 9.1 is chosen so that its
radial and perimeter walls accumulate to the boundary of Lambda.
At the boundary, the perimeter parameter vanishes identically,
and the radial parameter equals a fixed value. Thus the walls
"anchor" to the boundary in a way that is compatible with F_b's
boundary values.

Second, at each mesh edge (a codimension-2 stratum of the
calibrated mesh, in the sense of Section 10.1), the two scalars
must be "locally trivial" (a product of a circle or interval with
the standard parameter) so that they can be chained across
adjacent cells. This is the "edge matching" property of the
quantizer.

Lemma 11.1 (Common opening + edge matching). There is a single
common opening homeomorphism

        f_o : Omega -> Lambda,

finely close to F_b, that:

  (a) equals F_b on the exact boundary region of Omega;

  (b) has EXACTLY the carrier's original edge-crossing counts
      on every mesh edge of the calibrated mesh;

  (c) satisfies the pre-stack contract (Definition of section
      6 of the original) everywhere else.

The proof assembles the openings of Lemma 7.6 over the wall union
of Section 9.1, uses the boundary anchoring to derive (a), and
uses the mesh-edge structure of Section 10.1 to derive (b).

11.3. Marked-cube corrections for 2 < p < 3
...........................................

For 2 < p < 3, the marked cubes U_i from Lemma 7.7 may carry large
local Lipschitz constants that cannot be reduced by the mesh
(their parameters are outside the retained regular region). These
are handled by a source-side self-homeomorphism that compresses
the cube interior.

Lemma 11.2 (Marked-cube compression). Let U_i be a closed cube of
side ell_U, centered at y_i, with U_i^- subset interior(U_i) its
inner margin. Suppose the prescribed map k is locally bi-Lipschitz
on a neighborhood of U_i minus U_i^-, extends to a homeomorphism
Omega -> Lambda, and satisfies the collar budget

        sum_i (ell_U / d_U) integral_{C_i} |D k|^p  <  eps_k,

where C_i is a collar of U_i of width d_U. Then there is a local
homeomorphism K_i : R^3 -> R^3, fixing the boundary, compressing
the interior of U_i into a small ball of radius (1 - rho) ell_U,
and satisfying

        integral_{U_i} |D K_i|^p <= C_p ell_U / (3 - p) * 1,

for some fixed C_p depending only on p and the cube geometry.
The compressed cube can be placed inside any target open ball of
radius 2 rho ell_U.

Energy cost. In source-side cube polar coordinates, K_i sends
t in [0, rho] linearly to [0, (1-1/alpha)rho] (inner compression)
and t in [rho, 1] via a radial homeomorphism onto [(1-1/alpha)rho,
1]. The inner integral is bounded by C_p rho^{3-p}, which goes to
zero as rho -> 0 because 3 - p > 0 for p < 3. The outer integral
is bounded by

        C_p ell_U integral_{partial} |Dk|^p * integral_{alpha}^{1} t^{2-p} dt,

and the last integral is bounded by 1/(3 - p), a fixed constant
independent of rho.

Consequently, by choosing rho small enough (depending on eps and
the collar budget), we can make the total W^{1,p} energy of the
compression arbitrarily small, even though the map K_i may have
arbitrarily large local Lipschitz constant (since it has to
compress a finite-volume region into a tiny ball).

Applying K_i to all marked cubes simultaneously (they are
disjoint by construction) gives a locally bi-Lipschitz
homeomorphism whose composition with the carrier map has small
W^{1,p} error but whose local Lipschitz constant is huge inside
each U_i. This is acceptable for the final smoothing (Section 6
works for any locally bi-Lipschitz map, but the extremely
large Lipschitz constant inside U_i will be smoothed away by
the final diffeomorphism construction).

11.4. Strict substitution and final smoothing
.............................................

Put k := T_eta o P^eps o h, where:

    - P^eps is the strict polar quantizer from Section 8.5;

    - T_eta is the strict target quantizer from Section 8.5;

    - h is the realized homeomorphism from Section 9.

Then k is a locally bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism Omega -> Lambda
with the following properties (from Sections 8.5, 9.3, 10.4,
11.1):

    (a) k has exact boundary values (via h, F_b, and the exact
        data of Lemma 11.1);

    (b) integral_{Omega} |Dk - Df|_F^p < eps_1 for a prescribed
        eps_1 (from the sharp recovery in 11.1);

    (c) on each marked cube U_i (2 < p < 3), k has small W^{1,p}
        error (via 11.3) but potentially large local Lipschitz;

    (d) on the exact region O subset Lambda (Section 7.8), k is
        locally exactly an affine/PL map (preserved by the
        strict substitutions).

To finish, apply Theorem 6.1 (strong smoothing of locally
bi-Lipschitz maps) to k with Sobolev tolerance eps_2 << eps_1 and
value tolerance matching the fine tolerance q_*. The result is a
C^infinity diffeomorphism

        f_j : Omega -> Lambda

with

        ||f_j - k||_{W^{1,p}} < eps_2.

The final W^{1,p} error against f:

        ||f_j - f||_{W^{1,p}} <= ||f_j - k|| + ||k - f|| <= eps_2 + eps_1 < eps.

Both approximations have exactly the target Lambda. QED


12. PROOF OF THE MAIN THEOREM
-----------------------------

We are ready to give the complete proof. The structure is:

    Step 1. Fix the small constants (in the order of Section 5).
    Step 2. Construct the carrier (Section 7).
    Step 3. Construct the quantizer (Section 8).
    Step 4. Realize the walls (Section 9).
    Step 5. Extend to meshes (Section 10).
    Step 6. Assemble and correct (Section 11).
    Step 7. Smooth to C-infinity (Section 6).

Step 1. Fix constants.
    Prescribe eps > 0 (final Sobolev error target). Choose
    smallness parameters in the order specified in Section 5
    (eta, gamma, zeta, c_*, then various majorant/error ratios,
    then residual volumes a_Q, then capacities A_j, then guards,
    widths, meshes, samples). Verify the total error from all
    steps (which we have been designing to fit) is < eps.

Step 2. Carrier (Proposition 7.8).
    Construct F_b : Omega -> Lambda, close to f on retained
    rank-deficient data, locally affine/PL on a fat target
    region O subset Lambda, with isolated tame ball fibres
    (removable at the end). Fix residual volume a_Q and
    exceptional cubes U_i for 2 < p < 3.

    Discarded: any source data of mu-mass < eps where F_b
    differs from f by more than eps. Sum of discarded and
    worked-on is Omega up to mu-null.

Step 3. Quantizer (Section 8).
    Fix the strongly convex potential psi = (1/2)|x|^2 + b_1
    (small b_1). Define T = (DG)^{-1} and its strict version
    T_eta (Lemma 8.5). The image T(F_b(D_eps)) lies on true
    2-faces of the diagram (Lemma 8.4).

Step 4. Walls (Theorem 9.1).
    Choose guard net spacings small enough to be consistent with
    the capacities A_j. Construct h : M_x -> M_y local bi-
    Lipschitz homeomorphism, close to F_b on retained data,
    realizing parameters, agreeing with F_b on exact boundary.

Step 5. Mesh extension (Section 10).
    Fix slot placements, master system, broadening. Verify the
    sharp gradient contract (Proposition 10.4). Choose mesh
    resolution H, weight W such that:

        - W/H << 1 (dirty volume small),
        - jitter exists making capacities A_j hold,
        - all saddle-point estimates (Section 10.5) fit.

Step 6. Assembly (Section 11).
    Compose with strict quantizer to get q_h = T_eta o P^eps o h.
    Verify sharp recovery (Section 11.1). On marked cubes U_i
    for 2 < p < 3, apply marked cube compression (Lemma 11.2).
    Result: k locally bi-Lipschitz with

        ||k - f||_{W^{1,p}} < eps_1.

Step 7. Smoothing (Theorem 6.1).
    Apply strong smoothing to k with Sobolev tolerance eps - eps_1
    to get C^infinity diffeomorphism f_j : Omega -> Lambda with

        ||f_j - k||_{W^{1,p}} < eps - eps_1.

    Then ||f_j - f|| < eps. Both have image exactly Lambda.

Conclusion. Main Theorem holds. QED

Section-by-section summary of what was needed:

    Orientation. Because f is a homeomorphism between
    connected oriented domains, either f is orientation-
    preserving or we can reflect the target and handle the
    other case. Take f orientation-preserving.

    Existence of retained rank-deficient data. Section 7,
    Lemma 7.1 uses the Alberti-Marchese decomposability
    bundle to find a forbidden cone; Lemma 7.2 uses Sion
    minimax for a simultaneous null set; Lemma 7.3 flattens
    the retained data via the S_0 map.

    Exact endpoint rectangles. Section 7, Lemma 7.4 uses
    a Fubini argument to find endpoints with good inverse
    types; Lemma 7.5 (cubical cutoff) implants exact identity
    or PL cores inside the carrier without destroying
    injectivity; Lemma 7.6 (opening) removes the resulting
    tame ball fibres topologically.

    Carrier construction. Section 7.8 (Proposition 7.8)
    two rounds: endpoints, then exact affine/PL cores on
    the rest.

    Quantizer. Sections 8.1-8.5.

    Walls. Section 9.

    Meshes + sharp estimates. Section 10.

    Assembly + smoothing. Section 11 + Theorem 6.1.

This completes the proof.

Final remarks
-------------

This document is a compressed, annotated presentation of the
proof of the Main Theorem. Where the original requires lengthy
bookkeeping (finite template lists, LLL arguments, moment
estimates), we have summarized and cited the original. Where
the original requires topology (Schoenflies, Edwards-Kirby,
Hamilton), we have stated the tools and indicated their use.

A reader seeking the full lemma-by-lemma proof with all
constants and all references should consult the original
"Strong diffeomorphic approximation in three dimensions for
p > 2" document.

A reader who wants to understand the shape of the problem, the
five major obstacles, and how each is overcome, should be able
to follow the first twelve sections of this document without
any special mathematical background beyond multivariable
calculus and linear algebra.

We hope the result is useful to both audiences.
