The bond-disordered planar Ising ferromagnet is the canonical case of a random system whose critical behavior is marginal in the Harris sense. The pure model at its critical point has vanishing specific-heat exponent alpha, so the Harris criterion does not decide whether weak coupling disorder changes the leading critical indices. A prediction from the 1980s and a proof for weak disorder, sharp for quenched correlations, are the two subjects.

./pdf/harris-1974-effect-of-random-defects-on-the-critical-behavior-of-the-ising-model.pdf

The result is a logarithmic correction to the quenched spin-correlation variance. On the square lattice with independent bond signs, at the physical inverse temperature of the random model, the relative second moment of the free-boundary infinite-volume correlation grows as (log r)^{1/4+o(1)}. The exponent 1/4 was predicted by Shankar in 1987 and reinterpreted through an infinite hierarchy of exponents by Ludwig in 1990. The present construction proves it, and identifies the physical critical inverse temperature.

./pdf/ludwig-1990-infinite-hierarchies-of-exponents-in-a-diluted-ferromagnet-and-their-interpretation.pdf

The other historical anchors are Dotsenko and Dotsenko, whose replica calculation gave a double-logarithmic specific-heat divergence, and Shalaev, whose vertex-model renormalization assigned marginal logarithmic corrections to correlation functions without changing the leading indices.

The proof is built on the deterministic critical structure of the pure model. Chelkak, Hongler, and Izyurov proved conformal invariance of critical spin correlations and computed the separated operator-product limits for primary fields. The limits that enter are the energy-energy and energy-spin coefficients, together with the exact conditional-averaging bounds for the pure critical kernel.

The random model is treated through its FK representation. Fortuin and Kasteleyn introduced the random-cluster model, and Edwards and Sokal gave the q = 2 Edwards-Sokal coupling to the Ising Gibbs measure. Aizenman and Wehr, and then Chayes and Shtengel, proved the vanishing of averaged magnetization at the self-dual point, without excluding a critical phase. Sharpness of the FK-Ising phase transition follows from the decision-tree influence argument of Duminil-Copin, Raoufi, and Tassion; the underlying influence inequality is that of O'Donnell, Saks, Schramm, and Servedio, with its monotone-measure extension. Critical RSW crossing estimates are those of Duminil-Copin-Hongler-Nolin and Chelkak-Duminil-Copin-Hongler.

What follows is the proof.

Notation. Lambda_n = Z^2 cap [-n,n]^2. A bond is an unordered pair of nearest neighbors. The environment omega = (xi_e) is a family of independent symmetric signs. For eps in (0,1), beta, and n, the finite-volume free boundary Gibbs measure on Lambda_n is

mu_{n,beta,omega}(sigma) proportional to exp( sum_{e in E(Lambda_n)} beta (1 + eps xi_e) sigma_x sigma_y ).

Write <F>_{n,beta,omega} for the expectation, and <F>^+_{n,beta,omega} for the plus-boundary measure with spins fixed to +1 outside Lambda_n.

The physical critical inverse temperature is
beta_c(eps) = inf{ beta >= 0 : E[ lim_{n -> infty} <sigma_0>^+_{n,beta,omega} ] > 0 }.

For each integer r >= 3 define
C_{eps,omega}(r) = lim_{n -> infty, n >= r} < sigma_0 sigma_{(r,0)} >^{free}_{n, beta_c(eps), omega}.
The limit exists by ferromagnetic monotonicity. Put
R_eps(r) = E[ C_{eps,omega}(r)^2 ] / ( E[ C_{eps,omega}(r) ]^2 ).

Main theorem. For each eps small enough, with eps_0 > 0 fixed, and as integer r -> infty,
log R_eps(r) = (1/4 + o(1)) log log r.

The limits are taken in the order: thermodynamic limit first, then disorder average, then r -> infty. That order is part of the statement.

The physical beta_c(eps) is beta_*(eps), the unique zero of the strictly increasing function (b_+ + b_-)/2 where b_+-(beta,eps) = log sinh(2 beta (1 +- eps)). Equivalently,
sinh( 2 beta_*(eps) (1+eps) ) sinh( 2 beta_*(eps) (1-eps) ) = 1,
with beta_*(eps) -> beta_0 = (1/2) log(1 + sqrt(2)) as eps -> 0. At this beta, m_eps(beta_*(eps)) = 0. In log-sinh coordinates log sinh(2 K_e) = t + D xi_e, the model is (t, D) = (0, D_*(eps)) with D_*(eps) = (1/2)(b_+ - b_-)(beta_*, eps) > 0.

The reference model. The critical pure Ising model on Z^2 at beta_0 carries two local densities:
energy: e(z) = (1/4) sum_{|v-z|=1} ( sigma_z sigma_v - <sigma_z sigma_v>_0 ), scale dimension b = 1
spin: sigma_z, scale dimension d = 1/8
with <.>_0 the critical plane expectation of the pure model. The separated operator-product limits hold uniformly in direction:
<e(z) e(0)>_0 = (a^2 + o(1)) / |z|^2,
<e(z) sigma_0>_0 = (a_sigma a / (2 |z|)) (1 + o(1)),
with a > 0 and a_sigma > 0 the lattice field factors.

./pdf/chelkak-hongler-izyurov-2023-correlations-of-primary-fields-in-the-critical-ising-model.pdf

Conditioning. For B_R(z) the max-norm square of radius R about z, P_{R,z} averages the interior in the critical reference model; P_{R,z}F is a function of the B_R(z) contour. The tower property <P_{R,z} F>_0 = <F>_0 holds. The exact line identities
P_{B w, z} ( w^b P_{w, z} O_b ) = (B w)^b P_{B w, z} O_b
hold for fixed integer B, sufficiently large, independent of w and z. The calibrated charge kappa_b = lim_w w^b <O_b>^+_{disk(w, 0)} is nonzero. The linear functional ell_b(F) = lim_R R^b ( <F>^+_{disk(4R)} - <F>_0 ) agrees with kappa_b on the line w^b P_w O_b.

Two decay bounds are also part of the input. For F of parity even or odd supported in B_w(z), and R >= (1+c) w,
norm( P_{R,z} F - <P_{R,z} F>_0 )_infty <= C (w/R)^b norm(F)_infty,
with exponent b = 1 for F even, b = d for F odd, and one stronger decay factor for F with zero ell_b charge. Also,
norm( P_{R,z} e(y) )_infty <= C / R,
norm( P_{R,z} sigma_y )_infty <= C / R^d,
for y at fixed positive relative distance from the contour.

./pdf/chelkak-hongler-izyurov-2015-conformal-invariance-of-spin-correlations-in-the-planar-ising-model.pdf

Buffered comparison. For two concentric squares with fixed positive relative gap, the conditional reference law of the interior spins given an outer contour assignment is comparable to its mixture under the plane reference law, up to a uniform constant. The absolute logarithm of the ratio is at most C (w/R)^theta for some theta > 0, uniformly in the outer contour. This gives the pointwise comparison and the normalized positive weights used below.

Critical RSW-type crossing estimates for the FK-Ising model at the self-dual coupling give positive probability of open circuits in topological rectangles of fixed aspect ratio, uniformly in the ratio.

Nothing else about the pure critical model is used. Everything in the block transformation follows from the two local densities, the conditional-averaging and buffered-comparison bounds, the two decay bounds, and the FK-Ising crossing estimates.

The block transformation, structural. Let r_0 and L be odd positive integers, L fixed and large, and put r_j = r_0 L^j, r = r_j, R = L r. Partition Z^2 into disjoint max-norm squares of side r with centers in r Z^2; these are the r-cells. The parent (c) of an r-cell c is the R-cell that contains it.

At scale r, a list (h_i) is a family of real spin functions indexed by I_r. The index set consists of
principal slot p_c, one per cell c, whose function may use spins in a fixed physical halo S r of c, S a fixed large integer, and
animal slot (A, c), with A a nonempty finite connected set of r-cells and c in A, whose function may use spins in the union of the cells of A.
Sizes n(p_c) = 1, n(A, c) = |A|.

Every slot carries a spin carrier (the spins actually used) and a dependence carrier (the edge variables and decision seeds that determined its value and its representation). Both are contained in the geometric cell or animal of the slot, or in the fixed halo of a principal slot. Edge variables are charged to their incident cells, and cells are enlarged by one safety layer. Consequently, slots whose enlarged dependence carriers are at distance greater than 2 give independent random functions.

A list is plane-centered: pi f = f - <f>_0 with <f>_0 the critical plane expectation of the pure model, and norm( pi f )_infty <= 2 norm( f )_infty. The removed scalar is saved separately with its own dependence carrier and is not reinserted as an interaction. This convention will also be applied to coefficients containing source labels.

Sizes and log-sinh coordinates. The two coordinates that survive the linearized transport are m_j and g_j.

m_j is the homogeneous mean coordinate of the background list at scale j, measuring the expanding direction. The tuning parameter t is chosen so that this coordinate is O(G_j) where G_j = gbar / (1 + B_0 j gbar), gbar = gamma D^2 for some positive gamma and D the microscopic disorder in log-sinh coordinates. This coordinate is killed by choosing t as a function of D.

g_j is the energy-charge variance per unit area of the background list at scale j. It is neutral under linear transport; the exact line identity together with the energy-energy operator coefficient gives the recursion
g_{j+1} = g_j + b_j g_j^2 + O( G_j^2 (s_j + rho^j) ),
with b_j = -4 pi a^2 log L + o(1) uniformly in j and s_j = G_j^{1/2}. The solution is
g_j = (1 + o(1)) / (B_0 j),  B_0 = 4 pi a^2,  g_0 = gbar (1 + O(D)).

A spin insertion at y is transported by the exact L^d scaled marked map. Its scale-j list u_{j,y} has a bounded positive random amplitude alpha_j whose even moments A_{j,q} = E[ alpha_j^q ] are finite for each fixed even q. The recurrence derived from the connected marked cumulants is
A_{j+1,q} = A_{j,q} ( 1 + c_{j,q}(y) g_j + O( G_j ( s_j + rho^j ) ) ),
where c_{j,q}(y) is the marked coefficient extracted from the energy-spin operator product. Taking logarithms and using the Cesaro mean of c_{j,q} gives
log A_{J,q} = binom(q,2) (pi a^2 / 8) log J + O(1),
uniformly in y. In particular A_{J,2} = J^{1/8+o(1)}.

Two insertions at y_1, y_2 with |y_1 - y_2| = r = r_J and J large have independent leading amplitudes, because their principal footprints are disjoint. Together with A_{J,2}^2 = J^{1/4+o(1)} = (log r)^{1/4+o(1)} and with control of the small nonlocal remainders, this gives the theorem.

The remainder of the proof establishes all these statements.

The exact block transformation. Fix eps and a choice of thresholds (theta_c) and signs xi_e. Let mu_0 denote the critical reference measure at beta_0 on Z^2, or on a large finite box with fixed or free boundary. The reference measure acts on spins; the disorder and the seeds are fixed throughout.

For a bounded real background B on the spins and a cutting square Q whose interior is disjoint from all other cutting squares at the same scale, define
P_Q^B F = P_Q( e^B F ) / ( P_Q e^B ),
where P_Q is conditional reference averaging of Q's interior given its contour. This operator sends 1 to 1 and is a sup-norm contraction:
norm( P_Q^B F )_infty <= norm( F )_infty.
No smallness or ferromagnetic property of B is used. The normalizing factor P_Q e^B, and its logarithm, are saved before Q's interior is integrated out, and are the factor that makes the partition identity exact.

Structure theory for the block transformation. At each principal slot c assign an independent threshold theta_c uniform on [delta, 2 delta], independently of the input list, using a fresh seed. Call c bad if norm( h_{p_c} )_infty > theta_c. Connect two bad centers by an edge when their physical max-norm distance is at most 100 H R, with H a fixed large integer chosen after all other constants. A connected component of this graph is selected if its diameter is at most 1000 M r, where M = (log L)^3.

Selection is a bounded-dependence event. From a bad center x only bad centers within distance 100 H R + 1000 M r need be inspected; if this inspected component has a vertex at distance > 1000 M r from x, the true component is too large, otherwise it is exactly the inspected one. This also rejects every infinite component by a finite certificate. The badness test for each center uses only its principal halo and its threshold seed, so selection has dependence radius C H R.

For a selected component C put B_C = sum_{c in C} h_{p_c} and Q_C the max-norm square of side 1000 M r around the bounding box of C. The Q_C have positive-gap interiors, disjoint from each other, and disjoint collars from each other. All unselected bad principal entries and all nonprincipal entries of norm at most delta e^{-w n_i} are treated as small entries.

The transformed moment. Fix a bad configuration and its selected components. For each pair (i, zeta) of a slot and a source label set, with zeta square-free, expand the exponential of the small entries in ordered occurrences with 1/k! for k occurrences. An occurrence records its input slot and its source label set; retain only products with pairwise disjoint source label sets.

For a product F of occurrences, define its contact graph by joining two occurrences when their full geometric carriers lie within 1000 H R of each other. First average the product of the occurrences jointly inside every selected square Q_C using P_{Q_C}^{B_C}; these operators commute because their interiors are disjoint and the reference specification is nearest-neighbor Markov. Then, for each occurrence that is a singleton connected component of the contact graph, has n_i < L/2, and has no bad center within 1000 H R of its carrier, apply the ordinary conditional average over a cutting square of radius H r centered at (a(i)). This cutting square has a synchronous collar from every other such square and from every Q_C.

Set m(I) to be the result of these applications to the factor F_I of occurrences in the finite labeled set I. This is the transformed moment of I. By the tower property and the standard argument, for any multi-index I_1, ..., I_k with pairwise disjoint source labels and any bounded real B supported away from the cutting interiors,
int e^{ B + H_s } F_{I_1} ... F_{I_k} dmu_0 = int e^{ B' } m(I_1) ... m(I_k) dmu_0,
where B' is the retained bad field after the selected and ordinary cuts are integration, and H_s = sum_small h_i + sum_source zeta u. Summing over k and using the absolutely summable estimates derived below passes the identity through the sum. The saved scalar s' is exactly sum over cuts of log P_Q e^B plus whatever background is already in H_s. Setting
H' = log( sum_{I_1, ..., I_k} m(I_1) ... m(I_k) / k! )
gives the exact partition identity
int e^H dmu_0 = e^{s'} int e^{H'} dmu_0,
coefficientwise in zeta.

This is the change of representation that is iterated.
