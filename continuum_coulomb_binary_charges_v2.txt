A molecule in the clamped-nuclei picture is a finite list of point nuclei, with positive integer charges and positions in space, together with a number of electrons. In atomic units the electronic Hamiltonian on the antisymmetric space ∧^N L^2(ℝ^3;ℂ^2) is
 H(R,Z,N) = -(1)/(2)sum_{ell=1}^{N} Delta_{x_ell} - sum_{ell=1}^{N}sum_{alpha=1}^{M} (Z_alpha)/(|x_ell-R_alpha|) + sum_{1le ell<kle N} (1)/(|x_ell-x_k|), 
with form domain Q_N=H^1((ℝ^3)^N;(ℂ^2)^{ ⊗  N})cap∧^N L^2(ℝ^3;ℂ^2); antisymmetry acts on spatial and spin coordinates together. The three-dimensional Hardy inequality, applied in one electron coordinate with the others frozen, bounds every Coulomb multiplier by the kinetic form plus a constant multiple of the L^2 form. The closed form is lower semibounded and defines a self-adjoint operator. Write E_0(R,Z,N) for the infimum of its spectrum. Nuclear repulsion is omitted because the nuclei are clamped.

An instance of the promise problem consists of pairwise distinct positions R_1,...,R_Minℚ^3 with Mge1, positive integer charges Z_1,...,Z_M encoded in binary, an electron number Nge1 encoded in unary, and rational thresholds a<b. Coordinates and thresholds are signed binary rationals in lowest terms under one fixed self-delimiting encoding; the total bit length is L. The precision requirement is b-age L^{-c} for a fixed positive integer c. The YES promise is E_0(R,Z,N)le a and the NO promise is E_0(R,Z,N)ge b. An input outside the promise may be answered either way.

Three features of the encoding carry weight. No orbital basis, magnetic field, or independently adjustable external potential is supplied: the external potential is generated by the nuclei alone. The infimum runs over every total-spin sector and over the full continuum. And the threshold gap is a statement about output precision; it asserts neither a discrete eigenvalue at the bottom of the spectrum nor an efficiently preparable ground state.

Theorem. For c=1 the promise problem is QMA-hard under deterministic classical polynomial-time many-one reductions, and the reduction outputs instances with b-age1.

QMA-hardness means that every promise problem with polynomial-time quantum verification and a polynomial-size quantum witness reduces to this one by a deterministic classical polynomial-time map that preserves YES and NO instances. The reduction is a single classical map on encodings. Charges and inverse nuclear separations in the output may be exponentially large, with descriptions of polynomial length. Nothing is claimed about membership of the problem in QMA, and nothing is claimed under an additional bound on nuclear charges. The binary encoding of unrestricted integer charges is essential to the construction: the charges used in the construction are of order e^D for a large integer scale D.

Electronic-structure complexity has several distinct formulations, and the distinctions are not cosmetic. Liu, Christandl and Verstraete proved QMA-completeness of the finite-mode N-representability problem for general two-body fermionic Hamiltonians; the coefficients of those Hamiltonians are not required to arise from a Coulomb potential. Whitfield, Love and Aspuru-Guzik separated the roles of external potentials, magnetic fields, and basis restrictions in the early hardness results. O'Gorman, Irani, Whitfield and Fefferman kept the Coulomb interaction, dropped the magnetic field, and encoded a local Hamiltonian into a fixed single-particle basis with an external electric field, with the basis and the electron number part of the input.

None of these results is a statement about the Coulomb operator over the continuum. Each of them delivers part of the Hamiltonian as an input matrix, as a magnetic field with independently adjustable coefficients, or as a fixed basis whose matrix elements are supplied. The molecular Hamiltonian has no such free part: its external potential is a sum of integer multiples of 1/|x-R| over finitely many points, and nothing else.

The gap between those settings and the operator H(R,Z,N) is a lower bound question, and it is the only serious difficulty. Projecting H onto any chosen orbital space, and computing the matrix of the projected operator in that space, gives a variational upper bound for E_0. A reduction in the other direction needs a lower bound valid for every state of the full continuum: pair breaking into unoccupied orbitals, charge transfer between wells, and delocalization must all be excluded, even when such states lower the energy without leaving a trace in the compressed matrix. The construction pays for that lower bound. Encoded orbitals hop with exponentially small amplitudes, nuclei carry charges of size e^D, and every precision requirement has to be met by polynomial-bit arithmetic.

Source problem. On two spin-1/2 sites put h^s=X ⊗  X+Y ⊗  Y+Z ⊗  Z; its eigenvalue is -3 on the singlet and 1 on the triplet, so |h^s|=3. An instance consists of a finite non-wrapping subgraph G of the square lattice, rational weights J_e on its edges, and rational thresholds a_s<b_s. Every numeric quantity of the input has polynomial bit length and polynomial magnitude, the lattice extent is polynomial, there are no one-qubit terms, and b_s-a_sge1/poly(n) for the input size nge2. The Hamiltonian is H_{rm in}=sum_{e=LR}J_e,h^s_{LR}. The YES promise is E(H_{rm in})le a_s and the NO promise is E(H_{rm in})ge b_s. The universality classification of Cubitt, Montanaro and Piddock turns the square-lattice Heisenberg model with variable coupling strengths into a universal simulator, and Section 10.1 of that development records the QMA-completeness consequence; that consequence is the sole complexity-theoretic input used in the reduction.

Five layers carry the source Hamiltonian to a molecule. A signed Heisenberg Hamiltonian is converted to one with positive couplings only, at the cost of one singlet mediator pair per edge; the conversion shifts the energy by a known constant and costs at most 27V^3/√(Delta), with Delta a polynomially large integer and V the total mediator coupling. Every positive coupling then receives a unit-length contact between nuclear sites in three dimensions, in a layout whose contact graph is exactly the gadget graph. At half filling on that contact graph, the Hubbard model with hoppings t_{ij} has exchange energies t_{ij}^2/U in second-order perturbation theory; the choice t_{ij}=etasqrt{K_{ij}} reproduces the positive coupling pattern up to an affine shift, with U=5/8 computed from the exponential reference profile. Each site becomes a hydrogenic well of a nucleus of charge near Z_* together with a weak secondary nucleus at distance rho_iin[1,4]; tuning rho_i places every one-well ground level at -1/2. Finally, the full Coulomb operator is compared with the Hubbard model: the orthonormalized site orbitals reproduce the hopping matrix with exponentially small errors, the orthogonal complement carries a gap of size comparable to Z_*, and the interelectronic repulsion compresses to Usum_i n_{i↑}n_{i↓}.

A state of the molecule that leaves the encoded orbital space can gain kinetic energy without appearing in the compressed matrix, so a variational computation of the compressed matrix alone yields no lower bound. Three quantitative estimates close the gap: a positive gap for the one-particle matrix on the orthogonal complement, a square-completion bound that removes the many-particle coupling to that complement, and a coefficientwise comparison of the Coulomb interaction inside the encoded space with the Hubbard on-site term. Each error is exponentially small in D, the distance scale of the molecule in atomic units.

After the comparison, the physical ground energy satisfies E_0/Z_*+mZ_*/2=alpha(E_{rm in}-C_s)+o(1), where alpha is a rational number of inverse-polynomial size and C_s a computable constant. The factor Z_* is what turns an inverse-polynomial promise gap in the source problem into a constant promise gap in the output. That factor is also why the nuclear charges have to be encoded in binary.

The algorithm. First check the source encoding and the polynomial bounds, including the inverse gap bound. If a check fails, output the fixed instance with one electron, one unit-charge nucleus at the origin, and thresholds -1/2 and 1/2; the guard is polynomial-time and makes the reduction a total map, and it never rejects a promised source instance. Otherwise carry out the following steps, all deterministic and classical.

1. Finite models. Read the signed rational Heisenberg instance. Form the ancillary graph, the algebraic reference positions p_i, the contact set mathcal C,calC, the scales Delta,eta, and the positive rational hoppings t_{ij}. All square roots are approximated with certified rational intervals to the stated inverse-polynomial errors, and the contact classification is used instead of deciding geometric equalities numerically.

2. Energy constant. Compute C_s as a certified rational interval and the rational widehatwidehat CC with |C_s-widehatwidehat C|C|legamma/16.

3. Scale Z_*. Put s_D=sum_{j=0}^{4D}D^j/j! and Z_*=lfloor s_Drfloor. The tail obeys 0<e^D-s_D<1 because the ratio of successive omitted terms is at most 1/4, so e^D-1<s_D<e^D and therefore e^D/2le Z_*<2e^D.

4. Positions. Compute the logarithms d_{ij} to the accuracy tau/4, apply the rational right inverse of the displacement lemma to the approximations, and round to rational centers X_i. Define the displacements by s_i=X_i-Dp_i.

5. Tuning. With the final rational centers and integer charges Z_*q, Z_*q' fixed, apply the certified one-well oracle and the contraction iteration to obtain rationals rho_iin[1,4] with |lambda_i+frac12|le e^{-1.01D}. No subsequent change to a charge or center is made.

with their positive integer charges, output N=m in unary, and output the rational thresholds a and b.

Each step is classical and deterministic. The one-well Ritz spaces have dimension polynomial in D and in the rational coordinate bit lengths; matrix entries and conditioning bounds need only polynomially many bits, and the number of eigenvalue evaluations in the tuning procedure is polynomial. The integral evaluations and the elementary functions in calibration require only polynomial time and precision. No many-electron ground energy is queried at any point.

Bit length. The nuclei are polynomially many, each coordinate is a rational of polynomial bit length, each charge is an integer of polynomial bit length, N is unary with polynomial length, and the thresholds are rationals of polynomial bit length. Every intermediate quantity is produced with certified rational intervals of polynomial precision. Hence the output length L is polynomial in n, and since b-age1 the promise b-age L^{-1} holds without any circular estimate involving L.

This constructs a legal nuclear instance in deterministic polynomial time, preserves YES and NO promises with b-age1, and imposes no restriction on spin sectors or on continuum states. The promise problem is therefore QMA-hard for c=1.
