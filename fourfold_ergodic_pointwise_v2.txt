# Pointwise convergence of fourfold ergodic averages for mixing transformations



maketitle

Abstract: 
Let $T$ be an invertible, bimeasurable, measure-preserving, mixing
transformation of a probability space. For every fixed bounded
$f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4$ the fourfold ergodic averages along the times
$n,2n,3n,4n$ converge almost everywhere to the product of the four
integrals, along all positive integer lengths. No rate of mixing is
assumed, and the space need not be standard.


# Introduction and main theorem

Let $(X,mathcal F,calF,mu,T)$ be an invertible probability-preserving
system and let $f_1,...,f_din L^∞(mu)$. The multiple ergodic
averages are
[
 A_N(f_1,...,f_d)(x)=frac1Nsum_{n=1}^Nprod_{j=1}^d f_j(T^{jn}x).
]
The pointwise problem asks whether $A_N$ converges almost everywhere
for every finite $d$ on every system. Here we settle the case $d=4$
under a mixing hypothesis. Call $T$ mixing if

 mu(Acap T^{-n}B)→mu(A)mu(B)qquad(|n|to∞)

for all measurable $A,B$. The functions are fixed in each convergence
statement, and we assume no rate in (eq:mixing).


Let $(X,mathcal F,calF,mu)$ be a probability space and let $T:Xto X$ be
invertible, bimeasurable, measure-preserving and mixing in the sense of
(eq:mixing). For all $f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4in L^∞(mu)$,
[
 frac1Nsum_{n=1}^N
 f_1(T^nx)f_2(T^{2n}x)f_3(T^{3n}x)f_4(T^{4n}x)
 →prod_{j=1}^4int_X f_jddmu
]
for $mu$-almost every $x$, along every positive integer $N$. The
space need not be standard.


## Where this sits

Birkhoff's theorem [cite] is the case $d=1$. Furstenberg's
ergodic proof of Szemer'edi's theorem [cite] made
arithmetic-progression products central. Host and Kra
cite[Theorem~1.1]{HostKra}, and independently Ziegler [cite],
proved norm convergence of every finite consecutive length using
nilpotent characteristic factors; norm convergence alone says nothing
about a single point. Bourgain [cite] proved pointwise
convergence for two powers. Further positive pointwise results: $K$
systems [cite], weakly mixing systems with spectral
restrictions [cite], weakly mixing systems all of whose
pairwise-independent self-joinings are products
[cite], and every finite consecutive length on
ergodic measure-distal systems cite[Theorem~C]{HuangShaoYe}, extending
[cite]. Recent polynomial results [cite],
[cite] impose degree hypotheses that exclude
the four linear iterates treated here. Kuca's survey [cite]
collects the norm and pointwise problems.

Our proof rests on three companion inputs. The multiple-mixing theorem
of cite[Theorem~1.1]{MixingCompanion} upgrades (eq:mixing) to all
finite orders. The analytic construction of [cite],
built on the local estimates of [cite], gives an
oscillation estimate for threefold averages on arbitrary
invertible systems; mixing only later identifies the limit.
Appendix~sec:lower-length adapts that estimate to each
three-element subset of ${1,2,3,4}$ and derives pointwise convergence
for every proper subaverage. The local analytic theorems are cited with
their hypotheses; the new content here is the fourfold deduction.

The structural engine is the Furstenberg--Zimmer theory of relatively
compact and relatively weakly mixing extensions, recorded in the
nonergodic and power-stable form in Section~sec:structure. The
construction also stabilizes certain conditional expectations under
extension, in the spirit of sated extensions [cite].

## Why selected lengths are hard

Suppose the theorem fails. After removing means we get four centred
real functions and a positive-measure set on which the averages stay
bounded away from zero at arbitrarily late lengths. Pick one such
length $N_a(x)$ past scale $2^a$. Averaging the configurations
$(T^nx,T^{2n}x,T^{3n}x,T^{4n}x)$, $1le nle N_a(x)$, and passing to a
limit preserves a nonzero correlation; a constant-roof suspension turns
these limit laws into flow-invariant measures and allows later changes
of speed.

Selection is the whole difficulty: because the length is chosen from
the initial state, conditioning on it can distort the joint law of
auxiliary variables, and norm convergence without a rate cannot bound
all errors. We introduce three groups of auxiliary spatial coordinates
whose joint law is a product and any two of which are jointly
independent of the original coordinate, conditionally on the phase.
A length event may change the full three-group law but leaves every
two-group marginal a product. Toy model: independent symmetric signs
$U,V,W$ have a product independent of each pair, whereas conditioning
on $UVW=1$ constrains the triple.

The tool that survives selection is a finite-dimensional packing
estimate (Section~sec:packing): two bounded almost-orthogonal
families, with the large inner products confined to a bounded-degree
graph, have few tensor products landing substantially in any
slowly-growing subspace. A Gaussian comparison and a trace-moment bound
make it quantitative, uniform over all Hilbert-space dimensions and
free of any mixing rate. Applied to the leading covariance subspace it
controls the projected part of each selected average; the leftover has
a square-summable error over the length classes.

## From a correlation to a contradiction

A channel (Definition~def:channel) carries an original
spatial coordinate plus the three auxiliary groups, with independence
as above. In its selected four-coordinate law we look at correlations
where each slot uses a centred function of the original coordinate or
of the auxiliaries, weighted by a bounded function of the distal
parameters. Failure supplies one with no auxiliary slots. Choose a
channel and correlation maximizing the number of auxiliary slots.
Independence forbids four. An iterated array in which products along
distinct axes through a fixed corner are orthogonal but share the same
nonzero correlation with the corner function, killed by Bessel, forbids
three. So at most two.

The final step adds one. Enlarge the channel until the required
conditional expectations are stable under any further extension.
Then treat a selected four-coordinate law as a new system and select
again; the result is a square of states whose distinguished row and
column meet at one corner. Saturation identifies their conditional
laws. A reanchoring identity on the distal parameters shows the row--
column correlation cannot vanish, so regrouping one coordinate turns
an original-coordinate slot into an auxiliary slot, contradicting
maximality.

Sections~sec:structure and~sec:selected supply the
structural facts and selected laws; Section~sec:packing
the packing estimate; Section~sec:channels channel preservation;
Section~sec:activation the maximality argument and the theorem.
The coefficient work is in Appendix~sec:lower-length.

# Standard reduction and relative structure

The auxiliary systems built later are extensions and joinings of a
suspension of the original $T$, and they need not be ergodic. We record
the structural facts in the nonergodic setting and separate the
pointwise input for three functions from the norm input for any number.

## Reduction to a standard system

Put $I={1,2,3,4}$. It suffices to prove Theorem~thm:main on a
standard space. For a fixed tuple of bounded functions, the map that
records all their integer translates takes values in a countable product
of compact discs. The pushforward measure with the shift is a standard
factor, the functions descend to its coordinates, and mixing passes
down; almost-sure convergence there pulls back. We therefore write
$(X,mu,T)$ for a standard mixing system, all factors and conditional
expectations taken modulo null sets on completed spaces.

## The two companion inputs

The first is the multiple-mixing theorem cite[Theorem~1.1]{MixingCompanion}:
for every fixed finite $g_1,...,g_sin L^∞(mu)$,

 int_Xprod_{j=1}^s g_j(T^{n_j}x)ddmu(x)→prod_{j=1}^sint_X g_jddmu
 quadwheneverquadmin_{jne l}|n_j-n_l|to∞.

Simple-function approximation upgrades the set version of (eq:mixing)
to (eq:all-orders); all uses are for fixed functions, no rate assumed.

The second is a pointwise theorem on systems that are neither mixing nor
ergodic; we isolate its exact scope and prove it in
Appendix~sec:lower-length.

[Lower-length convergence]
Let $(Y,m,R)$ be an invertible probability-preserving transformation of
a standard space. If $∅ne J⊊ I$ and $g_iin L^∞(m)$
for $iin J$, then $frac1Nsum_{n=1}^Nprod_{iin J}g_i(R^{in}y)$
converges for $m$-almost every $y$.


## Distal factors and relative weak mixing

Let $(Y,m,R)$ be a standard system and $B$ a factor. We use the
conditional-correlation form of relative weak mixing: for
bounded $f,g$ with $E[fmid B]=0$,

 frac1Hsum_{h=1}^Hbigl|E[overlineoverline ff,(gcirc R^h)mid B]bigr|_2^2→0.

For $F_1,...,F_rin L^2(m)$ the finitely generated
$L^∞(B)$-module is ${sum_j a_jF_j:a_jin L^∞(B)}$, called
invariant if composition with $R$ and $R^{-1}$ preserves it.
An extension is relatively compact if its $L^2$ is generated by
invariant modules (closure after spanning the union, not each module
separately): the corrected compact-module characterization
cite[Theorem~4.1]{Jamneshan}cite[Theorem~2.5]{JamneshanCorrigendum}.
A relatively distal extension is a tower of relatively compact
extensions with inverse limits; distality with no base named means over
the trivial factor. The identity action is compact, so nonergodic
systems are included.

[Relative structure]
For standard probability-preserving systems:

item There is a maximal measure-distal factor $W_Y$ containing the
invariant factor, with $Yto W_Y$ relatively weakly mixing. A distal
factor pulls back into the maximal distal factor of any extension, and
every measure-preserving automorphism commuting with $R$ preserves $W_Y$.
item Factors and joinings of distal systems are distal; a distal system
is relatively distal over each factor; $R$ is distal iff $R^q$ is, for
each nonzero integer $q$.
item Relative weak mixing passes to nonzero integer powers and to
conditionally independent base change, and the product of relatively
weakly mixing fibre laws over any invariant joining of their bases is
relatively weakly mixing over that joining.
item A relatively weakly mixing and a relatively distal extension of
one base are relatively disjoint: the only invariant joining fixing the
base is the relatively independent one.
item If $R$ is distal then for every $dge1$ and bounded $g_1,...,g_d$
the averages $frac1Nsum_{n=1}^Nprod_{i=1}^dg_i(R^{in}y)$ converge
almost surely.

For a flow $(S_Y^t)_{tinR}$, the maximal distal factor $W_Y$ of
$S_Y^1$ is a flow factor, and its action is distal at every nonzero
rational time.


Proof. 
Existence and the relatively weakly mixing extension are
Furstenberg--Zimmer [cite]; the nonergodic
probability-algebra form is cite[Theorems~1.1,1.2]{Jamneshan}. We
verify the consequences used later, tracking base and time.

Compact modules under base change and joining. At a compact
stage an invariant finitely generated module stays finitely generated
after enlarging the base: truncate generators under their invariant
pointwise length when they transform by unitary matrices over the base.
Products of bounded generators lie in finitely generated tensor
modules, so joins of compact stages are compact over joined bases, and
the same for distal towers follows by closing at limit stages. Hence
distal factors of a system lie in its maximal distal factor (apply to
an automorphism commuting with $R$ and inverse to get preservation of
$W_Y$), and the invariant factor sits inside $W_Y$ since it is compact.

Time. Compact $R$-modules are compact $R^q$-modules, so distality
passes to powers. Conversely if $R^q$ is distal its compact tower is
preserved by $R$; at each successor stage replace an $R^q$-invariant
finitely generated module by the sum of its first $q$ translates, a
finitely generated $R$-module over the same base, and the resulting
tower proves distality of $R$. Inversion handles negative powers.

Relative weak mixing under powers and base change. The nonnegative
summands in (eq:rwm) show restriction to multiples of a positive
integer keeps Ces`aro limit zero; invariance and conjugation handle
negative multiples. For joined bases $Y_jto B_j$ with a base joining
$sigma$, give the fibres the law $int⊗_jm_j(dd y_jmid b_j)ddsigma$.
For bounded tensor tests the conditional correlation over the full base
is $a(b),c(S_B^hb)prod_jE[overlineoverline f_j(g_jf_j(g_jcirc R_j^h)mid B_j](b_j)$.
If some $f_j$ is conditionally centred the squared $L^2(sigma)$ norm is
bounded by a constant times $|E[overlineoverline f_j(g_jf_j(g_jcirc R_j^h)mid B_j]|^2_{L^2(B_j)}$,
using only the $j$th marginal of $sigma$, whose Ces`aro mean vanishes.
Centred tensors span a dense subspace of the orthocomplement of the base
after subtracting the conditional expectation and telescoping, so
bounded approximation proves the claim; an extra base coordinate proves
stability under conditionally independent base change.

Relative disjointness. Take a compact extension of the base. The
absolute weak-mixing disjointness principle is
Furstenberg's cite[Part~I, Sec.~7]{FurstenbergDisjointness}. A joining
gives a base-linear, time-intertwining conditional expectation
from its $L^2$ to the relatively weakly mixing side, carrying finitely
generated invariant modules to such modules; relative weak mixing puts
the image in the base, so the joining is relatively independent. Induct
along a distal tower: after independence at a stage, the far extension
is a conditionally independent base change and the compact-stage
argument repeats; conditional expectations pass to limits. No ergodicity
was used. This also gives closure of distal systems under factors: if $D$
is distal with factor $B$, then $D$ is distal over the pullback of $W_B$,
and relative disjointness of $Dto W_B$ and $Bto W_B$ forces the latter
to be trivial.

Pointwise assertion. The ergodic case is cite[Theorem~C]{HuangShaoYe}
(measure-theoretic distality). For nonergodic $R$, start the compact
tower at the invariant factor and disintegrate. On a standard space the
tower is countable, since increasing successor stages give nonzero
orthogonal differences in separable $L^2$. At each stage pick a
countable generating algebra and approximate its indicators by functions
in the compact modules (successor stages) or preceding factors (limit
stages) with summable squared $L^2$ errors. Fubini makes these summable
in the conditional $L^2$ spaces on a common conull set of components, so
the restricted algebras are dense and the invariance identities
disintegrate; successor stages stay compact and limit stages stay
generated by predecessors on that set. Every intermediate factor shares
the invariant algebra, so each component tower is ergodic with isometric
compact extensions, hence an ergodic distal system. The cited theorem
applies on almost every component.

Flows. Every flow time commutes with time one, hence preserves
$W_Y$. If $r=p/qne0$ is rational, the $q$th power of its action on
$W_Y$ is the distal time-$p$ action, so distality at time $r$ follows
from the power assertion.


The following norm estimate explains why proper subaverages look simple
over the distal factor; it uses only relative weak mixing.

[Relatively weakly mixing characteristic factor]

If $Yto B$ is relatively weakly mixing for invertible $R$ and
$a_1,...,a_d$ are distinct nonzero integers with bounded
$g_1,...,g_d$, then

 Bigl|frac1Nsum_{n=1}^N(prod_{i=1}^d g_icirc R^{a_in}
 -prod_{i=1}^dE[g_imid B]circ R^{a_in})Bigr|_2→0.



Proof. 
By telescoping and scaling, take the $g_i$ bounded by one with one, say
$g_s$, conditionally centred. Induct on $d$, empty product first. Set
$v_n=prod_ig_icirc R^{a_in}$ and, for fixed $hge1$,
$F_{i,h}=g_ibar(g_icirc R^{a_ih})$. Invariance gives
[

 =int F_{s,h}prod_{ine s}F_{i,h}circ R^{(a_i-a_s)n}dd m .
]
The other coefficients are distinct and nonzero; the induction
hypothesis replaces their factors, in the average over $n$, by their

are $B$-measurable and bounded by one, so
$limsup_N|frac1Nsum_n< v_n,v_{n+h}>|le|E[F_{s,h}mid B]|_1$
(and directly for $d=1$). The Hilbert-space van der Corput inequality

$frac1H+frac1Hsum_{hle H}|E[F_{s,h}mid B]|_1$ in the limit

Weak mixing of $T$ thus gives $L^2$ convergence to the product of the
means for all finite distinct nonzero linear powers. In a flow whose

the lemma to $g_icirc S_Y^{a_is}$ for each fixed $s$ and integrating in

continuous-time analogue.

# Selectors and selected laws

Failure of pointwise convergence lets the averaging length depend on the
initial state. We encode that in a law on four outputs. The selection may
distort their joint law, but Section~sec:structure determines every
proper marginal, even after all distal parameters are fixed.

# Tensor packing into a small subspace

Selecting an averaging length changes the joint law of the averaged
variables. The deterministic Hilbert-space estimate here controls that
change. In the application two independent groups supply the two $L^2$
spaces whose tensor product we test. The estimate is uniform over the
projection: a subspace of slowly growing rank captures a substantial part
of only a small fraction of an almost-orthogonal tensor family.
Hilbert spaces below are real, tensor products are Hilbert tensor
products, and $log$ is natural.

[Tensor packing]
There is a universal $C$ with the following property. Let
$0<εle1$ and integers $dge1$, $Lge0$. Let $x_1,...,x_m$,
$y_1,...,y_m$ lie in the unit balls of real Hilbert spaces $H_1,H_2$.

# Channels and preservation of independence

Selected laws can retain correlations that ordinary averaging destroys.
We specify extensions carrying independent outputs and show the class
survives passage to a selected law. There are three groups of outputs.
Every proper collection of groups is independent of the original input,
whereas the full triple may be correlated with it. That gap is what lets
us select lengths and still keep the correlations the final argument uses.

# Activating an additional output role

Channels supply independent spatial outputs even after the length is
selected. We use that to kill the nonzero correlation of
Proposition~prop:selectors. The argument counts how many of the four
factors can be read from channel outputs; it bounds that count, then passes
to an extension stabilizing certain conditional expectations, and finally
manufactures one more output role to contradict maximality.

## Nonvanishing and the extra role


Let $V$ be a square-integrable random vector in a separable real Hilbert

# Pointwise convergence for the proper subsets

We prove Lemma~lem:lower-length. Its systems are extensions of a
suspension, not mixing or ergodic, so we need a pointwise theorem on
arbitrary invertible systems. The analytic base is the oscillation estimate
cite[Prop.~2.2]{TripleCompanion} (call it P; also
H=[cite]). P states the oscillation estimate for
times $n,2n,3n$ before using mixing, so it already holds without mixing.
We need the three other triples inside ${1,2,3,4}$; we obtain them by
modifying the local four-linear construction of H and its depth-varying
extension in P, including their intermediate estimates (the bare boundedness
of the trilinear Hilbert transform would not give oscillation). We specify
the imported statements, verify the coefficient changes, then transfer to
arbitrary systems and all integer lengths.

## Convergence on arbitrary systems

Prove Lemma~lem:lower-length from Proposition~prop:ll-oscillation;
the oscillation-to-convergence route is classical
[cite]. A uniformly bounded sequence of
measurable functions satisfying (eq:ll-oscillation) is Cauchy a.e.: if
its tail oscillation exceeds $4ε$ on a positive-measure $E$, then for
every fixed initial endpoint $k$ the sup of $|V_ell-V_k|$ over $ellge k$
exceeds $2ε$ on $E$, and by monotone convergence one chooses a
deterministic later endpoint with integral maximum $geεnu(E)$;
recursing contradicts $C_varphisqrt W$ for large $W$. The finite sums
$V_k^varphi$ are uniformly bounded (bounded compactly supported profile), so
each fixed profile gives a.e. convergence.

P, Lemma~2.8 (Approximation with three vanishing moments): for each
$cin[1,2]$ and $ε>0$ there is a profile with

 |varphi-h_c|_1<ε,qquad h_c(t)=c^{-1}mathbf1_bf1_{(0,c]}(t),

and signed profiles are essential. Smooth $h_c$ preserving integral one,
then cancel the other three moments with four broad translated bumps at
distance and width $sim A$: the moment matrix for orders $0..3$, after
dividing row $j$ by $A^j$, is a fixed invertible Vandermonde-type matrix; the
prescribed zeroth correction is zero, the others are $O(A^{-1})$, so the
corrections are $O(A^{-1})$ in $L^1$ cost and the bumps keep
(eq:ll-profile) with profile-dependent constant.

Fix $J$ and $a_n(y)=prod_{jin J}g_j(R^{jn}y)$, $|a_n|le1$. For
$N_k=lfloor c2^krfloor$ the Riemann comparison gives, uniformly in $y$,

 limsup_{kto∞}|frac1{N_k}sum_{n=1}^{N_k}a_n(y)-V_k^varphi(y)|le|h_c-varphi|_1:

replace $N_k^{-1}$ by $(c2^k)^{-1}$ at cost $O(2^{-k})$, then bound by
$2^{-k}sum_{ninZ}|h_c(n2^{-k})-varphi(n2^{-k})|$, converging to the
integral (compact support, piecewise continuous).

Take countably many profiles with errors $to0$ for each rational
$cin[1,2]$. On their common conull set the smooth limits and
(eq:ll-profile-error) make the averages along $N_k$ Cauchy. Host--Kra
cite[Thm.~1.1]{HostKra}, for arbitrary invertible systems, gives an $L^2$
limit at all integer lengths (apply to the four consecutive powers with the
constant one at the omitted role); the bounded pointwise limit along each
$N_k$ equals this $L^2$ limit a.e., so all rational-$c$ subsequences share
one limit, with no identification and no mixing needed.

Finally for $Mle N$, $|a_n|le1$,
$|frac1Nsum_{nle N}a_n-frac1Msum_{nle M}a_n|le2(N-M)/N$. Any rational
mesh of $[1,2]$ catches every large integer within its relative mesh size
plus $O(N^{-1})$ of some $lfloor c2^krfloor$ from the mesh; the finitely
many subsequences share the limit, and meshes shrinking to zero prove
convergence along all lengths. Homogeneity removes $|g_j|le1$; a proper
subset with fewer than three roles embeds in a triple by the constant one.
This proves Lemma~lem:lower-length.
