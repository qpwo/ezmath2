# Catalan's constant is irrational



maketitle
Abstract: 
We prove that Catalan's constant
$G=sum_{jgeq0}(-1)^j/(2j+1)^2$ is irrational. The proof builds explicit
determinants whose entries are rational linear combinations of $1$, $G$
and $zeta(2)$, arranges a Taylor-contact cancellation that removes the
$zeta(2)$ terms, and then compares a sharp lower bound on the prime
valuations of these determinants with a sharp upper bound on their real
size. The two bounds are incompatible at the scale of the square of the
matrix size, so $G$ cannot be rational. The argument is self-contained
apart from the ordinary prime number theorem; every finite numerical
statement is certified by exact rational arithmetic.


# Introduction

## What we prove

Catalan's constant is the real number
[
G=beta(2)=sum_{j=0}^{∞}((-1)^j)/((2j+1)^2)
=1-frac19+frac1{25}-frac1{49}+...
approx 0.9159655941772190...
]
where $beta$ is the Dirichlet beta function. Equivalently
$G=L(2,chi_{-4})$ for the odd quadratic character of conductor $4$.
The same number is one quarter of the volume of the smallest orientable
complete finite-volume hyperbolic three-manifold with two cusps, and it
appears in lattice sums and in a large family of integrals. Its
arithmetic type is the question we settle.


Catalan's constant is irrational.


The word ``irrational'' means that $Gne p/q$ for all integers $p,q$.
Everything below is aimed at Theorem~thm:main; every other result
in the paper is a lemma, proposition or estimate feeding it.

## Why a good approximation is not enough

Because we can compute $G$ to billions of digits, one might think its
arithmetic is within reach. This is false, and the reason drives the
whole proof.

Suppose $G$ really is a fraction $p/q$. Then for any integers $A,B$ the
combination
[
A,G-B=(A,p-q,B)/(q)
]
is a rational with denominator dividing $q$, so it is either exactly
zero or at least $1/q$ in absolute value: it cannot be ``small but
nonzero''. This gives the standard criterion.

[Rationality criterion]
A real number $alpha$ is irrational if and only if there are integers
$A_mne0$ and $B_m$ with
[
0<|A_m,alpha-B_m|→0quad(mto∞).
]


Proof. 
Take $alpha=p/q$ rational. If $A_malpha-B_mne0$ then
$|A_malpha-B_m|ge1/q$, independent of $m$, so the limit cannot be $0$.
Conversely, if $alpha$ is irrational, the continued fraction of $alpha$
produces convergents $P_m/Q_m$ with $|Q_malpha-P_m|<1/Q_mto0$, and these
forms are nonzero (an exact equality would make $alpha=P_m/Q_m$
rational).


So to prove irrationality we must build nonzero integer combinations that
tend to zero. The hard part is the word nonzero: approximations
$P_m/Q_m$ only give $|Q_mG-P_m|=Q_m|G-P_m/Q_m|$, and multiplying by the
denominator $Q_m$ can easily destroy the decay.

## The obstruction for Catalan's constant, and earlier work

For odd positive values $beta(1),beta(3),...$ the question is
trivial, because these are rational multiples of powers of $pi$. The
even values are genuinely arithmetic. Rivoal and Zudilin proved that
infinitely many even beta values are irrational, and that at least one of
$beta(2),beta(4),...,beta(14)$ is irrational
cite[Theorems~1 and~2]{RivoalZudilin2003}. Zudilin reduced this list to
the six values through $beta(12)$ cite[Theorem~1]{Zudilin2019}, and
Lai and Zhou to the five values through $beta(10)$
cite[Theorem~6.1]{LaiZhou2022}. Fischler obtained stronger quantitative
linear-independence results for families of Dirichlet $L$-values
cite[Theorems~1 and~2]{Fischler2020}. All of these are ``at least one
of a family'' statements; none identifies $beta(2)$ itself.

For a single value one wants explicit integer forms $A_mG-B_mto0$.
Ap'ery's recurrences for $zeta(2)$ and $zeta(3)$ [cite] and
Beukers's integral proofs [cite] succeed because the analytic
decay survives the necessary denominator clearing.
Zudilin constructed Ap'ery-like recurrences and integral
representations for $G$ cite[Theorems~1--3]{Zudilin2003}, and the
discussion after his Theorem~1 makes the problem explicit: the rational
quotients converge, but after clearing denominators the integer forms
fail to tend to zero. The same obstruction appears in the Pad'e
approximation work of Rivoal cite[Theorems~1 and~2]{Rivoal2006Catalan}
and Krattenthaler--Rivoal cite[Theorems~1 and~2]{KrattenthalerRivoal2008Catalan},
in the hypergeometric identity work of Krattenthaler and Zudilin
cite[Section~2 and Theorem~2]{KrattenthalerZudilin2019}, in
Nesterenko's half-integer hypergeometric approximations
[cite], and in the Rhin--Viola exponent
$0.6293...$ reported by Viola [cite]. Explicit
families with exponents below $1$, such as those of Eskandari
cite[Theorem~1.1]{Eskandari2026Approximations}, still fall short of
Proposition~prop:criterion: an exponent below $1$ does not by
itself make $A_mG-B_mto0$.

At neighboring characters, Calegari, Dimitrov and Tang proved the
$mathbb Q$-linearbbQ$-linear independence of $1,pi^2,L(2,chi_{-3})$
cite[Theorem~A]{CalegariDimitrovTang2024}, and their Remark~11.1.17
again records the denominator cost as the obstruction for two Catalan
families. Calegari proved irrationality of a $2$-adic analogue of $G$
cite[Theorem~4.2]{Calegari2005}; as Calegari, Dimitrov and Tang explain
cite[Section~5.2, footnote~2]{CalegariDimitrovTang2025}, the real
identity carries an extra $pi^2$ term that the $2$-adic identity does
not, which is exactly why the $2$-adic result does not transfer. In our
construction the cancellation of an extra $zeta(2)$ term is likewise
essential. Sun has also announced the same qualitative statement
cite[Theorem~1.1]{Sun2026}; nothing from that preprint is used here.

## The plan: one determinant, two incompatible estimates

Instead of a single linear form we use a whole integer matrix. Its
determinant $Delta_N$ is signed, but that is fine: we only need that
$Delta_N$ is a nonzero rational number whose size we can bound from
below and from above.

Step 1 (build and cancel). For each $N$ we build an $ntimes n$
determinant $Delta_N$ with $n=48N$. Its entries are moments of two
elementary kernels against polynomial rows. Each moment is a rational
combination of $1$, $G$ and $zeta(2)$. The rows are chosen so that the
coefficients of $zeta(2)$ cancel exactly, by matching Taylor
series. Thus, under the single hypothesis $Ginmathbb Q$,bbQ$, every entry
and hence $Delta_N$ lies in $mathbb Q$.bbQ$. This is
Section~sec:foundations.

Step 2 (it is nonzero). For a large prime $p$ take $N=p$ and prove
$Delta_pne0$, indeed $v_p(Delta_p)=-96p$ exactly. The proof reduces
the $48ptimes48p$ matrix modulo $p$ to three fixed rational
$48times48$ matrices, and an exact computation over $mathbb F_bbF_{101}$
proves all three invertible. This is Section~sec:nonvanishing.

Step 3 (a lower bound from valuations). A nonzero rational number
is pinned down by its prime valuations,
$log|Delta_N|=sum_p v_p(Delta_N)log p$. Careful $p$-adic estimates
of the moments bound how negative $v_p(Delta_N)$ can be at each prime,
and summing $log p$ against the prime number theorem gives a lower
bound. This is Section~sec:odd-primes, with the prime $2$ handled
in Section~sec:foundations.

Step 4 (an upper bound from an integral). Independently of any
arithmetic hypothesis, $Delta_N$ has an integral representation as a
product of Vandermonde determinants. Estimating that integral gives an
upper bound on $|Delta_N|$. This is Sections~sec:real-place
and~sec:energy, with the numerical constants certified in
Section~sec:certificate.

The payoff is a pair of bounds of opposite type on the same normalized
logarithm. Define

mathcal L_N=(calL_N=(log|Delta_N|)/((48N)^2)-frac12log 2 .

If $Ginmathbb Q$,bbQ$, then along any sequence of $N$ with $Delta_Nne0$,
[
liminf_{Nto∞}mathcal L_N>-2.29084calL_N>-2.29084
qquad(Proposition~prop:finite-lower),
]
and unconditionally
[
limsup_{Nto∞}mathcal L_NcalL_Nle-2.290939875<-2.2909
qquad(Proposition~prop:real-upper).
]
If $G$ were rational we could take the sequence $N=p$, $pto∞$
prime, on which both bounds apply and $Delta_pne0$. Then
$-2.29084<liminfmathcal L_pcalL_plelimsupmathcal L_pcalL_ple-2.290939875$, but
$-2.29084>-2.2909$. Contradiction. Hence $G$ is irrational.

The whole proof is therefore an exercise in making the two constants
$2.29084$ and $2.290939875$ genuinely correct, with a margin.

Zudilin's determinantal criterion [cite] is a useful
precedent, but its determinant is a positive Hankel determinant, whereas ours
is mixed and signed, so nonvanishing and the real estimate need separate
arguments; the logarithmic interactions of the resulting Vandermonde products
are estimated as in logarithmic potential theory [cite].

## What makes the comparison close enough

Three design choices are responsible for the narrow positive gap.

Integral rows with two layers of divisibility. The rows are
integral Chebyshev polynomials in disguise, which supplies both the
Taylor contact that cancels $zeta(2)$ and useful arithmetic
divisibility. At the large odd primes that dominate the leading bound,
the entries have denominators of order $p^2$ and of order $p$; both
layers must be tracked separately, because cancelling only the first
layer would leave a denominator bound too weak to win.

Nonvanishing by arithmetic, not by positivity. The determinant is
mixed and signed, so there is no positivity to fall back on. Instead we
reduce a growing matrix to a fixed finite one using Frobenius and two
polynomial bases, and check the fixed matrices by exact modular
arithmetic; here the certificate over $mathbb F_bbF_{101}$ enters.

Respect for both branches of the parametrization. The rows live on
two branches of a rational parametrization, and the real estimate treats
both. A bounded holomorphic interpolant controls the mixed evaluations
when the integration nodes are spread out; a Hadamard bound controls them
when the nodes bunch up. The interpolation is uniform even as nodes
collide, because the evaluation determinant cancels algebraically inside
a finite-dimensional Hardy space, so no inverse-Vandermonde estimate is
needed. The resulting Vandermonde products turn the many-variable
supremum into an explicit energy problem, whose answer is the rational
trial data of Section~sec:certificate.



The only outside analytic input in the arithmetic half is the ordinary
prime number theorem, used in the form $theta(y)=sum_{ple y}log psim
y$ and proved locally in the form actually needed
cite[Step~VI, p.~707]{Zagier1997}. The real half uses Andr'eief's
integration identity cite[Equation~(1.7) and Section~2.2]{Forrester2018Andreief},
Cauchy's double alternant cite[Section~2.1, Equation~(2.7)]{Krattenthaler1999},
the classical bounded-interpolation picture [cite]
and a Chebyshev expansion of the logarithmic kernel
cite[Lemma~3.1]{GaroufalidisPopescu2013}, all restated and proved where
used. Section~sec:conclusion also records the hyperbolic-volume
consequences.

## Conventions

All logarithms are natural. We use $v_p(p)=1$ and $v_p(0)=+∞$; for
real estimates we put $log0=-∞$. Constants in asymptotic estimates
may depend on the fixed rational value hypothetically assigned to $G$,
but never on $N$ or on the integration variables. Finite certificates are
specified by rational data and rational arithmetic, so they can be
checked without numerical logarithms or root finding.

# Polynomial rows and mixed moments

This section builds the determinant $Delta_N$ and proves the one
arithmetic fact we need from it: under the hypothesis $Ginmathbb Q$,bbQ$,
every entry of $Delta_N$ is rational. The cancellation of $zeta(2)$ is
the whole reason to design the rows as we do, so we motivate it first and
then carry out the construction in full.

## The two kernels and why moments are rational

Two elementary kernels appear: on $(-1,1)$ the weight $|t|,dt/f(t)$, and
on $(0,1)$ the weight $ds$. Both integrate against $1/(1-ts)$, and the
geometric series $1/(1-ts)=sum_{jge0}(ts)^j$ converts these moments into
simple sums. The moments are governed by exactly two classical constants,
$G$ and $zeta(2)$, and this is what makes the construction arithmetic.

For each positive integer $N$ set


n=48N,qquad a=11N,qquad b=7N,qquad q=g=4N,qquad h=2N,
L=n+a=n+b+q=59N,qquad C=L+g=63N,
H=C+h=65N,qquad A=a+2g=19N.


The matrix has size $n=48N$; the integers $a,b,q,g,h$ and the resulting
$L,C,H,A$ are the bookkeeping that makes both the arithmetic and the real
estimates come out. The numbers $48,11,7,4,2$ are not natural, but the
outcome of optimizing the two final constants.

Write $f(t)=√(1-t^2)$ with the positive branch on $(-1,1)$, and let
$w=t/(1+f)$. Then $t=2w/(1+w^2)$ and $f=(1-w^2)/(1+w^2)$. Let the star
involution send $w$ to $w^{-1}$; it fixes $t$ and sends $f$ to $-f$. For
$0le r<n$ define the row polynomials

R_r=(1-t)^h t^{C-1}w^{r-g},qquad
P_r=(R_r+R_r^*)/(2),qquad
D_r=(t(R_r^*-R_r))/(2f).

Near $t=0$ we read $f$ through its Taylor branch with $f(0)=1$, so these
are polynomial identities rather than statements about irrationalities.


Let $T_d,U_d$ be the Chebyshev polynomials, normalized by $T_0=1$,
$T_1=x$, $U_{-1}=0$, $U_0=1$ and $S_{d+1}=2xS_d-S_{d-1}$. With
$d=|r-g|$,
[
P_r=(1-t)^h t^{C-1}T_d(1/t),qquad
D_r=sgn(r-g)(1-t)^h t^{C-1}U_{d-1}(1/t),
]
and these are integer polynomials, the second being zero when $r=g$.
Moreover

suppP_r⊆{A,...,H-1},qquad
suppD_r⊆{A+1,...,H-1}.



Proof. 
The identities $(w+w^{-1})/2=1/t$ and $(w^{-1}-w)/2=f/t$ turn
$R_rpm R_r^*$ into the displayed Chebyshev expressions; these are the
standard Laurent formulas cite[Equations~(18.5.1)--(18.5.2)]{DLMFChebyshev}.
The degree bound $dle n-1-g$ gives the stated supports after multiplying
by $(1-t)^h t^{C-1}$.


The key algebraic fact is the Taylor contact

(tP_r)/(f)-D_r=(tR_r)/(f)=O(t^{C+r-g})=O(t^{L}).

Since $w=t/2+O(t^3)$, the combination $tR_r/f$ vanishes to order at least
$L$, exactly the order of the highest column index $j$. This is the
cancellation mechanism, and it removes the $zeta(2)$ coefficient below.

## The moments and the determinant

For nonnegative integers $i,j$ define the two mixed moments

M(i,j)=int_{-1}^1int_0^1(|t|)/(f(t))(t^i s^j)/(1-ts),ds,dt,
qquad
Z(i,j)=int_0^1int_0^1(t^i s^j)/(1-ts),ds,dt,

extended bilinearly to polynomial first arguments. Both integrals converge
absolutely: integrating in $s$ leaves $(-log(1-u))/√(1-u^2)$, which
is integrable on $(0,1)$, and bounded polynomial factors do not disturb
this. Hence every determinant expansion below is absolutely integrable and
Fubini applies.

For a raw column index $ble j<L$ put
[
F_j(r)=M(P_r,j)-frac32 Z(D_r,j)qquad(0le r<n).
]
The determinant used throughout the proof is

Delta_N=det_{0le r,k<n}
(M(P_r,s^{b+k}(1-s)^q)
      -frac32Z(D_r,s^{b+k}(1-s)^q)).

If $mathcal F$calF$ is the $ntimes(n+q)$ matrix of raw columns $F_j$, then
$Delta_N=det(mathcal FcalFmathcal T)$calT)$ where the integer filter matrix
$mathcal T$calT$ has entries

qquad ble j<L,quad 0le k<n.

This filter is a fourth finite difference in disguise; it is why the raw
column range is $j<L$ and why the arithmetic estimate can be layered
(Section~sec:odd-primes).

# Odd primes and the finite-place lower bound

Throughout this section assume $Ginmathbb Q$,bbQ$, so the columns $F_j$ of
Section~sec:foundations are rational. For an odd prime $p$,
reduction modulo $p$ always means reduction of a member of $mathbb Z_p$;bbZ_p$;
every congruence below includes the assertion that both sides are locally
integral.

The raw matrix has $n+q$ columns while the filtered determinant has size
$n$. At the large primes treated first, each raw column has at most two
powers of $p$ in its denominator. We make invertible column changes over
$mathbb Z_p$bbZ_p$ and bound how many columns can still have denominator $p^2$
or $p$. Such bounds control every full minor of the raw matrix, and
Cauchy--Binet transfers them to the integer column filter. The first
reduction identifies residues after multiplication by $p^2$; for
$p>H/2$ a second reduction identifies the remaining residues after
multiplication by $p$.

## Digit reduction of the rational moments

We first prove the reductions needed at primes $2sqrt H<ple H$. We may
suppose $p$ does not divide the denominator of $G$: as $Nto∞$ every
fixed denominator prime eventually lies below $2sqrt H$. Put
[
E(t)=(1-t^2)^{(p-1)/2}=sum_{d=0}^{p-1}E_dt^dquadin mathbb F_p[t],bbF_p[t],
qquad ε=(-1)^{(p-1)/2},
]
and set $E_d=0$ outside $0le d<p$. Within this range,
[
E_d=c_{d/2}pmod p,&d even,0,&d odd,
qquad E_{p-1-d}=ε E_d,
]
the first from $(-1)^kbinom{(p-1)/2}{k}equiv4^{-k}binom{2k}{k}$ and the
second by reversing coefficients.

[Digit reductions]
For $0le i,j<H$ let
[
ell=jbmod p,quad d=(j-i-1)bmod p,
qquad
i'=(i+1+d-ell)/(p)-1,quad j'=Bigllfloorfrac jpBigrfloor,
]
with $0leell,d<p$. Then $i'ge-1$, and, using the convention
$M^0(-1,j')=k^+_{j'+1}$,


p^2M^0(i,j)&equiv E_dM^0(i',j')pmod p,
p^2Z^0(i,j)&equiv

Z^0(lfloor i/prfloor,j'),&iequiv jpmod p,
0,&inotequiv jpmod p.





Proof. 
We first give the parity and carry calculation for the factors $H_z^*$ of
(eq:h-star). Write $z=Pp+r$, $0le r<p$, $0le z<H$. Since
$H<p^2/4$, all factors at reduced indices $P$ are $p$-adic units. If $z$
is even,


H_z^*equiv H_P^*c_{r/2}pmod p,&r even,
v_p(H_z^*)=1,&r odd.


When $r$ is even, $P$ is even and the base-$p$ digits of $z/2$ are
$P/2,r/2$, so $(1+x)^p=1+x^p$ gives the first line including $4^{-z/2}$.
When $r$ is odd, $P$ is odd and the low digit of $z/2$ is $(p+r)/2$; the
factorial formula for $binom z{z/2}$ has exactly one carry,
$lfloor z/prfloor-2lfloor(z/2)/prfloor=1$, with no $p^2$ contribution.
For odd $z$ one has

frac1{H_z^*}inmathbb Z_p,bbZ_p,qquad
pH_z^*equiv

ε c_{r/2}H_P^*,&r even,
0,&r odd.


If $r$ is odd, both $z$ and $c_{(z-1)/2}$ are units; if $r$ is even, $P$ is
odd and digit expansion gives $c_{(Pp-1)/2}equivε c_{(P-1)/2}$ and
$pH_{Pp}^*equivε H_P^*$. The recurrence
$H_{z+2}^*/H_z^*=(z+1)/(z+2)$ through $r=0,2,...,p-1$ proves the nonzero
line. Since $m_{z-1}=2H_z^*$ for odd $z$ and vanishes for even $z$,
[
pm_{z-1}equiv E_{p-1-r}m_{P-1}pmod p,
]
including $z=0$ with $m_{-1}=0$.

For $u=Pp+r$, $0le u<H$, we claim

p^2k^-_uequiv E_{p-1-r}k^-_P,
qquad
p^2k^+_{u+1}equiv E_r k^+_{P+1}pmod p.

For odd $u$ write $p^2k^-_u=(pH_u^*)sum_{zle u,,z odd}2p/z$;
only $z=mp$ with $m$ odd survives, and if $r$ is even,
(eq:odd-odd-H) gives
$ε E_rH_P^*sum_{mle P}2/m=E_{p-1-r}k^-_P$, while if $r$ is odd
the prefactor vanishes. For even $u$ the quantities $p/(zH_z^*)$ with even
$zle u$ are integral, so $p^2k^-_u$ is integral and vanishes mod $p$ when
$r$ is odd; when $r$ is even the surviving summands form complete blocks
$z=mp-lambda$, $m=2,4,...,P$, $lambda=0,2,...,p-1$, and the
downward recurrence gives
$frac{p}{(mp-lambda)H_{mp-lambda}^*}equivfrac{c_{lambda/2}}{mH_m^*}$.
With $s=(p-1)/2$,
$sum_{j=0}^s c_j^2equivbinom{2s}{s}equivε$, and multiplying by
$H_u^*equiv E_rH_P^*$ proves the first congruence also for even $u$.

For the second congruence suppose first $u$ even. In
$p^2k^+_{u+1}=H_u^*sum_{zequiv u(2)}2p^2/(z^2H_z^*)$ every term with
$pnmid z$ vanishes since $v_p(H_z^*)le1$; at $z=mp$, $m$ even and
$H_{mp}^*equiv H_m^*$, giving $E_rk^+_{P+1}$ when $r$ even and zero when
$r$ odd. For odd $u$ write $(pH_u^*)sum_{z odd}2p/(z^2H_z^*)$; only
$z=mp$ survives, there $pH_{mp}^*equivε H_m^*$ and the two
$ε$ cancel. This proves the second congruence in every case and the
stated local integrality.

If $ige j$ put $u=i-j=Pp+r$, so $d=p-1-r$ and $i'=j'+P$, and use
$M^0(i,j)=k^-_u-sum_{k=1}^j m_{u+k-1}/k$. After multiplication by $p^2$
terms with $pnmid k$ vanish by $pm_{z-1}equiv E_{p-1-r}m_{P-1}$, and at
$k=mp$ the surviving term is $E_{p-1-r}m_{P+m-1}/m$, giving
$p^2M^0(i,j)equiv E_{p-1-r}(k^-_P-sum_{m=1}^{j'}m_{P+m-1}/m)=E_dM^0(i',j')$.
If $i<j$ put $u=j-i-1=Pp+r$, so $d=r$ and $i'=j'-P-1$, and use
$M^0(i,j)=k^+_{u+1}-sum_{k=u+2}^j m_{k-u-2}/k$. For a multiple $k=mp$,
$k-u-1=(m-P-1)p+(p-1-r)$, and the reduced summand is $E_rm_{m-P-2}/m$; a
possible first multiple with $m=P+1$ contributes zero, so the reduced sum
runs over $m=P+2,...,j'$ as in $M^0(i',j')$. Also
$i'=lfloor(i-ell)/prfloorge-1$; when $i'=-1$ and $j'=P$ the sum is
empty and the starting term is exactly $k^+_{j'+1}=M^0(-1,j')$. The reduced
indices are below $H/p<p/4$, so the right side is integral at $p$.

For $Z^0$, write $I=lfloor i/prfloor$ and $J=lfloor j/prfloor$. Since
$i,j<p^2$, $pB_i^{(1)}equiv B_I^{(1)}$ and $p^2B_i^{(2)}equiv B_I^{(2)}$.
The diagonal formula follows at once. Off the diagonal, if
$inotequiv jpmod p$ the denominator $i-j$ is a unit and $p^2Z^0(i,j)equiv0$;
if the residues agree, $i-j=p(I-J)$ with $I-J$ a unit, and the first
harmonic congruence gives $Z^0(I,J)$. This also proves integrality of
$p^2Z^0(i,j)$.


## The leading layer and paired raw columns

We gather the moment reductions into column vectors. For a fixed residue
$0leell<p$ define, over $mathbb F_p$,bbF_p$,
[
P'_u=[t^{(u+1)p+ell}],tP(t)E(t) (uge-1),qquad
D'_u=[t^{up+ell}],D(t) (uge0),
]
where $P,D$ denote the vectors of all row polynomials, with the dependence
on $ell$ understood. Their supports are finite.
Lemma~lem:odd-digit-reductions, gathered by coefficient residue,
gives

X_{kp+ell}:=p^2F_{kp+ell}bmod p
   =sum_{uge-1}P'_uM^0(u,k)-frac32sum_{uge0}D'_uZ^0(u,k).

For a term $[t^i]P$, its residue $d$ satisfies $i+1+d=(i'+1)p+ell$, so it
contributes exactly to $P'_{i'}$ including $i'=-1$; the $Z^0$ reduction
survives only when $i=up+ell$, which is the extraction defining $D'_u$.
The $G$ coefficient is a sum of integral multiples of $c_{(i-j)/2}$, hence
integral at every odd prime, so it disappears after multiplication by
$p^2$. Every raw column therefore lies in $p^{-2}mathbb Z_p^n$.bbZ_p^n$.

If $ellge H-2p$, the degree bounds $deg(tPE)le H+p-1$ and $deg D<H$
show $P'_u,D'_u$ vanish for $u>1$. Put
[
V_ell=2P'_1-tfrac32D'_1,qquad U_ell=2P'_{-1}-tfrac32D'_0,
]
and note the small reduced values
[
{c|rrr}
u&-1&0&1hline
M^0(u,0)&0&0&2
M^0(u,1)&2&0&-2

qquad
{c|rr}
u&0&1hline
Z^0(u,0)&0&1
Z^0(u,1)&1&-1
.
]
Consequently, whenever the indicated columns exist,

X_ell=V_ell,qquad X_{p+ell}=U_ell-V_ell.

Since $tP$ and $D$ have no terms below degree $A+1$, $U_ell=0$ for
$ellle A$.

We record how changes in the full column pool are used. Let $T$ be the $n$
by $(n+q)$ matrix of all raw columns. If $QinGL_{n+q}(mathbb Z_p)$bbZ_p)$
and every full minor of $TQ$ has valuation at least $-D$, the same holds
for every full minor of $T=(TQ)Q^{-1}$ by Cauchy--Binet, the coefficients
being minors of the integral matrix $Q^{-1}$. Applying Cauchy--Binet once
more to the integer filter matrix gives $v_p(Delta_N)ge-D$. This
transfer applies even when the columns or their leading residues are
linearly dependent.

For $2sqrt H<ple H/2$ use each pair of columns with
$max(b,H-2p)leell<min(p,L-p,A)$. Their number is exactly
[
d_0=(min(p,L-p,A)-max(b,H-2p))_+,
qquad y_+=max(y,0).
]
Replacing its high column by the sum of the pair is an integral elementary
column operation with integral inverse, and the pair sum lies in
$p^{-1}mathbb Z_p^n$bbZ_p^n$ by (eq:odd-paired-layer), while all other
columns still lie in $p^{-2}mathbb Z_p^n$.bbZ_p^n$. The pairs are disjoint, since
$ell<p$. Any selection of $n$ columns from the pool of $n+q$ contains at
least $(d_0-q)_+$ of the improved columns, so

v_p(Delta_N)ge-2n+(d_0-q)_+.


## The central layer and integral column elimination

For $p>H/2$ the next lemma identifies the residues of central columns
after multiplication by $p$, in terms of the same vectors $V_ell$. The
elimination retains noncentral columns and uses $p$ times those columns to
cancel the corresponding $V_ell$ terms.

[Central layer]
Suppose $H/2<ple H$ and write $K=L-p$, $J=H-p$. For every central column,
meaning
[
ble j<min(p,L),qquad jge J,
]
one has

pF_jinmathbb Z_p^n,bbZ_p^n,qquad
pF_jequiv-sum_{0leell<J}(V_ell)/(j-ell)pmod p.

All denominators $j-ell$ here are units at $p$.


Proof. 
For every contributing row degree $0le i<H$, $i-jle H-1-J=p-1$ and
$j-i<p$, so $|i-j|<p$ and the starting value $k^-_{i-j}$ or $k^+_{j-i}$ in
the diagonal reduction is integral at $p$. That reduction is the starting
value minus $sum_{0leell<min(i,j)}m_{i-ell-1}/(j-ell)$ with
$1le j-ell<p$. By $pm_{z-1}equiv E_{p-1-r}m_{P-1}$, a nonzero reduction
after multiplication by $p$ requires $ple i-ell<2p$, and then
[
pm_{i-ell-1}equiv2E_{2p+ell-1-i}.
]
Such an index has $0leell<J$, and conversely its coefficient can be
collected over all $i$ without truncation since $jge J>ell$ and
$ige p+ell>ell$. Hence
$pM^0(P,j)equiv-sum_{0leell<J}2P'_1/(j-ell)$ with $P'_1$ the extracted
vector of the residue $ell$. For $Z^0(i,j)$, the harmonic sums have a pole
only if $i=p+ell$ with $0leell<J$, where the denominator $i-j$ is a unit
(equality mod $p$ would need $j=ell<J$); then
$pZ^0(p+ell,j)equiv1/(ell-j)$, and all other $Z^0(i,j)$ are integral.
Combining these with the factor $-3/2$ proves (eq:central-layer); the
$G$ term is integral here as before.


We cancel in the full column pool, where $R$ counts retained columns with
possible denominator $p^2$ and $S$ bounds the other columns with possible
denominator $p$; every remaining column is integral.

[Elimination over the local integers]
For $H/2<ple H$ define

R&=K_++(K-A)_++(J-max(b,K))_+,
S&=(min(A,K)-b)_++(min(b,J)-max(0,K))_+.

Then every full raw minor, and hence the filtered determinant, satisfies

v_p(Delta_N)ge-min(2n,,n+R,,2R+S).



Proof. 
The noncentral columns are the high columns $F_{p+ell}$ for $0leell<K$
and the low columns $F_ell$ for $bleell<J$ (empty ranges allowed: $p>b$,
$J<p$, $K<J$). For $bleell<min(A,K)$ retain the low column and replace
the high column by $F_{p+ell}+F_ell$. Let
$B=(min(A,K)-b)_+$ be the number of these pair sums, each in
$p^{-1}mathbb Z_p^n$.bbZ_p^n$. Retain every other noncentral column as a column in
$p^{-2}mathbb Z_p^n$.bbZ_p^n$. The number retained is

R_0=K_++(J-b)_+-B,

which equals the stated $R$ (split $K$ into $Kle0$, $0<Kle b$,
$b<Kle A$, $K>A$, using $K<J$ in the last two cases).

These retained columns represent every $V_ell$ with $0leell<J$ except
possibly those in
[
mathcal E=calE={ell:max(0,K)leell<min(b,J)},quad
e=|mathcal E|=(calE|=(min(b,J)-max(0,K))_+.
]
For $bleell<J$ the retained low column has leading residue
$p^2F_ellequiv V_ell$; for $0leell<min(b,K)$ the unpaired high column
has leading residue $-V_ell$ since $ell<b<A$ makes $U_ell=0$. For each
represented residue choose a retained column $Y_ell$ and sign
$sigma_ell$ with $p^2Y_ellequivsigma_ell V_ell$, and for each central
$j$ choose $a_{ell j}inmathbb Z_p$bbZ_p$ reducing to $sigma_ell/(j-ell)$ and
replace that column by
[
C_j=F_j+psum_{substack{0leell<Jellnotinmathcal E}calE}a_{ell j}Y_ell.
]
This is a shear of the whole pool with integral coefficients and integral
inverse, preserving every retained column and pair sum; the resulting
central columns lie in $p^{-1}mathbb Z_p^n$bbZ_p^n$ and
$pC_jequiv-sum_{ellinmathcal E}calEV_ell/(j-ell)pmod p$. No information
about the next coefficient of $Y_ell$ is used: if
$Y_ell=p^{-2}y_0+p^{-1}y_1+y_2$, then $pY_ell=p^{-1}y_0+y_1+py_2$ has an
integral unknown second coefficient.

Let $c$ be the number of central columns and let their scaled residues
define $varphi:mathbb F_p^cbbF_p^ctomathbb F_p^n$,bbF_p^n$,
$(a_j)_jmapstosum_j a_j(pC_jbmod p)$. Its image lies in the span of the
$e$ exceptional vectors, so $d:=rankvarphilemin(c,e)$.
Choose a basis of $mathbb F_p^c$bbF_p^c$ whose last $c-d$ vectors span
$kervarphi$, lift its basis matrix to $WinMat_c(mathbb Z_p)$;bbZ_p)$;
then $WinGL_c(mathbb Z_p)$bbZ_p)$ by the adjugate formula. This
change leaves at most $d$ columns in $p^{-1}mathbb Z_p^n$;bbZ_p^n$; the other
$c-d$ are integral since their scaled residues vanish and they already
lay in $p^{-1}mathbb Z_p^n$.bbZ_p^n$. If $c=0$ this is empty.

The pool now has $R$ columns with possible denominator $p^2$, at most
$B+dle B+e=S$ other columns with possible denominator $p$, and integral
remaining columns. In a full minor with $r$ columns from the first group
and $s$ from the second, the loss is at most $2r+s$, and
$2r+sle2n$, $2r+sle n+R$, $2r+sle2R+S$ hold since $r+sle n$, $rle R$,
$sle S$. All changes and inverses are integral, so Cauchy--Binet proves
(eq:odd-two-layer-bound) for all original full minors and for
$Delta_N$.


The omission of the optional pair at $ell=A$ is harmless: its physical
column is included in $R$. The proof also covers $Kle0$ and $Jle b$; in
particular, if $Lle ple H$, then $R=0$ and $S=H-p$, and at the formal
endpoint $p=H$ all columns are integral.

## Summing the prime losses

For a nonzero rational determinant the weighted valuations sum to its
logarithmic absolute value; combining the odd-prime estimates with the
bound at $2$ gives the lower bound used in the final comparison.

[Finite-place bound]
Assume $Ginmathbb Q$.bbQ$. Along any sequence of positive integers $Nto∞$
for which $Delta_Nne0$, one has

liminf_{Nto∞}
((log|Delta_N|)/(n^2)-frac12log2)
ge -(8609)/(4608)-(frac12+(505)/(4608))log2
>-2.29084.



Proof. 
Put $x=p/N$. For $xle65/2$, (eq:odd-lower-pair-range) gives
[
(d_0)/(N)=(min(x,59-x,19)-max(7,65-2x))_+,
]
which is $0$ for $0le xle23$, $2x-46$ for $23le xle29$, and $12$ for
$29le xle65/2$. Only the part exceeding $q/N=4$ improves a full minor.
For $x>65/2$ the formulas of Lemma~lem:odd-local-elimination give the
following values, retaining the intermediate breakpoint $52$:
[
{c|cc}
x range&R/N&S/Nhline
[65/2,40]&105-2x&12
{}[40,52]&65-x&52-x
{}[52,58]&117-2x&x-52
{}[58,59]&59-x&6
{}[59,65]&0&65-x
.
]
The entries agree at common endpoints. Taking
$min(96,48+R/N,2R/N+S/N)$ yields the extra breakpoint $69/2$, so for every
relevant prime $p>2sqrt H$ the valuation is at least $-Nd(p/N)$ where $d$
is continuous, vanishes for $xge65$, and has the exact pieces

{c|c|c|c}
x range&d(x)&endpoints&displaystyleint_{range}d,dxhline
[0,25]&96&96,96&2400
{}[25,29]&146-2x&96,88&368
{}[29,65/2]&88&88,88&308
{}[65/2,69/2]&153-2x&88,84&172
{}[69/2,40]&222-4x&84,62&803/2
{}[40,58]&182-3x&62,8&630
{}[58,59]&124-2x&8,6&7
{}[59,65]&65-x&6,0&18
.

In particular

int_0^{65}d(x),dx=(8609)/(2).


The omitted small odd primes cost only $o(n^2)$. At any odd prime the
factorial formula gives $0le v_pbinom{2l}{l}le1+log H/log p$ for
$2lle H$, and the displayed formulas for $H_z^*,m_i,k_d^pm,Z^0$ and the
diagonal reduction give, with an absolute constant $C_0$ and with
$denG$ the reduced positive denominator of $G$,
[
v_p(F_j(r))ge-C_0(1+(log H)/(log p))-v_p(denG).
]
Expanding a determinant multiplies this by at most $n$, and summing over
$ple2sqrt H$ with weights $log p$ gives
$O(nsqrt Hlog H+nlog(denG))=o(n^2)$. For large $N$ every
prime $p>H$ is integral throughout, so these primes contribute nothing.

We use the prime number theorem $theta(y)=sum_{ple y}log psim y$
cite[Step~VI, p.~707]{Zagier1997}. Fix a partition
$0=x_0<...<x_m=65$; the theorem gives
$(theta(Nx_j)-theta(Nx_{j-1}))/Nto x_j-x_{j-1}$, and step functions
from the supremum and infimum of $d$ on each interval bound the weighted
sum, whose limiting difference tends to zero as the mesh shrinks. Thus

frac1Nsum_{ple65N}d(p/N)log p→int_0^{65}d(x),dx.

The prime $2$ and primes below $2sqrt H$ may be removed at cost $o(1)$,
using $|d|_∞=96$ and $theta(2sqrt H)le2sqrt Hlog(2sqrt H)$.

Combining with (eq:odd-loss-area) and $n^2=2304N^2$,
[
sum_{p odd}v_p(Delta_N)log pge-(8609)/(4608)n^2-o(n^2).
]
Proposition~prop:two-adic with $delta=10/48$ gives
[
v_2(Delta_N)ge-(fracdelta2+(delta^2)/(8))n^2-o(n^2)
=-(505)/(4608)n^2-o(n^2).
]
For a nonzero rational, $log|Delta_N|=sum_pv_p(Delta_N)log p$; dividing
by $n^2$ and subtracting $tfrac12log2$ proves (eq:finite-lower-final)
with $n=48N$, so $n^2=2304N^2$ and $Nto∞$ along the same sequence.

The last comparison has a short rational check. The identity
$log2=2sum_{jge0}3^{-(2j+1)}/(2j+1)$ and its positive geometric tail give
[
log2le2sum_{j=0}^{5}frac{3^{-(2j+1)}}{2j+1}
+frac{2cdot3^{-13}}{13(1-3^{-2})}<(693149)/(10^6).
]
So the lower constant exceeds
[
-(8609)/(4608)-(2809)/(4608)(693149)/(10^6)
=-(10556055541)/(4608000000)>-2.29084,
]
completing the proof.


# Nonvanishing along the prime sequence

The next proposition supplies the nonzero determinants needed in
Proposition~prop:finite-lower.


Assume $Ginmathbb Q$.bbQ$. For every sufficiently large prime $p$, the
determinant with scale parameter $N=p$ satisfies

v_p(Delta_p)=-96p.

In particular $Delta_pne0$ for every sufficiently large prime $p$.


We reduce a matrix of size $48p$ to three fixed rational matrices of size
$48$. Throughout, an underlined row polynomial is formed with $N=1$, so
[
n_0=48,quad b_0=7,quad q_0=g_0=4,quad h_0=2,quad
L_0=59,quad C_0=63,quad H_0=65.
]
In addition to the usual rows $0,...,47$, define the auxiliary row $48$
by the same formulas as in (eq:rows). For $0le rle48$ and
$0le k<48$ put

mathcal B(r,k)=calB(r,k)=sum_{j=0}^{4}(-1)^jbinom4j
bigl{M^0(underline P_r,7+k+j)-tfrac32 Z^0(underline D_r,7+k+j)bigr},

with $M^0,Z^0$ acting linearly on the first argument. Thus $mathcal B$calB$ is
a fixed rational $49times48$ matrix. Let
[
mathcal B_0=(calB_0=(mathcal B(r,k))_calB(r,k))_{0le r,k<48},qquad
mathcal B_1=(calB_1=(mathcal B(r+1,k))_calB(r+1,k))_{0le r,k<48}.
]
The certificate below proves

detmathcal B_0calB_0ne0,qquad
det(mathcal B_0+calB_0+mathcal B_1)calB_1)ne0,qquad
det(mathcal B_0-calB_0-mathcal B_1)calB_1)ne0.

We first show why these three fixed facts imply the proposition.

## Two palindromic bases

Let $p$ be an odd prime and work over $mathbb F_p$.bbF_p$. The space
[
mathcal P_p=calP_p={Qinmathbb F_p[w]:bbF_p[w]:deg Qle2p-2, w^{2p-2}Q(1/w)=Q(w)}
]
has dimension $p$. For $0leell,i<p$ consider
[
U_ell(w)=(2w)^ell(1+w^2)^{p-1-ell},qquad
Q_i(w)=w^isum_{j=0}^{p-i-1}w^{2j}.
]
Both families lie in $mathcal P_p$,calP_p$, with lowest terms $2^ell w^ell$ and
$w^i$. Triangularity in degrees $0,...,p-1$ makes each a basis. Define
$a_{ell i}inmathbb F_p$bbF_p$ by $Q_i=sum_ell a_{ell i}U_ell$ and
$mathbf a=(a_bfa=(a_{ell i})$. Lowest-term comparison gives

a_{ell i}=0 (ell<i),qquad a_{ii}=2^{-i},qquad
detmathbf a=2^bfa=2^{-p(p-1)/2}ne0,

so this varying-size matrix introduces no exceptional odd primes.

Let $Q'_0=0$ and $Q'_i=Q_{p-i}$ for $1le i<p$, written
$Q'_i=sum_ell b_{ell i}U_ell$. Then

mathbf b=bfb=mathbf abfaPi,qquad Pi e_0=0,qquad Pi e_i=e_{p-i} (1le i<p),

with $e_i$ the coordinate vectors. Explicitly
$Q_i=w^i(1-w^{2(p-i)})/(1-w^2)$ and $Q'_i=w^{p-i}(1-w^{2i})/(1-w^2)$. Their
sum is
[
Q_i+w^pQ'_i=w^isum_{j=0}^{p-1}w^{2j}=w^i(1-w^2)^{p-1},
]
since $binom{p-1}{j}=(-1)^j$ in $mathbb F_p$.bbF_p$. With $t=2w/(1+w^2)$,
$f=(1-w^2)/(1+w^2)$, $E(t)=(1-t^2)^{(p-1)/2}=f^{p-1}$, division by
$(1+w^2)^{p-1}$ gives the identity

## Frobenius and the two extraction shifts

Set $N=p$, write each row index uniquely as $r=pr_0+i$ with $0le r_0<48$
and $0le i<p$, and put $t'=t^p$, $w'=w^p$, $f'=f^p$. Frobenius gives
$t'=2w'/(1+(w')^2)$, $f'=(1-(w')^2)/(1+(w')^2)$ and $(f')^2=1-(t')^2$.
With $C=63p$, $h=2p$, $g=4p$ in (eq:rows),

index at most $48$. Since star fixes $t,t'$ and negates $f,f'$, and
$fE=f'$, the row identities give $tR_rE=tP_rE-f'D_r$ and
$t'underline R_j=t'underline P_j-f'underline D_j$. Taking the
star-invariant part of (eq:nonvanishing-frobenius-row), then its
anti-invariant part and cancelling $f'$, yields

(a_{ell i}underline D_{r_0}(t')+b_{ell i}underline D_{r_0+1}(t')).


These are polynomial identities, since both sides are polynomials and
$t=2w/(1+w^2)$ is injective on $mathbb F_p[t]$.bbF_p[t]$.

In the notation of (eq:column-layer) the extractions for a fixed
residue $ell$ are
$P'_u=[t^{(u+1)p+ell}],tP_rE$ and $D'_u=[t^{up+ell}],D_r$. Every
polynomial is uniquely $sum_ell t^ell A_ell(t^p)$, so the last two

bigl{a_{ell i}(underline P_{r_0},underline D_{r_0})(t')

The $t'$ in the first identity is exactly consumed by the $u+1$ shift in
the $P$ extraction; the $D$ extraction has none, and $P'_{-1}=0$ here.

There is a degree issue at the last successor row. The auxiliary
polynomials have

mindegunderline P_{48}=62-44=18,qquad
mindegunderline D_{48}=63-44=19.

For the last block $r=47p+i$, $i>0$, (eq:nonvanishing-transition)
gives $b_{ell i}=0$ for $ell<p-i$ and $b_{p-i,i}=2^{-(p-i)}ne0$, so the
successor contribution starts at $19p+(p-i)=20p-i$, while the first-row
contribution starts no earlier than $20p+i$. Directly from the rows,
$|r-g|=43p+i$ makes $tP_r$ and $D_r$ start at $C-|r-g|=20p-i$, matching
the extraction since $E(0)=1$. The leading Chebyshev coefficients are
powers of $2$, nonzero in odd characteristic; when $i=0$ the successor term
vanishes and the first term starts at $20p$. Thus the lower degree of the
auxiliary row injects no forbidden coefficient, and its contact
$tunderline R_{48}/f=O(t^{107})$ holds since $63+48-4=107>59$.

# The real-place determinant estimates

We estimate the determinant (eq:determinant) without any rationality
assumption. Write

## The integral and its two sheets

Put $dmu(t)=|t|,dt/f(t)$ on $(-1,1)$ and set
$A_r(t)=P_r(t)-frac32mathbf 1_bf1_{{t>0}}frac{f(t)}tD_r(t)$ and
$psi_k(s)=s^{b+k}(1-s)^q$. The value at $t=0$ is immaterial; the
combination has a finite limit there because $D_r(t)/t$ is a polynomial.
The determinant entries are exactly
$int_{-1}^1int_0^1A_r(t)psi_k(s)/(1-ts),ds,dmu(t)$. The measure has
mass $mu((-1,1))=2$, and the scalar majorant
$int!int(1-|t|s)^{-1}ds,dmu(t)$ is finite since integration in $s$
gives at worst an integrable logarithmic singularity at $|t|=1$; the
functions $A_r,psi_k$ are bounded for fixed $n$. Thus all permutation
expansions below are absolutely integrable.

Applying Andr'eief's identity twice
cite[Equation~(1.7) and Section~2.2]{Forrester2018Andreief}, expanding
determinants and relabelling,

Delta_N=frac1{(n!)^2}int_{(-1,1)^n}int_{(0,1)^n}
det[A_r(t_i)],det!Bigl[frac1{1-t_is_j}Bigr],
det[psi_k(s_j)],ds,dmu^n(t).

Fubini applies by the majorant. Cauchy's double alternant
cite[Section~2.1, Equation~(2.7)]{Krattenthaler1999} is

det!Bigl[frac1{1-t_is_j}Bigr]=frac{V(t)V(s)}{prod_{i,j}(1-t_is_j)};

multiplying by the denominator gives a polynomial alternating separately in
$t$ and $s$, and expanding at $s=0$ shows the constant is $1$. Also
$det[psi_k(s_j)]=V(s)prod_js_j^b(1-s_j)^q$.

Use the real coordinate

x_i=frac{t_i}{1+√(1-t_i^2)},qquad
t_i=(2x_i)/(1+x_i^2),qquad
dmu(t_i)=(4|x_i|)/((1+x_i^2)^2),dx_i,

which maps $(-1,1)$ bijectively to $(-1,1)$. Except on a null set we may
assume the $x_i$ are distinct and nonzero. Define
[
(xi_{mathrm f}rmf(x),xi_{mathrm n}rmn(x))=(1/2,1/2),&x<0,(-1/4,5/4),&x>0,
qquad

]
The subscripts refer to evaluation at $x_i$ (near) and $1/x_i$ (far),
inside and outside the unit circle. Since $fD_r/t=(R_r^*-R_r)/2$, the
negative half-interval has row $(R_r+R_r^*)/2$ and the positive half has
$(5R_r-R_r^*)/4$; factoring $(1-t_i)^ht_i^{C-1}$ from column $i$ gives the
exact identity

There is one $t$-Vandermonde and two $s$-Vandermondes, and all denominators
are positive.

## A uniform interpolating function

Put $c_i=xi_{mathrm n}rmn(x_i)/xi_{mathrm f}rmf(x_i)$, nonzero on both
halves. For $0le r<n$,

xi_{mathrm f}rmf(x_i)x_i^{g-r}+xi_{mathrm n}rmn(x_i)x_i^{r-g}
=xi_{mathrm f}rmf(x_i)x_i^{g-(n-1)}
(x_i^{n-1-r}+c_i x_i^{D_*}x_i^r).

Thus a holomorphic $h_*$ with $h_*(x_i)=c_ix_i^{D_*}$ turns the bracket into
$x_i^{n-1-r}+h_*(x_i)x_i^r$, and we bound the resulting evaluation
determinant through the norm of $h_*$. The first estimate applies when

sum_{i=1}^n(1-x_i^2)/(1+x_i^2)le D_*.

The construction is uniform even when the nodes cluster.


Let $x_1,...,x_n$ be distinct nonzero reals in $(-1,1)$ satisfying
(eq:case-condition). There is a function $h_*$ holomorphic on a
neighborhood of the closed unit disk with
[
h_*(x_i)=c_ix_i^{D_*},qquad
c_i=1,&x_i<0,-5,&x_i>0,
qquad
sup_{|z|le1}|h_*(z)|le K_0n,quad K_0=10e^{12}.
]
The neighborhood may depend on the nodes; the bound does not.


Proof. 
Set $B(z)=prod_i(z-x_i)/(1-x_iz)$ and $F(z)=z^{D_*}/B(z)$. For
$0<ule1$ and real $0<|x|<1$,
[
(d)/(dlog u)frac12log(u^2+x^2)/(1+x^2u^2)
=((1-x^4)u^2)/((u^2+x^2)(1+x^2u^2))le(1-x^2)/(1+x^2),
]
using $u^2+u^{-2}ge2$. Integrating from $u$ to $1$ gives
$|B(iu)|ge u^{sum_i(1-x_i^2)/(1+x_i^2)}ge u^{D_*}$; the same holds at
$-iu$, and $F(0)=0$. For $1le ule1+2/n$ each factor has modulus at least
one because $(u^2+x^2)-(1+x^2u^2)=(u^2-1)(1-x^2)ge0$, so

|F(iu)|le e^2qquad(|u|le1+2/n).


Orient $Gamma=[-i(1+2/n),i(1+2/n)]$ upwards and define, off $Gamma$,
$mathcal C(z)=calC(z)=frac1{2pi i}int_Gamma6F(w)/(w-z),dw$. Write
$c_-=1$, $c_+=-5$ and

The density is holomorphic near $Gamma$ (its poles $x_i$ are nonzero

two continuations agree, because
$(c_--c_+)z^{D_*}-6B(z)F(z)=0$; this glues $h_*$ holomorphically across

For the bound, in either radius-$1/4$ disk about $pm i$ the quantities

As $|F(pm i)|=1$, integration within those disks gives $|F(w)|le e^{12}$

near $pm i$; replacing the relevant portion of $Gamma$ by three sides of
a rectangle whose far vertical side has real part
$-sign(Rez)/n$ (in the radius-$1/4$ disk,
containing neither $z$ nor a pole) leaves $mathcal C(z)$calC(z)$ unchanged. Each
new side is within $3/n$ of $pm i$; the contour has length at most $3$ and

## Evaluation in a finite-dimensional Hilbert space

The next balance uses the kernel and compression viewpoint of bounded
analytic interpolation [cite]; the finite-dimensional facts are
proved directly.

[Interpolation estimate]

The prefactor is $exp(O(nlog n))$, uniformly in the nodes.


Proof. 
Let $Q(z)=prod_i(1-x_iz)$ and give
$mathcal H_Q=calH_Q={v/Q:vinmathbb C[z],bbC[z], deg v<n}$ the $H^2$ norm

The zeros of $Q$ lie outside the closed disk, so this is a genuine norm.
Define $widetilde v(z)=z^{n-1}v(1/z)$ and $R(v/Q)=widetilde v/Q$; this is
a complex-linear isometry, since $|widetilde v(e^{itheta})|=|v(e^{-itheta})|$
and $|Q(e^{itheta})|=|Q(e^{-itheta})|$.

$sqrt n$, so Hadamard gives $2^{-binom n2}n^{n/2}$. The far-to-near weight
ratio is
$|1/x|^{-g}(1+x^{-2})^{(n-1)/2}/(|x|^{-g}(1+x^2)^{(n-1)/2})=|x|^{-D_*}ge1$,
so all weights are bounded by their far-sheet values; and the sum of
absolute sheet coefficients is at most
$prod_i(|xi_{mathrm f}rmf(x_i)|+|xi_{mathrm n}rmn(x_i)|)le(3/2)^n$.


## The two principal integrands

Put $t_i=2x_i/(1+x_i^2)$ and
[
mathcal J_n(x,s)=calJ_n(x,s)=frac{mathcal V(t)calV(t)mathcal V(s)^2}calV(s)^2{prod_{i,j}(1-t_is_j)}
prod_js_j^b(1-s_j)^qprod_i|t_i|^{C-1}(1-t_i)^h.
]
Define


mathcal I_calI_{2,n}(x,s)&=mathcal J_n(x,s)calJ_n(x,s)mathcal V(1/x)calV(1/x)prod_i|x_i|^g,
mathcal I_calI_{1,n}(x,s)&=mathcal J_n(x,s)2^calJ_n(x,s)2^{-binom n2}
prod_i(1+x_i^2)^{(n-1)/2}|x_i|^{g-(n-1)}.


Let $Ω_{2,n}$ be the interior configurations with distinct nonzero
$x_i$ satisfying (eq:case-condition), and $Ω_{1,n}$ the
complementary case. We use the interpolation estimate on $Ω_{2,n}$ and
the Hadamard estimate on $Ω_{1,n}$ (valid in both cases). After
$t=2x/(1+x^2)$, the index $kappa$ is the exponent of the absolute
$x$-Vandermonde in the majorant: $kappa=2$ for interpolation, $kappa=1$
for Hadamard; the $s_j$ range over $(0,1)$.

The propositions and (eq:real-sheet-integral) give
[
|Delta_N|le(2^nK_n)/((n!)^2)max_{kappain{1,2}}sup_{(x,s)inΩ_{kappa,n}}mathcal I_calI_{kappa,n}(x,s),
quad K_n=max{(1+K_0n)^n,(3/2)^nn^{n/2}},
]
since $dmu^n(t)ds$ has total mass $2^n$ and the two case sets partition the
domain up to a null set. In logarithmic form, with $log0=-∞$,

(log|Delta_N|)/(n^2)-frac12log2
lemax_{kappain{1,2}}sup_{(x,s)inΩ_{kappa,n}}
(frac{logmathcal I_calI_{kappa,n}(x,s)}{n^2}-frac12log2)
+O(frac{log(n+2)}n).

The error is independent of the integration lists; factorials, measure
masses and sheet sums are all accounted for.

Repeated nonzero $x_i$ give a zero row determinant and are handled by
continuity in the first estimate; repeated $s_j$ give a zero $s$
Vandermonde. Sets with a zero or boundary node have measure zero, and near
them the original expression (eq:real-double-integral) supplies the
integrable definition. No separation condition is needed in any bound, so
all such configurations remain in the suprema of
(eq:real-supremum-reduction).

# A uniform energy bound

This section replaces the two many-variable majorants by one-variable
suprema plus explicit quadratic terms. The bound must be uniform before
taking those suprema; matched damping and retained endpoint corrections
provide this uniformity.

We retain $alpha,beta,gamma,eta$, the cases $kappa=2,1$, and the
nonnegative majorants $mathcal I_calI_{kappa,n}$ of
(eq:real-integrand-majorants). Put

W_kappa(x)&=(alpha+2gamma)log|x|+2etalog(1-x)
-(kappa/2+alpha+eta+gamma)log(1+x^2),notag
W_s(s)&=betalog s+gammalog(1-s),notag
D(x)&=2gamma-(2x^2)/(1+x^2).


Here $D(x)$ is a scalar function, distinct from the row polynomials $D_r$.
For a real sequence $u=(u_k)_{kge1}$ with $|u_k|le Kr^k$ for some
$K<∞$ and $0<r<1$, define

|u|_*^2=sum_{kge1}(u_k^2)/(k),qquad
T(u,x)=sum_{kge1}(u_kT_k(x))/(k),qquad
S(u,x)=sum_{kge1}(u_kx^k)/(k),

uniformly convergent for $-1le xle1$. The sequences $p,v$ below are freely
chosen trial coefficients; the inequality $-a^2le b^2-2ab$ replaces two
negative quadratic sums by affine upper bounds, leaving two one-variable
suprema. Each admissible choice gives a valid bound, and the certificate
supplies one successful choice per case.


Let $p,v$ be two such sequences and take $lambda=0$ in case $kappa=2$ or
any fixed $lambdage0$ in case $kappa=1$. Uniformly over configurations in
the indicated case,

&limsup_{Nto∞}sup_{case kappa}
(frac{logmathcal I_calI_{kappa,n}}{n^2}-frac12log2)notag
&qquadle(-1+alpha+gamma)log2+kappa|p|_*^2+frac12|v|_*^2notag
&qquadquad+sup_{substack{-1le x<1xne0}}{W_kappa(x)+lambda D(x)-2kappa T(p,x)-S(v,x)}notag
&qquadquad+sup_{0<s<1}{W_s(s)+2T(v,s)}.


Consequently the maximum of the right sides for the two cases bounds
$limsup_{Nto∞}(n^{-2}log|Delta_N|-tfrac12log2)$. We use
$log0=-∞$.


Proof. 
Write $< F(x)>=n^{-1}sum_iF(x_i)$, and similarly for $s$; in a

&=c_{kappa,n}log2
+Bigl< W_kappa(x)+(kappa+2)/(2n)log(1+x^2)Bigr>
+< W_s(s)>notag
&quad+frackappa2<log|x-x'|>_{ne}

&quad-<log(1-2xs+x^2)>,

The correction $(kappa+2)/(2n)<log(1+x^2)>$ is bounded by
$2log2/n$.

We use the classical Chebyshev expansion of the logarithmic kernel, in the
form of Haagerup's identity cite[Lemma~3.1 and its proof]{GaroufalidisPopescu2013}.
On $[-1,1]$, with $u=costheta$, $u'=costheta'$, $0le r<1$,

L_r(u,u')&=-log2+log|1-re^{i(theta+theta')}|+log|1-re^{i(theta-theta')}|notag
&=-log2-2sum_{kge1}(r^kT_k(u)T_k(u'))/(k).


Letting $r↑1$ at distinct $u,u'$ gives $L_1(u,u')=log|u-u'|$, since
the product of the two chord lengths is $2|u-u'|$. For $|z|,|z'|<1$,

log(1-zz')=-sum_{kge1}(z^kz'^k)/(k),qquad
-log(1-2zs+z^2)=2sum_{kge1}(z^kT_k(s))/(k),

the last being the log of $|1-ze^{iarccos s}|^2$. The singular kernels
cannot be summed at diagonals, so we regularize all appearances of the same
moment by the same factor, preserving completion of squares. Near
$(x,s)=(1,1)$, $|1-xe^{iarccos s}|^2=(1-x)^2+2x(1-s)$ identifies the
scales $1-x$ and $√(1-s)$.

Fix $0<ε<1/8$ and define

tau=1-ε,qquad
rho(x)=1-ε(1-x),qquad
sigma(s)=1-ε√(1-s).

Replace the $x$ cosine kernel by $L_{tau^2}(x,x')$, the $s$ cosine kernel by
$L_{sigma(s)sigma(s')}(s,s')$, the power kernel by
$log(1-rho(x)rho(x')xx')$, and the cross kernel by
$-log|1-rho(x)sigma(s)xe^{iarccos s}|^2$. Each original kernel is at
most its replacement plus $O(ε)$, with an absolute constant.

For $|zeta|=1$ and $0<rle1$,
$|1-rzeta|^2-r|1-zeta|^2=(1-r)^2ge0$, so each cosine kernel costs at
most $-log rle O(ε)$. For the power kernel put $u=xx'$ and
$r=rho(x)rho(x')$. If $uge0$, monotonicity gives
$log(1-u)lelog(1-ru)$. If $u<0$, write $q=-ule1$; then

log(1+q)-log(1+rq)le((1-r)q)/(1+rq)le1-rle4ε.

For the cross kernel write $a=1-x$, $b=√(1-s)$, $theta=arccos s$,
$z=1-xe^{itheta}$, $z_ε=1-rho(x)sigma(s)xe^{itheta}$; then
$|z_ε-z|leε|x|(a+b)$. On $xle0$, $|z|ge1$ and
$|x|(a+b)le3$; on $0le xle1/2$, $|z|ge1/2$ and $|x|(a+b)le1$; on
$1/2le x<1$, $|z|^2=a^2+2xb^2ge a^2+b^2$ and $|x|(a+b)lesqrt2|z|$.
Hence $|z_ε-z|le3ε|z|$ everywhere and, in the
direction needed,

-log|z|^2le-log|z_ε|^2+2log(1+3ε)le-log|z_ε|^2+6ε.


For each fixed finite interior configuration the damped series converge
absolutely. Put
[

]
Inserting diagonals gives two exact negative-square identities,

&=-frackappa2log2-kappasum_{kge1}(A_k^2)/(k),



&frac12<log(1-rho(x)rho(x')xx')>+< L_{sigma(s)sigma(s')}(s,s')>notag
&qquad-<log|1-rho(x)sigma(s)xe^{iarccos s}|^2>notag

The $B_k,C_k$ in the cross term are exactly those of the pure terms. Adding
the constants to $c_{kappa,n}log2$ gives
$(-1+alpha+gamma-(kappa+1)/(2n))log2$; the omitted-diagonal

Control this correction before taking any supremum. A damped cosine diagonal
is at least $-log2+2log(1-r)$, so the $x$ cosine diagonals cost

$1-rho(x)^2x^2ge1-x$ ($xge0$) and

For real $a,b$, $-a^2le b^2-2ab$. Apply this to the first square with

## Rational logarithms and all candidate values

For a positive rational $s=2^my$ with $1le yle2$,

H(y)=2sum_{j=0}^{17}frac1{2j+1}((y-1)/(y+1))^{2j+1},

with $log s$ replaced by $mH(2)+H(y)$. If $q=(y-1)/(y+1)$, the positive
remainder obeys
$0lelog y-H(y)le2q^{37}/(37(1-q^2))$. For a nonzero rational complex
$a+ib$, the real log part is half the real log of $a^2+b^2$; for the
principal argument choose an octant: rotate by $-kpi/4$, $-4le kle4$, so
the rotated real part is positive and the ratio $t$ satisfies $|t|le1/2$,
and select the rotation with $kpi/4+arctan t$ principal (value $pi$ on the
negative real axis). The ratio $t$ is rational, since the rotation is, up to
positive scale, a product of maps $(a,b)mapsto(a+b,b-a)$ or $(a-b,a+b)$.
With

J(t)=sum_{j=0}^{23}frac{(-1)^jt^{2j+1}}{2j+1},

$arctan(1/2)+arctan(1/3)=pi/4$ follows from the tangent addition formula
with both summands positive summing below $pi/2$, and
$|arctan t-J(t)|le|t|^{49}/49$.

All positive inputs lie between $2^{-100}$ and $2^{100}$: real arguments at
evaluation points are between $.0007$ and $2$, and squared moduli of complex

## From bracket values to global suprema

A single derivative bound suffices on every whole closed bracket. All its
points are at distance greater than $.0007$ from both $0$ and $1$; the bracket
nearest $s=1$ has right endpoint $9992037198/10^{10}$, distance exactly
$.0007962802$ from $1$. For $|x|le1$,
$U_{k-1}(costheta)=sin(ktheta)/sintheta$ gives $|U_{k-1}(x)|le k$,
including endpoint limits. The polynomial part of $X_kappa'$ is at most
$4sum_{k=1}^{10}k+10=230$; its endpoint terms are bounded by
$(19/48+1/12)/.0007<685$; its $x/(1+x^2)$ term is below $3$; its multiplier
term below $4lambda_1<10$; and its two log-tail derivatives by
$4cdot10/(.06)^2+13/.016<11924$. Hence $|X_kappa'|<12852<120000$
throughout every bracket. For $Y_kappa'$ the polynomial part is at most
$110$, the endpoint terms below $(7/48+1/12)/.0007<328$, and the log tails
below $26/(.016)^2=101562.5$, so $|Y_kappa'|<102001<120000$ on every whole
bracket. These hold in both cases, with empty tails in case $1$.

By the mean value theorem, moving from a left bracket endpoint to its
stationary point raises either value by less than
$120000cdot2/10^{10}=.000024$. Every interior stationary point lies in one
of these brackets or is a division point; all finite division points are in
Table~tab:certificate-values ($-1$ for $X_2$; $1/4,1/2,3/4$ for $Y_2$;
$-1,-1/2,1/2$ for $X_1$). The remaining endpoints are singular with limit
$-∞$: $xto0^pm$ and $xto1^-$ for $X_kappa$, $xto0^+$ and
$xto1^-$ for $Y_kappa$, unaffected by the bounded tangent and multiplier
terms. So the root exhaustion, the finite endpoint values and the derivative
estimate certify the global suprema, not just the sampled values.

Finally $-1+alpha+gamma=-11/16$. Apply Proposition~prop:energy-reduction,
use (eq:certificate-coarse-cutoffs), and add $.000024$ per supremum.
Since $log2>.693146$, the right side is strictly less than

-(11)/(16)(.693146)+.77844-.98399-1.60890+.000048&=-2.290939875,
-(11)/(16)(.693146)+.9321-1.3244-1.4280+.000048&=-2.296789875.

Taking the larger proves Proposition~prop:real-upper.

# Conclusion

Proof. [Proof of Theorem~thm:main]
Suppose $G$ is rational. Proposition~prop:rationality makes $Delta_N$
rational for every $N$, and Proposition~prop:nonvanishing gives
$Delta_pne0$ for every sufficiently large prime $p$. Along this prime
sequence, Proposition~prop:finite-lower gives
[
liminf_{substack{pto∞p {rm prime}}}
((log|Delta_p|)/((48p)^2)-frac12log2)>-2.29084,
]
while Proposition~prop:real-upper gives, along the same sequence, an
upper limit strictly below $-2.2909$. Since $-2.29084>-2.2909$, this is a
contradiction. Hence $G$ is irrational.


Hyperbolic volumes.: 
Normalize sectional curvature to $-1$. Agol's theorem identifies $4G$ as the
minimum volume of an orientable complete finite-volume hyperbolic
three-manifold with exactly two cusps, attained by the Whitehead-link and
$(-2,3,8)$-pretzel-link complements
cite[Introduction and Theorem~3.6]{Agol2010TwoCusps}. Theorem~thm:main
therefore makes this minimum and both link volumes irrational. More
generally, every orientable arithmetic hyperbolic three-orbifold defined over
$mathbb Q(i)$bbQ(i)$ has irrational volume. Its lattice
$GammalePSL_2(mathbb C)$bbC)$ is, up to conjugacy, commensurable
with the projective norm-one group $Gamma^1(mathcal O)=calO)=mathcal O^1/calO^1/{pm1}$
of a maximal order $mathcal O$calO$ in a quaternion algebra $B/mathbb Q(i)$bbQ(i)$
cite[Definition~38.3.4]{Voight2021Quaternion}. For each such algebra with
finite reduced discriminant $mathfrak D$,frakD$, the volume formula gives
[
vol(Gamma^1(mathcal O)calO)backslashmathbb H^3)bbH^3)
=(G)/(3)prod_{mathfrak pfrakpmidmathfrak D}frakD(Nmathfrak p-1),frakp-1),
qquad Nmathfrak p=|frakp=|mathbb Z[i]/bbZ[i]/mathfrak p|,frakp|,
]
obtained by inserting
$zeta_{mathbb Q(i)}bbQ(i)(2)=zeta(2)L(2,chi_{-4})=(pi^2/6)G$ into
cite[Theorem~39.1.13]{Voight2021Quaternion}. After conjugating, a common
finite-index subgroup $H$ gives
[
vol(Gammabackslashmathbb H^3)bbH^3)
=([Gamma^1(mathcal O):H])/([calO):H])/([Gamma:H])
vol(Gamma^1(mathcal O)calO)backslashmathbb H^3),bbH^3),
]
a positive rational multiple of $G$, hence irrational. In the split case
$B=M_2(mathbb Q(i))$,bbQ(i))$, $mathcal O=M_2(calO=M_2(mathbb Z[i])$,bbZ[i])$, the empty product
yields
$vol(PSL_2(mathbb Z[i])bbZ[i])backslashmathbb H^3)=G/3$bbH^3)=G/3$
cite[Example~39.1.16]{Voight2021Quaternion}.
