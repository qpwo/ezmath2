title[Arithmeticity of twisted finite correspondences]{Arithmeticity of twisted finite correspondences for lattices over local fields}




Abstract: 
We prove arithmetic exhaustion for bifinite correspondences between
arbitrarily scalar-twisted group factors of ICC groups commensurable with
property-(T) lattices over characteristic-zero local fields and twisted
group factors of arbitrary countable ICC groups. Every such correspondence
is a closed summand of a finite direct sum of models obtained from actual
finite-index subgroup isomorphisms and finite-dimensional projective
representations of the matched cocycle ratio. The proof includes reducible
products, mixed real and finite places, and quaternionic and Cayley
rank-one factors.

In one sentence: an analytic finite relation between the two quantum
shadows of the groups is always generated by a finite list of concrete
algebraic relations, together with their unavoidable phases.

# A Guide for the Curious Reader
addcontentsline{toc}{section}{A Guide for the Curious Reader}

This paper is technical, and the technical part starts in
Section~sec:intro. This first part is a guide: it explains, in
ordinary language and with examples, what the objects are, what the
question is, what the answer says, and how the proof reaches it. Nothing
here is needed for the formal statements, and nothing here is a proof. If
you are meeting this material for the first time, read this part once,
then read the introduction, then dive into whatever section you find
intriguing. The last subsection of the guide is a map of the paper.

## 1. Symmetry, and the grids inside it

A group is a mathematical object that records symmetries. The
rotations of a square form a group with four elements; the ways to
permute three objects form a group with six elements; the translations
of the plane form an infinite group. Whenever something has symmetry,
there is a group behind it, and the group tells you exactly which
transformations leave the object unchanged.

Some of the most important groups are continuous: instead of
finitely many or countably many symmetries, they form a smooth
space. For example, the set of all rotations of the plane about the
origin is a circle; the set of all invertible ntimes n real matrices
of determinant one, written SL_n(R), is a continuous group of
transformations of R^n that preserve volume and orientation.
Groups like these, that are also smooth manifolds, are called Lie
groups. They are the basic objects of the geometry of symmetry.

Inside a continuous group, one can sometimes find a lattice: a
discrete grid of symmetries that still "spreads out" through the whole
group, like the integer points
[
 Z^n R^nbbR^n
]
inside the group of all translations, or the famous
[
 SL_n(Z) R)bbR)
]
of integer matrices of determinant one inside all real matrices of
determinant one. A lattice is discrete, so its elements do not
accumulate, and yet it is large enough that the continuous group is
"almost" covered by translates of it. Lattices are hard, rich objects,
and they connect geometry, number theory, and group theory.

The groups in this paper are built by taking products of continuous
groups over different "places." The word place is from number
theory: the real numbers R, the complex numbers C,
and the p-adic numbers Q_p are all "completions" of the
rationals, and a formula that holds over the rationals has a face at
each place. A "local field" here means a finite extension of
R, C, or Q_p. Real place, complex place,
p-adic place: these are the three kinds of ground fields on which our
groups are built. A big lattice can have several faces at once: a real
face, and a p-adic face, and so on. The theory has to handle all of
them, and one of the achievements here is that it does, uniformly.

## 2. Rigidity: property (T)

A group has Kazhdan's property (T) if it is rigid: it cannot be
"almost" represented by symmetries without actually being represented.
Formally, every unitary representation that has almost-invariant unit
vectors already has an exactly invariant one. Intuitively, a group with
property (T) has a spectral gap: any attempt to wiggle a
representation produces a genuine, non-negligible displacement.

Rigidity is powerful. Many strong structural theorems in operator
algebras and geometry hold only for groups with property (T) (often,
conjecturally, for all higher-rank lattices, an expectation this paper
does not need). Property (T) is the main structural hypothesis that
the "source" side of our theorem carries. It is what lets us pin down
the geometry of the correspondence rather than chase a wild object that
squirms away.

The class of groups that this paper can handle is written
K, and it consists of the countably infinite groups that are
abstractly commensurable with a lattice in a product
G=prod_{i=1}^r G_i, where each factor G_i is the group of local
points of a connected, adjoint, absolutely simple algebraic group over a
local field k_iin{R,C,finitebbC,finite extension of Q_p},
generated by the unipotent radicals of the proper parabolic subgroups,
noncompact, and with property (T). Abstractly commensurable means: an
actual isomorphism between finite-index subgroups. No irreducibility,
cocompactness, product decomposition, or torsion hypothesis is imposed
on the lattice. The class is large; it contains the usual arithmetic
lattices in products of higher-rank real and p-adic groups, the
quaternionic and Cayley rank-one lattices, reducible lattices, and
nonuniform ones.

The "ICC" condition appears everywhere in the paper: every nonidentity
conjugacy class is infinite. This is a mild "non-abelianness" hypothesis
that keeps the group von Neumann algebra a factor (see below). It is
standard and holds for essentially all groups anyone cares about here.

## 3. Quantum shadows: group von Neumann algebras

To every group Gamma one can attach an algebra of operators that
serves as the group's "quantum shadow." The construction goes as
follows. Let ell^2(Gamma) be the Hilbert space of square-summable
functions on Gamma. For each ginGamma there is a unitary operator
lambda(g) shifting a function: (lambda(g)xi)(h)=xi(g^{-1}h). The
group von Neumann algebra L(Gamma) is the algebra of bounded
operators on ell^2(Gamma) generated by all these unitaries together
with their (weak) limits.

An algebra of this kind remembers a surprising amount of the group. It
also forgets a surprising amount. The central question of the field, in
one form or another, is how much does the shadow remember? If two
groups Gamma and Λ have isomorphic shadows L(Gamma)~=
L(Λ), must the groups be isomorphic? If the shadow retains, along
with any isomorphism, a controlled correspondence with the original
group, the group is called W^*-superrigid.

Recall the notion of a factor: a von Neumann algebra whose center
is trivial. A factor is the noncommutative analogue of an "indecomposable
system," a quantum object that cannot be split into independent
subsystems. The condition that Gamma be ICC is exactly what guarantees
that L(Gamma) is a factor of type II_1, the cleanest
infinite-dimensional kind, on which a natural trace (a kind of
dimension function) is defined.

## 4. Twisting by phases

Sometimes the symmetries of a system are only defined up to a phase: a
rotation by the identity angle might act as multiplication by a complex
number of modulus one. Such an "almost action" is a projective
representation, and its failure of multiplicativity is encoded in a
cocycle mu:GammatimesGammatoT. The rule is
[
 mu(g,h)mu(gh,j)=mu(h,j)mu(g,hj)
]
with values in the unit circle T. The twisted group von
Neumann algebra L_mu(Gamma) is built from Gamma together with
this cocycle: the generators u_g now satisfy u_gu_h=mu(g,h)u_{gh}.
Scalar cocycles are unavoidable in the geometry of these problems, and a
central theme of this paper is that they must be kept, not discarded:
they appear as the exact ratio between the two endpoint twists of
the final models.

## 5. Finite correspondences: quantized relations

A relation between two sets X and Y is a subset of Xtimes Y. In
the quantum world, a relation between two von Neumann algebras M and
N is an M--N bimodule: a Hilbert space on which M acts
from the left and N from the right, with the two actions commuting.
This is the operator-algebraic analogue of a bridge or a coupling
between the two systems.

There is a notion of size, or module dimension, for such a
bimodule, generalizing the dimension of a group factor over a subgroup
(after Jones' index theory and Pimsner--Popa). A bimodule is
bifinite if it has finite module dimension on both sides. Intuitively,
a finite correspondence is a bridge that is finite and
trace-preserving at both ends, the quantum analogue of a finite
relation.

The central question becomes: where can finite correspondences come
from? One family of examples is always available. Suppose Gamma and
Λ are groups and AleqGamma, BleqΛ are subgroups of
finite index, with an actual isomorphism delta:Ato B between them.
Suppose moreover there is a finite-dimensional projective unitary
representation sigma of A whose multiplier matches the cocycle
ratio, meaning
[
 sigma(a)sigma(b)=(mu(a,b))/(omega(delta(a),delta(b)))sigma(ab).
]
Then there is a canonical finite correspondence E(A,B,delta,sigma),
given by a relative tensor product (Definition and construction in
Section~sec:models). These are the arithmetic models.

## 6. The question, and the answer

The question: given a finite correspondence between the twisted group
factors L_mu(Gamma) and L_omega(Λ) for Gammainmathscr
KscrK and arbitrary countably infinite ICC Λ, is it built out of
arithmetic models? The answer proved here is yes.


Main theorem (Arithmetic exhaustion). Let GammainK,
let Λ be any countably infinite ICC group, and let mu,omega
be normalized scalar cocycles. Every bifinite
L_mu(Gamma)--L_omega(Λ) correspondence is unitarily
isomorphic to a closed bimodule summand of a finite direct sum of
arithmetic models E(A,B,delta,sigma), where AleqGamma and
BleqΛ have finite index, delta:Ato B is an actual group
isomorphism, and sigma is a finite-dimensional projective unitary
representation satisfying the matched-multiplier identity above.

Conversely, every such model, finite direct sum, and closed bimodule
summand is a normal bifinite correspondence. The elementary model has
dimensions n[Λ:B] on the left and n[Gamma:A] on the right,
where n=dim V_sigma.


Three things are worth stressing.

First the theorem is an exhaustion result: it says every finite
correspondence is contained in finitely many arithmetic models, up to a
closed summand. It does not claim every correspondence equals one
model; the summand and finite sum are necessary because a correspondence
can have irreducible pieces living in several distinct arithmetic models
at once.

Second, only the matched ratio mu/delta^*omega needs to admit a
finite-dimensional projective representation. Neither endpoint cocycle
is required to have finite order or finite type, and the image of
sigma may be infinite. This is exactly where the twisting appears as an
obstruction: existence of a nonzero correspondence of the simplest kind
forces Gamma and Λ to be abstractly commensurable.

Third, the theorem is unconditional within its class. It says nothing
about general property-(T) groups, and indeed property (T) alone
fails to give group reconstruction: independent concurrent
constructions give nonisomorphic ICC property-(T) groups with
isomorphic group factors. What is special to our lattice class is the
geometric rigidity available inside the correspondence, and the
arithmetic exhaustion is the exact finite-correspondence input for the
stable recovery results of the companion article.

## 7. Why this is hard

The comparison group Λ is only assumed countably infinite
and ICC. Its von Neumann algebra is as wild as any such algebra can be.
In particular we have no geometric action of Λ on any symmetric
space or building: the comparison group is an algebraic cipher, not a
geometric object. Yet we must produce actual finite-index subgroup graphs
of Λ. The only geometry available at the start is the geometry of
Gamma and its ambient groups G_i. The proof must manufacture
geometry inside the correspondence from the source side alone, then
must read Λ through it.

The second hardness is scalar. Even in the classical, untwisted
case, the argument must be careful. With twists in the game, phases
scatter everywhere and must be controlled exactly; an argument that only
controls them up to bounded error is not enough. This is why the paper
develops a fine analysis of Cartan projections, boundary maps, and their
Jacobians, rather than working at the level of coarse quasi-isometry.

## 8. How the proof works: a tour

The proof is long, but its shape is a single idea executed carefully:
read off the arithmetic from the geometry, working in small enough
pieces that phases stay visible. Here are the main steps.

Step 1. Reduce to a good lattice. First pass to an actual lattice
Gamma<G and decompose the finite commutant into compact projective
sectors, discarding the compact parts. What remains is a correspondence
with weakly mixing conjugation action on the commutant of the
source factor. This is the clean hypothesis under which the geometric
construction runs. Restoring the compact sectors at the end costs nothing
mathematically, but it must be done exactly, to restore the correct
projective multiplier (Sections~sec:setup and~sec:models).

Step 2. Build finite regular models and boundary measurements.
For the source lattice, construct finite regular models for the tensor
copies of the correspondence, in such a way that the diagonal right action
and the surviving left actions are all normal. Then use Fourier analysis
on these regular models to build positive operator-valued measurements of
the far-away Fourier index. Under a careful "ordered sharpening"
argument these become projection-valued measurements (PVMs) supported on
the source flag space: sharp observations of the boundary
(Sections~sec:links and~sec:transfer). The strong point is
that the construction is ordered: the image depends on the PVM's
order of integration, and the paper proves that the ordering survives the
limit exactly. This is what keeps the phases—which would otherwise be
invisible—under control.

Step 3. Control the speeds. The data of a correspondence carries
Cartan projections: real-valued coordinate functions on the source lattice
that record "how far out" an element is. One half of the proof is to
show that the corresponding target Cartan projection grows at most
linearly in the source speed, with a comparison constant independent of
everything else. This is the upper speed theorem, and it is
proved separately at three kinds of local places:
 Finite place (Section~sec:finite): compact-open fixed
algebras, a uniform p-adic logarithm lemma, and a Hausdorff-limit
argument on block stabilizers give the equality of source and target
dimensions over the prime field and the upper bound ell_T(g)le
d_St+O(1).
 Non-isolated archimedean place (Section~sec:panels):
optimal finite-dimensional modules of the rank-one Levi factor, and
L^2-trace inequalities on compact operators reconstructed from the
graph relation, give the analogous bound.
 Isolated real place (Sections~sec:ray--sec:scale):
ray densities on the symmetric space, a uniform radial-multiplier
(completely bounded) estimate, and a "Kazhdan tightening" that pins down
a linear subsequence give, instead of an upper bound, a lower
bound at linear speed—positive mass in a linear window on every fixed
input vector. This lower bound is what turns the isolated case into a
tractable one.


Step 4. Build heights and calibrate. On the isolated factors, the
boundary measurements have a derivative in the horizontal direction,
and the proof constructs a positive injective operator b_x at each
source label that commutes with the measurement P_x and transforms
under source and target translations by the inverse angular Jacobian.
This "height" is the analytic core of the isolated case
(Sections~sec:scale and~sec:heights). The height lets us
compare source and target Haar densities exactly, and calibrate the
whole correspondence to a genuine Haar-height isometry—an isometry
between the target chamber-variable space and the source chamber-variable
space, weighted by the height. This is the machine that converts "the
looks right" into "the same object" (Section~sec:heights).

Step 5. Prove locality and commutation. Boundary measurements at
different labels must commute. This is not automatic; they are
a priori noncommuting operators. Section~sec:locality proves
commutation for all labels of an isolated factor by a deep
finite-dimensional locality argument: it builds a graph domain
mathfrak D,frakD, proves that the coefficients of an arbitrary connected
piece are finite-jets, uses the geometry of the boundary kernel
K(w,z) to eliminate positive differential order, and concludes that
the joint spectral law of the angle-height operators is exactly the law
of evaluation of a smooth homogeneous diffeomorphism
of the boundary. Then it proves that the two normal copies of the boundary
calculus commute as well. This is where the "quantum relation" becomes
"a classical map between two compact spaces," which is the bridge step.

Step 6. Glue. Do this at every local type—every finite-place panel,
every real panel, every isolated factor—and prove that the local maps can
be glued into one norm-bisecting, essentially invertible chamber map
B_Sto B_T (Sections~sec:panels and~sec:gluing). The
finite-place panels give homogeneous analytic maps with isomorphic
p-adic differential; the real panels give smooth homogeneous maps
after a Taylor-density argument; the gluing sections install reflection
substitutions that propagate monotone commutativity along a longest Weyl
word.

Step 7. Read off the arithmetic. Now the boundary has become a
classical, invertible measure-class-preserving map. Uninduce the
correspondence over this classical datum. What appears is a target
transversal, and a finite family of atoms (minimal pieces of the
correspondence, which are finite-dimensional). Prove that the atoms form
a deterministic, finite-rank partition, permuted by both endpoint
groups with finite orbits and a free source action. Then, by pure group
theory—no more boundary analysis—the joint stabilizer of one atom is the
graph of a finite-index subgroup isomorphism Ato B, and the atom
carries the matched projective multiplier. This yields the arithmetic
models. Finally, undo the finite-index restriction and the compact
sector: everything restores and Theorem~thm:arithmetic-exhaustion
follows (Section~sec:recovery).



# Introduction

A finite correspondence between two group factors is a finite-dimensional
analytic relation, in the sense of von Neumann module dimension. For
lattice groups, we prove that such an analytic relation always comes from
a finite collection of concrete subgroup isomorphisms. The scalar
phases survive, and are not lost: the finite fibres are projective
representations whose multiplier is exactly the ratio of the two endpoint
cocycles along the subgroup isomorphism.

The source groups are lattices in products of higher-rank real and
nonarchimedean groups, together with the quaternionic and Cayley
property-(T) rank-one groups. The comparison group is only assumed to be
countably infinite and ICC. Thus the geometry that the proof uses must be
constructed inside the correspondence: no boundary or ambient Lie
group is available on the comparison side at the start.

## The class, conventions, and main theorem


Let K be the class of countably infinite ICC groups abstractly
commensurable with a lattice in a finite nonempty product
[
 G=prod_{i=1}^r G_i, G_i=mathbf H_i(k_i)^+.bfH_i(k_i)^+.
]
Here k_i is R, C, or a finite extension of
Q_p; the primes may differ. The algebraic group mathbf H_ibfH_i is
connected, adjoint, and absolutely simple over k_i. The superscript +
denotes the subgroup generated by the k_i-points of the unipotent
radicals of proper k_i-parabolic subgroups. Each G_i is required to be
noncompact and to have Kazhdan's property (T).

A lattice is discrete and has finite Haar covolume. Abstract
commensurability means an actual isomorphism between finite-index
subgroups. ICC means that every nonidentity conjugacy class is infinite.
No irreducibility, cocompactness, product decomposition, or torsion
hypothesis is imposed on the lattice.


Property (T) means that every continuous unitary representation with
almost invariant unit vectors admits a nonzero invariant vector. The real
factors in Definition~def:class are either higher rank or locally
Sp(n,1), nge2, or F_{4(-20)}; at a finite place the relative rank
is at least two. Complex places are treated as real Lie groups when
differential arguments are used. Standard structure and permanence
results are recalled in Section~sec:setup; see also
[cite].

All scalar cocycles are T-valuedbbT-valued and normalized:
[
 mu(g,h)mu(gh,j)=mu(h,j)mu(g,hj),
 mu(e,g)=mu(g,e)=1.
]
The twisted group factor L_mu(Gamma) has canonical unitaries u_g
with u_gu_h=mu(g,h)u_{gh} and trace tau(u_g)=mathbf 1_bf1_{{e}}(g).
For an ICC group this is a factor: the absolute values of Fourier
coefficients of a central vector are constant on conjugacy classes, so
square summability kills all nonidentity coefficients.

A bifinite M--N correspondence is a Hilbert space carrying
commuting normal unital left M and right N actions and finite von
Neumann module dimensions on both sides. The actions need not be mutual
commutants, and the correspondence need not be irreducible. Every
correspondence in this paper is separable. Every factor isomorphism is
normal, unital, complex-linear, and *-preserving. For a finite factor
Q and t>0 we write
[
 Q^t=p(M_n(C) ⊗⊗  Q)p,
  (Tr_n ⊗ tau_Q)(p)=t,
]
with the unnormalized matrix trace Tr_n; the corner itself carries its
normalized trace.

For finite-index subgroups AleGamma, BleΛ, a group
isomorphism delta:Ato B, and a nonzero finite-dimensional projective
unitary representation sigma satisfying

 sigma(a)sigma(b)=(mu(a,b))/(omega(delta(a),delta(b)))sigma(ab),
  sigma(e)=I,

define the arithmetic model

 =L^2(M),barbar ⊗⊗ _{L_{mu|_A}(A)}
       (V_sigma ⊗  L^2(N)),
  M=L_mu(Gamma), N=L_omega(Λ).

The right N-action is standard. The left subgroup action on the second
factor is u_a(xi ⊗ eta)=sigma(a)xi ⊗  v_{delta(a)}eta;
Equation~(eq:intro-multiplier) makes this an action with multiplier
mu|_A.

A right action is represented by rho(h)xi=xi v_h^*, so
rho(h)rho(k)=omega(h,k)barrho(hk). The left action is
pi(g)xi=u_gxi. Hence the joint action
U_{(g,h)}xi=u_gxi v_h^* has multiplier

[Arithmetic exhaustion]

[

The theorem keeps both finite sums and closed summands, because a
correspondence may have irreducible pieces in several distinct
arithmetic models at once. The displayed dimension formulas concern the
elementary models themselves. The finer problem of identifying every
summand and every bounded intertwiner is the subject of the companion

existence of a nonzero correspondence forces Gamma and Λ to be
abstractly commensurable. These conclusions cover nonuniform and
reducible lattices and their ICC commensurable groups.

## Rigidity and finite-correspondence precedents

The group-factor reconstruction problem asks which features of a discrete
group survive in its von Neumann algebra. Connes proved countability of
the fundamental group of a property-(T) group factor [cite];
his later problems ask whether nonisomorphic ICC property-(T) groups
have nonisomorphic factors, and ask for the fundamental groups of those
factors cite[Chapter~V, Appendix~B.ε, Problems~1--2]{Connes1994}.
Popa's survey states a stronger recovery question that controls the given
isomorphism, up to a character and inner conjugacy, together with its
amplification scale cite[Section~3, Equations~(3.2) and~(3.2')]{Popa2007}.

Property (T) alone does not ensure group reconstruction: independent,
concurrent constructions of OpenAI and Zhou give nonisomorphic ICC
property-(T) groups with isomorphic group factors
cite[Theorem~1.2]{OpenAI2026ConnesCounterexample}
cite[Theorem~A]{Zhou2026Counterexamples}. The present theorem concerns
the lattice class of Definition~def:class. Arithmetic exhaustion is
the finite-correspondence input used in the companion article to obtain
stable canonical recovery for this class.

Deformation and rigidity methods have produced strong reconstruction
theorems in several settings. Ioana's theorem recovers Bernoulli actions
of ICC property-(T) groups from their crossed products
[cite]. Ioana--Popa--Vaes obtained W^*-superrigid groups among
generalized wreath products [cite], and
Chifan--Ioana--Osin--Sun constructed property-(T) examples using
wreath-like products [cite]. These results recover groups or
actions from operator-algebraic data through constructions different from
the lattice geometry developed here.

Finite correspondences retain finite-index relations and their
compositions. The framework is supplied by Jones' index and module
dimensions [cite], the finite-index expectation inequality of
Pimsner--Popa [cite], and Connes' relative tensor product
cite[Chapter~V, Appendix~B]{Connes1994}. Vaes' classification for
generalized Bernoulli factors already organizes finite-index bimodules by
subgroup isomorphisms and finite-dimensional projective representations
of cocycle ratios, and computes their fusion by projective induction
cite[Theorem~2.2 and Section~6.2]{Vaes2008}. Donvil--Vaes prove rigidity
under arbitrary scalar twists and virtual isomorphism for certain
left-right wreath products, including the projective-ratio constraint and
reconstruction, up to amplification, of finite-index factor neighbours
cite[Theorems~A and~6.6]{DonvilVaes2025}.
Chifan--Fern'andez Quero--Osin--Tan prove stable canonical recovery for
twists of certain property-(T) wreath-like products when the source
cocycle is trivial on the abelian base cite[Theorem~F]{CFOT2026}. The
exhaustion theorem proved here treats arbitrary source cocycles in the
local-field lattice class.

A geometric line of operator-algebraic rigidity for lattices begins with
character and finite-factor representation results. Bekka classified the
characters of SL_n(Z), nge3 cite[Theorem~3]{Bekka2007};
Peterson established a finite-factor representation dichotomy for
irreducible higher-rank property-(T) lattices
cite[Theorem~A]{Peterson2014}. Boutonnet--Houdayer developed stationary
and noncommutative boundary methods that recover character rigidity
cite[Theorems~A--C]{BoutonnetHoudayer2021}. Their noncommutative factor
theorem subsequently classified intermediate von Neumann algebras of
specified lattice boundary crossed-product inclusions by parabolic
quotients cite[Theorem~B and Theorem~3.3]{BoutonnetHoudayer2023}.
Related results recover Weyl groups from the distinguished inclusion
associated with the action on the quotient by a split-torus centralizer
cite[Theorem~A and Corollary~B]{HI2026}, and classify intermediate
C^*-algebras for boundary crossed products of irreducible lattices in
products of real rank-one groups cite[Corollary~B]{HoudayerLeBars2026}.
These results show how boundary data retains the ambient geometry. For
the finite correspondence considered here, compatible boundary
measurements must be constructed in several module copies; their
simultaneous law then produces finite-rank subgroup-graph fibres.

The search for a classical equivariant map from an operator-valued
coupling is motivated in part by the self-coupling strategy in Furman's
measure-equivalence rigidity theorem
cite[Sections~3--4]{Furman1999}. The noncommutative classicalization and
finite-module reconstruction used below are proved here; they are not
consequences of that classical theorem.

## From boundary measurements to subgroup graphs

The proof first passes to an actual lattice and removes the compact
projective sectors of the finite commutant. The remaining conjugation
action is weakly mixing. In this reduced setting the goal is a fixed
orthogonal partition of the correspondence K into finite-dimensional
subspaces permuted by both endpoint groups, with free actions and finitely
many orbits. A joint stabilizer of one subspace is then the graph of an
actual finite-index subgroup isomorphism, and its action on that subspace
is the projective fibre in Equation~(eq:intro-multiplier).

The geometric construction produces this partition through boundary
measurements. Let B be the compact full flag space of the known ambient
group G. A projection-valued measure (PVM) assigns an orthogonal
projection to each Borel set, with countable additivity in the strong
operator topology; disjoint sets give orthogonal projections and the whole
space gives the identity. An angular measurement P_x on B records the
possible image of a source flag xin B: P_x(A) is the projection for
its image to lie in A⊆ B. Initially the construction supplies
positive operator-valued observations. Showing that they are PVMs, and
that observations at different labels commute, are separate steps.

To compare two source copies, use K_0 ⊗  K_1, with the left
Gamma-action in each slot and the right Λ-action acting
diagonally. The eventual joint angular law is a PVM Θ on the space
of nonsingular, essentially invertible maps Bto B. The two left actions
transport its source and target labels; diagonal rho(Λ) fixes
it. The same construction on K_0 ⊗  K_1 ⊗  K_2 compares slot
zero separately with slots one and two, and the required conclusion
includes
[

This common-slot commutation has a concrete purpose. Normal slices in the
separate branch slots generate an abelian algebra on K_0. Disintegrating
over it gives boundary-map PVMs on the fixed space K_1. Finite module
dimension makes their orbit transversals finite rank; weak mixing makes
the resulting projection partition independent of the disintegration
parameter. The boundary-map labels themselves need not become
deterministic. Section~sec:recovery proves this passage and the
subsequent graph-stabilizer identification.

Both copies of B in this construction belong to the source lattice. No
flag space for the unknown group Λ is assumed. To read the known
geometry from Fourier coefficients, omit one left action and put the
remaining actions, together with diagonal rho, in a finite regular
column. Inducing the omitted action to G/Gamma gives a finite tracial
algebra even when the lattice is reducible. Escape in all ambient factors
then creates an isometric boundary link. Escape in a subproduct instead
gives a pairing through the fixed algebra's conditional expectation.
Ordered Fourier transfer and incidence relations make the positive
observations sharp and identify exact residue graphs, while unrelated
measurements still retain their original order.

Three local mechanisms control the only remaining Cartan gaps.

At finite places, compact-open fixed algebras and a uniform subgroup
logarithm argument recover homogeneous residue maps together with upper
speed bounds (Section~sec:finite).

Real panels inside higher-rank components use optimal finite-dimensional
modules: trace inequalities force a nonzero scalar witness in their
section spaces, and positivity of its algebraic action excludes excess
speed (Section~sec:panels).

Isolated quaternionic and Cayley factors use uniform ray estimates and a
finite-module spectral gap to obtain positive mass in linear windows on
fixed vectors (Sections~sec:ray--sec:scale). Pair packing
then gives horizontal derivatives, whose closed module core produces an
injective positive height with its own-label covariance. The Carnot
boundary geometry belongs to the work of Cowling--Dooley--Kor'anyi--Ricci
and Pansu [cite]; the ray and finite-module
estimates used here are proved in Sections~sec:ray--sec:scale.

Full tuples bring these branches together. Comparing lattice packing with
Haar growth calibrates the speeds around the finite type permutation and
fixes the exact Jacobians. Ordered imaginary powers then produce a
Haar-height isometry. Its common graph core permits locality arguments for
the unbounded heights. Locality gives finite jets for the actual spectral
control measure, which may be singular; commuting cone multipliers
eliminate every positive-order symbol. The principle is related to
Peetre's locality theorem [cite], but the measured
operator-domain argument is proved here (Section~sec:locality).
Applying it also to the two shared-slot copies gives the isolated-type
commutation needed for the joint law.

For higher-component panels, pairing powers and exact residue graphs
supply the corresponding commutation. Simultaneous reflections and panel
galleries then glue all local types into the nonsingular, essentially
invertible chamber maps described above (Section~sec:gluing). After
extracting the fixed finite-rank partition and its graph fibres, we
restore every compact sector with its exact projective multiplier.
Two-sided finite-index induction returns the original correspondence as a
closed summand. This explains both the matched cocycle ratio and the
summand qualification in Theorem~thm:arithmetic-exhaustion
(Section~sec:recovery).

# Finite-module realizations and local-field geometry


The first analytic goal is a finite regular realization of each choice of
an omitted left action on the tensor copies of the correspondence. Each
realization includes the other left actions and the diagonal right
action. These realizations supply the finite traces from which the
boundary measurements of Section~sec:links are built. We first
remove the compact part of one conjugation action, then form the tensor
copies and the induced transports for the omitted source action.
Conjugation by these transports gives a genuine action of its ambient
group on the finite algebra. The remaining subsections describe the
geometric data to be read from the source-lattice Fourier indices.

Fix a nonzero bifinite L_mu(Gamma)--L_omega(Λ)
correspondence K as in Theorem~thm:arithmetic-exhaustion. A right
action of L_omega(Λ) is written as the projective representation
[
 rho(h)xi=xi v_h^*,
 rho(h)rho(k)=omega(h,k)barrho(hk).
]
The left representation is pi(g)xi=u_gxi. Thus pi and rho
commute, although their multipliers need not be trivial. Conjugation by
either representation is an ordinary group action. All Hilbert spaces and
von Neumann algebras below are separable in the appropriate sense for
their normal representations.

[Twisted regular absorption]

Proof.

[

 =alpha(h,g)nu(h,g)delta_{hg} ⊗ xi.

Proof.

restrictions to N^{op} are finite by right finiteness of K.
Hence L^2(P) has finite N^{op}-dimension, which is the index
assertion [cite].

precompact in L^2(P). Compact vectors of a unitary representation form
its closed almost-periodic subspace. Bounded compact elements are closed
under adjoints and products, since on uniformly bounded sets

]
and their bounded balls are strongly closed because bounded strong

nonexpansive. Thus D_c is a von Neumann subalgebra.

Twisted conjugation on L^2(N)ominusC1 has no nonzero
finite-dimensional subrepresentation: the diagonal of a finite-rank
invariant projection in the Fourier basis is constant on conjugacy

summable, and every nonidentity class is infinite, so only the identity
vector remains. The trace-preserving expectation

projection qin D_c has tr(q)geε. A finite
algebra with this lower bound has only finitely many orthogonal nonzero
projections, and is finite-dimensional.

## The source groups and full versus partial mixing

The induced algebra has only scalar whole-group invariants; to turn that
into limits along Cartan paths we need the mixing theorem for the actual
root-generated groups. We use the standard parabolic and root-group
structure, Cartan--Iwasawa decompositions, central-cover facts,
root-generated simplicity, and highest-weight theory
[cite].

[Factors and panel groups]

At a complex place the local group is regarded as a real Lie group. The
noncompact property-(T) archimedean factors are the higher-rank ones and
the groups locally isomorphic to Sp(n,1), nge2, or F_{4(-20)}. At
a finite place the factors have relative rank at least two. For
higher-rank property (T) see cite[Theorem~6]{DelarocheKirillov1968};
the real rank-one classification is in cite[Section~3.3 and
Theorem~3.5.4]{BHV2008}. Relative rank one at a finite place gives an
unbounded action on the Bruhat--Tits tree and is incompatible with
property (T) cite[Remark~1.6.3]{BHV2008}.

In a rank-one semisimple Levi position exactly one almost simple factor is
isotropic. Its minimal boundary is the full panel residue. The compact and
toric normal factors of the Levi act trivially on this boundary. Source
transports use its root-generated group. When an algebraic central cover
is needed, use the subgroup generated by its lifted unipotent radicals.
The root-group isomorphisms below map this subgroup onto the effective
root-generated group with finite central kernel. Every application of
Howe--Moore takes invariants for this root-generated group or lift.
Projectivities use the effective adjoint group; the adjoint point group is
allowed for changes of frame. We never replace a full flag by an oriented
or topological covering flag.


For the cover assertion, a central covering induces an isomorphism on the
corresponding relative root groups, so its image contains the subgroup
generated by the unipotent radicals cite[Theorem~2.20(iii) and
Proposition~2.24(ii)]{BT1972Complements}. For a central kernel C, the
rational-point quotient injects into H^1(k,C), which is finite over the
present local fields cite[Propositions~1.12 and~1.17, Theorem~6.1]{BorelSerre1964}.
This proves the finite-index assertion directly.

## Panel groups, Cartan coordinates, and Haar volumes

At a panel only one noncompact rank-one Levi factor acts on the residue;
on a Cartan path exact root coordinates will determine which flag
coordinates survive.

An archimedean panel inside a higher-rank component cannot have noncompact
Lie algebra mathfrak f_frakf_{4(-20)}: its complexification would have to
occur as a component of a proper absolute Dynkin subdiagram, and the
Dynkin list has no such occurrence. At a finite place the effective
rank-one Lie algebra is simple also over Q_p:bbQ_p: after restriction
of scalars its absolutely simple factors form one Galois orbit, so a
Q_p-idealbbQ_p-ideal is either zero or the whole algebra. An adjoint
automorphism centralizing an open subgroup centralizes all sufficiently
small inner exponentials, hence this Lie algebra, and is the identity.
These assertions concern the ambient local groups, not a discreteness
assumption on a lattice projection.

Let Pi be the union of the simple indivisible relative roots of
the factors. A face of type IsubsetPi has strictly positive simple
coordinates exactly in I; opposition of types is denoted by a star.
Choose a special maximal compact in each factor. Cartan and Iwasawa
decompositions can be made on the simply connected covers and descended.
Write mu(g) for the Cartan vector only when its argument is a
local-group element; this is distinct from the scalar two-cocycle
mu(g,h). For a positive root alpha put
[
 m_alpha=dim_{k}mathfrak g_frakg_alpha at a finite place,

and use the analogous real restricted-root multiplicities at archimedean

[Exact finite-place translation coordinates]

At a finite place use the module absolute value with |varpi|=q^{-1}.
The Cartan vector is the actual dominant translation vector of a Cartan
representative in the split centralizer: its pairing with every
centralizer character is the exact logarithm of that character's module.
We do not replace this by a coarsely equivalent length in identities
involving heights. All displacements and offsets along e_i range on a
sufficiently divisible mesh in this logarithmic translation lattice.
Cocharacter rationality and common multiples of relative coroot
translations and central isogenies ensure these elements and their
semisimple projections to every standard Levi have lifts in the
corresponding root-generated covering groups. There are only finitely
many standard Levis, so one divisible mesh suffices. At real places the
corresponding split exponentials lie in the generated identity lift. No
argument at a finite place requires every real parameter to be an actual
split displacement.


[Haar size of Cartan shells]

For a bounded-thickness shell about a dominant vector L, its Haar
measure is at most Cexp(2rho(L)). For one fixed sufficiently large
thickness there is a lower bound cexp(2rho(L)). The constants are
independent of L on the permissible Cartan mesh. The analogous
assertions hold for the finite product, with the sum of the factors' 2rho.


Proof. 
At a real place use the Cartan density, a product of the appropriate
powers of sinhalpha(L). The upper bound follows on every fixed bounded
neighborhood. For the lower bound, even when L lies on a wall, choose
inside the shell one fixed bounded offset box whose simple coordinates are
bounded away from zero; on this box every positive-root factor is bounded
below by a fixed multiple of its exponential, and the box has fixed
positive Euclidean volume.

At a finite place the Cartan double coset has measure
[K:Kcap aKa^{-1}] times the fixed compact measure, comparable to
exp(2rho(L)). In compact root charts a sufficiently small analytic
compact open subgroup has product coordinates in negative-root,
centralizer, and positive-root directions; under conjugation by the

parabolic product uniqueness give this calculation; changing the compact

Cartan representatives are within bounded distance of the chosen split

## Compactified matrices and admissible scalar tests

For each relative vertex type choose a highest-line representation: take
the determinant line of the relevant parabolic nilradical in an exterior
power and the cyclic submodule it generates. Over a splitting field choose
a compatible positive system. This line is a highest line, with weight
strictly positive on the omitted absolute nodes and zero on the
complementary coroots. Its relative weight is a positive multiple of the
relevant fundamental weight. The cyclic highest-weight module is absolutely
irreducible with one-dimensional top split space. Lowering along an omitted
absolute simple root separates a weight by a positive multiple of the
corresponding relative simple root; every other weight drops by a
nonnegative combination involving that root. Consequently a normalized
Cartan matrix has rank-one limit exactly when that simple gap diverges.

At a finite place these are k-rational modules, used only to form compact
projective matrix data: use compact-invariant lattice norms and compact
sup-norm frames, recording both the matrix and its inverse up to projective
normalization. At an archimedean target, for Hilbert highest-line
amplifications use a real proximal module irreducible on complexification
with a compact-invariant inner product; higher tensor weights are allowed.
No Hilbert space is tensored with a vector space over a nonarchimedean
field.

[Face blocks and residual projectivities]

Let a sequence have diverging simple gaps of types D and keep its
complementary gaps in a bounded window. Its compactified matrix data
determine an image D-flag, a reverse D^*-flag, and an invertible
residual Levi transport between their transverse refinement spaces,
independent of compact frame choices. On a deeper stratum the already
determined image vertices persist. On compact transverse windows the
residual rank-one panel lengths differ by bounded amounts from the actual
omitted Cartan gaps.


Proof. 
Pass to convergent compact Cartan frames. In each highest-weight module
precisely the weights of maximal value on the open face survive projective
normalization; they are the submodule generated from the highest line by
the complementary Levi. Bounded complementary gaps keep the surviving
block and its inverse bounded in those frames, so the block acts by an
invertible Levi transformation. The same analysis of the inverse matrix
identifies the reverse face.

Equivalently, write an ordinary flag in the open Bruhat cell transverse to
the reverse face. Conjugating its radical coordinates by the Cartan
translation contracts the coordinates of the diverging gaps, leaving the
complementary Levi coordinates. Project to the reverse refinement space,
then apply the surviving Levi transformation: this is the intrinsic
residual projectivity. A change of compact frame conjugates these
coordinates by the corresponding compact Levi projectivity and leaves the
intrinsic map unchanged. Passing to a deeper stratum preserves all
preceding image vertices. In a face omitting one type in a higher-rank
component the surviving rank-one split displacement is exactly that
omitted coordinate; bounded frames change its length by a uniformly
bounded amount. The argument works simultaneously for one omitted type in
several components.


Absolute growth on a highest line is measured with the compact-equivariant
norm; relative to the exact Cartan exponent, its correction is the test on
the input flag direction. On a transverse cell the normalized ratio
converges to the corresponding absolute pairing. At a finite place the
character valuations of the nonsplit centralizer give the same statement
with Convention~conv:setup-exact-lengths.

[Smooth and clopen symbols]

On real coordinate windows, a smooth symbol has the specified uniformly
bounded finite number of derivatives, increased whenever a separated
expansion is needed. On finite-place coordinate boxes its scalar-test
counterpart is locally constant at a uniformly fixed clopen level. The
underlying coordinate maps remain analytic. Uniformity means normalized
denominators are bounded away from zero on a buffered larger window.

the apartment and depends on y with a uniform Lipschitz constant. The
same holds for variation of x, after opposition and replacement of the
fractions by their complements. Constants for a finite product depend only
on its root systems.

## Compact matrix data and the surviving flags

Write Pi for the indivisible relative simple roots. A face of type
IsubsetPi has strictly positive simple coordinates exactly in I;
opposition of types is denoted by a star. Partial flags are transverse when
they are in the open relative position of subfaces of opposite chambers;
incidence means containment in one chamber.

[Matrix compactification and residual projectivities]

There is a finite family of projectively normalized matrix coordinates,
including coordinates of the inverse, with the following properties.
[label=(roman*)]
 The set DsubsetPi of diverging simple gaps is detected by rank one
in the fundamental highest-line coordinates. A stratum with gap set D
carries an image D-flag, a reverse D^*-flag, and a residual projectivity
between their Levi refinement spaces. Its closure has only gap sets
containing D.
 On a bounded complementary-gap window the residual blocks are

to a full flag incident with the image face.

multiplication leaves the image flag unchanged; left multiplication
transports it by the usual target action. Reverse data obey the opposite
rule, and residual projectivities change by the corresponding compositions.

spanned by the highest lines of flags incident with the observed face. A
rank-one Levi pencil has the effective rank-one projectivity group and its

projective local-field coordinates at finite places.


Proof.

at finite places use projective ratio charts with a maximal coordinate.
Lemma~lem:setup-face-blocks identifies the surviving Levi blocks,
proves their invertibility on a bounded complementary-gap window, and
describes the intrinsic transverse projection followed by residual
transport. In each fundamental highest-line coordinate, rank one detects
divergence of the corresponding simple gap; the finitely many coordinates
record the gap set, its image and reverse flags, and residual maps. If an
additional gap diverges the previous image vertices persist by that lemma.

For the multiplication rule, both a and a^{-1} have finite norm; thus
projectively normalized multiplication by a has denominator bounded away
from zero, so fixed multiplication changes each simple Cartan gap by a
bounded amount. Left multiplication carries the image top space by a;
right multiplication leaves it unchanged. The same calculation for inverse
matrices gives the reverse-flag rule. The residual map transforms by the
corresponding compositions.

The last assertion is the spanning and length statement of
Lemma~lem:setup-face-blocks: the Levi orbit of the highest line spans
its surviving top space, and on a panel the only noncompact isotropic
factor supplies the omitted simple gap that compact changes of frame shift
by a bounded amount. At a finite place the modules remain vector spaces
over the local field with no Hilbert-space amplification.


Compact windows in a stratum are exhausted by bounds on its complementary

and lower bounds for the invertible residual blocks. At real places these
tests are smooth functions of matrix coordinates; at finite places they are
locally constant on compact boxes at a fixed level. Increasing a highest

## Fourier transport before compression

Fourier tests here act on full regular arrays; they are not followed by a
corner projection before the next operation. This permits a diagonal
spectral calculus without claiming that an angular Fourier cutoff preserves
the operator norm of a column.

Choose a path a with a^{-1} split toward a source face F of type I,
its coordinates in I tending to infinity. A lexicographic face path
starts with a vertex translation and adds one new simple coordinate at each
successively slower scale; the fastest parameter is taken to its limit first
while slower parameters stay fixed, then the next limit, and so on. A
general face path may have several coordinates tending to infinity at once;
the gap upper bound below applies to both, though the exact successive count
is asserted for vertex-origin chains. Finite-place parameters live on the
divisible cocharacter meshes of Section~sec:setup.

The measured label is the compact matrix data of h^{-1}, where h is the
Fourier index in the chosen copy Gamma_j. Other group indices remain
coefficient indices. On full regular arrays, a scalar test of this label is
a diagonal Hilbert multiplier; these tests are not followed by corner
compression before another array operation.

The one-column map
[

]
is an isometry with the integrated tracial Hilbert norm, so its Hilbert
ultralimit V is an isometry. On the minimal spectral span of VH

[

]
is a positive effect on the original input. For A recording only a gap
stratum and its image flag this is the image measurement; it need not be a

Its output has its own diagonal spectral measure E. The link identities

restrict to the minimal closed spectral span of the input ranges and a
countable determining algebra of continuous tests. A Borel stratum always
denotes a projection of the limiting spectral measure, not a discontinuous
pointwise cutoff before the limit.

[Fourier--Gram transport]

On the limiting arrays, a fixed left or right Fourier shift transports
continuous compact matrix tests by its limiting geometric map, hence
transports the associated Borel spectral projections. For a stratum and
its image flag, a left Fourier matrix multiplier can be removed from a
quadratic form through its uncut Gram factor. This holds for bounded
base-dependent multipliers and for compact families of fixed source
translations reindexed at the end base. All projective defects cancel in
these sesquilinear identities.


Proof. 
For a fixed shift, normalized matrix multiplication and the inverse formula
have denominators bounded away from zero by Lemma~lem:links-matrix-data.
Their action on the compact label space is continuous; a diagonal continuous
test therefore intertwines the shift in the ultralimit, and equality on
continuous functions extends to Borel spectral measures. Base multiplication
and the reindexing commute with diagonal tests.

If a Fourier index h is changed to rh by a left multiplier, the recorded
inverse changes to h^{-1}r^{-1}; the gap set and image flag are unchanged
in the limit. Let D be a bounded test of those data and T a bounded left
Fourier multiplier with uniformly bounded finite Fourier approximants
T_lto T in trace L^2. In a form tested on uncut operator-bounded
columns, replacing either occurrence of T by T_l before moving D
costs at most C|D|,|T-T_l|_2, uniformly in the fixed column bounds
and matrix sizes. For each finite Fourier sum, move D through its
individual shifts by the already proved limiting identity; the remaining
product is the uncut Gram factor T_l^*T_l. Replacing this by T^*T costs
another quantity tending to zero since
|T_l^*T_l-T^*T|_2le(|T_l|_∞+|T|_∞)|T_l-T|_2. All errors
are estimated before applying the cutoff to the columns, so the argument
applies to partial unitaries T^*T=e and assigns no operator bound to Df.

For a field reindexed at ay, invariant probability on Y gives
int|T(ay)-T_l(ay)|_2^2dy=|T-T_l|_2^2, so the same estimates hold after
path transport. For a compact family of fixed translations, the cocycle
formula and continuity of translations in measure make their matrix fields
compact in trace L^2; a finite net gives uniform finite-Fourier
approximation of the compact family. No common finite-Fourier approximation
of the escaping paths themselves is used.

Finally, the group-valued section cocycle satisfies its exact composition
identity. Applying the projective source representation gives a scalar
function of the base in each composition formula; pulling a fixed source
translation from one end of an array to the other therefore multiplies the

entries of a sesquilinear form. The same observation applies to a quotient
path: after base reindexing, X_tX_a^* is the transporter for ta^{-1}
times a common scalar, and its localized Fourier norm is unchanged. Products
in these identities are actual twisted matrix products; their coefficient
phases cannot be replaced by absolute values of untwisted convolutions.


With one measured coordinate, the other group indices, finite matrix

## Additional invariant variables

[Additional invariant limiting variables]

Adjoin to the compact spectral data any bounded limiting tests
asymptotically unchanged by fixed left and right Fourier shifts. The
limiting effects retain base decomposability and the measured equivariance
established above. If a one-column effect is also source face-parabolic
invariant, including its inactive simple factors, and its event or test is
invariant under the measured target shifts, that effect is scalar. For a
mixed two-ended effect, independent target-shift invariance and independent
contracted-radical invariance make its mass scalar on both inputs. These
assertions apply to invariant Borel events of the limiting variables.


Proof. 
A fixed Fourier shift gives precisely the limiting intertwining identity
used in Lemma~lem:links-gram-transport; the finite-Fourier proof is
unchanged. Base decomposability and commutation with companions follow
before limits. An invariant one-column test gives an element of
N; face-parabolic invariance, followed by the active-factor
radical argument and the inactive-factor centralizer argument of
Proposition~prop:links-link, puts it in N^G= C1.bbC1.

For mixed links, independent target invariance puts the respective slices
in Nbar and N; independent
contracted-radical invariance makes them scalar, and polarization and
normal slice separation give the scalar joint effect.

Fixed left and right multiplications change Cartan coordinates by bounded
amounts; a radial variable divided by a scale tending to infinity has
bounded continuous tests in its compactification that are asymptotically
unchanged. The source face-parabolic invariance follows because a
contracted radical preserves the enlarged data law (sliding it to the far
end, the near-identity transporter changes any bounded input by a
Hilbert-small error against bounded diagonal tests) and a centralizer
element slides to the same end, its reindexing removed by invariant
probability and its left multiplier by finite-Fourier Gram. The additional
radial test adds no term because fixed Fourier shifts change its limiting
coordinate by zero. Thus image-and-stratum tests together with these radial
coordinates are face-parabolic invariant, so their target-invariant masses
are scalar even on a pure vertex ray. The argument supplies the required
invariant limiting tests; establish the continuous determining identities
first, and their spectral measures then transport identically.


## Ordering and genuine source actions

Two kinds of spectral operation are used. A single limiting compact
variable has its usual Borel functional calculus. For several possibly
noncommuting compressed effects, an ordered integral initially means a
product of single-slot continuous tests; smooth joint functions on compact
real charts are inserted by absolutely summable separated expansions, and
fixed-level finite-place functions by finite separated partitions. Support
of an ordered product on a closed relation means vanishing on open
rectangles disjoint from it; support alone supplies no general ideal of
discontinuous joint functions.

On a buffered transverse projective box, normalized matrix multiplication
has denominators bounded below. Over a finite local field, choose ratio
charts with a maximal coordinate; matrix operations are equicontinuous, and
a clopen test at a fixed level pulls back to tests at one uniformly bounded
level. Units and projective representatives do not alter this. At real
places the maps have bounded derivatives of each fixed order. The
geometric estimates for variable shifts in transverse products are in
Section~sec:transfer.

[Cancelling source multipliers]

After establishing the decomposable link forms, one may enlarge their
source actions to genuine strongly continuous group representations by an
opposite-multiplier regular factor at each used input base. The compressed
effects become their pointwise normal ampliations. This enlargement
imposes no new mixing or scalar-commutant assertion.


Proof. 
Write the source multiplier as eta. The regular representation with
multiplier overlineeta, induced with the same section, has the conjugate
of the scalar defect of X_a(y)=pi_0(c(a,y)), so the product of the two
fibre transporters satisfies the exact group composition law, and the
section continuity-in-measure argument gives strong continuity of the
resulting induced representation.

The added coordinate is unmeasured. At one base its two transported vectors
have exactly their original inner product, so a base-decomposable form is
tensored pointwise with the identity. For a two-ended form the two base
algebras commute, and the same construction is performed independently at
the two inputs. The generator identification of minimal spectral dilations
gives the enlarged dilation. A face spectral measure invariant under the
projective transport now commutes with a genuine source representation and
can be disintegrated with it. All mixing and scalarity statements continue
to refer to N, not the enlarged commutant.

[No all-bounded stratum]

## Gap counts and vertex quotients

[Gap count and vertex quotients]

In one measured copy, every positive stratum of a source path toward a face
of type I has at most |I| infinite gaps. A vertex path has exactly one
infinite gap. In a successive single-gap lex chain starting at a vertex,
the stage with k source gaps has exactly k target gaps, so each such
extension adds exactly one.

r-|I|; since D has at most r types, |D|+r-|I|le r, proving
|D|le|I|. If I=Pi, this is the automatic ceiling and no
remaining-Levi obstruction is invoked.

For a vertex, Lemma~lem:links-no-bounded-stratum excludes zero gaps
and the upper bound excludes two or more. The same strict-growth argument
applied successively from the vertex gives at least k gaps at stage k,
while the upper bound gives at most k.

Finally choose the common apartment and let the faster vertex be a dominant
fundamental ray v of type j. If the slower vertex is coaxial, the
quotient is again a vertex translation at the faster scale (possibly with
opposed orientation), and the vertex conclusion applies. If its vector w
is not coaxial, the projection to a simple factor of the remaining Levi is
nonzero, since the common kernel of Pi∖{j} is Rv.
Choosing a dominant chamber inside the remaining Levi, the slower quotient
component escapes in a noncompact simple factor with other split parts
commuting; strict growth from the one-gap faster limit forces at least one
additional target gap. All statements retain the faster-first order of
limits.

# Transverse Fourier products and geometric transfer


We continue with the induced finite-module realization of
Section~sec:links. All target indices here are indices of a measured

## A transverse product estimate

For a scalar function f of the measured Fourier index, let D_f denote
diagonal multiplication of the Fourier array; it is an L^2 operator, and
we do not assume it preserves operator-bounded columns. We estimate

 (D_g B_0)(D_f A_0), with index product qp,
 

where A_0,B_0 are operator-bounded with Fourier expansions
A_0=sum_pa_pw_p, B_0=sum_qb_qw_q, operator-valued coefficients in the
unmeasured algebra, p an index of A_0 and q an index of B_0.
Matrix sizes are fixed during every limit; a trace of mass t rescales an
operator-norm-only L^2 bound by sqrt t, but the homogeneous estimate
below uses the actual Hilbert norms. Rectangular matrices are included by
embedding in one fixed square matrix algebra and using the corresponding
corners.

[Transverse products]

Fix face types, a thickness bound, and sufficiently small compact
transverse face patches at the basepoint o. Suppose the supports of f
and g lie within that thickness of the respective positive cones, with
all specified positive gaps sufficiently large, and that the ideal
directions of po and q^{-1}o belong to the fixed transverse patches.
Then mu(qp)=mu(q)+mu(p)+O(1), uniformly on the supports. The product
in (eq:input-2) has a uniform tracial L^2 bound in either of the
following situations:
[label=(roman*)]
 For each coordinate contributed by both cones, one of its two
supports is restricted to a moving interval of a fixed bounded width. The
separate cutoff factors may be arbitrary uniformly bounded functions.
 One of the face types is a single vertex. The cutoff factors are
smooth with uniformly bounded sufficiently high seminorms in fixed compact
normalized matrix charts; at finite places this means locally constant with
a uniformly bounded level.

Write f=h_Af_1, g=h_Bg_1. In case (i), all four factors may be

 |(D_gB_0)(D_fA_0)|_2^2
 le C|A_0|_∞|B_0|_∞
     |D_{h_A}A_0|_2|D_{h_B}B_0|_2.

holds for a joint symbol sum_{q,p}h_B(q)h_A(p)m(q,p)b_qa_pw_qw_p, where
w_qw_p is the actual twisted monomial product and m is a smooth joint

Proof. 
Common apartments and addition. On compact transverse patches, the

ambient product and no discreteness of a proper projection.

We need Lipschitz stability of interpolation: from a fixed endpoint,
multiply the simple coordinates L_i of a dominant vector by t_iin[0,1].

The common shift. Suppose qp=q'p' and k=p(p')^{-1}. With

The fractions are bounded and held fixed while applying the estimate.

In case (i), every overlapping coordinate has bounded variation on at

In case (ii), an unbounded difference can occur only in the single overlap

## Fullness and the permutation of types

For an archimedean target vertex module V, let n(v) be a unit
representative of its highest line; n(v)n(v)^* is independent of the
phase. We also allow the trivial one-dimensional module; at a finite target
place only this trivial-module version is used.

[Fullness of open patches]

Let Phi_z be a nonzero family of image measurements as in
Proposition~prop:links-link, transported over its source label space
and normalized by its positive scalar stratum mass to be unital. For every
nonempty open target patch O, the closed sum, over conull source labels
z, of the ranges of

 int_O n(v)n(v)^* ⊗ Phi_z(dv)
 

is the full amplified input space. For the trivial module this is the
ordinary patch-fullness assertion; the assertion passes to normal copies
in the three-slot system.


Proof. 
Omit O first. Source covariance makes the uncut range join a projection
over (A_1)^; target covariance transports its range by
finite-dimensional linear target matrices, unchanged by positive
renormalization of n(v). Inducing the target lattice action with its
finite invariant trace gives an induced range field intertwining a
trace-isometric ambient action with the inverse linear transport on V.

Let a positive matrix A have weight spaces with strictly increasing
eigenvalues and let P be a projection over a finite algebra. Filtering
ran P by its intersections with the weight filtration and taking the
successive leading projections onto the weight spaces gives, by polar
decomposition, an orthogonal sum Q with tr(Q)=
tr(P) and ran Q strongly approximated by A^nran P.
Since the range projections have constant finite trace, they converge in
trace L^2 to Q. Apply pointwise to the induced field; a convergent
orbit of a trace-isometric action is fixed (the distance between
consecutive orbit points is constant and tends to zero). Invariance under
all positive split matrices forces the range to split off the whole matrix
factor; the remainder descends to the scalar joint fixed algebra. Thus
the uncut join is full.

Restore O: the finite trace dimension of the patch join is constant
along target lattice translates. For any finite scalar test measure on
Z, choose a regular contracting direction with attractor in O and
repeller transverse to almost every point of the measure; all-factor
mixing supplies lattice elements whose two compact frames approximate
the chosen frames along increasingly long translations, so suitable
translates of O exhaust the measure. Applied to a faithful
scalarization of a countable total family, the complements of the
translated patch joins have trace tending to zero, giving patch fullness.
Normal copies follow from normal ampliation in Proposition~prop:links-link.


A normalized image branch of a pure vertex path is a candidate. Its
label is a source vertex, its values are target vertices. Different
candidates may use different paths, limits, and strata. Face-parabolic
invariance makes their transport well defined on the vertex space, and
strong continuity is used only for these constructed families.

[Type comparison]

There is a unique permutation p of the full relative type list Pi
such that every candidate at source type i takes values in target type
p(i); it preserves opposition, p(i^*)=p(i)^*. For noncoaxial source
vertices of dual types, all ordered products of candidates vanish on
transverse target patches. The permutation preserves the collection of
isolated types.


Proof. 
Choose paths t,a whose inverses tend to the two source vertices in a
common apartment. Take successive limits, t faster. Pairing the ordered
cut effects between bounded columns and using trace cyclicity gives a
pairing of (D_AX_tf)(D_BX_af')^* with the quotient-path matrix X_tX_a^*
after base reindexing; the latter is the transporter of ta^{-1} up to a
common scalar, with index product pq^{-1}.

If coaxial, the quotient path has exactly one target gap
(Proposition~prop:links-gap-count). Nondual transverse target types
would force two distinct large gaps. The bounded-overlap version of
Proposition~prop:transfer-transverse bounds the cut-product norm
uniformly at fixed angular patches and thickness, and the localized
quotient matrix norm tends to zero on the forbidden strata by the finite
frame columns; Cauchy--Schwarz makes the pairing zero.

If noncoaxial, the quotient has at least two gaps. Dual transverse target
vertex cones put the cut product in a fixed thickened single-vertex cone;
the smooth version of Proposition~prop:transfer-transverse with
fixed smooth cuts again compares with a forbidden localized quotient norm
and gives zero.

Fullness gives nonzero ordered cut product for generic source pairs
(apply Lemma~lem:transfer-fullness successively). Generic dual
source vertices are opposite, hence coaxial; generic nondual source types
are noncoaxial. Thus candidates at dual source types have dual target
types, and at nondual source types nondual target types. If T_i is the
possible target type set at source i, every jin T_i and kin
T_{i^*} satisfy k=j^*. Since the T_i are nonempty, each is a
singleton, and the resulting map is injective and hence a permutation.

At higher-rank self-opposite source types, extend nonopposition by
continuity: if the image type were isolated, nonopposition would mean
equality; diagonal support of two unital effects would make them equal,
and gallery connectivity would make the candidate family constant, giving
a source-invariant target-equivariant flag probability against contraction
(Lemma~lem:transfer-fullness). Thus self-opposite higher-rank types
do not map to isolated types; non-self-opposite ones do not either by
opposition preservation. Since p is a permutation, it permutes the
isolated types.

## Sharp flags and Coxeter compatibility

[Propagation of incidence words]

Consider a finite word of operations, each replacing either one of a pair
of vertices by an incident vertex of a prescribed type, and ending with
dual types. The word is successful if some sequence of choices ends at
opposite vertices. Success is increasing in the initial Bruhat position.
If a word fails at a source pair, the ordered candidate product at that
pair is supported on target pairs for which the permuted word fails. The
sets of words successful at the least position are unchanged by the type
permutation p.


Proof.

(Proposition~prop:transfer-types); at equal self-opposite vertices
it follows by continuity. Suppose all incident replacements of one or both

# Finite-place panels: fixed algebras and uniform upper speed


Take a mixed lexicographic face path missing one type in a non-isolated
component, or one type in each of several. Write B_S,B_T for the source
and target panel boundaries. Let S be the effective root-generated

over finite extensions of a prime local field. The prime-field Lie algebra
of T is simple, S is simple after removal of its ineffective center,
and T embeds faithfully in the automorphism group of its prime-field Lie

# Length multipliers and ray lifts


This section supplies the length estimate needed for the isolated boundary
factors: uniform ray lifts (Proposition~prop:ray-lift) combined with
property~(T) averaging give addition of rescaled spherical length laws
(Proposition~prop:ray-addition). Addition lets us later compare
repeated source segments and rule out total superlinear growth.

The isolated components are real Lie factors (Sections~sec:transfer
and~sec:finite): quaternionic hyperbolic spaces of dimension at least
two, and the Cayley hyperbolic plane. Fix such a space X; normalize its
metric so r(g)=d(o,go) is the logarithm of the dilation in the
indivisible restricted root. The argument uses an entire ambient factor;
a rank-one residue in a larger factor does not supply this axis. All other
measured coordinates, including all finite places, stay in the coefficient
algebra.

Constants may depend on X and the specified smooth functions but not on
the number of Fourier indices or coefficient-algebra dimensions. The
analytic estimates are stated homogeneously in operator and Hilbert norms,
allowing arbitrary finite coefficient traces. Varying finite factors for
spherical laws have normalized trace; the quotient base has probability
measure.

# Linear subsequences and heights for isolated components


At an isolated type, Section~sec:transfer has already supplied
sharp boundary measurements x|-> P_x. We now construct a height
at each source label: a positive injective self-adjoint operator
commuting with P_x and transforming by the inverse boundary
Jacobians. The construction takes an inverse power of the modulus of a
horizontal derivative of x|-> P_x; the main analytic requirement
is a square-integrable derivative on a dense invariant domain.

The first part controls Fourier lengths: along long source intervals,
every fixed input retains positive mass below a linear length bound.
The addition theorem (Proposition~prop:ray-addition) plus
property~(T) rules out total superlinear growth. Lattice packing then
costs one factor of the length, canceled by averaging over a source
stabilizer interval of comparable length. The resulting estimate for
ordered products at opposite source labels controls small horizontal
differences; we then build the closed derivative modulus and prove its
kernel is zero.

The source ray lies in an entire simple factor. Isolated factors are
real and locally Sp(n,1), nge2, or F_{4(-20)}. Complementary
factors may be archimedean or nonarchimedean; a compact window in those
coordinates is retained in each lattice count. Horizontal metrics and
angular probabilities are the compatible compact-invariant choices of
Section~sec:ray.

We use the admissibility and statistical conventions of
Definition~def:ray-admissible. All varying finite factor traces
are normalized, Y=G/Gamma has probability measure, and extra finite
matrices carry the unnormalized matrix trace with fixed size. Each
application of the ray estimates has both the stated operator bounds
and bounded integrated Hilbert norms; a lower effect bound concerns a
fixed Hilbert vector, not a bound uniform over a moving unit ball.

The earlier geometric conclusions used: complementary regular
realizations and all-factor mixing
(Lemma~lem:setup-normal-tensors, Proposition~prop:setup-induced-algebras,
Lemma~lem:setup-product-mixing); full-gap escape on complete
vertex-origin lexicographic chains (Proposition~prop:links-gap-count);
sharp, strongly continuous pure-vertex measurements and their
covariances from Propositions~prop:transfer-types,
prop:transfer-isolated-sharpness, and~prop:transfer-sharp-flags;
scalarity of limiting rescaled one-column effects
(Lemma~lem:links-invariant-variables). The compact-change argument
for those effects uses bounded approximants of the fixed transporters
and has arbitrary fixed slack in radial windows. These are conclusions,
not new assumptions.

# Upper speeds at real panels


This section treats a non-isolated source position and its matched target
position when both places are archimedean. The finite-place argument is
in Section~sec:finite; the isolated-axis differentiation of
Section~sec:scale is not used here.

## The estimate and the order of limits

Fix one measured coordinate of the exact graph relation
(eq:input-6), possibly one of the two measured coordinates of a
two-leg link. Begin with a mixed many-panel lexicographic face path that
selects every isolated gap and omits one gap in each higher-rank
component. Let S and T be the noncompact simple rank-one Levi groups
of an omitted source position and its matched target position; compact
and ineffective factors are discarded. Source actions may be lifted to
the simply connected cover. Write e_i for the coordinate vector dual
to the omitted simple root, so translation by te_i has simple gap t.

[The real-panel upper estimate]
There are positive constants d_R, determined by each real rank-one
panel type R and unchanged at the opposite type, with the following
property. Complete the mixed path by split-chamber translations along
its missing coordinates, taking their times to infinity successively
and more slowly than the original path. Additional slower translations
along already selected isolated coordinates are allowed. If t is the
completing simple gap at the selected panel and ell(g) the residual
target Cartan gap, then

 d_Tell(g)le d_St+O_{tight}(1).
 

Here tight upper error means the event where
d_Tell(g)-d_St tends to positive infinity has zero mass in the
iterated limit, on every fixed input. One may add offsets fixed during
preceding limits and then take further limits in those offsets; t in
(eq:input-11) is the resulting shifted gap. Gap signs are the
eventual orientations on the chosen filters.


The angular information used during completion: at the first, mixed
limit the residual missing gap is finite and its Cartan gap differs by
a bounded quantity from the corresponding inverse Fourier-index gap. A
completing translation fixed at this first limit is an exact input
precomposition of the link; it retains all the sharp angular marginals.
At each subsequent limit the growth and upper vertex-count of
Section~sec:transfer applies to the enlarged path. If a previously
selected gap is repeated, maximal vertex count on that face is already
attained, so the repetition adds no additional gap. In the stated order
of limits the total split parameters still contract the relevant face
radicals. Thus every resulting link is mixed and isometric, has its
full sharp marginals, and satisfies (eq:input-6) with the
indicated input reindexing. The same reasoning applies to outer
translations in the common split torus after a full path, including the
offsets in Proposition~prop:panels-upper. These assertions use
only the exact precomposition and angular statements of
Sections~sec:links and~sec:transfer; neither the height
calibration nor the classicality conclusion proved later is used.

The structural classification of Section~sec:setup leaves the
orthogonal, unitary, and symplectic groups as the real rank-one panel
groups. The exceptional rank-one group F_{4(-20)} cannot occur as the
noncompact simple factor of a panel Levi in a higher-rank component:
its complexification would be a component of a proper absolute Dynkin
subdiagram, whereas no proper subdiagram of an irreducible Dynkin
diagram has a component of type F_4. Equivalently, in the real
restricted-root list a multipliable simple root arising in such a Levi
has double-root multiplicity 1 or 3, and not 7. The reduced
low-rank isomorphisms lie on the orthogonal line.

## Optimal modules for a real rank-one panel

Let R be a noncompact rank-one panel factor with split generator
H_R, normalized so the simple restricted gap is one. For a
finite-dimensional complex module V extended to the complexification,
write
[
 V_t=ker(dpi(H_R)-t),
 h(V)=sum_{t>0}dim V_t, z(V)=dim V_0.
]
For a nontrivial irreducible V, dim V/h(V) is the inverse
proportion of positive-weight vectors. We extract finite-dimensional
source representations from the measured graph relation and compare
their stage dimensions with those of a target module: a sharp ratio
and a uniform gap away from the maximizers bound the dimension lost
when retaining only maximizing source summands, whose largest split
weights then bound source growth. These are the different roles of the
constants c_R and d_R.

We allow representations of the simply connected covering group, so
the weight lattices below are full and spin representations are
included. Isomorphic rank-one groups are named only once: SU(1,1)
uses the SO(2,1) line and Sp(1,1) uses the SO(4,1) line, with the
reduced restricted root in both cases.

## Algebraic stages and compact graph operators

Choose a target maximizing module V with largest split weight d_T,
n_0=dim V, and (if a compact diagram symmetry interchanges
inequivalent modules) replace V by the direct sum of its diagram
conjugates; every summand has the same optimal ratio, so still

 h(V)=n_0/c_T.
 

Take the compact-form Hilbert norms and preserved Hermitian forms of
Lemma~lem:panels-optimal-modules, with form symmetry Q, and in
compact projectivity frames choose local or Borel lifts pi_T(g) of
target projectivities such that

 pi_T(g)^*Qpi_T(g)=Q,
 |pi_T(g)|=exp(d_Tell(g)).
 

The connected action lifts to the simply connected module; compact
Cartan factors act unitarily, compact diagram factors permute the
chosen summands, and the invariant forms agree: on each irreducible
they are unique up to a real scalar, and positivity on the nonempty
zero-weight space fixes the sign. Fix Borel lifts for the coefficient
calculations; changing a covering lift acts by a modulus-one scalar on
each summand and so preserves the weight planes, form and norm
identities, and limiting top split-weight projector. The calculations
use fixed lifts and do not require a multiplication law for them.
