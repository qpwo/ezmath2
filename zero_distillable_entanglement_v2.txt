# Entanglement with zero distillable secret key in local dimension ten



maketitle

Abstract: 
We construct an entangled density operator (rho) on (C^{10} ⊗ C^{10})
whose range contains no nonzero product vector and whose distillable
secret key is zero. More precisely: for every integer (nge1) and every
admitted protocol on (rho^{ ⊗  n}), the unhalved trace-norm distance
from any completed bit output to an ideal secret bit (with arbitrary
eavesdropper state) is at least (1/5). The construction disproves the
unrestricted two-map PPT-composition conjecture of Christandl,
M"uller-Hermes, and Wolf. A separate trace-preserving PPT channel on
(Mat{21}) has a square that is not entanglement breaking.


# What we prove, and why it is surprising


Fix two finite-dimensional complex Hilbert spaces (C^a) and (C^b).
A density operator (rho) on (C^a ⊗ C^b) is a positive
semidefinite matrix of trace one. It is separable if it is a
finite convex combination of positive product matrices, and
entangled otherwise.

We consider two-party protocols with one-dimensional quantum
instruments, private ancillas, private randomness, and authenticated
two-way classical communication. The input is the only shared private
resource. An eavesdropper Eve starts with a purification of the input and
sees the complete public transcript. Distillable secret key
(K_D(rho)) is defined as the supremum of bits-per-copy rates that
these protocols can extract, with zero asymptotically vanishing error.
Section~sec:transcript gives the precise operational model.

The sentence we are trying to falsify has been current since
2005~[cite]:


Every entangled state has positive distillable secret key.


The paper produces an explicit counterexample.

[Entangled state with zero secret key]

There is an explicitly specified entangled density operator (rho) on
(C^{10} ⊗ C^{10}) whose range contains no nonzero product vector
and for which (K_D(rho)=0). More precisely: for every integer (nge1)
and every admitted protocol on (rho^{ ⊗  n}) that produces a
completed bit output (tau_{K_AK_BE'}) with (E') retaining an initial
purification and the full public transcript, we have, for every
eavesdropper state (sigma_{E'}),

norm{
tau_{K_AK_BE'}-frac12sum_{i=0}^1 |i,i>< i,i| ⊗ sigma_{E'}
}_1gefrac15.

Thus the traditional trace distance to an ideal bit is at least (1/10).


The bound (1/5) is uniform in (n), in the number of rounds, in the
protocol itself, and in the eavesdropper's purification. Proposition~%
prop:operational-extensions presents three natural extensions:
conditioning on a positive-probability transcript event, vanishing abort
probabilities, and trace-norm limits of completed actual outputs whose
Eve registers retain the full information.

The state is derived from maps. A linear map (F:Mat atoMat b) is
completely positive (CP) if (id_k ⊗  F) is
positive for every (kge1). It is PPT if both (F) and
(T_bcirc F) are CP, where (T_d(X)=X^{T}) is the (complex-linear)
transpose on (Mat d). A CP map (F) is entanglement breaking
(EB) if applying it to one subsystem of any positive bipartite input
always outputs a separable state. Equivalently, its
Choi matrix is separable~[cite]. Our second main result is a
counterexample to composition.

[Two PPT maps whose composition is not EB]

There are explicitly specified PPT maps
(Phi_1,Phi_2:Mat{10}toMat{10}) such that (Z=J(Phi_2circPhi_1))
is nonzero and its range contains no nonzero product vector. Hence
(Phi_2circPhi_1) is not entanglement breaking.


The maps (Phi_1,Phi_2) come from a (6times4) real integer matrix
pencil (M(x)=x_0M_0+x_1M_1+x_2M_2+x_3M_3). Section~%
sec:certificate constructs them; Section~sec:geometry
proves the tensor-criterion that turns them into Theorem~%
thm:main-pair. Theorem~thm:main-state is then the
corresponding statement for the density operator
(rho=Z/tr Z).

A trace-preserving channel version of the same phenomenon is stated next.

[Trace-preserving PPT channel whose square is not EB]

There is an explicitly specified trace-preserving PPT channel
(Θ:Mat{21}toMat{21}) whose square (ΘcircΘ) is not
entanglement breaking.


The dimension (21) is (2·10+1): the pair from Theorem~%
thm:main-pair is embedded, rescaled, and completed by an absorbing
flag, following Christandl--M"uller-Hermes--Wolf~[cite] and
Filippov~[cite]. Section~sec:applications presents the
details.

# Foundations: maps, PPT, and the represented class


All matrix algebras are finite-dimensional over (C), with fixed
orthonormal bases and product bases. We write (X^*) for conjugate
transpose, (overlineoverline XX) for entrywise conjugation,
(T_d(X)=X^{T}) for the complex-linear transpose on (Mat d). For a
linear map (F), its Hilbert--Schmidt adjoint (F^dagger) satisfies
(tr(Y^*F(X))=tr((F^dagger(Y))^*X)). If (V) is a matrix, we write
(Ad_V(X)=VXV^*).

Our Choi matrix convention is

J(F)=sum_{i,j=1}^a E_{ij} ⊗  F(E_{ij})
 =(id_a ⊗  F)(Ω_aΩ_a^*),
  Ω_a=sum_{i=1}^a e_i ⊗  e_i.

So (norm{Ω_a}^2=a), and the first tensor factor is the input
reference. This fixes every dimensional factor in the transfer formula
and in every channel corollary. It is the map--operator correspondence
of Jamiol{}kowski and Choi~[cite].

# A four-effect coherence inequality


The first step of the operational obstruction is a purely algebraic
inequality about any state in (C and four arbitrary positive
local matrices. It compares the "coherence" between two equal-bit
branches with the weights of branches in which the bits disagree. No
pointwise normalization is assumed: later the matrices will be densities
at a fixed public transcript, where pointwise normalization is not
required.

Proof.

sum of outer products of block columns (binom{a_k}{b_k}). So
(H=sum_k a_ka_k^*), (J=sum_k b_kb_k^*), (Z=sum_k a_kb_k^*).
Since a rank-one (ab^*) has trace norm (norm{a}norm{b}), triangle
and C-S give
(norm{Z}_1lesum_knorm{a_k}norm{b_k}
le(sum_knorm{a_k}^2)^{1/2}(sum_knorm{b_k}^2)^{1/2}

# Complete public transcripts


At each public transcript, a protocol induces the same two pairs of
positive local matrices on Alice's and Bob's input spaces, for all four
possible bit values. These matrices are not just the final measurements;
they are the aggregated effects of the whole protocol on its input. This
section proves that this factorization holds at measurable transcripts
of the admitted protocol model, using a probe experiment that turns
classical independence conditional on the transcript into matrix-valued
product structure.

## Admitted protocol model

The general statement replaces the preceding matrices by densities with
respect to a single transcript measure, and covers continuous transcript
spaces and infinite public schedules. The proof runs the protocol on a
product of two local probes with untouched references, uses conditional
independence of the two local histories given the transcript, and then
transfers to the actual input via a filter on the references.

[Admitted local protocols]

(A,B) are finite-dimensional complex input spaces. An admitted protocol
consists of local quantum instruments, local memories and randomness, and
authenticated two-way public classical communication. Data and joint
laws satisfy:

Initial resources. A recorded standard Borel public variable
(C_0), possibly constant, with a law fixed once the protocol is chosen.
It is independent of the input plus any purifying or untouched reference.
Conditional on (C_0), the joint state of input-reference and the two
initial private auxiliaries factors position by position: input-reference
state times one local resource per laboratory. Shared initial randomness
is included in (C_0). No preshared secret key.

Local instruments. Each instrument assigns CP operations to
measurable outcome sets, countably additively on states, with measurable
outcome laws and measurable conditional local updates, and total
operation trace-preserving. Outcome space is standard Borel. Instruments
may depend measurably on local private history and preceding public
record.

Local aggregation and chronological history. A local subroutine
completed before a later public step may be aggregated into one
instrument that retains its continuing memory. Public communication stays
in the public schedule. Genuine classical history, including private
outcomes and public records, is a finite sequence or an
(N-indexed sequence in standard Borel coordinate spaces. Local
choices use only that laboratory's preceding private history and the
preceding public record; anything affecting the other party is
communicated through a public step.

Public steps and the complete record. Public discussion is
correspondingly finite or (N-indexed. Each message is
produced by its sender's local process from that sender's private
history and the preceding public record. A later public-source draw
uses a measurable probability kernel (kappa_m(t,dc)) depending only on
the preceding public record and identical for every input; the draw
multiplies by the scalar kernel and acts as the identity on quantum
registers. Public choices of speaker and stopping decisions use these
same two rules. (T) records (C_0), all public messages, draws, speaker
choices, and stopping decisions. Private outcomes and quantum memories
stay home.

Reference-compatible prefix laws. The specified instruments act
on arbitrary positive trace-class inputs. Unnormalized branch laws give
countably additive positive matrix-valued measures on any untouched
finite-dimensional reference for finite prefixes. Integrating the next
record recovers the preceding reference measure after tracing out the
current laboratory memories.

Terminal outputs. A terminal classical output may be a measurable
function of that laboratory's instrument history and received (T). A
terminal quantum readout is supplied with its incoming memory (via one
fixed measurable local CP process on those histories) and a selected
local instrument on that memory, selected using only that laboratory's
private history and (T). Together with the schedule, these give one
countably additive unnormalized instrument law on the complete history
and terminal outcomes for all inputs and finite untouched references.
Each terminal readout includes a tag for missing or non-invoked. Summing
either laboratory's terminal outcomes removes that local refinement;
summing both and tracing memory recovers the reference measure on the
complete history.

Completion on a specified input. A completed bit-output protocol
on a specified input has a bit in each laboratory almost surely on that
input, with normalized joint output. More generally a completed
key-output protocol produces a bit in a specified finite classical
alphabet per party.



For finite protocols of the specified type, composing the given
instruments supplies prefix laws and any finite terminal refinement.
The separate complete-history and terminal-process requirements matter
for countable schedules and for aggregated subroutines. No uniform bound
on communication, message size, or round count is imposed. A terminal
classical bit can depend on an entire infinite record.

# Separation from an ideal secret bit


The coherence inequality applies at each transcript. Take
(S = the trace-norm distance from the actual transcript joint
state to the ideal secret-bit transcript joint state. Two forces compete:
correctness forces little mass in unequal-bit branches, and secrecy
forces the equal-bit branches to have matching Eve densities of at least
half mass each. A Cauchy--Schwarz calculation turns these into incompatible
requirements. Below (eta < 1/5) they cannot both hold.

Throughout, the trace norm is unhalved:
(norm{H}_1=tr√(H^*H)). A classical-quantum state is a positive
matrix-valued measure of total trace one. If a Hermitian difference of
such measures has density (H(t)) with respect to a common finite
dominating measure (lambda), then
(norm{H}_1=intnorm{H(t)}_1,dlambda(t)), independent of the
dominating measure by homogeneity.

# A finite combinatorial realization
