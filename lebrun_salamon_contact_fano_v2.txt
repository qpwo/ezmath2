Contact Fano manifolds reach Riemannian geometry through twistor theory. Wolf classified the compact quaternionic symmetric spaces, and the twistor correspondence of Salamon, independently noticed by Berard-Bergery, assigns to each closed positive quaternionic-Kahler manifold M^{4m} a contact Fano manifold X^{2m+1}, its twistor space. LeBrun's positive twistor theorem singles out the twisted contact Fano manifolds as those admitting a Kahler-Einstein metric. The LeBrun-Salamon conjecture pairs two statements: every closed positive quaternionic-Kahler manifold is symmetric, and every contact Fano manifold is homogeneous. Partial cases were settled by Poon and Salamon in real dimension eight, by Ye and Druel in contact dimensions three and five, by Beauville for generically finite contact moment maps, by Yasukura when the contact line bundle is immersive, in the sense that the manifold admits a holomorphic immersion into projective space, by Kebekus for the covering family of contact lines, by Buczynski, Wisniewski, and Occhetta, Romano, Sola Conde and Wisniewski under reductive torus actions of large rank, and by Brendle and Semmelmann under nonnegative sectional curvature. The two conjectures are both consequences of the classification theorem.
