The objects classified are contact Fano manifolds. A contact structure on a smooth complex projective manifold $X$ of odd dimension $2n+1$ is a short exact sequence of holomorphic vector bundles
$$0 \to F \to T_X \xrightarrow{\theta} L \to 0$$
with $L$ a line bundle, such that the $L$-valued two-form $\omega(v,w)=\theta([v,w])$ on $F$ is everywhere nondegenerate. Equivalently, in a local frame $\theta=\alpha$, the form $\alpha\wedge(d\alpha)^n$ is nowhere zero. The manifold is a contact Fano manifold when $-K_X$ is ample. Taking determinants of the sequence gives
$$\det F = L^{\otimes n}, \qquad -K_X = L^{\otimes(n+1)},$$
so a contact Fano manifold is Fano with $L$ ample.

Classification theorem. Every smooth connected complex projective contact Fano manifold $(X,F)$ of dimension $2n+1\ge 3$ is contact-isomorphic to the projectivization
$$Y_{\mathfrak g}=\mathbb P(\mathcal O_{min}(\mathfrak g))\subset\mathbb P(\mathfrak g)$$
of the minimal nonzero nilpotent orbit of a simple complex Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, the contact structure being the projectivized Kirillov-Kostant-Souriau form on the orbit, and the contact line bundle being the restriction of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(\mathfrak g)}(1)$.

The two members of low Picard rank govern the exceptional cases. For $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_{n+2}$ the minimal orbit consists of rank-one nilpotents, and $Y_{\mathfrak g}$ is the incidence variety of points and hyperplanes $\{([v],[f]) : f(v)=0\}\subset\mathbb P^{n+1}\times(\mathbb P^{n+1})^*$ with $L=\mathcal O(1,1)$. For $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sp}_{2n+2}$ the minimal orbit is the cone of rank-one symmetric tensors, and $Y_{\mathfrak g}=\mathbb P^{2n+1}$ with the contact structure of a nondegenerate alternating form and $L=\mathcal O(2)$. These are exactly the contact Fano manifolds with Picard number above one, and with Picard number one and Fano index above one, respectively.

Contact Fano manifolds reach Riemannian geometry through twistor theory. Wolf classified the compact quaternionic symmetric spaces, and the twistor correspondence of Salamon, independently noticed by Berard-Bergery, assigns to each closed positive quaternionic-Kahler manifold $M^{4m}$ a contact Fano manifold $X^{2m+1}$, its twistor space. LeBrun's positive twistor theorem singles out the twisted contact Fano manifolds as those admitting a Kahler-Einstein metric. The LeBrun-Salamon conjecture pairs two statements: every closed positive quaternionic-Kahler manifold is symmetric, and every contact Fano manifold is homogeneous. Partial cases were settled by Poon and Salamon in real dimension eight, by Ye and Druel in contact dimensions three and five, by Beauville for generically finite contact moment maps, by Kebekus for the covering family of contact lines, by Buczynski, Wisniewski, and Occhetta, Romano, Sola Conde and Wisniewski under reductive torus actions of large rank, and by Brendle and Semmelmann under nonnegative sectional curvature. The two conjectures are both consequences of the classification theorem.

Three corollaries follow. Every contact Fano manifold $(X,F)$ of dimension $2n+1\ge 3$ admits a Kahler-Einstein metric of positive scalar curvature for its given complex structure, because the adjoint variety $Y_{\mathfrak g}$ carries one and the contact isomorphism is biholomorphic; positive Ricci curvature then makes the twistor space, and hence any positive quaternionic-Kahler manifold with that twistor space, symmetric. A smooth connected complex projective contact manifold has $b_2\ge 1$; it is an adjoint variety when $b_2=1$, and the projectivized cotangent bundle $\mathbb P(T^*Z)$ of a smooth projective $Z$ of dimension $n+1$ when $b_2\ge 2$, these two families exhausting the underlying manifolds of smooth connected complex projective contact manifolds. Every closed connected positive quaternionic-Kahler manifold of real dimension $4m\ge 8$ is homothetic to a compact symmetric Wolf space, so its curvature is parallel.

Reduction to the primitive Picard case. A contact Fano manifold $X$ of dimension $d=2n+1$ satisfies
$$-K_X=L^{\otimes(n+1)},$$
so it is Fano. Fano manifolds are simply connected with free Picard group. Yau's prescribed Ricci-form theorem and the Bonnet-Myers theorem make the universal cover $\rho:\tilde X\to X$ compact of finite degree $r$; Kodaira's projectivity theorem and Serre's GAGA make $\rho$ algebraic and etale; Kodaira vanishing gives $H^i(\tilde X,\mathcal O)=0$ for $i>0$, so both holomorphic Euler characteristics equal one; Riemann-Roch on the finite cover gives $\chi(\mathcal O_{\tilde X})=r\,\chi(\mathcal O_X)=r$, whence $r=1$. The exponential sequence together with $H^1(\mathcal O)=H^2(\mathcal O)=0$ identifies $\operatorname{Pic}(X)$ with $H^2(X,\mathbb Z)$, which is free by the universal coefficient theorem and $H_1(X,\mathbb Z)=0$.

Suppose $\operatorname{Pic}(X)$ has rank above one, so $b_2(X)>1$ and $K_X$ is not nef because $-K_X$ is ample. Kebekus, Peternell, Sommese and Wisniewski prove that a projective contact manifold with non-nef canonical bundle is either Fano with $b_2=1$ or a projectivized tangent bundle. In the line convention the second alternative is $X=\mathbb P(T^*Z)$ for a smooth projective $Z$ of dimension $n+1$, with contact line bundle $L=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(T^*Z)}(1)$. The canonical bundle of this bundle satisfies $K_X=\pi^*\bigl(K_Z\otimes\det T^*Z\bigr)\otimes\mathcal O(-(n+1))=\mathcal O(-(n+1))$, consistent with $-K_X=L^{\otimes(n+1)}$; ample $L$ makes $\mathcal O(1)$ ample, which is ampleness of $T_Z$, and Mori's theorem on ample tangent bundles forces $Z=\mathbb P^{n+1}$. Thus $X$ is the incidence variety $Y_A\subset\mathbb P^{n+1}\times(\mathbb P^{n+1})^*$, with $L=\mathcal O(1,1)$ and the tautological contact form; this is the type $A$ adjoint variety, on which $PGL_{n+2}$ acts transitively by contact automorphisms.
./localrefs/kpsw-2000-projective-contact-manifolds.pdf

Suppose $\operatorname{Pic}(X)$ has rank one. Write $L=A_1^{\otimes k}$ for the ample generator $A_1$ and an integer $k>0$. The Fano index is $k(n+1)$. If $k\ge 2$ the index is at least $2n+2=d+1$, and the Kobayashi-Ochiai index theorem, bounding the index of a smooth Fano $d$-fold by $d+1$ with equality only for projective space, gives $k=2$, $X=\mathbb P^{2n+1}$, $L=\mathcal O(2)$. The contact form is then a nondegenerate alternating two-form, and $Sp_{2n+2}$ acts transitively; this is the type $C$ adjoint variety. In the remaining case
$$\operatorname{Pic}(X)=\mathbb Z[L],$$
and all further arguments take place in this primitive case.

Contact lines. In the primitive case $\operatorname{Pic}(X)=\mathbb Z[L]$. A contact line is a map $f:\mathbb P^1\to X$ with $f^*L=\mathcal O(1)$; it is birational onto its image, since a nontrivial cover would multiply the positive degree. The composition $\theta\circ df$ is a homomorphism $\mathcal O(2)\to\mathcal O(1)$ and hence vanishes, so a contact line is horizontal, $df(T_{\mathbb P^1})\subset f^*F$.

Kebekus constructs a covering family of contact lines on a projective contact Fano manifold with $b_2=1$. The minimal contact degree of such a family is one or two, and degree two forces projective space, excluded in the primitive case; a covering family of contact lines is therefore available. Through a general point the lines form a proper parameter space, and for general $x$ that space is smooth with a tangent-direction morphism and the splitting
$$f^*T_X=\mathcal O(2)\oplus\mathcal O(1)^{\oplus(n-1)}\oplus\mathcal O^{\oplus(n+1)}.$$
The tangent directions of the lines through $x$ span the contact projective space $\mathbb P(F_x)$.

Freeness in a fixed degree. Let $A$ be ample on a smooth projective $M$ and fix a degree $e>0$. A dense open $M_e\subset M$ has the property that every degree-$e$ map $f:\mathbb P^1\to M$ whose image meets $M_e$ is free, meaning $f^*T_M$ is globally generated. The degree-$e$ map scheme is of finite type and splits into finitely many smooth locally closed strata; on each stratum the critical locus of evaluation at a source point fails to dominate unless evaluation is submersive, so its image is a proper closed set. The complement $M_e$ of these finitely many proper closed sets is dense and open. At a map with $f(0)\in M_e$, stratum tangent vectors span $T_{f(0)}M$; writing $f^*T_M=\bigoplus_i\mathcal O(a_i)$, a negative summand could not be generated at $0$, so every $a_i\ge 0$.
./localrefs/araujo-kollar-2003-rational-curves-on-varieties.pdf

Positive part of a free contact line. For a free contact line $f$, let $P_f\subset f^*T_X$ be the sum of the positive-degree summands. The subbundle is intrinsic, since a homomorphism from a positive line bundle to a trivial bundle is zero, and the quotient $f^*T_X/P_f$ is trivial. Moreover $\theta(P_f)=0$, and $P_f$ is isotropic for the Levi form, so $\operatorname{rank}P_f\le n$. If $\theta$ were nonzero on a positive summand $\mathcal O(a)$, then $a=1$ and the map to $f^*L=\mathcal O(1)$ would split the contact sequence, making the globally generated $f^*T_X$ a direct sum with $f^*F$; but $f^*F$ is not globally generated, because pairing $df$ with the nondegenerate Levi form gives a nonzero map $f^*F\to T^*_{\mathbb P^1}\otimes f^*L=\mathcal O(-1)$. Isotropy holds because each component of the Levi pairing on positive summands is a homomorphism $\mathcal O(a+b)\to\mathcal O(1)$ with $a,b\ge 1$, hence zero, and the rank-$2n$ Levi form is nondegenerate.

Consequently there is a smooth irreducible family $H$ of parametrized free contact lines with smooth dominant evaluation $H\times\mathbb P^1\to X$. Freeness is open, and at a free map both $H^1(f^*T_X)$ and $H^1(f^*T_X(-t))$ vanish for every source point $t$; the first vanishing makes the map scheme smooth, and the second makes evaluation submersive at every pair $(f,t)$.

Addition of Legendrian cones. In a symplectic vector space $(V,\omega)$ of dimension $2n$, let $Z\subset\mathbb P(V)$ be a smooth projective Legendrian variety, possibly disconnected, with affine cones $U_i$ over its irreducible components. Each cone has dimension $n$, is smooth away from the vertex, and has Lagrangian tangent spaces there. If $\omega(a,b)\ne 0$ for some $a\in U_i$, $b\in U_j$, then the addition map $U_i\times U_j\to V$, $(a,b)\mapsto a+b$, is dominant. Suppose it is not; the source has dimension $2n$, so a general fiber has positive dimension, and the nonorthogonal pairs form a nonempty open subset. Take a general pair in a positive-dimensional fiber and an irreducible curve $C$ in that fiber through it, with coordinate maps $a(t),b(t)$ and constant sum $s$. On the smooth open where both are nonzero, $\dot a=-\dot b$, and Lagrangianity of the cones gives
$$\frac{d}{dt}\omega(a,b)=\omega(\dot a,b)+\omega(a,\dot b)=-\omega(\dot b,b)-\omega(a,\dot a)=0,$$
so $\omega(a,b)=c\ne 0$ is constant on $C$ and neither coordinate vanishes. Normalize $C$ and pass to its smooth projective completion; $a$ is nonconstant and some coordinate has a pole. At a pole, choose a uniformizer $t$ and the maximal pole order $k$, so $p=t^ka$ and $q=-t^kb$ are regular with $p(0)=q(0)\ne 0$ and $p-q=t^ks$. The closed conical property puts $p(0)\in U_i$ and $q(0)\in U_j$; distinct components of the smooth $Z$ are disjoint, forcing $i=j$ and a common smooth cone germ $U$. On $U\times U$ the form $\omega$ vanishes along the diagonal to order three: with $r(z)$ the inclusion and $\omega(r,r_i)=0$, $\omega(r_j,r_i)=0$, differentiating gives $\omega(r,r_{ij})=0$, so $\omega(r(z),r(z+h))$ lies in the cube of the diagonal ideal. Hence $\omega(p(t),q(t))=O(t^{3k})$, while its exact value is $-t^{2k}\omega(a,b)=-ct^{2k}$, of order $2k$. This contradiction proves dominance.

Suitable contact-line pair. There are a point $x\in X$ and free parametrized contact lines $f_1,f_2:\mathbb P^1\to X$ with $f_1(0)=f_2(0)=x$ such that, for $a=f_1'(0)$ and $b=f_2'(0)$,
$$d\alpha_x(a,b)\ne 0,\qquad (P_{f_1})_0+(P_{f_2})_0=F_x.$$
The two positive subspaces are then complementary Lagrangians of $F_x$. Choose $x$ in the common general locus of the contact-line results and of freeness, so every line through $x$ is free, and put $\omega=d\alpha_x|_{F_x}$. The direction variety $Z_x$ is a smooth Legendrian variety spanning $\mathbb P(F_x)$ for $n\ge 2$; in dimension three the reduction $f^*T_X=\mathcal O(2)\oplus\mathcal O^{\oplus 2}$ makes the fixed-value map space smooth of dimension two with a free reparametrization orbit, so the line images through a general point form a finite proper family and $Z_x$ is a finite set spanning $\mathbb P(F_x)=\mathbb P^1$. Since the cones span $F_x$, some pair has $\omega(a,b)\ne 0$, and the addition lemma makes the sum of the corresponding cones dominant (a linear isomorphism when $n=1$). Taking derivatives of maps with $f(0)=x$ gives algebraic maps to $F_x$ whose derivative-sum is dominant, and restriction to the open set of nonzero pairing preserves dominance; generic smoothness then yields a pair whose derivative-sum surjects onto $F_x$. A tangent vector to maps with $f(0)=x$ is a section of $H^0(f^*T_X(-0))$, whose projection to the trivial quotient $f^*T_X/P_f$ is a constant section vanishing at $0$ and hence zero; it takes values in $P_f$, and its derivative at $0$ lies in $P_f|_0$. The surjective derivative-sum therefore has image inside $(P_{f_1})_0+(P_{f_2})_0$, and the positive-part lemma puts this inside $F_x$, proving the two identities. Each summand is isotropic of dimension at most $n$; a sum of dimension $2n$ forces both to have dimension $n$ and trivial intersection.

Smoothing two contact lines. The pair $f_1,f_2$ has $a=f_1'(0)$, $b=f_2'(0)$, $d\alpha_x(a,b)\ne 0$ and $(P_{f_1})_0+(P_{f_2})_0=F_x$, with affine parameters $u,v$ on the two sources. The lines are smoothed while their outer values move, and the resulting conic is very free, every summand of its pulled-back tangent bundle having positive degree.

The source family. In coordinates $[U_0:U_1]$, $[V_0:V_1]$ with $u=U_1/U_0$ and $v=V_1/V_0$, consider
$$\mathscr C=\{U_1V_1=sU_0V_0\}\subset\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb A^1_s,\qquad \pi:\mathscr C\to\mathbb A^1_s.$$
The equation $uv=s$ makes this a projective flat family of nodal genus-zero curves. The central fiber $C=C_1\cup C_2$ is the union of the axes $C_1=\{v=0\}$ and $C_2=\{u=0\}$, meeting at $o$. For $s\ne 0$ the fiber is smooth and projects isomorphically to the first factor. The disjoint sections $p(s)=(\infty,0)$ and $q(s)=(0,\infty)$ lie in the smooth locus. Because $f_1(0)=f_2(0)$, the two lines glue to a map $f_0:C\to X$. Put $E=f_0^*T_X$ and $E_i=f_i^*T_X$.

Relative deformation. For a projective flat family of nodal curves $\mathscr Y\to S$ over a smooth curve and a smooth projective target $Z$, the relative scheme $\operatorname{Hom}_S(\mathscr Y,Z\times S)\to S$ is locally of finite type; at a map $f$ its relative tangent space is $H^0(Y_{s_0},f^*T_Z)$, and the morphism is smooth at $f$ when $H^1(Y_{s_0},f^*T_Z)=0$. This is the relative deformation theorem of Araujo and Kollar, and it needs no smoothness of the special source.
./localrefs/araujo-kollar-2003-rational-curves-on-varieties.pdf

The normalization sequence
$$0\to E\to (i_1)_*E_1\oplus(i_2)_*E_2\xrightarrow{\ \operatorname{ev}_o-\operatorname{ev}_o\ }T_xX\otimes\mathcal O_o\to 0$$
has $H^1(C_i,E_i)=0$ and surjective evaluation at $o$ by freeness, so $H^1(C,E)=0$. Let $\mathscr H$ be the relative scheme of maps near $[f_0]$, with projection $\sigma:\mathscr H\to\mathbb A^1$ smooth at $[f_0]$, and let
$$\operatorname{Ev}:\mathscr H\to X\times X,\qquad [f:\mathscr C_s\to X]\mapsto (f(p(s)),f(q(s))),$$
with $y_1=f_1(\infty)$ and $y_2=f_2(\infty)$.

Vertical cokernel. The cokernel of evaluation on the vertical tangent space is canonically
$$\operatorname{coker}\bigl[H^0(C,E)\to T_{y_1}X\oplus T_{y_2}X\bigr]\simeq H^1(C,E(-p-q))\simeq T_xX/F_x,$$
of dimension one. Twisting the normalization sequence by $\mathcal O(-p-q)$ and using that the images of $H^0(C,E_i(-\cdot))$ at $o$ are exactly $(P_{f_i})_0$ identifies the cokernel with $T_xX/F_x$; the evaluation sequence $0\to E(-p-q)\to E\to E|_p\oplus E|_q\to 0$ together with $H^1(C,E)=0$ identifies the same group with the evaluation cokernel.

Fixed ends. No tangent vector $\zeta\in T_{[f_0]}\mathscr H$ satisfies $d\sigma(\zeta)=1$ and $d\operatorname{Ev}(\zeta)=0$. Such a vector would give a map to $X$ from the pullback of the source family under $s=\epsilon$, $\epsilon^2=0$, reducing to $f_0$ and fixing both endpoint values to first order. Near the node the source has equation $uv=\epsilon$; on $C_1\setminus\{o\}$ it is trivialized by $u$ (at infinity, $v=\epsilon w$ with $w=1/u$). The infinitesimal difference from $f_1$ is an intrinsic section $A$ of $E_1$ on the punctured component, and from $f_2$ a section $D$ of $E_2$; the fixed endpoints give $A(\infty)=D(\infty)=0$. In common target coordinates centered at $x$, with $g_1,g_2$ the coordinate expressions of $f_1,f_2$, the hypothetical map has the form $g_1(u)+g_2(v)+\epsilon R(u,v)$ in the completed local ring $\mathbb C[[u,v,\epsilon]]/(uv-\epsilon,\epsilon^2)$. Substituting $v=\epsilon/u$ and $u=\epsilon/v$ and using $g_1'(0)=a$, $g_2'(0)=b$, $\epsilon^2=0$ gives
$$A(u)=\frac{b}{u}+c+O(u),\qquad D(v)=\frac{a}{v}+c+O(v),\qquad c=R(0,0),$$
with the constant $c$ independent of the choice of $R$ and invariant under changes of target coordinates (the derivative and Hessian contributions $Jc+B(a,b)$ and $Jc+B(b,a)$ agree by symmetry of $B$). The sections $W_1=uA$ and $W_2=vD$ extend over the node and, because the simple poles at infinity are canceled by the zeros of $A$ and $D$, are global sections of $E_1$ and $E_2$ with jets $W_1(0)=b$, $W_1'(0)=c$, $W_2(0)=a$, $W_2'(0)=c$. Freeness makes the ordinary map scheme smooth at each $f_i$, so $W_i$ is the velocity of a local deformation of the degree-one map $f_i$. Every such deformation is horizontal, and differentiating horizontality $\theta\,df_i=0$ in a frame gives
$$0=(\partial_{W_i}\alpha)(f_i')+\alpha(W_i').$$
At the node this yields $(\partial_b\alpha)_x(a)+\alpha_x(c)=0$ and $(\partial_a\alpha)_x(b)+\alpha_x(c)=0$; subtracting gives $d\alpha_x(a,b)=0$, contradicting the suitable-pair condition. The contradiction forbids first-order smoothing with fixed ends.

Very free conic. There is a map $g:\mathbb P^1\to X$ with $\deg g^*L=2$ and
$$g^*T_X=\mathcal O(2)\oplus\mathcal O(1)^{\oplus 2n}.$$
The full scheme $\Hom_2(\mathbb P^1,X)$ of degree-two maps is smooth at $g$, and evaluation at two fixed distinct parameters is submersive there, so the component through $g$ dominates $X\times X$; moreover $g$ is an immersion and the strict positivity of its tangent splitting persists on an open neighborhood. Let $T=T_{[f_0]}\mathscr H$ and $T_0=\ker d\sigma$; the relative deformation theorem supplies $\zeta$ with $d\sigma(\zeta)=1$, and the vertical-cokernel computation makes $d\operatorname{Ev}(T_0)$ have codimension one. If $d\operatorname{Ev}(\zeta)$ lay in $d\operatorname{Ev}(T_0)$, subtracting a vertical vector would produce a fixed-end smoothing, which is forbidden; hence $d\operatorname{Ev}:T\to T_{y_1}X\oplus T_{y_2}X$ is surjective and $\operatorname{Ev}$ is smooth at $[f_0]$. Over $\mathbb G_m$ the source family is $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb G_m$ and $\operatorname{Ev}$ is $(h,s)\mapsto(h(\infty),h(0))$, so the base parameter adds no evaluation direction; taking $s\ne 0$ gives a point $g$ at which $\Hom_2(\mathbb P^1,X)$ is smooth and
$$H^0(\mathbb P^1,g^*T_X)\to T_{g(\infty)}X\oplus T_{g(0)}X$$
is surjective. Writing $g^*T_X=\bigoplus_j\mathcal O(a_j)$ with $d=2n+1$ summands, evaluation at two distinct points is surjective precisely when every $a_j\ge 1$; the degree identity $\sum_j a_j=\deg g^*(-K_X)=2(n+1)=d+1$ then forces the splitting $\mathcal O(2)\oplus\mathcal O(1)^{\oplus 2n}$. The nonconstant map $g$ has nonzero differential, and a homomorphism $T_{\mathbb P^1}=\mathcal O(2)\to g^*T_X$ lands in the unique degree-two summand as an isomorphism, so $g$ is an immersion. Strict positivity is the open condition $H^1(\mathbb P^1,g^*T_X(-2))=0$.

Extension over the unmarked boundary. In the primitive case, with the very free conic $g$ fixed, write $\mathcal E_{12}=T_X\boxtimes L$, $\mathcal E_{21}=L\boxtimes T_X$, $\mathcal M_X=L\boxtimes L$ on $X\times X$; the contact form induces maps $\mathcal E_{12}\to\mathcal M_X$ and $\mathcal E_{21}\to\mathcal M_X$.

Moving-pair construction. There are a proper integral algebraic space $Y$, a dominant morphism $p:Y\to X\times X$, and sections
$$W_{12}\in H^0(Y,p^*\mathcal E_{12}),\qquad W_{21}\in H^0(Y,p^*\mathcal E_{21})$$
with common contact value $\xi=(\theta\boxtimes 1)(W_{12})=(1\boxtimes\theta)(W_{21})\in H^0(Y,p^*\mathcal M_X)$. There is an involution $\iota$ of $Y$ over the exchange of the two factors of $X\times X$, under which $\xi$ is invariant. For a full open neighborhood $\mathcal H$ of $g$ in $\Hom_2(\mathbb P^1,X)$ there is a morphism $j:\mathcal H\times\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\to Y$ over $(h,z,w)\mapsto(h(z),h(w))$, with
$$j^*W_{12}=(z-w)^2 h'(z)\otimes\theta(h'(w)),\qquad j^*W_{21}=(z-w)^2\theta(h'(z))\otimes h'(w),$$
and $j^*\xi=(z-w)^2\theta(h'(z))\otimes\theta(h'(w))$, independent of the affine coordinate.

Stable-map compactification. Because $\operatorname{Pic}(X)=\mathbb Z[L]$, contact degree two fixes the numerical class $\beta$. The stack $\overline{\mathcal M}_{0,0}(X,\beta)$ of unmarked genus-zero stable maps of class $\beta$ to the smooth projective $X$ is proper of finite type. The very-free computation makes its unique component through $[g]$ smooth there; give that reduced component a normalization, obtaining a proper integral normal stack $\mathcal S$, isomorphic to the original component on a full neighborhood of $[g]$. Over $\mathcal S$ sit the universal source $\pi:\mathcal C\to\mathcal S$ with evaluation $e:\mathcal C\to X$, and the fibre square
$$\mathcal D=\mathcal C\times_{\mathcal S}\mathcal C,\qquad \tilde p=(e_1,e_2):\mathcal D\to X\times X.$$
The points of $\mathcal D$ select two points on the unmarked source, which may coincide or be nodes and which carry no stability marking. Since two-point evaluation is submersive at $g$, the morphism $\tilde p$ is dominant.

Invariant tensor. On $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$ set
$$\mathfrak b(z,w)=(z-w)^2\partial_z\otimes\partial_w\in H^0(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1,T_{\mathbb P^1}\boxtimes T_{\mathbb P^1}).$$
Under a fractional-linear change $T$, $(T(z)-T(w))^2=(z-w)^2T'(z)T'(w)$, so $\mathfrak b$ is coordinate-independent, regular at infinity, vanishes to order two on the diagonal, and is invariant under simultaneous reparametrization. A smooth proper genus-zero family acquires parametrizations locally in the smooth topology, so $\mathfrak b$ descends to its relative square and accounts for every finite automorphism of a stable map. Over the nonempty open $\mathcal S^\circ\subset\mathcal S$ of smooth sources define
$$W_{12}^\circ=(d_\pi e_1\otimes\theta d_\pi e_2)(\mathfrak b),\qquad W_{21}^\circ=(\theta d_\pi e_1\otimes d_\pi e_2)(\mathfrak b),$$
sections of $\tilde p^*\mathcal E_{12}$ and $\tilde p^*\mathcal E_{21}$ whose contact values agree and are invariant under the exchange of the two source variables. The relative differentials are taken in the two source variables of $\mathcal D$.

Full family. The neighborhood $\mathcal H$ maps to $\mathcal S$ with pulled-back source $\mathcal H\times\mathbb P^1$, giving a morphism $\tilde j:\mathcal H\times\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\to\mathcal D$ over $\tilde p$; because $\mathcal H$ is open in the full $\Hom_2(\mathbb P^1,X)$, it retains the entire nearby parametrized family.

Normality and divisorial points. Let $B$ be a normal integral locally Noetherian scheme and $C_1,\dots,C_k\to B$ flat nodal-curve families of finite presentation whose entire generic fibers are smooth and geometrically integral. Then $P=C_1\times_B\cdots\times_B C_k$ is integral and normal, and every height-one point of $P$ lies either over the generic point of $B$ or at a generic point of a fiber component over a height-one point of $B$, where every selected point of every $C_i$ is relatively smooth. The morphism $P\to B$ is flat and of finite presentation with geometrically reduced fibers that are local complete intersections (a product of nodal curves is cut out by nodal equations in disjoint variables) and hence Cohen-Macaulay. Reducedness follows because on affine charts the coordinate algebra injects into a reduced generic localization, and irreducibility because every nonempty open meets the geometrically integral generic fiber. Serre's condition $S_2$ ascends along flat maps from $S_2$ base and fiber, and $R_1$ holds at each height-one point by the flat local dimension formula $1=\dim\mathcal O_{P,q}=\dim\mathcal O_{B,b}+\dim\mathcal O_{P_b,q}$ together with smoothness of the relevant points. Serre's criterion gives normality and the stated description of height-one points. The hypothesis is satisfied because the smooth-source open contains $[g]$, so on every normal etale chart of $\mathcal S$ the entire generic source is a smooth geometrically integral rational curve; applying the lemma for $k=1,2$ makes $\mathcal C$ and $\mathcal D$ integral and normal. In particular no height-one point of $\mathcal D$ lies over a base stratum of codimension at least two, and over a base divisor it is a generic point of a product of source components.

Degree-two source trees. A geometric source of an unmarked stable map of genus zero and $L$-degree two is either smooth or exactly two copies $A,J$ of $\mathbb P^1$ joined at one node, with $L$-degree one on each, and no contracted components. The dual graph of a connected nodal genus-zero curve is a tree and every component is a smooth rational curve; ampleness of $L$ gives positive integral degree on every nonconstant component, so there are at most two, and a contracted component would need at least three nodes. Every leaf is nonconstant, a nontrivial tree has at least two leaves, so there are exactly two leaves of degree one and the tree is a path; an interior vertex would be contracted of valence two and unstable, so none occurs.

The node. By normality it suffices to extend at height-one points, since the target bundles $\tilde p^*\mathcal E_{12}$ and $\tilde p^*\mathcal E_{21}$ are locally free. The unexamined points lie over divisors of $\mathcal S$ with singular source. Take the corresponding DVR $R_0$ on a normal etale scheme chart and pass to its completed strict henselization $R$, a faithfully flat DVR with algebraically closed residue field $k$ and uniformizer $\tau$. Let $C\to\operatorname{Spec}R$ be the pulled-back source with evaluation $e$. The entire generic fiber is smooth and geometrically integral, so $\mathcal C$ and $\mathcal C\times_R\mathcal C$ are integral normal. By the tree description the special fiber is $C_k=A\cup J$ with one node $o$, and the completed local ring is
$$\widehat{\mathcal O_{C,o}}\simeq Q=R[[u,v]]/(uv-\tau^r),\qquad r\ge 1,\qquad A=(v,\tau),\quad J=(u,\tau).$$
The smoothing parameter is a unit times $\tau^r$ because the generic fiber is smooth, and the unit is absorbed into a coordinate; regularity of the total space at $o$ and Cartier-ness of the special components are not assumed.

Contact vanishing at the boundary. On the relatively smooth locus at the generic point of either $A$ or $J$, the bundle map $\theta d_\pi e:T_{C/R}\to e^*L$ is divisible by $\tau^r$. Nodal fibers are Gorenstein, so $\omega_{C/R}$ is invertible and its formation commutes with base change. Put $\mathcal N=e^*L\otimes\omega_{C/R}$; on either special component the dualizing degree is $-1$, so $\mathcal N$ has multidegree $(0,0)$, and trivializations on $A$ and $J$ rescale to agree at $o$, giving $\mathcal N|_{C_k}\simeq\mathcal O$ with $h^0=1$ and $H^1=0$. Multiplication by $\tau$ and Nakayama over the Noetherian $R$ then give $H^1(C,\mathcal N)=0$ and $H^0(C,\mathcal N)/\tau H^0(C,\mathcal N)\simeq H^0(C_k,\mathcal N|_{C_k})$, so the free rank-one pushforward makes evaluation an isomorphism and yields a nowhere-vanishing generator $m$. The pullback of the contact form followed by $\Omega^1_{C/R}\to\omega_{C/R}$ is a section $\eta$ of $\mathcal N$, say $\eta=h_R m$. At the node a dualizing generator is $\gamma=du/u=-dv/v$, and the canonical map sends $du\mapsto u\gamma$ and $dv\mapsto -v\gamma$, so in a frame the coefficient of $\eta$ lies in $(u,v)$; with $m=c\,(\text{frame})\,\gamma$ and $c$ a unit, this forces $h_R\in(u,v)\cap R=(\tau^r)$. At a component generic point $m$ is a unit and $\Omega^1\to\omega$ is an isomorphism, so the contact differential has order at least $\tau^r$ on both components.

Contraction. Choose a $k$-point of $A\setminus\{o\}$. Smoothness there and henselianity give a section $\sigma:\operatorname{Spec}R\to C$ in the relatively smooth locus, a Cartier divisor. The line bundle $\mathcal O_C(\sigma)$ has multidegree $(1,0)$ on $A\cup J$; sections on $A$ extend uniquely as constant sections on $J$ with matching value at $o$, proving global generation, $h^0=2$ and $H^1=0$ on the special fiber. The DVR base-change then gives a free rank-two pushforward and surjective evaluation on $C$, and a basis defines
$$\phi:C\to\mathbb P^1_R,$$
an isomorphism on $A$ and a contraction of $J$. On the generic fiber $\phi$ is an isomorphism, checked after an algebraic closure and then descended, so the argument never requires a split-conic assumption on the original source.

Transporting the tensor. The differential of $\phi$ composed with $\Omega^1_{C/R}\to\omega_{C/R}$ is a section $\delta_\phi$ of $\mathcal M=\omega_{C/R}\otimes\phi^*T_{\mathbb P^1_R/R}$, nowhere zero on the entire generic fiber. Its zero divisor is effective and vertical, written on the normal surface $C$ as
$$\operatorname{div}(\delta_\phi)=b_AA+b_JJ.$$
At the generic point of $A$ the ordinary differential of $\phi|_A$ is nonzero, so $b_A=0$.

Contracted-component order. $b_J=r$. Set $b=b_J$ and normalize $\delta'=\tau^{-b}\delta_\phi$, of order zero in the component DVR of $J$, with a nonzero generic restriction $s_J$ to $J$. In $Q$ the divisors are $\operatorname{div}(v)=rA$ and $\operatorname{div}(\tau)=A+J$, and $Q$ is a normal hypersurface domain: at every height-one prime one of $u,v$ is a unit and the hypersurface is regular there, and Serre's criterion gives normality. Choose a frame of $\mathcal M$ and let $a$ be the coefficient of $\delta_\phi$; since $\delta_\phi$ is nowhere zero on the generic fiber, $a$ and $a^{-1}$ lie in $\mathcal O_{C,o}[1/\tau]$ and become units of $Q[1/\tau]$, so completion creates no horizontal zero or pole. The coefficient $f=\tau^{-b}a$ of $\delta'$ satisfies $\operatorname{div}_Q(f)=-bA$, so $f^rv^b$ has zero valuation at every height-one prime; by normality (a normal Noetherian domain is the intersection of its height-one local rings in its fraction field) applied to the function and its inverse, $f^r=v^{-b}\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon$ a unit. Reducing at the generic point of $J$, where $f$ has order zero, the image of $v$ is a parameter on $J$ at $o$ and the image of $\varepsilon$ is a unit of $k[[v]]$, so $s_J^{\otimes r}$ has order $-b$ at $o$ and no other zeros or poles. Since $\deg(\mathcal M|_J)=\deg(\omega_{C/R}|_J)+\deg(\phi^*T_{\mathbb P^1_R/R}|_J)=-1+0=-1$, the divisor of $s_J^{\otimes r}$, equal to $r$ times a divisor of degree $-1$, has degree $-r$; comparison gives $b=r$.

The four product components. The special fiber of $C\times_R C$ has components $A\times A$, $A\times J$, $J\times A$, $J\times J$ over $k$, and at each generic point its local ring is a DVR with uniformizer $\tau$. The generic-fiber identity $(d\phi\otimes d\phi)(\mathfrak b)=(\phi\times\phi)^*\mathfrak b_{\mathbb P^1_R}$ has regular right-hand side; since $d\phi$ has component order $0$ on $A$ and $r$ on $J$, inverting the two differentials costs $0$ on $A\times A$ and at most $r$ on either mixed component, and the analogous contraction preserving $J$ gives $0$ on $J\times J$. Hence $\operatorname{ord}(\mathfrak b)$ is at least $0$ on $A\times A$ and $J\times J$, and at least $-r$ on $A\times J$ and $J\times A$.
$$ \begin{array}{c|cccc} \text{component} & A\times A & A\times J & J\times A & J\times J \\ \hline \operatorname{ord}(\mathfrak b)\ge & 0 & -r & -r & 0 \end{array} $$

Extension. The contact differential has order at least $r$ in either variable; using it in the second variable for $W_{12}^\circ$ and in the first for $W_{21}^\circ$ cancels the only possible negative order. The remaining ordinary differential is regular at these relatively smooth points, so each vector section has nonnegative order on all four product components. Every height-one point of $\mathcal D$ is thus checked, and a rational section of a locally free sheaf on a normal Noetherian scheme that is regular at all height-one points is regular; on the scheme charts of $\mathcal D$ this yields unique sections $\widetilde W_{12}\in H^0(\mathcal D,\tilde p^*\mathcal E_{12})$ and $\widetilde W_{21}\in H^0(\mathcal D,\tilde p^*\mathcal E_{21})$, compatible with the stack descent data. Their contact values agree everywhere and are invariant under the involution.

Coarse space. The coarse morphism $\rho:\mathcal D\to Y$ is proper, surjective and quasi-finite with $\rho_*\mathcal O_{\mathcal D}=\mathcal O_Y$ on the etale site, and $Y$ is a proper integral algebraic space; this is the Keel-Mori theorem in the form given by Conrad. The projection formula gives $H^0(\mathcal D,\rho^*\mathcal V)=H^0(Y,\mathcal V)$ for either bundle, so $\widetilde W_{12}$ and $\widetilde W_{21}$ descend to $W_{12},W_{21}$ with the same contact value and symmetry, and $\tilde p$ factors as the dominant $p$. Setting $j=\rho\tilde j$ produces the morphism of the moving-pair construction with its identities on the full neighborhood $\mathcal H$, completing it.

Descent of the common contact value to the square. Put $Z=X\times X$ and $M=L\boxtimes L$. Let $p:Y\to Z$ be dominant with $Y$ a proper integral algebraic space, and suppose there are regular sections $W_{12}\in H^0(Y,p^*(T_X\boxtimes L))$ and $W_{21}\in H^0(Y,p^*(L\boxtimes T_X))$ with common contact value $\xi=(\theta\boxtimes1)W_{12}=(1\boxtimes\theta)W_{21}\in H^0(Y,p^*M)$. Then there is a unique $B\in H^0(Z,M)$ with $p^*B=\xi$.

Proof. The section $\xi$ defines a morphism $a:Y\to\operatorname{Tot}(M)$ over $Z$, proper with reduced closed image $S_0$, which is integral; $a$ factors through a proper surjection $Y\to S_0$. Universal closedness descends along this surjection, so $S_0$ is proper over $\mathbb C$; being closed in the total space it is affine over $Z$, hence finite over $Z$ and surjective. Let $S\to S_0$ be the normalization and $q:S\to Z$ the composite, finite dominant with $S$ normal and generically etale in characteristic zero. Deleting the finite images of the nonnormalized locus and of the non-etale locus gives a dense open $U\subset Z$ over which $S\times_ZU\to U$ is finite etale. No morphism from $Y$ to $S$ is required.

Every generic sheet splits over contact lines. Choose a smooth family $H$ of free contact lines with smooth dominant evaluation. For $f\in H$ let $P_f$ be the positive part of $f^*T_X$, so that $f^*T_X/P_f\simeq\mathcal O^{\oplus r_f}$ and $\theta(P_f)=0$. For a test line $c=(f,y):\mathbb P^1\to Z$ meeting $U$, let $D$ be any reduced component of $\mathbb P^1\times_ZY$ dominating $\mathbb P^1$; it is proper and has $H^0(D,\mathcal O_D)=\mathbb C$, because a regular function gives a morphism to $\mathbb P^1$ whose proper irreducible image avoids infinity. Pulling back $W_{12}$ and projecting to $\pi_D^*((f^*T_X/P_f)\otimes L_y)$ gives a constant vector in a global trivialization, and since the contact map factors through this quotient, $\xi|_D=\pi_D^*a_D$ for some $a_D\in H^0(\mathbb P^1,c^*M)$; the analogue with $W_{21}$ covers the other type $c=(y,f)$. Over $K=\mathbb C(\mathbb P^1)$ every point of $T_\eta$ belongs to a generic fiber of such a $D$; trivializing $c^*M$ over $K$ places the image at a $K$-rational point, and surjectivity of $Y_\eta\to(\mathbb P^1\times_ZS_0)_\eta$ forces the whole finite scheme to be $K$-rational. By construction of $U$ it equals $(\mathbb P^1\times_ZS)_\eta$, finite etale, hence a product of copies of $K$. Consequently, for every such $c$, each dominating component $C\subset(\mathbb P^1\times_ZS)_{\mathrm red}$ satisfies $\deg(C^\nu/\mathbb P^1)=1$.

Ramification is excluded. Suppose $q$ is not unramified at the generic point of a prime divisor $R\subset S$, with image the prime divisor $\Delta\subset Z$. Over a dense smooth open $\Delta^0\subset\Delta$ the reduced divisors above $\Delta$ are smooth, disjoint and etale over $\Delta^0$, and in analytic coordinates with $\Delta=(x_1=0)$ the map is $(z_1,\ldots,z_N)\mapsto(z_1^{e_i},z_2,\ldots,z_N)$ with some $e_i>1$. A contact line of one of the two types meeting $\Delta^0$ transversely exists: if $\Delta\to X$ under the second projection is dominant, its fiber $\Delta_y$ over general $y$ is a Cartier divisor with $\mathcal O_X(\Delta_y)=L^{\otimes a}$ and $a>0$, so a general line of the first type meets $\Delta$; if $\Delta$ is not dominant it equals $X\times D$ with $D$ a prime divisor, and a general line of the second type meets it. Dominant evaluation makes a meeting transverse and off the codimension-two set. At such a transverse intersection the analytic local ring of $\mathbb P^1\times_ZS$ is $\mathbb C\{z_1,t\}/(z_1^{e}-a(t))$ with $a(0)=0$, $a'(0)\ne0$, which is smooth of dimension one but on which $t$ has order $e$; the unique reduced component through it has normalization mapping to $\mathbb P^1$ with degree at least $e$, contradicting the splitting of every sheet. Hence $q$ is unramified at every codimension-one point.

Purity and simple connectedness. At an arbitrary point the local hypotheses of purity of the branch locus hold (Noetherian, $S$ normal, base regular, $q$ finite of positive relative dimension), and $q$ unramified at all codimension-one generizations makes $q$ etale; flatness is a conclusion, not a premise. Since $Z^{\mathrm{an}}$ is simply connected and $S$ is integral, the finite etale cover $S\to Z$ is connected of degree one, so $S_0\to Z$ is finite birational and hence an isomorphism because $Z$ is normal. The closed inclusion of $S_0$ in $\operatorname{Tot}(M)$ is the graph of a global section $B$ with $p^*B=\xi$; pullback of sections along a dominant map of integral spaces is injective, giving uniqueness.

Symmetry and the full local family. Applying the descent to the moving-pair space gives $B\in H^0(X\times X,L\boxtimes L)$ symmetric under the exchange of the factors: the involution of $Y$ satisfies $p\iota=\sigma p$ and $\iota^*\xi=\xi$, so $\sigma^*B=B$ by dominance. Pulling $p^*B=\xi$ back along the full family gives
$$B(h(z),h(w))=(z-w)^2\theta(h'(z))\otimes\theta(h'(w)),\qquad h\in\mathcal H,$$
for all pairs of source points including the diagonal, with coordinate independence and regularity at infinity following from $(z-w)^2\partial_z\otimes\partial_w$.

Diagonal jets and contact transitivity. The symmetric section $B\in H^0(X\times X,L\boxtimes L)$ satisfies
$$B(h(z),h(w))=(z-w)^2\theta(h'(z))\otimes\theta(h'(w))$$
on the full neighborhood $\mathcal H$ of the very free conic $g$, with $z,w$ in one affine chart of $\mathbb P^1$. The diagonal symbol of $B$ is first determined at every point, then a criterion converts it into transitivity.

Value and velocity. Fix $0\in\mathbb P^1$ with its affine coordinate. A Zariski-open neighborhood $\mathcal U\subset\mathcal H$ of $g$ has the property that the value-velocity morphism
$$\mathcal U\to T_X,\qquad h\mapsto(h(0),h'(0)),$$
is smooth, with open image in $T_X$; projection to $X$ is a nonempty open $U\subset X$, and for each $x\in U$ the velocities of maps in $\mathcal U$ with $h(0)=x$ form a nonempty open subset of $T_xX$. Every summand of $E=g^*T_X$ has degree at least one, so $H^1(E(-2\cdot0))=0$, and the sequence $0\to E(-2\cdot0)\to E\to E|_{2\cdot0}\to 0$ makes $H^0(E)\to H^0(E|_{2\cdot0})$ surjective. The Hom space is smooth at $g$ with tangent space $H^0(E)$, and in target coordinates the joint value-velocity differential is the restriction map $\psi\mapsto(\psi(0),\psi'(0))$, so the smooth locus contains the open $\mathcal U$ and its image is open.

Second diagonal symbol. Let $\Delta\subset X\times X$ be the diagonal, $\mathcal J$ its ideal, and $M=L\boxtimes L$. The identification $\mathcal J/\mathcal J^2\simeq\Omega_X^1$, $f(y)-f(x)\mapsto df$, gives $(\mathcal J^k/\mathcal J^{k+1})\otimes M|_\Delta\simeq\operatorname{Sym}^k\Omega_X^1\otimes L^{\otimes2}$. Write $\theta^2$ for the symmetric square of the $L$-valued one-form $\theta$, of quadratic value $v\mapsto\theta(v)^2$.

Proposition. The section $B$ vanishes to order at least two along $\Delta$, and its second diagonal symbol is
$$B\in H^0(X\times X,\mathcal J^2\otimes M),\qquad [B]_2=\theta^2\quad\text{in }H^0(X,\operatorname{Sym}^2\Omega_X^1\otimes L^{\otimes2}).$$
Choose local coordinates and one frame $e$ of $L$ in both factors, write $\theta=e\alpha$ and $B(x,y)=b(x,y)e(x)\otimes e(y)$, and fix $x\in U$. Setting $z=0$ in the family identity for a nearby map with $h(0)=x$ and $h'(0)=a$ gives
$$b(x,h(w))=w^2\alpha_x(a)\alpha_{h(w)}(h'(w))=w^2\alpha_x(a)^2+O(w^3).$$
The constant term makes $B|_\Delta=0$ on $U$, hence everywhere as a holomorphic section of $L^{\otimes2}$ on connected $X$. The first symbol is now a section of $\Omega_X^1\otimes L^{\otimes2}$ whose value on $a$ is the coefficient of $w$, which vanishes for a nonempty open set of velocities and hence is the zero linear form on all of $X$. Thus $B$ lies in $\mathcal J^2\otimes M$ globally. Because the linear term vanished, the acceleration $h''(0)$ contributes nothing to the coefficient of $w^2$; the quadratic symbol takes the value $\alpha_x(a)^2$ on a nonempty open set of $a$ and therefore equals $\alpha_x^2$. The difference $[B]_2-\theta^2$ is holomorphic and zero on $U$, hence zero on $X$. Each continuation is performed after the preceding lower symbol vanished, so all three identities are intrinsic.

Local expansion. Let $B$ be symmetric with $[B]_2=\theta^2$ on a complex contact manifold. In local coordinates and a frame, put $s=y-x$ and $A_x(u,v)=(\partial_u\alpha)_x(v)$, where $\partial$ differentiates the coefficients of $\alpha$. Then
$$b(x,x+s)=\alpha_x(s)^2+\alpha_x(s)A_x(s,s)+O(s^4).$$
Write the expansion as $Q_x(s)+C_x(s)+O(s^4)$ with $Q_x(s)=\alpha_x(s)^2$ and $C_x$ cubic. Symmetry in the same frame gives
$$Q_x(s)+C_x(s)=Q_{x+s}(-s)+C_{x+s}(-s)+O(s^4)=\alpha_x(s)^2+2\alpha_x(s)A_x(s,s)-C_x(s)+O(s^4),$$
so $C_x(s)=\alpha_x(s)A_x(s,s)$. Differentiating in the first slot with the second held fixed, where $s=y-x$ has derivative $-v$, gives
$$D^{(1)}_vb(x,x+s)=-2\alpha_x(s)\alpha_x(v)+\alpha_x(s)\,d\alpha_x(v,s)-\alpha_x(v)A_x(s,s)+O(s^3),$$
using $d\alpha_x(v,s)=A_x(v,s)-A_x(s,v)$. Under a frame change $e'=qe$, $\rho=q^{-1}$, the data transform as $\alpha'=\rho\alpha$, $b'=\rho(x)\rho(y)b$, the cubic coefficient becomes $C'_x(s)=\alpha'_x(s)(\partial_s\alpha')_x(s)$, and the fixed-second derivative acquires exactly the first-slot frame term $d\rho_x(v)\rho(y)b$. The symbol $[B]_2$ is intrinsic while the cubic is a coordinate expression.

Contact Hamiltonian fields. Contact value identifies the holomorphic vector fields preserving $F$ with $H^0(X,L)$. For $a\in H^0(X,L)$, in a frame $\theta=e\alpha$ and $a=ef$, the field $K_a$ is characterized by
$$\alpha(K_a)=f,\qquad df(w)+d\alpha(K_a,w)=0\quad(w\in F),$$
and its value at a point depends only on the ordinary first jet of $a$. Writing $K_a=fR+Z$ with $\alpha(R)=1$ and $Z\in F$, the second equation determines $Z$ uniquely and holomorphically by nondegeneracy of $d\alpha|_F$; Cartan's formula identifies it with $(\mathcal L_{K_a}\alpha)|_F=0$, equivalently $[K_a,F]\subset F$. Under a frame change the equations transform by the same factor and the fields glue, so uniqueness is frame-independent and natural under contact automorphisms.

Symmetric-jet criterion. Let $X$ be a connected compact complex contact manifold and $B\in H^0(X\times X,L\boxtimes L)$ symmetric with
$$B\in H^0(X\times X,\mathcal J_\Delta^2\otimes(L\boxtimes L)),\qquad [B]_2=\theta^2\quad\text{in }H^0(X,\operatorname{Sym}^2\Omega_X^1\otimes L^{\otimes2}).$$
Then the first-jet evaluation $H^0(X,L)\to J_x^1L$ is surjective at every $x$, global contact fields span $T_xX$ at every point, and the identity component of the contact automorphism group acts transitively.

Proof. Put $V=H^0(X,L)$. Point evaluations followed by fiber functionals span $V^*$, since only the zero section vanishes everywhere. Choose a basis $\varepsilon_j$ among these functionals with dual $s_j\in V$; contracting the second factor of $B$ gives global sections $t_j$ of $L$ with $B=\sum_jt_j\otimes s_j$, so $B\in H^0(X,L)\otimes V=H^0(X,L\otimes(V\otimes\mathcal O_X))$. The vector $B_x\in L_x\otimes V$ is nonzero, since $B_x=0$ would make $B(x,y)$ vanish identically in $y$, contradicting the nonzero quadratic term $\alpha_x(s)^2$. Thus $B$ gives a line subbundle and a holomorphic map
$$L^{-1}\hookrightarrow V\otimes\mathcal O_X,\qquad \beta:X\to\mathbb P(V),\qquad \beta^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(V)}(1)\simeq L.$$
The map $\beta$ is an immersion. In a local frame $B=e\otimes w$ with $w$ a $V$-valued function, if $d\beta_x(v)=0$ then $\partial_vw(x)=kw(x)$ for a scalar $k$ independent of the second variable, so $D^{(1)}_vb(x,y)=kb(x,y)$. The linear terms of the fixed-second formula give $\alpha_x(v)=0$, because $\alpha_x$ is nonzero; the quadratic terms give $\alpha_x(s)d\alpha_x(v,s)=k\alpha_x(s)^2$, and cancelling $\alpha_x(s)$ gives $d\alpha_x(v,\cdot)=k\alpha_x$, whose restriction to $F_x$ is zero. With $v\in F_x$ and contact nondegeneracy, $v=0$.

Every $\lambda\in V^*$ gives $t_\lambda=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\lambda)B\in H^0(X,L)$ with local value $\lambda(w)$. For a basis $v_1,\ldots,v_d$ of $T_xX$ the $d+1$ vectors $w(x),\partial_{v_1}w(x),\ldots,\partial_{v_d}w(x)$ are independent: a dependence with nonzero tangent part contradicts immersion, and one with zero tangent part contradicts $w(x)\ne0$. Dualizing gives surjectivity of $V^*\to L_x\oplus(T_x^*X\otimes L_x)$, the local-frame description of $J_x^1L$, hence the first-jet evaluation $H^0(X,L)\to J_x^1L$ is surjective. Given $u\in T_xX$, prescribe a first jet with $f(x)=\alpha_x(u)$ and $df_x|_{F_x}=-d\alpha_x(u,\cdot)|_{F_x}$, extend the covector from $F_x$ to $T_xX$, and realize the jet by a global section $a$; then $K_a(x)=u$. Global contact fields therefore span $T_xX$. A global holomorphic field has a global flow for small complex time, compactness gives a common time disk, and the flows of a spanning set give surjective differential at the identity, so the orbits of the identity component are open; connectedness of $X$ leaves one orbit.

Algebraic action. For every contact Fano manifold the identity component $G=\operatorname{Aut}(X,F)^0$ is a connected linear algebraic group acting transitively. Choose $m>0$ with $-mK_X$ very ample; its functoriality embeds $\operatorname{Aut}(X)$ as the closed stabilizer of $X$ in the projective linear group, and preserving $F$ is the closed condition of stabilizing $\mathbb P(F)\subset\mathbb P(T_X)$. Holomorphic automorphisms of projective $X$ are algebraic, and the contact flows belong to this identity component, so it acts transitively.

Identification with a simple adjoint variety. Let $(X,F)$ be a smooth connected complex projective contact manifold with ample contact line $L$, and suppose a connected algebraic group $G$ acts effectively and transitively on $X$ preserving $F$. Then $\mathfrak g=\operatorname{Lie}(G)$ is simple, and contact values of fundamental vector fields define an isomorphism
$$\Phi:(X,F)\to\bigl(\mathbb P(\mathcal O_{min}(\mathfrak g)),F_{can}\bigr),$$
with $L$ the restriction of the hyperplane line bundle of $\mathbb P(\mathfrak g)$.

Proof. The contact identity $-K_X=L^{\otimes(n+1)}$ makes $X$ Fano. The action linearizes a sufficiently high anticanonical power, giving an algebraic homomorphism to the projective linear group of the resulting embedding, with trivial kernel by effectivity; in characteristic zero $G$ is a linear algebraic group. Let $R(G)$ be its connected solvable radical. The Borel fixed-point theorem gives an $R(G)$-fixed point $x_0$ on the complete $X$, and normality gives, for $r\in R(G)$ and $g\in G$, $r(gx_0)=g(g^{-1}rg)x_0=gx_0$; transitivity and effectivity force $R(G)=1$, so $G$ is semisimple.

For $D\in\mathfrak g$ let $D_X$ be the fundamental field. The quotient $L=T_X/F$ is $G$-linearized, and transitivity in characteristic zero makes evaluation of fundamental fields surjective onto $T_X$, so contact values give a surjection $\mathfrak g\otimes\mathcal O_X\to L$, $D\mapsto\theta(D_X)$. Its dual is a line subbundle of $\mathfrak g^*\otimes\mathcal O_X$ and defines the $G$-equivariant morphism
$$\Phi:X\to\mathbb P(\mathfrak g^*),\qquad \Phi^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(\mathfrak g^*)}(1)\simeq L.$$
The morphism is finite onto its image, since a positive-dimensional projective fiber would contain a curve on which $L$ has degree zero, contrary to ampleness; its image is a single closed $G$-orbit because $X$ is projective and $G$ transitive.

Choose a Borel subgroup $B=TU$ and a $B$-fixed point of $X$. The image is a $B$-stable line $\mathbb C\ell\subset\mathfrak g^*$. Decompose $\mathfrak g=\bigoplus_i\mathfrak g_i$ into simple ideals, so $\mathfrak g^*=\bigoplus_i\mathfrak g_i^*$ with each $\mathfrak g_i^*$ irreducible and $U$-fixed subspace its one-dimensional highest-weight space; the weights $\lambda_i$ are nonzero and distinct, each supported on one Cartan subalgebra. Since $U$ has no nontrivial character it fixes $\ell$, while $T$ acts on $\mathbb C\ell$ by one character, so in $\ell=\sum_i\ell_i$ every nonzero $\ell_i$ has the same weight $\lambda_i$; distinctness forces exactly one nonzero component. Thus $\mathbb C\ell$ is the highest-weight line of one simple coadjoint summand.

Let $H=\operatorname{Stab}_G(x)$ and $Q=\operatorname{Stab}_G([\ell])$. Equivariance gives $H\subset Q$, and finiteness of $\Phi$ gives $\dim H=\dim Q$. The subgroup $Q$ contains $B$, so it is parabolic and connected. A connected algebraic group is irreducible, and a closed subgroup of the same dimension equals the whole; hence $X\simeq G/H\to G/Q=\Phi(X)$ is an isomorphism sending $L$ to the hyperplane bundle. Every other simple factor acts trivially on the chosen coadjoint summand and hence on $X$, so effectivity excludes it and $\mathfrak g$ is simple.

The Killing form identifies $\mathfrak g^*$ with $\mathfrak g$ equivariantly; the highest-weight line becomes the line of a highest-root vector $e$, nilpotent, whose orbit $\mathcal O=G\cdot e$ is stable under nonzero scalars. Thus $\mathbb P(\mathcal O)=\Phi(X)$. Every nonzero nilpotent orbit is a cone by the Jacobson-Morozov triple, its projective closure contains a Borel-fixed point, necessarily the highest-weight line, so it contains $\mathbb P(\mathcal O)$ with dimension at least $\dim\mathbb P(\mathcal O)$ and equality only when the closures agree; both being cones of equal dimension, the orbits coincide. This identifies $\mathbb P(\mathcal O)$ with the unique smallest nonzero nilpotent orbit.

The given distribution is recovered on the coadjoint cone with the Kirillov-Kostant-Souriau form
$$(\operatorname{ad}^*(A)\ell)(D)=-\ell([A,D]),\qquad \omega_\ell(\operatorname{ad}^*(A)\ell,\operatorname{ad}^*(D)\ell)=\ell([A,D]).$$
With $E$ the Euler field and $\lambda=\iota_E\omega$, one has $d\lambda=\omega$ and $\lambda(E)=0$; $\lambda$ has weight one, so it descends under $\mathcal O\to\mathbb P(\mathcal O)$ to an $\mathcal O(1)$-valued one-form whose kernel is the canonical contact distribution, and $\lambda\wedge\omega^n=\frac1{n+1}\iota_E(\omega^{n+1})\ne0$ makes it nondegenerate. At $\Phi(x)=[\ell]$ choose the functional on $L_x$ representing $\ell$; with $\mathfrak h=\operatorname{Lie}(H)$, $T_xX=\mathfrak g/\mathfrak h$ and $\mathfrak h\subset\ker\ell$, so $F_x=(\ker\ell)/\mathfrak h$. Taking $A_0$ with $\operatorname{ad}^*(A_0)\ell=\ell$ (a Cartan element of weight one) gives $\lambda_\ell(\operatorname{ad}^*(D)\ell)=\ell([A_0,D])=-\ell(D)$, whose kernel on the projective tangent space is exactly $F_x$. Hence $d\Phi$ carries $F$ to $F_{can}$, and $L$ to the hyperplane bundle.

The three Picard cases. In the primitive case the symmetric-jet criterion and the algebraic-action statement give a connected transitive contact group; in the two remaining Picard cases the reduction already exhibits one. Applying the adjoint identification to the effective identity component of the contact automorphism group identifies both $F$ and $L$, proving the classification theorem in every dimension.

The two low-Picard-rank models are the adjoint varieties of $\mathfrak{sl}_{n+2}$ and $\mathfrak{sp}_{2n+2}$. For $V=\mathbb C^{n+2}$ the incidence embedding $([v],[f])\mapsto[v\otimes f]$, $f(v)=0$, is the orbit of a highest-root line, the rank-one nilpotents, with hyperplane line $\mathcal O(1,1)$; the contact value of $D\in\mathfrak{sl}(V)$ is $f(Dv)=\operatorname{tr}((v\otimes f)D)$, a multiple of the Killing pairing. For a symplectic space $(W,\sigma)$ of dimension $2n+2$ the map $[v]\mapsto[N_v]$, $N_v(w)=\sigma(v,w)v$, is the Veronese embedding under $\mathfrak{sp}(W)\simeq\operatorname{Sym}^2W$, with hyperplane line $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(W)}(2)$ and contact value $\sigma(v,Dv)=\operatorname{tr}(N_vD)$. Both preserve the given distributions.

Twistor spaces and the quaternionic-Kahler metric. Let $(M^{4m},g)$, $m\ge 2$, be a closed connected Riemannian manifold with holonomy in $\operatorname{Sp}(m)\operatorname{Sp}(1)$ and positive scalar curvature. Holonomy containment makes $g$ Einstein, $\operatorname{Ric}_g=(s/4m)g$ with $s>0$. Replacing $g$ by
$$\widehat g=\frac{s}{16m(m+2)}\,g,\qquad \operatorname{Scal}_{\widehat g}=16m(m+2),$$
leaves the Levi-Civita connection and the parallel quaternionic structure unchanged.

Twistor construction. The holonomy reduction gives a parallel rank-three bundle $Q\subset\operatorname{End}(TM)$ with imaginary-quaternion fibers; the sphere of complex structures is the twistor bundle $\pi:X=\{J\in Q:J^2=-\mathrm{id}\}\to M$. The Levi-Civita connection gives a horizontal distribution, and combining it with the standard complex structure on the sphere fibers gives the twistor complex structure. The positive twistor theorem of Salamon, in the form given by LeBrun, makes it integrable and makes $X$ a complex contact manifold of dimension $2m+1$ with horizontal contact distribution $F$ and a Kahler-Einstein metric of scalar curvature $8(m+1)(2m+1)$; the fibers are holomorphically embedded $\mathbb P^1$ with normal bundle $\mathcal O(1)^{\oplus2m}$, and the antipodal map is a free antiholomorphic involution.

$X$ is projective Fano. The total space $X$ is compact and connected. The positive Ricci form of its Kahler-Einstein metric is the curvature form of the anticanonical bundle, so Kodaira's embedding theorem makes $X$ projective with $-K_X$ ample, and GAGA makes the contact sequence algebraic. Writing $L=T_X/F$ gives $\det F=L^{\otimes m}$ and $-K_X=L^{\otimes(m+1)}$, so $L$ is ample. The Fano property gives $\pi_1(X)=0$; since the fiber $S^2$ is connected and simply connected, the homotopy sequence of $X\to M$ gives $\pi_1(M)=\pi_1(X)=0$. Kodaira vanishing gives $H^1(\mathcal O_X)=H^2(\mathcal O_X)=0$, so $\operatorname{Pic}(X)=H^2(X,\mathbb Z)$ is finitely generated and torsion-free.

Twistor fibers as transverse conics. Every point of $X$ lies on its twistor fiber. For a parametrization $u:\mathbb P^1\hookrightarrow X$ of such a fiber, horizontality makes $u^*F$ complementary to $du(T_{\mathbb P^1})$, and the normal-bundle assertion with the contact quotient gives
$$u^*F\simeq N_{u(\mathbb P^1)/X}\simeq\mathcal O(1)^{\oplus2m},\qquad \theta\,du:T_{\mathbb P^1}\xrightarrow{\sim}u^*L,\qquad u^*L\simeq\mathcal O(2),$$
so $u^*T_X\simeq\mathcal O(2)\oplus\mathcal O(1)^{\oplus2m}$. These are contact-degree-two curves transverse to $F$ through every point.

Metric recovery. The classification theorem gives a contact isomorphism from $(X,F)$ to the adjoint contact variety $Y_{\mathfrak g}$ of a simple complex Lie algebra, and identifies $L$ with the restriction of the hyperplane bundle. Wolf's correspondence identifies $Y_{\mathfrak g}$ with the complex twistor space of a compact simply connected symmetric positive quaternionic-Kahler model $(M_0,g_0)$ of real dimension $4m$. Rescaling $g_0$ to scalar curvature $16m(m+2)$, LeBrun's metric uniqueness theorem applies to the biholomorphism of the two complex twistor spaces and gives an isometry $(M,\widehat g)\simeq(M_0,\widehat g_0)$; this uses only the complex isomorphism class of the twistor space and does not require preservation of the initially chosen real structures. Undoing the two constant rescalings shows $(M,g)$ homothetic to the Wolf space $(M_0,g_0)$. Constant rescaling leaves the Levi-Civita connection and multiplies the covariant curvature tensor by a constant, so it preserves $\nabla\operatorname{Rm}=0$; hence $\nabla\operatorname{Rm}_g=0$ and the curvature of $g$ is parallel.
