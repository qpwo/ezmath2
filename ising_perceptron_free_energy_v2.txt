Fix density alpha > 0, prior nu_N the uniform probability on the cube
x in {-1,+1}^N, and M = floor(alpha N) independent standard Gaussians
g^1, ..., g^M in R^N, the patterns. The projection of a state x onto
pattern a is

    b_a(x) = N^{-1/2} sum_{i=1}^N g^a_i x_i,

standard normal for each fixed x and each a. A bounded Borel
log-potential f: R -> R weights each state by exp(sum_a f(b_a(x))),
and the pressure is

    Z_N(f) = int exp( sum_{a=1}^M f(b_a(x)) ) nu_N(dx),
    p_N(f) = N^{-1} log Z_N(f).

Counting measure in place of nu_N shifts p_N by log 2 and leaves the
limit alone. Writing f = beta phi with beta an inverse temperature, the
physical free energy per spin is -p_N/beta. The object of study is the
limiting pressure lim_N p_N(f).

The energy sum_a f(b_a(x)) is a sum of M = alpha N nonlinear functions
of correlated Gaussians; as a function of x it is not a Gaussian
process, so the Gaussian mixed p-spin Parisi formula, which requires an
energy Gaussian in x, does not evaluate the pressure. What survives is
the geometry of the winners. Two independent samples x, x' from the
reweighted Gibbs measure

    G(dx) = Z_N(f)^{-1} exp( sum_a f(b_a(x)) ) nu_N(dx)

have overlap R(x,x') = N^{-1} sum_i x_i x'_i in [-1,1]. At small
density R sits near 0; at large density G freezes into clusters whose
pairwise overlaps take very different values, and the law of R, not one
number, has to encode the pressure.

Storage in random patterns was first studied by Gardner (1988) and
Gardner and Derrida (1988) using the replica method, and Krauth and
Mezard (1989) treated the binary-coupling case. Talagrand (2000, 2002)
made the small-density case rigorous for Gaussian Ising perceptrons,
and the finite-temperature case with bounded log-potential was settled
at small density by Bolthausen, Nakajima, Sun, and Xu (2022) via
moment methods. Abbe, Li, and Sly (2021) proved the lognormal
partition-function limit for the symmetric perceptron at zero margin,
and Perkins and Xu (2024) established the frozen 1-RSB structure of
the symmetric Ising perceptron. Ding and Sun (2025), Huang (2024), and
Nakajima and Sun (2022) have settled related capacity and universality
questions. The Gaussian-pattern Ising model at finite temperature and
with a general bounded log-potential is the case treated here.

Two direct computations anchor the result. With f = 0, Z_N = 1 and
p_N = 0 identically. With f(s) = t s, set theta_i = (t/sqrt N) sum_a
g^a_i; then theta_i ~ N(0, H_N) with H_N -> H = alpha t^2 and the
state sum factors coordinate by coordinate, Z_N = prod_i cosh(theta_i),
so

    p_N -> E log cosh( sqrt(H) G ).

The general formula has to reproduce both.

Because R lies in [0,1] in the limit (a consequence of the
ultrametricity theorem, not an assumption), its law is encoded by its
nondecreasing inverse cdf

    q: (0,1) -> [0,1], law(R in [0,r]) = |{u: q(u) <= r}|.

Such a map q is an overlap path; the set of them is Q. Two degenerate
cases: q = 0 (every overlap zero, no clustering), and q = 1 (every
overlap one, one frozen cluster). Every nondecreasing path between
describes a nested tree of clusters. The pressure is a function of q,
minimized over Q.

Notation. E averages over patterns and any other randomness; angle
brackets <...> average over independent Gibbs replicas. G alone denotes
a standard normal inside an expectation summand. 1{P} is the indicator
of event P. log is natural, |.| is Lebesgue measure, and a -> b means
"a tends to b". Displays are indented two spaces.

Fix s >= 0 and 0 <= d <= 1. For a suitable function U: R -> R,

    (T_{s,d} U)(x) = d^{-1} log E exp( d U(x + sqrt(s) G) ), d > 0,
    (T_{s,0} U)(x) = E U(x + sqrt(s) G) .

Reading: add a Gaussian of variance s to the argument of U, then take
a soft maximum with stiffness d. As d -> 0 the soft maximum becomes a
plain average. Three facts, used constantly:

    (a) T is monotone: U <= W pointwise implies T U <= T W.
    (b) If U takes values in [a,b], so does T_{s,d} U.
    (c) T_{0,d} U = U.

In every application, d is one of the partition depths zeta_i defined
below and s is a variance increment along the partition. That is not
an accident: it is exactly where the tree of clusters meets the
Gaussian field.

The pattern recursion V_f

A step path is a nondecreasing function q: (0,1) -> [0,1] that is
constant on the intervals of a finite partition

    0 = zeta_0 < zeta_1 < ... < zeta_k = 1,
    w_i = zeta_{i+1} - zeta_i, q_i = value of q on (zeta_i, zeta_{i+1}).

Append q_{-1} = 0 and q_k = 1. Set U_k = f and iterate down:

    U_{i-1} = T_{q_i - q_{i-1}, zeta_i} U_i , i = k, k-1, ..., 0,
    V_f(q) = U_{-1}(0) .

The last transform (i = k) uses zeta_k = 1 and variance 1 - q_{k-1}: an
honest log-average over the part of a fresh pattern not explained by
the shared structure. Levels k-1 ... 1 each use zeta_i and variance
q_i - q_{i-1}: the field climbs one level and is reweighted with
stiffness zeta_i. Level 0 uses zeta_0 = 0, so its first transform is a
plain average over variance q_0 at the root. Three values:

    q = 0: V_f(0) = log E exp( f(G) ),
    q = 1: V_f(1) = E f(G),
    q = r constant:
        V_f(r) = E_G log E_{G'} exp f( sqrt(r) G + sqrt(1-r) G' ).

Refining a partition without changing q inserts a zero-variance
transform, so V_f does not depend on the partition. For smooth
compactly supported f, V_f is 1/2-Lipschitz times ||f'||_inf^2 in the
L^1 distance between step paths. That Lipschitz bound
gives a unique continuous extension to all of Q.

A field path is a nonnegative nondecreasing step function h: (0,1) ->
[0, infinity) on a partition, written (h_0, ..., h_{k-1}). Append
h_{-1} = 0. Set U_{k-1}(x) = log cosh(x) - h_{k-1}/2 and iterate:

    U_{i-1} = T_{h_i - h_{i-1}, zeta_i} U_i , i = k-1, ..., 0,
    ell(h) = U_{-1}(0) .

Same climb, no patterns. The terminal value log 2 cosh(x) is the
one-coordinate spin sum; the -h_{k-1}/2 is the diagonal subtraction
that cancels the diagonal of the hierarchical Gaussian field in every
interpolation below. One-level case:

    ell(H) = E log cosh( sqrt(H) G ) - H/2 .

The -H/2 is also what makes ell concave. Refining a
partition does not change ell, so ell extends continuously to every
bounded nonnegative nondecreasing field.

For q in Q,

    S_I(q) = sup over field step paths h of
               { ell(h) + (1/2) int_0^1 h(u) q(u) du } .

Reading (i), convex dual. ell is concave, so the
supremum of ell(h) plus a linear functional of h is the concave
conjugate of ell: the value that makes ell(h) + (1/2) int h q an upper
bound for all h at once. It counts how many spin configurations are
compatible with the clustering pattern q.

Reading (ii), matching. For each h there is a derivative path rho_h
with 0 <= rho_h <= 1. Then

    S_I(rho_h) = ell(h) + (1/2) int_0^1 h(u) rho_h(u) du ,

so the supremum is attained exactly when q = rho_h. Every field h
names the overlap path it produces; S_I is the upper envelope of that
family of concave functions of q. Reading (ii) is the entire strategy
of the lower bound: show that the system's field produces the
system's own overlap path, and the supremum is attained.

THEOREM (Gaussian Ising perceptron). For every alpha > 0 and every
bounded Borel f: R -> R,

    lim_{N -> infinity} E p_N(f)
      = P_f(alpha)
      := inf over q in Q of { alpha V_f(q) + S_I(q) } ,

and p_N(f) converges to P_f(alpha) in probability. The value is
finite, with

    -alpha ||f||_inf <= P_f(alpha) <= alpha log E exp( f(G) ) .

No sign condition on f, no symmetry, no concavity, no restriction on
beta when f = beta phi, and no restriction on alpha.

The bounds in the theorem are immediate from the pattern recursion V_f-8. V_f lies in
[-||f||_inf, ||f||_inf]. Also S_I >= 0 (take h = 0), S_I(0) = 0
(ell(0) = 0 and the linear term vanishes), and S_I is nondecreasing
in q. Taking q = 0 gives the upper bound alpha V_f(0); any q gives
the lower bound -alpha ||f||_inf. So the value is finite. What
remains is convergence.

The upper bound produces, for each fixed trial q, the
inequality limsup E p_N <= alpha V_f(q) + S_I(q). Minimizing over q is
then free. The lower bound cannot choose q in advance; it
must find the system's own overlap path p, read off from the replica
overlaps, and produce the reverse inequality for that p. That
asymmetry is the reason the proof is long.

Check: f constant, and f linear

f = c. Then U_i = c plus constants and V_f(q) = c for every q. Since
S_I >= 0, S_I(0) = 0, the infimum is alpha c, matching the direct
computation Z_N = exp(M c).

f(s) = t s, done completely. Affine U(x) = c + a x survives T exactly:

    T_{s,d} U(x) = c + a x + d a^2 s / 2 , d > 0,

because d^{-1} log E exp( d a (x + sqrt(s) G) ) = a x + d a^2 s / 2.
The slope a is unchanged; only the constant grows. When d = 0 the
same formula holds with the growth term dropped. In the pattern
recursion the slope stays t and constants add zeta_i t^2 (q_i -
q_{i-1}) / 2 per level. Summing with q_{-1} = 0, q_k = 1 and
summation by parts,

    sum_{i=0}^k zeta_i (q_i - q_{i-1})
      = zeta_k q_k - sum_{i=0}^{k-1} (zeta_{i+1} - zeta_i) q_i
      = 1 - int_0^1 q(u) du ,

hence V_f(q) = (t^2 / 2)(1 - int_0^1 q). Put H = alpha t^2. The
objective is

    alpha V_f(q) + S_I(q) = H/2 - (H/2) int q + S_I(q) .

Trial h = H inside S_I gives S_I(q) >= ell(H) + (H/2) int q, so every
objective is at least H/2 + ell(H). Independence of q is the first
hint that the answer is exactly H/2 + ell(H). Matching upper bound:
take the constant path

    q_* = E tanh^2( sqrt(H) G ) .

For constant h = H the chain has a single level with zeta_0 = 0, so

    rho_H = E [ U_0'(Z_0)^2 ] = E tanh^2( sqrt(H) G ) = q_* .

By reading (ii) of the Ising entropy functional the supremum defining S_I(q_*) is
attained at h = H, hence

    S_I(q_*) = ell(H) + (H/2) q_* .

Substituting, the two int q_* terms cancel:

    alpha V_f(q_*) + S_I(q_*) = H/2 + ell(H) = E log cosh( sqrt(H) G ) .

This is exactly the anchor computations(b). Every constant matters: H = alpha t^2,
the field trial is H (not H/2), and the -H/2 inside ell is what makes
the endgame cancel.

The two halves:

    UPPER. limsup E p_N <= alpha V_f(q) + S_I(q) for every trial q.
    LOWER. liminf E p_N >= min over q of { alpha V_f(q) + S_I(q) }.

Six stages, in order.

    1. Variations. Derivatives of V_f and ell in the path,
       Lipschitz bounds, a tail-order monotonicity, and concavity of
       ell with an explicit supporting inequality.

    2. Upper bound. Enrich the model with a tree label, a
       hierarchical field, a Poissonized pattern count, and a priced
       vanishing perturbation. At a deterministic minimizer of a
       comparison functional, concentration and convexity give
       fluctuation control, hence Ghirlanda-Guerra identities for the
       joint array. Ultrametricity forces the trial tree and the real
       clusters not to cross. Field and time derivatives at the
       minimizer then contradict the assumed failure.

    3. Restored marks. Delete two patterns, restore them as
       independent Gaussian marks, identify the empirical gradient
       covariance B_N as a deterministic nondecreasing function a(R) of
       the overlap. First and second moments kill the variance; a
       separate integration-by-parts gives c + a_d = int r a dmu.

    4. Cavity. Add L spins, Taylor expand, reduce the
       increment to one explicit exponential tilting against a
       Gaussian cavity field, evaluate it on a cascade.

    5. Exchangeability. Coordinate symmetry identifies the
       cavity magnetization with the bulk overlap, hence rho_h = p and
       the entropy supremum is attained.

    6. Telescoping. Sum increments over residue classes
       mod L, divide by N, let L -> infinity, then remove the
       perturbation.

Stages 1 and 6 are abstract; stages 3-5 are where the perceptron
differs from a Gaussian spin glass. Stage 2 and stage 6 do not use f
being an activation; stages 3-5 use the pattern structure three
separate times.

    1. Derivatives in the path.
    2. Tail order: spreading overlaps upward cannot raise V_f.
    3. Concavity of ell; ell(h) + (1/2) int h q is a supporting plane.
    4. Enrich with a tree and a priced perturbation; Poissonize M.
    5. Concentration and convexity give fluctuation control at the
       deterministic minimizer; hence the joint overlap identities.
    6. Utrametricity of R, T, R+T forbids crossed support; sync.
    7. Two contradictions at the minimizer: in h, in t. Upper bound.
    8. Delete two patterns, restore twice; B_N = a(R);
       c + a_d = int r a dmu.
    9. Add L spins; increment = L(ell(h) + (1/2) int p h) + d V_f.
   10. Exchangeability gives rho_h = p, so S_I(p) is attained;
       telescope mod L; remove the perturbation.

In either recursion the increments are s_i and the coefficients are
zeta_i. Define, for the Gaussian law N(x, s) of mean x and variance s,

    K_i(x, dy) = exp( zeta_i ( U_i(y) - U_{i-1}(x) ) ) N(x, s_i)(dy) .

This is a probability kernel: the normalizing identity
exp( zeta_i U_{i-1}(x) ) = E exp( zeta_i U_i(x + sqrt(s_i) G) ) is
exactly the statement that K_i(x, .) has mass one. When zeta_i = 0
the exponential is one and K_i is plain Gaussian convolution; when
s_i = 0 it is the point mass at x. The kernel defines a Markov chain:
start at Z_{-1} = 0 and sample Z_i from K_i(Z_{i-1}, .). Every path
expectation below is along that chain.

Three elementary identities. Write W = T_{s,d} U and let K be the
kernel above. Then

  (a) Spatial derivatives propagate: W'(x) = (K U')(x).
  (b) A perturbation of the terminal function propagates the same way:
      (d/d eps) T_{s,d}(U + eps A) at eps = 0 equals K A.
  (c) Heat equation: partial_s W = (1/2) { W'' + d (W')^2 }
                                = (1/2) K { U'' + d (U')^2 } .

Identity (c): for d = 1 write f_s = E exp(U(x + sqrt s G)), so
W = log f_s satisfies the heat equation and W_s = (1/2)(f_{s,xx} /
f_s) = (1/2)(W_{xx} + W_x^2). For general d apply to e^{dW} =
E exp(d U(x + sqrt s G)).

Consequence: m_i := U_i'(Z_i) is a martingale along the chain. Indeed
K_i U_i' = U_{i-1}' by (a). Hence E[U_i'(Z_i)^2] is nondecreasing in
i by conditional Jensen on the martingale.

The derivative path. For the field recursion define

    rho_h(u) = E[ U_i'(Z_i)^2 ] for u in (zeta_i, zeta_{i+1}) .

Then 0 <= rho_h <= 1 and rho_h is nondecreasing: the terminal value 1
is |tanh| <= 1, and monotonicity in i is the martingale statement.
Subdividing inserts a zero-variance transition that repeats the same
derivative and chain value, so rho_h is a well-defined overlap path:
the overlap quantile that the field h produces.

LEMMA. On a fixed partition with w_i = zeta_{i+1} - zeta_i,

    d/da V_f(q + a qdot) = -(1/2) sum_{i=0}^{k-1} w_i qdot_i E[U_i'(Z_i)^2] ,

    d/da ell(h + a hdot) = -(1/2) sum_{i=0}^{k-1} w_i hdot_i E[U_i'(Z_i)^2] ,

with all functions and chains at the current path. Endpoint
derivatives are from within the feasible segment.

Derivation, pattern case. Moving q_i upward lengthens the transition
that ends at level i and shortens the transition that leaves level i,
with the same spatial function U_i at level i. By (c) the incoming
derivative, expressed at level i, is (1/2){ U_i'' + zeta_i (U_i')^2 },
and the outgoing derivative is -(1/2){ U_i'' + zeta_{i+1} (U_i')^2 }.
Their sum is -(1/2) (zeta_{i+1} - zeta_i)(U_i')^2 = -(1/2) w_i (U_i')^2.
Identity (b) carries this back to the root, and counting the levels
gives E[U_i'(Z_i)^2]. Summing gives the formula. The field case is
identical except at the last level, where the terminal value
log cosh x - h_{k-1}/2 contributes -1/2, and

    (1/2){ (log cosh)'' + zeta_{k-1} ((log cosh)')^2 - 1 }
      = -(1 - zeta_{k-1}) (1/2) tanh^2

supplies the same formula with w_{k-1} = 1 - zeta_{k-1}. Degenerate
increments are legitimate by uniform integrability on compact
parameter sets and continuity.

COROLLARY (Lipschitz). Integrate the first variations along the segment between
two paths and use |f'| <= ||f'||_inf and |tanh| <= 1:

    |V_f(p) - V_f(q)| <= (1/2) ||f'||_inf^2 ||p - q||_1 ,
    |ell(h') - ell(h)| <= (1/2) ||h' - h||_1 .

Consequently V_f extends uniquely and continuously from step paths to
Q, and ell extends to all bounded nonnegative nondecreasing fields.
This is the extension used in the theorem.

PROPOSITION. If two paths satisfy

    int_s^1 p(u) du >= int_s^1 q(u) du for every s in [0,1],

then V_f(p) <= V_f(q).

Proof. On a common partition put a_i = w_i (p_i - q_i) and
A_j = sum_{i=j}^{k-1} a_i, so the hypothesis is A_j >= 0 for every j.
For any nonnegative nondecreasing sequence c_i, summation by parts:

    sum_{i=0}^{k-1} a_i c_i = c_0 A_0 + sum_{j=1}^{k-1} (c_j - c_{j-1}) A_j >= 0 .

Take c_i = E[U_i'(Z_i)^2] at each point of the segment from q to p.
By the derivative path this is nonnegative and nondecreasing in i. The
derivative of V_f along the segment is -(1/2) sum a_i c_i <= 0, so
V_f decreases and V_f(p) <= V_f(q). For general paths average both
on finer and finer uniform partitions and use the Lipschitz bound.

Why this is right. The hypothesis says p sits above q in tail mass,
i.e. p is spread upward. Spreading overlaps upward cannot increase
the reward: it means the good configurations are less alike, so the
patterns are answered by a less concentrated crowd of configurations.
This is the monotonicity used in the upper bound.

COROLLARY. S_I >= 0, S_I(0) = 0, S_I is nondecreasing in q, |V_f| <=
||f||_inf, V_f(0) = log E exp(f(G)). The variational infimum is
finite, and it is unchanged if one minimizes only over step paths
with top value q_{k-1} < 1.

Proof. h = 0 gives S_I >= 0. ell(0) = 0 (the terminal log cosh
averages to 0 at 0), and integrating the field variation along a ->
a h gives ell(h) <= 0. Monotonicity in q is immediate since h >= 0.
V_f(0) is the pattern recursion V_f. Bounds on V_f and S_I >= 0 give

For the reduction: take any q and let q_m on each interval of the
uniform m-partition be (1 - 1/m) times the essential infimum of q on

and inf over the restricted class is no larger than the general inf.
The reverse is trivial since the restricted class is smaller.

LEMMA (folded transition preserves monotone evenness). Let U be
even and convex with finite normalizers E exp(d U(x + sqrt(s) G)) for
all x, s >= 0, d >= 0. Let K be the tilted kernel. If A
is bounded, even, and nondecreasing in |x|, then K A has the same
three properties; and if A is bounded, even, and nonincreasing in |x|,
so is K A.

Proof. Evenness is reflection. s = 0 is immediate. For s > 0 the law
of the next absolute value y >= 0, started at x >= 0, has density
proportional to

    exp( -y^2 / (2s) ) cosh( x y / s ) exp( d U(y) ) .

For x_2 >= x_1 >= 0 the ratio of the two normalized densities is a
positive constant times cosh(x_2 y / s) / cosh(x_1 y / s). Its
logarithmic derivative in y is

    (x_2 / s) tanh( x_2 y / s ) - (x_1 / s) tanh( x_1 y / s ) >= 0,

because a -> a tanh(a y / s) is nondecreasing on [0, infinity). So the
density ratio L(y) is increasing. For independent Y, Y' with the law
at x_1, the covariance identity

    Cov(A(Y), L(Y)) = (1/2) E[(A(Y) - A(Y'))(L(Y) - L(Y'))] >= 0

holds because both factors have the same sign. Since E L(Y) = 1 this
says K A(x_2) >= K A(x_1). Negating A gives the reverse statement.
The normalizers are absorbed into L and preserve its monotonicity.

Set-up. Work in the open cone 0 < h_0 < ... < h_{k-1}. Write
m_i = U_i' and rho_i = E[m_i(Z_i)^2] by the derivative path. Because of
the first variations the matrix

    A_{ij} = w_i d rho_i / d h_j = -2 d^2 ell / d h_i d h_j

is symmetric. To be proved:

    (i) A_{ij} <= 0 for i != j,
    (ii) sum_j A_{ij} >= 0 for every i.

Given (i) and (ii), positivity is a two-line check. For any vector v,

    v^T A v = sum_i ( sum_j A_{ij} ) v_i^2 + sum_{i<j} ( -A_{ij} )( v_i - v_j )^2 >= 0 .

So A is positive semidefinite, the Hessian of ell is negative
semidefinite, and ell is concave along segments in the open cone.

First, each U_i is even and convex. Evenness is preserved by the
transforms. For convexity with d > 0 use convexity of U and Holder:
for 0 <= a <= 1,

    E exp( d U( a x + (1-a) y + sqrt(s) G ) )
      <= ( E exp( d U(x + sqrt(s) G) ) )^a
         ( E exp( d U(y + sqrt(s) G) ) )^{1-a} ;

take logarithms. For d = 0, expectation preserves convexity. The
terminal subtraction in the field recursion is constant in x. Hence
m_i is odd, m_i(x) >= 0 for x >= 0, m_i^2 is even and nondecreasing
in |x|, and all U_i have at most linear growth, so the normalizers in
are finite.

Claim (i): cross signs. Stop the propagation of the first variations at
level i. For j > i,

    d U_i(x) / d h_j = -(w_j / 2) ( K_{i+1} ... K_j m_j^2 )(x) .

By 17.1 this function is even and nonincreasing in |x|. Its derivative
is therefore <= 0 for x > 0 and >= 0 for x < 0, and m_i has opposite
sign, so the direct derivative of m_i^2 is pointwise <= 0. Remaining
terms come from differentiating the probability kernels in
rho_i = K_0 ... K_i (m_i^2). At each level l with l <= i < j, the

Then

    d log K_l(x, dy) / d h_j = zeta_l { A_l(y) - (K_l A_l)(x) } .

Indeed, d U_{l-1} / d h_j = K_l A_l by (b). Let H_l =
K_{l+1} ... K_i (m_i^2), with H_i = m_i^2. H_l is even and
nondecreasing in |x| by repeated 17.1. A_l is even and nonincreasing
in |x| by the display above. Hence the kernel derivative contributes

    zeta_l Cov_{K_l(x, .)}( H_l, A_l ) <= 0

at a fixed starting point. The l = 0 term is zero because zeta_0 = 0.
Propagation through preceding probability kernels preserves the sign.
Together with the direct derivative these are all terms in the product
rule. So (i) holds.

Claim (ii): row sums. Increase every h_i by the same a >= 0. No
increment h_i - h_{i-1} changes, and every U_i with i >= 0 changes
only by the additive constant -a/2, so no spatial derivative and no
kernel changes. The only change is that the initial variance grows
from h_0 to h_0 + a. The function reached at level i is even and
nondecreasing in |x|, so coupling the initial values as
sqrt(h_0 + a) G proves (ii).

THEOREM. ell is concave on nonnegative nondecreasing step paths
and, for any two such paths on a common partition,

    ell(h') <= ell(h) - (1/2) int_0^1 (h'(u) - h(u)) rho_h(u) du .

Concavity extends to the continuous extension on bounded nonnegative
nondecreasing fields.

Proof. 17.2 gives negative semidefinite Hessian on the open cone, so
ell is concave there and, using the first variation at the starting
point, the displayed inequality holds. For arbitrary steps h, h' on
the partition, replace h_i and h_i' by h_i + eps(i+1) and
h_i' + eps(i+1); both lie in the open cone with the same difference,
and eps -> 0 with continuity of the first variations gives the closed cone.
Common refinement handles distinct partitions. Bounded paths follow
by L^1 approximation and the Lipschitz bound.

What it buys. Taking h' = h + a phi and differentiating at a = 0
recovers the first variation. Taking h' equal to any trial field
shows that h maximizes ell(h') + (1/2) int h' rho_h over h'. Hence

    S_I(rho_h) = ell(h) + (1/2) int_0^1 h(u) rho_h(u) du ,

and the supremum defining S_I is attained at h whenever the trial
overlap path equals rho_h. Equivalently, h/2 lies in the subgradient
of the concave functional S_I at rho_h. This is what the cavity
calculation of the cavity step makes true for the model itself.

This part builds the array-probabilistic machinery. Everything here is
proved from scratch. The lemmas are used in the upper bound 
via Ghirlanda-Guerra identities, and in the bulk restoration 
and cavity increment via marked cascades and array limits.

An array B = (B_lm)_{l,m>=1} is symmetric if B_lm = B_ml for all l, m,
weakly exchangeable if the law is invariant under every finite
permutation of the indices, and a Gram array if every finite restriction
(B_lm)_{l,m<=n} is positive semidefinite. These properties pass to
limits in distribution of finite restrictions. Bounded arrays admit
such subsequential limits by compactness of [a,b]^n and diagonal
extraction.

DEFINITION. Let B be a weakly exchangeable array with values in a
compact space, and let mu = law(B_12). B has the Ghirlanda-Guerra
(GG) property if, for every n >= 2,

  law( B_{1,n+1} | (B_lm)_{l,m <= n} )
    = (1/n) mu + (1/n) sum_{l=2}^n delta(B_1l), (GG)

or, equivalently, if for every bounded measurable F of the first n×n
block and every bounded measurable psi,

  E[F psi(B_{1,n+1})]
    = (1/n) E[F] E[psi(B_12)] + (1/n) sum_{l=2}^n E[F psi(B_1l)] .

The same definition applies to a joint array, with B_lm a tuple of
entries.

./ghirlanda-guerra-1998.pdf

Two immediate consequences used without comment. It suffices to
verify (GG) for continuous F, psi: the equality then identifies the
finite measures and holds for all bounded measurable tests. The GG
property passes to weak limits on a compact entry space. A
measurable entrywise image of a joint array retains the property, with
conditioning on its own first block, by the tower rule.

Reading, discrete case. If B takes finitely many scalar off-diagonal
values 0 = q_0 < ... < q_{k-1} < 1 (say), then conditionally on the
first n×n block, B_{1,n+1} equals one of the existing values B_{1l}
(l = 2, ..., n) with total probability (n-1)/n, and equals a fresh
draw from mu with probability 1/n. So the (n+1)-th replica either
joins an existing cluster (at level B_{1l}) or opens a new cluster
(level from mu). That is exactly the sampling rule of a cascade.

THEOREM. Suppose B is a bounded, symmetric, weakly exchangeable Gram
array with constant diagonal D and the GG property. Then almost
surely, for distinct indices,

  B_lm >= 0 and B_12 >= min( B_13, B_23 ) .

This is the single result that turns "GG identities" into a
geometric tree structure. The proof is due to Panchenko (2013),
included because both halves of the main theorem use it.

./panchenko-2013.pdf

Positivity. First suppose b = P(B_12 < -eps) > 0 for some eps > 0.
Let A_n be the event that all distinct pairs among the first n indices
have B < -eps. Suppose A_n has positive probability. Conditional on
the first n×n block, GG gives B_{1,n+1} a mixture (1/n)mu +
(1/n)sum_{l=2}^n delta(B_1l). The delta part lies below -eps, so
P(B_{1,n+1} >= -eps | block) = (1/n) P(mu >= -eps) = (1-b)/n. A union
bound over the n fresh links gives

  P( at least one of B_{1,n+1}, ..., B_{n,n+1} >= -eps | block )
    <= n * (1-b)/n = 1 - b .

So P(A_{n+1} | A_n) >= b, hence P(A_n) >= b^{n-1} > 0 for every n.

Fix n large. On A_n the quadratic form of the n×n block on the
all-ones vector is

  sum_{l,m} B_lm = n D + sum_{l != m} B_lm <= n D - n(n-1) eps .

For n > 1 + D/eps this is negative, contradicting positive
semidefiniteness. So P(A_n) = 0 for large n, contradicting
P(A_n) >= b^{n-1} > 0. Therefore b = 0 for every eps > 0. Taking a
countable sequence eps -> 0 proves B_lm >= 0 almost surely.

Duplication. Fix n >= 2 and suppose (a_lm)_{l,m <= n} is in the
topological support of the law of the first n×n block. Fix
b_* > max_{l < n} a_ln. Then there exists a supported extension to
n+1 with

  a_{i, n+1} = a_in for i < n, and a_{n, n+1} <= max_{l < n} a_ln .

Proof. Fix a positive-probability neighborhood A_0 of the first
(n-1)x(n-1) block (a_lm)_{l,m <= n-1}, chosen small enough that the
prescribed values of a_ln (for l < n) are strictly below b_*. Say an
index j >= n matches if its links to 1, ..., n-1 all fall in the
neighborhoods prescribed for n. Thus A = A_0 ∩ {n matches} has
positive probability.

Suppose, for contradiction, that a.s. on A, no fresh j >= n both
matches and satisfies B_{nj} < b_*.

Let W_B be the limiting frequency over j >= n of {j matches} and W the
frequency of j > n with B_{nj} >= b_*. Both frequencies exist in L^2:
for an exchangeable indicator sequence the empirical averages are
L^2-Cauchy by the standard second-moment calculation, and the limit is
permutation-invariant. On A, the supposition and exchangeability give
W_B <= W.

Put b = P(B_12 >= b_*). Since A restricts B_1n < b_* with positive
probability and B_1n has the same law as B_12, b < 1 holds.

Now compute. Conditional on A (so the first n×n block is fixed in a
small neighborhood and B_ln < b_* for l < n), a fresh j > n has, by
GG,

  P(B_{nj} >= b_* | A, block)
    = (1/n) P(mu >= b_*) + (1/n) sum_{l=2}^n 1{B_nl >= b_*}
    = b/n ,

where the delta part is zero because B_nl < b_* for l < n and
l = n contributes D but the neighborhood can be chosen small enough
that b_* > a_{ln} for all l < n and D is at most max so also
excluded; when a_ln < b_* < D the l = n term is 0 as well). After j
successive "high links" B_{n, j_r} >= b_* (j_1 < j_2 < ... < j_j),
conditioning on the enlarged block (which includes those j indices and
their links to n) and using GG with block size n+j gives

  P(B_{n, j_{j+1}} >= b_* | previous )
    = (b + j - 1) / (n + j - 1) .

Indeed, the n+j-1 block has j "high" links to n (contributing j) and
mu has mass b at >= b_*; the other delta terms are zero by the
neighborhood choice.

Telescoping, for every d >= 1,

  E[ 1_A W^d ]
    = P(A) prod_{r=0}^{d-1} (b + r) / (n + r) .

Binomial series. For 0 < z < 1,

  E[ 1_A (1 + z W)^{-n} ]
    = sum_{d>=0} (-1)^d binom(n+d-1, d) z^d E[ 1_A W^d ]
    = P(A) sum_{d>=0} (-1)^d binom(n+d-1, d) z^d
        prod_{r=0}^{d-1} (b+r)/(n+r)
    = P(A) sum_{d>=0} (-1)^d (b)_d z^d / d!
    = P(A) (1 + z)^{-b} ,

using (b)_d / d! * (n)_d / (n)_d = (b)_d / d! and the binomial expansion
of (1+z)^{-b}. Initially valid on 0 < z < 1; both sides are real
analytic on (0, infinity), the left by boundedness of W and local
power-series domination, so the identity holds on the whole half-line.

Upper bound. Let I_j = 1_{j matches}. The event A_0 uses only the first
n-1 indices, and W_B is unchanged by finite permutations of the
remaining indices, including n. Hence for every bounded continuous phi,

  E[ 1_{A_0} I_n phi(W_B) ]
    = E[ 1_{A_0} (1/M) sum_{j=n}^{n+M-1} I_j phi(W_B) ]
      -> E[ 1_{A_0} W_B phi(W_B) ] as M -> infinity,

using L^2 convergence of W_B and invariance of the frequency under
adding index n. Take phi(x) = (1+zx)^{-n}. On A, W_B <= W, and the map
y -> (1+zy)^{-n} is decreasing, so

  E[ 1_A (1+zW)^{-n} ]
    >= E[ 1_A (1+zW_B)^{-n} ]
    = E[ 1_{A_0} W_B (1+zW_B)^{-n} ]
    <= E[ 1_{A_0} 1/z ]
    = P(A_0)/z ,

where the last bound uses x/(1+zx)^n <= 1/z for x in [0,1], z > 0,
n >= 1: since 1 + zx >= zx and n >= 1, (1+zx)^n >= 1+zx >= zx, so
x/(1+zx)^n <= x/(zx) = 1/z.

Combine. For every 0 < z < infinity,

  P(A) (1+z)^{-b} <= P(A_0) / z .

As z -> infinity, (1+z)^{-b} ~ z^{-b}. If b < 1 then z^{-b} >> z^{-1},
so the left side dominates for large z, contradicting the inequality.
Since b < 1 was established, contradiction. So the assumption was
false: there exists a supported extension with all the required
properties. Shrinking the neighborhoods and decreasing b_* to its
target value, by compactness of the finite-block support, proves the
duplication claim. Permutation symmetry permits duplication of any
chosen index.

Ultrametricity. If ultrametricity failed, a supported triple with
a_12 < a_13 <= a_23 exists (possibly at the boundary; shrink
neighborhoods). Repeated duplication as in 19.2 makes three groups
I_1, I_2, I_3 each of size m, preserving cross-group entries
a_12, a_13, a_23 and keeping within-group entries at most the largest
cross-group entry of that group. The latter property holds
inductively: when duplicating index n to n+1, the duplication gives
a_{n, n+1} <= max_{l < n} a_ln, and every a_ln either lies across two
different groups (bounded by the corresponding cross entry) or lies
within the group of n (bounded by induction).

For group k with largest cross-group entry e_k = max_{k' != k} a_{k k'},
within-group entries are at most e_k. Group 1 has e_1 = a_13. Group 2
has e_2 = a_23 (because a_12 < a_13 <= a_23, so a_23 > a_12). Group 3
has e_3 = a_23 (because a_13 <= a_23).

Represent the resulting finite Gram matrix by vectors and let
u_1, u_2, u_3 be their group averages. Then

  ||u_2||^2, ||u_3||^2 <= D/m + (1 - 1/m) a_23,
  u_2 . u_3 = a_23,
  ||u_1||^2 <= D (trivially).

Therefore

  ||u_2 - u_3||^2 = ||u_2||^2 + ||u_3||^2 - 2 u_2 . u_3
                  <= 2(D/m + (1 - 1/m) a_23) - 2 a_23
                   = 2(D - a_23)/m -> 0 .

On the other hand,

  u_1 . (u_3 - u_2) = a_13 - a_12 > 0

because all cross-group 1-2 entries are exactly a_12 and all 1-3 entries
are exactly a_13. Cauchy-Schwarz then gives

  a_13 - a_12 = u_1 . (u_3 - u_2) <= ||u_1|| ||u_3 - u_2||
              <= sqrt(D) sqrt(2 (D - a_23) / m) -> 0 ,

a contradiction. So no such triple exists. Applying exchangeability to
all triples completes the proof. Ultrametricity of any supported point
implies ultrametricity almost surely (shrink neighborhoods, use closed
support).

Fix levels 0 = zeta_0 < zeta_1 < ... < zeta_k = 1, w_i = zeta_{i+1} -
zeta_i. Build a rooted tree of depth k-1: at each vertex of depth
i-1 (i = 1, ..., k-1), the outgoing edge weights are the points of an
independent Poisson process on (0, infinity) with intensity
zeta_i x^{-1-zeta_i} dx. Children may be indexed in decreasing order of
those points. If u_gamma is the product of the edge weights on the
path to leaf gamma, define

  T = sum_gamma u_gamma, v_gamma = u_gamma / T .

For depth zero there is one leaf and T = v_gamma = 1. Write
gamma ^ eta for the depth of the deepest common vertex of two leaves
(it ranges from 0 to k-1, including when the leaves are equal). The
shape of sampled labels records their nested ancestral partitions
without the numerical child indices.

LEMMA (cascade law). The total T is almost surely finite and positive
with E[(log T)^2] < infinity. For replicas sampled from (v_gamma), the
common-level array satisfies the GG property and

  P( gamma^1 ^ gamma^2 = i ) = w_i , 0 <= i <= k-1 . (level)

Conversely, a weakly exchangeable ultrametric scalar array with the GG
property and finitely many off-diagonal values has the same off-diagonal
law as such a cascade, with the levels replaced by its ordered values
and with w_i their marginal probabilities. Values of zero probability
are omitted.

Ruelle (1987) introduced this cascade construction and computed its
two-replica overlap law; the converse, from an ultrametric GG array to
the cascade law, is the ultrametricity theorem of Panchenko (2013).

https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01210613
./panchenko-2013.pdf

Poisson facts. Three facts about the intensity C x^{-1-b} dx on
(0, infinity), 0 < b < 1.

(a) Image under multiplicative marks. If X is a positive random variable
with 0 < E X^b < infinity and independent of the process, then
multiplying each point by X produces a process of intensity
C E X^b x^{-1-b} dx, and after this change the attached mark is biased
by X^b independently of the image point. Proof: substitute y = xX in
the marked intensity (Poisson mapping from disjoint sets).

(b) Total sum. The sum S of the points has Laplace transform

  E exp( -t S ) = exp( -c t^b ), c = C Gamma(1-b)/b > 0 .

Proof: Poisson exponential formula gives the exponent
-C int_0^infty (1 - e^{-tx}) x^{-1-b} dx = -c t^b.

Consequently, for 0 < r < b, int (1 - E e^{-tS}) t^{-1-r} dt gives
E S^r < infinity; and E S^{-r} = (1/Gamma(r)) int_0^infty t^{r-1}
exp(-c t^b) dt < infinity for every r > 0. These imply E (log S)^2 <
infinity.

(c) Descendant totals. Summing a tree from its last level upward: the
descendant total at a vertex has intensity exponent strictly bigger
than that of the incoming edge, so every required positive fractional
moment exists. Hence the tree total T is finite and positive a.s.

At a parent, let b be the exponent on its outgoing edges. Multiply
each edge point by its descendant total. The resulting child masses
are Poisson points of intensity C x^{-1-b} dx, and their attached
normalized descendant trees are independent of the points and biased
by the descendant total to power b. If the parent tree itself is
biased by its total to a power lambda < b, only the point process is
further biased by lambda-th power of its sum, and the conditional
descendant description is unchanged. At the root lambda = 0, and
thereafter lambda is the incoming exponent.

Partition law. Denote unbiased child masses by (X_j) and their sum
by S. Under the bias S^lambda, the probability of a specified partition
of m visits into d nonempty child classes of sizes m_1, ..., m_d is

  p_lambda(m_1, ..., m_d)
    = [ E ( S^{-m+lambda} sum_{j_1, ..., j_d distinct} prod_r X_{j_r}^{m_r} ) ]
      / E S^lambda
    = [ prod_r C Gamma(m_r - b) ] / [ Gamma(m - lambda) E S^lambda ]
      * int_0^infty t^{db - lambda - 1} e^{-c t^b} dt .

Proof: insert the Laplace integral for S^{-m+lambda}, use the Poisson
exponential formula with d distinct point insertions. Each insertion
contributes C int_0^infty x^{m_r-b-1} e^{-tx} dx =
C Gamma(m_r - b) t^{b - m_r}. The insertion rule itself follows by
truncating to a finite-intensity process and applying Tonelli to the
nonnegative integrands.

The ratio of the partition law after and before a visit to an existing
class r is

  (m_r - b) / (m - lambda) .

The complementary probability of creating a new child is
(db - lambda) / (m - lambda). Observed deeper shapes do not alter these
ratios, because the attached descendant trees are independent of the
child masses.

Along an existing class at level i (exponent zeta_i), these ratios
telescope: after n replicas the probability that the next replica joins
that class is

  (m_* - zeta_i) / n ,

where m_* is the current size of that class. At level 0 the class is
the whole tree and the probability is one. With one previous replica
the formula gives P(gamma^1 ^ gamma^2 >= i) = 1 - zeta_i, proving
(level). With n previous replicas, applying it to the class of
replica 1 gives exactly (GG) for upper-threshold tests, hence for every
test on the finite set of levels.

Uniqueness. Conversely, the upper-threshold relations of a finite
ultrametric array are nested equivalence relations. Conditional on its
first n indices, GG gives P( gamma^1 ^ gamma^2 >= i | block ) = 1 -
zeta_i for each existing class, using a representative of the class.
These probabilities specify the distribution of the deepest existing
class joined by the new index, and therefore of all its new pair
entries. They coincide with the cascade probabilities. Induction on n,
starting from the prescribed two-replica law, proves uniqueness.

The overlap sampling law does not by itself determine observables of
fresh Gaussian fields after reweighting. For smooth f, restoring a
pattern changes the sampled values of f'. What is needed is the joint
transformation of cascade weights and marks.

Draw root data independently of the Poisson edge processes. Conditional
on an ancestor's marked path, give its children independent marks with
a common probability kernel, independently of the edge points. These
kernels may depend on the marked path and its depth, but not on
numerical child indices or Poisson weights. A shared Gaussian field
with covariance levels 0 <= b_0 <= ... <= b_{k-1} is obtained by a
common root increment of variance b_0 and independent vertex increments
of variances b_i - b_{i-1}.

LEMMA (marked cascade transform). Let X_{k-1} be a terminal log factor
depending on the marked path. For i >= 1 let E_i integrate the original
level-i child-mark kernel, conditional on the marked ancestor path
through level i-1, without averaging the Poisson weights or root data.
Define

  X_{i-1} = (1/zeta_i) log E_i exp( zeta_i X_i ), i = k-1, ..., 1 .

Assume these transforms are finite (the bounded log factors and the
Gaussian log factors with at most linear growth below satisfy this).
For Z = sum_gamma v_gamma exp( X_{k-1}(gamma) ),

  E[ log Z | root data ] = X_0 ,
  E[ (log Z - X_0)^2 | root data ] <= 4 E[ (log T)^2 ] .

After reweighting by exp(X_{k-1}) and relabeling children, the joint law
of normalized weights and marks is generated by: draw an ordinary
unmarked cascade, independently of the root data, and draw marks on its
vertices with kernels of densities

  exp( zeta_i (X_i - X_{i-1}) ), 1 <= i <= k-1,

relative to the original kernels, independently on distinct children
given ancestor marks. Root data retain their original law. Sampling
reweighted leaves preserves the shape law; conditioning on the shape
leaves exactly the tilted mark kernels on sampled branches. Independent
mark components remain independent under these kernels when the
original kernels factor and the terminal log factor is a sum of the
separate component log factors.

Proof. At every edge from level i-1 to i, multiply its Poisson point by
exp(X_i - X_{i-1}) and reorder children. By the recursion, the
conditional zeta_i-th moment of this multiplier is one. The marked
Poisson mapping (the Poisson intensity facts(a)) therefore leaves the intensity
unchanged and changes the attached mark kernel exactly to the tilted
one stated.

The independence assertion is proved from the last level upward.
Inductively, conditional on the path to a child, its transformed
unmarked subtree has the ordinary unmarked law, independently of that
path; its marks follow the transformed descendant kernels. Attach the
entire transformed subtree as part of the child's mark in the mapping
at its parent. The multiplier depends only on the path through that
child, so the mapping tilts the child kernel while keeping this
conditional subtree law. At the root the transformed unmarked tree has
its original law independently of root data.

Products of the edge multipliers telescope. If T^* is the transformed
raw total, then Z = exp(X_0) T^* / T. Both T and T^* have the same
conditional law given root data, with finite squared logarithms by
the Poisson intensity facts. Taking conditional expectation of the logarithm gives the
first identity; the elementary square bound gives the second. No
independence between T and T^* is asserted or needed.

The reweighted normalized masses are precisely the transformed masses,
up to relabeling that preserves ancestry. Sampling them gives the
ordinary cascade shape law. Conditional on the transformed unmarked
tree, its mark kernels have the description just proved; sampling or
conditioning on a shape does not further tilt them. Finally, factoring
the expectations in the recursion for an additive terminal log factor
shows that the transforms add and the tilted component kernels factor.

Terminal variance and repeated visits. Suppose a Gaussian mark has
true diagonal variance D and shared off-diagonal levels b_i, with
b_{k-1} <= D. The cascade represents the shared field y_gamma. On each
replica visit introduce an independent residual Gaussian of variance
D - b_{k-1}. Thus, for a positive leaf factor A, the leaf factor in the
cascade recursion is

  Abar(y) = E_eta A( y + sqrt(D - b_{k-1}) eta ) .

After reweighting, a visited residual has density
A(y + sqrt(D - b_{k-1}) eta) / Abar(y) relative to its standard
Gaussian law, independently on different visits conditional on their
shared marks. This is an ordinary coefficient-one tilt. Two replicas
visiting the same leaf still use two independent residuals. Giving a
leaf one permanent residual would incorrectly raise their off-diagonal
covariance from b_{k-1} to D. These statements also hold for
independent vector residuals and for additional finite-spin variables
integrated in the leaf factor. The residual prescription realizes
exactly the final diagonal term in the Gram decomposition of ordered quantization.


LEMMA (ordered quantization with the original diagonal). Let B be
nonnegative and ultrametric off the diagonal, with constant diagonal D
and entries at most D. Applying any nonnegative nondecreasing
finite-valued map to its off-diagonal entries, with image in [0,D], and
retaining diagonal D, produces a Gram array. If B has the GG property,
its quantized off-diagonal law is the finite cascade law.

More generally, suppose finitely many desired off-diagonal covariance
entries are bounded nonnegative nondecreasing functions a_j(r) of the
scalar entries r = B_lm, l != m, with a_j(r) <= D_j. One can choose
finite ordered bins of r that approximate r and every a_j(r)
uniformly on their domain, with errors tending to zero, while keeping
each covariance diagonal equal to D_j. All the resulting covariance
arrays are Gram. The binning depends only on r, so it never splits an
atom of the scalar overlap law.

Proof. Let the distinct quantized values be c_0 < ... < c_s. For a
finite restriction, let A^{(j)} be the matrix with entries 1 when two
indices are in the same class at level c_j (and diagonal 1). Each A^{(j)}
is positive semidefinite: its quadratic form is a sum over classes of
the squared sum of the coordinates in that class. The quantized matrix
equals

  Q = c_0 1 1^T + sum_{j=1}^s (c_j - c_{j-1}) A^{(j)} + (D - c_s) I .

Every coefficient is nonnegative, so Q is positive semidefinite. The GG
property passes through the quantization map by the tower rule, so
the cascade partition law identifies its law.

For the joint assertion, bin r and each a_j(r) on meshes of size eps and
take their common refinement. Each coordinate is nondecreasing, so
nonempty joint bins are totally ordered; choosing one actual vector
(r, a_1(r), ..., a_d(r)) in each bin gives ordered representatives and
error at most eps in each coordinate. Use the decomposition above
separately for each coordinate, with its own diagonal D_j. The bins
depend only on r, including at atoms. If the functions are specified
only on a full-measure ordered domain, the construction there suffices
for every sampled entry.

LEMMA (uniqueness and bounded-Lipschitz continuity). For fixed diagonal
D, the law of a nonnegative ultrametric GG array with values in [0,D]
is determined by its two-replica marginal. Such a law exists for every
probability marginal on [0,D]. If marginal quantiles q_m, q satisfy
||q_m - q||_{L^1(0,1)} -> 0, then the laws of every fixed finite replica
block converge in bounded-Lipschitz distance.

Proof. Uniqueness: round two candidate arrays on finer and finer finite
meshes. Their rounded marginals coincide, so the cascade partition law gives identical
rounded array laws. Uniformly vanishing rounding error identifies their
original finite-block laws.

Existence: approximate the desired quantile by ordered step functions,
use finite cascades, extract a limit of the bounded arrays. Gram
property from the quantized decomposition; ultrametricity and GG pass
to the limit.

Continuity: if q_m -> q in L^1, their marginals converge weakly, and
coupling them by the same uniform variable bounds the Wasserstein
distance by ||q_m - q||_1. Any subsequential limit of the array laws
has GG, is nonnegative and ultrametric, and has this limiting marginal.
Uniqueness identifies it, proving convergence of the whole sequence.
Weak convergence on the compact space of finite blocks is equivalent to
convergence in bounded-Lipschitz distance; no rate is needed.

This is the technical workhorse that lets us pass through array limits
even for reweighted and log-normalized expectations. Its point: on
finitely many replicas, each object that must be computed is a
bounded continuous function of the array entries and of finitely many
fresh Gaussian fields, so it passes to the limit of the base
probability. The reweighted and log versions are handled by polynomial
approximation.

LEMMA. Let a sequence of random base probabilities P_N have jointly
convergent arrays of bounded observables and of the conditional
covariances of finitely many fresh centered Gaussian fields. The
fields are sampled independently of P_N given its data, with any
specified independence between components. Then the following averages
converge, as determined by those limiting arrays:

  (i) bounded continuous functions of the listed observable and field
      evaluations on finitely many base replicas;

  (ii) the same tests after reweighting by a fixed continuous function
       of the listed evaluations at one replica, bounded between two
       fixed positive constants;

  (iii) the expected logarithm of the normalizing integral of that
        factor.

Independent finite-spin averages may be included. The assertion remains
valid at singular limiting covariance matrices. If the limiting
off-diagonal covariance entries are ordered functions of a scalar
ultrametric GG array, its evaluations can be obtained by the quantized
cascades of ordered quantization, retaining the true diagonal variances and using
the residual prescription of the terminal-variance correction, provided the base
observables and tests used are also approximated by these bins in
probability on every finite replica restriction. This condition
includes constants and continuous tests of the scalar overlap and of
the quantized covariance coordinates.

Proof. On any fixed number of base replicas, the conditional joint
Gaussian law is a continuous function of its covariance matrix, also on
the boundary of the positive semidefinite cone. For example, couple it
as C^{1/2} g; the nonnegative matrix square root is continuous and the
Gaussian vector g has fixed dimension. Bounded continuous tests
therefore pass to the joint array limit.

Write the positive reweighting factor as A, and let Z = P(A) for base
probability P. If 0 < c <= A <= C, then c <= Z <= C. An r-replica
reweighted test has expectation

  E[ Z^{-r} P^{otimes r}( F prod_{l=1}^r A_l ) ] .

Uniformly approximate z^{-r} on [c, C] by polynomials. Each monomial
Z^j is represented by j additional base replicas, evaluated in the same
fresh fields. The resulting terms are finite-replica tests covered by
(i), and uniform approximation controls the error. Approximating log z
on the same interval proves the logarithmic assertion. Finite-spin
variables are simply additional independent base variables.

Quantize all ordered covariance coordinates jointly. Their
finite-replica matrices converge by ordered quantization, and the other
observables and tests converge by the additional approximation
hypothesis. Gaussian continuity and the same polynomial argument
therefore apply. The shared Gaussian construction with independent
per-visit residuals has exactly those matrices, including on repeated
leaf visits. The cascade partition law identifies the quantized shape
law, and the marked-cascade transformation evaluates its bounded reweightings. Unbounded
factors require an additional uniform-integrability argument; the
cavity calculation supplies the specific truncation
estimate it needs.

LEMMA. Let P be a probability reference measure, possibly random but
independent of the Gaussian fields under consideration. For independent
centered Gaussian energies with covariances C_0, C_1, interpolate
H_t = sqrt(1-t) H_0 + sqrt(t) H_1 and put Delta = C_1 - C_0. Then

  d/dt E log P(e^{H_t}) = (1/2) E < Delta_11 - Delta_12 >_t .

For a bounded r-replica test F independent of these fields,

  d/dt E < F >_t
    = (1/2) E < F ( sum_{a,b=1}^r Delta_ab
        - 2r sum_{a=1}^r Delta_{a,r+1}
        + r(r+1) Delta_{r+1,r+2}
        - r Delta_{r+1,r+1} ) >_t ,

whose absolute value is at most (2 r^2 + r) ||F||_inf ||Delta||_inf.

If an independent centered Gaussian field Y of covariance C enters the
energy with coefficient s, Gaussian integration by parts gives

  E < Y(1) F >
    = s E < F ( sum_{a=1}^r C_{1a} - r C_{1,r+1} ) > .

These formulas hold for finite spin spaces with countably many cascade
labels whenever the covariances are bounded at the fixed system size.
They can be integrated up to the endpoints of covariance segments.

Proof. For finitely many states, differentiate the coupling H_t and
apply ordinary Gaussian integration by parts. This contracts one half
of the energy Hessian with Delta. A derivative of a normalized r-replica
integral inserts the sum over its r sampled states minus r times one
extra replica; a second derivative and contraction give the second
display. The sum of absolute coefficients of the replica derivative,
including the factor 1/2, is 2 r^2 + r. The first derivative of the log
partition gives the log formula; a single integration by parts gives
the insertion.

Countable labels. Truncate to increasing finite label sets chosen
independently of the added fields, renormalize reference weights, and
use the following elementary Gaussian moment bounds. If a Gaussian
energy has pointwise variance at most K, then, conditionally on its
independent probability reference,

  E Z^p <= exp(p^2 K / 2), E Z^{-p} <= exp(p^2 K / 2),
  Z = P(e^H), p >= 1 .

The first inequality is Jensen for x^p; the second uses [P(e^H)]^{-p}
<= P(e^{-pH}). Cauchy-Schwarz and the same single-state moments control
polynomial Gaussian insertions, and exponentials of sums of absolute
values of finitely many such fields. Thus Gibbs derivatives, their
Gaussian insertions, and their logarithms have uniformly integrable
bounds on compact coefficient sets. The truncated integrals converge
almost surely and in the necessary moments (bounded pattern log factors
are absorbed into the reference; if retained explicitly, their
exponential costs are integrable for a fixed count or an independent
Poisson count).

Integrate the finite-state covariance derivative first and pass to the
limit using its uniform bound. Gaussian coupling and the same moment
bounds give continuity at segment endpoints. Ordinary energy parameter
derivatives and the insertion pass to the limit by the
polynomial-insertion bounds.

LEMMA. Three elementary bounds.

  (V1) A function of finitely many standard Gaussian variables with
       Euclidean Lipschitz constant A has variance at most A^2.

  (V2) A function of independent inputs whose value changes by at most
       c_i when input i is replaced has variance at most sum_i c_i^2.

  (V3) If a: Z -> R has Lipschitz constant c and K is Poisson with mean
       lambda, then Var(a(K)) <= c^2 lambda.

Proof. (V1): For smooth f, interpolate two standard Gaussian copies
X_s, Y_s from independent (s = 0) to identical (s = 1) with cross
covariance s I. Differentiate E f(X_s) f(Y_s) in s; Gaussian
integration by parts turns the derivative into E grad f(X_s) . grad
f(Y_s). Integrating s from 0 to 1 and bounding by A^2 proves
Var(f) <= A^2; truncation and smoothing extend to Lipschitz.

(V2): Reveal inputs successively. The function is a sum of martingale
differences plus its mean; each difference has variance at most c_i^2
by the conditional Jensen inequality. Sum the variances.

(V3): Let K' be an independent Poisson copy of K. Then

  Var(a(K)) = (1/2) E[(a(K) - a(K'))^2]
            <= (c^2/2) E[(K - K')^2] = c^2 lambda,

because Var(K - K') = 2 lambda. Done.

Fix a step path q on 0 = zeta_0 < ... < zeta_k = 1 with q_{k-1} < 1
and S_I(q) < infinity. This is the comparison trial path. The
goal is

  limsup E p_N <= alpha V_f(q) + S_I(q) .

Minimising over q is then free by the statement of the main theorem. An indirect
argument: if the inequality fails, the enriched model is forced to
disclose its overlap structure, and two clean derivative estimates
contradict the disclosure.

Enrich the Gibbs measure by a cascade label gamma of depth k-1
(the cascade partition law): leaf weights v_gamma, common level gamma^eta in
{0, ..., k-1}. The reference probability on pairs (x, gamma) is
nu_N(dx) v_gamma.

Add a hierarchical Gaussian magnetic field. Take N independent
centered Gaussian fields z_1, ..., z_N on the tree, each with covariance
h_{gamma^eta} at two leaves gamma, eta. This is constructed from a
common root increment of variance h_0 and independent vertex increments
of variances h_i - h_{i-1} for i = 1, ..., k-1. Add the energy

  z(gamma) . x - (N/2) h_{k-1} .

The subtraction cancels the diagonal of the hierarchical field.

Replace the deterministic pattern count M_N by a Poisson count K with
mean N t, 0 <= t <= alpha. The K patterns are independent standard
Gaussians.

Add the vanishing perturbation. Enumerate the monomials
kappa_j(R,T) = R^p T^d with p, d >= 0, p+d >= 1, by a fixed index j.
Take independent centered Gaussian fields Y_j(x, gamma) with covariances
E[Y_j(x,gamma) Y_j(x',gamma')] = kappa_j(R(x,x'), T(gamma,gamma')).
An explicit construction contracts a standard Gaussian tensor with
(x/sqrt N)^{tensor p} for the R^p factor and uses tree-increment
square roots for the T^d factor. Set e_N = N^{-1/16} and add

  sqrt(N) e_N sum_{j=1}^N 2^{-j} u_j Y_j(x, gamma), u in [1,2]^N.

The covariance of the perturbation is at most C N e_N^2 since each
kappa_j has absolute value at most 1. By the Gaussian Gibbs calculus, turning it on or
off changes E P_N by at most C e_N^2, uniformly in t, h, u. At the
energy scale, however, the coefficient sqrt(N) e_N = N^{7/16} of each
fixed Y_j is large.

Let P_N(t, h, u) = N^{-1} log Z_N(t, h, u) denote the pressure of the
enriched model, and F_N(t, h, u) = -E P_N(t, h, u). Every parameter is
kept deterministic inside the expectation.

Initial value. At t = 0 with the perturbation removed, spin integration
at a leaf gives prod_{a=1}^N cosh(z_a(gamma)) times the diagonal
subtraction. Applying the marked-cascade transformation with the leaf log factor
sum_a (log cosh z_a) - N h_{k-1}/2 and propagating through the
recursion, the recursion value at the root is X_0 = N ell(h) exactly,
because the scalar recursion sum decomposes as a sum over the N
independent coordinates. Hence

  E P_N(0, h, no perturbation) = ell(h) .

With the perturbation present, E P_N(0, h, u) = ell(h) + O(e_N^2) and
F_N(0, h, u) = -ell(h) + O(e_N^2).

Label law. With patterns and perturbation present, apply the marked-cascade transformation to
the complete spin log integral at each leaf. Conditional on patterns,
Poisson count, and common root Gaussian variables, the remaining vertex
marks satisfy the conditional independence required there. Hence the
sampled label shape has the original cascade law:

  E < 1_{ gamma^1 ^ gamma^2 = i } > = w_i for every N, t, h, u.

LEMMA. For each fixed H < infinity there is a constant C_H (also
depending on the partition, alpha, f) such that

  Var( P_N(t, h, u) ) <= C_H / N

whenever 0 <= t <= alpha, 0 <= h_0 <= ... <= h_{k-1} <= H, u in
[1,2]^N.

Proof. Write L_N = N P_N. Put the patterns, their count, and all common
root Gaussian variables into the root data. Apply the marked-cascade transformation to the
leaf spin log integral. If X_0 is the resulting recursion value at the
root, the marked-cascade transformation gives

  E[L_N | root data] = X_0,
  E[(L_N - X_0)^2 | root data] <= C_zeta .

The second assertion also follows directly: L_N - X_0 is the
logarithmic difference between the transformed and original
unnormalized cascade totals; both totals have the original conditional
law, so their squared logarithms are controlled by the cascade
constants. No independence between the two is needed.

Conditional on K, the coefficient vector of the common Gaussian
variables in the energy at any fixed state has norm at most C_H sqrt(N):
its squared norm is the sum of common variances, bounded by N H plus
the perturbation. The spin log integral is 1-Lipschitz in those
variables (a difference of log integrals is bounded by the supremum of
the difference of energies), and every log-transform in the recursion
preserves a uniform change bound. Therefore X_0 has Gaussian variance
at most C_H N by (V1).

Replacing one of the K patterns changes X_0 by at most 2 ||f||_inf,
because this bound holds at every leaf and is preserved by each
transform. The independent-input bound (V2) adds at most C K to the
variance.

The conditional mean E[X_0 | K = m] changes by at most ||f||_inf
between m and m+1, by adding one pattern under a coupling. For an
independent Poisson copy K', (V2) for integer-valued functions bounds
the variance of this conditional mean by ||f||_inf^2 E[(K - K')^2]/2 =
||f||_inf^2 N t.

Combining independent blocks: Var(L_N) = O_H(N). Dividing by N^2
gives Var(P_N) <= C_H / N.

Field and time derivatives of F_N

LEMMA. F_N is continuous on the parameter region above. For every
nonnegative nondecreasing direction lambda on the fixed partition, its
right directional derivative in h is

  partial_{h, lambda} F_N = (1/2) E < R_12 lambda_{gamma^1 ^ gamma^2} > .

For t > 0, its time derivative is

  partial_t F_N = -E log < exp f(ghat . x / sqrt N) >_N ,

where ghat is an independent standard Gaussian pattern, and <...>_N is
the Gibbs expectation in dimension N+1 (bulk + one pattern).

Proof. Field. Increasing h by a lambda adds to the hierarchical field a
Gaussian energy with covariance a N R(x,x') lambda_{gamma^1 ^ gamma^2},
and simultaneously changes the deterministic subtraction to
-N(h_{k-1} + a lambda_{k-1})/2, i.e. adds -Na lambda_{k-1}/2 to the log
partition. Apply the Gaussian Gibbs calculus to the Gaussian energy:

  d/da E log Z_N = (1/2) E < a N lambda_{k-1} - a N R_12 lambda_{gamma^1 ^ gamma^2} >
                    - N a lambda_{k-1} / 2 .

At a = 0 the first and last terms cancel, leaving

  d/da E log Z_N = -(1/2) N a E < R_12 lambda_{gamma^1 ^ gamma^2} >
                    -> -(1/2) N E < R_12 lambda_{gamma^1 ^ gamma^2} > as a -> 0 .

Dividing by N and reversing the sign (since F_N = -E P_N) proves the
field derivative.

Time. Poisson differentiation gives, at fixed (h, u, kappa_j),

  d/dt E log Z_N = N E[ log Z_N(K+1) - log Z_N(K) ] .

Adding a pattern to K multiplies the unnormalized partition function of
every state by exp f(ghat . x / sqrt N) with ghat a fresh standard
Gaussian; hence log (Z_N(K+1) / Z_N(K)) = log < exp f(ghat . x /
sqrt N) >_K. Differentiating the Poisson series is justified by
|f| <= ||f||_inf. Dividing by N and reversing the sign gives the time
derivative.

Continuity. In t by coupling Poisson counts; in h by coupling vertex
increments and the Gaussian interpolation estimates, including at zero
increments; in u by ordinary Gaussian-coupling differentiation. The
countable label set is handled by field-independent finite truncations
and the moment bounds of the Gaussian Gibbs calculus.

LEMMA. For the fixed partition,

  ell(h) <= (E|G|) sqrt(h_0) + C_zeta - (1/2) sum_{i=0}^{k-1} w_i h_i ,

  C_zeta = sum_{i=1}^{k-1} (log 2) / zeta_i (zero if k = 1).

In particular for sufficiently large fixed H, every sufficiently large
dimension along a violating sequence has a minimizer of the comparison
functional of the two-derivative contradiction below with t > 0 and h_{k-1} < H.

Proof of the growth bound. From log cosh x <= |x| and |x+y| <= |x|+|y|,
the bound T_{s,d}(|.| + c)(x) <= |x| + c + d s / 2 + (log 2)/d holds
for d > 0, since e^{d|x + sqrt(s)G|} <= e^{d|x|}(e^{d sqrt(s) G} +
e^{-d sqrt(s) G}) and E of the bracket is 2 e^{d^2 s/2}. Propagate this
bound through levels k-1, ..., 1 and average |sqrt(h_0) G| at the root.

  -h_{k-1}/2 + (1/2) sum_{i=1}^{k-1} zeta_i (h_i - h_{i-1})
    = -(1/2) sum_{i=0}^{k-1} w_i h_i

gives the bound with C_zeta as stated.

Coercivity. Adding the patterns changes pressure by at most
alpha ||f||_inf in expectation, and the perturbation costs O(e_N^2).
Therefore F_N(t, h, u) >= -ell(h) - alpha ||f||_inf - O(e_N^2). Since
S_I(q) >= 0, |V_f(q)| <= ||f||_inf, and the quadratic penalty is
nonnegative, on the face h_{k-1} = H the comparison functional J_N of
the two-derivative contradiction is at least

  w_{k-1}(1 - q_{k-1}) H / 2 - (E|G|) sqrt(H) - C_zeta
    - 2 alpha ||f||_inf - O(e_N^2) .

The linear coefficient w_{k-1}(1 - q_{k-1})/2 is strictly positive
because q_{k-1} < 1. Choose H large enough that this lower bound is
positive for all large N along the violating sequence.

Define the comparison functional, for the same fixed trial (q, h):

  J_N(t, h, u)
    = F_N(t, h, u)
      - (1/2) sum_{i=0}^{k-1} w_i q_i h_i
      + S_I(q)
      + t { V_f(q) + delta }
      + sum_{j=1}^N 2^{-j} (u_j - 3/2)^2 .

This is continuous on the compact parameter region

  D = { 0 <= t <= alpha, 0 <= h_0 <= ... <= h_{k-1} <= H, u in [1,2]^N } ,

so it attains a minimum. Its structure is chosen for three reasons:
F_N carries the model pressure; -(1/2) sum w_i q_i h_i cancels the
linear part of ell(h) so that minimality in h at a contact point gives
exactly the comparison against q; t { V_f + delta } carries the
density derivative; and the quadratic u-penalty with scale 2^{-j}
defeats fluctuations while vanishing in the final limit.

Suppose the claimed upper bound failed. Then for some eta > 0 and an
infinite sequence of dimensions,

  E p_N >= S_I(q) + alpha V_f(q) + eta .

Couple the floor(alpha N) patterns to a Poisson count of mean N alpha
by using the same initial rows. The expected pressure change is at most

  (||f||_inf / N) E|K - floor(alpha N)| = O(N^{-1/2}) .

Choose delta > 0 with alpha delta < eta/4.

LEMMA. For sufficiently large fixed H, along the violating sequence
every sufficiently large N has a minimizer (t_N, h_N, u_N) of J_N with
t_N > 0, h_{N, k-1} < H, and for every fixed j,

  |u_{N,j} - 3/2| <= C_j e_N , sum_j 2^{-j} (u_{N,j} - 3/2)^2 = O(e_N^2) .

Proof. The face t = 0. By the initial-value identity,
F_N(0, h, u) >= -ell(h) - O(e_N^2). By reading (ii) of the Ising entropy functional, the
entropy dominates every trial field: for the same h on this partition,

  S_I(q) >= ell(h) + (1/2) sum_i w_i q_i h_i = ell(h) + (1/2) int_0^1 h q .

Therefore

  J_N(0, h, u)
    = F_N(0, h, u) - (1/2) sum_i w_i q_i h_i + S_I(q) + penalty
    >= -ell(h) - O(e_N^2) - (1/2) int_0^1 q h
       + ell(h) + (1/2) int_0^1 h q
    = -O(e_N^2) ,

and the quadratic penalty is nonnegative, so J_N >= -O(e_N^2) on the
whole t = 0 face.

The face h_{k-1} = H was handled in the field-coercivity lemma: J_N >= positive on all
of it for large H and large N.

The candidate (t = alpha, h = 0, u = 3/2) gives J_N <= -eta/2 for
sufficiently large N, by the Poisson coupling, the choice of delta,
and the perturbation bound. Indeed, F_N(alpha, 0, u) at u = 3/2 equals
the pressure of the original model at equilibrium plus O(e_N^2) minus
alpha ||f||_inf - alpha V_f(0) - alpha delta; combining with the
violating lower bound and the interior-value bound gives the claimed
-eta/2.

Therefore for large N the minimizer lies on neither the t = 0 nor the
h_{k-1} = H face. Interior minimality in u_j, together with the fact
that u_j = 3/2 is a global minimum of the quadratic penalty, gives
the displayed estimates.

Fix j and hold all other coordinates at the minimizer. Write

  g(v) = E P_N( t_N, h_N, u_{N, 1}, ..., u_{N, j-1}, v, u_{N,j+1}, ..., u_{N,N} ) ,
  a_{N,j} = sqrt(N) e_N 2^{-j} .

The energy is affine in u_j with coefficient a_{N,j} Y_j, because
Y_j is a Gaussian field on (x, gamma) with variance kappa_j(R,T)
(linearly scaled by a_{N,j}).

Three facts. First, P_N is convex in u_j because it is a log integral
of an energy affine in that coordinate. Second, ordinary Gibbs
differentiation gives

  d P_N / d u_j = a_{N,j} < Y_j > ,
  g''(u_{N,j}) = a_{N,j}^2 E< (Y_j - <Y_j>)^2 > .

Third, interior minimality in u_j gives

  g'(u_{N,j}) = 2 * 2^{-j} (u_{N,j} - 3/2) ,
  g''(u_{N,j}) <= 2 * 2^{-j} .

The second displayed identity holds on an interval on which the other
coordinates are fixed and u_j is in (1, 2); since u_{N,j} is interior,
such an interval exists.

LEMMA (thermal fluctuation). E< |Y_j - <Y_j>| > <= C_j N^{-7/16}.

Proof. From the two derivative identities above, a_{N,j}^2
E< (Y_j - <Y_j>)^2 > <= 2 * 2^{-j}. Since a_{N,j}^2 = N e_N^2 4^{-j},

  E< (Y_j - <Y_j>)^2 > <= 2 * 2^{-j} / (N e_N^2 4^{-j})
                        = 2 * 2^j / (N e_N^2) .

Taking square roots and using Cauchy-Schwarz bounds the first moment by
C_j 2^{j/2} / (sqrt(N) e_N) = C_j N^{-7/16}, using e_N = N^{-1/16}.

LEMMA (disorder fluctuation). E< | <Y_j> - E <Y_j> | > = o( sqrt(N) e_N ).

Proof. For a differentiable convex random function its derivative at a
point lies between the backward and forward difference quotients. Apply
this to P_N with step s and subtract g'(u_{N,j}). The mean quotient
error is at most 2 * 2^{-j} s by interior minimality. Replacing each of
the three random function values by its expectation costs at most
C_H / (s sqrt(N)) in L^1 by Var(P_N) <= C_H / N. Hence

  E| d P_N / d u_j - g'(u_{N,j}) |
    <= C_j s + C_H / (s sqrt(N)) .

Take s = N^{-1/4} (allowed by the u_j penalty bound u_{N,j} = 3/2 +
O_j(e_N)). The right side is then O_j(N^{-1/4}). Also g'(u_{N,j}) =
a_{N,j} E<Y_j>, since differentiating g = E P_N inside the Gibbs
average commutes with the u_j derivative. Dividing the display by
a_{N,j} = sqrt(N) e_N 2^{-j} = N^{7/16} 2^{-j} gives

  E| <Y_j> - E <Y_j> | <= (C_j N^{-1/4}) / (N^{7/16} 2^{-j})
                          = C_j 2^j N^{-11/16}
                          = o( sqrt(N) e_N ) .

Combining with the thermal bound,

  E< | Y_j - E <Y_j> | >
    <= E< |Y_j - <Y_j>| > + E< |<Y_j> - E<Y_j>| >
    <= C_j N^{-7/16} + C_j N^{-11/16}
     = O_j( N^{-7/16} )
     = o( sqrt(N) e_N ) ,
which is exactly the fluctuation bound of the contact-point mechanism.
Every statement above concerns one fixed j at a time. No uniformity in
j is used or claimed.

Joint Ghirlanda-Guerra identities for (R, T)

Fix j and n >= 2. Let D be a bounded continuous function of the first
n×n joint overlap block (R_lm, T_lm). Gaussian integration by parts in
the field Y_j, whose two-replica covariance is kappa_j(R_12, T_12),
gives

  E< Y_j(1) D >
    = a_{N,j} u_{N,j} E< D ( sum_{l=1}^n kappa_j(R_1l, T_1l)
                             - n kappa_j(R_{1,n+1}, T_{1,n+1}) ) > .

The first sum runs over the n replicas of the block, including the
diagonal term l = 1, which contributes kappa_j(1, (k-1)/k) because
R_11 = 1 and T_11 = (k-1)/k. The second piece is the n-fold
counterpart in a fresh replica n+1. The construction of the
overlaps uses only x and gamma, never the Gaussian coordinates of Y_j,
so the Gaussian Gibbs calculus applies in this conditional setting.

By the fluctuation estimate of the contact-point fluctuation control, the left side equals
E<Y_j> E<D> + o(sqrt(N) e_N). The single-replica version of the same
integration by parts reads

  E< Y_j > = a_{N,j} u_{N,j}
    E< kappa_j(1, (k-1)/k) - kappa_j(R_12, T_12) > .

Substitute, cancel the constant diagonal, and divide by n a_{N,j}
u_{N,j}. The result is

  E< D kappa_j(R_{1,n+1}, T_{1,n+1}) >
    = (1/n) E<D> E< kappa_j(R_12, T_12) >
      + (1/n) sum_{l=2}^n E< D kappa_j(R_1l, T_1l) > + o(1) .

The remainder is the fluctuation error: the left side of the IBP
identity above exceeds E<Y_j> E<D> by at most o(sqrt(N) e_N), and the
single-replica identity identified E<Y_j>/(a_{N,j} u_{N,j}). Dividing
by n a_{N,j} u_{N,j}, whose value is n N^{7/16} 2^{-j} u_{N,j}, sends
that error to o(2^j). For each fixed j, 2^j is a constant, so the
result is o(1). Every quantifier below concerns one fixed j.

Uniformity and limit. The identity holds for every fixed monomial
kappa_j = R^p T^d. By linearity it holds for finite combinations, and
by uniform polynomial approximation on the compact set R in [0,1],
T in {0, 1/k, ..., (k-1)/k} it holds for every bounded continuous
psi(R_12, T_12). A bounded continuous function of the (n+1)-st
replica's links to the block is a weak limit of such combinations.
Therefore, passing to a joint array subsequence that exists by
compactness,

  E< D psi(R_1,n+1, T_1,n+1) >
    = (1/n) E<D> E< psi(R_12, T_12) >
      + (1/n) sum_{l=2}^n E< D psi(R_1l, T_1l) > .

Continuous tests of the first (n+1)×(n+1) joint block determine its
measure, so the identity holds with psi replaced by any bounded
measurable function and D by any bounded measurable function of the
first n×n block. This is the joint Ghirlanda-Guerra property for the
array (R, T) in the sense of the Ghirlanda-Guerra property.

Propagation to a good parameter set. The subsequence and polynomial
arguments above are performed for one fixed deterministic parameter

set of parameters, the good parameter sets are used of the bulk restoration: on those

Synchronization. In any joint array limit from the joint Ghirlanda-Guerra identities, the
two-replica law of (R_12, T_12) is supported on a set ordered in both
coordinates. If p is the quantile of R_12, the conditional law of R_12
given T_12 = i/k is the law of p(U) for U uniform on (zeta_i,
zeta_{i+1}).

Proof. R is a bounded symmetric exchangeable Gram array with constant
diagonal 1 and the GG property (inherited from the joint array by
marginalization). T is a deterministic-valued Gram array: its diagonal
is (k-1)/k and it is ultrametric by construction. R + T is a Gram array
with constant diagonal 1 + (k-1)/k, and the joint GG property gives the
scalar GG property for R + T after applying the continuous map
(r, t) -> r + t; the two-replica marginal of R + T is the pushforward
of the joint marginal.

By positivity and ultrametricity applied to R, T, R + T separately, all three arrays are
ultrametric and nonnegative off the diagonal.

Suppose the two-replica support of (R_12, T_12) contained two strictly
crossed points: (r, t) and (r', t') with r < r' and t > t'. Choose
small product neighborhoods A = I_r × J_t and B = I_{r'} × J_{t'},
disjoint in the sense that every point of A has strictly smaller first
and strictly larger second coordinate than every point of B. Both A and
B have positive two-replica probability.

Apply the label law's label law with the joint GG property. For n = 2,

  P( (R_12, T_12) in A, (R_13, T_13) in B )
    >= (1/2) P( (R_12, T_12) in A ) P( (R_12, T_12) in B ) > 0 .

On this event, ultrametricity of R forces R_23 = R_12: the three
inequalities R_12 >= min(R_13, R_23), R_13 >= min(R_12, R_23) and
R_23 >= min(R_12, R_13) applied to the ordering R_12 < R_13 (which
holds on the event A ∩ B up to the neighborhoods) give R_23 = R_12.
Identically, ultrametricity of T forces T_23 = T_13. Therefore
R_23 + T_23 = R_12 + T_13 < R_12 + T_12 and also R_23 + T_23 < R_13 +
T_13. Ultrametricity of R + T forbids this strict separation. Contradiction.

Therefore no strictly crossed pair in the support exists, and the
support is ordered in both coordinates. Choose a version of the
conditional law of R_12 given T_12 = i/k that is monotone in i. Such a
choice gives an ordered coupling by the standard construction on the
real line. The mixture over i with masses w_i reorders into blocks of
lengths w_i, and on each block the quantile is p evaluated on
(zeta_i, zeta_{i+1}).

The field contradiction. Minimality of J_N in h at the minimizer
(t_N, h_N, u_N) implies that the right directional derivative of J_N
in every nonnegative nondecreasing direction lambda vanishes from
above. By the field and time derivatives the h-derivative of F_N is (1/2) E <R_12
lambda_{gamma^1^gamma^2}>. Grouping the linear terms in J_N gives, for
every such lambda,

  (1/2) E < R_12 lambda_{gamma^1^gamma^2} >
    >= (1/2) E < q(lambda_{gamma^1^gamma^2}) > , (*)

Now evaluate both expectations. By the ordered-support argument, conditional on
gamma^1 ^ gamma^2 = i, the spin overlap R_12 has the law of p(U) with
U uniform on (zeta_i, zeta_{i+1}). Since lambda equals lambda_i there,

  E < R_12 lambda_{gamma^1^gamma^2} >
    = sum_i lambda_i E < R_12 1_{gamma^1^gamma^2 = i} >
    = sum_i lambda_i w_i (1/w_i) int_{zeta_i}^{zeta_{i+1}} p(u) du
    = int_0^1 lambda(u) p(u) du .

Likewise sum_i w_i q_i lambda_i = int_0^1 lambda(u) q(u) du, because
lambda and q are both step functions with jump partition zeta. So (*)
reads

  int_0^1 lambda(u) p(u) du >= int_0^1 lambda(u) q(u) du
    for every nonnegative nondecreasing step lambda on this partition.

Taking lambda = 1_{u>s} for each s in [0,1] gives the tail inequality

  int_s^1 p(u) du >= int_s^1 q(u) du for every s.

By the tail-order property of continuity and tail order, V_f(p) <= V_f(q).

The time contradiction. Minimality of J_N in t at the minimizer
gives d/dt J_N |_{t_N} <= 0, since t_N > 0 and a small leftward
displacement is feasible. On the other hand, by the field and time derivatives the
t-derivative of F_N is -E log <exp f(ghat . x / sqrt N)> at the current
state, and the derivative of the linear term t{V_f(q)+delta} is
V_f(q)+delta.

Now the fresh pattern in that expectation has covariance R with unit
diagonal. By the array limit of bounded reweighting of arrays, applied to the marginal
limiting spin-overlap array p, the fresh-pattern expectation converges:
there is a bias or fresh-pattern contribution whose limit is the
fresh-pattern functional V_f(p) (defined by the same pattern recursion
evaluated at the overlap quantile p). Precisely, the Gaussian Gibbs calculus's
fresh-pattern evaluation gives

  E log < exp f( ghat . x / sqrt N ) > -> V_f(p) ,

along the extracted subsequence, where p is the quantile of the
limiting spin-overlap law. The quadratic u-penalty contributes 0 to
the t-derivative.

Therefore

  d/dt J_N |_{t_N}
    = - E log < exp f(ghat . x / sqrt N) > + V_f(q) + delta + o(1)
    -> - V_f(p) + V_f(q) + delta .

Combining with V_f(p) <= V_f(q) from the field-derivative comparison:

  lim inf d/dt J_N |_{t_N} >= delta > 0 .

This contradicts the required sign d/dt J_N |_{t_N} <= 0. The
contradiction shows that the violating lower bound in the comparison functional was
false. Hence

  limsup E p_N <= alpha V_f(q) + S_I(q)

on every subsequence of dimensions, for every fixed finite-entropy
trial path q with q_{k-1} < 1. Minimising over such q, then using
the finite-trial reduction to remove the q_{k-1} < 1 restriction, gives

  limsup E p_N <= inf over q in Q of { alpha V_f(q) + S_I(q) }
                = P_f(alpha) .

This is the upper half of the theorem.

The upper bound is complete. The lower bound is the rest of the
argument. It cannot choose an overlap path in advance: it has to read the
model's own overlap path p and its own field h off the bulk, and then

the bulk gradient covariances B_N and C_N as deterministic functions of the
overlap. The cavity step uses that to build the cavity increment. The
general-activation extension finishes the theorem by removing smoothness.

The perturbation used here is a separate one from the upper bound.
Its scale is s_N = N^{3/8}, and its per-coordinate amplitude is

Delete the last two patterns. Retain the perturbation with parameters

Denote pi the uniform probability on [1,2]^N.

Two exact finite-size facts. First, differentiation in v_j gives

  partial_{v_j} P_N^circ = (t_j / N) < Y_j >_circ .
  partial_{v_j} E P_N^circ = (t_j^2 v_j / N)(1 - E < R_12^j >_circ) ,
  partial_{v_j}^2 E P_N^circ = (t_j^2 / N) E < (Y_j - <Y_j>_circ)^2 >_circ ,

where R_12^j is the overlap in the pattern field j, and the middle

because it is the expectation of a squared centered quantity. The
first derivative is bounded in absolute value by C t_j^2 / N on

Integrating the third identity from v_j = 1 to v_j = 2 and bounding
the increment of the second identity by C t_j^2 / N gives

  int_1^2 E < (Y_j - <Y_j>_circ)^2 >_circ dv_j <= C . (T1)

The constant does not depend on j or on the other coordinates.

Second, convexity in v_j. Because P_N^circ is a log-integral of an
energy affine in v_j, it is convex as a random function. Bounding the
forward and backward difference quotients of P_N^circ around v_j by
its curvature, using the comparison functional's concentration Var(P_N^circ) <=
C_H / N and the first and third identities, gives for any 0 < eta < 1:

  int_1^2 E | partial_{v_j} P_N^circ - partial_{v_j} E P_N^circ | dv_j
    <= C_j eta t_j^2 / N + C / (eta sqrt N) . (T2)

Choose eta = N^{-1/8}. Since t_j = N^{3/8} 2^{-j} and hence
t_j^2 / N = N^{-1/4} 2^{-2j}, both terms are O_j(N^{-3/8}).

Now define the good set. Let J_N = floor(log_2 N) and
a_{j,N}(v) = E < |Y_j - E < Y_j >_circ | >_circ. Put

  eps_N = sum_{j <= J_N} int a_{j,N}(v) pi(dv) ,
  delta_N = sqrt(eps_N) + 1 / N ,
  G_N = { v in [1,2]^N : sum_{j <= J_N} a_{j,N}(v) <= delta_N } .

LEMMA. pi(G_N) -> 1.

Proof. Markov: pi(G_N) >= 1 - delta_N^{-1} eps_N. With the choice
delta_N = sqrt(eps_N) + 1/N gives delta_N^{-1} eps_N <= sqrt(eps_N).
So it suffices that eps_N -> 0.

The two integral bounds (T1) and (T2) supply exactly this. Recall that
partial_{v_j} P_N^circ = (t_j / N) < Y_j >_circ, so the convex
fluctuation estimate (T2) controls the integrated deviation of the

Restoring the two patterns preserves the overlap law

Start with the omitted system on a good parameter value v(N) in G_N.
Its overlap array satisfies the GG identities by the good-parameter-set lemma, so by
the earlier array theorems, it is nonnegative, ultrametric, and realized by a finite
cascade. Restore the last two patterns b_1*, b_2*.

Conditional on the omitted system, b_1* and b_2* are independent fresh
Gaussian fields on R^N with covariance matrix (R(x,x')) and unit
diagonal. If f is bounded and |f| <= ||f||, then the reweighting
factor exp(f(b_1*) + f(b_2*)) is bounded above and away from zero:

  e^{-2||f||} <= exp( f(b_1*) + f(b_2*) ) <= e^{2||f||} .

Let A = exp(f(b_1*)+f(b_2*)), Z = <...>_circ-integral of A. Then
e^{-2||f||} <= Z <= e^{2||f||}. An r-replica reweighted expectation has
the form

  E[ Z^{-r} (P^circ)^{tensor r} ( F prod_{l=1}^r A_l ) ] ,

where F is a bounded continuous test of the first n first-block
replicas, and (b_l*, x^l) are the fresh fields and configurations of
each restored visit. Uniformly approximate z^{-r} by polynomials on
[e^{-2||f||}, e^{2||f||}]. Each monomial Z^j is represented by j extra
base replicas, all evaluated in the same fresh fields. The resulting
terms are finite-replica tests of the omitted array, so they pass to
the limit by the good-parameter-set lemma's GG property. The uniform approximation
controls the error. The logarithmic case is handled by approximating
log z on the same interval.

Therefore the full system's limiting overlap law equals the omitted
system's. In particular, if p denotes the quantile of the limiting
overlap law, p is the same for the full and omitted systems whenever the
omitted sequence is good.

First and second moments of the restored marks

Fix a rounding of the overlap law into levels and work under the marked
cascade of the marked-cascade transformation. The restored pattern b_1* is a Gaussian field
with covariance R; its leaf factor after reweighting is a marked
quantity. Write M_i for the tilted conditional mean of f'(b_1*) at a
leaf at level i under the kernels of the marked-cascade transformation, given the mark path
through that level. The construction also supplies an independent final
residual of variance a_d - h_top on each visit; do not
share one residual between two visits.

Then, writing d_i for the tilted conditional mean of the product
f'(b_1*(x^1)) f'(b_1*(x^2)) given common level i,

  d_i = E[ M_i^2 ], 0 <= d_i <= d_{i+1} <= d_d <= ||f'||_inf^2 .

Monotonicity is the martingale property of conditional means. The
single-visit tilted mean of f'^2 is the diagonal d_d.

The same computation applied to g(z) = f''(z) - z f'(z) gives a
diagonal-only quantity e, the tilted mean of g, with |e| <= ||f''|| +
||f'|| times the field magnitude, uniformly bounded on the compact range
of b. Averaging the tilted mark path over the root, whose law is

Two continuations below the common level use conditionally independent
kernels. Independence across components gives product

B_N and C_N as deterministic functions of the overlap

Recall the empirical gradient covariance and dilation correction,

  B_N(x,x') = N^{-1} sum_a f'(b_a(x)) f'(b_a(x')) ,
  C_N(x) = N^{-1} sum_a ( f''(b_a(x)) - b_a(x) f'(b_a(x)) ) .

Pattern exchangeability in the full system, for bounded continuous D,
gives

  E< D(R_12) B_N(x^1,x^2) > = (M_N/N) E< D(R_12) A_{M_N} > ,
  E< B_N(x^1,x^2)^2 >
    = (M_N(M_N-1)/N^2) E< A_{M_N-1} A_{M_N} > + O(N^{-1}) ,

with A_a = f'(b_a(x^1)) f'(b_a(x^2)). The repeated-row term is
O(N^{-1}) because A_a is uniformly bounded.

Restoring the two patterns and using the marked moments of the restored-mark moment calculation,
the expressions pass to the limit:

  E[ D(R_12) B_12 ] = int D(r) a(r) mu(dr) ,
  E[ B_12^2 ] = int a(r)^2 mu(dr) ,

where mu = law(R_12) has quantile p, and a(r) is built from the limit
of the d_m via ordered quantization. Continuous tests determine the conditional
mean, so

  E[ B_12 | R_12 ] = a(R_12) .

Subtracting the second moment of that conditional mean from E[B_12^2]
gives

  E[ (B_12 - a(R_12))^2 ] = 0 ,

hence B_lm = a(R_lm) almost surely for l != m. The identical first- and
second-moment computation applied to f'(b_a(x^1))^2 and to
g(b_a(x^1)) proves that B_ll = a_d and C_l = c almost surely. Replica
exchangeability and a countable intersection give all the assertions

  B_lm = a(R_lm) (l != m), B_ll = a_d, C_l = c,
  0 <= a <= a_d <= alpha ||f'||_inf^2

simultaneously. The bounds are the limits of 0 <= d_m <= d_d <=
||f'||_inf^2 and the fact that M_N/N -> alpha. No continuity of a at an
overlap atom is used.

Gaussian integration by parts in the coordinates of one pattern row,
including the derivative of the normalized Gibbs density, gives

  E< b_a(x^1) f'(b_a(x^1)) >
    = E< f''(b_a(x^1)) + f'(b_a(x^1))^2
         - R_12 f'(b_a(x^1)) f'(b_a(x^2)) > .

The perturbation is independent of that row and contributes no
derivative. Summing over a and dividing by N gives the exact finite-size
identity

  E< C_N(x^1) + B_N(x^1,x^1) > = E< R_12 B_N(x^1,x^2) > .

Passing to the joint array limit of the array-limit extraction,

  c + a_d = int_0^1 r a(r) mu(dr) = int_0^1 p(u) h(u) du ,

where p is the quantile of mu and h = a composed with p. This is the
identity that makes the diagonal pieces of the cavity increment
assemble into the entropy term. It is used in the cavity step.

Now the lower bound. Work on a good parameter value v(N) in G_N so
that the omitted system has the GG identity and the
constants a, a_d, c of the array-limit extraction are deterministic. Pass to a joint
array subsequence so that all the good-parameter and array-limit quantities converge.
Let mu be the omitted system's overlap law with quantile p; set
h(u) = a(p(u)). The goal is

  liminf_N (F_{N+L} - F_N) >= L { S_I(p) + alpha V_f(p) } - ||f||_inf

for any fixed L; then L -> infinity recovers the lower bound.

Add L fresh spins. Write a spin in dimension N+L as (x, z), with
x in {-1,+1}^N and z in {-1,+1}^L. A pattern vector in dimension N+L
is (g^a, y_a); the first M rows are the old patterns extended by fresh
Gaussian y_a in R^L, and there are d = M_{N+L} - M_N fresh rows, with
|d - alpha L| <= 1.

For the old rows, the projection is

  b_a^+(x, z) = ( g^a . x + y_a . z ) / sqrt(N + L)
              = lambda_N b_a(x) + y_a . z / sqrt(N+L) ,

  lambda_N = sqrt(N/(N+L)) .

Expand f at lambda_N b_a(x) and shift by y_a . z / sqrt(N+L) through
second order. The third-order remainder is bounded by C_L N^{-3/2}
|y_a|^3, and replacing denominators sqrt(N+L), N+L by sqrt(N), N adds

the M_N = O(N) old rows gives total expected uniform error O_L(N^{-1/2}).

Each fresh row a > M can be replaced by f(b_a(x)) with expected uniform
cost O_L(N^{-1} + N^{-1/2} E|y_a|); there are only d = O_L(1) of them,
so this is o(1) in total.

Up to a remainder whose supremum has expected size o(1), the energy is

  sum_a f(b_a(x)) + H^v_N(x) + log W(x,z) + Q(x,z) ,

where H^v_N is the perturbation energy of the original dimension (of

  W(x,z) = exp( z.Y(x) + (L/2)C_N(x) + sum_{a=M+1}^{M+d} f(b_a(x)) ) ,
  Y(x) = N^{-1/2} sum_{a=1}^M f'(b_a(x)) y_a ,

Conditional on the bulk data, Y(x) is a vector Gaussian field with
covariance B_N in each of its L coordinates, independent of the d new
bulk pattern fields. The deterministic quadratic part
L sum_a f''(b_a(x)) / (2N) together with the dilation term is exactly
(L/2) C_N(x).

Put G_0 = G_N^v x nu_L, the bulk measure times the independent cavity

  G_0(W) = G_N^v( exp(A(x)) prod_{i=1}^L cosh(Y_i(x)) )
          >= exp(-C_L) =: kappa_L > 0 .

This bound concerns the untilted probability. It makes every

Q need not be uniformly small over all spin states, but its Gaussian

the variables y_a . z are independent N(0, L). So

  E_y Q(x,z)^2 <= C_L / N ,
  E_y exp( t |Q(x,z)| ) <= C_{L,t} for every t > 0 ,

uniformly for large N. To check the second, put c_a = f''(b_a(x))/(2N).
For fixed t and large N,

  log E_y exp( t c_a ( (y_a.z)^2 - L ) )
    = -t L c_a - (1/2) log( 1 - 2 t L c_a ) = O_{L,t}(c_a^2) .

The linear terms cancel, and summing over a is O_{L,t}(N^{-1}).
Using exp(t|Q|) <= exp(tQ) + exp(-tQ) proves the claim.

The linear energy z.Y(x) has Gaussian moment bound
E_y exp( t z.Y(x) ) <= C_{L,t}, because its conditional variance is
L B_N(x,x) <= C_L.

Now remove Q. Write P = G_0( . W) / G_0(W) for the measure G_0

  log <W e^Q> - log <W> = log P( e^Q ) .

By Jensen, log P(e^Q) >= P(Q) >= -E|Q| >= -C_L N^{-1/2}, and by the

  | log P(e^Q) | <= C_L N^{-1/2} ,

and adding Q back changes the log-partition function by O_L(N^{-1/2}).
This passage removes Q from every expression that appears below.

Exchangeability: the derivative path equals the overlap path

The cascade evaluation of the cavity increment requires knowing that the
field h produces the overlap path p, i.e. that rho_h = p.
This is where the finite-dimensional coordinate symmetry enters.

Under the cavity tilt, the conditional mean of z_1^1 z_1^2 given the
common level i of the two sampled replicas equals (rho_h)_i. Verify
this coordinate by coordinate. Fix the shared leaf field t of coordinate
one, and let eta be the independent residual of variance
a_d - h_{k-1}. Then

  E_eta exp( z_1 ( t + eta ) )
    = exp( z_1 t ) exp( ( a_d - h_{k-1} ) / 2 ) ,

and the second factor is independent of z_1. So after residual averaging
the spin magnetization of coordinate one at the leaf is tanh t. Two
visits, even to the same leaf, use separate residuals and separate spin
draws, and below their common level their mark paths are conditionally
independent. Propagating the leaf magnetization backwards
along the tilted kernels therefore gives U_i'(z) at level i, by the
spatial derivative martingale for the field recursion, and the product
mean of z_1^1 z_1^2 given common level i is the averaged square E[U_i']^2
= (rho_h)_i. All other coordinates and all new pattern marks use separate
kernels by the additive component property of the marked-cascade transformation. The shape law
itself is unchanged by the tilt.

On the other hand, the finite-size model in dimension N+L, at any fixed
parameter value v, is invariant under all coordinate permutations.
Therefore for every bounded continuous D_0: [-1,1] -> R,

  E< D_0(R^+_12) z_1^1 z_1^2 >_{N+L}
    = E< D_0(R^+_12) R^+_12 >_{N+L} .

Indeed the left side is unchanged if the distinguished coordinate 1 is
replaced by any of the N+L coordinates, because of the permutation
symmetry, and the average of the products z_k^1 z_k^2 over k is R^+_12.

Now combine. The bounded-reweighting array limit of bounded reweighting of arrays, applied
to the finite-size expectation, together with the cascade computation
(shaped by the marked-cascade transformation) and the truncation passage of the Q-removal step, gives

  lim_m int_0^1 D_0( pbar_m(u) ) (rho_{hbar_m})_u du
    = int_0^1 D_0( p(u) ) p(u) du ,

where (pbar_m, hbar_m) are the finite ordered roundings of (p, h)
constructed in the truncation passage. For the right side, the unclipped
cascade tilt preserves the rounded overlap law by the marked-cascade transformation, and the
truncation estimates of the Q-removal step transfer it to the finite-size limit,
whose overlap law has quantile p.

Since D_0 is an arbitrary bounded continuous function on the compact
interval [-1,1], and pbar_m -> p in L^1 (ordered quantization), the identity
forces rho_{hbar_m} -> p in the same integral sense. Passing to the
limit m -> infinity, the derivative path rho_h of the field recursion at
the field h equals the overlap quantile p. Therefore S_I attains its
supremum at rho_h = p:

  S_I(p) = ell(h) + (1/2) int_0^1 p(u) h(u) du .

This is the equality that turns the concavity supporting inequality into
entropy attainment.

Define the finite-N cavity increment by

  Delta_{N,L}(v) := E log Z_{N+L}(v) - E log Z_N(v) ,

at the parameter value v, where Z_N(v) is the partition function of the
bulk model with the perturbation turned on. The two second-order
computations of the cavity step evaluate this increment.

Expansion error. The second-order cavity expansion bounds the
supremum of the energy difference by O_L(N^{-1/2}) in expectation. The
log-partition function is 1-Lipschitz in the supremum of the energy, so

  E log Z_{N+L} - E log Z_N
    = E log ( G_0-normalized integral of e^{H^v_N}A' prod_i cosh(Y_i) )
      + O_L(N^{-1/2}) ,

with A' = exp((L/2)C_N(x) + sum_{a>M} f(b_a(x))) and W carrying the
z.Y(x) factor. The Q removal contributes O_L(N^{-1/2}).

Cascade increment. Insert the array limits established earlier. The
cavity factor prod_i cosh(Y_i(x)) together with the pattern log factor
sum_{a>M} f(b_a(x)) and the deterministic term (L/2) C_N(x) is exactly
a sum over L independent "field components" plus d independent "pattern
components" (the terminal-variance correction, additive component property):

  - Each field component contributes a term log cosh at a leaf, giving
    after the marked-cascade tilt of the marked-cascade transformation the field recursion value
    on the overlap array. Since the conjugate-field argument identifies rho_h = p, the
    joint contribution of the L field components is L times
    ( ell(h) + (1/2) int_0^1 p(u) h(u) du ) = L S_I(p).

  - Each pattern component (one for each of the d fresh rows) contributes
    a fresh-pattern expectation. By the Gaussian Gibbs calculus and the array limit,
    each has limit V_f(p), so the total is d V_f(p) + o(1).

  - The C_N term contributes only through the field recursion's
    identification (the identity c + a_d = int_0^1 p h of the array-limit extraction is
    what lets the C_N diagonal and the B_N covariance assemble into the
    L S_I(p) term without leftover).

Therefore

  Delta_{N,L}(v(N)) = L S_I(p) + d V_f(p) + o(1) , (INCR)

uniformly over v(N) in G_N and over a further array subsequence. Since
|d - alpha L| <= 1 and the variational value satisfies
S_I(p) + alpha V_f(p) >= P_f, the right side is at least
L(S_I(p) + alpha V_f(p)) - ||f||_inf, and hence

  lim_{N} Delta_{N,L}(v(N)) >= L P_f - ||f||_inf .

Because the good parameter value v(N) was arbitrary in G_N and the
evaluation used only the (INCR) display, taking the infimum over v in
G_N and the liminf over N gives

  liminf_N inf_{v in G_N} Delta_{N,L}(v) >= L P_f - ||f||_inf . (CAV)

The argument is: if the inequality failed on a subsequence, one could
choose a deterministic v(N) in G_N and pass to the array subsequence to contradict (INCR).

Telescoping. Sum (CAV) over the chain j = 0, 1, ..., m-1 with increments
of length L:

  E log Z_{N+mL}(v) - E log Z_N(v)
    = sum_{j=0}^{m-1} Delta_{N+jL, L}(v)
    >= m ( L P_f - ||f||_inf - o(1) ) .

Divide by N + mL and let m -> infinity with N and L fixed. The leftmost
term becomes P_{N+mL} for some sequence, and the second term
E log Z_N/(N+mL) -> 0. The right side becomes
m(L P_f - ||f||_inf)/(N+mL) -> P_f - ||f||_inf/L. Hence

  liminf_m P_{N+mL} >= P_f - ||f||_inf / L .

Now let N -> infinity and then L -> infinity. Both steps are on
subsequences, but by the standard liminf-over-subsequence argument (the
good sets have pi-measure tending to 1, and each array subsequence gives
the same right-hand side), the conclusion holds for the full sequence:

  liminf_N E p_N >= P_f(alpha) .

This is the lower half of the theorem. Combining with the upper half of
the upper-bound argument gives

  lim_N E p_N(f) = P_f(alpha) .

The earlier argument assumes f is smooth and compactly supported. That
assumption let every derivative be taken and gave sharp fluctuation
estimates. The general-activation extension removes it in three stages: bounded Borel f,
integrable unbounded f, and random-sign patterns.

LEMMA (uniform approximation). If f, g are bounded Borel with
|f|, |g| <= K, then

  | E p_N(f) - E p_N(g) | <= alpha e^{2K} || f - g ||_{L^1(gamma)} ,
  sup over step q of | V_f(q) - V_g(q) | <= e^{2K} || f - g ||_{L^1(gamma)} ,

where gamma is standard Gaussian probability.

Proof of the first. Interpolate f_t = (1-t)f + t g. Gibbs differentiation
gives

  d/dt E p_N(f_t) = (1/N) sum_a E < (g-f)( b_a(x) ) >_t .

Remove pattern a from the measure and restore it. The removed log factor
lies in [-K, K], so a nonnegative Gibbs average changes by a factor at
most e^{2K} on restoration. Conditional on all other patterns and on a
sampled spin x, the fresh field b_a(x) is standard Gaussian, so each
summand is at most e^{2K} ||g-f||_{L^1(gamma)}. Summing over a and using
M_N/N <= alpha finishes.

Proof of the second. Fix a step path. Interpolating the terminal function
from f to g and differentiating the recursion, the derivative propagates
through the kernels and equals the tilted expectation of (g-f)(Z_k),
where Z is the tilted chain, and the density of the tilted path measure
with respect to untilted independent Gaussian increments is
exp(telescoped exponent) with

  telescoped exponent = f_t(Z_k) - sum_{i=0}^{k-1} w_i U_i(Z_i) .

Check for one level: the exponent is U_1(Z_1) - U_0(Z_0) =
f_t(Z_1) - w_0 U_0(Z_0) since zeta_1 = 1 and w_0 = 1 - zeta_0. Every
function on the right lies in [-K, K], so the density lies in
[e^{-2K}, e^{2K}]. Without the tilt, Z_k is standard normal, because
the total variance is q_k - q_{-1} = 1. Hence the derivative is at most
e^{2K} E|g-f|(G) in absolute value.

CONSEQUENCES. Choose f_j in C_c^infinity with |f_j| <= K and
||f_j - f||_{L^1(gamma)} -> 0 (truncate; approximate on a compact
interval; mollify with a nonnegative smooth kernel). Then

  | P_f(alpha) - P_{f_j}(alpha) |
    <= alpha sup over q of | V_f(q) - V_{f_j}(q) | -> 0,

because the entropy functional S_I does not depend on f at all. Together
with Lemma 44 for the pressure, the smooth formula gives the theorem for
bounded Borel f.

Convergence in probability. Changing one whole pattern row changes
log Z_N by at most 2||f||_inf, so Var(p_N) <= 4 M ||f||_inf^2 / N^2 =
O(1/N), and Chebyshev converts the expectation limit into convergence in
probability. No smoothness of the overlap path is used, so this applies
to the variational value directly.

CLASS. Suppose f: R -> R is Borel with E|f(G)| + E exp( f(G) ) < infinity.
For J, K > 0 put f_{J,K} = min{ K, max{ -J, f } }. Then the ordered
limit

  V_f = lim_{J -> infinity} lim_{K -> infinity} V_{f_{J,K}}

exists uniformly on Q, is continuous in L^1, and with this V_f the
formula holds in expectation and in probability, with finite value.

CLIPPING ESTIMATES. Let P be any probability on R, t the coordinate
function, t_J = max(t, -J), r = (-J - t)_+. Then

  (lower) 0 <= log P(e^{t_J}) - log P(e^t) <= P(r) ,
  (upper) 0 <= log P(e^{t_J}) - log P(e^{min(t_J,K)})
             <= e^J P( (e^t - e^K)_+ ) .

Proof of the lower bound. Write the difference as minus log of
P(e^{t_J} e^{-r}) / P(e^{t_J}). By log <= exp - 1 and Jensen under the
e^{t_J} tilt, it is at most the tilted mean of r, itself at most P(r).
The final step is the covariance inequality: e^{max(t,-J)} increases in
t while (-J - t)_+ decreases in t, so their covariance under P is
nonpositive.

Proof of the upper bound: after the lower clip, the exponential is at
least e^{-J}, so log(1 + x) <= x gives the bound.

UNIFORM ERROR. Define

  eps_{J,K} = E( -J - f(G) )_+ + e^J E( e^{f(G)} - e^K )_+ .

Replacing one pattern factor at a time in the finite spin system, and
using that the omitted factor is independent of the base probability
with a standard normal fresh field, gives

  E| p_N(f) - p_N(f_{J,K}) | <= alpha eps_{J,K} ,
  sup over step q of | V_f(q) - V_{f_{J,K}}(q) | <= eps_{J,K} .

The order of the two limits matters and the estimates explain why: the
lower clip is controlled by integrability of |f|, the upper clip after
the lower clip by integrability of e^f, and the factor e^J must not meet
the tail of e^f until the lower clip is already paid for.

FINITENESS. Two applications of Jensen to the same cascade integral
give, first on step paths and then on all of Q,

  E f(G) <= V_f(q) <= log E exp( f(G) ) ,

with equality on the right at q = 0. Since S_I >= 0 and S_I(0) = 0,

  alpha E f(G) <= P_f(alpha) <= alpha log E exp( f(G) ) ,

so the value is finite. At fixed J the bounded case applies. Letting K
-> infinity, the bounded functionals are uniformly Cauchy on the dense
set of step paths and hence on Q. Letting J -> infinity, the lower
clipping bound with f replaced by max(f, -J') shows the limits differ by
at most E(-J - f(G))_+, which vanishes. Convergence in probability
follows from Markov applied to the clipped difference, taking N ->
infinity, then K -> infinity at fixed J, then J -> infinity. No
variance estimate for the unbounded model is needed.

Random-sign patterns

STATEMENT. Replace the Gaussian pattern entries by independent uniform
signs in {-1, +1}. If f is bounded Borel and its discontinuity set has
Gaussian measure zero, then the expected pressure and the pressure in
probability converge to the same P_f(alpha).

SMOOTH CASE BY COORDINATE REPLACEMENT. Let f be smooth and compactly
supported. Regard the normalized pressure as a function of one pattern
entry. Three differentiations of the finite spin sum give a bound
C_f N^{-5/2}: each derivative of the pattern argument supplies x_i/sqrt(N),
the normalized log partition supplies a factor 1/N, and the rest are
bounded Gibbs moments of f', f'', f'''. Taylor expansion through degree
two, with matching first and second moments of the Gaussian and the
sign, bounds each entry replacement by C_f N^{-5/2}. There are
M_N N = O(N^2) entries, so replacing them one at a time gives
expected-pressure error O_f(N^{-1/2}), which vanishes.

GENERAL CASE. Take smooth approximants f_j with |f_j|, |f| <= K and
||f_j - f||_{L^1(gamma)} -> 0. The pattern-deleting argument applies
verbatim to sign patterns, so

  | E p_N^sign(f) - E p_N^sign(f_j) |
    <= alpha e^{2K} E | f(X_N) - f_j(X_N) | ,

with X_N the fresh normalized field, of standard Gaussian law by the
local CLT for signs. Letting j -> infinity controls the right side by
the hypothesis on the discontinuity set of f. Therefore the sign and
the Gaussian cases agree in the limit.

WHY A REGULARITY HYPOTHESIS IS NEEDED. Take f = 1_{rat}, the indicator
of the rationals. For Gaussian patterns, every field at every state is
irrational almost surely (finitely many states and rows at each size),
so p_N = 0. For sign patterns and square N, every field is rational, so
p_N = M_N/N. For odd nonsquare N the numerator of each field is a
nonzero odd integer while sqrt(N) is irrational, so p_N = 0. The two
subsequences give distinct limits. The function is discontinuous
everywhere and is excluded by the hypothesis.

Wholly unrestricted real activations need not have finite limiting
pressure. For f(s) = s^4 choose x_i = sign(g_i^1). The law of large
numbers gives

  b_1(x) = N^{-1/2} sum_i |g_i^1| -> sqrt(2/pi) sqrt(N) in probability.

All other pattern contributions are nonnegative, and the probability
weight of this one configuration is 2^{-N}. Thus

  p_N(f) >= b_1(x)^4 / N - log 2 -> infinity in probability.

The integrability class of the integrable activation extension, not unboundedness in general,
is therefore the natural scope.
