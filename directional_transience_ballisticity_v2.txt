
Zero occupation on the projected diagonal. The preceding comparison
applies when the second-coordinate gap is of order r. It does not
exclude a limiting pair that spends positive time at zero gap. The next
estimate bounds that occupation by counting common true cuts first;
exact-truth factorisation transfers it to each deterministic layer.

Proposition 17 (Vanishing diagonal occupation). Along every Gaussian
sequence r -> infinity, under P_{0,0}^{++}, for each fixed finite T,
  lim_{rho -> 0} limsup_{r -> infinity}
  (1 / n(r)) E_{0,0}^{++} sum_{0 <= h <= T n(r)} 1_{|Z_h| <= rho r}
  = 0.                                                    (4.2)

Proof. Choose 1 < alpha < gamma < 2. The potential is a flattened,
capped version of u^alpha, where u is the absolute gap at an observed
common cut. Its expected increase pays for the number of near-diagonal
cuts before the next observation. One estimate is needed at nearly
Gaussian radii and another below the largest remaining exceptional
radius.

Drift at nearly Gaussian radii. Fix a sufficiently small t_0 > 0.
There exist l in (0, 1), eps > 0, and u_0 < infinity such that every
radius satisfying u >= u_0 and n(u) P( |S| > l u ) <= eps has,
uniformly over shared starts with |Z_0| = u,
  E^{++}[ (|Z_{sigma(k(u))}|/u)^alpha ^ 2^alpha ] >= 1 + c_* t_0,
  P^{++}( min_{0 <= h <= k(u)} |Z_h| <= u/2 )
  <= C_* exp(- c_* / t_0),                         (4.3)
  k(u) := floor( t_0 n(u) ) >= 1.
Call these radii good. The constants c_*, C_* can be fixed independently
of small t_0; l, eps, u_0 may depend on t_0.

The compactness argument giving this uniform assertion. Along any
Gaussian sequence of radii u, the independent gap at height k(u),
divided by u, converges to +-1 + G where G ~ N(0, 2 sigma_*^2 t_0).
For small t_0,
  E[ |1 + G|^alpha ^ 2^alpha ] >= 1 + c t_0.
Indeed |z|^alpha has strictly positive second derivative near one,
while the Gaussian probability of leaving that neighbourhood is
exponentially small in 1/t_0. The off-diagonal comparison changes the
expectation by at most C exp(-c'/t_0) in the limit. Replacing k(u) by
sigma(k(u)) costs o(1): by (3.2) the unscaled gap difference is
uniformly tight, and the tested function is bounded and uniformly
continuous. No moment of the overshoot is used.

For the second estimate, shrinking the gap by u/2 requires at least one
centered marginal to move by u/4. The shared marginal limit and the
Brownian maximal bound
  P( sup_{s<=t_0} |B(s)| >= x ) <= 2 exp(-x^2 / (2 sigma_*^2 t_0))
give the required limit bound. The maximal bound follows by applying
the nonnegative submartingale inequality to the two exponentials of
Brownian motion on finite grids, then using continuity. Choosing strict
slack in both limiting inequalities gives (4.3); if no triple
(l, eps, u_0) satisfied (4.3), a violating sequence with l, eps -> 0
would form a Gaussian sequence contradicting those limits.

Cutoff above all exceptional radii. Fix A > 1. Along the given
Gaussian sequence r, the nongood radii in [u_0, Ar] are all o(r). For
every fixed xi > 0 this follows uniformly on [xi r, Ar] from the
Gaussian tail tests, monotonicity of n, and n(Ar) <= A^2 n(r). On any
subsequence further restrict as follows. If the nongood radii stay
bounded, choose a fixed cutoff b_circ above them and above u_0.
Otherwise choose a nongood radius D_* -> infinity within a factor two
of their supremum and set b_circ = 4 D_*. In either case b_circ = o(r),
every radius between b_circ and Ar is good, and
  n(u) / n(r) <= C_A (u/r)^gamma    (b_circ <= u <= Ar).   (4.4)
For this last bound, at a good radius
  m(2u) <= m(u) + 4 u^2 P( |S| > u ) <= (1 + 4 eps) m(u),
so n(2u) >= 2^gamma n(u). Iterate while the doubling stays below r;
for r <= u <= Ar, monotonicity of m gives the remaining estimate. The
constant C_A need not depend on t_0.

Observation times and potential increase. Starting at the common cut
zero, observe only the following subset of common cuts. Stop when an
observed gap is at least Ar. From an observed gap u < Ar, choose the
next observation by the rule
  first common cut at offset at least k(u) if u > b_circ,
  first subsequent common cut with gap at least 2 b_circ or offset at
  least K if u <= b_circ.
These are stopping indices of the common-word chain. At each observation
the conditional continuation is the fresh shared conditioned pair law
from the observed sites.

Use the bounded potential
  Phi(u) = [ (u v b_circ) ^ 2 A r ]^alpha.
For b_circ < u < Ar, (4.3) implies an expected increase at least
c_* t_0 u^alpha. Flattening below b_circ only increases the terminal
value, and the upper cap is at least 2u. For u <= b_circ, the potential
cannot decrease; the escape event proved below makes it increase by at
least (2^alpha - 1) b_circ^alpha with a uniformly positive probability.
Telescope up to observed exit, first with a deterministic truncation of
the number of observations. Since Phi <= (2 A r)^alpha,
  E^{++} sum_{(pre-exit obs, u > b_circ)} t_0 u^alpha
  <= C (A r)^alpha,
  E^{++} sum_{(pre-exit obs, u <= b_circ)} b_circ^alpha
  <= C_circ (A r)^alpha.
The first constant is independent of small t_0.

Escape from a small gap. Fix v_0 > 0. From a shared start with
|Z_0| = u <= b_circ, the walks escape to double the gap in a bounded
number of blocks. Each block starts at a common cut and runs
h = floor(n(r)) layers. Endpoint spread (Prop 13) assigns a deviation
of size at least c_0 D_* to the walk selected by the sign of the
current gap. For the other walk, (3.6) supplies fixed positive raw mass
within c_0 D_* / 3 of the median b_H except on an environment event of
probability O(t_1^2). Subtracting that error from the preceding
Omega(t_1) probability gives a uniformly positive probability that both
masses are available in the same environment. Multiplying the quenched
masses gives a uniformly positive raw probability of two successes
whose absolute gap has increased by at least c_0 D_* / 2. Their
prefixes use only rows below the top. Fresh joint truth there has
probability at least p_2^0, so the top is an exact common cut and both
initial no-drop events hold.

Repeat a fixed number of these blocks, choosing the outward-moving walk
at each new common cut. The shared continuation rule applies at every
repetition. Enough repetitions enlarge any initial gap in [0, 4 D_*]
beyond 8 D_*, with uniformly positive probability, and their total
height is a fixed multiple of H. This proves the escape with K <=
C n(b_circ).

Counting all common cuts. Allocate each common cut to the last
observation at or before it, excluding the next observation. An
upper-regime observation at gap u receives at most k(u) cuts: each
allocated offset is an integer strictly below k(u). A lower-regime
observation receives at most K cuts. These bounds do not require a
bound on the height overshoot of the next observation.

Let N_rho_pre count allocated common cuts with gap at most rho r,
before observed exit. Split observations into three sets. For fixed
rho > 0, take r large enough that b_circ <= 2 rho r. Equation (4.4)
and the drift bounds give
  gap at observation u <= b_circ:  C_{A, circ} (b_circ / r)^{gamma - alpha},
  b_circ < u <= 2 rho r:           C_A rho^{gamma - alpha},
  2 rho r < u < Ar:                C_A exp(-c_* / t_0).
For the first row use K <= C n(b_circ). For the second use
k(u) <= t_0 n(u) and u^{gamma - alpha} <= (2 rho r)^{gamma - alpha}.
For the last row, an allocated near-diagonal cut requires shrinking the
gap from u to at most u/2 before offset k(u). Its conditional
probability is bounded by (4.3); the number of allocated cuts is at
most k(u). In both upper-regime rows, the factor t_0 from k(u) cancels
the one in the drift budget, so their constants can be independent of
small t_0.

Under the same-site initial law, joint marginal tightness makes the
probability of an observed gap >= Ar before height T n(r) tend to zero
as A -> infinity, uniformly in the upper bound for r. All common cuts
after that exit and below the target height contribute at most
T n(r) + 1 times its indicator.

From common cuts to deterministic layers. Let C_h denote common truth
at height h, and A_h the raw probability that both paths reach h before
the initial drop with |Z_h| <= rho r. Imposing the initial shared
conditioning gives a numerator at most A_h. Instead, impose both fresh
truths at height h. Their conditional probability is at least p_2^0,
and the resulting event implies the initial conditioning and C_h.
Dividing by the same initial normalizer gives
  P_{0,0}^{++}( |Z_h| <= rho r )
  <= (p_2^0)^{-1} P_{0,0}^{++}( |Z_h| <= rho r, C_h ).
Sum over h <= T n(r) and use the common-cut bounds above. On the chosen
further sequence the lower-regime term vanishes because b_circ = o(r).
The remaining pre-exit bound is
  C_A rho^{gamma - alpha} + C_A exp(-c_* / t_0).
Every subsequence admits this further-sequence construction, so the
bound controls the original limsup. Send rho -> 0, then t_0 -> 0, and
finally A -> infinity. This proves (4.2).
