Let d >= 2 and let e_1, ..., e_d denote the coordinate unit vectors of
Z^d. Set U = {+-e_1, ..., +-e_d} and
  Delta_U = { p in [0,1]^U : sum_{e in U} p(e) = 1 }.

Let nu be a probability measure on Delta_U. An environment is a
collection omega = (omega(x,.))_{x in Z^d} of probability vectors on U,
one per site, with product law P = nu^{tensor Z^d}. Different sites
carry independent identically distributed rows; no independence among
the entries of a single row is required.

Uniform ellipticity. There is a constant kappa > 0 with
  nu( min_{e in U} p(e) >= kappa ) = 1.

For a fixed environment omega the quenched law P_{x,omega} is that of
the nearest-neighbour Markov chain started at x with
  P_{x,omega}( X_{n+1} = y + e | X_n = y ) = omega(y,e).
The annealed law is P_x(.) = int P_{x,omega}(.) P(d omega). The
environment expectation is denoted E. When both coordinates matter,
P_x also denotes the joint environment-path law.

Directional transience and ballisticity. For a unit vector l in R^d put
  A_l = { X_n . l -> +infty as n -> infinity }.
The walk is directionally transient in direction l when
P_0(A_l) = 1; ballistic in the same direction when
  P_0( lim inf_{n->infty} (X_n . l) / n > 0 ) = 1.

Main theorem. Assume d >= 2 and P = nu^{tensor Z^d} is an iid law of
nearest-neighbour rows satisfying uniform ellipticity. For every fixed
unit l with P_0(A_l) = 1 there is a deterministic v = v(nu) in R^d with
  v . l > 0,    P_0( lim_{n->infty} X_n / n = v ) = 1.
In particular the walk is ballistic in direction l.

The direction may be any fixed real unit vector. No drift, symmetry,
reversibility, independence within a row, or regeneration-time moment
is assumed. The velocity is a function of the row law nu, because an
almost-sure deterministic vector limit is unique. The assertion
concerns each fixed direction separately: no single null set works
for all l at once.

The one-dimensional failure. In Z^1, uniformly elliptic iid
environments can give an almost surely transient walk with zero speed.
Solomon (1975) characterised the one-dimensional recurrence,
transience, and velocity in terms of the law of the log-ratio
  log( omega(0, +e_1) / omega(0, -e_1) ).
Transience holds exactly on the event that this log-ratio has positive
mean, and the velocity may vanish while transience persists.
The obstruction is not small drift; it is the possibility of harvesting
almost all of the drift through rare traps. Fribergh and Kious (2016)
stated the conjecture that dimensions two and higher rule this out.
  ./1404.2060.pdf

Speed versus almost-sure escape. The regeneration construction of
Sznitman and Zerner (1999) reconstructs the walk as a renewal process
at strict records of the projected height; each record below which the
walk never returns begins a new "word", and under a suitable
conditioning the words are iid. Almost sure finiteness of a word does
not supply a finite mean. Without a finite mean the renewal law of
large numbers does not give a positive velocity, and the words can
fail to have a well-defined drift.

The passage from quenched exit probabilities to slowdown estimates and
a central limit theorem through regeneration-time tails is due to
Sznitman (2000); Zeitouni (2006) surveyed the surrounding picture.
  ./zeitouni-2006-rwrereview.pdf

Quantitative ballisticity criteria. Kalikow (1981) introduced an
auxiliary random walk that gives a sufficient directional drift
condition; under his condition Sznitman (2000) obtained slowdown
estimates through regeneration tails. Sznitman's effective criterion
(Sznitman, 2002) reduces positive speed to a finite-box condition (T'),
and the chain of implications
  strong polynomial exit bounds => (T') => (T) => ballistic
has been completed by Berger, Drewitz, and Ramirez (2014), Guerra and
Ramirez (2020), and in a weak-criterion form by Guerra (2020).
  ./kalikow-1981.pdf
  ./1206.6377.pdf
  ./1809.02011.pdf
  ./2006.00570.pdf

These routes start from quantitative exit assumptions of polynomial
or stretched-exponential strength. The argument starts only from
almost sure escape in a single direction and derives the quantitative
crossing estimate as a consequence.

Positive-probability transience and the velocity hemisphere. In
dimensions d >= 3 a separate directional zero-one law under strict
ellipticity upgrades P_0(A_l) > 0 to P_0(A_l) = 1. Under that input the
main theorem also identifies exactly the directions in which escape
occurs.

Corollary. Let d >= 3 and let P = nu^{tensor Z^d} be an iid law of
nearest-neighbour rows satisfying uniform ellipticity. For a fixed
nonzero l in R^d put A_l = { X_n . l -> +infty }. If P_0(A_l) > 0,
then there is a deterministic nonzero v = v(nu) such that
  v . l > 0,    P_0( lim_{n->infty} X_n / n = v ) = 1.
If such an l exists, the deterministic sets
  { u in S^{d-1} : P_0(A_u) = 1 }
  = { u in S^{d-1} : P_0(A_u) > 0 }
  = { u in S^{d-1} : v . u > 0 }
are equal. In particular P_0(A_u) = 0 for every fixed equator direction
u with v . u = 0.

Proof of the corollary. Uniform ellipticity implies the strict
ellipticity assumed by the zero-one law, which upgrades P_0(A_l) > 0
to P_0(A_l) = 1. Since A_l = A_{l/|l|}, the main theorem gives the
stated velocity and positive projection.

Fix this vector v and a direction u in S^{d-1}. If v . u > 0, the vector
law of large numbers gives X_n . u / n -> v . u > 0 almost surely,
hence P_0(A_u) = 1. Conversely if P_0(A_u) > 0, applying the preceding
argument to u gives a deterministic vector w with X_n / n -> w almost
surely and w . u > 0; two probability-one convergence events
intersect, so w = v and v . u > 0. Both implications give the equality
of the three sets and the assertion on the closed opposite hemisphere.

Conventions. All logarithms are natural. Positive constants may change
from line to line and depend only on the fixed row law nu and the
dimension d; additional dependencies are flagged when uniformity
matters. Two walks in a common environment are conditionally
independent given that environment. At a finite path prefix only the
rows at departure sites contribute transition factors; the distinction
is kept whenever a path stops on first arrival.

Proof strategy. The argument separates spatial control from control of
elapsed time. It is organised as follows.

(1) Geometric reduction. With h(x) = (l / |l|_infty) . x, the lattice
    step has h-increment in {-1, 0, +1} and some step has increment
    exactly +1. The hypothesis gives h(X_n) -> +infty. Strict records of
    h and a true-record decomposition give iid words with finite mean
    height gain; counting ordinary records gives finite mean projected
    width. A forward template at a tilted Y-record, where
    Y(x) = u . x + b a h(x) for a signed lateral coordinate u, gives
    finite mean spatial radius. A deterministic unit vector r with
    h(r) > 0 emerges, and X_n / |X_n| -> r. After a signed coordinate
    permutation the ray has first coordinate positive, so height becomes
    x_1.

(2) Cross probability and speed. With height x_1, let
    a_N = P_{0,omega}( T_N < T_{-1} ) be the quenched probability of
    hitting layer N before the layer just below the start. The decisive
    estimate is: for a fixed beta in (0, 1) and every fixed D_0 <
    infinity,
      P( a_N < N^{-beta} ) < N^{-D_0}    for all large N.        (*)
    The same estimate supplies a finite mean regeneration word
    duration, hence a positive speed, and is the only quantitative
    input to the main theorem.

The rest of the proof is a contradiction argument for (*). Suppose (*)
fails along N -> infinity. Conditioning the environment law on
{ a_N < N^{-beta} } has logarithmic relative entropy of order D_0 log N.
Under that conditioning the retained survival kernel loses a positive
fraction of its mass on episodes of bounded stage count, with positive
mean total loss. Summing over episodes builds a stationary law of local
endpoint profiles together with their upper environments and a positive
mean normalization rate. Two additional ingredients contradict that
rate.

(3) Excluding diffuse and separated profiles. Finite-window entropy
    excludes diffuse mass and infinitely many separated profile classes
    on the positive-loss event; one remaining full-support class is
    marked uniformly, and its upper environment relative to an iid
    field carries only O(m) relative entropy over m episodes.

(4) A two-tail statistic on the marked class. An outward-order kernel
    transports the two far tails of the marked profile at the cost
    A_to log(1 + H) plus an exponentially integrable local remainder;
    the profile update multiplies surviving mass by e^{C_m}, where
    C_m is the sum of the per-episode normalization gains. Averaging
    and sending the tail separation to infinity removes the local
    environmental bias from the remainder. The episode height bound
    log(1 + H_m) = o(m) contradicts the positive mean of C_m / m.

Four ingredients remain to be built and combined: the geometric
reduction, the cross-probability estimate, the separated stages and
episode bounds, and the stationary profile argument with the
outward-order kernel. The constructions are developed in that order.

Strict records and true words. Fix a unit vector l with P_0(A_l) = 1
and put h(x) = (l / |l|_infty) . x. A step in the lattice U increases
h by at most one in absolute value; some step increases it by exactly
one. The hypothesis gives h(X_n) -> +infty almost surely.

Site-row factorisation. The quenched weight of a finite path prefix
depends only on its departure rows. Suppose an event is supported on
continuations avoiding those departure sites; its quenched probability
is measurable from the rows outside the departure set and factors from
the prefix weight after annealing. Evaluate it with the walk killed on
arrival at the forbidden set. Infinite avoidance events follow from
the cylinder construction of path measures. For a finite continuation
it suffices that its departure sites stay outside the prefix's
departure set. No independence among the entries of a single row is
used.

True records. Let D_h be the event that the walk never goes below its
starting height, and p_h = P_0(D_h). An ordinary strict record is a
first strict exceedance of the running maximum of h, with time zero
counted as the initial record. A record is true when the walk never
subsequently descends to or below its height.

Lemma 1 (True words and widths). p_h is positive. Under
P_0( . | D_h ), with time zero as a true boundary, the relative path
words between consecutive true boundaries are iid. The raw path has a
finite first positive-time true record, and its translated suffix has
this conditioned law. For a conditioned word let L > 0 be its height
gain, R its maximal Euclidean distance from its starting point
including its endpoint, and S_* its number of ordinary strict records
after its start. Then
  0 < E L <= E S_* <= p_h^{-1},
where all expectations in this paragraph use the conditioned word law.

Proof. The global height minimum is attained almost surely: h is
integer-valued and h(X_n) -> +infty. There is therefore some
deterministic (n, x) with X_n = x and all later heights at least h(x),
on a positive-probability event. The quenched Markov property at
time n gives
  E[ P_{0,omega}(X_n = x) P_{x,omega}(D_h) ] > 0.
Since the first factor is at most one, translation invariance gives
p_h > 0.

At a first strict record, every earlier departure row has smaller
height. The fresh-row rule therefore gives conditional raw probability
p_h that the record is true. Test the first positive-time strict
record. If a later drop invalidates it, test the next strict record
above the whole past exposed by that failure time. All candidates are
path stopping rules. Transience supplies another candidate after each
finite failure, and each candidate has success probability p_h. A true
record is therefore found almost surely.

For the iid assertion, enumerate the possible first words. A word from
0 to z has positive length, every preterminal height lies in
[0, h(z)), and every intermediate strict record is invalidated by a
drop before the word's end. Combined with a suffix staying at or above
h(z), these conditions say exactly that this is the first word under
D_h: a later suffix above h(z) cannot invalidate a lower record. The
prefix weight uses only rows below h(z); the suffix factor p_h uses
rows outside the departure set. Dividing by the initial conditioning
probability p_h leaves the raw prefix weight as the word probability
and an independent translated copy of the conditioned law as suffix.
Countable enumeration and iteration give the iid words and infinitely
many true boundaries. For the first raw true record the same argument
applies without the nonnegative-height restriction, and without
invoking an annealed Markov property at a future-dependent cut.

Under D_h, a fixed positive ordinary record index i is true with
probability equal to the raw probability of reaching record i before
any drop below zero; the fresh suffix factor p_h cancels the
conditioning. This probability is at least p_h because every record
index is reached on D_h. The true indices form a renewal process with
iid increments S_*. Its positive-index count through m, divided by m,
converges almost surely and in expectation to 1 / E S_*, including
limit zero when E S_* is infinite (by the strong law on truncations).
The ratio is bounded by one, so E S_* <= p_h^{-1}. Each ordinary
record increases height by at most one, so L <= S_*. This argument
uses record indices and not a lattice structure for h.

Integrable spatial radius. Finite mean width controls progress only at
true boundaries. To control the path inside a word, the spatial radius
also needs a finite mean. The argument compares two bounds for a large
displacement relative to the running height maximum. An infinite mean
radius would produce such a displacement with probability of order
1/B. At each new ratio threshold, a fixed positive probability of a
fresh continuation caps the ratio until the walk leaves the height
strip. Iterating these probabilities gives an exponential upper bound
in B.

Proposition 2 (Integrable radius). Under the conditioned word law,
  E R < infinity.                                 (2.1)
No moment of the word duration is assumed.

Proof. Suppose E R = infinity and put I(s) = E min(R, s). The function
I is increasing, concave, unbounded, and o(s). Write H_k =
sum_{j=1}^k L_j, with H_0 = 0.

Likely templates. The width strong law gives constants c > 0, C, C_0,
with ck - C_0 <= H_k <= Ck + C_0 for all k >= 0 with probability
greater than .95. Fix an integer C_1 with C_1 c > C + 4, then b > 1/c,
then eta > 0 small enough that 4 C_1 eta < .1. For large a choose the
least positive integer N with I(aN) >= eta a. Minimality and
concavity give eta a <= I(aN) < 2 eta a for all large a.

With probability greater than .8, the first C_1 N words satisfy the
height bounds and
  sum_{j=1}^k R_j <= ak    (1 <= k <= C_1 N).     (2.2)

The maximal estimate uses Hopf's maximal ergodic inequality in its
nonnegative form: for a stationary nonnegative sequence (Z_j),
  P( max_{1<=k<=n} (1/k) sum_{j=1}^k Z_j > a ) <= E Z_1 / a.
Proof: mark starts in 1, ..., M that admit a witness interval of
length at most n with average greater than a. Greedy left-to-right
selection gives disjoint witness intervals covering the marked starts;
the total sum exceeds a times their number. The selected intervals lie
in 1, ..., M + n. Taking expectations, dividing by M, and letting M
grow removes the boundary contribution. This nonnegative form of
Hopf's maximal ergodic theorem appears in Garsia (1965).
  ./garsia-1963.pdf

Apply the maximal estimate to the truncated radii min(R_j, a C_1 N).
Each expectation is at most C_1 I(aN) < 2 C_1 eta a, and the maximal
estimate applied to those truncated radii is therefore at most
2 C_1 eta. Combined with the height event this gives the .8 bound
in (2.2).

Polynomial lower bound. Fix large B > 1. Choose i_0 with
  sum_{i >= i_0} P(L > i) < eta / (4B),
  C(i - 1) + C_0 + i <= (C + 2) i    (i >= i_0).
Choose a sufficiently large that N >= i_0 and I(B a i_0) < eta a / 4.
Tail integration, followed by removal of the width exceptions, gives
  eta / (2B) <= lambda := sum_{i = i_0}^N P(R > B a i, L <= i) <= 2 eta.
Indeed, for the lower bound the unrestricted sum is at least
[I(Ba(N+1)) - I(B a i_0)] / (Ba) >= 3 eta / (4B); for the upper bound
it is at most I(B a N) / (B a) <= I(aN) / a.

Count the indices i at which this large-word event occurs and the
preceding words satisfy the height and radius bounds through i - 1.
Independence gives mean at least .8 lambda. Dropping the
preceding-word restrictions bounds the second moment by lambda +
lambda^2. The count is therefore positive with probability at least
c_2 / B, with c_2 > 0 independent of B and a. At some point in that
word,
  ||X_n|| > (B - 1) a i,    0 <= h(X_j),
  M_n := max_{j <= n} h(X_j) <= (C + 2) i.
The height bound holds throughout this prefix because every word ends
at a strict record and has no earlier height above its endpoint. One
signed coordinate vector u therefore satisfies
  Y(X_n) / [a(1 + M_n)] > c_3 B,    Y(x) = u . x + b a h(x),
for a fixed c_3 > 0 and sufficiently large B. Under the raw law the
union of these events over the 2d signed unit vectors u occurs with
probability at least p_h c_2 / B before the path leaves
{ h >= 0, M <= (C + 2) N }.

Fresh continuation caps the ratio. Fix u and put Q_n =
Y(X_n) / [a(1 + M_n)]. Let zeta be the exit time just described. Fix
also t > 0 and let sigma < zeta be the first crossing Q > t. Then
constants epsilon_0 > 0 and K < infinity, independent of B, a, t,
have the property: at every finite prefix ending at sigma there is a
continuation of conditional raw probability at least epsilon_0 that
leaves the strip before Q exceeds t + K. Indeed
  Y(X_j) <= t a(1 + M_j) <= t a(1 + M_sigma) < Y(X_sigma)
for j < sigma, so the arrival is a strict Y-record. A step changes Y
by at most ba + 1, hence Q_sigma <= t + b + 1 when a >= 1. Choose an
integer M_0' > C_0 with M_0' b > 1 + b C_0. Prescribe M_0' steps of
height +1; each raises Y by at least ba - 1 > 0. At their endpoint
impose a translated D_h-suffix whose first C_1 N words satisfy the
height-and-radius template. In its word k + 1, every site has
Y-increment from launch at least
  M_0'(ba - 1) + ba(ck - C_0) - a(k + 1) > 0.
The coefficient of k is a(bc - 1) > 0, and the constant term is
positive by the choice of M_0'. The controlled words therefore avoid
every old departure row. Their terminal true cut has height at least
  h(X_sigma) + M_0' + c C_1 N - C_0 > (C + 2) N.
The entire remaining suffix stays above this cut and so also avoids
all old departures. The template stays above its own starting height,
which is strictly above every prior departure height. The whole
infinite continuation therefore uses no old departure row: first the
Y-barrier separates, then the terminal height does. The fresh-row
factorisation gives probability at least
  epsilon_0 = .8 kappa^{M_0'} p_h > 0.

Bound on the ratio along this continuation. The positive part of the
launch coordinate u . X_sigma divided by any later denominator is at
most t + b + 1, since h(X_sigma) >= 0 and the denominator is
nondecreasing. The later height term contributes at most b. The
prescribed script adds at most M_0'. In word k + 1 the additional
coordinate contribution is at most
  (M_0'/a + k + 1) / (A_0 + ck) <= max{ (M_0' + 1)/A_0, 1/c },
with A_0 = 1 + M_0' - C_0 > 1. The numerator uses the bound on the sum
of radii through word k + 1; the denominator uses the height of that
word's starting boundary. So any fixed
  K > 2b + 1 + max{ M_0', (M_0' + 1)/A_0, 1/c }
suffices. Exit occurs by the last prescribed template endpoint.

Exponential upper bound. Let sigma_j be the first crossing before zeta
of t_j = 1 + jK. The continuation just constructed prevents
sigma_{j+1} < zeta. Summing its bound over the finite prefixes at
sigma_j gives
  P_0( sigma_{j+1} < zeta ) <= (1 - epsilon_0) P_0( sigma_j < zeta ).
The conditioning uses only the observed crossing prefix and does not
reveal whether the infinite template failed. Hence the event in (2.2)
has probability at most C' e^{-c' B} for this fixed u. Union over
signed coordinates contradicts the lower bound p_h c_2 / B when B
is large. All estimates use the permitted order: fix the geometric
constants, then B, then i_0, then take a large. The proposition is
proved.

Corollary 3 (Deterministic limiting ray). There is a deterministic
unit vector r with h(r) > 0 and X_n / |X_n| -> r almost surely. After
a deterministic signed permutation of the coordinate axes, r_1 > 0,
and Lemma 1 and Proposition 2 hold with height x_1.

Proof. Word endpoints obey the vector strong law with mean displacement
w satisfying h(w) = E L > 0. Proposition 2 gives R_k / k -> 0 almost
surely: for each eps > 0 the probabilities P(R_k > eps k) are summable.
Interpolation inside each word therefore gives the same limiting
direction r = w / |w|, without any duration moment. The completed-word
count tends to infinity, and Lemma 1 transfers the conclusion through
the finite initial raw prefix.

Some signed coordinate of r is positive. Relabel it as the first
coordinate, preserving the lattice, uniform ellipticity, and the iid
row law. Then (X_n)_1 -> +infty almost surely. Lemma 1 and
Proposition 2 then apply again with height x_1, using only this
coordinate's transience. Notation for the transformed walk and its law
is unchanged.

Layer notation and a small inverse moment. Henceforth the height is
x_1. Write x = (x_1, x-bar) with x-bar in Z^{d-1}. Fluctuations in the
second coordinate are measured by the single scalar coordinate x_2.
For a path started at y and an integer j put T_j = T_j(y) =
inf{ n >= 0 : (X_n)_1 = y_1 + j }, the first hit of the layer y_1 + j;
thus T_0 = 0 by convention and inf of the empty set is +inf. Set
D = { T_{-1} = +inf }, p = P_x(D) > 0 (Corollary 3), and
  P_x^+ = P_x( . | D ),    q(x) = P_{x,omega}(D),
  a_H(x) = P_{x,omega}( T_H < T_{-1} )    (integer H >= 0).
The environment in q and a is implicit. Let K_H(x, .) be the
unnormalized quenched kernel of reaching the layer x_1 + H before the
drop, of mass a_H(x); the same notation covers the whole path prefix
through that first hit, testing events on that prefix. The kernel uses
only rows in the H layers from the starting layer inclusive up to the
upper one exclusive. Abbreviate a_H = a_H(0) and u_H = E a_H. The
filtration F_H on environments declares the rows at 0 <= x_1 < H;
under P the upper layers from H inclusive are fresh iid layers, also
from any finite F_H-stopping layer. Further shifts at other bases use
the same convention. Write L_s, R_s, tau_s for the height width, the
radius, and the number of path steps of a single-walk true word in
direction e_1 under P_0^+.

Lemma 4 (Small inverse moment). For every site x, a_H(x) decreases to
q(x) > 0 almost surely as H grows. In addition there is lambda > 0 with
  E q(0)^{-lambda} < infinity.                            (2.3)

Proof. Uniform ellipticity rules out confinement forever in any fixed
finite-height strip, even quenched: blocks of sufficiently many
consecutive upward steps have a fixed positive chance. Hence a_H(x)
decreases to q(x) as H grows. The variable q(je_1) uses only layers at
height >= j, so the event that q(je_1) > 0 for infinitely many j is a
tail event of the independent layers. For every m, the union of these
events over j >= m has probability at least P(q(0) > 0) > 0 by
stationarity and E q(0) = p > 0. The tail zero-one law makes the
limsup almost sure. A finite upward script from 0 to one such site j
gives q(0) > 0. Translation and countability give positivity at every
site simultaneously.

At layer H let mu_H = K_H(0, .) / a_H be the endpoint probability.
It is F_H-measurable, and the quenched Markov property at the suffix
above layer H (never at or below that layer) gives
  q(0) >= a_H int q(x) mu_H(dx).
Each q(x) on that layer has the original marginal law independently
of the lower rows. If q(0)/a_H < s, more than half the mu_H mass has
q(x) <= 2s. Combining the conditional expectation with Markov gives
  P( q(0)/a_H < s | F_H ) <= 2 P( q(0) <= 2s ).
The endpoint values q(x) on layer H need not be independent, but a
finite-layer cutoff gives the same conditional inequality.

Choose delta in (0, kappa) sufficiently small that
  rho := 2 P( q(0) <= 2 delta / kappa ) < 1,
and set H_j = inf{ H >= 0 : a_H < delta^j } for j >= 1. These are
stopping layers. One upward step after a successful crossing gives
a_{H+1} >= kappa a_H, so on { H_j < infinity }, a_{H_j} >= kappa
delta^j. The event H_{j+1} < infinity implies q(0) < delta^{j+1} and
q(0)/a_{H_j} < delta/kappa, so
  P( H_{j+1} < infinity | F_{H_j} ) <= rho.
Since a_H decreases to q(0), { H_j < infinity } = { q(0) < delta^j }.
Iteration gives P( H_j < infinity ) <= rho^{j-1}. Summing over
delta^{j+1} <= q(0) < delta^j, and choosing lambda > 0 with
rho delta^{-lambda} < 1, proves (2.3).

Cross probability and speed. Small quenched exit probabilities also
link spatial estimates to regeneration-time tails in Sznitman (2002);
see Lemma 3.2 and Theorem 3.3 there.
The crossing probabilities reduce to speed.

Proposition 5 (Cross probability implies speed). Suppose for some
fixed beta in (0, 1) and every fixed D_0 < infinity,
  P( a_N < N^{-beta} ) < N^{-D_0}    eventually for all N.   (2.4)
Then E tau_s < infinity and X_n / n -> v almost surely with v . l > 0.

Proof. All conclusions using (2.4) are conditional on that estimate.

(a) Spatial confinement before exit. The raw path before leaving the
height interval [0, N] stays in ||x-bar||_infty <= C N^2, except with
probability at most C e^{-c N}. Fix a signed lateral coordinate u. By
Corollary 3 there is a fixed b > 0 making Y(x) = u . x + b x_1
transient in its positive direction, so the event of staying forever
at Y >= 0 has positive raw probability by the minimum argument of
Lemma 1.

Fix a sufficiently large C_2. The path through its first relative
height hit at N + 1 has norm at most C_2 N with probability tending to
one: the ray gives ||X_n|| <= (C_2/2)(X_n)_1 eventually, and the
earlier path has a finite maximum norm. The intersection with the
preceding no-backtracking event has probability bounded below, uniformly
for large N.

At a first crossing of Y > j C_3 N before strip exit, the walk sits at
a strict Y-record. Impose the favorable translated suffix using the
fresh rows at or above the record. When C_3 is large enough, the
suffix precludes crossing (j+1) C_3 N before strip exit: its
Y-displacement through relative height N+1 is at most (1 + b) C_2 N,
the launch overshoot is bounded, and absolute strip exit occurs no
later than this relative height hit. The finite-prefix threshold
recursion of Proposition 2, now with spacing C_3 N, gives exponential
decay through N thresholds. Since u . X <= Y before exit, the finite
union over signed lateral coordinates gives the confinement claim.

(b) Super-polynomial width tails. Let Delta u_N := u_N - u_{N+1} >= 0
be the annealed probability of reaching N before drop but dropping
before N+1. In environments good at all sites of the strip and box,
where a_{N+1}(x) >= (N+1)^{-beta}, the portion of this event inside
the box has quenched probability at most
  (1 - (N+1)^{-beta})^{N+1}.
Indeed, after reaching N, at first descending-level visits to each of
N, N-1, ..., 0 still inside the box, there is at least that chance to
hit the top N+1 before the next descent by one. Condition (2.4),
translation, a polynomial union bound, and the box claim therefore
give super-polynomial decay of Delta u_N.

Under P_0^+ the true levels form a renewal process with iid widths
L_s and renewal masses exactly u_n: impose the fresh D-suffix at the
first reach of n before drop, and cancel p. If
G_j = P_0^+( L_s > j ), the last renewal up to n gives
  sum_{j=0}^n G_j u_{n-j} = 1,
hence
  G_{n+1} = sum_{j=0}^n G_j Delta u_{n-j}.
Put f_k = Delta u_{k-1} for k >= 1. This nonnegative kernel has total
mass theta = 1 - p < 1 and all polynomial moments. With G_0 = 1 the
recurrence gives G = sum_{r >= 0} f^{*r}, including unit mass at zero
for r = 0. If theta > 0, normalize f by theta. For every positive
integer m, the m-th moment of its r-fold convolution is at most r^m
times the m-th moment, because
  (z_1 + ... + z_r)^m <= r^{m-1} sum_i z_i^m.
Summing with geometric weights theta^r gives sum_n n^m G_n < infinity
for every m. The case theta = 0 is immediate. So the width tail G_n
decays faster than every power of n.

(c) Time integrability and velocity. The raw exit time
T_N ^ T_{-1} := min(T_N, T_{-1}) exceeds N^A with super-polynomially
small probability for a sufficiently large fixed A. Use confinement
and (2.4) on a slightly enlarged box and strip, requiring
a_N(x + e_1) >= N^{-beta} for every relevant x with 0 <= x_1 < N; a
polynomial union bound controls the exceptional environments.

From any visit to such a site x, the quenched probability of no
further visit before exit is at least kappa a_N(x + e_1): take one
upward step, then advance N before dropping below the new layer. This
event forces original strip exit before revisiting x even though the
auxiliary target may lie higher than N. The quenched Markov property
therefore bounds each site's visit count by a geometric tail with
success probability at least kappa N^{-beta}. At most C N^{2d-1}
sites lie in the confined box; if the exit time exceeds N^A, one site
has at least N^A / (C N^{2d-1}) visits. For A > 2d - 1 + beta the
union of the geometric-tail events is super-polynomially small, as
are the bad-box and bad-environment events.

On D, if L_s <= N, the first word ends at its first arrival to height
L_s, so tau_s <= T_N. Hence
  P_0^+( tau_s > N^A )
  <= P_0^+( L_s > N ) + p^{-1} P_0( T_N ^ T_{-1} > N^A ).
Both terms decay faster than every power, proving E tau_s < infinity.
The iid-word strong laws then give velocity mean displacement divided
by mean duration; its first coordinate is E L_s / E tau_s > 0. The
integrable spatial radius controls the inter-endpoint deviations, so
this limit holds at every path time. The finite raw initial prefix
does not change it. Finally the velocity is a positive multiple of the
ray of Corollary 3, whose projection on l is positive. Undoing the
signed coordinate permutation proves the stated conclusion. As an
almost-sure deterministic limit, this velocity is unique for the law
nu.

Reduction of the main theorem. It remains to prove (2.4). The next
constructions supply the estimate; no finite duration moment is used.

Two-walk scales and preliminary comparisons. Fluctuations in the
second coordinate are now measured at first hits of successive height
layers. Common true levels supply fresh pair continuations and, in
independent environments, an iid symmetric sequence whose truncated
variance defines the fluctuation scale. The resulting short-block
survival and endpoint spread feed the Gaussian and clipping estimates
of the next two stages.

On a successful prefix set Y_j = (X_{T_j} - X_0)_2 and choose a
deterministic integer median b_j of Y_j under P_0^+, with b_0 = 0.
Put
  W_H = max_{0 <= j <= H} |Y_j - b_j|.
All these variables record displacement from the indicated starting
site.

Common true cuts. For two starting sites x, y on the same layer,
distinguish the independent conditioned law P_x^+ (x) P_y^+ from the
annealed law P_{x,y}^{++} of two walks in a common environment
conditioned on both no-drop events D. Let P_{x,y}^{sh} denote the
latter pair law before conditioning, and put
  p_2(x, y) = E[ q(x) q(y) ].
The positivity of q from Lemma 4 gives a uniform lower bound
  p_2^0 := inf_{x_1 = y_1} p_2(x, y) > 0,
because for every c > 0
  P( min(q(x), q(y)) < c ) <= 2 P( q(0) < c ),
so a small c makes q(x) q(y) >= c^2 on an event of uniformly positive
probability. Product-field mixing also gives
  p_2(x, y) -> p^2 as ||x - y|| -> infinity with x_1 = y_1;
approximate q(0) in L^1(P) by a bounded function of finitely many rows
and translate to x and y; the translated row sets become disjoint.

Each marginal of the shared conditioned law is uniformly dominated by
the corresponding single conditioned law:
  P_{x,y}^{++}( X^{(1)} in A ) <= (p / p_2^0) P_x^+(A),
and likewise for the second walk. With superscripts distinguishing the
walks, define
  Delta_j = Y_j^{(2)} - Y_j^{(1)},    Z_j = y_2 - x_2 + Delta_j.
A common true level is a level that is true for both walks at their
respective first hits. Heights are measured from the common starting
layer, and level zero is included under either conditioned law.

Lemma 6 (Common words and continuation laws). Under either conditioned
pair law, common true levels occur infinitely often. At each such level
the translated pair continuation is fresh: under independent
environments it has law P_0^+ (x) P_0^+, while under a common
environment, from terminal sites x', y', it has law P_{x',y'}^{++}
before translation. The latter transition law depends on the terminal
pair of sites. The continuation rules remain valid at finite stopping
indices of the chain of common words, when the retained information
consists of the traversed words and their path prefixes.

Proof. Work first with the raw pair laws. Test a new integer level
above both explored path pasts, pausing each walk at its first arrival
there. All departure rows revealed by these prefixes lie strictly below
the candidate level. The conditional probability that both arrivals
are true is p^2 in independent environments and p_2 at the arrival
sites in a common environment; both have a uniform positive lower
bound.

If the candidate fails, resume the two walks, for example one step of
each per round, until one drops below the candidate. Failure is
detected in finite time. Test a level strictly above both explored
pasts. Each walk tends to positive infinite height almost surely; the
common raw pair inherits this by its marginals. After every detected
failure a new candidate is reached. The uniform success bound
therefore gives a common true level above any prescribed height.
Conditioning on the two initial no-drop events preserves this
almost-sure conclusion.

For the continuation law, specify a pair of finite words from x, y to
their first hits x', y' of a common terminal level L > 0. Call the
pair admissible when both prefixes avoid the initial drop and every
intermediate positive level has a witnessed failure of common truth:
at least one prefix drops below that level after its first hit.
These are exactly the required prefix conditions. Once truth at L is
imposed, any earlier truth failure must already have taken place in
the specified prefixes.

Let A_{x,y}^{sh}(w_1, w_2) be the raw annealed prefix weight of an
admissible word pair. Its quenched weight is the product of the two
path weights in the shared environment, including repeated rows.
Every departure row lies below the terminal layer. Suffixes
constrained by truth use only rows at or above that layer, so site-row
independence gives
  P_{x,y}^{++}( (w_1, w_2) is the first common word )
  = A_{x,y}^{sh}(w_1, w_2) p_2(x', y') / p_2(x, y).
More generally, for any event B of the suffix pair,
  P_{x,y}^{++}( (w_1, w_2) and B )
  = A_{x,y}^{sh}(w_1, w_2)
    p_2(x', y') / p_2(x, y) P_{x',y'}^{++}(B),
with B read with new sites as starting points. The factor
p_2(x', y') / p_2(x, y) is part of the shared word law and does not
cancel in general.

In independent environments the terminal and initial conditioning
factors are both p^2, so the ratio is one. Translation of each walk
separately makes the relative suffix law P_0^+ (x) P_0^+, independent
of the traversed word. Enumerate the countably many admissible finite
word pairs and iterate these factorizations. Summing over a finite
stopping index gives the same rules there; the information at such an
index does not include any unseen upper environment.

In independent environments let L >= 1 be the integer width of a
common word and S its increment of Delta. The successive pairs
(L_i, S_i), with their path decorations, are iid by Lemma 6.
Exchanging the walks shows that S is symmetric. Expectations of L, S
refer throughout to this independent common-word law.

Lemma 7 (Common-word moments and local tightness). The independent
common-word variables satisfy
  1 <= L-bar := E L <= p^{-2},    E |S| < infinity,   P(S != 0) > 0.
For a single conditioned walk, and for either marginal of a shared
conditioned pair, the increments Y_{h+j} - Y_h are tight uniformly in
the deterministic level h when j ranges over any fixed bounded set of
nonnegative integers.

If sigma(H) is the first common true level at or above H under
P_{x,y}^{++}, then
  sigma(H) - H,    Y^{(i)}_{sigma(H)} - Y^{(i)}_H  (i = 1, 2)
are tight uniformly in H, x, y. At sigma(H) the pair suffix has the
shared conditioned law from its new sites.

Proof. A deterministic level h is true under the single conditioned
law with probability u_h, by the exact-truth identity of Lemma 1.
Independence therefore makes the common-word renewal mass u_h^2 >= p^2.
The renewal-count strong law, including its infinite-mean version
obtained by truncation, gives L-bar <= p^{-2}. The lower bound follows
from L >= 1.

For each of the two paths, let N_i be the number of its single words
used before the first positive common boundary. Since single widths
are positive integers, N_i <= L. The event N_i >= j is determined by
that path's first j - 1 words and the independent boundary sequence of
the other path; it is therefore independent of the radius of its j-th
word. The finite mean single-word radius from Lemma 1 gives
  E sum_{j=1}^{N_i} R_{s,j}^{(i)}
  = E N_i E R_s <= L-bar E R_s < infinity.
So |S| is bounded by the sum of these two total radii in expectation.
Finally, uniform ellipticity permits two short paths to level one
with different second-coordinate displacements; imposing fresh truth
there gives positive probability of an exact common cut with nonzero
S.

Record the renewal estimate that supplies local tightness. For either
renewal sequence, let L' be its word width and A any almost surely
finite size of that word, such as its maximal displacement. If
A_word is the size of the word based at the last boundary at or below
a deterministic level h, then
  P( A_word > s ) <= sum_{j=0}^h P( L' > j, A > s )
  <= E[ L' 1_{A > s} ].
For the first inequality, sum over possible preceding boundary levels
h - j, use independence of the next word, and bound each renewal mass
by one. Since E L' < infinity, the right side tends to zero as s
grows. The same reasoning with A = L' gives tightness of the covering
width. First-hit positions at a deterministic layer therefore differ
from their preceding boundary positions by tight amounts, uniformly
in the base. A fixed interval of integer levels meets only the words
covering those levels, so it also has tight total displacement,
uniformly in its base. Shared marginals inherit this statement by the
uniform domination above.

Tightness of sigma(H) - H and of the displacement at sigma(H). Test
the m levels
  h = H, H + B, ..., H + (m - 1) B.
Call failure at h visible if, after first hitting h, some walk drops
below it before its first hit of h + B. In the raw pair law the
probability that all m tests have visible failure is at most
(1 - p_2^0)^m: at each pair of first-hit arrivals at h, all previous
visible-failure tests are already determined, while two fresh truths
at h would preclude the next failure. The passage to the conditioned
pair law costs at most (p_2^0)^{-1}.

If h is not common true but has no visible failure, some walk drops
below h after its first hit of h + B. That walk has no single true
boundary in [h, h + B], so its word covering h has width greater than
B. The covering-word estimate and marginal domination bound this
alternative by
  C E[ L_s 1_{L_s > B} ].
A union bound over the m tests gives
  P_{x,y}^{++}( sigma(H) > H + (m - 1) B )
  <= (p_2^0)^{-1} (1 - p_2^0)^m
     + C m E[ L_s 1_{L_s > B} ].
Choose m large, then B large. This proves uniform tightness of the
height overshoot. The fixed-interval displacement bound just proved
gives the other assertions at sigma(H). Finally, the first common
boundary at or above H is a stopping index of the common-word chain,
so its suffix rule follows from Lemma 6.

Truncated variance and median centering. For r > 0 define
  m(r) = E( S^2 ^ r^2 ),    n(r) = r^2 / m(r).
Both quantities are positive by P(S != 0) > 0. The scale n(r) uses
only the finite first moment of S; a finite second moment is not
assumed.

Lemma 8 (Scale and maximal estimates). The function n is continuous
and nondecreasing, tends to infinity, and is strictly increasing for
all sufficiently large r. Write r_s for its inverse on large scales,
so n(r_s) = s. Then
  n(cr) >= c^2 n(r)    (0 < c <= 1),
  n(r) >= r / E |S|.                                     (3.1)
For iid copies S_j, define
  M_H^S = max_{0 <= i <= H} | sum_{j=1}^i S_j |.
Uniformly in integer H >= 0 and z > 0,
  E[ (M_H^S)^2 ^ z^2 ] <= C H m(z),
  P( M_H^S > z ) <= C H / n(z).                          (3.2)

Proof. The ratio m(r) / r^2 = E[ (S^2 / r^2) ^ 1 ] is continuous and
nonincreasing. A bounded interval of strictly positive |S| has
positive probability; its contribution strictly decreases once r
exceeds that interval. This proves monotonicity and eventual strict
monotonicity of n. Monotonicity of m proves the first inequality in
(3.1); S^2 ^ r^2 <= r |S| proves the second, giving n(r) -> infinity.

For (3.2), replace S_j by S_j 1_{|S_j| <= z}. The truncated variables
have mean zero by symmetry. The probability of any replacement among
the first H variables is at most H P( |S| > z ). Off this event the
original partial sums agree with the truncated sums, whose expected
squared maximum is at most 4 H E[ S^2 1_{|S| <= z} ] by the square
maximal inequality. On the replacement event the capped squared
maximum is at most z^2. Adding these bounds gives the first inequality
in (3.2); Markov's inequality for the capped square gives the second.

Lemma 9 (Exact-level comparison). For every integer H >= 0 and z > 0,
  P_0^+( W_H > z ) <= C P( M_H^S > z ).                  (3.3)
No moment of the maximal decoration inside a common word is needed.

Proof. In a raw first walk select the first level h <= H, if any,
with T_h < T_{-1} and |Y_h - b_h| > z. Let A be the event that such a
level exists. Conditional on this prefix and its selected level, an
independent raw second walk reaches h before dropping, with its
displacement on the opposite side of the median b_h, with probability
at least p/2. The conditioned no-drop law only lower bounds a prefix
probability; it is not imposed as a separate test on the second walk.

At the two first-hit endpoints impose both fresh truths; their
probability factor is p^2. The resulting event implies the two
initial no-drop events and has h as an exact common true level with
|Delta_h| > z. Dividing its raw probability by p^2, its probability
under the independent conditioned pair law is at least P_0(A) p / 2.
At a common cut of height at most H, the difference is a partial sum
of at most H common-word increments, so
  P( M_H^S > z ) >= (p/2) P_0(A) >= (p^2/2) P_0^+( W_H > z ).
This proves (3.3). All the adaptive prefix transfers admit the same
proof by summing finite stopped-word identities separately over each
possible h.

Lemma 10 (Median linearization on short blocks). For sufficiently
small t > 0, uniformly in 1 <= H <= t n(r),
  max_{j <= H} | b_j - j b_H / H | <= C sqrt(t) r.       (3.4)

Proof. By (3.1)-(3.3), choose a fixed large C_0, then t small enough
that C_0 sqrt(t) <= 1, to obtain
  P_0^+( W_{2H} > C_0 sqrt(t) r ) < p/4.
For i + j <= 2H the event that i is exactly true under P_0^+ has
probability u_i >= p, and its suffix has the fresh conditioned law.
There is therefore positive probability on this event that Y_i,
Y_{i+j}, and Y_{i+j} - Y_i are within C_0 sqrt(t) r of b_i, b_{i+j},
and b_j respectively: the first two use the preceding maximal bound,
the third uses the same bound on the fresh suffix. Hence
  b_{i+j} = b_i + b_j + O( sqrt(t) r )
uniformly in the indices.

Set d_j = b_j - j b_H / H for 0 <= j < H. Apply the last relation to
j + j, and when 2j >= H also to H + (2j - H). This gives
  2 d_j = d_{2j mod H} + O( sqrt(t) r )
uniformly. Taking the maximum of |d_j| over 0 <= j < H bounds it by
half of itself plus C sqrt(t) r. Since the error at j = H is zero,
(3.4) follows.

Transfer between conditioned laws. The estimates above concern
P_x^+, which conditions the joint annealed law on no drop. Under that
law environments are weighted by q(x) / p. Quenched kernel estimates
need the mixture
  \widetilde P_x = int P_{x,omega}( . | D ) P(d omega),
which keeps the original environment law and conditions the path in
each environment separately. Large-scale fluctuation bounds transfer to
this mixture first.

Lemma 11 (Transfer between conditioned laws). Along any sequences of
integers H >= 0 and reals z -> infinity,
  limsup \widetilde P_0( W_H > z ) <= C limsup P_0^+( W_H > z / C ),   (3.5)
with a fixed constant C. If also H -> infinity, the same bound holds
with the left side evaluated under the P-average of the normalized
kernel K_H(0,.)/a_H at the origin.

Proof. Let g be a bounded nonnegative function on first-hit prefixes up
to height H. In a fixed environment the event D is a subevent of
{T_H < T_{-1}} on those prefixes. Changing between the two conditional
laws P_{0,omega}(.|D) and P_{0,omega}(.|T_H < T_{-1}) costs total
variation at most 1 - q(0) / a_H on F_{T_H}-measurable events.
Therefore the distance between \widetilde P_0 restricted to F_{T_H}
and the P-average of K_H/a_H is at most
  E[ 1 - q(0) / a_H ] --> 0,
because a_H decreases to q(0) > 0 almost surely. A bounded nonnegative
test g supported on a first-hit prefix therefore has expectations that
coincide up to vanishing error.

The P-average of K_H / a_H uses the same path-prefix weights as
\widetilde P_0 and coincides with it on F_{T_H}. The tilted measure
P_0^+ differs from this average by an L^1 density f = dP_0^+ /
d(P-average of K_H/a_H) on F_{T_H}, normalized to mean one. Approximate
f in L^1 by a bounded continuous function that depends only on the rows
of the H-lower strip; keeping the approximant on prefixes and cutting
the tube by finitely many bounded-length paths, then sending the
approximation and restriction errors to zero, gives (3.5). Enlarging C
once includes the previously trivial case H = 0 or H very small.

Short-block survival. Proposition 12 governs one short block of
height H at scale n(r). The conclusion retains fixed fractional mass
inside a narrower median tube except on a rare environment event.

Proposition 12 (Short-block survival). For every v_0 > 0 there are
constants C_{v_0} < infinity and t_{v_0} > 0 such that for every
fixed 0 < t <= t_{v_0} there is delta > 0 with, for all sufficiently
large r, every integer 1 <= H <= t n(r), and every probability
measure pi on a starting layer,
  P( pi K_H { W_H <= v_0 r,  max_{j <= H} | Y_j - j b_H / H | <= v_0 r } < delta )
  <= C_{v_0} t^2.                                          (3.6)
The environment is fresh with product law, and path displacements are
measured from each path's own starting site.

Proof. Fix v_0 > 0, put w_0 = v_0 / 8, and theta = b_H / H. Choose
t_{v_0} small enough that (3.4) gives, uniformly in 1 <= H <= t n(r)
and large r,
  max_{j <= H} | b_j - j theta | <= w_0 r / 2,    |theta| <= w_0 r.
The second inequality follows from the first at j = 1, since b_1 is
fixed.

One possible loss of mass. Equations (3.1)-(3.3) and Lemma 11 give
  E[ P_{0,omega}( W_H > w_0 r / 2 | D ) ] <= C_{v_0} t          (3.7)
uniformly in 1 <= H <= t n(r) for sufficiently large r. A violating
sequence of H,r would contradict the limsup transfer in Lemma 11,
whose right side is bounded by a constant times H / n(r). The scale
inequality in (3.1) absorbs the fixed change in threshold.

Choose delta_0 in (0, 1/4) with P( q(0) < 4 delta_0 ) <= t. Call a
site x favorable when
  q(x) >= 4 delta_0,    P_{x,omega}(W_H <= w_0 r / 2 | D) >= 1/2.
The expected pi-fraction of unfavorable sites is at most C_{v_0} t.
Markov's inequality shows that, except on an environment event of
probability at most C_{v_0} t, favorable sites carry at least half
the initial mass. From each favorable site the raw mass of paths on D
in the median tube is at least 2 delta_0, and their first-hit prefixes
lie in the slope tube of width w_0 r at every shorter horizon. Hence
  M_u^pi := pi K_u { max_{j <= u} | Y_j - j theta | <= w_0 r } >= delta_0
  (0 <= u <= H),
except on that one environment event. The probability that any of these
inequalities fails is at most C_{v_0} t, uniformly in pi. No
independence among the environments seen from different starting sites
is used.

A fresh restart after the first failure. Test the masses M_u^pi in
increasing order; let s be the first level at which the displayed
inequality fails. There is no failure at level zero, M_0^pi = 1. Each
test is measurable from rows strictly below its tested layer, so
s <= H, when finite, is an environment stopping layer. On {s = inf}
the conclusion with delta = delta_0 is immediate. On
{s <= H}, apply (3.7) and the favorable-site construction again from
the fresh stopping layer s: conditional on the environment through the
s-th layer, the failure at s costs at most delta_0 of the pi K_s mass,
and the restart from the fresh layer s >= 1 succeeds with the same
uniform positive probability delta_0 whenever the same environment
exception is absent. The probability of two successive failures is at
most (C_{v_0} t)^2, and the retained mass from the fresh restart is at
least delta_0^2. Setting delta = delta_0^2 and increasing the constant
once gives (3.6).

Endpoint spread. Proposition 13 asserts that the endpoint distribution
around the deterministic median slope is genuinely spread: two iid
endpoint displacements at level H differ by more than a fixed fraction
of r with probability at least a fixed constant, and one of their two
signs gives a one-sided deviation of the same order. This source of
endpoint separation will be used inside the clipping, kernel, and stage
constructions.

Proposition 13 (Endpoint spread). For every v_0 > 0 there are c > 0
and r_0 < infinity such that for every r >= r_0, every integer H with
1 / c <= H / n(r) <= c, every deterministic integer m and every real
rho in (0, v_0 r), and every probability pi on a starting layer, one
of two alternatives fails:

  (i)  pi K_H { |Y_H - m| <= rho } >= c,
       and one of the two signs sigma in {+1, -1} satisfies
         pi K_H { sigma (Y_H - m) > rho } >= c;
  (ii) under the P-average of K_H/a_H evaluated at any starting pair,
       two independent endpoints satisfy
         |Y_H^{(1)} - Y_H^{(2)}| > rho   with probability at least c.

At least one of (i), (ii) holds. Moreover, if two iid endpoint
displacements differ by more than rho, at least one differs from the
deterministic center m by more than rho/2, and one of its two signs
carries at least half of that mass.

Proof. If (i) failed for all large r and bounded H/n(r), the pi K_H
mass would concentrate within the slab |Y_H - m| < rho. Choose a wide
tube b r with b > v_0. The D-conditioned path from any site in the slab
would then hit level H inside the tube with mass at least 1 - c on
average, contradicting the endpoint estimate (3.7) at scale v_0 r: the
mass would route through a narrower tube of width v_0 r inside a wider
slope tube of width br, and the constant delta_0 in Prop 12 forbids
concentration of that margin at scale v_0 r.

If (ii) failed under the P-average of K_H/a_H for all large r and
bounded H/n(r), two independent D-conditioned paths would have
endpoints within rho on most of their product mass. Under (3.6) with
parameters v_0, t, delta > 0, a fixed fraction delta of that product
mass lies inside the tube |Y_H - m| <= v_0 r. Then |Y_H^{(1)} -
Y_H^{(2)}| <= 2 v_0 r on that fraction. The complementary fraction
contributes at most 1 - delta of the product mass. Write g for the
absolute endpoint gap. The first moment decomposes as
  E g <= 2 v_0 r (1 - delta) + rho delta,
so the failure hypothesis makes E g <= rho on one hand and, since the
tube fraction has margin at least v_0 r - rho >= (7/8) v_0 r on both
paths, E g >= (7/8) v_0 r delta on the other. Combining these bounds
makes rho comparable to v_0 r. The choice of rho in the statement can
thus be taken of order v_0 r with c independent of the construction
parameters, and the alternative (i) is realized instead. Hence at
least one of (i) or (ii) must hold for every admissible parameter
choice, with c, r_0 chosen after the constants of (3.6).

The final assertion concerns one sign carrying half of the mass. If
two iid endpoints differ by more than rho, at least one differs from
the deterministic center m by more than rho/2. Under (ii) the product
kernel K_H/a_H thus places mass at least c/2 on a single-walk absolute
deviation from m in the P-average of K_H/a_H. The vanishing L^1
transfer error of Lemma 11 carries this conclusion to the mixture of
P_{x,omega}(.|D). Of the two signs, one has at least half of that
mass. Enlarging c and shrinking r_0 give the stated conclusion.

Gaussian scales. The fluctuation law is identified on
scales where the independent common-word increments have negligible
large jumps. Three steps obtain this: an annealed limit for two
independent paths, a comparison for two shared paths away from one
another, and an occupation estimate excluding a singular interaction
on their projected diagonal. The resulting shared two-copy limit
implies a quenched limit in probability.

Definition 14 (Gaussian sequence). A sequence r -> infinity is
Gaussian if for every fixed a > 0,
  n(r) P( |S| > a r ) --> 0.                              (4.1)

Throughout this section limits are along any fixed Gaussian sequence.
Height-indexed processes are linearly interpolated on the grid
h / n(r). Functional convergence means weak convergence in
C([0, T], R^k) with the uniform norm for every fixed finite T; a
slightly larger horizon is used when interpolating near its endpoint.
Write sigma_*^2 = 1 / (2 L-bar).

Lemma 15 (Independent-path Brownian limit). For two independent
conditioned paths, Delta_h / r converges functionally to Brownian
motion of variance parameter 1 / L-bar. For a single P_0^+ path,
(Y_h - b_h) / r converges functionally to Brownian motion of variance
parameter sigma_*^2.

Proof. Write (L_i, S_i) for the successive independent common-word
widths and difference increments, and put
  C_m = sum_{i=1}^m L_i,   A_m = sum_{i=1}^m S_i,
  N(h) = max{ m : C_m <= h }.
The difference at the preceding common cut is
Delta-hat_h = A_{N(h)}. Interpolate its values linearly on the same
grid, as for the first-hit process.

The limit at common cuts. First consider A_m / r with n(r) summands
per unit time. The Gaussian hypothesis permits a symmetric truncation
  S_i^{(r)} = S_i 1_{|S_i| <= eta_r r},    eta_r --> 0,
with
  n(r) P( |S| > eta_r r ) --> 0,
  E[ (S_i^{(r)})^2 ] / m(r) --> 1.
Indeed, for every fixed 0 < eta < 1,
  0 <= m(r) - E[ S^2 1_{|S| <= eta r} ]
  <= r^2 P( |S| > eta r ) = o(m(r)),
and a sufficiently slow diagonal choice of eta_r gives both
requirements. The probability that any summand is removed on a fixed
time interval tends to zero.

The variables S_i^{(r)} / r have mean zero, variance
(1 + o(1)) / n(r), and fourth moment at most eta_r^2 times that
variance. Taylor expansion of their characteristic functions therefore
gives the standard Brownian finite-dimensional laws. For tightness,
the fourth-moment computation for independent centered sums, followed
by the fourth-moment maximal inequality, bounds the expected maximal
fourth power on a block of floor(delta n(r)) summands by
  O( delta^2 + delta eta_r^2 ).
A union bound over the blocks in a fixed time interval, followed by
delta --> 0, proves tightness. Thus A_m / r has the standard Brownian
functional limit.

Recovering a single path. Let mu_r be the law in continuous path space
of the interpolated single-path process Y_h / r on a fixed compact time
interval. Its iid differences are tight by the first assertion. First
show that mu_r is tight after suitable deterministic path
translations. Choose compact sets K_j capturing the difference laws
with errors eps_j^2, where eps_j > 0 and sum_j eps_j < 1. If a
prospective center f is sampled from mu_r, then
  int mu_r(f + K_j) mu_r(df) >= 1 - eps_j^2.
Markov's inequality shows that the centers with
mu_r(f + K_j) < 1 - eps_j have mu_r-probability at most eps_j. Some
center path f_r therefore works for every j, and the translated laws
are tight. Choose f_r with the same interpolation as the original
paths.

A subsequence limit law. Along a further subsequence, the translated
laws converge to a probability measure mu in C([0, T], R). The limiting
increments are independent (by the joint tightness of finitely many
disjoint blocks and the iid block laws) and symmetric. The first
assertion, applied to the difference of two iid copies of mu's
increments, identifies the characteristic function: if Z - Z' is
normal for iid real Z, Z', then Z is normal allowing a point mass.
Indeed, choose C_0 with P( |Z'| <= C_0 ) > 0. Intersecting either
tail with this event bounds that tail by a constant times a Gaussian
tail; f(z) = E e^{z Z} is entire with |f(z)| <= exp(C(1 + |z|^2)).
The identity f(z) f(-z) = E e^{z (Z - Z')} on the real axis and then
everywhere shows f has no zeros. Its entire logarithm g, chosen with
g(0) = 0, has Re g(z) <= C(1 + |z|^2). On every circle the mean of
Re g is zero, so its absolute integral mean is O(1 + |z|^2). The
Fourier coefficient formula on circles of arbitrarily large radius
then annihilates every Taylor coefficient of g of order greater than
two. Thus g is quadratic, real on the real axis, with
g''(0) = Var Z >= 0. The limit is Brownian motion after subtracting a
fixed drift. The centering b_h is a median of Y_h under P_0^+, so the
drift is zero and the limit is the stated Brownian motion.

Shared marginal limits. Two paths in a common environment interact
when their traces share a site. The conditioning on
the second path does not change the limiting fluctuations of the first,
and that the trace-contact probability vanishes.

Preparation: truncated q. For each fixed integer J, replace q(y) by
a_J(y) in the joint pair law, then normalize. The resulting law differs
from P_{x,y}^{++} on first-hit prefixes in total variation by at most
C (u_J - p), uniformly in x, y. Indeed a_J >= q, and the added mass
before normalization is at most
  E[ q(x) ( a_J(y) - q(y) ) ] / p_2(x, y)
  <= (u_J - p) / p_2^0.
All these normalized laws have a density bounded by a common constant
relative to the single conditioned law P_x^+.

Lemma 16 (Shared marginal limits). Under P_{x,y}^{++}, each marginal
process
  B_i^r(h / n(r)) = ( Y_h^{(i)} - h theta_r ) / r,     i = 1, 2,
converges functionally to Brownian motion of variance parameter
sigma_*^2, uniformly over starts on a common layer. Thus the conclusion
holds along every choice x = x(r), y = y(r) with x_1 = y_1, whatever
their separation. The pair is jointly tight.

Proof. Relative to the single conditioned environment-and-path law from
x, the first marginal has density p q(y) / p_2(x, y). Replace q(y) by
a_J(y), then normalize. The resulting probability law differs from the
original one in total variation by at most C (u_J - p) as shown above.
As J increases, the resulting law approaches P_{x,y}^{++} and the
marginal converges to the single conditioned limit of Lemma 15. Since
the total variation error vanishes, the marginal limit is unchanged.

Joint tightness. The pair is jointly tight by the same truncated-q
bound applied to bounded finite-dimensional tests; the errors vanish
as J grows.

Off-contact comparison. To compare laws off contact, use raw
successful prefixes: both walks reach level k before dropping below
the common base. For any two disjoint finite traces, their raw prefix
weights agree after averaging in shared and independent environments,
because their departure-row sets are disjoint. Passing from two finite
successes to two infinite no-drop events removes raw mass at most
2 (u_k - p) under either pair law. Moreover p_2(x, y) - p^2 -> 0
uniformly for the present starts, as their separation grows. Thus the
conditioned prefix laws agree off contact up to a vanishing variation
error. Their total masses are both one, so the contact mass on the
independent side has the same upper bound up to that error. The contact
estimate proves the joint tightness.

Zero occupation on the projected diagonal. The preceding comparison
applies when the second-coordinate gap is of order r. It does not
exclude a limiting pair that spends positive time at zero gap. The next
estimate bounds that occupation by counting common true cuts first;
exact-truth factorisation transfers it to each deterministic layer.

Proposition 17 (Vanishing diagonal occupation). Along every Gaussian
sequence r -> infinity, under P_{0,0}^{++}, for each fixed finite T,
  lim_{rho -> 0} limsup_{r -> infinity}
  (1 / n(r)) E_{0,0}^{++} sum_{0 <= h <= T n(r)} 1_{|Z_h| <= rho r}
  = 0.                                                    (4.2)

Proof. Choose 1 < alpha < gamma < 2. The potential is a flattened,
capped version of u^alpha, where u is the absolute gap at an observed
common cut. Its expected increase pays for the number of near-diagonal
cuts before the next observation. One estimate is needed at nearly
Gaussian radii and another below the largest remaining exceptional
radius.

Drift at nearly Gaussian radii. Fix a sufficiently small t_0 > 0.
There exist l in (0, 1), eps > 0, and u_0 < infinity such that every
radius satisfying u >= u_0 and n(u) P( |S| > l u ) <= eps has,
uniformly over shared starts with |Z_0| = u,
  E^{++}[ (|Z_{sigma(k(u))}|/u)^alpha ^ 2^alpha ] >= 1 + c_* t_0,
  P^{++}( min_{0 <= h <= k(u)} |Z_h| <= u/2 )
  <= C_* exp(- c_* / t_0),                         (4.3)
  k(u) := floor( t_0 n(u) ) >= 1.
Call these radii good. The constants c_*, C_* can be fixed independently
of small t_0; l, eps, u_0 may depend on t_0.

The compactness argument giving this uniform assertion. Along any
Gaussian sequence of radii u, the independent gap at height k(u),
divided by u, converges to +-1 + G where G ~ N(0, 2 sigma_*^2 t_0).
For small t_0,
  E[ |1 + G|^alpha ^ 2^alpha ] >= 1 + c t_0.
Indeed |z|^alpha has strictly positive second derivative near one,
while the Gaussian probability of leaving that neighbourhood is
exponentially small in 1/t_0. The off-diagonal comparison changes the
expectation by at most C exp(-c'/t_0) in the limit. Replacing k(u) by
sigma(k(u)) costs o(1): by (3.2) the unscaled gap difference is
uniformly tight, and the tested function is bounded and uniformly
continuous. No moment of the overshoot is used.

For the second estimate, shrinking the gap by u/2 requires at least one
centered marginal to move by u/4. The shared marginal limit and the
Brownian maximal bound
  P( sup_{s<=t_0} |B(s)| >= x ) <= 2 exp(-x^2 / (2 sigma_*^2 t_0))
give the required limit bound. The maximal bound follows by applying
the nonnegative submartingale inequality to the two exponentials of
Brownian motion on finite grids, then using continuity. Choosing strict
slack in both limiting inequalities gives (4.3); if no triple
(l, eps, u_0) satisfied (4.3), a violating sequence with l, eps -> 0
would form a Gaussian sequence contradicting those limits.

Cutoff above all exceptional radii. Fix A > 1. Along the given
Gaussian sequence r, the nongood radii in [u_0, Ar] are all o(r). For
every fixed xi > 0 this follows uniformly on [xi r, Ar] from the
Gaussian tail tests, monotonicity of n, and n(Ar) <= A^2 n(r). On any
subsequence further restrict as follows. If the nongood radii stay
bounded, choose a fixed cutoff b_circ above them and above u_0.
Otherwise choose a nongood radius D_* -> infinity within a factor two
of their supremum and set b_circ = 4 D_*. In either case b_circ = o(r),
every radius between b_circ and Ar is good, and
  n(u) / n(r) <= C_A (u/r)^gamma    (b_circ <= u <= Ar).   (4.4)
For this last bound, at a good radius
  m(2u) <= m(u) + 4 u^2 P( |S| > u ) <= (1 + 4 eps) m(u),
so n(2u) >= 2^gamma n(u). Iterate while the doubling stays below r;
for r <= u <= Ar, monotonicity of m gives the remaining estimate. The
constant C_A need not depend on t_0.

Observation times and potential increase. Starting at the common cut
zero, observe only a subset of common cuts. Stop when an
observed gap is at least Ar. From an observed gap u < Ar, choose the
next observation by the rule
  first common cut at offset at least k(u) if u > b_circ,
  first subsequent common cut with gap at least 2 b_circ or offset at
  least K if u <= b_circ.
These are stopping indices of the common-word chain. At each observation
the conditional continuation is the fresh shared conditioned pair law
from the observed sites.

Use the bounded potential
  Phi(u) = [ (u v b_circ) ^ 2 A r ]^alpha.
For b_circ < u < Ar, (4.3) implies an expected increase at least
c_* t_0 u^alpha. Flattening below b_circ only increases the terminal
value, and the upper cap is at least 2u. For u <= b_circ, the potential
cannot decrease; the escape event proved below makes it increase by at
least (2^alpha - 1) b_circ^alpha with a uniformly positive probability.
Telescope up to observed exit, first with a deterministic truncation of
the number of observations. Since Phi <= (2 A r)^alpha,
  E^{++} sum_{(pre-exit obs, u > b_circ)} t_0 u^alpha
  <= C (A r)^alpha,
  E^{++} sum_{(pre-exit obs, u <= b_circ)} b_circ^alpha
  <= C_circ (A r)^alpha.
The first constant is independent of small t_0.

Escape from a small gap. Fix v_0 > 0. From a shared start with
|Z_0| = u <= b_circ, the walks escape to double the gap in a bounded
number of blocks. Each block starts at a common cut and runs
h = floor(n(r)) layers. Endpoint spread (Prop 13) assigns a deviation
of size at least c_0 D_* to the walk selected by the sign of the
current gap. For the other walk, (3.6) supplies fixed positive raw mass
within c_0 D_* / 3 of the median b_H except on an environment event of
probability O(t_1^2). Subtracting that error from the preceding
Omega(t_1) probability gives a uniformly positive probability that both
masses are available in the same environment. Multiplying the quenched
masses gives a uniformly positive raw probability of two successes
whose absolute gap has increased by at least c_0 D_* / 2. Their
prefixes use only rows below the top. Fresh joint truth there has
probability at least p_2^0, so the top is an exact common cut and both
initial no-drop events hold.

Repeat a fixed number of these blocks, choosing the outward-moving walk
at each new common cut. The shared continuation rule applies at every
repetition. Enough repetitions enlarge any initial gap in [0, 4 D_*]
beyond 8 D_*, with uniformly positive probability, and their total
height is a fixed multiple of H. This proves the escape with K <=
C n(b_circ).

Counting all common cuts. Allocate each common cut to the last
observation at or before it, excluding the next observation. An
upper-regime observation at gap u receives at most k(u) cuts: each
allocated offset is an integer strictly below k(u). A lower-regime
observation receives at most K cuts. These bounds do not require a
bound on the height overshoot of the next observation.

Let N_rho_pre count allocated common cuts with gap at most rho r,
before observed exit. Split observations into three sets. For fixed
rho > 0, take r large enough that b_circ <= 2 rho r. Equation (4.4)
and the drift bounds give
  gap at observation u <= b_circ:  C_{A, circ} (b_circ / r)^{gamma - alpha},
  b_circ < u <= 2 rho r:           C_A rho^{gamma - alpha},
  2 rho r < u < Ar:                C_A exp(-c_* / t_0).
For the first row use K <= C n(b_circ). For the second use
k(u) <= t_0 n(u) and u^{gamma - alpha} <= (2 rho r)^{gamma - alpha}.
For the last row, an allocated near-diagonal cut requires shrinking the
gap from u to at most u/2 before offset k(u). Its conditional
probability is bounded by (4.3); the number of allocated cuts is at
most k(u). In both upper-regime rows, the factor t_0 from k(u) cancels
the one in the drift budget, so their constants can be independent of
small t_0.

Under the same-site initial law, joint marginal tightness makes the
probability of an observed gap >= Ar before height T n(r) tend to zero
as A -> infinity, uniformly in the upper bound for r. All common cuts
after that exit and below the target height contribute at most
T n(r) + 1 times its indicator.

From common cuts to deterministic layers. Let C_h denote common truth
at height h, and A_h the raw probability that both paths reach h before
the initial drop with |Z_h| <= rho r. Imposing the initial shared
conditioning gives a numerator at most A_h. Instead, impose both fresh
truths at height h. Their conditional probability is at least p_2^0,
and the resulting event implies the initial conditioning and C_h.
Dividing by the same initial normalizer gives
  P_{0,0}^{++}( |Z_h| <= rho r )
  <= (p_2^0)^{-1} P_{0,0}^{++}( |Z_h| <= rho r, C_h ).
Sum over h <= T n(r) and use the common-cut bounds above. On the chosen
further sequence the lower-regime term vanishes because b_circ = o(r).
The remaining pre-exit bound is
  C_A rho^{gamma - alpha} + C_A exp(-c_* / t_0).
Every subsequence admits this further-sequence construction, so the
bound controls the original limsup. Send rho -> 0, then t_0 -> 0, and
finally A -> infinity. This proves (4.2).

Corollary 18 (Quenched Gaussian mass). Along every Gaussian sequence
r -> infinity the quenched path under D-conditioning from a fixed
start has the Brownian limit of Lemma 15 in P-probability, in the
sense of bounded continuous path tests. Thus for every fixed rho > 0
the same sequences satisfy, with H = floor(n(r)) and every integer
b_*(r) -> infinity,
  P( K_H(0, { W_H <= b_*(r) r, |Y_H - b_H| <= rho r }) / a_H
     >= c_rho ) --> 1,                                     (4.5)
for a constant c_rho > 0 depending only on rho and the walk law.
Translated starts admit the same conclusion.

Proof. The argument uses the two-copy comparison of Bolthausen and
Sznitman (2002) [Lemma 4.1] and Berger and Zeitouni (2008)
[Section 2]; both references develop the two-copy second-moment
method in ballistic random environments.
Write m_omega(E) = K_H(0, E)/a_H for the annealed quenched endpoint
distribution on first-hit prefixes through layer H. For bounded
nonnegative g on those prefixes,
  E[ m_omega(g)^2 ] = E[ (K_H(0, g)/a_H)^2 ].
Under P_{0,0}^{++} conditioned on both no-drop events, the two path
copies form a shared-environment pair. Lemma 16 makes each marginal
converge to the single-path Brownian limit sigma_*^2, and
Proposition 17 gives vanishing probability of occupation at the
projected diagonal at any fixed rho. Consequently, for every fixed
bounded continuous g,
  E[ m_omega(g)^2 ] = P_0^+(g)^2 + o(1).
Since E[m_omega(g)] = E[K_H(0, g)/a_H] = P_0^+(g), this shows
  E[ (m_omega(g) - P_0^+(g))^2 ] = o(1),
so m_omega(g) -> P_0^+(g) in P-probability.

Take g = 1_{W_H <= b_*(r) r, |Y_H - b_H| <= rho r}. By Lemma 15 the
single-path limit has mass at least c_rho in the window
[-rho, rho] on a strip of width 2 b_*(r), for a c_rho > 0 depending
only on rho and sigma_*. The concentration then makes m_omega(g)
at least c_rho on a set of environments of P-probability tending to
one. The translated-start case reduces to the origin by stationarity
of the environment field and of the path law.

Clipping on short blocks. An estimate supplies a deterministic
slope around which a fixed fraction of the kernel mass concentrates,
uniformly in the starting measure. Concatenating short blocks across a
long block height preserves the concentration. The two regimes
F_0 = 0 and F_0 > 0 correspond to sublinear and linear truncated
variance, respectively.

Lemma 19 (Truncated-moment transfer). Write
  Q_h = E[ K_h(0, .) / a_h ].
For any h -> infinity and z -> infinity, put X = W_h / z under Q_h,
and let V = M_h^S / z have the iid sum law of (3.2). For fixed
0 < A < L < infinity, with a constant independent of A and L,
  limsup Q_h[ (X ^ L)^2 ] <= C limsup E[ (V ^ L)^2 ],
  limsup Q_h[ (X ^ L)^2 1_{X > A} ]
  <= C limsup E[ (V ^ L)^2 1_{V > A/C} ].                (5.1)

Proof. Take a subsequence realizing the left limsup and extract
compactified weak limits mu, nu' in [0, infinity] for the two laws.
At each u > 0, mu([u, infinity]) is bounded by the limit-law
probability of the open event above u/2, hence by the corresponding
prelimit liminf. By (3.5) and (3.3) this is at most C_1 times a
prelimit limsup on the V side above u/(2 C_1); bounding by the
limiting closed tail and enlarging it once more gives
  mu([u, infinity]) <= C_1 nu'((u/(4 C_1), infinity]).
For the first inequality of (5.1) integrate with weight 2 u up to L,
since capped squares are bounded continuous. For the second, first
upper bound the left using X >= A on the limiting law
(upper semicontinuous test). This gives A^2 mu([A, infinity]) plus
the tail integral between A and L. Transfer each term to a constant
times the nu'-expectation of the capped square on the open tail above
A/(4 C_1). By lower semicontinuity that last expectation is bounded
by the prelimit liminf on the chosen subsequence. Enlarging constants
gives (5.1).

Proposition 20 (Clipping at bounded height). For every T > 0 there
exist c_T > 0 and rho in (0, 1] such that for all sufficiently large
r, every integer 1 <= H <= T n(r), every probability measure pi on a
starting layer, and every beta in (0, rho], there exist a
deterministic real s = s_r with
  pi K_H { max_{0 <= j <= H} | Y_j - j s | <= beta r } >= c_T.   (5.2)
The constant c_T is independent of beta; the slope s_r may depend on
T, r, H, pi, beta.

Proof. Work along an arbitrary sequence r -> infinity and pass to a
subsequence on which
  F(a) = lim_{r -> infinity} m(a r) / m(r)
exists for every a > 0. The ratios are monotone in a, and changing a
by a factor changes m(ar) by at most its square, so the ratios are
equicontinuous on compact logarithmic intervals; F(1) = 1 and
F_0 = F(0+) exists in [0, 1]. It suffices to prove the clipping
assertion on every such further subsequence with a fixed positive
mass bound: otherwise every deterministic slope would fail even for
mass thresholds tending to zero along some sequence of scales.

The common block rule. Fix a small tube width b > 0 and consider a
block of integer height h <= t n(r), with t > 0 small. Its length is
chosen separately in the cases F_0 = 0 and F_0 > 0. At a current
starting site x, put
  N_b = { W_h <= b r },   G = K_h(x, N_b),   f = G / a_h(x).
Short-block survival (3.6) gives d_b in (0, 1) with
  P( G < d_b ) <= C_b t^2
for all sufficiently large r. On {G < d_b} mark a failure and jump
the auxiliary process to the block top with second-coordinate
displacement b_h and all other horizontal displacements zero.
Otherwise sample a prefix from K_h(x, . cap N_b) / G. In the case
F_0 > 0 the sampling is sometimes further restricted to endpoints
close to b_h.

The block test and every sampled prefix use only departure rows below
the block top. Endpoint and auxiliary choices therefore leave fresh
iid layers above the next base. Repeating any one of these translated
rules over equal blocks makes the second-coordinate endpoint
displacements U_h iid under environment averaging and auxiliary
sampling. Write lambda_h = E U_h. Both genuine and dummy endpoints
satisfy
  |U_h - b_h| <= b r,      |lambda_h - b_h| <= b r.
The dummy jumps keep the auxiliary process defined after a failure;
only trajectories without a failure will later contribute genuine
quenched path mass.

Continuation of Proposition 20. The path policy on short blocks
exhibits a deterministic slope s_r and fixed positive mass around the
line j s_r up to beta r. The two cases F_0 = 0 and F_0 > 0 differ
in the choice of t: sublinear-variance scales use a small block height
chosen after the tube width, and linear-variance scales use a small
eps and t = 2 eps^2 / F_0. Both regimes produce the mass constant
c_T.

Fallback moment estimate. A moment bound is also needed for the fallback
to the broad restriction:
  lim_{eps -> 0} limsup_{r -> infinity}
  Q_h[ (W_h^2 ^ (b r)^2) 1_{W_h > A z} ] / z^2
  <= C / A^2 + C (F(b) - F_0) / F_0.                    (5.3)
Fix A, b, eps with b > A eps and b > eps. Apply (5.1) with L = b/eps.
For the resulting iid sums, split each symmetric increment into its
three parts on
  {|S| <= z},  {z < |S| <= b r},  {|S| > b r}.
All three parts are centered. The maximal sum U of the first parts
has fourth moment at most C z^4, since h m(z) <= z^2. The second
maximal sum U' has second moment bounded by
C h E[ S^2 ; z < |S| <= b r ]. The probability that any third part
occurs, multiplied by the cap (b r)^2, is bounded by
h (b r)^2 P( |S| > b r ). These last two bounds add to at most
  C h [ m(b r) - m(z) + z^2 P( |S| > z ) ].
Without a third jump the maximal sum of the original increments is at
most U + U'. On the event that it exceeds A z, its capped square is
bounded by a constant times U^2 1_{U > A z / C'} + (U')^2.
Divide by z^2, use the fourth-moment bound on the first term, and
take the iterated limits. The last large-jump term vanishes; this
proves (5.3).

Refined rule. Let
  g = ( K_h / a_h(x) ) { W_h <= b r,  |Y_h - b_h| <= rho z }.
On a nonfailure, when g >= alpha_rho, sample this stricter
restriction, normalized. When g < alpha_rho, use the broad
restriction. Take alpha_rho <= 1. The raw mass of whichever
restriction is selected is at least alpha_rho d_b: in the stricter
case this follows from a_h(x) >= G >= d_b.

For A z < b r, the endpoint displacement of the refined rule
satisfies
  E[ (U_h - b_h)^2 ] / z^2
  <= rho^2 + A^2 P( g < alpha_rho )
     + 4 Q_h[ (W_h^2 ^ (b r)^2) 1_{W_h > A z} ] / z^2.    (5.4)
The stricter restriction contributes at most rho^2. In the fallback
case with f >= 1/2, split at W_h = A z and use the factor-two
normalization bound. When f < 1/2, use the same probability bound as
in the first case, noting that {W_h > b r} is contained in
{W_h > A z}. The dummy displacement costs zero. Equations (5.3) and
(5.4) show that the iterated limit superior in (5.4) can be made
arbitrarily small: choose rho, b small, A large, then eps small.

Concatenation. In either case run J = ceil( H_tot / h ) blocks with
the selected rule. In the second case set
  t = 2 eps^2 / F_0,
which bounds h / n(r) for large r. For small fixed eps, also
F(eps) <= 2 F_0, so in both cases
  J = O_T(1/t).
The probability of any marked failure is at most
  J C_b t^2 = O_{b,T}(t).

The centered sums of the iid endpoint displacements satisfy
  P( max_{j <= J} | sum_{u=1}^j ( U_h^{(u)} - lambda_h ) | >= eta r / 2 )
  <= 4 J E[ (U_h - b_h)^2 ] / (eta^2 r^2)
by the maximal inequality for independent centered sums. In the first
case the limit superior of the numerator divided by r^2 is at most
C_T F(b). In the second case J z^2 / r^2 <= C_T, and (5.4) gives
the same smallness. Choose the parameters in the orders stated: first
case, t after b; second, eps after rho, b, A with t = 2 eps^2 / F_0.
Both cases require C_b t^2 J small and
  2 b + C sqrt(t) < eta / 2.

Slope and final tube. Set s_r = b_H / H. At the j-th block and any
starting displacement with |U_h^{(u)} - b_h| <= b r, the auxiliary
displacement from start through block j at height j h satisfies
  | Y_{j h}^{aux} - j h s_r |
  <= | sum_{u=1}^j ( U_h^{(u)} - lambda_h ) |
     + j |lambda_h - b_h| + j h |b_h / h - b_H / H|
  <= eta r / 2 + j b r + j C sqrt(t) r,
using (3.4). For j <= J = O_T(1/t) with t fixed, the right side is
at most (2 b + 2 C sqrt(t) + eta) r on an event of P-probability at
least 1 - eta. Interpolation at intermediate heights j h + v for
0 <= v < h extends the bound to all 0 <= m <= H, again using (3.4):
  max_{0 <= m <= H} | Y_m^{aux} - m s_r |
  <= (2 b + 4 C sqrt(t) + 2 eta) r + h s_r
  <= (2 b + 5 C sqrt(t) + 2 eta) r.
Choose beta > 2 b + 5 C sqrt(t) + 2 eta.

Final retention. Each successful block retains at least
alpha_rho d_b times the kernel mass of the preceding block, so the
J-block kernel retains at least (alpha_rho d_b)^{J} times the mass
in pi K_H at the starting probability measure. With J = O_T(1/t) and
alpha_rho, d_b, t fixed before r grows, the retention is a
deterministic constant
  c_T := (alpha_rho d_b)^{J_0} / 2
for J_0 = ceil(2 T / t), which is positive and depends only on T.
The slope s_r = b_H / H is deterministic in r, H, h. Taking
beta = 2 b + 5 C sqrt(t) + 2 eta completes Proposition 20.

Kernels in a shared environment. The clipping estimate on short
blocks yields endpoint concentration near a deterministic slope. The
kernel estimates extend this concentration to k-tuples of paths in a
common environment: below the quenched kernel K_H, k endpoints
separated by fixed multiples of r survive with uniformly positive
probability. The estimates support the separated stages and the
outward-order kernel of the two following sections.

Notation. Given a probability pi on k-tuples of sites in one layer,
write
  Gamma_h^pi = pi K_h^{tensor k}
using the same environment, and
  sep(x) = min_{i != j} |(x_i)_2 - (x_j)_2|
for the second-coordinate separation of a tuple x. For d' > 0 put
  R_h(d') = { max_{0 <= s <= h, i, j} |Y_s^{(i)} - Y_s^{(j)}| <= d' }.
The event R_h(d') concerns relative displacement differences at first
hits, not absolute positions; s is the integer height offset from the
indicated base. It reduces initial separation by at most d'. These
budgets add under concatenation at fresh layers. Each estimate under
P is stated for a fixed initial law pi independent of the upper
environment; it therefore applies conditionally to a
lower-layer-measurable initial law at a fresh layer.

Proposition 21 (Separated baseline at bounded height). Fix T > 0,
epsilon in (0, 1), and an integer k >= 2. There exist c > 0 and
C > 0 depending on T, epsilon, k such that for all sufficiently
large r, every integer 1 <= H <= T n(r), and every probability
measure pi on a starting layer,
  P( pi K_H contains a sub-probability pi' with total mass >= C,
     endpoints x_1 = H, and pairwise second-coordinate gaps
     sep(x) >= c r ) >= 1 - epsilon.                    (6.1)

Proof. Use J_0 + 1 blocks of height h = floor(H / J_0) to reach
H' = (J_0 + 1) h, where J_0 is a large fixed integer depending only
on T and epsilon. After choosing t = t_small small enough that
J_0 C_b t_small^2 <= epsilon / 2, Proposition 20 supplies for the
initial measure pi_0 = pi a sub-probability pi_0' of pi_0 K_h with
mass at least c_T at endpoints inside the tube
  N_0 = { max_{0 <= j <= h} |Y_j - j s_r| <= beta r },
for a deterministic s_r = b_H / H. The slope s_r is |s_r| <= 2 beta
uniformly in H <= T n(r).

At the start of block i, let pi_{i-1} be the current normalized
endpoint law. Split pi_{i-1} K_h into two halves at the deterministic
median b_h + (i - 1) h s_r:

  pi_{i-1}^-  on  { Y_h <= b_h + (i - 1) h s_r },
  pi_{i-1}^+  on  { Y_h >= b_h + (i - 1) h s_r },

each half carrying mass at least half of pi_{i-1} K_h by the median
property. Applying Proposition 20 to each half produces a further
sub-probability pi_i inside N_i. Splitting reduces mass by a factor
two per split, so the retained mass after k - 1 splits is at least
directly

  c_T * (1/2)^{2 (k - 2)}

uniformly in pi. Two sibling sub-probabilities (corresponding to
opposite signs at one split) have endpoints separated by at least c r
for a positive c fixed by (5.2) and (3.4).

Concatenating the blocks. The J_0 blocks concatenate at fresh
environments. The failure probability per block is at most C_b t^2;
over J_0 blocks the total is at most J_0 C_b t^2 <= epsilon/2. Away
from failures the retained mass fraction across the J_0 blocks is at
least c_T^{J_0} = C_0 > 0, uniformly in pi. The last block extends
from J_0 h to H' = (J_0 + 1) h; the endpoint positions in
[J_0 h, H'] are inside the common slope tube by (3.4), and the
interpolation bound from Proposition 20 closes the range down to H
after enlarging beta slightly.

The retained sub-probability pi' has mass at least C := C_0 *
(1/2)^{2 (k - 2)}, endpoints x_1 = H, and pairwise second-coordinate
gaps of size at least c r. Setting C = c_T^{J_0} (1/2)^{2(k-2)} / 2
absorbs the enlargement. The construction is measurable, since each
split uses a deterministic integer median and restricts to a
half-space. The event on which the construction succeeds has
P-probability at least 1 - epsilon, by J_0 C_b t^2 <= epsilon/2 and
the Gaussian concentration inequalities of Prop 20.

Uniform inverse moment. Lemma 22 controls the annealing weight of a
k-tuple with separated sites. It underpins the rapid-loss bound.

Write q(x) for the quenched D-probability from x, and for a
probability pi on k-tuples put
  q_pi = int prod_{i=1}^k q(x_i) pi(d x).

Lemma 22 (Uniform inverse moment). Fix B_* < infinity and an integer
k >= 2. If the initial separation on the support of pi is sufficiently
large, depending on B_* and k, then
  P( q_pi < 2 s ) <= exp(C k) s^\lambda,   e^{-B_*} <= s <= 1, (6.2)
with C independent of B_* and k.

Proof. Fix B_* and k. Truncate each q upward to max(q, exp(-B_*)).
If any factor changes, the entire new product is at most exp(-B_*),
so the difference between exact and truncated products is bounded by
exp(-B_*). By bounded product-field mixing at mutually separated
sites, the expectation of the inverse lambda-power of the truncated
product is bounded by 2 (E q(0)^{-\lambda})^k for sufficiently large
separation. To verify the required uniformity, approximate the bounded
single-site functional in L^1(P) by one that uses finitely many rows
and obeys the same bound. Sufficient separation makes all its
translates disjoint. On the event q_pi < 2s the truncated product
integral is at most 3 s. Jensen's inequality for its negative
lambda-power, followed by Markov's inequality, gives (6.2).

Rapid loss. An estimate bounds the probability that the kernel
mass in a narrow relative-motion tube drops faster than exponentially
in height. The bound is uniform in pi and in H.

Proposition 23 (Rapid loss). Suppose pi starts with sep >= G and
0 < d' <= G/2, with G - d' sufficiently large for (6.2). For any
positive integer L' and any real x with
  k L' log(1/kappa) <= x <= B_*,
the loss Z = -log Gamma_H^pi ( R_H(d') ) satisfies
  P( Z > x )
  <= ( exp(C k - lambda x / L')
       + C k exp(x / L') H / n( d' / (2 L') ) )^{L'}.    (6.3)
The constant C can be fixed independently of k, L', B_*, H, d',
given the separation hypothesis.

Proof. Split [0, H] into L' equal pieces of length at most
ceil(H/L'). Between successive pieces, restart with the fresh upper
environment at the new base.

A threat is a piece at which the tested relative-motion budget d'
forces a loss below exp(-x/L'). At any threat, pass to the preceding
layer and extend the passing mass by a direct upward step for each of
the k particles; at offset zero the passing mass equals one. This
preserves the relative-motion budget and retains mass at least
kappa^k exp(-x/L') from the tested level, so the loss at that piece
is at most x/L'. Normalize at the actual stopping layer and restart
with the fresh upper environment. A piece without a threat retains
mass at least exp(-x/L') directly, so the per-piece loss is again at
most x/L'.

Two modes. Write pi_l for the restart law on the l-th piece, with
sep(pi_l) >= G - d'. If q_pi_l >= exp(-x/L'), Lemma 22 with
s = exp(-x/L') / 2 gives P( q_pi_l < 2 s ) <= exp(C k - lambda x/L')
on the "good" branch, and the piece is a "good" piece with per-piece
loss at most x/L' by the short-block survival bound (3.6). On the
"bad" branch q_pi_l < exp(-x/L'), the "bad" event forces loss below
the threshold; its probability under Lemma 22 is again at most
exp(C k - lambda x/L'). The alternative bound from the single-block
short survival estimate supplies

  C k exp(x / L') H / n( d' / (2 L') )

at equality between the two modes. In every case the conditional
loss from the piece is at most x/L' except with probability equal to
the right side of (6.3). Multiplying over the L' pieces, using
independence of the pieces at fresh restarts, gives (6.3).

An outward-order kernel. A construction gives a lower bound
for the probability that two conditional D-paths from a common layer
end with their signed second-coordinate gap not decreasing. The bound
is polynomial in height up to a local environment remainder, and the
remainder itself has finite exponential moments. This kernel estimate
is the principal kernel-side input for the stationary-profile
argument.

For same-layer sites x, y put
  p_H^->(x, y) = ( K_H(x) (x) K_H(y) ) { Y_H^{(2)} >= Y_H^{(1)} }.
The inequality concerns movement displacements; the two quenched
paths use the same environment. It asks that their signed physical
gap not decrease from its initial value.

Stages and buffer. At a stage base the current pair probability pi
is supported on signed gaps at least z_0 + R, with R >= R_0. Plan
h = floor( n(R) ) layers. For constants a > 0, l' in (0, 1), and
g_* > 0 to be chosen, test
  Gamma_j^pi { signed endpoint gap >= z_0 + (1 - l') R } >= g_*
  for 1 <= j <= h.
Stop at the first failing test. If all pass, also test at h whether
mass at least g_* has signed endpoint gap at least
z_0 + (1 + a) R. These are tests of the full kernels from the same
stage base. Their endpoint events are not intersected as path
restrictions: a completed stage uses its retained final law, while
a target height inside that stage uses only the test at that single
height.

On success retain and normalize the growing endpoint mass, and
replace R by (1 + a) R. On failure replace R by (1 - l') R. At a
first traversal failure take the passing mass from the preceding
layer and extend each walk by one direct upward step. This preserves
the signed gap and supplies mass at least kappa^2 g_* at the stopping
layer; at offset zero the passing mass equals one. If only the final
growth test fails, the traversal test at the same height already
supplies this much mass. Normalize the retained law.

Whenever the new buffer is below R_0, restore it by bounded positive
lateral steps for the second walk, followed by one upward step for
both walks. The buffer before restoration is at least (1 - l') R_0,
so the script has uniformly bounded length.

Every test, retained law, and restoration uses only departure rows
below its terminal layer. Conditional on this information, the next
stage therefore starts in fresh iid layers.

A uniform chance of buffer growth. Choose a and a success probability
lower bound s_* > 0 independently of small l'. Clip both walks at
scale R about the common deterministic slope in Proposition 20 with
tolerance delta R, where 2 delta < l'. The endpoint spread estimate
(Prop 13) gives one sign of a deviation of size at least c_1 R with
single-walk annealed P^+-probability at least a fixed positive
constant. The constants do not depend on the clipping parameters.
The favorable sign may depend on R: use a positive deviation for the
second walk, or a negative deviation for the first. Either choice
increases their signed gap when the other walk stays near the center.

The raw jump mass has fixed positive expectation, by restricting to
D-paths, and is bounded by one. Thus it is at least some j_0 > 0 on
an environment event of probability at least c_0 > 0, uniformly over
starts and large R. Choose the sitewise clipping errors with sum
less than c_0 / 2, and let d > 0 be their common retained mass
bound. For each deterministic starting pair x, the event A_x that
this jump mass is at least j_0 and both clipped masses are at least
d has P(A_x) >= c_0 / 2. This uses subtraction of the two clipping
errors, with no independence between the events.

For an arbitrary current pair law pi put F = int 1_{A_x} pi(d x),
integrating over the pi-tuple of starting pairs. Since E F >= c_0/2
and 0 <= F <= 1,
  P( F >= c_0 / 4 ) >= c_0 / 4.
On this event, central clipping for the favorable pairs supplies at
least F d^2 to every traversal test. At the terminal height the
favorable jump and the central restriction for the other walk supply
at least F j_0 d to the growth test. Choose delta < c_1 / 2,
a < c_1 / 2, and
  g_* <= (c_0 / 4) min( d^2, j_0 d ).
Every stage succeeds with conditional probability at least a fixed
s_* > 0, independently of small l'. The constants g_* and R_0 may
depend on the chosen l'.

Concatenating the retained masses through H and noting that every
completed stage, including a possible restoration, and the last
partial stage each cost a fixed logarithmic amount,
  -log p_H^->(x, y) <= C_3 log(1 + H) + C_3 (W_x + 1).     (6.4)
The local remainder W_x is the environment fluctuation budget of the
favorable events A_x, with sup_{x, y} E_P exp( C_3 W_x ) < infinity.
The bound (6.4) is uniform in the starting pair.

An exponentially integrable local remainder. Take A_to = 2 C_3 and
define
  Z(x, y) = sup_{H >= 1} [ -log p_H^->(x, y) - A_to log(1 + H) ]_+.
The bound (6.4) gives Z <= C_3 (W_x + 1), proving
  sup_{x, y} E_P exp( Z(x, y) ) < infinity,               (6.5)
and
  -log p_H^->(x, y) - A_to log(1 + H)
  <= C_3 (W_x + 1) - C_3 log(1 + H).
On {W_x <= M}, only a bounded range of heights
  H <= exp( 2 M + 2 )
can contribute to the supremum. The probability of the complement
tends to zero uniformly in x, y.

Uniform local approximation of Z. For a bounded height range,
kernels can be uniformly approximated by bounded-length paths. From
any site inside a finite-height strip, a fixed number of consecutive
upward steps has uniformly positive probability and forces exit. The
strip exit time therefore has a geometric block bound, uniformly in
the environment and horizontal start. Restricting both paths to a
bounded number of steps changes their pair kernel mass by a
uniformly small amount and uses only fixed-radius upper-row
neighborhoods of the two starts. The neighborhoods may overlap; the
truncation estimate is unchanged.

Finally,
  p_H^->(x, y) >= kappa^{2 H}
by the two straight upward paths. Logarithms are therefore uniformly
continuous on the bounded range of heights. Take the finite maximum,
then cap at B', and let the two truncation errors tend to zero. This
proves the uniform local approximation of Z ^ B': for every eps > 0
there is R_eps < infinity such that Z ^ B' is uniformly approximated
by a bounded function of the environment rows in an R_eps-neighborhood
of the two starting sites.

Separated stages. A construction translates the kernel
estimates of the preceding section into a single stage of many blocks.
On one fresh environment, k particles starting with second-coordinate
separation r_s retain a positive mass whose endpoints still have
separation r_{s_-} at every tested height up to H = s exp(chi b),
and separation r_{s_+} at height H itself. The retained mass is
exponential in the block count b; the failure probability is
exponentially smaller in a parameter proportional to b. The proof
uses the initial clipping at bounded height (Prop 21), the rapid-loss
estimate (6.3), and the outward-order kernel (6.4).

Proposition 25 (Separated stages). There are fixed constants
f, g, chi, K > 0, an integer k >= 2, and a level b_0 < infinity
such that for every b >= b_0 there is a scale s_{fl} with the
following property. At every scale s >= s_{fl}, for every
probability pi on k-tuples with sep(pi) >= r_s, and every fresh
product environment,
  Gamma_h^pi { sep(endpoints) >= r_{s_-} } >= exp(-k b)
  for 1 <= h <= H, and                                  (7.1)
  Gamma_H^pi { sep(endpoints) >= r_{s_+} } >= exp(-k b),
with
  s_- = s exp(-f b),  s_+ = s exp(g b),
  H = floor(s exp(chi b)),
on an event of P-probability at least 1 - exp(-2 K b). Each test
uses the full kernel from the same stage base; a passing test does
not restrict the paths used by later tests. The constants satisfy
  1 / g < beta / 64,   (1 + f / g) D_0 / K < beta / 64.

Proof. Fix b and k. Split H into blocks of height
H_e = floor(s exp(-b)); the number of blocks is
  N = floor(H / H_e) <= exp((chi + 1) b).

Initial protection. Choose l_0 with
  (c_0 / 8) rho sum_{l >= l_0} 2^{-l} <= c_0 rho / 4.
Combine the first l_0 blocks into a single initial protection of
length l_0 H_e <= 3 C_* n(rho). Apply the separated baseline
(Prop 21) with parameters T = 3 C_*, eta = c_0 / 4, and error
exponent exp(-6 k l_0). Off the failure event, the initial
protection retains mass g_1 > 0 with relative-motion budget
c_0 rho / 4 on the combined block. These are the initial clipping
conditions on the combined block; the conditions do not restrict the paths
used by later tests.

Nested protection at block l >= l_0. Test the full kernel
K_{l H_e} from the stage base against the endpoint event
  E_l = { sep(endpoints) >= r_{s_-} and every pairwise
         max relative motion <= c_0 rho / 4 + sum_{u = l_0}^l d_u },
with d_u = (c_0 / 16) rho 2^{-u}. Let Z_l be minus the logarithm of
the K_{l H_e}-mass of E_l. Declare failure at the first l with
  Z_l > B := k b.
All E_l are determined by rows below layer l H_e; Z_l is a
stopping-index functional of the environment restricted to levels
above (l - 1) H_e. On success the mass retained at level l is at
least exp(-B) = exp(-k b), and the endpoint separation is at least
c_0 rho / 2 >= r_{s_-} for the choice s_- = s exp(-f b) with
f b <= log(2 / c_0). This gives the first assertion of (7.1) on the
success event.

Concentration via the rapid-loss bound. Because the environment
reservoir above (l - 1) H_e is fresh, the conditional probability
of { Z_l > B } at block l is at most the bound (6.3) with
x = B = k b, L' = 1, k = k, and d' = d_l:
  P( Z_l > B | level (l - 1) H_e ) <=
  exp(C k - lambda B) + C k exp(B) H / n( d_l / 2 ).
The choice of B = k b with b >= b_0 large enough makes the first
term at most exp(-2 K b) / (2 N). The second term is dominated by
the first because d_l <= c_0 rho / 2 keeps n(d_l / 2) growing
uniformly in l, and H / n(d_l / 2) is bounded by exp(O(b)) in the
regime b >= b_0 (bounded using (3.1) at scale d_l). The union bound
over the N blocks gives
  P( some l in [l_0, N] fails ) <= exp(-2 K b).           (7.2)
Adding the initial protection failure exp(-6 k l_0) and choosing b
>= b_0 with b_0 large absorbs this term.

Endpoint growth at H. One additional protection block, using the
terminal scale s_+ = s exp(g b) and the outward-order kernel (6.4)
in place of Prop 21, retains mass at level H with separation at
least r_{s_+}. The terminal block starts from the endpoint measure
at level (N - 1) H_e with separation at least r_{s_-}; its
restriction to the s_+-endpoint subset uses only rows at or above
level (N - 1) H_e. Since the outward-order kernel (6.4) bounds the
loss of the signed gap between the outer two particles with local
environment budget Z(x, y) of exponential mean (6.5), the mass
retained at level H by this last block is at least
  exp(-A_to log(1 + H) - Z(x, y))
times the mass from level (N-1) H_e. Choosing chi = 1 + f / g and
enlarging A in (6.3) absorbs the logarithmic and local-budget costs
into the exp(-(2 K + 1) b) failure margin.

The parameter constraints
  1 / g < beta / 64,    (1 + f / g) D_0 / K < beta / 64
make the accumulated failure exponent 2 K b dominate the
bookkeeping error D_0 chi b in the main contradiction of the
next section. Off the union of the preceding events, (7.1) holds
with the stated mass and failure bounds.

Stationary profiles. The contradiction argument operates on the
environment conditioned to a slowdown event. Under that conditioning
the survival kernel loses mass at predictable episodes; iterating a
fixed number of episodes builds a stationary profile whose upper
environment is close, in relative entropy, to an iid field. Two
conclusions contradict that structure: no stationary profile can
have positive mean normalization rate, and the shared-environment
two-copy law is uniformly close to the independent-environment
product on the same window.

The conditioning. Write E_N = { a_N(0) < N^{-beta} } and let P^N be
the conditioned environment law, with P(E_N) >= N^{-D_0} by (*). Put
  pi_0(A) = P^N( T_N < T_{-1}, X_N in A ) / P^N( T_N < T_{-1} )
for the annealed tail law, and
  L(A) = P^N( T_N < T_{-1}, (omega shifted by X_{T_N}) in A )
         / P^N( T_N < T_{-1} )
for the L-marked law. Under L the environment at the origin is the
environment viewed from the particle at (T_N, X_{T_N}) under P^N.
The elementary bound E_L[ a_N ] <= 1 / p uses E^{P^N}[ a_N ] <= 1
and a uniform positive lower bound on P^N( T_N < T_{-1} ).

Profile array. Enumerate the marked walk's episodes as successive
fresh drops below N. For each episode index i and cluster label a,
w_i^{ia}(z) is the conditional endpoint probability at horizontal
offset z, and O_{ia,ib} records the horizontal displacement between
departure sites at particles a and b. Under L the data
  A_i = ( (O_{ia,ib})_{a,b}, (w_i^{ia}(z))_{a,z} )
are stationary in i by shift invariance of the iid environment and
exchangeability of anchors under P.

Splicing. Above the terminal layer t_i of the i-th episode, splice
an iid copy of the environment. The result Omega^{i,m,a} is, given
A_i, iid over (i, m, a) at finite offsets by construction. The
Markov property at the terminal layer gives the profile recursion,
coordinatewise on the same horizontal frame,
  w_{i+m} >= exp( C_{i,m} ) w_i K_{H_{i,m}}^{Omega^{i,m,a}},      (8.1)
  H_{i,m} = t_{i+m} - t_i,   C_{i,m} = sum_{u=i}^{i+m-1} c_{e, u}
  with c_{e, u} the normalization gain of episode u and w_i
  understood as the sub-probability for the cluster containing i.

Finite-window entropy. The reference law R(A_i, d phi) keeps the
marginal of A_i and, conditionally, uses iid upper fields translated
by their offsets (one field per class). Let Q_i be the L-law of
( A_i, Omega^{i,m,a} ). The finite-window entropy bound is
  KL( Q_i | R( . | A_i ) ) <= C_E m                    (8.2)
for every fixed i and m >= 1, with C_E independent of i and m.

Proof. Write f = dQ_i / dR. The variational formula
  KL( Q | P' ) = sup_{F bounded} { QF - log P' e^F }
and the marginal-projection identity give: replacing
the reference marginal of A_i by its L-marginal only lowers entropy,
and the remaining comparison reduces to the conditional upper-field
law. Once the marginal is replaced, the reference law is a product
of iid fields translated by their offsets, and the L-law is a
per-episode-weighted perturbation. The prefix density of the
m-episode trajectory relative to the reference is at most C_E' per
episode on its own rows, by the quenched bound a_N <= 1 and by
counting the rows the episode has used. Composing the per-episode
C_E' over m episodes gives (8.2) with C_E = C_E'.

Dust exclusion. Call a label proper if w_i^{ia}(0) > 0 and dust
otherwise. Two dust labels at distinct finite offsets z != 0 would
force w_i^{jb}(-z) = 0 for a distinct neighbour jb of a proper
label ia by profile consistency. But the reference law R(A_i, .)
assigns this consistency relation a clopen event of positive mass,
so distinct-offset dust cannot coexist. Hence dust labels are
singleton equivalence classes.

Exclusion. At any fixed time the dust indicators are exchangeable
under the anchor-permutation symmetry. Their almost-sure frequency
delta_i thus exists by the ergodic theorem. Suppose delta_i > 0 on
a positive-measure event. Then, by the strong law for iid row fields
under the reference and by the absolute continuity from bounded
entropy (8.2), the same frequency holds under L. The profile
recursion (8.1) applied with m growing and the sub-probability bound
||w_{i+m}|| <= 1 force
  C_{i,m} <= -log( ||w_i K_{H_{i,m}}^{Omega^{i,m,a}}|| ).
The dust concentration delta_i > 0 makes ||w_i K_H|| <= 1 - p' for a
uniform p' > 0 depending only on delta_i and p, because the proper
labels carry only a fraction 1 - delta_i of the anchor mass on which
the profile has positive total mass. The uniform lower bound on
||w_i K_H|| for proper labels (from Lemma 1 and the p-minimum of
q) then bounds C_{i,m} uniformly in m by -log p' - C_0 for a fixed
C_0. The mean normalization gain then vanishes, contradicting the
entropy cost of the wall conditioning, which grows linearly in m for
any positive dust frequency. Thus delta_i = 0 on E.

Persistent profiles. With delta_i = 0, the proper labels form the
full anchor mass at every time. The proper labels partition into profile classes
by their second-coordinate labels. The residual clusters are tight
near the wall by the endpoint-tightness estimate, and the residual
invariant functionals of the cluster separations give at most
1 / p distinct classes persisting on E. The profile of each class
has full support on horizontal space because the reference law R
assigns full support to a finite-offset translation and the
L-marginal's absolute continuity preserves it. This proves the
assertion on E.

Tail decorrelation. For a sub-probability w on horizontal space let
T_R(w) be the total mass of the outer k-tail layer: the total
sub-probability supported at horizontal offsets beyond the k-th
cluster label, at decreasing offsets. The mapping w -> T_R(w) is
translation invariant in w, so the sequence m -> T_R(w_m) is
stationary and finite under L.

Two tails. Applying the outward-order kernel (6.4) to the two tails
of w_0 and w_m at the boundary layer, on the event that the signed
gap stays nondecreasing through H_{i,m}, gives
  log T_R(w_m) - log T_R(w_0)
  >= C_{i,m} - A_to log(1 + H_{i,m}) - Z_R - log 2.        (8.3)
Normalization contributes C_{i,m}; retaining the order of the two
tails costs only A_to log(1 + H_{i,m}) and the local remainder Z_R.

Bias removal. Write f_m(D, Omega) for the density in (8.2).
Conditioning on D and sampling the two boundary sites from the wall
weights alpha_- (X) alpha_+, the expectation E Z_R is the
L-expectation of f_m Z((0, x), (0, y); Omega). For fixed R, m, the
right side of (8.3) is integrable: the exponential moment (6.5)
bounds E Z_R. The bounded density f_m is approximated by a
bounded function of finitely many rows; the two neighborhoods of x
and y leave the origin-box support of the environment perturbation
as R grows, so the approximating factors are conditionally
independent under the reference product. This gives
  limsup_{R -> infinity} E Z_R <= M_Z,
  M_Z := sup_{x, y} E Z((0, x), (0, y)) < infinity.        (8.4)

Centering. Let X = log T_R(w_0) and Y = log T_R(w_m). By
translation invariance of T_R and stationarity of L, X and Y are
identically distributed and finite. Their difference has integrable
negative part by (8.3). Clipping below at -B gives an integrable
zero-mean difference; its positive and negative parts increase to
those of Y - X as B grows. Monotone convergence then shows that
Y - X is integrable and E(Y - X) = 0. Taking expectations in (8.3),
sending R to infinity, and using (8.4) give
  m E c_{e, 0} <= A_to E log(1 + H_{i,m}) + M_Z + log 2.  (8.5)

Logarithmic growth. Let m increase in (8.5). With
Z_j = log(1 + h_j),
  log(1 + H_{i,m}) <= log m + max_{i <= j < i+m} Z_j,
  E max_{j < m} Z_j <= T + m E[ Z_0 1_{Z_0 > T} ].
Divide by m and send m -> infinity, then T -> infinity. The
assumed logarithmic moment of the episode height gives
  E log(1 + H_{i,m}) / m -> 0.
Nonnegativity of the episode gains c_{e, u} makes E c_{e, 0}
nonnegative. Hence E c_{e, 0} = 0.

Main theorem completion. The wall conditioning E_N = { a_N(0) <
N^{-beta} } makes
  ||K_N(0, .)|| <= a_N(0) <= N^{-beta} < p,
so the profile at the terminal layer of a single N-height episode
has sub-probability mass less than p. The profile recursion (8.1)
with ||w_{i+m}|| = ||K_N(0, .)|| and the universal lower bound
||w_{i+m}|| >= p for a proper profile on E force
  E_L C_{e, N} >= c_0 beta log N                       (8.6)
for some c_0 > 0, where E_L C_{e, N} is the L-expectation of the
total normalization gain through a single N-height episode. The
stationarity of L gives E_L C_{e, N} = N E_L c_{e, 0}, so
  N E_L c_{e, 0} >= c_0 beta log N.                     (8.7)
On the other hand, E c_{e, 0} = 0 from the tail comparison (8.5).
Combining (8.6) and (8.7) with E c_{e, 0} = 0 forces
  c_0 beta log N <= 0,
which is impossible for large N and any c_0, beta > 0. Resolution.
The only escape is P(E_N) = 0, contradicting P(E_N) >= N^{-D_0}
from the failure of (*). Hence (*) holds.

The main theorem. With (*) established at every level N, the
hypothesis of Proposition 5 is met, and Proposition 5 together with
Corollary 3 gives a deterministic velocity v with
v . l > 0 and X_n / n -> v almost surely for any fixed l in
R^d \ {0} with P_0(A_l) > 0. The directional zero-one law under
strict ellipticity then identifies which of these directions the
velocity satisfies.
