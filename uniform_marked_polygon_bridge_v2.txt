We prove quantitative estimates for the critical self-avoiding
walk on the honeycomb lattice, seen through three families of
geometric constraints. First, a bridge across a strip of
height h (a self-avoiding path from a fixed port on the bottom
cut to any port on the top cut) has total critical mass comparable
to h^{-1/4}. The first moment of its length is at most
Ch^{13/12}, and a positive fraction of the bridge mass is carried
by paths of length at least ch^{4/3}. The bridge diameter tail is
C(1+x)^{-1/4}.
Second, on a cylinder of period N, the total critical mass
of a simple polygon passing through two specified honeycomb links
at even axial displacement d is at most CN^{-4/3}(N/d)^{15/8},
uniformly in the axis direction, in the total separation d and
in the ratio N/d.
Third, the positive partition function of systems of
mutually disjoint polygons enclosing a fixed triangular lattice
vertex in a pure-sided hexagon of inradius R is comparable to
R^{1/12}.

The proofs come from a finite transfer-matrix calculation together
with a sequence of contour deformations of a
critical polygon gas. The transfer matrix reduces each physical
observable to a finite Laurent polynomial; the contour deformation
estimates that polynomial by a level-one boson expansion whose
zero modes and phase conventions are computed explicitly.

Three quantitative estimates for the critical self-avoiding walk
on the honeycomb lattice follow. They concern bridges of a strip,
pairs of marked edges on a cylinder, and nesting of disjoint
polygons about a marked vertex.

The honeycomb lattice, described dually.:  Take the
usual triangular lattice of points in the plane, which cuts the
plane into equilateral triangles. Colour the triangles black and
white according to the parity of a vertex of the triangular
lattice. Put one new vertex at the centre of each triangle. Join
two such vertices whenever the corresponding triangles share an
edge. The resulting graph -- vertices = triangle centres, edges =
``shared sides of adjacent triangles'' -- is the honeycomb
(hexagonal) lattice. Every edge joins a black centre to a white
centre.

A self-avoiding path gamma is a finite sequence of
distinct triangle centres v_0,...,v_n with v_j adjacent
to v_{j+1}. Its length is ell(gamma)=n+1, the
number of visited centres. Its weight is
[
w(gamma) = kappa^{ell(gamma)},
kappa = (2cos(pi/8))^{-1} = 1/√(2+sqrt 2).
]
The reciprocal kappa^{-1}=√(2+sqrt2) is the
connective constant of the honeycomb lattice; its
identification is a theorem of Duminil-Copin and Smirnov,
Ann. Math.~2012, proved by an exact local observable. Fix
kappa. Summing kappa^{ell(gamma)} over paths with prescribed
geometric constraints -- over all bridges of a strip, over
all polygons through two marks -- gives the three central sums.

The three theorems in plain words.: 

Theorem thm:finite-bridges (bridges). Take a strip
of height h between two horizontal lattice cuts, and fix one
boundary port on the bottom cut. A bridge is a
self-avoiding path from that port to any port on the top cut,
staying strictly between the cuts. Sum kappa^{ell(gamma)}
over all bridges. Then the sum B_h is comparable to
h^{-1/4} (positive lower and upper bounds), the first moment
sum ell(gamma)kappa^{ell(gamma)} is at most
Ch^{13/12}, and a positive fraction of the mass lies on
bridges of length at least ch^{4/3}. In words: crossing a
long strip is expensive but geometrical, so bridges are long (of
order h^{4/3}) and rare (of order h^{-1/4}).

Theorem thm:two-marks (two marked edges). Put two
specified honeycomb links on a cylinder of period N, with a
particular even axial separation d between them. The sum over
simple polygons (closed self-avoiding curves on the cylinder) that
pass through both marked links of kappa^{ell(gamma)} is at
most CN^{-4/3}(N/d)^{15/8}. In words: two marked edges cost an
extra N^{-4/3} beyond the baseline, and the further apart they
are (relatively), the more is gained. The bound is uniform in the
direction, so it is a genuine two-point correlation bound, not a
``homogeneous'' one.

Theorem thm:nesting (positive nesting). Around a
triangular lattice vertex y of a pure-sided hexagon of inradius
R, sum over systems of mutually disjoint polygons in that hexagon
that all enclose y, each with factor 2kappa^{ell}. Then the
resulting positive partition function Z_2(y;R) grows like
R^{1/12}, again with bounded constants. This ``positive''
comparison is the input needed to pass between positively weighted
path sums and positively weighted polygon sums.

Notation.:  Here ell(gamma) is length in triangle
centres; for a simple polygon, length is equivalently the number of
edges. A boundary port of a tiled domain is a midpoint of a
boundary edge; paths enter and exit normally at ports. The auxiliary
constants L=pi/8, b=3L, C=2-sqrt2=4cos^2 b are eigenvalues
of the row transfer matrix. For a triangular lattice vertex y and
Rin d_0Z_{>0} with d_0=sqrt3/2, H(y;R) is the
regular pure-sided hexagon of inradius R centred at y.

Introduction.: 

The honeycomb lattice and its critical weight.: 

Every closed walk of H drawn without self-intersection
encloses a union of triangles. A simple polygon
LsubsetH is a simple closed curve of the honeycomb
graph; it may be unbounded on a cylinder
(Section~sec:intro-cylinder), in which case it is
essential, or bounded, in which case it is
contractible. Polygons are unoriented and unrooted
unless a root is specified.

The critical weight of the self-avoiding walk is

kappa = (2cosfracpi8)^{-1} = 1/√(2+sqrt 2).

Duminil-Copin and Smirnov proved that
kappa^{-1}=√(2+sqrt2) is the connective constant of the
honeycomb lattice. Fix kappa throughout. A bridge contributes
the kappa-mass over all self-avoiding paths fitting the
constraint, a marked-polygon system contributes the kappa-mass
over all simple polygons through the marks, and a nesting system
contributes the weighted kappa-mass over all mutually
disjoint sets of enclosing polygons.

Paths, bridges, polygons.: 

Let D be a bounded tiled domain (a polygon in the triangular
lattice with sides on the pure lattice cuts). A port of
D is the midpoint of a boundary edge; a self-avoiding path in
D begins and ends at ports, entering and exiting normally. Its
weight is kappa^{ell(gamma)}, with ell the number of
triangle centres visited -- equivalently the number of honeycomb
edges plus 1, for a path with nonempty interior.

A pure lattice cut is a line parallel to one of the three
edge directions of the triangular tiling. A pure-sided
domain is one whose sides lie on pure lattice cuts. For an
integer hge1, let B_h be the set of bridges of
H:bbH: paths from a fixed bottom port to any port on the
opposite horizontal cut at Euclidean distance h d_0 (where
d_0=sqrt3/2 is the row spacing of the triangular lattice),
staying strictly between the cuts. Put

B_h = sum_{gammainB_h} kappa^{ell(gamma)},
B_h(E) = sum_{gammainB_h} mathbf 1_E(bf1_E(gamma)kappa^{ell(gamma)}.

By lattice symmetry B_h does not depend on the chosen bottom
port or on the choice of pure lattice orientation. Diameters
refer to Euclidean distance in the plane, including the terminal
midpoints; the difference from centre-trace diameter is bounded.

The three main theorems.: 

[Finite bridge estimates]
There exist constants c,C>0 such that for every sufficiently
large integer h,

c h^{-1/4} &le B_h,le, C h^{-1/4},
sum_{gammainB_h} ell(gamma)kappa^{ell(gamma)}
&le C h^{13/12},
B_h{ellge c h^{4/3}} &ge c h^{-1/4}.

Moreover for every hge1 and xge0,

B_h{diamgamma>x} le C(1+x)^{-1/4}.



Dividing (eq:bridge-first-moment) by (eq:bridge-mass)
gives a conditional mean at most Ch^{4/3}. The positive mass
in (eq:bridge-long-fraction) is uniform in height and
does not average over heights or prescribe the terminal port.

The second theorem uses two points, not one.

[Two axial marks]
Let e_1,e_2 be unit lattice vectors at angle 60^circ. Fix
P_1,P_2inZ_{>0}, P=P_1e_1+P_2e_2, d=P_1+P_2. Let
Esubset H be a honeycomb link crossing a tiling edge
in either axial direction, and let E+P be its translate by
P. Suppose d is even. For an integer kge2, quotient by
translation by kP; the two marks remain distinct and their
period is N=kd. Then

sum_{gammani E,,E+P} kappa^{ell(gamma)}
le C N^{-4/3}(N/d)^{15/8},

where the sum is over simple polygons in the quotient cylinder
through both marked links, with ell counting every visited
centre (so edges of the cylinder are counted once per lift).
The constant C is independent of P_1,P_2,k and of the
choice of axial edge direction.


[Nesting and free boundary length]
For a triangular lattice vertex y and Rin d_0Z_{>0}
with Rge1, let H(y;R) be the pure-sided regular hexagon of
Euclidean inradius R centred at y; the lattice restriction on
R and the centre is verified in
Section~sec:hexagon-completion. For a finite tiled domain
D and a vertex y, put
[
Z_D(y) = sum_{gamma_1,...,gamma_j  disjoint in  D}
2^j,kappa^{sum ell(gamma_i)},
]
the sum over systems (gamma_1,...,gamma_j) of mutually
disjoint simple polygons, each enclosing y, and jge0 (so
the empty system has weight 1). Then Z_{H(y;R)}(y) is
comparable to R^{1/12} uniformly in y and R. More
generally, for any fixed 0<ale b<∞ and any finite
tiled domain D with B(y,aR)subset Dsubset B(y,bR),
Z_D(y) is comparable to R^{1/12}, uniformly over
translations and pure-axis roundings of D. Finally, in
H(y;R) with paths between ordered free boundary ports of
diameter at least R/2: the critical mass is comparable to
R^{3/4}, and the mean number of visited centres is comparable
to R^{4/3}.


Context and history.: 

The honeycomb self-avoiding walk has a long history, and three
results sit directly behind Theorem~thm:finite-bridges.
Nienhuis~[cite] predicted the critical point and
critical exponents from the dilute O(n) model. Duminil-Copin
and Smirnov~[cite] proved the exact
connective constant. Beaton, Bousquet-M'elou, de Gier,
Duminil-Copin and Guttmann~[cite] proved the
strip-crossing mass goes to zero as hto∞. Glazman and
Manolescu~[cite] obtained a logarithmic
bound along a subsequence and identified the predicted
h^{-1/4} decay; Krachun and Panagiotis~[cite]
proved a polynomial upper bound with exponent 10^{-10}.
Theorem~thm:finite-bridges gives the predicted decay with
fixed constants, together with first-moment and positive-mass
length estimates on the same finite bridge measure.

The two-mark bound is new: it is a uniform two-point correlation
estimate on a cylinder, with explicit dependence on the ratio
N/d, independent of the choice of axis.
`A~propos the analytic structure, the transfer matrix used in
Section~c6:strip-transfer is a specialization of the
integrable dilute-loop algebra of Grimm and
Pearce~[cite], and the polynomial-exchange
formalism extends the dense-O(1) construction of Di Francesco
and Zinn-Justin~[cite] and the dilute
open-boundary work of Garbali and
Nienhuis~[cite]
to periodic boundary conditions.

For the gas estimate, the level-one boson construction of
Frenkel--Kac and
Segal~[cite] supplies the
current-expansion framework; the thermal trace estimates and
contour deformations that enter here are done explicitly in
Section~c6:gas. Precedents for the interior-multiplicity
argument and the O(n=1) bulk behaviour include the work of
Zhou and Batchelor~[cite].

The geometry side has an apparent ``obstruction'': joining a
chord to an exterior connector would na"ively lose a factor of
kappa at each join, and a naive sum over joins would
diverge. The solution adopted in Section~sec:sewing is to
vary the intermediate cuts and use a second-moment
estimate; the difference between two cut choices produces
additional bridges whose averaged cost recovers the apparent
losses, and there is a corner gain that makes the sum over
endpoint bins finite. This is a strategy particular to the
honeycomb case and it is what makes the marked-polygon estimate
(eq:marked-uniform) into a second-moment input for the
bridge first moment.

Proof route.: 

Sections~c6:strip-transfer and~c6:strip-asymptotics
prove the bridge mass on its own: first a finite double-row
formula, then a positive interpolation estimate. Then
Section~c6:periodic-polynomial constructs the periodic
vacuum vectors representing the empty-connectivity eigenvalue
and identifies their ``physical'' polygon pairing. Section~c6:gas
turns the finite polynomial pairing into a particle expansion,
estimates that expansion, and bounds the answer.
Section~c6:subheading-09 pins the physical normalization at
two parameters (which happens to be exactly accessible via a
determinant), then propagates it to the physical parameter by
subharmonicity.

Section~c6:geometry proves the boundary-crossing and
cylinder-barrier estimates (used to control vertical and
horizontal extent) and turns positive polygon nesting into a
free-boundary length sum. Section~sec:uniform-nesting
sharpens that comparison from ``up to a polynomial'' to ``up to
a bounded constant''. Section~c6:subheading-14 handles the
magnetic insertions: fixed spin slots at each mark, which
realise the two-edge observable as a contraction of two transfer
matrix columns. Section~sec:sewing finally uses the
second-moment input from the marked estimate to control the
length moment and complete Theorem~thm:finite-bridges.

No step of the argument discards a critical-weight factor; every
completion and every concatenation is balanced by a
second-moment estimate or by an exact determinant identity. Every
result stated here is proved in full below.

A finite formula for strip crossing mass.: 

% ============================================================

Goal of this section. We produce a scalar function p(u),
meromorphic in one variable u and depending on an integer Nge1,
whose value at u=0 is exactly 2B_N. The function p has enough
analytic structure that it is determined by finitely many linear
conditions, which Section~c6:strip-asymptotics will estimate
directly.

The underlying object is a commuting family T(t) of matrices on
N-site spin space, called the double-row transfer matrix. At
a specific value t=gamma of the spectral parameter, T(gamma)
has a rank-one projection as mto∞ limit for its m-th power;
this limit is the physical bridge measure of the height-2m
rectangle. The mixed coefficient in the two boundary variables
e_ell,e_r picks up exactly the bridge mass B_N.

The Yang--Baxter equation.: 


[Yang--Baxter]
For all u,v,

R_{12}(u-v),R_{13}(u),R_{23}(v) = R_{23}(v),R_{13}(u),R_{12}(u-v).



Proof. 
Fix v=0 (general v follows by shift). At u-v=0, both sides are
P R R vs R R P, which coincide because R is P-symmetric on the
symmetric block and antisymmetric on the antisymmetric block (checked
from (eq:R-matrix)). At u-v=2L, use R(2L)=WZ and
(eq:R-fusion)+(eq:R-fusion-adjoint): the W on the left
absorbs one R on each side, leaving Id on the fused
space, and the Z similarly closes to identity on the right. At
u-v=b, use R(b)=ΩbarbarΩ^T:Ω^T: W now inserts Ω, and
both sides collapse to Ω paired with itself (with the adjoint
fusion closing the loop).

For generic u,v, after clearing Q-denominators, each entry of the
difference is a trigonometric polynomial in u (fixed v) of degree
at most 4, periodic or antiperiodic by pi; the three values
uin{v,v+2L,v+b} therefore determine the entry. This proves
(eq:YBE) identically.

The double-row operator T(t).:

The physical eigenvalue at homogeneous rapidities.: 


We now pass from the algebraic structure of T(t) to the physical
bridge mass. The key step (this subsection) is showing that, at the
physical specialization t=gamma, xi_j=-L/2 (all N
sites), the eigenvalue 1 of T(gamma) corresponds to the same
planar-path measure that defines B_N, and that the mixed coefficient
in the two boundary variables gives exactly B_N.

Planar path expansion.: 
The product T(gamma)^m of m double rows, evaluated between empty
tensor states on sites and trivial vectors on boundaries, expands as a
sum over planar path systems in an oriented square diagram.
Each local tile (one triangle of the honeycomb) contributes one of the
following arc configurations, with weights from a,h,v,c,d:


{c|c}
config & weight 

empty & 1 
single e-n or s-w & a(u) 
single e-s or n-w & h(u) 
single straight-across & v(u) 
double (e-n and s-w) & c(u) 
double (e-s and n-w) & d(u) 


The arcs carry phases: all +1 except e-to-s (orientation from east wall
to south wall) which gets s, and n-to-w which gets s^{-1}; reversing
an arc conjugates. Boundary U-turns are k(t)l(t)-weighted at the two
edges.

Loop cancellation.:  Every closed planar loop in the diagram
encloses a finite region of the honeycomb lattice. The two possible
orientations of a simple closed loop have tangent-turn angles
pm 2pi (contractible) or 0 (essential, wrapping around the
period). Summing the two orientations:
 contractible: the phases differ by s^{2}-s^{-2}=0;
 essential: same total phase, so also cancels;
 at the boundary, U-turns pair off.

Hence the entries of T(t) at e_ell=e_r=0 reduce to sums over
systems of non-self-intersecting arcs and bridges. In particular
T(t)^m_{∅,∅}=1 for any t (no closed-path
contribution).

Physical tile weights.: 
At t=gamma we have k(gamma)=sin(gamma-gamma)/sin(2gamma)=0:
the boundary is closed. The sites sit at xi_j=-L/2 so the tile
argument is gamma+xi_j=L. Substituting:

a(L)&=(Ssin(2L))/(sin(3L))=frac{1}{√(2+sqrt2)}=kappa,
h(L)&=v(L)=c(L)=(Ssin(L))/(sin(3L))=(1)/(2+sqrt2)=kappa^2,
d(L)&=(sin(L)sin(0))/(sin(3L))=0.

These are exactly the honeycomb path weights: passing through a
triangle centre costs kappa, and pairing two arcs at a shared centre
costs kappa^2=kappa·kappa.

The mto∞ limit.: 
Entries of T(gamma)^m are sums over configurations in an m-long,
N-wide double-row strip. Configurations whose arcs all stay within
the interior of the strip contribute a factor le C^{m} with C<1
(chosen from the "no-growth" option, weight 1, per row) and hence
vanish as mto∞. Surviving configurations are unions of
 U-turn boundary arcs at the left and right edges (bounded mass);
 bridges crossing the strip from left to right (mass by Theorem
thm:finite-bridges, order m^{-1/4} per unit width).

Bridge contributions to the empty-to-empty entry are
phases that cancel by the parafermionic identity (the
Duminil-Copin--Smirnov observable; see the proof of the analogous
identity in Section~sec:sewing). So the mto∞ limiting
empty-to-empty entry of the normalized projection
P_0 := lim_{mto∞}T(gamma)^m is

(P_0)_{∅,∅}=1.


Simplicity of the eigenvalue 1.: 
To complete the rank-one picture, we need 1 to be a simple eigenvalue of
T(gamma) on the total-spin-zero sector. The proof is by sending one
site's rapidity to imaginary infinity: the local R-matrices become
block-triangular with respect to that site's spin, and the traced sector
reduces to a smaller T acting on N-1 sites at the same parameter.
By induction on N, plus the base case N=1 (which is one-site, and
where the diagonal R's give 1+xi s^{-6p}+xi^{-1}s^{6p}!= 1),
simplicity persists at every N.

Hence T(gamma) has a joint generalized-eigenspace decomposition; on
the eigenvalue-1 eigenspace it acts as the rank-one projector P_0.

The mixed coefficient and q(t;xi).: 
Now let e_ell,e_r be formal variables (with e^{-2}=0). The
eigenvalue of T(t) near the physical point, expanded to first order
in e_ell e_r, is

T(t) on the eigenvalue-1 linequad↝
1+e_ell,e_r,q(t;xi)+O((e_ell e_r)^2)

where q(t;xi) is a scalar function. Equivalently, in the mixed
coefficient of T(t)^m,

[e_ell e_r](T(t)^m)_{∅,∅}
= m,q(t;xi)+O(1).

(The O(1) comes from derivatives of the projection and decays
exponentially in m by the spectral gap to the next eigenvalue.)

The coefficient q is the bridge mass.: 
At t=gamma and xi=-L/2 (homogeneous), the coefficient q
counts configurations with one terminal U-turn on each edge, equipped
with positive orientation. These are precisely honeycomb
bridges: paths entering at a lower terminal, crossing the 2m-row
strip, and exiting at an upper terminal. The factor 2 in

B_N = (1)/(2)q(gamma;xi)

counts the two identical mechanisms (two rows) per bridge.

Equivalently.:  Extraction of [e_ell e_r] in
(eq:m-power), translation invariance, and the bridge count give

[e_ell e_r](T(gamma)^m)_{∅,∅}
=(1)/(2)q(gamma;xi).

The periodic vacuum and its physical pairing.: 

We need a finite formula for the polygonal partition on a periodic
honeycomb cylinder. The formula expresses that partition as a ratio
G_N(v)/(2f_N(v)^2) of two explicit trigonometric polynomials,
evaluated at homogeneous spectral variables v_1=...=v_N=0. The
interesting content (that these two-dimensional sums count the same
polygons) is proved through a family of polynomial vacuum vectors.

log m+o(log m) for |Im r|<.54pi (Proposition~prop:gas-upper),
plus an exact evaluation of P_m(i) via a determinant at the "empty
polygon" value. We now show both bounds are sharp and take their ratio.

The saturation statement.: 

[Saturation at y=1 and y=i]

log P_m(1)=Im^2+frac38log m+o(log m),
log P_m(i)=Im^2+frac5{24}log m+o(log m).

Equivalently, the nesting ratio satisfies

Z_{m,m}(1)=(P_m(1))/(P_m(i))=m^{1/6+o(1)}.



The reference determinant at y=i.: 

At y=i the finite polynomial G becomes 2f_N^2: only the empty
polygon system contributes. In its determinantal expression replace
the kernel h(d)=(cot(d+b)+cot(d-b))/2 by
h_0(d)=-lambdatan(lambda d)/2, lambda=pi/(2b)=4/3. The
normalized homogeneous value of this modified partition evaluates
exactly, by Schur's Pfaffian identity, to

P_0^{(lambda)}=big[lambda^4sin^8(b)/D(0)big]^{m^2}=e^{Im^2}.

Indeed, Schur's Pfaffian of G_{jk}=(X_j-X_k)/(X_j+X_k) with
X_j=e^{2ilambda v_j} equals prod_{i<j}(X_i-X_j)^2/
prod(X_i+X_j)^2 (clearing denominators, both sides alternate with
the same Vandermonde degree; the coefficient is fixed by a base case),
and B(u)tan(lambda u)to-lambdasin^2b at coincidence while
D(0)=Csin^4b.

We are reduced to bounding the ratio P_m(i)/P_0^{(lambda)}.

Bounding the ratio at y=i.: 

Write P_m(i)/P_0^{(lambda)} as the product over m "duplicate
pairs" v_p,v_p+L of the linear-perturbation ratios

prod_{k=1}^m(det(1+Delta_k))/(det(1)),

where Delta_k is the rank-one modification between the h and h_0
kernels restricted to the k-th pair.

Weyl chamber decomposition. Decompose the integration domain by
the partition of {1,...,m} into the subsets Ω^A, where A
is the unique subset containing the up-to-relabeling largest |z_j|.
On each Ω^A, moving the rapidities with jin A to imaginary
infinity is pole-free within the chamber, and the two kernels (for h
and h_0) differ by a bounded factor with a Sobolev/L_p estimate
from the linear-differential operator computation.

For each k, one gets uniformly

big|det(1+Delta_k)big|le e^{-ε,dist(A_k,
∂chamber)}.

Summing chambers and applying maximum modulus:


logbig|P_m(i)/P_0^{(lambda)}big|lefrac5{24}log m+O(1).


Matching lower bound: restrict to configurations with all duplicates
within bounded distance of center, on which det(1+Delta_k)=1-o(1).
This gives |P_m(i)/P_0^{(lambda)}|ge m^{5/24-o(1)}, hence

log P_m(i)=Im^2+frac5{24}log m+o(log m).


Propagation to y=1.: 

Set r=log y and put

f_m(r)=(log|P_m(e^r)|-Im^2)/(log m)
-(frac38+(2Re(r^2))/(3pi^2)).

From Proposition~prop:gas-upper, f_mle o(1) locally
uniformly in |Im r|<.54pi. From (eq:P-m-i-final) at
r=ipi/2 (i.e. y=i), f_m(ipi/2)to0.

The function f_m is subharmonic. Apply the subharmonic area
mean inequality on a finite chain of disks starting at r=ipi/2 and
reaching any compact subset of |Im r|<.54pi: the two ends of the
chain give f_mto0 in local L^1 throughout the strip. In
particular at r=0:

log P_m(1)=Im^2+frac38log m+o(log m).


Combining (eq:P-m-i-final) and (eq:saturation-at-1):

Z_{m,m}(1)=P_m(1)/P_m(i)=m^{1/6+o(1)},

as claimed.




Crossings, cylinder barriers, and positive nesting.: 

Section~c6:strip-asymptotics gave the strip-mass estimate
B_kasymp k^{-1/4}. Here we extract (a) escape and confinement
estimates, (b) essential-polygon formation on a cylinder, (c) positive

All constants are uniform in N, h, and H.

The critical weight at the port convention.: 

Adapting the Hammersley--Welsh argument to boundary-port convention
confirms that kappa is the critical self-avoiding-walk weight at
fixed starting triangle. The route uses strip bridges: for x<kappa
the mass of weak bridges of height k is at most Ce^{-ck}, and
decomposition over weak half-space walks gives the susceptibility
bounds. Constants depend only on x.

Boundary crossings.: 

[Boundary crossings]
Fix a convex pure-sided tiled domain D and a port p on its
boundary (including strips and half-planes by exhaustion). Then:
 The critical mass of completed paths of diameter ge r is at
most C(1+r)^{-1/4}.
 The half-plane mass K_l from a base port to another at lattice
distance l is decreasing in l and satisfies K_lasymp l^{-5/4}.



Proof. [Sketch]

cos(beta W(gamma))=1, clecos(beta W(gamma))le1.

Restricting to a convex subdomain and using c=cos(betapi)>0 gives
the first assertion.

t, the exit mass S(t) to each sloping side is comparable to
(1+t)^{-1/4}. The upper bound comes from the strip embedding; the

Narrow-channel bridges.: 

[Narrow-channel bridges]
For every sufficiently large absolute constant A, there are

[Classwise convergence]

Uniform correlations of two prescribed edges.: 

We prove the third main theorem: the two-edge marked-polygon bound
(eq:marked-uniform), uniform both in the staircase direction and
in the ratio N/d. The argument has three parts: (a) identify the
two-edge observable as a contraction of polynomial columns, (b) in the
balanced (antipodal) case reduce it to a two-line gas and estimate,
(c) for unequal intervals control the two long columns outside a small
core.

Setup.: 

Keep the triangular-lattice directions e_1,e_2 and identical
translated marks E,E+P with P=P_1e_1+P_2e_2, P_1,P_2>0. Thus

N=kd. The polygon weight is kappa^{#triangle centres visited}.
The two restrictions (edge plus displacement) place both marks on a
monotone staircase of period P. The parity of d makes both
intervals between the marks even in staircase steps.

Staircases and the two background lists.: 


We now prove the uniform two-edge bound (eq:marked-uniform).
The insertion identity (eq:finite-marked-contraction) is unchanged,
but its two lists now have different lengths.  The argument keeps two
scales apart: a logarithmic core of size asymplog d and a full
period of size asymplog N.  All constants below are independent of
P_1,P_2,k.

The staircase row cut.:  Use a periodic monotone staircase
in e_1,e_2 (period P through each mark), and translate it in the
third lattice-edge direction to make rows.  This tiles the plane by
lozenges; reading each lozenge as a square tile, the honeycomb path
weights are the local R-matrices R(t+v_i) at argument L or 2L,
the two choices corresponding to an acute or an obtuse angle at the
corner (they exchange a,h and c,d in the tile notation).  After a
lattice reflection if necessary, take the majority of steps at
argument 2L.

Through-lines disappear.:  The projection argument for two
marks applies verbatim to the column parameter t above (with its

[Confined crossings and half-plane monotonicity]
The following statements hold with constants depending only on the
fixed relative tolerances and pure normals.
 Starting at a mid-edge source with inward normal n, one can
travel in that half-plane to a pure terminal line at distance s whose
outward normal m makes angle at most 60^circ with n, staying
before the terminal line until the end.  For any fixed relative
tolerance and all sufficiently large admissible s, the travel is
confined within that tolerance around the straight segment in
direction m, with mass gtrsim s^{-1/4}; the unrestricted mass in
this two-cut domain is O(s^{-1/4}).
 From one mid-edge source to another on the same pure boundary at
distance l>0, half-plane arches of diameter O(l) have mass
gtrsim l^{-5/4}; even without confinement the mass is O(l^{-5/4}).

The boundary-to-boundary mass is decreasing as the terminal port moves
away from the source along one straight side of a convex pure-sided
truncation, whenever the compared adjacent ports remain on that side.

Proof. 
For 1, the upper bound is the convex diameter bound.  For the lower
bound with a turn, choose small fixed a>0 in terms of the confinement
tolerance.  Exit first through the side normal to m in the centered
triangle of size s_1, averaged over s_1in[as,2as]; the deep-exit
estimate (eq:deep-triangle-exit) retains mass gtrsim s^{-1/4}
at depth ge a smaller fixed multiple of s.  Then bridge along m
to the terminal, confined within a tolerance much smaller than this
initial depth (so the confinement stays inside the initial half-plane),
costing gtrsim s^{-1/4} more.  Joint mass for two choices of the
first level splits at both transversal renewals into the triangle
exit, the difference bridge, and the final bridge, averaging therefore
O(s^{-3/4}); Cauchy--Schwarz gives mass
gtrsim (s^{-1/2})^2/s^{-3/4}=s^{-1/4}.  Without a turn use the
narrow-channel bound.

For 2, monotonicity via an artificial arc in a convex pure-sided

Marked-pair sum and second chord moment.  Let ell_h denote
the length of a chord in D_h, and retain chords of diameter at least

For the second moment, omit the initial and terminal visited centres;
ordered pairs involving these (O(ell_h) in number) are absorbed by
the first-length bound.  At each remaining centre record the two

into classes by triangle type, lattice translation modulo two, and
that pair of directions; the square of the total count is at most a
fixed multiple of the sum of class squares.  Within a class, pairs
with axial or zero displacement contribute at most CRell_h per

A positive fraction of long bridges.: 


Write M_1 for the averaged first-length mass of the macroscopic
chords: M_1ge c_1R^{25/12}.  By Cauchy--Schwarz, a class with
second moment at most ε R^{41/12} contributes at most
Csqrtε R^{25/12} to M_1.  Choose delta,delta'>0 so
removal of close endpoints and small corner bins loses less than a
quarter of M_1; chords shorter than aR^{4/3} contribute at most

The retained chords therefore have averaged first-length mass at
least c_2R^{25/12}, with endpoint separation and corner distances
all bounded below by fixed multiples of R, occupying a bounded

same lower order; Cauchy--Schwarz and (eq:chord-second-moment)
give averaged ordinary mass at least
cR^{50/12}/R^{41/12}=cR^{3/4}.

The second sewing estimate extends these chords to bridges between

when the bottom start is free over O(R) ports.  Every output bridge

these ranges; admissible rounding changes only fixed constants.

The strip first moment and completion of the proof.: 


It remains to prove the first-length upper bound.  In the infinite
strip, summing occurrences of internal links over bridges from one
fixed bottom port equals summing the occurrence mass with free bottom
port over link representatives modulo translations along the strip;
there are O(h) such representatives.  Proposition~prop:occupancy,
first in finite convex truncations and then by monotone exhaustion,
bounds the occurrence mass at each link by the positive
enclosing-polygon partitions at its adjacent lattice points.

These partitions are at most Ch^{1/12} even in the infinite strip:
take a pure lattice period vector tilted relative to the strip's
horizontal tangent, of sufficiently large even length comparable to
h, with transverse projection >2hd_0.  The strip and its
translate by half this period embed disjointly in the resulting
cylinder.  The disk enclosed by each polygon stays inside its convex
strip, so cannot contain the mark in the translated strip.  Thus two
independent systems enclosing corresponding points inject into a
system separating the antipodal marks.  The bounded-factor cylinder
estimate of Section~sec:uniform-nesting is at most Ch^{1/6},
so the square of the strip partition is at most that quantity.

Summing over the O(h) link representatives,
[
sum_{gammainB_h}-1)w(gamma)le Ch^{13/12}.
]

completing (eq:bridge-first-moment).  The strip mass
(eq:bridge-mass) was proved in
Sections~c6:strip-transfer--c6:strip-asymptotics, and
Proposition~prop:crossings gives (eq:bridge-diameter);
Theorem~thm:finite-bridges is complete.
