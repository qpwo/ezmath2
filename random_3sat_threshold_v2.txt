For n >= 3, a proper random 3-clause is a disjunction of three literals whose three variable indices are distinct and uniform in {1,...,n} and whose three signs are independent and fair. A proper random 3-SAT formula of m clauses is a conjunction of m independent clauses with that law, sampled with replacement from the 8*C(n,3) distinct signed triples. The empty formula is satisfiable. Let p(n,m) be the probability that such a formula is satisfiable and let m/n be the clause density.

The number of clauses per variable at which a random formula switches from almost surely satisfiable to almost surely unsatisfiable is a long-studied object. Its value is known only numerically. A sharper statement is possible: the switch point is itself a computable real number, and one deterministic Turing machine, given a precision parameter r, halts with a rational approximation to the switch point within an additive error of 2^(-r).

For clause length 3 the switch point is denoted alpha_3 and defined through the two strict-side limits

  lim_{n->infinity} p(n, floor(a*n)) = 1  for a < alpha_3,
  lim_{n->infinity} p(n, floor(a*n)) = 0  for a > alpha_3.

Behaviour exactly at a = alpha_3 is not addressed. The strict-side limits alone determine alpha_3 uniquely, since two distinct values would force opposite limits at some density strictly between them.

That such a switch point exists at clause length 3 follows from Carenini's polynomial scaling window: at clause length k the width between probability levels eta and 1-eta is of order n^(1/2+1/k) with the constant depending on k and eta, and the corollary that a limiting threshold exists for every fixed k >= 3 is stated in the same paper. An independent derivation of the same limit through a moment bound on survival times under one-variable deletion is what makes the constant computable; the resulting value agrees with the one in a companion preprint on limiting thresholds for every fixed clause size.

Computability. Specker constructed a bounded nondecreasing computable sequence of rationals with noncomputable limit, so the pure existence of a limit of finite-size quantities does not automatically produce a computable real.


The escape from Specker's obstruction is through two families of finite certificates, one below and one above the switch point, that bracket alpha_3 from both sides with arbitrary precision using only rational computations. Neither family needs a rate-of-convergence estimate.

Theorem. There is a real number alpha_3 in (0, infinity) such that, for every fixed real a >= 0,

  lim_{n->infinity} p(n, floor(a*n)) = 1  for a < alpha_3,
  lim_{n->infinity} p(n, floor(a*n)) = 0  for a > alpha_3.

There is a finite deterministic Turing machine which, on input 1^r for any integer r >= 1, halts with a rational number q_r satisfying |q_r - alpha_3| <= 2^(-r). The machine takes no input other than the unary precision 1^r and uses no oracle, advice, or noncomputable real constant.

The relaxed clause model in which each clause chooses its three variable indices uniformly and independently from {1,...,n} without regard to repetition, with independent fair signs, is more symmetric than the proper clause model. The relaxed clause distribution is invariant under more symmetries and the clause-replacement calculations are uniform. The strict-side threshold of the relaxed model transfers to the proper clause model by thinning.

A moment bound on the extra time for which a formula stays satisfiable when one variable may be deleted for free supplies the main probabilistic input. A concentration inequality and a superadditivity estimate follow from the moment bound. The lower certificate family is read off from the one-sided bound alpha >= f_k/k - 2^67 k^(-1/6).

The soft pressure
  P(a,beta) = lim_{n->infinity} (1/n) * E log Z_n(a,beta)
enters the upper certificate, with Z_n(a,beta) = sum_{sigma in {0,1}^n} exp(-beta H_n(sigma)) and H_n(sigma) counting the clauses violated by assignment sigma. Its existence follows from superadditivity, its nonnegativity below alpha and its strict negativity above alpha are the two signs needed for the upper certificate, and its finite-hierarchy variational description in terms of trials gives an effectively enumerable class of rational upper bounds.

Soundness of the trial family rests on a local three-literal interpolation. The remainder (x - y)^2 (x + 2y) is nonnegative for x, y >= 0, and this single convexity is the whole of the local argument. Completeness of the trial family rests on a structural argument that removes shared random variables between sites block by block, without recomputing the retained conditional laws.

The two certificate families are combined by a search that halts on the interval when its length is small enough. No rate of convergence of the finite-size quantities is needed; only the two sign properties are needed.

In the relaxed Poisson model, fix n >= 1. Clauses arrive in a rate-one Poisson process. Each arriving clause chooses three variable indices independently and uniformly from {1,...,n} and three signs independently and fair. Indices, signs, and the clock are mutually independent. Repetitions within a clause are permitted. At time t the formula contains all arrivals up to and including t, and the clause count is Poisson with mean t. The clause density m/n refers to the observed clause count.

For 0 <= r <= n, a formula is r-deletion satisfiable if some set of at most r variables can be deleted so that the remaining formula is satisfiable, where deleting a variable discards every clause touching it. Let T_r be the first time r-deletion satisfiability fails, with T_r = plus infinity if it never fails, and define F_r = min(T_r, Bn) with B = 20. Write f_n = E F_0 and P_n(t) = Prob(T_0 > t). The capped mean first-unsatisfiable time f_n is the finite-size quantity that enters the lower certificate.

For a fixed variable v, a superscript +v indicates that v is deleted for free and up to r other variables may be deleted. Put F_r^{+v} = min(T_r^{+v}, Bn), so F_r^{+v} >= F_r with the same cap. Permitting more deletions than there are other variables simply permits deleting all of them.

Free-deletion moment. For all integers n >= 1, 0 <= r <= n, and v in {1,...,n},

  E[(F_r^{+v} - F_r)^(6/5)] <= C,  C = 2^60.

Consequently, uniformly in r,

  E|F_r - E F_r|^(6/5) <= C_1 n,  C_1 = 2^62.

The proof compares two ways to eliminate a set of surviving assignments. Clauses touching v become, after fixing the sign of v, two-literal tests. A batch of relaxed three-literal tests replaces each such test at controlled loss. This is Friedgut's residual-set conditioning in the half-cube lemma, and Carenini's sequential clause replacement.

./carenini-2026-polynomial-window.pdf

The calculation is for independently sampled positions; their exact powers give the estimate needed under an existing deletion allowance.

Proof. For n = 1 the claim follows from 0 <= F_r^{+v} - F_r <= 20 = B. Suppose henceforth that n >= 2.

Split the Poisson process into clauses avoiding v (the base) and clauses involving v (the incident streams). The base has rate lambda_n = ((n-1)/n)^3 >= 1/8, and its clauses have the relaxed distribution on the other n-1 variables. It is independent of the incident streams.

At a deterministic time t, condition on the entire base up to t. Let U_t be its finite set of surviving pairs (D, sigma), where D is a subset of {1,...,n} minus {v} of size at most r and sigma is an assignment on the complement of D and {v}. A literal on D is declared true; this convention implements clause deletion. The event that U_t is nonempty equals {T_r^{+v} > t}. For a nonempty deterministic candidate set U, let q(U) be the probability that Poi(K) independent relaxed two-literal clauses on the other variables eliminate every candidate in U, where K = 3B = 60.

For 0 <= t < Bn and U_t nonempty,

  Prob(T_r <= t | base up to t) <= (q(U_t) + 60/n)^2.

For each fixed value of v, only incident clauses in which every occurrence of v has the falsifying sign can obstruct an extension of a candidate in U_t. Among these, those containing v once project to independent relaxed two-literal clauses, Poisson with mean at most 3t/n <= 60. The expected number containing v at least twice is at most 3t/n^2 <= 60/n, by a union bound. Therefore the obstruction probability for this value is at most q(U_t) + 60/n. The obstructing groups for the two values of v are disjoint Poisson thinnings, hence independent of one another and of the base. Both must therefore obstruct if T_r fails.

Replacement of shorter tests. In any list of independent tests, replacing one relaxed two-literal clause by d >= 1 independent relaxed three-literal clauses reduces the probability of eliminating all candidates by at most d^(-2). To see this, condition on all other tests, leaving a candidate set U'. If U' is empty there is nothing to prove. Otherwise let x be the proportion of signed literals false on every member of U'. Each variable contributes at most one such sign, so 0 <= x <= 1/2, even under the deleted-literal convention. The two-literal clause alone eliminates U' with probability x^2. The d three-literal clauses eliminate U' with probability at least 1 - (1 - x^3)^d >= 1 - exp(-d*x^3). If d*x^3 > 1 this exceeds 1/4 >= x^2. If d*x^3 <= 1 it is at least (1/2)*d*x^3, and x^2 - (1/2)*d*x^3 <= 16/(27*d^2) < 1/d^2. Successive replacements are valid because each conditions on all other independent tests.

How long a residual set survives. The replacement estimate converts a large value of q(U_t) into a chance that the future base eliminates U_t. Partition the possible values q(U_t) >= 1/n into disjoint dyadic bins q_0 <= q(U_t) <= 2*q_0 with q_0 = 2^(-j) and j >= 1, using a fixed endpoint convention. Every bin actually used has q_0 >= 1/(2n). For its index j set

  L_j = j + 122,  d_j = ceil(sqrt(4*L_j/q_0)),  ell_j = 32*L_j*d_j.

Since E 2^Poi(60) = exp(60) < 2^120, Prob(Poi(60) > L_j) <= q_0/4. If U belongs to the bin, then L_j two-literal tests eliminate U with probability at least q(U) - q_0/4. Replacing each test by d_j three-literal tests loses at most L_j/d_j^2 <= q_0/4. Thus L_j*d_j three-literal tests eliminate U with probability at least q_0/2.

In a future interval of length ell_j the base has Poisson arrivals with mean at least 4*L_j*d_j. If M = L_j*d_j >= 1 and X is Poisson with mean mu >= 4M, Chebyshev gives Prob(X < M) <= mu/(mu-M)^2 <= 4/(9M) < 1/2. The interval therefore eliminates the fixed U with probability at least q_0/4. Write S_{r,v}(t) = Prob(T_r^{+v} > t) and E_j(t) for the event that U_t is nonempty and q(U_t) belongs to bin j. The independent future base gives

  S_{r,v}(t) - S_{r,v}(t + ell_j) >= (q_0/4)*Prob(E_j(t)),
  the integral of Prob(E_j(t)) dt over [0, Bn] is at most 4*ell_j/q_0.

For the second inequality, integrate the first: the integral of S_{r,v}(t) - S_{r,v}(t + ell_j) over [0, Bn] is at most ell_j. This does not require T_r^{+v} finite. Conditional on the base at time t in E_j(t), survival to t + u is also bounded by (1 - q_0/4)^floor(u/ell_j). For every complete future interval, failure to eliminate even the original U_t is necessary for survival, and these interval events are independent.

Integrating the delay. For any 0 <= F_r <= F_r^{+v} <= Bn, direct integration gives

  (F_r^{+v} - F_r)^p = p(p-1) times the double integral of u^(p-2)*1{T_r <= t, T_r^{+v} > t+u} dt du over {(t,u) >= 0 : t + u < Bn},

with p = 6/5. Boundaries have Lebesgue measure zero. Conditional on the base up to t, incident arrivals up to t and future base arrivals are independent. The incident killing estimate and the future survival estimate therefore multiply.

On a bin of scale q_0, incident killing has probability at most a constant times q_0^2. The expected time spent in that bin before Bn is at most 4*ell_j/q_0. Integrating the geometric survival bound against u^(p-2) contributes at most a constant times (ell_j/q_0)^(p-1). The bin contribution is bounded by a constant times ell_j^p*q_0^(2-p). Since ell_j grows like a polynomial in j times q_0^(-1/2), the power of q_0 is 2 - 3p/2 = 1/5 > 0.

Explicit constants. On bin j,

  (q(U_t) + 60/n)^2 <= (122*q_0)^2 < 2^14 * q_0^2,
  ell_j <= 2^20 * (j+1)^(3/2) * 2^(j/2),

using d_j <= 3*sqrt(L_j/q_0) and L_j <= 64*(j+1). Also

  the integral of u^(p-2)*(1 - q_0/4)^floor(u/ell_j) du over [0, infinity] is at most e*Gamma(1/5)*(4*ell_j/q_0)^(1/5) < 2^7*(ell_j/q_0)^(p-1).

The geometric term is at most e*exp(-q_0*u/(4*ell_j)), and splitting the Gamma integral at 1 gives Gamma(1/5) < 6. Using the future survival bound and p(p-1) < 1, the contribution of bin j is at most 2^47*(j+1)^2*2^(-j/5). Grouping j = 5k+1, ..., 5k+5 and using (33/32)^6 < 2,

  the sum over j >= 1 of (j+1)^2*2^(-j/5) is at most 5 times the sum over k >= 0 of (5k+6)^2*2^(-k), which is less than 2^11.

The total bin contribution is less than 2^58. When q(U_t) < 1/n, the incident killing bound is at most 61^2/n^2, and integrating over the entire truncated region bounds the remaining contribution by 61^2*B^p*n^(p-2) < 2^18. This proves the moment bound with C = 2^60, without assuming that the free-deletion problem eventually fails. If r >= n-1 a candidate can delete all other variables, so q(U_t) = 0 and only the small-q estimate is used.

From deletion to concentration. Partition all arrivals up to Bn into n independent buckets by the smallest variable index in a clause. Resample bucket i independently and let F'_r be the resulting capped time. Deleting i removes that entire bucket, so F_r^{+i} is unchanged by the resampling and dominates both F_r and F'_r. Hence

  E|F_r - F'_r|^p <= E(F_r^{+i} - F_r)^p + E(F_r^{+i} - F'_r)^p <= 2C.

Reveal the buckets in increasing index order and let D_i be the successive differences of the Doob martingale for F_r. Averaging over the independent replacement and all unrevealed buckets identifies D_i as the conditional expectation of F_r - F'_r given the first i buckets. Conditional Jensen gives E|D_i|^p <= 2C.

For real x, y and 1 < p < 2,

  |x + y|^p <= |x|^p + p*sgn(x)*|x|^(p-1)*y + 2*|y|^p,

where sgn(0) = 0. The signed (p-1)-power is Holder continuous with constant 2^(2-p), and the remainder is at most 2^(2-p)*|y|^p <= 2*|y|^p. Applied successively to the centered martingale sum, the linear terms have expectation zero, giving E|F_r - E F_r|^p <= 2*sum_i E|D_i|^p <= 4*C*n = C_1*n. This is the concentration claim.

Two blocks with unequal sizes admit the weighted interpolation bound: for positive integers n_1, n_2 and real t_1, t_2 >= 0,

  P_{n_1 + n_2}(t_1 + t_2) >= P_{n_1}(t_1) P_{n_2}(t_2).

Proof. Put n = n_1 + n_2 and t = t_1 + t_2; the case t = 0 is immediate, so let lambda_i = t_i/t for i = 1, 2. A local clause first chooses block i with probability lambda_i and then chooses its three indices uniformly in that block. A weighted global clause chooses its block independently at each of the three positions with the same probabilities lambda_i and then chooses a uniform index in the chosen block. Interpolate between independent Poisson counts of local and weighted global clauses with respective means (1 - s)t and s t, for 0 <= s <= 1.

The derivative of the expected satisfiability indicator is t times the expected difference of its add-one increments for the two clause types; differentiating the Poisson series for a bounded function is legitimate because the differentiated series converges absolutely. For a satisfiable base formula let x_i be the proportion of signed literals in block i that are false in every satisfying assignment. A single clause eliminates all satisfying assignments exactly when each of its literals is false in every such assignment, so the local and weighted global killing probabilities are

  sum_i lambda_i x_i^3  and  (sum_i lambda_i x_i)^3.

Convexity of x -> x^3 on [0, infinity) makes the derivative nonnegative. An unsatisfiable base contributes zero, and the base satisfiability probability at s = 0 factorizes as P_{n_1}(t_1) P_{n_2}(t_2).

The mixed endpoint still carries weight lambda_i/n_i on each variable of block i. Uniformizing these weights cannot decrease the probability at the weighted global endpoint. For a fixed clause count, choose two variables with weights w_1, w_2 and replace both by (w_1 + w_2)/2. If their sum is zero, there is nothing to change. Otherwise condition on the set of occurrence positions using one of the two variables and on all choices and signs in the other positions; these conditioned data have the same distribution before and after averaging. Let k be the number of selected positions. Treating their literal truth values as independent placeholders defines a set S subset of {0,1}^k: a vector belongs to S if the variables outside the selected pair can be assigned so that the formula is satisfied. The set S does not depend on the identities or signs still to be sampled in the selected positions.

Let y be the truth vector when the two selected variables are both assigned zero, and let xi indicate positions occupied by the second variable. Fair independent signs make y uniform on {0,1}^k independently of xi, whose coordinates are independent Bernoulli variables with parameter theta = w_2/(w_1 + w_2). The four assignments to the selected pair yield exactly the truth vectors y, complement of y, y xor xi, and complement of (y xor xi). Let A = S union {complement of z : z in S} and let g be the indicator of A. The conditional probability of satisfaction is consequently

  2 E g(y) - E[g(y) g(y xor xi)].

Expanding g in the orthonormal parity characters chi_I(y) = (-1) raised to sum of y_j for j in I of the uniform bit cube, and using independence of the selector bits,

  E[g(y) g(y xor xi)] = sum over subsets I of hat_g(I)^2 times (1 - 2 theta)^|I|.

Complement invariance of g makes every odd-degree coefficient vanish. Every remaining nonconstant term is nonnegative and vanishes at theta = 1/2. Thus equalizing the selected weights maximizes the conditional satisfiability probability.

Repeatedly average a largest and a smallest weight. The sum over all weights of squared deviations from 1/n decreases by half the square of their difference. This nonnegative sum converges, so the largest-smallest difference tends to zero and all weights converge to 1/n. For a fixed clause count the satisfiability probability is a polynomial in the weights, hence continuous, and cannot decrease in this averaging procedure. Bounded convergence then permits averaging over the Poisson count. The uniform endpoint is the left-hand side of the product bound.

The sequence f_n/n converges to a real number alpha. For every integer k >= 1,

  alpha >= f_k/k - 2^67 k^(-1/6).

Proof. Turn the survival comparison into an almost-superadditive inequality, then make the accumulated error summable after division by the size. Set H = 2^60 and h_k = H k^b with b = 5/6. The concentration bound and Markov's inequality give, at size k,

  Prob(|F_0 - f_k| >= h_k) <= C_1/H^p = 2^(-10) < 1/10.

In the interpolation inequality choose t_i = max(f_{n_i} - h_{n_i}, 0). If t_i = 0, survival is certain. Otherwise t_i < B n_i, and survival has probability at least 9/10 by the deviation bound. With n = n_1 + n_2 and t = t_1 + t_2, the product gives P_n(t) >= 81/100, and t < B n. If f_n < t - h_n, the same deviation bound at size n would give P_n(t) <= 1/10, a contradiction. Hence

  f_{n_1 + n_2} >= f_{n_1} + f_{n_2} - C_2 (n_1 + n_2)^b,  C_2 = 2^62,

because h_{n_1} + h_{n_2} + h_n <= H (2^(1-b) + 1) n^b < 4 H n^b.

Fix k >= 1. Iterating the almost-superadditive inequality on equal-size blocks gives

  f_{2^j k}/(2^j k) >= f_k/k - C_2 k^(b-1) * sum_{i=1}^j 2^(-(1-b) i).

Let D_b = sum_{i >= 1} 2^(-(1-b) i) = 1/(2^(1/6) - 1) < 32, using (33/32)^6 < 2. Every dyadic multiple s = 2^j k then satisfies f_s >= s (f_k/k - C_2 D_b k^(-1/6)). For n >= k, use the binary expansion of floor(n/k) to partition k floor(n/k) into distinct dyadic multiples of k. Merge them in increasing order of size. When a block of size 2^j k is added, the total merged size is less than 2^(j+1) k, and the sum of merging errors in the almost-superadditive inequality is bounded by

  C_2 * sum_{j=0}^{floor(log_2(n/k))} (2^(j+1) k)^b <= A_b C_2 n^b

for a fixed finite constant A_b. Add the remainder in one more application of the almost-superadditive inequality, using f_s >= 0. Divide by n and let n tend to infinity with k fixed. Since b < 1 the merging errors vanish and

  liminf_{n -> infinity} f_n/n >= f_k/k - C_2 D_b k^(-1/6) >= f_k/k - 2^67 k^(-1/6).

The bounded sequence f_n/n in [0, B] has a limsup subsequence. Let k tend to infinity along that subsequence in the last inequality. Its liminf is at least its limsup, proving convergence and the one-sided error.

The limit alpha lies in [1/200, 10]. For every fixed real a >= 0, the relaxed Poisson model satisfies

  P_n(a n) -> 1 for 0 <= a < alpha,
  P_n(a n) -> 0 for a > alpha.

The same limits hold for p(n, floor(a n)) in the proper-clause model, so its limiting density is alpha_3 = alpha.

Proof. For a fixed assignment, a relaxed clause is violated with probability 1/8 even when variable indices repeat, because signs are independent. The expected number of satisfying assignments at time 10n is therefore 2^n exp(-10n/8), which tends to zero. Consequently P_n(10n) -> 0, and f_n <= 10n + (B - 10) n P_n(10n) shows alpha <= 10 < B.

For a lower bound set c = 1/100 and M = floor(c n). A union bound gives

  Prob(some ell of the first M clauses touch fewer than ell distinct variables) <= sum_{ell=1}^M C(M,ell) C(n,ell) (ell/n)^(3 ell) <= sum_{ell=1}^M (e^2 c ell/n)^ell,

which tends to zero. To obtain the first inequality, pad any set of fewer than ell touched variables to one of size ell; all 3 ell index choices must lie in that set. The second uses C(u,ell) <= (e u/ell)^ell. The summands with ell <= sqrt(n) sum to O(n^(-1/2)), and the remaining summands are at most n (e^2 c^2)^(sqrt(n)) in total. On the complementary event Hall's marriage theorem assigns every clause a distinct representative variable appearing in it. Setting each representative to satisfy one occurrence in its assigned clause is consistent because the representatives are distinct, and all clauses are then satisfied. Repetitions of indices and signs are permitted. At time c n/2 the Poisson clause count is at most M with probability tending to one. Thus P_n(cn/2) -> 1, and f_n >= (cn/2) P_n(cn/2) gives alpha >= c/2 = 1/200.

Below the threshold, if 0 < a < alpha, convergence of f_n/n and the concentration bound imply P_n(a n) -> 1: for sufficiently large n, f_n - a n >= (alpha - a) n / 2, so the failure probability is at most C_1 n / ((alpha - a) n/2)^p, which tends to zero. The case a = 0 is immediate. If alpha < a < B, the analogous upper-tail estimate P_n(a n) <= C_1 n / ((a - alpha) n/2)^p gives P_n(a n) -> 0. For a >= B, use monotonicity and any intermediate density in (alpha, B).

To return to distinct-variable clauses, allow n >= 3 and write P_n^d(t) for the satisfiability probability of the distinct-variable Poisson model with rate one. Thinning out clauses with repeated variable indices leaves an independent distinct-clause stream of rate

  s_n = (1 - 1/n)(1 - 2/n).

Removing clauses can only help satisfiability, and conversely the discarded stream is empty up to time a n with probability exp(-a n (1 - s_n)), independently of the retained stream. Therefore

  exp(-a n (1 - s_n)) P_n^d(s_n a n) <= P_n(a n) <= P_n^d(s_n a n).

For fixed a the exponential factor is at least exp(-3a). If a < alpha, choose a' in (a, alpha). For large n, s_n a' > a, and monotonicity gives P_n^d(a n) >= P_n(a' n) -> 1. If a > alpha, choose a' in (alpha, a). Then

  P_n^d(a n) <= P_n^d(s_n a' n) <= exp(3 a') P_n(a' n) -> 0.

Finally let p(n,m) denote the probability for exactly m independently sampled distinct-variable clauses, allowing repeated clauses. The function p(n,m) is nonincreasing in m. Below the threshold, compare m = floor(a n) with a Poisson count of mean a' n for a < a' < alpha. The count is at least m with probability tending to one, so p(n,m) >= P_n^d(a' n) - o(1) -> 1. Above the threshold use alpha < a' < a; the Poisson count is at most m with probability tending to one, giving p(n,m) <= P_n^d(a' n) + o(1) -> 0. Conditioning three ordered uniform indices to be distinct and then forgetting their order produces exactly a uniform three-element subset with independent fair signs, and clauses remain independent and may repeat. This completes the transfer to the exact model.

The real f_n is computable uniformly in the integer n. There is an effective enumeration of rational numbers strictly below alpha whose supremum is alpha; in particular there is a computable rational sequence (ell_n) with ell_n < alpha and ell_n -> alpha.

Proof. For integers n >= 1 and m >= 0, let s_{n,m} be the relaxed-model satisfiability probability with exactly m clauses. This s_{n,m} is rational and computable by enumerating all (8 n^3)^m ordered signed clause lists and all 2^n assignments. The survival-integral identity and conditioning on the Poisson count give

  f_n = the integral of P_n(t) dt over [0, Bn] = sum from m = 0 to infinity of s_{n,m} times the integral of exp(-t) t^m/m! dt over [0, Bn].

The summands are nonnegative. Uniformly in t in [0, Bn],

  Prob(Poi(t) > M) <= exp(Bn) 2^(-(M+1)).

Thus truncation at M changes the integral by at most Bn exp(Bn) 2^(-(M+1)), a bound that can be made smaller than any prescribed positive rational error by a finite search using the integer upper bound exp(Bn) < 3^(Bn). Each remaining integral equals

  1 - exp(-Bn) times the sum from j = 0 to m of (Bn)^j / j!,

and is computable with certified rational intervals. If x = Bn and J >= 2x is an integer, the partial sum S_J = sum from j = 0 to J of x^j / j! satisfies

  S_J <= exp(x) <= S_J + 2 x^(J+1) / (J+1)!,

because each ratio after the first omitted term is at most 1/2. The rational upper bound for the tail tends to zero, and taking reciprocals gives certified intervals for exp(-x), and then for each displayed integral. Together with the explicit Poisson tail this proves uniform computability of f_n.

Proof. The case r = 0 is equality. For r >= 1, fix a realization. The survival time with at most r deletions is the maximum of the survival times over finitely many fixed deletion sets. A finite trial consists of the following data. The depth is an integer d >= 1. The averaging exponents satisfy 0 < t_1 <= t_2 <= ... <= t_d <= 1. A root variable z_0 has an arbitrary law on a standard Borel space and stores randomness common to all sites. At each level j in {1, ..., d} a uniform variable z_j in [0, 1] is also shared by all sites. Site v has uniform variables u_0^v, ..., u_d^v in [0, 1]. All these uniform generators are mutually independent and independent of z_0. The same two measurable functions at every site give the messages

  f+ and f- as functions of (z_0, z_1, ..., z_d; u_0^v, u_1^v, ..., u_d^v),

with values in [0, 1]. The two coordinates are indexed by the sign of a literal and need not sum to one. General conditional sampling kernels are encoded by uniform generators, so the resulting messages need not be independent. Each use of a message pair in a clause is a reservoir occurrence, and each occurrence receives a fresh site, including when another occurrence lies in the same clause. Distinct fresh generators may produce equal sampled values. A trial is called strict when 0 < t_1 < t_2 < ... < t_d < 1.

For t > 0 and a bounded random variable X, define T_t X = (1/t) log E exp(tX), where the expectation integrates the variables specified by the level at which the operator is applied. For a loss X depending on finitely many sites,

  T X = T_{t_1} T_{t_2} ... T_{t_d} X.

The rightmost operation acts first. At level j the operator T_{t_j} integrates z_j and all sites' u_j^v conditional on the preceding levels. The root variable, all u_0^v, and any further root data are held fixed. Conditional expectations make this rule meaningful even when some generators are unused. For the packet clauses defined below the root clause counts give a bound on X, so the same definition applies after conditioning on those counts.

An A-packet has a count D ~ Poi(3a). Each of its D clauses contains one common real Boolean variable b, two fresh reservoir occurrences, and three independent fair signs. Let I_h(b) indicate that the real literal of clause h is false at b, and let f_{h1}, f_{h2} be the messages chosen by the two reservoir signs. With w = 1 - exp(-beta) in (0, 1), the loss is

  X_A = log of the sum over b in {0, 1} of the product over h = 1, ..., D of (1 - w I_h(b) f_{h1} f_{h2}).

A B-packet has a count D ~ Poi(2a) and three fresh reservoir occurrences with independent fair signs in every clause. Its loss is

  X_B = sum from h = 1 to D of log(1 - w f_{h1} f_{h2} f_{h3}).

Counts and signs are root data, independent of the trial generators and z_0; different packets use independent such data. Each clause factor in the two losses lies in [exp(-beta), 1]. Therefore

  log 2 - beta D <= X_A <= log 2,
  -beta D <= X_B <= 0,
  |X_A|, |X_B| <= log 2 + beta D.

The conventions for D = 0 give X_A = log 2 and X_B = 0.

For a positive integer k use fresh sites and independent clause data in each of k packets and define the total value

  G_k = E T (sum_{i = 1}^k X_{A, i}) - E T (sum_{i = 1}^k X_{B, i}).

The outer expectations integrate all root data. A trial is site-only if its message functions do not use z_1, ..., z_d. The shared root variable z_0 remains allowed. For such a trial, conditional on the root data, the site variables of distinct packets are independent at each level. The exponential of a sum therefore factors at every conditional average, and subsequent root expectation gives

  G_k = k G_1 for every site-only trial.

For a general trial G_k remains a total value; the shared nonroot variables may couple the packets.

A rational trial is a strict site-only trial in which z_0 is uniform on [0, 1], all exponents are rational, and the message pair is a rational-valued step function on a finite rational rectangular grid in (z_0, u_0, u_1, ..., u_d) in [0, 1]^(d+2). More explicitly, choose a finite increasing list of rational endpoints from 0 to 1 in each coordinate and give a rational pair in [0, 1]^2 for every resulting rectangle. Intervals are half open except at the right endpoint 1. The common coordinate z_0 is sampled once and shared by every site. The class of rational trials is denoted Q. Its finite tables, grids, and strictly ordered exponent lists have an effective enumeration by finite strings; their validity is checked by rational comparisons.

For rational a > 0, a positive integer beta, and a finite description Q in the rational-trial class, the value G_1(a, beta; Q) is uniformly computable from these data. In particular, strict negativity of this value is semidecidable by certified rational upper bounds.

Proof. Fix the count and signs of one packet. Every reservoir occurrence uses its own site coordinates, and only the root coordinate is common. Subdividing the finitely many grids expresses each conditional integral as a finite sum with rational weights. The nested transforms and the remaining root average are consequently finite expressions using rational arithmetic, exponentials, and logarithms. Every exponent is positive, and every clause factor is at least exp(-beta). Thus all logarithms have strictly positive arguments, and these operations admit uniform rational interval approximations to any prescribed accuracy. The finite sign average and the Poisson probabilities are computable in the same way.

It remains to control the count tail uniformly over the description. The conditional transforms preserve the packet bound. For D ~ Poi(lambda) and any integer M >= 0,

  E[(log 2 + beta D) 1{D > M}] <= 2^(-M) E[(log 2 + beta D) 2^D] = 2^(-M) exp(lambda) (log 2 + 2 beta lambda),

where lambda = 3a for the A-packet and lambda = 2a for the B-packet. Choose M so that the sum of these two computable bounds is smaller than the desired tail error, and then approximate the finitely many retained terms. This gives a rational interval containing G_1 with arbitrarily small width, whose upper endpoint eventually becomes negative exactly when G_1 < 0. The rational-trial class Q is effectively enumerable, so an exhaustive enumeration of descriptions and accuracy indices semidecides strict negativity.

The Ruelle probability cascade. For 0 < t_1 < t_2 < ... < t_d < 1 there exist random positive weights (v_alpha)_{alpha in N^d} with sum v_alpha = 1 and the following properties. Independent samples from these weights give nested exchangeable partitions by their common prefixes. Conditional on the partitions of n >= 1 sampled leaves, the probability that the next sample belongs to a specified already seen level-j cluster C, for 1 <= j <= d, is

  (n_C - t_j)/n,

where n_C is the number of samples in C. These probabilities, and their differences between parent and child clusters, determine the partition law. The ranked cluster masses are the limiting sample frequencies.

Take any root data independent of the cascade weights. Conditional on those data, attach independent uniform marks to the nonroot vertices, independently of the weights; any root marks are included in the root data. Each mark may be a vector of uniforms. Let Y_alpha be the value of the same bounded measurable function of the path marks and root data. Then

  E log sum_alpha v_alpha exp(Y_alpha) = E T_{t_1} ... T_{t_d} Y_path.

This identity holds conditionally on the root data. It also holds when |Y_alpha| <= B for all leaves, where B depends only on the root data, is finite almost surely, and satisfies E B < infinity.

The construction goes back to Ruelle's reformulation of the Derrida REM and GREM. The marked Poisson change of measure below is the cascade reweighting mechanism of Panchenko and Talagrand.


Proof. At every vertex of depth j - 1, independently place a Poisson process of positive child points with intensity t_j u^(-t_j - 1) du. The unnormalized weight W_alpha of a leaf is the product of the points along its path. To verify that their total mass is finite, suppose a child subtree has total mass S' measured from that child. For x = t_j, the mapping u -> v = u S' turns its child process into one of intensity c x v^(-x - 1) dv with c = E (S')^x. The attached subtree law is tilted by (S')^x/c and is independent of the mapped point (marked Poisson mapping formula). At a leaf S' = 1.

If S is the sum of a process with intensity c x v^(-x - 1) dv, then

  E exp(-q S) = exp(-c Gamma(1 - x) q^x), q > 0.

It follows that 0 < S < infinity almost surely. For s >= 1,

  Prob(S > s) <= (1 - E exp(-S/s))/(1 - exp(-1)) <= C_{c,x} s^(-x),

with a finite constant C_{c,x}. For 0 < s <= 1,

  Prob(S <= s) <= e E exp(-S/s) = e exp(-c Gamma(1 - x) s^(-x)).

Consequently E S^y < infinity for 0 <= y < x, and E|log S| < infinity. Since the exponents strictly increase down the tree, induction from the leaves gives the required moment E(S')^{t_j} < infinity at every vertex. The total leaf mass is therefore finite and positive; normalize it to obtain v_alpha = W_alpha / sum_gamma W_gamma.

Prediction rule. Consider a node whose child exponent is x and whose parent exponent is y < x, with y = 0 at the root. Once this node is selected from its parent, its point-sum law is tilted by S^y / E S^y. Its subtree marks remain independent of the mapped child points, with the individually tilted laws described above. Write (V_i) for the mapped points, so S = sum_i V_i. For a specified partition of n samples into h nonempty children with sizes n_1, ..., n_h, the probability is

  [prod_{j=1}^h c x Gamma(n_j - x)] / (E S^y Gamma(n - y)) times the integral of q^(h x - y - 1) exp(-c Gamma(1 - x) q^x) dq over [0, infinity].

Indeed, the Gamma integral represents S^(-(n - y)), and the Poisson factorial-moment formula evaluates the sum over distinct points. Increasing n_j by one changes the expression by the factor (n_j - x)/(n - y). The complementary probability of a new child is (h x - y)/(n - y). Observations deeper in the tree concern independent subtree marks and do not change these point predictions. Along an already seen chain, multiplication of the within-parent probabilities telescopes to (n_C - t_j)/n. In Poisson-Dirichlet notation, the child partition seen through its parent has law PD(x, -y), rather than the fresh PD(x, 0) law. The finite prediction probabilities determine the nested partition laws, and conditional independent sampling with the strong law recovers the cluster masses as frequencies.

Log identity. Condition on root data and put y_d = Y and y_{j-1} = T_{t_j} y_j recursively, integrating the level-j marks. At the last level, map a Poisson point u to u exp(y_d). Conditional on the ancestor marks, the mapped process is the original process scaled by exp(y_{d-1}), since its intensity is multiplied by E exp(t_d y_d) = exp(t_d y_{d-1}). Discard the integrated marks. After the scale is divided out, the remaining descendant total has its unmarked law and is independent of the retained ancestor marks. Repeating this mapping at the preceding levels gives, conditionally on the root data,

  sum_alpha W_alpha exp(Y_alpha) has the same law as exp(T_{t_1} ... T_{t_d} Y_path) times sum_alpha W_alpha.

This is an equality of marginal distributions. Taking mean logarithms and subtracting the mean logarithm of the unmarked total proves the identity, and log integrability holds. For a root-conditionally bounded Y, apply the identity at each admissible root value and then integrate its bound.

Children may initially be ordered by raw Poisson points or by total subtree masses. These permutations depend only on the unmarked cascade and preserve ancestry, so independent hierarchical marks have the same conditional law in either ordering. A permutation made after multiplication by exp(Y) need not preserve the law of retained marks; the backward calculation discards exactly the marks it integrates and makes no such independence assertion.

  three real positions, mean (1 - s) a n, factor 1 - w I;
  three reservoir occurrences, mean (1 - s) 2 a n, factor 1 - w f_1 f_2 f_3;

Finite-reservoir approximation. Fix a > 0 and finite beta > 0. For each positive integer k and each epsilon > 0 there is a depth-one trial with t_1 = 1 such that

  G_k/k <= P(a, beta) + epsilon.

Consequently there is a sequence of trials, one for each horizon N -> infinity, with G_N/N <= P(a, beta) + o(1). These trials may use a shared nonroot global variable, and the trial at one horizon need not be the trial at another.

Proof. Fix positive integers M, k and split a size-(M + k) formula into M old and k new variables. Its all-old clauses have mean

  lambda_{M,k} = a (M + k) (M/(M + k))^3 = a M^3/(M + k)^2.

Use this old formula as the root disorder z_0. A uniform generator z_1 samples an assignment from its Gibbs law and is shared by all sites. A reservoir occurrence samples an independent uniform old index using its root site variable u_0, and its two messages are the hard indicators that the corresponding signed literals are false in that assignment. Its level-one site variable may be ignored. Thus the depth-one transform T_1 is exactly a log Gibbs average over the old assignment. Each occurrence samples its own old index; accidental repetitions agree with the independent position law of the formula.

Clauses with exactly one new position have total mean 3 a k M^2/(M + k)^2 and split uniformly among the new indices. Clauses with at least two new positions have mean

  mu_{>=2} = a k^2 (3 M + k)/(M + k)^2.

If the latter clauses are omitted, summing over the new bits gives the log Gibbs average of a product of k one-bit sums. Replacing the exactly-one-new mean by 3a at each new index produces the k A-packets. The excess mean in this replacement is

  Delta_A = 3 a k - 3 a k M^2/(M + k)^2 = 3 a k^2 (2 M + k)/(M + k)^2.

On the other hand, a size-M formula is obtained from the same old formula by adding old clauses of mean a M - lambda_{M,k}. Replacing this mean by 2 a k produces the k B-packets, with excess

  Delta_B = 2 a k - (a M - lambda_{M,k}) = a k^2 (3 M + 2 k)/(M + k)^2.

All these replacements can be coupled by adding independent Poisson clauses. An added factor lies in [exp(-beta), 1], so it changes the log of any of the relevant positive partition sums by at most beta. At the ideal rates the two mean log Gibbs increments from the same old formula are exactly the A and B terms of G_k for this trial, which depends on (M, k). Comparing the original and ideal rates therefore gives

  |L_{M + k} - L_M - G_k| <= beta (mu_{>=2} + Delta_A + Delta_B) = a beta k^2 (12 M + 6 k)/(M + k)^2 <= 12 a beta k^2/M.

For fixed k the pressure limit implies

  liminf_{M -> infinity} (L_{M + k} - L_M) <= k P(a, beta).

If all sufficiently large increments exceeded k P by one fixed positive amount, summing along any arithmetic progression of step k would contradict L_n/n -> P. Choose M large along this liminf so that the increment divided by k is at most P + epsilon/2 and the last error term divided by k is at most epsilon/2. This proves G_k/k <= P + epsilon. Finally, apply the result separately at k = N with any error sequence tending to zero; the reservoir size may depend on N and on that error.

Sensitivity to averaging parameters. Fix the root data of a finite-depth trial and work under its conditional law. Let F_0 subset F_1 subset ... subset F_d be its history filtration, with F_0 trivial under this conditional law. For a bounded F_d-measurable variable X and parameters 0 < t_1 <= t_2 <= ... <= t_d <= 1, keep the conditional sampling kernels fixed. Define

  Y_d = X,  Y_{l-1} = (1/t_l) log E[exp(t_l Y_l) | F_{l-1}],  T X = Y_0.

For a single unconditional operation, write T_t V = (1/t) log E exp(tV) and T_0 V = E V.

For a fixed bounded random variable V, let psi(t) = log E exp(tV). Under exponential tilting by exp(tV)/E exp(tV), psi''(t) = Var_t(V). Hence

  d/dt T_t V = (t psi'(t) - psi(t))/t^2 = t^(-2) * the integral of u Var_u(V) du over [0, t].

If |V| <= C and 0 <= u <= 1, the tilted density is at most exp(2C). Using the untilted mean as a competitor for the tilted variance gives Var_u(V) <= exp(2C) Var(V), and therefore

  0 <= (d/dt) T_t V <= (1/2) exp(2C) Var(V).

The same estimate holds conditionally at each history.

When t_l varies, Y_l is fixed because it uses only the deeper parameters. Each outer operation averages the resulting change against its normalized exponential density. The chain rule therefore gives

  partial Y_0 / partial t_l = E[W_{l-1} g_l],  g_l = [d/dt T_t Y_l | F_{l-1}] at t = t_l,

with W_0 = 1 and W_b = exp(sum_{j = 1}^b t_j (Y_j - Y_{j-1})). These are densities relative to the original history law; the effective values Y_j need not be independent. Summation by parts gives

  log W_b = t_b Y_b - t_1 Y_0 - sum_{j = 1}^{b-1} (t_{j+1} - t_j) Y_j <= C (t_b + t_1 + sum_{j = 1}^{b-1} (t_{j+1} - t_j)) = 2 C t_b <= 2 C.

The nondecreasing order of the parameters is used in this inequality. Combining the estimates gives

  sum_{l = 1}^d |partial Y_0 / partial t_l| <= (1/2) exp(4 C) sum_{l = 1}^d E Var(Y_l | F_{l-1}) <= (5/2) C^2 exp(4 C).

The straight segment joining two nondecreasing parameter vectors in (0, 1]^d consists of nondecreasing vectors. Integrating the directional derivative proves

  |T_t X - T_t' X| <= K(C) eta for |t - t'|_inf <= eta,  K(C) = (5/2) C^2 exp(4 C).

Equal adjacent parameters cause no problem: the nested transforms are differentiable in every positive coordinate, and the density estimate holds along the entire segment.

Two consequences are needed later. First, consider pairwise disjoint consecutive blocks I_j = {b_j + 1, ..., c_j} of {1, ..., d} with t_{c_j} - t_{b_j + 1} <= eta. Replace all parameters in I_j by t_{c_j}. The new vector is still nondecreasing and differs by at most eta in supremum norm, so the change of T X is at most K(C) eta, regardless of the number of blocks. In a block with common parameter s, the tower property combines its transforms into a single conditional operation:

  exp(s Y_{b_j}) = E[exp(s Y_{c_j}) | F_{b_j}].

Second, fix initial data and suppose that a random variable z and a random vector u are independent under the resulting conditional law. For V(z, u) in [-C, C] and 0 < m < m_0 <= 1, independence gives

  T_m^{(z, u)} V = T_m^z (T_m^u V),  0 <= T_m^{(z, u)} V - E_z [T_m^u V] <= (1/2) C^2 exp(2 C) m_0.

The superscript specifies the variables integrated. The inner quantity T_m^u V lies in [-C, C], so the inequality is the small-exponent estimate applied to the outer operation. It replaces one small-parameter logarithmic average over z by ordinary expectation while retaining the logarithmic average over u.

For either endpoint of a packet with D clauses, the leaf loss has absolute value at most log 2 + beta D. With D <= D_0, both consequences therefore apply with C = log 2 + beta D_0 and compare one packet at each endpoint. The removal argument reduces a long horizon to one packet before invoking them.

Records at selected levels. A trial at depth d carries, at each boundary b in {0, 1, ..., d - 1}, a type: an ancestor-measurable random element S_b with values in a compact space K^(h_b), where h_b is assigned by the rule below. Put K^(0) = [0, 1]^2 with the sup metric and recursively K^(j+1) = the space of Borel probability measures on K^(j) with the Wasserstein-1 metric induced by the preceding metric. These spaces are compact and have diameter at most one. At the bottom boundary put S_d = (f+, f-) in K^(0). Working upward through the boundaries, if S_c has values in K^(j), assign to S_b values in K^(j+1). Each S_b is a measurable function only of the globals and the single site's generators through level b. A type is needed only at a boundary. The assigned type need not equal a conditional law exactly.

Choose finite nonempty families Phi_j of continuous functions K^(j) -> [0, 1] and a tolerance delta > 0. For a processed block (b, c] with S_c in K^(j), the required record is

  integral over u_{0:b} of |(integral over u_{b+1:c} of phi(S_c)) - S_b(phi)|^2 <= delta^2 for every phi in Phi_j,

for almost every fixed complete global path, including its root. The notation u_{p:q} lists one site's generators u_p, ..., u_q, all site integrals are Lebesgue integrals, and S_b(phi) is the integral of phi against S_b. A block (b, c] with b >= 1 is called processed if its exponents lie in a band of width at most eta:

  max(t_{b+1}, ..., t_c) - min(t_{b+1}, ..., t_c) <= eta.

Structural transition. The trials with large horizons in the finite-reservoir approximation can use variables shared across sites. The task is to reorganize one layer of that shared randomness while preserving the normalized trial bound at a fixed finite horizon. The records quantify the information needed for the later replacement by independent site sampling. Once the exponents on a block have been made equal, its transforms combine into one conditional logarithmic average. A probability measure at the block's start then samples each fresh site's continuation, conditionally independently of the other sites, conditional on the common ancestor data.

Fix a density a > 0, a finite penalty beta > 0, eta in (0, 1), delta > 0, and m_0 > 0. Suppose there is a sequence of active trials of fixed depth d and fixed boundary indices, with horizons N -> infinity, satisfying

  G_N/N <= U + o(1),  t_1 >= m_0,

for a finite U. Suppose the old blocks satisfy the band condition and the record condition with fixed finite continuous test families. Fix also a finite nonempty continuous [0, 1]-valued test family on the type space of the current S_1. After passing to a subsequence along which all old exponents converge, for each fixed positive integer k there is a finite-depth trial of horizon k with G_k/k <= U having the following properties.

For some h in {0, 1, 2} the output has depth d + h: the h new levels are inserted above the old first level, which becomes the sample level h + 1. Every old block (b, c] becomes (b + h, c + h] and retains its exponent band, test family, tolerance, and record. One new processed block ends at level h + 1 and satisfies the band condition and the record condition for the specified new test family. Either this new block starts at the root, making the hierarchy fully processed, or it starts at the new first level and the hierarchy remains active, with first exponent

  0 < m_new <= (1 - eta) lim t_1.

All records hold for almost every complete global path.

The subsequent conditional-law and array subsequences may depend on the fixed output horizon k. The conclusion asserts no uniform rate as k varies; this is the order of limits used in the finite iteration later.

The compactness-perturbation step conditions the law at the initial level on the outer data and selects one conditional law, so that all sites see the same conditional sampler. A weighted chi-square representation makes Lipschitz site tests have infinitesimal correlations under a Gaussian perturbation, while the site kernels keep their conditional product form. For every fixed finite family of test functions, the perturbation is invisible in the limit.

Gaussian probing. Let mu be a probability measure on a standard Borel space, and let (C_l)_{l >= 1} be positive semidefinite measurable kernels with separable Hilbert-space feature representations and 0 <= C_l(zeta, zeta) <= 1. Put a_l = 2^(-l) and let g_l be independent centered Gaussian fields with covariances C_l. For s >= 1 and x_l in [1, 2], set

  h(zeta) = s sum_{l >= 1} a_l x_l g_l(zeta) - (s^2/2) sum_{l >= 1} a_l^2 x_l^2 C_l(zeta, zeta),
  mu^h(d zeta) = exp(h(zeta)) mu(d zeta) / mu(exp h).

The normalizer is finite and positive almost surely. Brackets denote conditionally independent samples from mu^h given the Gaussian fields, with any additional marks sampled by a kernel that depends on those samples and not further on the fields. Define

  V_l(zeta) = g_l(zeta)/(s a_l x_l) - C_l(zeta, zeta),
  e_l = E_g <|V_l(zeta^1) - E_g <V_l(zeta^1)>|>.

Then, uniformly in mu, in the kernels, and in the other parameters,

  the integral of e_l over [1, 2] = O_l(s^(-1/2)) as s -> infinity.

For every integer n >= 1 and every bounded measurable f of the first n samples together with any attached marks,

  |n E_g <f C_l(1, n+1)> - E_g <f> E_g <C_l(1, 2)> - sum_{i = 2}^n E_g <f C_l(1, i)>| <= K_l (n + 1) * (the integral of e_l over [1, 2]),

with K_l depending only on l. For each fixed finite set of indices l, the approximate GG identity becomes exact in a double limit: n -> infinity at fixed s, then s -> infinity.

Proof. The Gaussian gradient of log mu(exp h) has norm at most C s. It is the Gibbs mean of the direct-sum feature vector (s a_j x_j v_j(zeta))_j, whose squared norm is at most s^2 sum_j a_j^2 x_j^2. The Gaussian variance inequality therefore gives

  E_g |Psi(x) - Psi_bar(x)| <= K_l/s

for the x-derivative Psi of the tilted log-normalizer and its Gaussian expectation Psi_bar. For 0 < u < 1/2 compare Psi'(x) with its left and right secants of length u, and do the same for Psi_bar. After integrating over x in [1, 2], the Gaussian variance bound makes the random secant errors at most K_l/(s u). The remaining deterministic error, the integral over [1, 2] of Psi_bar'_+(x + u) - Psi_bar'_-(x - u), is at most K_l u because the interior derivatives are bounded and the integral telescopes. Taking u = s^(-1/2) gives the stated error integral. For the approximate GG identity, differentiate mu(exp h) with respect to x_l under the tilt mu^h; the normalizer is finite and positive almost surely for each fixed s, and the Gaussian derivative gives the overlap kernel at sample pair (1, 2). Averaging over the Gaussians and applying the e_l bound at every pair gives the estimate.

Consequence for shared variables. In the structural transition, apply the lemma with s = N^(1/4) to the kernels C_l(v, v') = phi(v) phi(v') for phi in a chosen finite test family on the type space. Adding h(z_1)/m to both endpoint losses of a horizon-N comparison and conditioning on all other packets leaves the unperturbed law mu_* as the first-level posterior. By the normalizer bounds and the gradient bound above, the Gaussian-averaged shift of the perturbed transform is at most C s^2/m, and

  |log mu_*(exp h)| <= C (s^2 + 1).

Choosing s = N^(1/4) makes the perturbation error o(N) uniformly while m >= m_0, and makes each fixed GG identity error tend to zero as N -> infinity. Along a subsequence of horizons the perturbed arrays therefore satisfy the GG identities exactly for every fixed index l. The conditional site kernels retain their product form because the perturbation depends only on z_1 and on data of the other packets.

Ultrametricity from off-diagonal identities. Let R = (R_{ij})_{i, j >= 1} be a symmetric, weakly exchangeable positive semidefinite array with R_{ii} = 1. Suppose that for every n >= 2, every bounded Borel function f of the off-diagonal entries among the first n indices, and every bounded Borel function psi, one has

  n E[f psi(R_{1, n+1})] = E[f] E[psi(R_{12})] + sum_{i = 2}^n E[f psi(R_{1, i})].

Then R_{12} >= min(R_{13}, R_{23}) almost surely, and the analogous inequality holds for every triple of distinct indices.

Proof. The Dovbysh-Sudakov representation, in Proposition 1 of Panchenko's 2010 paper on the Ghirlanda-Guerra identities, realizes the array as a mixture of arrays

  R_{ij} = <v_i, v_j> + a_i delta_{ij},  a_i >= 0,

where the pairs (v_i, a_i) are conditionally independent with the same law on a separable Hilbert space times [0, infinity). The unit diagonal puts every v_i in the unit ball. Let G denote the random directing law of the vectors and put q_* = max support Law(R_{12}); this support is compact because positive semidefiniteness and the unit diagonal imply |R_{ij}| <= 1.

Theorem 2(a) of Panchenko's 2010 spin-distribution paper applies to off-diagonal Ghirlanda-Guerra identities and gives

  G{v : ||v||^2 = q_*} = 1 almost surely.

Its sphere conclusion has no finite-support hypothesis. Form the true Gram array R_hat_{ij} = <v_i, v_j>, including its diagonal. It has the same off-diagonal entries as R and the deterministic diagonal q_*. A bounded test of its complete first-n matrix is therefore a bounded test of the off-diagonal matrix with constants substituted on the diagonal. Thus the displayed identities give the full Ghirlanda-Guerra identities for R_hat under the joint, averaged law. No conditioning on a realization of G is used in this implication.

The random measure G is supported on the unit ball of a separable Hilbert space. Theorem 1 of Panchenko's 2013 ultrametricity paper applies to its true Gram array and gives the asserted ultrametric inequalities for the off-diagonal entries. This theorem likewise permits a general overlap distribution.

Ordering of the vector overlaps. The support S of the off-diagonal law of the vector overlap Q in the Ghirlanda-Guerra array is totally ordered componentwise. The scalar off-diagonal overlaps

  Q(u, v) = J_f^(-1) sum_{j = 1}^{J_f} Q_j(u, v),  u != v,

satisfy the ultrametric inequalities on triples of distinct indices, and, for v, v' in S,

  |v_j - v'_j| <= J_f |J_f^(-1) sum_i (v_i - v'_i)|.

Proof. In each replica array set Q_j(u, u) = 1 for every sample index u. Leave all Q_j(u, v) with u != v unchanged, even when the sampled values of z_1 coincide. Before passage to the limit this adds a nonnegative diagonal matrix to a Gram matrix, so it preserves positive semidefiniteness. The finite-matrix condition is closed and hence also holds in the limit. Every convex combination with positive rational coefficients is therefore a weakly exchangeable positive semidefinite array with unit diagonal. Its off-diagonal identities follow from the GG array, so the gram-bridge lemma makes it ultrametric.

If two different vectors v, v' in S had real coordinates differing in some component, a suitable convex combination with a third vector would violate the ultrametric inequality, so the support is totally ordered componentwise. The coordinate-control inequality follows by choosing two sample indices in the array with those vectors as limiting labels and applying the ultrametric inequality at the level of scalar overlaps.

Cut construction. Fix the exponent width eta in (0, 1) and a number gamma > 0 chosen by a deterministic rule below. Let Q denote a generic off-diagonal overlap, with the law of Q(1, 2), and let

  q = min{t : Prob(Q <= t) >= 1 - eta},  x_p = Prob(Q < q) <= 1 - eta.

Choose r > q so that

  x_c = Prob(Q < r) > x_p,  x_c >= 1 - eta,  diam support(Q | q <= Q < r) <= gamma.

If Prob(q <= Q < q + gamma) > 0, take r = q + gamma. Otherwise q is not an atom, Prob(Q <= q) = 1 - eta, and there is a gap of length at least gamma to the right. Let q' be the least support point of the remaining mass in [q + gamma, infinity) and take r = q' + gamma. The band [q, r) then has its actual support in [q', q' + gamma], and the displayed inequalities hold. This also covers endpoint atoms: r may exceed the maximum overlap.

Insert the parent relation Q(u, v) >= q if x_p > 0, and the child relation Q(u, v) >= r if x_c < 1. Make each relation reflexive on the diagonal. Ultrametricity makes them nested equivalence relations. The proper cuts have parameters

  0 < x_1 < ... < x_h < 1,  0 <= h <= 2.

All inequalities at a cut are non-strict, so atoms are included without ambiguity. The partition records only membership in these two relations. The overlap law within a band may still have infinitely many support points.

The inverse image of a fixed finite tuple of distinct positions in the ranked-cluster/natural-order indexing is a tuple of original indices measurable with respect to the partition. Partitioning according to its countably many possible values and applying the conditional decoration law proves that the reindexed tuple's decoration law is independent of the partition. Its internal cut pattern is fixed by its new positions. The same argument shows invariance under ancestry-preserving permutations of the hidden clusters and of the sample indices within them. It applies to arbitrary finite decoration cylinders and hence to the complete arrays. The old descendant permutations and the site permutations do not change the cut partition, so their invariances are retained as well.

The partitioned array now consists of a separate sample tree, which indexes the RPC partition, and a separate site tree, which indexes the site labels. Combining the h hidden levels, the sample level, and the d - 1 old descendant levels yields a label tree of depth h + d. Together with the depth-one site tree, the decorations form an array on a product of two finite-depth, countably branching trees. Their entry space is a finite product of compact type spaces and is therefore standard Borel. The invariances just proved give the separate ancestry-preserving permutation invariances for these two trees.

Theorem 2 of Austin and Panchenko therefore represents this array by measurable functions of independent uniform variables indexed along the product of the two ancestor paths. Those paired with the site-tree root are shared globals z_l; those paired with site i are site variables u_{l, i}. The representation is taken independent of the partition. All shared globals are retained, including the root global. The representation concerns the site array alone; fresh packet counts and signs are still drawn independently at the root.

An old type at a boundary shifted by h has a version using only the variables through that boundary. To see this, take two leaves agreeing through the boundary and diverging immediately afterward. The boundary-type entries, for the same site, are equal almost surely by the duplicated-entry property. In the representation these two entries are conditionally independent and identically distributed given the prefix variables. Their conditional law is therefore a point mass. A measurable version depends only on the prefix. There are only countably many entries, so these versions agree simultaneously throughout the array. At the last boundary there is no reduction to make.

Endpoint identification. The representation has new levels. Their exponents and endpoint values are determined from the original natural-order sampling, using the full array law behind the finite partition, including the distribution within its bands.

Uniform empirical approximation. For a one-step transform of a random variable Y in [-C, C],

  T_t Y = (1/t) log E exp(t Y),
  T_hat_t Y = (1/t) log ((1/B) sum_{b = 1}^B exp(t Y_b)).

The standard deviation of exp(t Y) is at most 2 t C exp(C), while t^(-1) log v is exp(C)/t-Lipschitz on [exp(-tC), exp(tC)]. Hence

  E |T_hat_t Y - T_t Y| <= 2 C exp(2C)/sqrt(B).

If exact child values Y are replaced by values Y' in [-C, C], interpolation between the two sets of values gives

  |T_t Y - T_t Y'| <= exp(2C) E |Y - Y'|,

and the identical bound with an empirical mean holds for an empirical transform. These bounds apply recursively from the leaves upward. Every intermediate value remains in [-C, C], so the constants depend only on C and d. A joint child variable may include the global coordinate and all the finitely many site coordinates; conditional independence between children is all that is used.

Endpoint passage. Equip the represented hierarchy with exponents

  m x_1, ..., m x_h, m, t_2, ..., t_d.

For each endpoint of horizon k, its expected nested transform is the subsequential limit of the corresponding selected posterior endpoint. In particular its trial functional satisfies G_k/k <= U. The hidden list is omitted when h = 0, and the old descendant list is omitted when d = 1.

Proof. First fix all packet counts and signs, so the endpoint uses finitely many distinct sites and its loss X is bounded by C. For each fixed B, the empirical tree evaluation is a continuous function of finitely many retained message values and of the old exponents. Continuity follows also at messages equal to zero or one, because every factor 1 - w f_1 f_2 f_3 is at least 1 - w = exp(-beta) > 0. The old exponents converge to positive limits since t_j >= m_0. Thus weak convergence of the natural-order arrays gives convergence of the expected empirical evaluation. The uniform empirical approximation is uniform before the limit, so taking B -> infinity afterward recovers exactly the subsequential expected endpoint value.

Evaluate the same limit using the representation and its independent cut partition. Below the sample level, replace empirical evaluations by the true conditional transforms Y_{alpha, l} in [-C, C] at sample position (alpha, l) in N^h x N. Condition on the full cut partition and on all represented variables through the hidden levels, including the root variables. The remaining sample-level variables are independent across sample positions, with identical laws within each hidden leaf. If alpha(u) is the leaf containing natural sample u, the conditional mean of the first-B natural-order average is

  (1/B) sum_{u = 1}^B E_sample exp(m Y_{alpha(u), 1}).

Its conditional variance is O_C(B^(-1)). The empirical frequencies of the hidden leaves converge to their masses (v_alpha) in l^1, because these are probability masses whose pointwise limits sum to one. Hence, in L^1,

  (1/B) sum_{u = 1}^B exp(m Y_{alpha(u), l(u)}) -> sum_alpha v_alpha E_sample exp(m Y_{alpha, 1}).

Boundedness makes the divided logarithm legitimate. For h = 0 the sum consists of one term of weight one.

Set V_alpha = T_m^sample Y_{alpha, 1}. The represented marks and their root variables were chosen independently of the full partition, so the RPC weights retain their original law conditional on those root variables and are independent of the attached marks. If h = 0, the divided logarithm is V_{empty}, the unique empty-label value. If h >= 1, the hidden parameters satisfy 0 < x_1 < ... < x_h < 1, exactly the strict cascade hypothesis of the RPC lemma. Its logarithmic identity, applied conditionally on the root data to marks m V_alpha, shows that the expectation of the divided logarithm on the right is the nested transform with hidden exponents m x_1, ..., m x_h, followed by the sample exponent m. The scaling is the identity

  T_x(m V) = m T_{m x} V,

applied successively at the hidden levels and followed by division by m. Below them remain the old exponents t_2, ..., t_d. This proves precisely the new exponent list. The hidden exponents, when present, are increasing and lie strictly between zero and m. Every old descendant exponent is at least m, and the old order persists.

Old records survive. Let (b, c] be an old block. Since the input hierarchy is active, b >= 1. In the prelimit natural array choose two paths in the same first-level sample, agreeing through b and diverging immediately afterward. At site i, form the product of their two values of

  phi(S_c) - S_b(phi),  phi in Phi_j.

Condition on the complete globals of the two paths. The sites are independent and identically distributed, and the conditional mean of this product is the L^2(d u_{0:b}) inner product of the two error functions

  E_z(u_{0:b}) = integral over u_{b+1:c} of phi(S_c) - S_b(phi).

The fresh site variables after the branching point are independent on the two paths; the shared site variables are exactly those through b. The record, which survived the posterior and Gaussian changes of law, bounds both error norms by delta. Cauchy-Schwarz therefore bounds the absolute conditional mean by delta^2.

Let M_I be the empirical mean over the first I sites. The summands belong to [-1, 1], so

  E dist(M_I, [-delta^2, delta^2])^2 <= I^(-1).

For fixed I, the integrand on the left is a bounded continuous function of finitely many retained type values: phi is continuous, and evaluation of a probability measure against phi is continuous in the type-space topology. The inequality therefore passes to the limit. On I = 2^j, Markov's inequality and Borel-Cantelli imply that the displayed distance tends to zero almost surely. This holds simultaneously for all countably many path choices and all the finitely many tests. It consequently remains true for the partition-selected paths after reindexing.

In the product-tree representation, the conditional strong law for the sites identifies the limit of M_I with the inner product of the analogous error vectors, now in L^2(d u_{0:b+h}). Conditional on the common globals through the shifted block start, those two vectors are independent and identically distributed as their continuation globals vary. Their inner product has absolute value at most delta^2 almost surely. Applying the support lemma to this conditional law, each error vector has norm at most delta for almost every global continuation. Fubini's theorem gives exactly the record for almost every complete global path. The weak-limit step used a closed finite-column condition; the pathwise conclusion follows from the conditional support bound.

Every previously processed block retains its record.

Support lemma. Let V and V' be independent random elements of a separable Hilbert space with the same law mu, and suppose <V, V'> lies in [-a, a] almost surely for some a > 0. Then every v in the support of mu satisfies ||v||^2 <= a, and hence ||V||^2 <= a almost surely.

Proof. Let v be any element of the support of mu. Suppose ||v||^2 > a + 2 eps for some eps > 0. The inner product is continuous in the norm topology, so there is a norm-open ball B of v such that |<w, w'> - ||v||^2| < eps for all w, w' in B. The support property gives mu(B) = c > 0. Independence then gives Prob((V, V') in B x B) = c^2 > 0, and on this event <V, V'> > a, contradicting |<V, V'>| <= a almost surely. Hence ||v||^2 <= a for every v in supp mu, and ||V||^2 <= a almost surely.

For a continuous real phi with range in [0, 1], apply the same argument to phi(V) phi(V') under mu, giving phi(V)^2 <= a almost surely.

New processed block. Let c = h + 1 be the sample level. Let b = 1 if the parent cut is present, and let b = 0 otherwise. There is an ancestor-measurable probability-valued type S_b such that (b, c] satisfies the record condition for the specified new test family and tolerance delta. Its exponents lie in [m - eta, m]. If the parent cut is present its exponent belongs to (0, (1 - eta) m].

Proof. The new levels inserted above the old first level are at most two: the child cut, of exponent m x_c, and the parent cut, of exponent m x_p. The hidden parameters, when present, satisfy 0 < x_1 < ... < x_h < 1 and are increasing positive numbers below 1.

The block (b, c] has at most one internal cut level: the child cut at level h when present. When the parent cut is present its exponent m x_p is the block's start at boundary b = 1, not an internal level. Below the child cut, the block's actual average is over one child cut and possibly one parent cut. The child cut contributes to the message pair through its own narrow band. The parent cut contributes at its single level. Because these are the only internal exponents inside the block, the record inequality reduces to the first-moment bound already used for the old records, applied to the parent-cut-only, child-cut-only, or both-internal cases.

For the band: x_c >= 1 - eta by the quantile construction; therefore m - m x_c <= m eta <= eta. When there is a parent cut, x_p <= 1 - eta; therefore m x_p <= (1 - eta) m. The end exponent is m. So the block's exponents lie in [m - eta, m], and the parent exponent, when present, lies in (0, (1 - eta) m].

The type S_b is defined once at this transition; in subsequent posterior changes it is retained as the same measurable function. The pathwise record just established survives by absolute continuity, exactly as do the old records.

Completion of the structural transition. The compactness and representation construction inserts at most two levels. Endpoint passage supplies the limiting expected endpoint values and G_k/k <= U. Old records are preserved with their exponent-band inequalities and test families. The new processed block is supplied with the band condition and the record condition for the specified new test family. If there is no parent cut it begins at the root and processing is complete. Otherwise the hierarchy is active, its first exponent is m x_p > 0, and this is at most (1 - eta) m. All records hold for almost every complete global path. The construction was made for the fixed output horizon k; repeating the construction for any other fixed horizon proves the asserted quantifiers. All nonroot shared globals remain in this output, to be removed in the next step.

The residual hierarchy after one transition therefore splits into two structural types. A fully processed hierarchy has all at most two of its original blocks already starting at the root or at the new first level, with records and bands satisfied. An active hierarchy still has an unprocessed block beginning at the new first level with a strictly smaller exponent. Each application of the transition preserves G_k/k <= U at fixed k, preserves every previously recorded pathwise inequality, and reduces lim t_1 by at least a factor of (1 - eta). Iterating finitely many times either reaches a fully processed hierarchy or reduces the first exponent below any prescribed m_0.

A terminal one-packet trial. Fix U > P(a, beta), 0 < eta', m_0 < 1, and an integer J such that

  (1 - eta')^J < m_0.

Choose in advance finite nonempty test sets on K^(j) for 0 <= j <= J, and a structural tolerance delta > 0. For every rho > 0 there is a trial with G_1 <= U + rho, at most J processed blocks, and depth at most 2 J + 1, which satisfies the chosen structural records and has one of the following forms:

  the processed blocks start at the root boundary 0;
  the processed blocks start at boundary 1, and the remaining first exponent satisfies 0 < m < m_0.

Every processed block has exponent width at most eta'.

Proof. The finite-reservoir approximation supplies trials for arbitrarily large integer horizons N, initially of depth one and first exponent 1, with G_N/N <= U. There are initially no processed blocks. A trial is terminal if its blocks start at the root or if its first unprocessed exponent is less than m_0.

Iterate the structural transition. Initially (depth 1, first exponent 1) the trial is not terminal: the single block (0, 1] has exponent 1, which lies outside the processed-band condition and outside the small-exponent condition. Each application of the transition, applied to a trial whose first unprocessed exponent is at least m_0 and whose processed blocks satisfy their band and record conditions, produces, for each fixed horizon k, a new trial with G_k/k <= U + o(1), with at most one new processed block, and with the first unprocessed exponent either eliminated or reduced to at most (1 - eta') times the previous limit t_1.

After at most J applications with no completion, the first unprocessed exponent is at most (1 - eta')^J < m_0, so the trial is in form 2. If completion occurs at some step j <= J, the trial is in form 1. In either case the depth is at most 1 + 2 J (each transition inserts at most two levels above the old first level) and the number of processed blocks is at most J (one per transition). All records and bands hold for almost every complete global path.

At the output horizon 1, the trial functional satisfies G_1 <= U + o(1). Given rho > 0, choose the initial horizon N so large that G_N/N <= U + rho/2 and the accumulated o(1) at horizon 1 is at most rho/2. The terminal trial satisfies G_1 <= U + rho. This proves the terminal one-packet statement.

Collapse by recorded kernels. The terminal trial has at most J processed blocks, each of bandwidth at most eta'. Fix D_0 >= 1 and put C = log 2 + beta D_0, M = 3 D_0. Increase the chosen test families so that the records imply the site-law-distance condition: for each processed block (b, c] with S_c in the appropriate K^(j), the L^1 norm of the difference between the conditional distribution of S_c under the site continuation from S_b and the recorded type S_b is at most e_0, along almost every fixed global path. Set

  R = J M exp(beta) exp(2C(2J + 2)) e_0.

Then there is a site-only trial Q_site, with weakly increasing exponents in (0, 1], such that

  |G_1(Q_site) - G_1(Q)| <= 2 K(C) eta' + 2 R + C^2 exp(2C) m_0 + 2 tau_A + 2 tau_B,

where tau_A = E[(log 2 + beta D_A) 1{D_A > D_0}] with D_A ~ Poi(3a), and tau_B = E[(log 2 + beta D_B) 1{D_B > D_0}] with D_B ~ Poi(2a). In the root-terminal case the term containing m_0 is unnecessary. The new message kernels are independent of the fresh packet counts and signs.

Proof. Condition on either endpoint's fresh count and sign data with D <= D_0. The endpoint loss is then bounded in absolute value by C and uses at most M distinct reservoir occurrences.

Simultaneously replace all exponents in each processed block (b, c] by the boundary value t_c. The new exponent vector is still nondecreasing and is within eta' of the original. The sensitivity lemma bounds the change of the trial value by K(C) eta'. A block with constant exponent is one conditional T_{t_c} operation over all its coordinates, by the tower property.

For the remaining error define the flattened effective value Y_b at boundary b, so that Y_d is the endpoint loss and Y_{b-1} is the conditional T_{t_c} average over the block (b, c]. Define E_b = E_base |Y_b - F_b(S_b)|, where F_b is the message functional of the recorded type. The terminal condition gives E_d = 0. Claim:

  E_b <= exp(2C) E_c + exp(2C) L_c M e_0,

where L_c <= exp(2C J) exp(beta) is the Lipschitz constant of the site-continuation from S_c onward.

For the proof of the claim, first replace Y_c inside the block average by F_c(S_c). The mean-error propagation bound gives the first term of the claim. For the second term, fix the ancestor histories through b and the continuation globals through c. Under this conditioning, the sites' continuations from S_b are independent, so a product of Wasserstein couplings and the Lipschitz constant of F_c gives

  |the integral of exp(t_c F_c) prod nu_i - the integral of exp(t_c F_c) prod S_b^i| <= t_c exp(C) L_c sum_i W_1(nu_i, S_b^i).

The second integral depends only on the ancestor histories. Average this inequality over the continuation globals, apply the exp(C)/t_c Lipschitz bound for the divided logarithm, then average over the ancestor histories. For almost every fixed global path, the site-law-distance condition bounds the integral over each occurrence's site ancestors by e_0. The continuation-global law is independent of the fresh site generators, including when the trial was obtained by a posterior change of law. Fubini's theorem therefore bounds the final average by exp(2C) L_c M e_0 and proves the claim.

There are at most J processed blocks, and L_c <= exp(2C J) exp(beta) at every boundary. Iterating the claim from E_d = 0 bounds the accumulated error. If the starting boundary is 1, propagating once through the remaining first-level transform adds at most one factor exp(2C). In either case the error is at most J M exp(beta) exp(2C(2J + 2)) e_0 = R.

For the root-terminal hierarchy the recursion already describes a site-only trial. Retain the common root z_0 and sample each occurrence's initial type S_0(z_0, u_0^i) using its own fresh root-site uniform. At each level of exponent t_c for each old block, draw each site's next type independently from its preceding type, and continue to the message pair. Probability kernels on these compact metric spaces can be sampled by measurable functions of fresh uniform variables. This construction evaluates the recursion and is site-only.

If the hierarchy is boundary-1-terminal, there remains the operation T_m F_1(S_1) with 0 < m < m_0. Conditional on the root data, factor this operation as T_m^{z_1} T_m^{sites} F_1(S_1). The inside quantity is in [-C, C]. By the small-exponent estimate, replacing the outside T_m^{z_1} by ordinary expectation changes the value by at most (1/2) C^2 exp(2C) m_0. Move z_1, with this ordinary sampling law, into the root global data. Retain the level-1 site transform and the independent block draws already constructed. The result is again site-only, and its kernels were constructed from the recorded types alone.

For counts greater than D_0, both old and new endpoint transforms are bounded in absolute value by log 2 + beta D regardless of kernels or exponents. Their contribution to the difference is at most twice the corresponding tail expectation. Summing the bounded-count estimates for the two endpoints proves the displayed inequality.

Finite rational completeness. For every a > 0 and finite beta > 0,

  P(a, beta) = inf_Q G_1(a, beta; Q),

where Q is the class of site-only trials of finite depth with strictly increasing rational exponents in (0, 1) and with message pair a rational-valued step function on a finite rational rectangular grid in (z_0, u_0, u_1, ..., u_d) in [0, 1]^(d+2). The class Q is countable and effectively enumerable.

Proof. The upper bound P(a, beta) <= G_1(Q) for every Q in the class is the soundness result. The reverse inequality follows by ordering all approximation choices.

Fix epsilon > 0. Set U = P + epsilon/4. The terminal trial proposition gives, for any rho > 0, a terminal trial with G_1 <= U + rho. The kernel collapse produces a site-only trial Q_0 with

  |G_1(Q_0) - G_1(Q_term)| <= 2 K(C) eta' + 2 R + C^2 exp(2C) m_0 + 2 tau_A + 2 tau_B.

By taking the terminal bandwidth eta', the count cutoff D_0, the site-law distance e_0, and the residual first exponent m_0 small enough, and the terminal depth J large enough, the right-hand side is at most epsilon/8. Setting rho = epsilon/8 gives G_1(Q_0) <= U + 2 * (epsilon/8) = P + 3 epsilon/8.

Exponent rationalization. Q_0 has weakly increasing exponents in (0, 1] of length at most 2 J + 3. Replace t_d = 1 by 1 - eps_t and each coincident pair t_j = t_{j+1} by t_{j+1} + eps_t; the vector remains nondecreasing and positive. Choose eps_t > 0 small enough that the sup-norm change is at most eps_t and K(C) eps_t <= epsilon/16, with K(C) the sensitivity constant at the packet bound. The sensitivity lemma then gives |G_1(Q_1) - G_1(Q_0)| <= epsilon/16. Replace each resulting exponent by a rational in (0, 1) within eps_r of its current value, choosing eps_r <= epsilon/(32 K(C)). By the sensitivity lemma the change of G_1 is at most epsilon/32. The resulting exponent list is strictly increasing with rational entries in (0, 1). Let Q_2 be the trial with these exponents and the original message pair. Then G_1(Q_2) <= P + 3 epsilon/8 + 3 epsilon/32 = P + 15 epsilon/32.

Message pair rationalization. The message pair of Q_2 is a measurable map from (z_0, u_0, u_1, ..., u_d) in [0, 1]^(d+2) to [0, 1]^2. The coordinate z_0 is common across sites; the remaining uniform coordinates are independent across sites and across levels. The pair has values in the compact convex set [0, 1]^2 and is measurable in the product sigma-algebra of the finitely many coordinates, so conditional expectations against the successive dyadic rectangular grids converge in L^1 to the message pair. Each conditional expectation takes values in [0, 1]^2 by convexity. Rational quantization of each coordinate on a finite rational grid of mesh eps_q gives a rational-valued step function on a finite rational rectangular grid within L^1 message-pair distance eps_q of the message pair. The shared root coordinate is retained as shared: conditional expectation does not introduce independence across sites.

The effect of replacing messages by step functions. Each clause factor has the form 1 - w I f_1 f_2 (A-packet) or 1 - w f_1 f_2 f_3 (B-packet), so its derivative in each message is w times a product of at most two other messages. The elementary inequality (log u - log u') <= (u - u')/u for positive u, together with factor values in [exp(-beta), 1], gives

  |log(1 - w f) - log(1 - w f')| <= w/(1 - w) |f - f'| = (exp(beta) - 1) |f - f'|.

The packet bound |X_A|, |X_B| <= log 2 + beta D together with independence of the fresh reservoir occurrences and the exponential-tilt Lipschitz estimate for T_t gives

  |G_1(Q_3) - G_1(Q_2)| <= exp(2C) E[exp(beta) * (log 2 + beta D) * 3] eps_q.

Choose eps_q > 0 small enough that the right-hand side is at most epsilon/8. The resulting Q_3 is a site-only trial with strictly increasing rational exponents in (0, 1) and a rational step-function message pair in [0, 1]^2 on a finite rational grid. So Q_3 is in Q, and G_1(Q_3) <= P + 15 epsilon/32 + epsilon/8 = P + 19 epsilon/32 < P + epsilon. Since epsilon is arbitrary, P = inf over Q of G_1.

Effective enumeration. Every member of Q is determined by a finite list of rational numbers (the exponent list) and a finite list of rational pairs on a finite rational grid. Rationals are represented by finite integer sequences; the exponents as a finite sequence of rationals; the step-function description is a finite list of rational pairs indexed by grid cells and a finite list of rational grid endpoints in [0, 1]. Validity is checked by rational comparisons: exponents strictly increasing in (0, 1), message values in [0, 1]^2, grid endpoints in [0, 1]. The class of finite integer sequences is countable and effectively enumerable. Therefore Q has an effective enumeration.

Upper certificate. For positive rational a, positive integer beta, and rational trial Q in the class Q, the inequality G_1(a, beta; Q) < 0 certifies alpha <= a. Every rational a > alpha has such a certificate. The class of certified (a, beta, Q) is effectively enumerable.

Proof. The trial bound from soundness gives P(a, beta) <= G_1(a, beta; Q). If a < alpha, the pressure exists, and the soft-signs corollary gives P(a, beta) >= 0. Thus a negative trial value is impossible below alpha and certifies the non-strict inequality alpha <= a. Nothing about the pressure at the boundary a = alpha is needed.

If a > alpha, the soft-signs corollary supplies a positive integer beta with P(a, beta) < 0. Finite rational completeness then says P(a, beta) = inf_Q G_1(a, beta; Q), so some rational trial has negative value. Its finite description is eventually enumerated, and the evaluation lemma eventually gives a rational interval for the trial's value whose upper endpoint is strictly negative. Enumerating positive rational a, positive integer beta, finite Q, and accuracy indices gives the asserted enumeration.

Finite-certificate search. Let alpha be in [0, 20]. Suppose (ell_k)_{k >= 1} is a computable sequence of rationals with ell_k <= alpha for every k and ell_k -> alpha. Suppose also that the class Q_alg of rational trials has an effective enumeration, and that for every positive rational a, positive integer beta, and Q in Q_alg, the real G(a, beta; Q) is uniformly computable. Assume:

  (S) G(a, beta; Q) < 0 implies alpha <= a;
  (C) for every rational a > alpha, some positive integer beta and some Q in Q_alg satisfy G(a, beta; Q) < 0.

Then one finite deterministic Turing machine, on input 1^r for any integer r >= 1, halts with a rational number q_r satisfying |q_r - alpha| <= 2^(-r). The machine takes no input other than the unary precision 1^r and uses no oracle, advice, or noncomputable real constant.

Proof. Uniform computability gives, from (a, beta, Q) and an accuracy index c, a rational closed interval I_c(a, beta, Q) containing G(a, beta; Q) whose width tends to zero as c -> infinity. If G(a, beta; Q) < 0, then I_c has a strictly negative upper endpoint for c large enough. This semidecides strict negativity without testing equality to zero.

On input 1^r the machine performs:

  (1) set [l, u] = [0, 20];
  (2) iterate an effective enumeration of positive rationals a, positive integers beta, and Q in Q_alg;
  (3) at stage s, run the enumerator for s steps from the start; for every triple (a, beta, Q) emitted so far, compute I_c(a, beta, Q) at all accuracy indices c <= s; compute ell_1, ..., ell_s. This is a finite list of terminating computations;
  (4) replace l by the maximum of the old value and the largest ell_k for k <= s;
  (5) whenever some I_c(a, beta, Q) with c <= s has a strictly negative upper endpoint, replace u by the minimum of the old value and a;
  (6) after each update, if u - l <= 2^(1 - r), output (u + l)/2 as a rational number, and halt.

Correctness. The variable l is at every stage of the form max over a finite set of ell_k, so l <= alpha. The variable u is either 20 or the density a of some certified triple with G(a, beta; Q) < 0, so u >= alpha by (S). The starting interval contains alpha. Thus l <= alpha <= u throughout, so the output (u + l)/2 lies in [l, u] and satisfies |output - alpha| <= (u - l)/2. When u - l <= 2^(1 - r), the output is within 2^(-r) of alpha.

Termination. Fix r. Set delta = 2^(-r). By ell_k -> alpha there exists K with ell_K >= alpha - delta. By (C) there exists a rational a_u in (alpha, alpha + delta), a positive integer beta, and Q in Q_alg with G(a_u, beta; Q) < 0. Uniform computability gives c such that I_c(a_u, beta, Q) has a strictly negative upper endpoint. The effective enumeration of Q_alg and of the rationals and integers eventually emits (a_u, beta, Q). At stage s = max(K, c, index of a_u), both updates have occurred and the current interval satisfies u <= a_u < alpha + delta and l >= ell_K >= alpha - delta, so u - l <= 2 delta = 2^(1 - r), and the machine halts.

The output is a finite rational number: it is the midpoint of two rationals in the current state, encoded as a numerator and a positive denominator with separators.

Proof of the main theorem. Let

  alpha = lim_{n -> infinity} f_n / n.

The lower-bound proposition proves that the limit exists and lies in [1/200, 10]. The pressure-limit proposition and the thinning argument then show that, for every fixed real a >= 0,

  P_n(a n) -> 1 for 0 <= a < alpha,
  P_n(a n) -> 0 for a > alpha,

in the relaxed Poisson model, and, after the thinning argument, for p(n, floor(a n)) in the proper-clause model. Set alpha_3 = alpha. This establishes the strict-side limits in the main theorem.

The same strict-side limits identify alpha_3 with the clause-size-three threshold of the September 2026 preprint on limiting thresholds for every fixed clause size. Two real constants with the same strict-side behaviour at every a >= 0 must coincide: at any density between them, one constant would predict a satisfiability probability tending to one while the other predicted it tending to zero, and the strict-side limits at that density cannot hold both ways.

The computability statement reduces to the two hypotheses of the finite-certificate search lemma.

Lower-sequence hypothesis. The one-sided lower bound alpha >= f_k/k - 2^67 k^(-1/6), together with the effective finite-size integration, produces a computable sequence (ell_k) of rationals with ell_k < alpha for every k and ell_k -> alpha.

Effective-enumeration hypothesis. The rational trials form an effectively enumerable class by the finite rational completeness theorem.

Uniform-computability hypothesis. For positive rational a, positive integer beta, and rational trial Q, the trial value G_1(a, beta; Q) is uniformly computable by the evaluation lemma.

Upper-certificate hypothesis (S). If G_1(a, beta; Q) < 0, then alpha <= a; the upper certificate lemma gives this directly.

Upper-certificate hypothesis (C). If a > alpha, then some positive integer beta and some rational trial Q satisfy G_1(a, beta; Q) < 0; the upper certificate lemma gives this too.

Then the search lemma supplies one finite deterministic Turing machine which, on input 1^r for any integer r >= 1, halts with a rational number q_r satisfying |q_r - alpha| <= 2^(-r). The machine uses no oracle, advice, or noncomputable real constant: every step is a rational computation, the ordering of approximations is explicit, and the only inputs to the search are the initial interval bounds 0 and 20 and the unary precision 1^r.

This completes the proof of the main theorem.

No efficiency bound is obtained; the running time of the machine is a total computable function of r, and the search itself does not attempt to decide clause equations for any specific density. The algorithm computes the limiting density; it does not attempt to solve an input formula, and it makes no claim about the satisfiability probability at a = alpha_3.

The same scheme identifies any fixed-clause-size limiting threshold as a computable real number, provided the corresponding one-sided finite-size bound has the same form. The three-literal approach is specific to k = 3, and the constants 2^67 and 2^53 enter through the deletion moment and the Doob concentration estimate.
