VARIABLE-DIMENSION WEISFEILER-LEMAN EQUIVALENCE
ON GENERAL AND SUB CUBIC GRAPHS

A complete, self-contained proof.

Version 2. Meant to be read from front to back.


ABSTRACT

Deciding whether two graphs are indistinguishable by joint-update
k-dimensional Weisfeiler-Leman refinement is EXPTIME-complete when the
two graphs are given by explicit adjacency matrices and the integer
k >= 2 is written in binary. The hardness holds even when the two graphs
are connected, simple, uncolored, of the same positive order, and of
maximum degree at most three.





1. WHAT IS PROVED, AND WHY IT IS SURPRISING.

1.1 The two problems.

Let X be a finite simple undirected graph, with vertex set V(X). Fix an
integer k >= 2. A k-tuple is an ordered list v = (v_1,...,v_k) of k
vertices of X.

Weisfeiler-Leman refinement colors the k-tuples and refines the coloring
in rounds. We use the joint-update convention.

Round 0. The initial color of v records, for every ordered pair (i,j) of
positions, whether v_i = v_j and whether v_i is adjacent to v_j.

Round s+1. For a tuple v and a vertex z, let v[i <- z] mean v with its
i-th entry replaced by z. The new color of v is the pair

    ( old color of v , the multiset of all vectors
      ( color_s(v[1<-z]), ..., color_s(v[k<-z]) ) as z ranges over V(X) ).

So all k replacements coming from one and the same vertex z stay grouped
together inside one vector, and the multiset ranges over vertices z, not
over positions i. That grouping is what "joint update" means.

Color names are shared between any two graphs under comparison. Write

    G =_k H

if for every round s the multisets of round-s colors on V(G)^k and on
V(H)^k are equal. This is k-dimensional Weisfeiler-Leman equivalence.

Two languages.

  WL is the set of triples (G,H,k) such that G and H are finite simple
  uncolored undirected graphs, given by explicit adjacency matrices,
  k >= 2 is given in binary, and G =_k H.

  SUBWL is the same, with the extra requirements that G and H are
  connected, have the same positive number of vertices, and have maximum
  degree at most three.

Inputs that violate the stated conditions are outside the languages.

1.2 The main theorem.

  THEOREM A. Both WL and SUBWL are EXPTIME-complete under deterministic
  polynomial-time many-one reductions.

"EXPTIME" is the class of languages decidable in time 2^(polynomial in
input length). "Complete" means: the language is in EXPTIME, and every
language in EXPTIME reduces to it by a polynomial-time map that preserves
yes/no answers.

Two remarks on the statement.

  The degree bound is an upper bound only. The constructed graphs of
  maximum degree three need not be regular.

  The theorem is about equivalence of a supplied pair at a supplied
  dimension. It is not about finding the least dimension that identifies
  one graph among all possible mates; a companion paper treats that
  different question.

2. REFINEMENT, THE COUNTING GAME, AND THE UPPER BOUND.

This section has two jobs. It pins down the exact refinement rule and
proves that deciding its outcome is in EXPTIME. Then it states and proves
the equivalence with a two-player game where Duplicator must announce a
whole bijection before Spoiler names a vertex. That game is the target of
every reduction in the rest of the paper.

2.1 The refinement rule, stated exactly.

  DEFINITION 2.1 (joint-update k-dimensional refinement).
  Let X be a finite simple graph and k >= 2. For v in V(X)^k let
  v[i <- z] be v with coordinate i replaced by z. The initial color
  chi_0(v) records, for every ordered pair (i,j), whether v_i = v_j and
  whether v_i v_j is an edge. The recursive color is

      chi_{s+1}(v) = ( chi_s(v),
                       { ( chi_s(v[1<-z]), ..., chi_s(v[k<-z]) ) :
                         z in V(X) } ),

  where the outer braces denote a multiset. Color names are common to
  all graphs under comparison. Write G =_k H if for every s >= 0 the
  multiset of chi_s values over V(G)^k equals the multiset of chi_s
  values over V(H)^k.

A warning that the definition makes the reader's life easy: the multiset
in the definition ranges over vertices z, and each element of the multiset
is a whole k-vector of old colors. If instead one took k separate
multisets, one per coordinate, one would get a different, weaker
relation. We never do that.

2.2 Capping the dimension.

  LEMMA 2.2 (capping). Let G and H have the same positive order n. For
  every integer k >= n with k >= 2, G =_k H if and only if G and H are
  isomorphic. Consequently, for n >= 2 and k >= 2,

      G =_k H   <=>   G =_{min(k,n)} H.

  Proof. If f: V(G) -> V(H) is an isomorphism, it acts coordinatewise on
  tuples. It preserves the initial color (it preserves equality and
  adjacency), and by induction on s it preserves every color chi_s. So it
  carries each color class bijectively onto a class with the same name,
  which makes the two histograms equal at every round. This gives one
  direction.

  Conversely assume k >= n and the round-0 histograms agree. Choose a
  tuple v in V(G)^k whose n distinct entries are all the vertices of G;
  this is possible because k >= n. The histogram equality gives a tuple w
  in V(H)^k with chi_0(w) = chi_0(v). The equality part of the initial
  color says v_i = v_j exactly when w_i = w_j. Hence the rule
  v_i -> w_i is well defined on the n distinct entries of v and is
  injective into the n entries of w; since both graphs have n vertices,
  it is a bijection onto all of V(H). The adjacency part of the initial
  color says v_i v_j is an edge exactly when w_i w_j is an edge. So the
  bijection is an isomorphism. For the displayed equivalence at k >= n,
  apply the same argument once at k and once at min(k,n); at k < n the
  two statements are literally the same. QED

The point of Lemma 2.2 is computational. It lets the decision procedure
replace an astronomically large binary k by min(k,n), with a comparison
that is polynomial in the input length even when the numeric value of k
is enormous.

2.3 Membership in EXPTIME.

  PROPOSITION 2.3 (upper bound). Both WL and SUBWL belong to EXPTIME.
  More precisely, after the input conditions are checked, an input of
  common order n >= 2 and total length L bits is decided in time

      2^(O(n log n)) * L^(O(1)),

  with absolute constants in the exponents.

  Proof. First check the input format. Read the two adjacency matrices,
  verify symmetry, zero diagonal, entries in {0,1}, and that the k field
  is a binary integer k >= 2. For SUBWL also check equal positive orders,
  maximum degree at most three, and connectedness. Reject any failure.
  For WL, unequal orders are rejected immediately because the two tuple
  tables have different sizes; orders zero or one give trivial graphs and
  are accepted.

  Now assume the common order is n >= 2. Replace k by min(k,n) using
  Lemma 2.2. This is a comparison of one binary integer with n, so it
  costs polynomial time in L regardless of the magnitude of k. From here
  on 2 <= k <= n.

  Keep two tables, one over V(G)^k and one over V(H)^k, and give all
  entries of both a common set of integer color labels. The total number
  of entries is

      A = 2 n^k.

  One round works as follows. At each entry v and each vertex z, form the
  vector of the k old colors chi_s(v[i<-z]) for i = 1,...,k; this vector
  belongs to that graph alone, we never mix the two graphs at this step.
  Sort the n resulting vectors with multiplicities by any fixed total
  order on color tuples, prepend the old label chi_s(v), and use the
  sorted result as the next signature. Sorting all signatures over both
  tables simultaneously assigns the next common labels.

  Every new partition refines the old one, because the old label is part
  of the signature. A strict refinement strictly increases the number of
  color classes, which is at most A. Hence after at most A rounds either
  no class splits, or the process has reached a fixed number of classes.
  If a round makes no split, its new labels are a bijective renaming of
  the old classes; substituting those names into the next signatures
  cannot create a split. So stabilization happens within A rounds,
  counting the final no-split round. Compare the two histograms initially
  and after every round, rejecting as soon as they differ. If they agree
  through stabilization, every later round is a renaming and they agree
  forever.

  Size accounting. An integer label uses O(1 + log A) bits, a replacement
  vector has k labels, and a signature has n replacement vectors plus an
  old label. Each round is a fixed polynomial in A + n + k + L, even with
  sequential access only. Since k <= n and n >= 2, multiplying by at most
  A = 2 n^k rounds gives

      (A + n + k + L)^(O(1)) = 2^(O(n log n)) * L^(O(1)).

  Finally the order n is bounded by a polynomial in L, because the input
  lists all matrix entries explicitly. So the displayed bound is a bound
  of the form 2^(poly(L)), which is EXPTIME. QED

2.4 The bijective game.

The game is played on a fixed pair of graphs G and H of the same order
n, with q >= 1 named pair slots. A slot is either vacant or occupied by
one pair (v,w) with v in V(G) and w in V(H). Initially every slot is
vacant. A move is one of the following two things.

  Spoiler may discard any set of occupied slots.

  Spoiler may name a vacant slot and ask for a new pair. Duplicator must
  first announce one bijection f: V(G) -> V(H) of the entire vertex sets.
  Only after hearing f does Spoiler choose a vertex v in V(G). The named
  slot then receives (v, f(v)).

The same vertex may appear in several slots, and so may the same pair.
Duplicator wins a play if after every move the occupied slots form a
partial isomorphism: for every two occupied slots (v,w) and (v',w'),
v = v' exactly when w = w', and v v' is an edge in G exactly when w w' is
an edge in H. Duplicator wins the game if it has a strategy that wins
against every finite play. Strategies may depend on the entire finite
history, and there is no bound on their computational cost.

The order of choices is the whole point: the whole bijection comes
before the individual vertex. A construction that only answered an
already chosen vertex would prove a strictly weaker statement.

2.5 Refinement and announced bijections are the same thing.

  LEMMA 2.4 (game characterization). For graphs G and H of the same
  positive order and every integer k >= 2, G =_k H if and only if
  Duplicator wins the game with k+1 slots.

  Proof. First, one symmetry of the colors. Claim: if two tuples have
  equal round-s colors, then applying the same coordinate permutation to
  both gives tuples of equal round-s colors. At s = 0 this is clear from
  the definition of the atomic data. For the induction step, equal
  next-round colors give equal old colors and a matching between
  replacement vertices whose entire old-color vectors agree. By the
  inductive hypothesis, applying the coordinate permutation to each
  replaced tuple preserves these equalities. Reordering the vector
  components by the same permutation then gives the replacement vectors
  of the permuted tuples, and their multisets agree. The statement is
  about equality of colors, not about individual color names being fixed
  by a permutation.

  Direction 1: from equivalence to a winning strategy.

  Assume G =_k H. On the union of the two tuple tables let ~ mean
  equality in a stable common refinement partition. Stability means the
  labels no longer split, so for every pair of related tuples there is a
  bijection of replacement vertices matching the whole replacement-color
  vectors. That is: for v ~ w there is a bijection f: V(G) -> V(H) with

      (2.1)  v[i <- z] ~ w[i <- f(z)]   for all z in V(G) and all
             i = 1,...,k, simultaneously.

  Indeed the stable-color vectors in the two multisets can be paired with
  their multiplicities, and such a pairing matches replacement vertices
  bijectively.

  Because the two stable histograms agree and are nonempty, some related
  pair v, w exists. Append to it any vertex and its image under a
  bijection f satisfying (2.1). The resulting pair of (k+1)-tuples has
  this property: deleting any one coordinate gives related k-tuples. For
  the deletion of the new entry it is the original pair; for the deletion
  of an old entry it is one of the replacements in (2.1), up to the
  common coordinate permutation covered by the symmetry claim above.

  Duplicator keeps one such pair of (k+1)-tuples as auxiliary data,
  arranged so that the occupied slots equal the actual marked pairs and
  the vacant slots are virtual. Discarding a slot leaves the auxiliary
  data unchanged. When slot j is named, delete its virtual entries; the
  remaining coordinates are a related pair of k-tuples, and Duplicator
  announces the bijection from (2.1) for that related pair. Spoiler then
  chooses v, and slot j receives (v, f(v)). The projection omitting j is
  unchanged. A projection omitting any other slot is, up to a common
  coordinate permutation, one of the replacements in (2.1). Hence the
  auxiliary invariant survives every move. Every two occupied
  coordinates occur together in some k-projection because k >= 2, so
  their equality and adjacency agree in the two graphs. This is exactly
  the partial-isomorphism condition, and this strategy wins.

  Direction 2: from a winning strategy to equivalence.

  Fix a winning strategy for Duplicator. We prove by induction on s that
  at every reached history with exactly k occupied slots, the two tuples
  read in any common ordering of the occupied slots have equal round-s
  colors. At s = 0 this is the partial-isomorphism condition. Suppose it
  holds at round s, and consider such a history with tuples v, w. Name
  the one vacant slot and let f be the announced bijection. For every
  choice of z in V(G) and every i, there is a continuation that first
  places (z, f(z)) and then discards the old i-th slot and reorders the
  remaining k slots to put the new slot in position i. The inductive
  hypothesis applies to that reached history and gives

      chi_s( v[i <- z] ) = chi_s( w[i <- f(z)] ).

  The bijection f was announced before z was chosen and is the same for
  all choices, so it matches the entire replacement vectors; the old
  tuple colors agree by induction. By the recursion, the round-(s+1)
  colors agree. This proves the induction.

  Finally build a map F: V(G)^k -> V(H)^k as follows. From the empty
  history, fill k fixed slots in a fixed order, never discarding, always
  following the strategy. This defines F. It is bijective: given any
  target tuple, invert the first announced bijection to recover the
  first source entry; the recovered history determines the second
  announced bijection, which recovers the second entry; continue. This
  inversion remains valid even though the strategy depends on history.
  The induction just proved says that F preserves round-s colors for
  every s. Hence the two histograms agree at every round. QED

2.6 The reached-history principle.

We will repeatedly need a small piece of logical hygiene about winning
strategies at a fixed history. It is the following observation.

  Suppose a winning strategy for Duplicator is fixed, and a history h has
  been reached. Let P be a property of the marked pairs at h. If failure
  of P would allow Spoiler, starting from h, to reach a lost position
  after discarding any chosen occupied slots and making some further
  moves, then P already holds at h.

Why: a winning strategy must avoid every lost continuation, from every
reached history. So if P failed at h, the strategy could not win. The
statement does not ask us to actually play that continuation during the
intended line of play, and it does not require restoring any discarded
marks. We will use this pattern constantly: to test a property of a
marked pair, we imagine a continuation that discards all unrelated pairs,
retains the pair, and performs the test. The conclusion is a fact about
the original history, whatever the occupancy there.


3. A COMPUTATION WRITTEN AS A SMALL CIRCUIT.

This is Link 1. We take an arbitrary halting computation of a fixed
deterministic machine and describe the whole computation by polynomially
many rules, called a product description. It is a circuit whose gates live
at binary addresses; there is one gate of each type per address, but the
wiring is compressed. The circuit succeeds or fails on a single input
depending on whether the machine accepts.

3.1 Circuit conventions.

A circuit is built from two kinds of gates and no others.

  A SCALAR gate has one output. Its value is the OR of the values
  arriving along all incoming connections. It may additionally be
  declared a seed, in which case it is true no matter what arrives. An
  empty OR is false. So a scalar gate is true exactly when it is a seed
  or at least one of its incoming sources is true.

  An AND gate has two input ports, called port 1 and port 2. Each port
  computes the OR of the values arriving at it. The gate is true when
  both port ORs are true.

A designated TEST is any scalar gate with no outgoing connections. The
circuit is finite and acyclic, so for a fixed input the rules determine
all truth values uniquely: process the gates in any topological order.

3.2 What a product description of a circuit is.

A PRODUCT DESCRIPTION has an address length r, and contains gates of a
few fixed types at each of the 2^r addresses. A specific circuit is then
obtained by expanding every schema into individual connections.

  A GATE TYPE is one of the types just described (one scalar type per
  tape letter for the circuits below, plus two AND types per triple of
  letters). Each type has one gate at each binary address in {0,1}^r.

  A CONNECTION SCHEMA specifies: a source type, a destination type, a
  destination port (irrelevant for scalar destinations), and for every
  coordinate i = 1,...,r a nonempty coordinate domain A_i subset {0,1}
  and an injective coordinate map

      sigma_i : A_i -> {0,1}.

  The schema describes the following connections. The source address
  ranges over the product

      A = A_1 x A_2 x ... x A_r,

  and the connection carries the source value at address a = (a_1,...,a_r)
  to the destination at the componentwise image

      sigma(a) = ( sigma_1(a_1), ..., sigma_r(a_r) ),

  at the specified destination port.

  A SEED REQUEST or TEST REQUEST specifies a scalar type together with a
  product domain of addresses; every address in that domain receives a
  seed, or becomes a test.

A product description lists only schemas and requests, each by a small
table of coordinate entries. It does not list the 2^r individual
connections and gates.

3.3 The machine and parameters.

Let M be a fixed deterministic one-tape machine whose tape is doubly
infinite. Its tape alphabet is finite and fixed. Head displacements are
in {-1,0,1}. On a word w of length m the head starts at position 0, the
word w occupies positions 0,...,m-1, and every other cell is blank.
Halting configurations are frozen: once M halts, nothing changes.

Let p be a fixed integer polynomial with p(m) >= m + 2 and such that
M halts on every length-m input within T = 2^{p(m)} steps. Put

    d = p(m) + 2,   H = 2^d = 4T,   r = 2d,   K = r + 1.

An address is a pair (t,j) of d-bit nonnegative integers t,j with
0 <= t,j < H. The first group of bits is time; the second is tape
position. Time is not cyclic; tape position is read modulo H. Since
p(m) >= 2 we have r >= 8.

  PROPOSITION 3.1 (uniform product-circuit compilation). Fix M and p
  as above. For every word w of length m one can generate a product
  description of a finite acyclic circuit at address length r = 2(p(m)+2),
  with scalar terminal tests, such that at least one test is true if and
  only if M accepts w.

  The description has O_M(1) gate types and O_M((r+1)^2 + m) connection
  schemas and seed or test requests. Each schema has r constant-size
  coordinate entries. All coordinate domains are nonempty binary sets
  and all coordinate maps are injections. The total number of coordinate
  entries is O_M( r ((r+1)^2 + m) ). The description is generated
  deterministically in time polynomial in r + m, with constants depending
  only on M and p, and no loop is ever run over all 2^r addresses or all
  H times.

  Proof. There are three parts: simulate the machine on a ring, build the
  gates that compute one ring step, and encode time and position shifts by
  polynomially many product cases. Then a short count.

  PART A. THE RING SIMULATION.

  Let Gamma be the fixed tape alphabet. Let Sigma be its plain letters
  together with tagged copies carrying a head state of M; there is an
  additional fixed tag for the "halted" state. If M halts, it stays put,
  so every halting configuration is frozen.

  There is a fixed map

      F : Sigma x Sigma x Sigma -> Sigma

  that updates the centre cell of every ring configuration that has
  exactly one head-tagged cell. Concretely, scanning a length-3 window
  (a,b,c) with b the centre:

    - if b is head-tagged, its state and scanned symbol determine the
      symbol written in the centre and whether the head remains;

    - if the head tag is on a (the left neighbour), a's tag determines
      whether the head moves right into the centre and, if so, its new
      state; the tape symbol in the centre is b;

    - if the head tag is on c (the right neighbour), the symmetric
      rule applies;

    - if there is no head tag in the window, the centre is unchanged.

  F is defined arbitrarily on windows that cannot arise from a one-head
  configuration; those windows never appear in the simulation.

  The same rule F implements one M-step on a cyclic tape of length H,
  not just on the line. Start with exactly one head. The head writes a
  symbol and moves by the prescribed displacement, so the ring still has
  exactly one head. A window that straddles the seam of the ring is a
  legitimate local case, not an arbitrary entry of F.

  Initialization of the ring: put w in positions 0,...,m-1, the head at
  position 0, and blanks elsewhere; when m = 0 the head sits on a blank.

  Why the ring computes the same thing as the machine for H-1 steps.
  Positions stay in [-T,T] during T steps because m <= T. The reduction
  modulo H is injective on [-T,T], since that interval has length 2T and
  H = 4T > 2T. So every visited position of the machine corresponds to a
  distinct ring cell. By induction on time, each scanned ring cell has
  received exactly the same writes as its unique line counterpart. The
  next transition therefore writes the same symbol and moves to the same
  corresponding cell. A cell whose 3-window meets the head lies in the
  halo [-T-2,T+2]; that halo has 2T+5 cells, which are distinct modulo
  H = 4T because 2T + 5 <= 4T for T >= 3 (here T >= 4). Other cells have
  no head in their window and are unchanged. Hence ring and line agree
  for H-1 steps, and the ring freezes at halting. The configuration at
  time H-1 therefore records the outcome of M.

  PART B. THE GATES.

  For each letter f in Sigma introduce one scalar gate type S_f.

  Seed the gate S_f(0,j) precisely for the initial letter at position j,
  head tag included. There are only m' = max(1,m) such positions.

  For each triple (a,b,c) in Sigma^3 introduce two AND gate types U_{abc}
  and V_{abc}. For t > 0 use these connections:

    (3.1)  S_a(t-1, j-1)  -->  U_{abc}(t,j),  port 1,
    (3.2)  S_b(t-1, j)    -->  U_{abc}(t,j),  port 2,
    (3.3)  S_c(t-1, j+1)  -->  V_{abc}(t,j),  port 2.

  At every time, including time 0, use these connections:

    (3.4)  U_{abc}(t,j)  -->  V_{abc}(t,j),          port 1,
    (3.5)  V_{abc}(t,j)  -->  S_{F(a,b,c)}(t,j).

  All position indices in (3.1) through (3.5) are read modulo H.

  Acyclicity. Assign rank 3t to U-gates at time t, 3t+1 to V-gates at
  time t, and 3t+2 to S-gates at time t. Every connection strictly
  increases rank. So the circuit has no directed cycle.

  Correctness. At time 0 the U-gates have no incoming connections from
  t-1, and the V-gates only see the same-time connection from U, which
  is false. Exactly the intended scalar seeds are true. Suppose the true
  scalars at time t-1 specify the actual letter at every cell of the
  ring. At (t,j), the AND gate U_{abc} is true exactly when a and b are
  the actual letters at j-1 and j; the AND gate V_{abc} is true exactly
  when c is also the actual letter at j+1. Exactly one triple of actual
  letters makes V true there, so exactly the scalar for the next letter
  appears at S(t,j). Induction proves that the scalar gates record the
  ring computation at every time.

  TESTS. For each letter f carrying an accepting state, test every gate
  S_f(H-1,j). Some test is true exactly when M accepts w. These gates
  have no outgoing connections: a scalar's outgoing connections advance
  time, and none starts at time H-1. The same-time connections (3.4) do
  not leave scalar gates. So the tests are terminal.

  PART C. CARRY AND BORROW TABLES.

  Number bits within either d-bit group from 0 to d-1, least significant
  first. For 0 <= h < d define two product domains:

    I_h^+ = { x in {0,1}^d : x_i = 1 for i < h, and x_h = 0 },
    I_h^- = { x in {0,1}^d : x_i = 0 for i < h, and x_h = 1 }.

  Bits above h are unrestricted. On either domain use the componentwise
  map

      theta_{h,i}(x_i) = 1 - x_i for i <= h,  and  x_i for i > h.

  Its restriction to each coordinate domain is injective.

  The domains I_h^+, for h = 0,...,d-1, partition all numbers strictly
  below H-1, and theta_{h,i} on I_h^+ implements addition of one. So
  these d domains implement time increment without overflow.

  For cyclic position increment, add the all-ones singleton and flip all
  bits on that additional domain. For cyclic position decrement, the
  domains I_h^- partition the nonzero numbers, and theta on I_h^-
  subtracts one; add the all-zero singleton, flipping all bits, for the
  wraparound case. Each cyclic shift thus uses d + 1 product cases.

  In source coordinates the temporal connections (3.1) to (3.3) are the
  maps

      (t,j) -> (t+1, j+1),  (t,j) -> (t+1, j),  (t,j) -> (t+1, j-1).

  Combine an I_h^+ case on the time bits with a position case on the
  position bits. The two digit groups are disjoint, so the combination is
  again a product domain. The two shifted kinds need d(d+1) schemas each;
  the unshifted kind needs d. There is no all-ones time case, because the
  time group is not cyclic. Each connection in (3.4) uses one identity
  schema, likewise (3.5).

  PART D. INITIAL AND FINAL REQUESTS.

  Put m' = max(1,m), so m' < H. At time 0, seed one singleton position
  request per 0 <= j < m', using the actual initial letter. When m = 0
  this seeds the head-bearing blank at position 0. Every other initial
  position carries the plain blank.

  Write the d bits of m' as mu_0, ..., mu_{d-1}. The set of positions
  j >= m' decomposes disjointly as the singleton {m'} together with, for
  every h with mu_h = 0, the domain

      j_i = mu_i for i > h,  j_h = 1,  j_i in {0,1} for i < h.

  Indeed, for j > m', the largest bit where j and m' differ has exactly
  this shape. Fixing all time bits to zero makes each such blank request a
  product in all r coordinates. There are at most d + 1 blank domains and
  at most m' + d + 1 seed requests in total. Each accepting letter needs
  one test request, fixing every time bit to one and leaving all position
  bits free.

  PART E. COUNTING AND GENERATION TIME.

  Write q = |Sigma|. There are q + 2 q^3 gate types. The connection
  schemas number

      q^3 ( 2d(d+1) + d + 2 )  =  q^3 ( 2 d^2 + 3 d + 2 ).

  The seed and test requests add O_M(d + m + 1) domains. Their coordinate
  tables have O_M( r ((r+1)^2 + m) ) constant-size entries.

  To generate them, loop over the fixed alphabet, over the carry or
  borrow index h, over the m' initial positions, and over the bit
  positions of m' that equal zero. Each coordinate entry is filled by an
  identity rule, a flip rule, or a fixed-bit rule. All loop lengths are
  polynomial in r + m, and all counters and small position numbers use
  O_M(log(r + m + 2)) bits. No loop is ever run through H times or 2^r
  addresses. QED

The polarity corollary that turns Proposition 3.1 into a reduction from
an arbitrary EXPTIME language is stated and proved as Corollary 4.3 at
the end of Section 4, after the scalar-image machinery is in place.


4. CONSISTENCY OF SCALAR IMAGES.

This is Link 2. We replace a circuit by a constraint system on nonempty
sets of small vectors. The system is solvable exactly when every test is
false. The point of the strange-looking "image of a scalar form" phrasing
is that the constraints are local to each comparison: they do not ask for
one globally satisfying vector, only that each comparison can be witnessed
somewhere in each direction.

4.1 The constraint system.

Throughout this section all vector spaces are over F = GF(2), so the only
scalars are 0 and 1. For a nonempty set Q of vectors and a scalar affine
form phi (a map phi(x) = lambda(x) + c with lambda linear and c in F),
write phi(Q) for the image set { phi(u) : u in Q }; it is a nonempty
subset of F.

  DEFINITION 4.1 (product consistency instance). Fix an address length r.
  The instance has a finite nonempty set P of REAL TYPES. Each real type
  P has a value dimension d_P in {1,2}. For every P in P and every
  address a in {0,1}^r there is one VARIABLE with value space F^{d_P}.

  A SCHEMA e in E consists of:
    - ordered endpoint types P and R (they may coincide),
    - a product domain A_e = A_{e,1} x ... x A_{e,r}, with each A_{e,i} a
      nonempty subset of {0,1},
    - coordinate injections sigma_{e,i} : A_{e,i} -> {0,1},
    - a scalar affine form phi_{e,0} on F^{d_P} and a scalar affine form
      phi_{e,1} on F^{d_R}.

  Write sigma_e for the componentwise address map. The instance is
  CONSISTENT if there exist nonempty sets Q_{P,a} subset of F^{d_P}, for
  every real type P and every address a, such that for every schema e and
  every a in A_e,

      (4.1)   phi_{e,0}( Q_{P,a} )  =  phi_{e,1}( Q_{R, sigma_e(a)} ).

  Each equality compares one scalar image at each end. Several
  comparisons are never combined into a joint image condition.

The linear part of an affine form may be zero, so constant forms are
allowed. Parallel schemas between the same pair of types are allowed,
and a schema may have P = R.

4.2 A circuit becomes a consistency instance.

The first construction is on the fully expanded finite circuit, before
compression.

  Given a finite acyclic circuit with the conventions of Section 3,
  build an instance as follows.

  SCALAR GATES. A scalar gate owns one scalar variable, so one real type
  of dimension 1. Its input direction and its output direction are both
  the identity form on F.

  AND GATES. An AND gate owns a variable (x1,x2) in F^2, so one real type
  of dimension 2. Its two input directions are the projection forms
  s_1(x1,x2) = x1 and s_2(x1,x2) = x2. Its output direction is the sum
  form o(x1,x2) = x1 + x2.

  A DUMMY scalar variable is introduced once, with its own scalar real
  type.

  SEEDS and TESTS. For each seed at a scalar gate, compare the scalar's
  identity direction with the constant-zero form on the dummy. For each
  test at a scalar gate, compare the scalar's identity direction with the
  constant-one form on the dummy. The dummy variable may take any
  nonempty set of values, because its two constant forms have images {0}
  and {1} regardless of that set.

  CONNECTIONS. For each individual connection, let o be the source's
  output direction (identity for a scalar source, x1 + x2 for an AND
  source) and let s be the destination's input direction (identity for a
  scalar destination, the appropriate coordinate projection for an AND
  destination). Create a PRIVATE BUFFER variable (z,z') in F^2, one
  buffer per individual connection, and impose these three separate image
  equalities, all over that connection's endpoints:

      (4.2)   o = z,    o = z',    z + z' = s.

  Here z and z' are the two coordinate forms on the buffer, and o and s
  act on the source and destination variables respectively. Notice the
  three comparisons are separate: the first two do not require the same
  source value to equal both buffer coordinates at once.

Before proving the expanded construction's correctness, here is the point
of the buffer.

  If a source output image is {0}, then (4.2) forces every buffer vector
  to have both coordinates zero, and since the buffer set is nonempty, its
  set is exactly {(0,0)}; the third comparison then forces the
  destination direction image to be {0}. So a zero output propagates
  forward.

  If a source output image is the full F, the buffer can be chosen to
  have full coordinate images on both coordinates while still realising
  any prescribed nonempty destination image; the symmetric support runs
  backwards. This is what makes the condition "per comparison" rather
  than a global assignment.

  PROPOSITION 4.2 (circuit consistency). For every finite acyclic circuit
  with the conventions of Section 3, the construction above gives nonempty
  scalar-image constraints with vector dimensions at most two that are
  consistent if and only if every test is false.

  More quantitatively, if the circuit has a product description with g
  gate types, c connection schemas, and b seed and test requests in
  total, the result is an instance with g + c + 1 real types and 3c + b
  comparison schemas, at the same address length. Its coordinate domains
  and maps are copied from that description, or are identities. In
  particular, the circuit of Proposition 3.1 gives O_M((r+1)^2 + m) real
  types and comparison schemas, with dimensions in {1,2}.

  Proof. First we handle the expanded circuit, then the product
  representation.

  DIRECTION 1: A TRUE TEST PREVENTS CONSISTENCY.

  Suppose a family of nonempty sets Q satisfies all comparisons. We show
  that every true gate has output image {0}, by induction in any
  topological order of the circuit.

  A true scalar gate is either a seed or has a true predecessor. If it is
  a seed, the seed comparison equates its own output image with the
  constant-zero image {0}, so its output image is {0}. If it is not a
  seed, it has a true incoming source, whose output image is {0} by the
  induction hypothesis; the first two comparisons in (4.2) force the
  buffer set to {(0,0)}, and the third forces the gate's own input
  direction image to be {0}; since the gate is scalar, its output image
  is also {0}.

  At a true AND gate, each of the two input ports has at least one true
  incoming source, so each port's image is {0}. The port image is the
  image of a coordinate projection over the AND gate's own variable set,
  so every vector in that set has both coordinates zero; the AND set is
  exactly {(0,0)}, and the sum output image is {0}.

  Hence every true gate has output image {0} under every consistent
  family. But a true test has the constant-one comparison, which forces
  its output image to be {1}. It cannot be both {0} and {1}. So no
  consistent family exists. This proves: if some test is true, then no
  consistency.

  DIRECTION 2: A CONSISTENT FAMILY WHEN ALL TESTS ARE FALSE.

  Assume every test is false, and build sets.

    - At each test's scalar gate, use the set {1}.
    - At every other scalar gate, use {0} if the gate is true, and F if
      the gate is false.
    - At each AND gate, use

        (4.3)   { (x1,x2) in F^2 : x_j = 0 for each true input port j }.

  All these sets are nonempty. At a true AND gate both ports are true,
  so the set is {(0,0)}; its sum output image is {0}. At a false AND gate
  at least one port is false, so at least one coordinate is unrestricted;
  as that coordinate ranges over F, the sum output takes both values, and
  the output image is F. Thus every true source has output image {0} and
  every false source has output image F, and the exceptional false test
  scalar is never a source (tests have no outgoing connections).

  Buffers. For a connection whose source output is true ({0}), the
  destination port is true, and the destination direction image is {0};
  choose the buffer set {(0,0)}, and all three comparisons in (4.2) hold.

  For a connection whose source output is false (F), let D subset F be
  the already chosen nonempty image of its destination direction. Use

      (4.4)   B_D = { (z,z') in F^2 : z + z' in D }.

  Then the sum image is exactly D, and each coordinate image over B_D is
  all of F: given any d in D, the pairs (u, u+d) as u runs over F already
  fill both coordinate images. So all three comparisons (4.2) hold: the
  first two because the source output image is F while the coordinate
  images of B_D are both F, and the third because z+z' has image D and
  the destination direction s has the same image D.

  The three cases for D are small enough to write in a table:

      D      B_D                       each coordinate image
      {0}    {(0,0),(1,1)}              F
      {1}    {(0,1),(1,0)}              F
      F      F^2                        F

  Hence all three comparisons hold in every case. In particular, a false
  source can drive a zero-forced port through the diagonal buffer, or a
  false test through the off-diagonal buffer, while keeping its own full
  output image. Different connections have their own private buffers and
  impose no coupling beyond these individual equalities.

  Seed and test comparisons hold by construction: seed gates have image
  {0} = constant-zero image, test gates have image {1} = constant-one
  image, with the dummy taken to be F.

  This builds a consistent family when every test is false.

  PRODUCT REPRESENTATION. Give each gate type its one- or
  two-dimensional real type, with variables at all addresses. Give each
  connection schema its own two-dimensional buffer type, indexed in the
  source address system. On its source product domain, the first two
  comparisons in (4.2) use identity address transmission from the source
  gate to its buffer; the third uses the schema's own componentwise map
  from buffer to destination. Hence an allowed buffer address corresponds
  to exactly the intended individual connection of that schema.
  Overlapping domains of distinct schemas use distinct buffer types, so
  they never interfere. Buffer variables outside their schema's domain
  are unused; they may take the full set F^2.

  Use one scalar dummy type with variables at all addresses. Seed and
  test requests become identity-transmission comparisons on the same
  product domains, with the appropriate constant form at the dummy end.

  This gives exactly one real type per gate type, one per connection
  schema, and one dummy; and exactly three comparisons per connection
  schema plus one comparison per request. All coordinate domains stay
  nonempty and all transmitted maps are injective. QED

4.3 The polarity: from an EXPTIME language to a consistency instance.

Now stack Proposition 3.1 on top of Proposition 4.2 and choose the
machine to decide the complement of the source language. This gives the
clean reduction with the polarity we want.

  COROLLARY 4.3 (the polarity of the reduction). For every binary
  language L_0 in EXPTIME there are a fixed deterministic machine M and a
  fixed integer polynomial p, with p(m) >= m + 2, such that a word w of
  length m can be transformed in time polynomial in m into a product
  scalar-image instance at address length r = 2(p(m)+2) >= 8 that is
  consistent exactly when w belongs to L_0. The instance has
  O_M((r+1)^2 + m) real types and comparison schemas, with dimensions in
  {1,2}.

  Proof. Start with a decider for L_0 and swap its two halting outputs to
  obtain a decider for the complement. Turn it into a fixed one-tape
  machine with a polynomial time overhead: store all the tapes and head
  markers of a multitape machine on fixed tracks, and after every
  simulated step scan the used interval once to collect the scanned
  symbols and once to write the updates. After t simulated steps the used
  interval has length O_M(m + t + 1), so a fixed number of scans per step
  gives polynomial total overhead. Enlarge a fixed integer polynomial p
  to absorb that overhead and to ensure p(m) >= m + 2. Apply Proposition
  3.1 to this normalized complement decider; then apply Proposition 4.2.

  Consistency is equivalent to all tests being false, which for the
  complement decider is equivalent to M not accepting w, which is
  equivalent to w in L_0.

  The exact buffer counts (three comparisons per connection schema, one
  per request) give the stated number of real types and comparison
  schemas. Because r is polynomial in m for the fixed polynomial p, the
  generation time is polynomial in m as well. QED


5. AFFINE BLOCKS AND EXACT PROJECTION.

This is Link 3, the technical heart. We compress the exponentially many
addressed comparisons into polynomially many constant-size blocks. Each
block has a small vector space of legal "homogeneous" valuations and a
small affine family of "version-one" valuations. Translating the
homogeneous solutions by a vector (an offset) gives the version-one
solutions. Offsets on different blocks must agree on every shared readout.

The key proposition says: if the scalar-image instance is consistent, then
for every set U of at most K blocks there is a nonempty family H_U of
compatible offset lists, and restricting a family from U to a subset V
gives exactly H_V. In other words, a compatible choice on a small set can
always be extended when one more block is added. That is precisely what a
Duplicator strategy needs when it must announce a whole bijection before
Spoiler names a vertex.

Throughout this section fix a product scalar-image instance as in
Definition 4.1 at an address length r >= 3, and set K = r + 1. All
constructions here use the instance alone. The witness sets Q_{P,a} are
used only in proofs, never in the algorithms.

5.1 Subdivided comparison wires.

Write each affine endpoint form of a schema e: P -> R as

    phi_{e,j}(v) = lambda_{e,j}(v) + c_{e,j},   j in {0,1},

with lambda_{e,j} linear and c_{e,j} in F.

Replace e by its own oriented WIRE

    P = t_0,  t_1, ...,  t_{4K},  t_{4K+1} = R.

The 4K internal types are private to this wire and have value dimension
one. The endpoints are the original real types, with their dimensions
d_P, d_R in {1,2}. If P = R, the two endpoints are identified as one
type, but the internal types and all 4K+1 links remain distinct. In
particular every individual link has distinct endpoints. Write T for the
resulting set of all types, and d_t for the value dimension at t in T.

For each coordinate i in {1,...,r}, the allowed labels at a type are

    A_{t,i} = {0,1}                          if t is real,
    A_{t,i} = A_{e,i}                        if t is internal to the wire
                                             of e.

For a link l = tu, oriented toward the second endpoint of its wire, let

    M_{l,i}  subset of  A_{t,i} x A_{u,i}

be its coordinate matches. On every link except the last of the wire they
are (a,a) for a in A_{e,i}; on the last link they are
(a, sigma_{e,i}(a)) for a in A_{e,i}. Thus one full address a in A_e is
unchanged along the internal types, and is sent to sigma_e(a) at the
second real endpoint.

Associate to every link one scalar equation

    (5.1)   L_{l,t} h_t + L_{l,u} h_u = b_l,
            h_t in F^{d_t},  h_u in F^{d_u}.

Here the form at an internal type is the identity. On the first link of
the wire, the form at P is lambda_{e,0} and b_l = c_{e,0}; on the last
link, the form at R is lambda_{e,1} and b_l = c_{e,1}; on every other
link b_l = 0. Consequently the first equation says

    h_{t_1} = phi_{e,0}( h_P ),

the last equation says

    h_{t_{4K}} = phi_{e,1}( h_R ),

and the interior equations identify all the internal scalars. This
interpretation covers constant endpoint forms (where lambda is zero) and
loop wires (where P = R and the two endpoints keep their respective
forms).

5.2 Readouts, blocks, and local affine fibers.

Index boundaries cyclically by i in {1,...,r}, so that r+1 means 1, and
put

    delta_i = 1 if i = r,  and  0 otherwise.

The CORNERS are triples (i,t,a) with a in A_{t,i}. There are two kinds
of READOUT NAMES, viewed also as named undirected edges between corners.

    A HORIZONTAL readout x_{i,t,a,a'} joins (i,t,a) to (i+1,t,a'), for
    every a in A_{t,i} and a' in A_{t,i+1}. Its value lies in F^{d_t}.

    A VERTICAL readout y_{i,l,a,b}, for l = tu, joins (i,t,a) to (i,u,b)
    for every (a,b) in M_{l,i}. Its value lies in F.

"Horizontal" and "vertical" refer only to the auxiliary corner diagram.

The set of BLOCKS is written B. Each block has a set R(B) of readout
names, as follows.

    SITE BLOCKS. For each horizontal readout there is one site block
    whose only readout is that horizontal.

    SQUARE BLOCKS. For each row i, each link l = tu, and each pair of
    matches (a,b) in M_{l,i} and (a',b') in M_{l,i+1}, there is one square
    block with readouts

        x_t = x_{i,t,a,a'},   x_u = x_{i,u,b,b'},
        y_i = y_{i,l,a,b},    y_{i+1} = y_{i+1,l,a',b'}.

So each block has a small connected corner diagram: one edge for a site,
or the four sides of a square.

For eps in {0,1} let Z_B^eps be the set of LEGAL VALUATIONS of the
readouts of B. A site admits every valuation. A square admits exactly the
valuations satisfying

    (5.2)   L_{l,t} x_t + L_{l,u} x_u + y_i + y_{i+1}
                = eps * delta_i * b_l.

Readout values in (5.2) are local to a block. Agreement between different
blocks is an additional condition, imposed through offsets.

  LEMMA 5.3 (local affine fibers). For every block B, the set Z_B^0 is a
  vector space and Z_B^1 is a nonempty affine translate of it:

      Z_B^0 + xi = Z_B^1   for every xi in Z_B^1.

  The projection of either fiber to any one readout name is onto that
  readout's entire value space. Every valuation uses at most six bits.

  Proof. A site has 2^{d_t} <= 4 valuations in either version. A square at
  a link tu has 2^{d_t + d_u + 1} <= 16, because at least one link
  endpoint is internal (dimension 1).

  For a site the assertion is immediate.

  For a square, the equation (5.2) is one scalar linear equation with a
  possibly nonzero right-hand side. Its two verticals are distinct, each
  with coefficient one, so it is not the zero equation. The homogeneous
  solution set Z_B^0 is a vector space, and each nonempty fiber is a coset
  of it. Nonemptiness and the projection assertion follow from this: after
  fixing any one readout, at least one remaining readout is free and can
  be set to satisfy the equation. Explicitly, if the prescribed readout is
  a vertical, the other vertical can be solved for; if it is a horizontal,
  both verticals are free and one of them can be solved for. Both fibers
  have the same nonemptiness and fullness by translation.

  Bit count. The two horizontals contribute at most 2 + 2 = 4 bits; the
  two verticals contribute 2 more; total at most six bits. QED

5.3 The exact projection target.

  PROPOSITION 5.4 (compatible offsets with exact projection). Suppose the
  product scalar-image instance is consistent. Then there exist nonempty
  families H_U, one for every U subset of B with |U| <= K, of lists

      xi = ( xi_B : B in U ),   xi_B in Z_B^1,

  such that the offsets within a list agree on every shared readout and

      (5.3)   H_U |_V  =  H_V    whenever V subset of U.

  Here H_U |_V deletes the entries belonging to U minus V.

The rest of this section proves Proposition 5.4. The statement is exact
restriction equality, not just surjectivity in one direction: every list
in the smaller family extends to a list in every permitted larger family
and vice versa.

Before the proof it helps to see the mechanism on the simplest possible
case, a ring of sites.

  THE SITE-RING MODEL. Fix a type t and an allowed address a in the
  product of the A_{t,i}. The r site blocks with readouts

      x_i = x_{i,t,a_i,a_{i+1}},   i in {1,...,r} cyclic,

  form a cycle on the corners (i,t,a_i). Try to write the r readouts as

      x_i = M_i + M_{i+1} + delta_i h,   M_{r+1} = M_1,

  with vectors M_1,...,M_r and h in F^{d_t}. Every potential M_i occurs
  twice in the ring sum, and only delta_r is nonzero, so

      sum_{i=1}^{r} x_i = h.

  A complete ring therefore determines the shift h. Conversely, if this
  one equality holds, choose M_1 freely and solve successively around the
  cycle; all readouts are realised.

  After deleting one site, the retained edges form a path. For any new
  shift h', its potentials can be solved successively along that path
  while every retained x_i stays fixed; the missing edge removes the
  ring-sum constraint. Put g = h' + h. Adding g to all retained
  potentials from the missing edge onward preserves every retained x_i.

  The general proof must handle squares, partial address domains, and
  several corner components that merge when a block is added. The next
  lemmas generalise the cycle distinction:

    - a closed walk that crosses the row-r seam an odd number of times
      ("wrapping") needs at least r blocks; with exactly r blocks it
      determines one full address;

    - on any component that does not wrap, a change of shifts can be
      absorbed by adjusting corner potentials without changing any
      retained readout.

That dichotomy, plus one existence statement for shifts, proves exact
projection.


5.4 Small supports and recovered addresses.

For U subset of B let Gamma_U be the union of the corner diagrams of the
blocks in U, with shared corners and shared named edges identified. A
block belongs wholly to one connected component of Gamma_U.

The SUPPORT of a component C of Gamma_U is the set of types and the set
of distinct wire links that appear in C: horizontals stay at one type,
while a vertical readout projects to its wire link. In particular several
square blocks on the same link do not create several support edges. Write
T(C) for the set of types in the support of C.

  LEMMA 5.5 (small supports). If |U| <= K, the support of every
  component of Gamma_U is a tree with at most one real type. Without a
  real type it lies in the interior of a single wire. With a real type it
  consists of that type together with possibly initial arms of wires
  incident with it.

  Proof. The support is connected because the corner component is
  connected. Only square blocks introduce support links, so it has at most
  |U| <= K distinct links. Internal wire types are private and have degree
  two in the full wire layout. A path connecting two distinct real types,
  or a cycle in that layout, must therefore contain a whole wire, which
  has 4K+1 > K links. Neither can occur inside the support. This also
  excludes a cycle created by a loop schema or by parallel schemas. The
  stated descriptions follow by following the private wire interiors from
  the unique real type, if one is present. QED

ALIGNMENT. For every coordinate of every oriented link, fix a permutation
pi_{l,i} of the ambient two-element set {0,1} that extends its matches.
Such a permutation exists because those matches are the graph of an
injection on a nonempty binary subset. Use the identity for identity
matches, including partial identities, and use pi_{l,i}^{-1} in the
reverse direction. These permutations are analytical choices; actual
vertical edges still use only actual matches.

In a support tree, fix a reference type. Unique paths transport the
ambient labels at each type to the reference type, separately at each
coordinate. These transported labels are the ALIGNED LABELS. Every
vertical edge preserves its aligned label. Changing the reference applies
the same coordinate permutation to all labels, and restricting to a
subtree gives the same alignment up to this change of reference.
Consequently an aligned binary address can be expressed at any type in
the support. Its expression at an internal type need not satisfy that
wire's full address domain unless a further argument supplies this fact.
Such an argument appears at the end of this subsection.

MARKERS AND WRAPPING. Assign a marker omega in F to each named edge: a
horizontal in row i has marker delta_i, and a vertical has marker zero. A
collection of blocks, or a corner component, is WRAPPING if its diagram
contains a closed walk whose marker sum is one. Traversals, including
repeated traversals, are counted in this sum.

  LEMMA 5.6 (wrapping and address recovery). Let |U| <= r+1.

  (1) Every wrapping subcollection of U has at least r blocks.

  (2) A wrapping subcollection with exactly r blocks has one block in each
      row, lies in a single corner component, and determines a unique full
      aligned address.

  (3) Any two wrapping r-block subcollections of U lie in the same corner
      component and determine the same address in that component's
      alignment.

  (4) If the support of a wrapping r-block subcollection has no real type,
      it lies in a single wire e, and its recovered address, expressed in
      that wire's internal labels, belongs to A_e.

  Proof. Take a marker-one closed walk. Let c_i in F be the parity of its
  horizontal traversals in row i. Count incidences of the walk at all
  corners on boundary i. The total incidence count at those corners is
  even (each traversal of a named edge has two endpoints, and both
  endpoints of a horizontal are on that boundary), and a vertical
  traversal contributes twice on the same boundary. So

      c_{i-1} + c_i = 0.

  Since the marker sum is c_r = 1, all the c_i equal one. Each row
  therefore contributes a horizontal edge to the walk, and every block has
  horizontals in just one row. This proves (1).

  With exactly r blocks there must be one block in every row. The witness
  walk meets all of them, so all lie in its corner component. Use the
  alignment of that component, or of any containing corner component in
  Gamma_U.

  The horizontals of the unique row-i block prescribe one aligned label at
  boundary i and one at boundary i+1: for a square the two horizontals
  have the same aligned labels because of its vertical matches.

  Now count incidences at boundary i separately for each aligned label.
  Vertical traversals contribute an even number of incidences to that
  label. The total horizontal contribution from each neighbouring row is
  odd, and it occurs at that row's prescribed label. The labels prescribed
  by rows i-1 and i must therefore agree. Their common value is the i-th
  coordinate of the recovered address. This proves existence and
  uniqueness, hence (2).

  Two r-element subcollections of a set of size at most r+1 share at least
  r-1 blocks. In the present setting these common blocks have distinct
  rows. In particular the two subcollections are in the same corner
  component. Every boundary is incident with a common row, since at most
  one row is missing from the overlap and r >= 3. Its address coordinate
  is consequently the same for both subcollections. This proves (3).

  Finally a support without a real type is inside one wire by Lemma 5.5.
  All its internal matches are identities. Every recovered coordinate is
  the label of a corner at an internal type, hence lies in A_{e,i}.
  Reading all coordinates gives an address in A_e. This proves (4). QED

Only wrapping subcollections of exactly r blocks will select an address
in the construction below. A component with r+1 blocks may wrap without
containing such a subcollection; in that case no address condition is
imposed on it.

5.5 Admissible shifts and corner potentials.

Assume now that the scalar-image instance is consistent, and fix nonempty
witness sets Q_{P,a} satisfying (4.1). For every wire e: P -> R and every
a in A_e define the nonempty scalar set

    (5.4)   D_{e,a}
              = phi_{e,0}( Q_{P,a} )
              = phi_{e,1}( Q_{R, sigma_e(a)} ).

These are individual image equalities, with no requirement on joint
images of different forms.

Fix U subset of B with |U| <= K, and a component C of Gamma_U. A SHIFT
SYSTEM on C is a vector (h_t : t in T(C)) with h_t in F^{d_t} satisfying
(5.1) on every support link. Its coordinates are indexed by the types of
this component: different corner components may use different shifts at
the same type.

A shift system is ADMISSIBLE if it also satisfies the following
condition, whenever C contains a wrapping r-block subcollection. By
Lemma 5.6, the recovered address is independent of that subcollection.

    If the support contains a real type P, express the recovered address
    there as a_P, and require h_P in Q_{P, a_P}.

    If the support has no real type, let e be its wire, express the
    recovered address in the internal labels as a, and require the common
    internal scalar to belong to D_{e,a}. The address lies in A_e by
    Lemma 5.6.

If C contains no wrapping r-block subcollection, there is no additional
condition. Write S(C) for the set of admissible shift systems.

  LEMMA 5.7 (existence of shifts). Every set S(C) just defined is
  nonempty.

  Proof. If the support has no real type, all its types lie in one wire
  interior. Its equations merely identify their scalar shifts. Choose any
  scalar, or any element of D_{e,a} when that condition is present.

  If the support has a real type P, choose any vector in F^{d_P}, or any
  vector of Q_{P,a_P} when required. The endpoint equation then determines
  the scalar along each incident arm, and the interior equations propagate
  it along that arm. The arms do not meet, so there is no further
  compatibility requirement. QED

Notice that when a real type is present we do not additionally impose an
image-set condition on every arm. Some arm may not admit the recovered
address in its full wire domain. Its shift is nevertheless well defined by
the endpoint equation, which is the only condition needed there.

Now choose independently an admissible shift system on each corner
component. At every corner v = (i,t,a) choose an arbitrary potential
M_v in F^{d_t}. Define values on its component's named edges by

    (5.5)   x_{vw} = M_v + M_w + delta_i h_t
                     for a horizontal in row i at type t,
            y_{vw} = L_{l,t} M_v + L_{l,u} M_w
                     for a vertical on l = tu.

Every square satisfies (5.2) with eps = 1. Indeed, each corner potential
occurs twice in its boundary expression and cancels, leaving

    delta_i ( L_{l,t} h_t + L_{l,u} h_u ) = delta_i b_l.

Sites have no equation. Since a named edge is assigned only one value,
the resulting offsets agree on all readouts shared between blocks.

Define H_U to be the set of all lists

    xi = ( xi_B : B in U ),   xi_B in Z_B^1,

obtained in this way from admissible shifts and arbitrary corner
potentials. The shifts exist by Lemma 5.7, so H_U is nonempty. For
U = empty, it consists of the empty list. There is no uniqueness claim
for representations by shifts and potentials; all permitted
representations contribute to the same family.

5.6 Restriction and extension.

Two lemmas isolate the roles of wrapping and nonwrapping components. The
first concerns the shifts themselves; the second permits shifts to change
while preserving the represented offsets.

  LEMMA 5.8 (lifting shifts from a wrapping component). Let V be a proper
  subset of U subset of B, with |U| <= r+1. If C_0 is a wrapping component
  of Gamma_V and C is its containing component in Gamma_U, then
  restriction of shift systems defines a surjection

      S(C) -> S(C_0).

  Proof. We have |V| <= r. By Lemma 5.6, C_0 uses exactly r blocks. Its
  recovered address is the one used for C, expressed in the respective
  coordinate systems. There are three cases.

  If the support of C_0 contains a real type P, it is also the unique real
  type of C. The admissible systems on either component are parameterized
  by the same vector h_P in Q_{P,a_P}; that vector determines the scalars
  on all of their arms. Restriction preserves this parameter and is onto.

  If the support of C has no real type, both components are inside the
  same wire e. Their admissible systems are parameterized by the same
  scalar in D_{e,a}, so restriction is again onto.

  In the remaining case C contains a real type P, but C_0 does not. The
  connected support of C_0 lies inside one arm at P, belonging to a wire
  e. Its recovered internal address a belongs to A_e. Therefore the
  ambient permutation transport from that address to the incident real
  endpoint agrees, on every coordinate, with the actual endpoint map: it
  is the identity if P is the first endpoint of e, and sigma_e if P is the
  second. Thus the map from the parameter for C to the parameter for C_0
  is respectively

      phi_{e,0}: Q_{P,a} -> D_{e,a},   or
      phi_{e,1}: Q_{P,sigma_e(a)} -> D_{e,a}.

  The applicable map is onto by (5.4). Once its preimage has been chosen,
  all the other arm scalars of C are determined and admissible. Only the
  incident endpoint of this arm is used, so the argument also covers a
  loop wire. Injectivity of the endpoint form is not required. QED

  LEMMA 5.9 (changing shifts on a nonwrapping component). Let C_0 be a
  nonwrapping corner component. Suppose two shift systems (h_t) and
  (h~_t) satisfy the link equations on its support. Every collection of
  offsets represented by the first system and corner potentials can also
  be represented by the second system after changing those potentials.

  Proof. Put g_t = h~_t + h_t. Adding the two copies of the link equation
  gives

      (5.6)   L_{l,t} g_t = L_{l,u} g_u
              on every support link l = tu.

  Since C_0 is nonwrapping, the marker sum is zero on every closed walk.
  Choose one corner as a root, and define s_v in F to be the marker sum
  along a path from the root to v. This is independent of the path:
  concatenating two such paths, one reversed, gives a closed walk, whose
  marker sum is zero, and reversing a path negates its marker sum, which
  is the same as itself over F. Consequently

      s_v + s_w = omega(vw)

  on every named edge.

  Replace the old potential at a corner of type t by
  M~_v = M_v + s_v g_t. On a horizontal in row i the change in (5.5) is

      (s_v + s_w) g_t + delta_i (h~_t + h_t)
        = delta_i g_t + delta_i g_t = 0.

  On a vertical, s_v = s_w, so the change is

      s_v L_{l,t} g_t + s_w L_{l,u} g_u
        = s_v ( L_{l,t} g_t + L_{l,u} g_u ) = 0

  by (5.6). Thus all old readout values are preserved. The argument allows
  vector shifts at a two-dimensional real type and does not require its
  endpoint forms to be nonzero. QED

  Proof of Proposition 5.4. Use the families defined by (5.5).
  Nonemptiness, legality, and agreement on shared readouts have already
  been proved. It remains to establish the projection equality. The case
  V = U is immediate, so suppose V is a proper subset of U. Then |V| <= r.
  Each component C_0 of Gamma_V lies in a component C of Gamma_U.

  First take a represented list in H_U and restrict its shifts and
  potentials to each C_0. These shifts satisfy all old link equations. If
  C_0 is nonwrapping, it has no additional address condition. If it wraps,
  its admissibility follows from Lemma 5.8. The restricted representation
  therefore belongs to H_V, proving H_U |_V subset of H_V.

  For the other inclusion, fix an arbitrary list in H_V, and choose any one
  of its representations by admissible old shifts and potentials. There is
  at most one wrapping old component: one would already require r of the
  at most r old blocks. For each component C of Gamma_U, choose an
  admissible new shift system. If C contains the wrapping old component
  C_0, choose it to extend the selected old shifts on C_0, using Lemma 5.8.
  On all other new components choose any admissible shifts.

  Keep the old potentials on a wrapping old component. On every
  nonwrapping old component, apply Lemma 5.9 to replace its old shifts by
  the restriction of the chosen new shifts without changing any old
  offset. Distinct old components have disjoint corner sets, so these
  potential changes coexist even when several old components merge into a
  single new component or have different old shifts at the same type.
  Assign arbitrary potentials to the additional corners of Gamma_U.

  The new shifts and these potentials represent a member of H_U. They
  satisfy every new square equation by (5.5), while on each old named edge
  the value is unchanged. Hence this member restricts to the prescribed
  list in H_V, proving the reverse inclusion. QED

Two features of the proof are worth isolating, because later arguments
use them.

    First, if a component first becomes wrapping only when all r+1 blocks
    are present, it has no wrapping proper subcollection and no address
    condition is needed: all the old components are covered by the gauge
    lemma.

    Second, a wrapping old component asks for at most one scalar preimage
    from a real-type witness set. Thus the individual image equalities in
    (5.4) suffice, even for non-affine witness sets or noninjective
    endpoint forms.

The families H_U, their shifts, and the witnesses Q_{P,a} are used only in
this existence argument. The reduction never enumerates them.


6. THE GENERAL GRAPH REALIZATION AND THE GAME TRANSFER.

This begins Link 4. We have compatible offsets on small sets of blocks.
Now we build a pair of graphs whose parts are indexed by blocks, so that
an offset on a block acts as a bijection of that part. Duplicator's
announced bijection is assembled block by block. The converse reading of
the game, which recovers the scalar images, is the "game transfer"
proposition of 6.3.

6.1 Valuation vertices and private leaves.

Fix the affine block data of Section 5, at an address length r >= 3. For
eps in {0,1} build a graph G^eps as follows.

    BASE VERTICES. One base vertex (B,z) for every block B in B and every
    valuation z in Z_B^eps.

    BASE ADJACENCY. Two base vertices (B,z) and (C,w) are adjacent exactly
    when all three of the following hold:

        (6.1)   B =/= C,
                R(B) intersect R(C) is nonempty,
                z(rho) = w(rho) for every rho in R(B) intersect R(C).

    There are no edges within a block. Agreement is required on every
    common readout; a disagreement on even one readout gives a nonedge.

Write J = |B|, and let N be the common number of base vertices over the
two versions. The base-vertex counts in the two versions agree by
Lemma 5.3. Number blocks by j(B) in {1,...,J} in the same order in both
versions. At each (B,z) attach j(B)(N+1) PRIVATE LEAVES, adjacent only to
that owner. Denote them leaf(B,z,nu), for 1 <= nu <= j(B)(N+1). Call the
resulting graphs G^0, G^1, and let V_B^eps contain all base vertices of
block B together with all their leaves. These parts partition the graph.

6.2 The general realization and its decoding.

  PROPOSITION 6.1 (general realization). For the affine block data at
  address length r >= 3, the graphs G^0, G^1 are simple, uncolored, and
  of the same positive order. They have O(J^3) vertices.

  Every offset xi_B in Z_B^1 determines a fixed bijection
  Psi_{B,xi_B}: V_B^0 -> V_B^1, depending only on B and xi_B, by

      (B,z)             |-->  (B, z + xi_B),
      leaf(B,z,nu)      |-->  leaf(B, z + xi_B, nu).

  For any set U of blocks, offsets agreeing on all shared readouts make
  the union of these maps an isomorphism of the induced subgraphs on the
  corresponding parts.

  At every history reached by a winning K = r+1-slot strategy, a marked
  base vertex (B,z) is answered by (B,z') with z' in Z_B^1. If zero base
  vertices of distinct blocks are simultaneously marked, their responses
  agree on every shared readout, even when all K slots are occupied.

  Proof. The adjacency rule and the owner rule introduce no loops and no
  multiple edges. Each block has nonempty legal fibers of equal
  cardinality, so the two graphs have equal positive orders. Each block
  has at most 64 valuations, a convenient loose constant, hence
  J <= N <= 64 J. The total order is at most

      N + N * J * (N + 1) = O(J^3).

  LOCAL MAPPING. Translation by a legal affine offset bijects Z_B^0 with
  Z_B^1. All owners in block B have the same number of leaves, so the
  displayed map is a bijection of the entire part.

  Within a base block the translation preserves equality, and there are
  no base edges inside a block to preserve. Between two distinct parts of
  an active set U the offsets agree on every shared readout; adding equal
  offsets preserves both agreement and disagreement on each name, hence
  the entire adjacency condition (6.1). Blocks with no common name stay
  nonadjacent.

  Cross-part edges. A leaf is adjacent only to its owner. For a base
  vertex in the owner's block the same translation is applied to the base
  and to the owner, whether or not the owner itself is marked, so their
  equality is preserved. A base vertex in a different block remains a
  nonowner. Distinct leaves stay distinct because owner translation is
  injective and the local index is unchanged. Repeated selections,
  different leaves at one owner, and leaves at different owners are
  therefore all handled. Leaf--leaf adjacency is absent, and no leaf is
  identified with a base vertex. This proves the induced-subgraph claim
  on any union of compatible parts.

  RECOGNITION. First observe that a marked pair reached under any winning
  strategy with at least two slots has equal degrees. After discarding
  every other pair, request another slot. The announced bijection must
  preserve adjacency to the retained pair for every possible choice of
  Spoiler's vertex, so it carries the one neighborhood bijectively onto
  the other. This is a property of the reached history; it does not need
  a spare slot at that history in the intended play.

  Every leaf has degree one. A base vertex in block number j has degree
  in

      [ j (N+1),  j (N+1) + N - 1 ].

  These intervals are disjoint, and their lower endpoints are greater than
  one. Thus every reached base response is a legal valuation in the same
  block. Distinct homogeneous zero vertices sharing a readout are
  adjacent, so their responses must satisfy (6.1) and agree on all shared
  names. QED

Proposition 6.1 supplies the two interfaces that the game needs:

    compatible offsets induce blockwise maps on all vertices, including
    the leaves;

    zero-valued queries decode to legal affine valuations, with
    shared-readout agreement.

The next subsection isolates these two interfaces as a general criterion
and proves that in any pair of graphs satisfying it, the game on one side
is equivalent to consistency of the scalar-image instance on the other.


6.3 The realization interface, and the game transfer.

We isolate exactly what the rest of the argument uses. Any pair of graphs
with the two properties below turns the game into a test of consistency of
the scalar-image instance, and conversely.

  PROPOSITION 6.2 (a realization interface for the game). Let a product
  scalar-image instance have address length r >= 3, put K = r+1, and form
  its affine block data. Suppose finite simple graphs W^0, W^1 of the same
  positive order have partitions

      V(W^eps) = disjoint union over B in B of V_B^eps,   eps in {0,1},

  with the following two properties.

  (1) LOCAL MAPS. For every block B and every offset xi_B in Z_B^1 there
  is a fixed bijection Psi_{B,xi_B}: V_B^0 -> V_B^1 depending only on that
  offset and the constructed graphs. Whenever offsets on a set of blocks
  agree on all shared readouts, the union of their maps is an isomorphism
  of the induced subgraphs on the corresponding parts.

  (2) ZERO DECODING. For every B, eps and z in Z_B^eps there is a nonempty
  set D_B^eps(z) subset of V_B^eps; for fixed B and eps these sets are
  pairwise disjoint. At every history reached under any winning K-slot
  strategy, a marked vertex in D_B^0(0) is answered by a vertex in
  D_B^1(zeta_B) for some legally determined zeta_B in Z_B^1. For
  simultaneous such marks in distinct blocks B and C, the decoded
  valuations zeta_B and zeta_C agree on every shared readout. Both
  assertions hold even when all K slots are occupied.

  Then

      the instance is consistent
        <=>  Duplicator wins on W^0, W^1 with K slots
        <=>  W^0 =_r W^1.

  For the general construction the query sets are simply
  D_B^eps(z) = { (B,z) }, and both hypotheses follow from Proposition
  6.1. The sets D_B^eps(z) are allowed to be bigger because the subcubic
  construction will use a whole triangle for each valuation.

  Proof. Lemma 2.4 gives the game/WL equivalence, because a K-slot game
  with K = r+1 slots tests dimension r and r >= 3. We prove the two
  directions that involve consistency.

  DIRECTION 1: WITNESSES GIVE A WINNING STRATEGY.

  Suppose the instance has witnesses, and take the nonempty families H_U
  supplied by Proposition 5.4 for every set U of blocks with |U| <= K.
  Recall that their restriction maps satisfy (5.3) and that every list in
  such a family agrees on all shared readouts.

  Call a block ACTIVE when its part V_B^0 contains a currently marked
  first-graph vertex. Duplicator maintains the following invariant: if U
  is the active set, then there is a list xi = (xi_B : B in U) in H_U
  such that every marked vertex v in V_B^0 is paired with
  Psi_{B,xi_B}(v). By the local-map hypothesis, the union of these maps
  is an isomorphism between the induced subgraphs on the active parts, so
  in particular the marked pairs form a partial isomorphism.

  Initially U is empty, and the invariant holds with the empty list.
  After any discards, restrict the list to the remaining active set; the
  restriction inclusion of (5.3) keeps it in the required family.

  Suppose a vacant slot is requested. There are at most K-1 = r occupied
  slots, so |U| <= r. For EACH inactive block B choose a list

      (6.2)   eta^B in H_{U union {B}},   eta^B |_U = xi.

  Such a list exists by the extension direction of exact projection
  (5.3). All these choices are made before Spoiler chooses a vertex; they
  may be made by a fixed total order on the finite sets of possible lists.
  Define a map f on the whole first graph by

      f |_{V_B^0} = Psi_{B, xi_B}       if B in U,
      f |_{V_B^0} = Psi_{B, eta^B_B}    if B not in U.

  Every piece is a bijection from V_B^0 onto V_B^1, and these parts
  partition the two vertex sets. Hence f is one bijection of the entire
  graphs, and Duplicator announces f.

  If Spoiler chooses a vertex in an active block, keep the current list.
  Otherwise the chosen vertex lies in V_B^0 for a single inactive block B,
  and Duplicator uses the particular list eta^B from (6.2) as the new
  invariant. It agrees with every old active offset and gives the
  announced image of the newly chosen vertex. No second inactive block
  becomes active during this move. Therefore compatibility between the
  choices made for two different inactive blocks is never needed: only one
  of those choices is ever used to update the invariant. Duplicator wins
  through every finite history.

  DIRECTION 2: A WINNING STRATEGY GIVES ATTAINED IMAGE SETS.

  Fix a winning strategy. For each block B choose once and for all a
  vertex z_B in D_B^0(0) representing its zero valuation; this set is
  nonempty because 0 is in Z_B^0. A ZERO-QUERY HISTORY is a finite history
  from the empty position under the fixed strategy in which every vertex
  chosen by Spoiler is one of these z_B; discards are unrestricted.

  By the zero-decoding hypothesis, a response to z_B lies in
  D_B^1(zeta_B) for a unique legal valuation zeta_B in Z_B^1. If zero
  representatives of distinct blocks are simultaneously marked, the
  response valuations agree on every shared readout, by the second
  realization hypothesis applied at the current history, even when all K
  slots are occupied. No further test has to be run during a zero-query
  history. Once a pair is marked, its actual response vertex and its
  decoded valuation stay fixed until that pair is discarded.

  For a type t and an allowed full address a in the product of the
  A_{t,i}, let S_i(t,a) be the site block with readout
  x_{i,t,a_i,a_{i+1}}, where indices are cyclic. These r blocks are
  distinct because they have distinct row indices. We call them the SITE
  RING at (t,a). At a zero-query history having precisely one
  representative of each of these sites marked, define its RING SUM to be

      (6.3)   h_t = sum over i = 1..r of
                    zeta_{S_i(t,a)}( x_{i,t,a_i,a_{i+1}} )
              in F^{d_t}.

  There are exactly r occupied slots at such a history, in any arrangement,
  and one slot is vacant.

  For a real type P and an address a in {0,1}^r, let H_{P,a} be the set of
  all finite zero-query histories from empty under the fixed strategy that
  end with precisely the site ring at (P,a) marked, one pair per site, in
  any arrangement of the slots. Define

      (6.4)   Q_{P,a}
                = { h_P(h) : h in H_{P,a} }  subset of  F^{d_P}.

  The definition ranges over ALL such histories under the one fixed
  strategy. Directly querying the r ring sites from the empty position
  shows that every Q_{P,a} is nonempty. We will verify the individual
  image equalities for these sets without requiring the strategy to give
  the same response when a ring is visited again.

  TRANSFERRING ONE RING ACROSS A LINK.

  Let l = tu be a link. Fix allowed addresses a at t and b at u with
  (a_i,b_i) in M_{l,i} for every i. For this paragraph write

      T_i = S_i(t,a),   U_i = S_i(u,b).

  Let Q_i be the square block in row i determined by the matches
  (a_i,b_i) and (a_{i+1},b_{i+1}). Its horizontal names are the readouts
  of T_i and U_i, and its vertical names are

      y_i = y_{i,l,a_i,b_i},   y_{i+1} = y_{i+1,l,a_{i+1},b_{i+1}}.

  The two endpoint types of a link are distinct, including on a subdivided
  loop schema. In each of these lists the row blocks are distinct, and the
  sites are distinct from the squares.

  Start at any zero-query history with precisely the T_i marked. Denote
  their decoded horizontal vectors by alpha_i, and fix the numerical value
  h_t = sum of alpha_i from this starting history. For i = 1,...,r place
  z_{Q_i} in the vacant slot BEFORE discarding the pair at T_i. During
  that placement there are r+1 = K occupied slots. Since Q_i and T_i are
  then simultaneously marked and share their t-horizontal, the new square's
  response has that horizontal equal to alpha_i.

  More explicitly, after processing rows 1,...,j the marked blocks are
  exactly

      Q_1, ..., Q_j, T_{j+1}, ..., T_r.

  For i <= j the marked square keeps alpha_i on its t-horizontal. For
  i > j the original site pair is still present and keeps alpha_i. This
  proves by induction that after the first phase all r squares are marked
  and that their old-side horizontals are the original vectors alpha_i,
  not newly selected ring values.

  At this all-square history, write beta_i for the response's
  u-horizontal in Q_i. The adjacent squares Q_{i-1} and Q_i share the
  name y_i, so their values on it agree; denote the common value by eta_i.
  The legal valuation equation (5.2) in version one reads

      L_{l,t} alpha_i + L_{l,u} beta_i + eta_i + eta_{i+1}
        = delta_i b_l.

  Summing over all rows cancels each vertical value twice in F. Since the
  sum of the delta_i is 1, this gives

      (6.5)   L_{l,t} h_t + L_{l,u} ( sum_i beta_i ) = b_l.

  Now replace each Q_i by U_i, again placing the site's zero
  representative BEFORE removing the square's pair. The shared
  u-horizontal copies beta_i into the new site's response. After
  processing rows 1,...,j the marked blocks are

      U_1, ..., U_j, Q_{j+1}, ..., Q_r;

  the new sites keep their copied beta_i and the remaining squares keep the
  values fixed at the all-square history. At the end precisely the u-ring
  is marked and its ring sum is h_u = sum_i beta_i. Hence the starting and
  ending ring sums satisfy

      (6.6)   L_{l,t} h_t + L_{l,u} h_u = b_l.

  Every replacement changes the occupied-slot count from r to r+1 and back
  to r. The whole transfer uses 2r placements and 2r discards, and every
  chosen vertex is still a fixed zero representative.

  The same construction works starting at the u-ring: first replace its
  sites by the same squares, then the squares by the t-sites. The same
  equation holds, with the starting value at u kept fixed. No response is
  required to recur after its pair has been discarded.

  TRANSFERRING ALONG A WHOLE COMPARISON WIRE.

  Fix a schema e: P -> R and an address a in A_e. Along its wire use the
  address a at P and at every internal type, and the address sigma_e(a) at
  R. These are allowed addresses, and their adjacent coordinates are
  actual matches at every link: all but the last link use the restricted
  identity, while the last uses (a_i, sigma_{e,i}(a_i)) at coordinate i.

  Take any h_P in Q_{P,a}, choose one witnessing history in (6.4), and
  continue it by the link transfers just proved. The final history is
  another finite zero-query history, now ending with exactly the ring at
  R, sigma_e(a). Its ring sum h_R therefore belongs to Q_{R,sigma_e(a)}.
  The successive ring sums satisfy (6.6). Interior link equations equate
  consecutive scalar shifts, while the two endpoint equations identify
  their common value with the endpoint affine forms. Consequently

      phi_{e,0}(h_P) = phi_{e,1}(h_R).

  This proves

      phi_{e,0}( Q_{P,a} ) is contained in
      phi_{e,1}( Q_{R, sigma_e(a)} ).

  For the opposite inclusion, start from any history witnessing an element
  of Q_{R,sigma_e(a)} and traverse the wire in reverse. At the last link,
  invert each sigma_{e,i} only on its image: the specified coordinate
  sigma_{e,i}(a_i) has the unique allowed preimage a_i. All remaining
  reverse matches are restricted identities. Thus the reverse transfer
  reaches the ring at P, a and proves the other inclusion. No
  surjectivity of a partial coordinate injection onto the ambient binary
  set has been used.

  If P = R, the two endpoint rings are still successive occurrences in one
  and the same history. Their decoded responses may differ, even if the
  two addresses are equal. Both attained values belong to the appropriate
  set in (6.4), which is all that the image equality requires. Likewise,
  separate schemas need no simultaneous choice of endpoint witnesses. We
  have proved (4.1) for every schema and every address in its domain,
  establishing consistency. QED

  COROLLARY 6.3 (correctness of the general realization). For every
  product scalar-image instance at address length r >= 3, its general
  realization satisfies

      the instance is consistent  <=>  G^0 =_r G^1.

  Proof. Apply Proposition 6.2 with the parts, maps and singleton query
  sets supplied by Proposition 6.1. QED


7. THE CONNECTED SUBCUBIC REALIZATION.

This is Link 5. We reuse the affine blocks of Section 5, but replace the
valuation graph and its private leaves by a different graph: connected, of
maximum degree three, and still with a block part for every block. Every
vertex of a block part must admit a local map depending only on that
block's offset.

The one new difficulty compared to Section 6 is recognition: the game
must be able to read the encoded data even at a history with all K slots
occupied. We handle this with the reached-history principle of 2.6:
hypothetical continuations that first discard unrelated pairs establish
invariants of the original history.

Throughout this section take r >= 8, so K = r + 1 >= 9. Write N = |B|.

Facts from Lemma 5.3 that we use freely:

    a block has at most four readout names, each of dimension 1 or 2;

    the total number a_B of scalar coordinates is at most six;

    Z_B^0 is a vector space and Z_B^1 = Z_B^0 + xi for every xi in Z_B^1,
    and both are nonempty;

    projection onto any one named readout is full;

    coordinates of a common name appear in the same prescribed order at
    every block that contains it.

For a valuation z and a name rho in R(B), write z|_rho for its value on
that readout.

  PROPOSITION 7.1 (subcubic realization). From the block data one can
  construct graphs X^0, X^1 with partitions

      V(X^eps) = disjoint union over B in B of V_B^eps,   eps in {0,1},

  having the following properties.

  (1) Both graphs are finite, simple, connected, uncolored, of maximum
  degree at most three, and of the same positive order. Their orders are
  O(N^4), and the construction is polynomial in the size of the
  explicitly listed block data.

  (2) For each z in Z_B^eps, the part V_B^eps contains a specified
  triangle T_B^eps(z); the triangles are pairwise disjoint. For every
  xi_B in Z_B^1 there is a fixed bijection
  Psi_{B,xi_B}: V_B^0 -> V_B^1, depending only on B, xi_B and the
  constructed graphs, and it sends T_B^0(z) onto T_B^1(z + xi_B).

  If U subset of B and the offsets (xi_B : B in U) agree on all names
  shared by blocks in U, then the following map is an isomorphism of
  induced subgraphs:

      disjoint union over B in U of Psi_{B,xi_B}:
      X^0[ union of V_B^0, B in U ]  ->  X^1[ union of V_B^1, B in U ].

  (3) Fix a winning Duplicator strategy in the K-slot game, with
  K = r+1 >= 9. At every reached history, a marked vertex in T_B^0(z) is
  paired with a vertex in T_B^1(z') for a unique z' in Z_B^1. If
  simultaneous marked vertices lie in T_B^0(z) and T_C^0(w) with B =/= C,
  then for every rho in R(B) intersect R(C),

      z|_rho = w|_rho   implies   z'|_rho = w'|_rho.

  Both conclusions hold even when all K slots are occupied.

  We construct the graphs and prove the proposition in stages.

7.1 Channels, prefix forests, anchors, equality matchings.

For every block B form an ordered list of CHANNELS. The first member is
channel 0, which specifies no readout. For every other block C =/= B and
every shared name rho in R(B) intersect R(C), add a channel denoted
c_B(C,rho). The channels c_B(C,rho) and c_C(B,rho) are called OPPOSITE.
Their order is fixed by the block data and is the same in both versions.

In each channel c choose an order of the a_B scalar coordinates, again
common to the two versions. In a proper channel c = c_B(C,rho), the
coordinates of rho in their prescribed order must come first. Put

    q_{Bc} = 0          if c = 0,
    q_{Bc} = dim(rho)   if c = c_B(C,rho).

For a valuation z, write pi_{Bc,h}(z) for its length-h prefix in channel
c's order.

Now build a vertex-colored graph Y^eps. All vertex positions in the
following four parts are distinct.

    (1) SPINES. For every channel c and legal valuation z in Z_B^eps,
    create a vertex s_{Bc}^eps(z). For consecutive channels c, c+1, join
    s_{Bc}^eps(z) to s_{B,c+1}^eps(z). Thus each valuation has one path
    through the channel list. Set s_B^eps(z) = s_{B0}^eps(z), the
    DISTINGUISHED VERTEX of that valuation.

    (2) PREFIX FORESTS. For q_{Bc} <= h <= a_B and every prefix u of
    length h that extends to a valuation in Z_B^eps, create a vertex
    f_{Bc,h}^eps(u). For h > q_{Bc}, join it to the vertex indexed by its
    truncation to length h-1. These form rooted trees whose roots have
    level q_{Bc}. Join the leaf f_{Bc,a_B}^eps( pi_{Bc,a_B}(z) ) to
    s_{Bc}^eps(z).

    (3) ANCHORS. Create an anchor alpha_B^eps and join it to
    f_{B0,0}^eps(empty prefix). Join all anchors in a path through the
    blocks in a fixed order.

    (4) EQUALITY MATCHINGS. For opposite proper channels specifying rho,
    join their roots with the same value of rho. Write c = c_B(C,rho),
    c' = c_C(B,rho), and q = dim(rho) = q_{Bc} = q_{Cc'}. For each u in
    the value space of rho, join f_{Bc,q}^eps(u) to f_{Cc',q}^eps(u).
    Full projection onto rho ensures that both roots exist for every u,
    so this is a perfect matching of the two root sets.

A special case: if q_{Bc} = a_B, a root is also a leaf. It is one vertex,
adjacent both to its spine vertex and to its opposite root. There is no
additional copy and no truncation edge in this case.

The SORTS are:

    one anchor sort for each block;

    one spine sort for each block and channel;

    one tree sort for each block, channel and level.

If there are s sorts, assign them the consecutive integers 1,...,s in a
fixed enumeration order, common to the two versions. Let col(v) be the
resulting color of a vertex. Different labels within a sort receive the
same color. Assign every vertex constructed at B to its BLOCK PART.

  LEMMA 7.2 (colored promises). Each Y^eps is simple, connected, and has
  maximum degree at most three. Each block part is connected. There are
  O(N^2) vertices and O(N^2) sorts.

  Proof. Degrees. A spine vertex has at most two spine neighbours and one
  leaf neighbour. A nonroot internal tree vertex has one parent and at most
  two children. A nonleaf root has at most two children and one further
  neighbour: its anchor in channel zero, or its matched opposite root in a
  proper channel. A leaf has its spine neighbour and either its parent or,
  when it is also a root, its matched opposite root. An anchor has its
  channel-zero root neighbour and at most two neighbours on the anchor
  path. So every degree is at most three.

  Simple. All tree, spine and anchor vertices occupy distinct positions. A
  proper root belongs to just one channel and has just one matching edge;
  its opposite root belongs to a different block. The remaining edge types
  have their distinct indicated endpoints, so no loop and no multiple edge
  is introduced.

  Connected. Every legal prefix has a full legal extension, so it reaches
  a leaf and then the spine at that channel. The spine path reaches
  channel zero at the same valuation, and the channel-zero tree reaches
  the anchor. So each block part is connected. The anchor path joins all
  the block parts.

  Counts. There are at most

      N + sum over B =/= C of |R(B) intersect R(C)|
        <=  N + 4 N (N-1)

  channels in total. A channel has at most 2^6 spine vertices and at most
  sum_{h=0}^{6} 2^h tree vertices. It also has at most eight sorts: one
  spine sort and at most seven tree-level sorts. Adding the N anchors and
  N anchor sorts proves both O(N^2) estimates. QED


7.2 A walk that tests a shared readout.

  LEMMA 7.3 (readout walk). For distinct blocks B, C and a shared name rho
  in R(B) intersect R(C), there is a fixed sequence of sorts such that
  s_B^eps(z) and s_C^eps(w) are joined by a walk following that sequence
  if and only if z|_rho = w|_rho. The sequence is common to both versions.

  Proof. Start at channel zero of B. Follow its spine to c_B(C,rho), then
  go to the leaf and ascend its tree to the root. Cross the matching to the
  opposite root in C. Descend to a leaf, go to its spine, and follow the
  spine to channel zero of C. Record the sort at every position of this
  route. When a root is also a leaf it contributes just one position.

  The sorts specify the block and channel throughout, and the level of
  each tree vertex. Every spine step preserves the full valuation. The leaf
  is indexed by that valuation, and truncation to the root retains exactly
  the coordinates of rho. The prescribed step to the opposite root can
  only use the matching of equal values. A descent on the other side
  extends that same root value, and its subsequent spine steps preserve
  the chosen extension. Consequently every walk with the specified sorts
  has equal endpoint readouts.

  Conversely, if the endpoint readouts agree, their two prefix paths reach
  a matched pair of roots and give the required walk. QED

7.3 Translations that use one block offset.

  LEMMA 7.4 (colored block translations). Every xi_B in Z_B^1 determines a
  color-preserving isomorphism between the block parts of B in Y^0 and
  Y^1. It fixes the anchor, sends

      s_{Bc}^0(z)  |-->  s_{Bc}^1(z + xi_B),
      f_{Bc,h}^0(u) |--> f_{Bc,h}^1( u + pi_{Bc,h}(xi_B) ),

  and depends on no other block's offset. On a union of parts, these maps
  form an induced-subgraph isomorphism whenever the chosen offsets agree
  on shared names.

  Proof. Translation by xi_B bijects Z_B^0 with Z_B^1. Its effect on a
  prefix depends only on that prefix and commutes with truncation. The
  displayed maps therefore give bijections at every sort, preserving the
  tree, leaf-to-spine and consecutive-channel edges and their nonedges.
  The empty prefix is fixed, so the anchor edge is preserved.

  The only edges between parts are anchor-path edges and root-matching
  edges. Anchors are fixed individually. For a root matching on rho, roots
  with values u, v are adjacent exactly when u = v. If the two block
  offsets have the same restriction d to rho, then

      u = v   <=>   u + d = v + d,

  which preserves adjacency and nonadjacency across that matching.
  Different channels stay different channels, so no other cross-part
  adjacency can arise. QED

7.4 Removing the sort colors.

At each vertex v of Y^eps, assign its incident edges injectively to ports
1, 2, 3 in any fixed construction order. Replace v by a triangle with
vertices

    t(v,1), t(v,2), t(v,3).

For each j, create a connector b(v,j) adjacent to t(v,j). Attach a private
path of length col(v) to this connector, with the connector as the path's
initial vertex. All other vertices on this path are new. For an edge vw of
Y^eps assigned to ports j, j', add the edge b(v,j) b(w,j'). No other edges
or colors are retained. Call the resulting uncolored graph X^eps.

Every new vertex made at v belongs to v's block part. In particular the
two endpoints of an edge between different blocks belong to their
respective blocks. Denote these parts by V_B^eps. The triangle replacing
s_B^eps(z) is denoted T_B^eps(z).

  LEMMA 7.5 (uncolored promises and local lifts). The graphs X^eps are
  simple, connected, uncolored, and of maximum degree at most three. They
  have the same positive order, of size O(N^4). Each colored block
  translation in Lemma 7.4 has a fixed lift Psi_{B,xi_B}: V_B^0 -> V_B^1,
  depending only on that offset, satisfying the induced-isomorphism
  assertion of Proposition 7.1.

  Proof. Degrees. Every triangle member has degree three. A connector has
  its triangle neighbour and its first path neighbour, and, precisely when
  its port is used, one external connector neighbour; its degree is two or
  three. All other path vertices have degree at most two. The edge lists
  introduce neither loops nor repeated edges. Each replacement is
  connected, and replacements are joined exactly along the original
  graph's edges, so X^eps is connected. There is at least one block, each
  with nonempty legal valuations, so the order is positive.

  Local ports. Fix a block and offset, and write v' for the image of a
  vertex v under the colored block translation. Define a permutation of
  the three ports at v to those at v' as follows.

      A port used by an edge within the block goes to the port used by the
      image of that edge under the colored translation.

      At an anchor, a port used by an external anchor-path edge goes to
      the port for the anchor-path edge toward the same other block.

      At a proper-channel root, the port used by its edge to the opposite
      root goes to the port for the unique such edge at its image root.

      The unused ports are matched in their fixed numerical orders.

  These prescriptions are disjoint and exhaustive. Internal incidences are
  bijected by a within-block isomorphism. Each anchor keeps the same
  external anchor incidences, and each proper root has exactly one
  external incidence at both source and image. So the unused port counts
  agree, and the prescriptions really give a permutation. Crucially, the
  third prescription does not require the neighbouring block's offset.

  Using this port permutation, map triangle members to triangle members,
  connectors to connectors, and private paths in order to their
  corresponding private paths. The colors of v and v' agree, so the path
  lengths agree. This gives a bijection Psi_{B,xi_B} of the block parts,
  with the asserted action on the distinguished triangles.

  Compatible unions. For compatible offsets on a union of blocks, every
  internal edge and every anchor edge is carried to the edge prescribed by
  its endpoint port permutations. For an equality matching, the root
  values are shifted by the same readout offset, so the equality condition
  is preserved in both directions. At its two endpoints the unique
  external ports are exactly the ports used by that matching. Unused ports
  remain unused, and all channels remain their own channels. It follows
  that all edges and nonedges between replacements in the union are
  preserved. The maps inside the replacements are isomorphisms too, which
  proves the induced-subgraph assertion. Edges leaving the union impose no
  condition on unchosen offsets.

  Orders. For equal orders, choose an arbitrary legal offset separately at
  each block. The resulting bijections of disjoint parts give a bijection
  of the two vertex sets; compatibility is not needed for this count.
  Finally, if the number of sorts is s, a colored vertex of color c
  produces exactly 6 + 3c vertices. By Lemma 7.2 both s and |V(Y^eps)| are
  O(N^2), giving the order bound O(N^4). All enumerations use at most
  six-bit valuations and prefixes, explicit block pairs and shared names,
  and paths of polynomial length. They therefore implement the
  construction in polynomial time. QED


7.5 What a winning game must preserve.

To establish the decoding hypothesis of Proposition 6.2 for X^0 and X^1,
we use the reached-history principle of 2.6. A hypothetical continuation
may discard unrelated pairs to test the retained pairs. If failure of an
asserted property would let Spoiler force a loss, that property must
already hold at the original history. The test need not return to that
history or restore discarded answers. Thus the tests below establish
invariants even when all slots are occupied.

  LEMMA 7.6 (recognition under a winning strategy). Let K >= 6, and fix a
  winning strategy on X^0, X^1. At every reached history:

  (1) Paired vertices have the same degree and agree as to whether they
  lie in a triangle.

  (2) A triangle member over v in V(Y^0) is paired with a triangle member
  over some v' in V(Y^1) of the same sort.

  (3) If two marked triangle members lie over adjacent vertices v, w of
  Y^0, their responses lie over adjacent vertices v', w' of Y^1.

  (4) A marked member of T_B^0(z) has a unique decoded response z' in
  Z_B^1, meaning that its response belongs to T_B^1(z'). For simultaneous
  marked members at distinct blocks, equality of any shared readout
  implies equality of the decoded response readouts.

  None of the conclusions requires a free slot at the history in
  question.

  Proof. DEGREES AND TRIANGLES. Retain a paired vertex and request one
  further slot. For every vertex Spoiler could choose, Duplicator's
  announced bijection must preserve adjacency to the retained vertex. The
  bijection therefore takes its neighbour set onto the other neighbour
  set, proving degree equality with two slots. To test membership in a
  triangle, retain the vertex and mark the two other triangle members,
  using three slots. The converse can be tested in the second graph:
  after a bijection is announced, Spoiler can choose the preimage of any
  desired second-graph vertex. Partial isomorphism then proves triangle
  membership in both directions.

  The only triangles in X^eps are the designated replacement triangles.
  Indeed, private path vertices cannot lie in a triangle; a connector has
  only one triangle-vertex neighbour; and edges between connectors form a
  matching, since each connector is a single port. There are no other
  edges between replacements. Thus a response to a triangle member lies in
  a unique replacement triangle.

  SORT RECOVERY. Retain such a pair, and mark the connector attached to
  its first-graph triangle member. By triangle membership and adjacency,
  its response is the unique nontriangle neighbour of the second-graph
  triangle member. At a connector, the first private-path vertex is its
  unique neighbour of degree below three. The triangle member has degree
  three, and any connector reached across an external connector edge has
  degree three because that port is used. This assertion also covers an
  unused connector and a private path of length one. Consequently a third
  mark forces the first private-path step.

  Continue along the source path, retaining the previous and current
  vertices and using a third slot for the next vertex. Adjacency and
  inequality from the previous vertex force the forward step in the
  target private path. The original triangle mark can be discarded once
  the traversal has started. If the lengths differed, at their first
  discrepancy a terminal vertex of degree one would be paired with an
  internal vertex of degree two. The degree invariant excludes this. The
  path lengths, and therefore the sort numbers, are equal.

  All assertions used during this test are invariants of reached winning
  histories, established by their own alternative continuations.

  UNDERLYING ADJACENCY. Retain the two marked triangle representatives
  over v, w, and also mark the actual triangle ports used by vw and the
  two connectors attached to those ports. This requires at most six slots.
  An actual port may already be the representative; duplicate marking is
  allowed, or its extra mark may be omitted. Its response lies in the same
  triangle as its original representative, by equality in the coincident
  case and by adjacency and triangle membership otherwise. Each connector
  response must be the unique nontriangle neighbour of its port response.
  The two connectors are adjacent, so their underlying auxiliary vertices
  are adjacent in Y^1. The two response representatives cannot belong to
  one triangle: the source representatives are distinct and nonadjacent,
  whereas distinct members of one triangle are adjacent.

  DISTINGUISHED VERTICES AND SHARED READOUTS. Sort recovery sends a
  triangle over a channel-zero spine vertex of block B to a triangle over
  another channel-zero spine vertex of that same block. The legal
  valuation indexing this vertex gives the unique decoded response.

  Suppose now that the two source valuations at distinct blocks agree on
  a shared readout. Their distinguished vertices admit the sort-prescribed
  walk from Lemma 7.3. First discard all marked pairs except these two
  endpoint pairs. Mark triangle members over the intermediate vertices
  successively, always retaining both endpoints and at most two
  consecutive intermediate pairs. Four slots suffice. Sort recovery gives
  the required sorts of all response vertices. Underlying adjacency,
  applied to each consecutive pair when both are marked, shows that the
  responses trace a walk of precisely the prescribed sorts. Lemma 7.3 now
  forces equality of the response readouts.

  The four walk slots need not be supplemented by six adjacency-test
  slots. Underlying adjacency is already an invariant of every reached
  winning history: its proof may discard the unrelated endpoint pairs in
  a different continuation. Similarly, the unary tests need not retain
  the other walk marks. The largest simultaneous test uses six slots, and
  the conclusion about the retained endpoints therefore holds already at
  the original history, regardless of its occupancy. QED

7.6 Proof of the subcubic realization.

  Proof of Proposition 7.1. The graph promises, equal orders, size bound,
  local lifts and compatible-union isomorphisms are Lemmas 7.2 and 7.5.
  The distinguished triangles have the stated indexing by construction.
  Since K = r+1 >= 9, Lemma 7.6 supplies the decoding and shared-readout
  invariants. QED

  COROLLARY 7.7 (correctness of the subcubic realization). For every
  product scalar-image instance at address length r >= 8, its connected
  subcubic realization satisfies

      the instance is consistent   <=>   X^0 =_r X^1.

  Proof. Use D_B^eps(z) = T_B^eps(z) in Proposition 6.2. The block maps and
  decoding properties are exactly those proved in Proposition 7.1; its
  parameter condition holds because r >= 8. QED


8. UNIFORM SIZE AND TIME BOUNDS; COMPLETENESS.

This is Link 6. The algebraic witnesses are existential and are never
supplied to the graph compiler. The compiler enumerates only the product
schemas, the local blocks, the constant-size valuations, and the graph
vertices. The two realizations have different costs, so their bounds are
kept separate.

8.1 The common size calculation.

For the instance compiled from a fixed machine M and polynomial p as in
Section 3, on a word of length m, put

    r = 2(p(m) + 2),   b = r + 2,   K = r + 1.

Since p(m) >= m + 2 we have m <= r, and since p(m) >= 2 we have r >= 8.

    The computation construction produces O_M(b^2) real types and
    comparison schemas, each of dimension at most two, with r constant-size
    coordinate entries.

    Subdividing each comparison wire multiplies the type and link count by
    O(r), giving O_M(b^3) types and links. Copying an r-coordinate table
    onto each link costs O_M(b^4) entries at worst.

    For each type or link and each row there are at most four blocks: one
    chooses two binary labels, or two label matches each determined by a
    binary label. So |B| = O_M(b^4).

    A site has at most four legal valuations; a square has at most 2^6.
    So the common number N of base vertices is again O_M(b^4), and J <= N.

The two realizations then have this growth.

    Quantity                    General              Subcubic
    intermediate vertices       O_M(b^4) base        O_M(b^8) auxiliary
    block or sort encoding      O_M(b^12) vertices   O_M(b^16) vertices
    output matrix entries       O_M(b^24)            O_M(b^32)

The subcubic construction has O_M(b^8) channels, with constantly many
auxiliary vertices per channel, and O_M(b^8) sorts. Replacing each
auxiliary vertex by a gadget whose paths have length at most the sort
count gives the final O_M(b^16) order.

The constants below may depend on the fixed M and p; the exponents in the
graph-size and compiler-time bounds are absolute.

8.2 The smaller general compiler.

  PROPOSITION 8.1 (uniform general-graph compiler). Fix the machine M and
  polynomial p used in Proposition 3.1. For a word w of length m, put
  r = 2(p(m)+2). The constructions of Propositions 3.1, 4.2 and 6.1
  produce two equally large simple uncolored graphs of order
  O_M((r+1)^12). Their explicit adjacency matrices and the binary
  parameter k = r can be produced by a deterministic multitape machine in
  time O_{M,p}((r+2)^80).

  Proof. Since p(m) >= m+2 we have m <= r. The number of original
  consistency types and schemas is O_M(r^2), and every vector dimension is
  at most two. Each schema has r constant-size binary coordinate entries.
  Subdividing each wire with 4(r+1) internal types gives O_M(r^3) types
  and links.

  Blocks and vertices. For each type-row or link-row pair there are at
  most four blocks. A site has at most four legal valuations; a square has
  two vector readouts of dimensions at most two and two scalar readouts,
  hence at most 2^6 valuations, a deliberately loose bound. Thus both J
  and N are O_M(r^4), and J <= N because every block has a legal
  valuation in each version. There are at most J(N+1) leaves at any base
  vertex, so the total order is at most

      N + N J (N+1) <= N + N^2 (N+1) = O_M((r+1)^12).

  The two output matrices have O_M((r+1)^24) entries in total. There is at
  least one scalar real type, which alone supplies four sites per row and
  two valuations per site, so the graph order is at least 8r and the
  output parameter is below the order.

  An explicit polynomial-time implementation. All lists are stored
  sequentially, so no unit-cost random access is assumed.

      (i) Count m, evaluate p, and set d, r, K. Enumerate the fixed
      alphabet, the carry and borrow cases, the initial positions, and the
      final-time tests. Fill each product table coordinate by coordinate.
      Only the max{1,m} exceptional initial positions and the d+1
      possible blank suffix domains are generated. Apply the buffer and
      dummy construction and enumerate wire and link identifiers.

      (ii) Enumerate the constant number of local label choices for each
      type-row or link-row pair. Record the named readouts of each block
      and enumerate all of its local valuations. Test its affine or
      homogeneous equation directly. Count N and enumerate leaves by an
      owner identifier and a local index. This produces vertex lists in
      both graph versions.

      (iii) Enumerate ordered pairs of vertices and stream the
      corresponding matrix bits. For base vertices, compare the constant
      number of named readouts and their values, and check that the blocks
      differ. For a pair involving a leaf, test owner incidence. Leaf-leaf
      pairs are nonadjacent. The diagonal is zero. Finally write r in
      binary.

  Bit cost. The descriptions use O_{M,p}((r+2)^12) records, each of at
  most O_{M,p}((r+2)^2) bits; this includes full coordinate tables,
  identifiers, valuation lists and counters. Allow the larger total
  working-storage length

      D = O_{M,p}((r+2)^16) bits.

  The stated loops need at most O_{M,p}((r+2)^24) record generations and
  matrix-entry writes. Each uses at most O_{M,p}((r+2)^2) elementary
  lookups, copies, comparisons or arithmetic operations; constructing a
  coordinate table can scan all r entries. A sequential list lookup or an
  elementary binary arithmetic operation on at most D bits costs O(D^2)
  time by ordinary scanning and schoolbook algorithms. The total cost is
  thus at most

      O_{M,p}((r+2)^24) * O_{M,p}((r+2)^2) * O(D^2)
        = O_{M,p}((r+2)^(24+2+32))
        = O_{M,p}((r+2)^58),

  which is within the asserted exponent 80.

  Polynomial evaluation also fits. If p(x) is the sum of c_i x^i and A is
  1 + sum of |c_i|, every Horner intermediate at m >= 0 has absolute value
  at most A (m+1)^q, where q is the fixed degree. Its bit length is
  O_p(log(r+2)), and there are only q arithmetic stages. The case m = 0
  has p(0) >= 2 and causes no exception. Reading the input and writing the
  O(log r)-bit dimension are included. At no stage is the list of 2^r full
  addresses, the 2^d computation times, a consistency witness, or an
  offset family enumerated. QED

8.3 The connected subcubic compiler.

  PROPOSITION 8.2 (uniform construction). Fix a machine M and polynomial
  p satisfying Proposition 3.1, and apply the buffer construction of
  Proposition 4.2. On an input word of length m put

      r = 2(p(m)+2),   K = r+1,   b = r+2.

  The two graphs X^0, X^1 and the binary dimension k = r can be produced
  deterministically in time O_{M,p}(b^160). Each output graph has
  O_M(b^16) vertices. They have the same positive order and are connected,
  simple, uncolored, undirected graphs of maximum degree at most three,
  regardless of whether the scalar-image instance is consistent.

  Proof. We give explicit polynomial bounds and a construction that uses
  only the compressed descriptions. Since p(m) >= m+2 we have r >= 8 and
  m <= b.

  Types, links, blocks. The computation construction produces
  O_M(b^2) real types and comparison schemas, each described by endpoint
  identifiers, two constant-size affine forms, and r constant-size
  coordinate domains and maps. Each comparison wire has 4K private
  internal types and 4K+1 links, so the subdivided layout has O_M(b^3)
  types and links. At a fixed row and type there are at most four choices
  of a site's pair of labels; at a fixed row and link there are at most
  four choices of a square's two coordinate matches. Multiplying by r rows
  gives

      |B| = O_M(b^4).

  Channels and auxiliary vertices. A block has at most four readout names,
  so the number of proper channels, indexed by ordered pairs of distinct
  blocks and a shared name, is at most 4|B|^2. Including channel zero at
  every block, the total number of channels is O_M(b^8).

  Every legal valuation uses at most six bits by Lemma 5.3. Hence a
  channel has at most 2^6 spine vertices, at most seven prefix levels, and
  at most 2^6 vertices on any one of those levels; these are absolute
  constants, independent of r. There are only constantly many vertex sorts
  per channel and one anchor and anchor sort per block. Consequently

      |V(Y^eps)| = O_M(b^8),   s = O_M(b^8),

  where s is the number of positive integer sort colors.

  Final order. A colored vertex of color c is replaced by three triangle
  vertices, three connectors, and three private paths with c new vertices
  each, so it produces exactly 6 + 3c vertices. Since 1 <= c <= s,

      |V(X^eps)| <= (6 + 3s) |V(Y^eps)| = O_M(b^16).

  The two explicit output matrices have O_M(b^32) entries, and the output
  dimension r has O(log b) binary bits.

  All structural promises follow from Proposition 7.1. Equal order does not
  need a compatible global offset: each individual block has a nonempty
  affine valuation space, any of its legal offsets supplies a bijection
  between its two block parts, and their cardinalities agree and can be
  added over the partition. There is at least one site block and a legal
  valuation in each version, so the common order is positive.

  An implementation. The construction uses these finite lists and loops.

      (i) Evaluate p and generate the circuit's schema domains and
      coordinate maps by the carry, borrow and initialization cases of
      Proposition 3.1. Expand each connection into its comparison schemas,
      subdivide each comparison wire, and enumerate the type, link and
      block lists. A coordinate scan has length r; no step ranges over the
      full set of addresses.

      (ii) Compare the at most four names of each ordered pair of blocks to
      enumerate channels and their opposite-channel identifiers. For every
      channel, enumerate bit strings of length at most six to list its
      legal valuations and occurring prefixes: a prefix occurs if one of
      the constantly many full strings extending it satisfies the local
      equation. Enumerate the sorts to assign their positive color
      numbers. Fix all ordering choices by the same nested enumeration
      rules in the two versions.

      (iii) Form the auxiliary vertex and edge lists from the defining
      rules. For example, enumerate unordered pairs of auxiliary vertices
      and test those rules. Assign a vertex's incident ports in generated
      edge order, a list of length at most three. Generate the triangle,
      connector and private-path vertices using the sort numbers as
      path-length counters.

      (iv) Enumerate pairs of output vertices and write their adjacency
      bits. A vertex record contains its original auxiliary vertex, its
      triangle/connector/path kind, its port, and, for a path vertex, its
      distance from the connector. With the old edge and port lists, the
      replacement rules decide adjacency from such records.

  All local valuation tests have constant dimension. The witness sets and
  the families H_U are used only in the correctness proof and are never
  constructed by this algorithm.

  Bit cost. Individual records, coordinate tables and counters can be
  padded to O_{M,p}(b^2) bits. Identifiers and counters need only
  O(log b) bits, and individual schema tables use O(r) entries; even a
  time or position address has only d = r/2 bits. There are at most
  O_{M,p}(b^32) records if the output matrix bits are included, so
  O_{M,p}(b^40) bits suffice for lists and working storage with scratch
  space. The fixed polynomial p is evaluated on polynomially bounded
  integers by ordinary integer arithmetic.

  The ordered block-pair loop has O_M(b^8) iterations, the auxiliary
  vertex-pair loop has O_M(b^16), and the output pair loop has O_M(b^32).
  The other enumeration loops have no larger count. The work of each
  iteration can be expressed using O_{M,p}(b^2) basic record operations:
  identifier lookup or update, copying, comparison, and elementary integer
  arithmetic. Edge information is stored as endpoint and port records, so
  no whole adjacency row is copied into a vertex record. The total number
  of these operations is at most O_{M,p}(b^40).

  Implement an identifier lookup by a sequential scan of the stored lists;
  every basic record operation is then bounded by the square of the
  allowed tape length, using sequential scans and schoolbook arithmetic.
  With workspace D = O_{M,p}(b^40) bits, each such operation costs
  O(D^2) = O_{M,p}(b^80) time, and the preceding count gives time at most

      O_{M,p}(b^40) * O_{M,p}(b^80) = O_{M,p}(b^120),

  which is within the stated O_{M,p}(b^160) allowance. No constant or
  threshold in this implementation depends on a separately fixed value of
  the growing dimension. Finally b is polynomial in m for the fixed
  polynomial p, so this is deterministic polynomial time in the source
  input length. QED

8.4 The two completeness reductions.

  Proof of Theorem A. Membership for both languages is Proposition 2.3,
  including invalid encodings and dimensions larger than the graph order.

  For hardness, fix any binary language L_0 in EXPTIME. Choose a
  normalized deterministic decider M for its complement and a fixed
  polynomial p as in Corollary 4.3. On w of length m, generate the product
  circuit and its scalar-image instance at r = 2(p(m)+2) >= 8. For WL use
  the general realization of Section 6; for SUBWL use the connected
  subcubic realization of Section 7. Output the two explicit adjacency
  matrices and k = r in binary. The compiler bounds are, respectively,
  O_{M,p}((r+2)^80) and O_{M,p}((r+2)^160). Since p is fixed, both are
  polynomial in m; their polynomial degrees may depend on the source
  language, as is permitted for a many-one reduction.

  All required graph promises hold on every input, whether accepted or
  rejected by M. Proposition 6.2 verifies its hypotheses from Proposition
  6.1 in the general case and from Proposition 7.1 in the subcubic case.
  Together with the computation reduction this gives, for either output
  pair W^0, W^1,

      w in L_0
        <=>  M does not accept w
        <=>  the circuit has no true terminal test
        <=>  the scalar-image instance is consistent
        <=>  W^0 =_r W^1.

  Thus each construction is a deterministic polynomial-time many-one
  reduction with the required polarity. Because L_0 was arbitrary, both
  target languages are EXPTIME-hard, and with membership this completes
  Theorem A. QED

  COROLLARY 8.3 (unary dimension). If the supplied dimension is encoded in
  unary instead of binary, both WL and SUBWL remain EXPTIME-complete under
  deterministic polynomial-time many-one reductions.

  Proof. For membership, convert the unary dimension to binary and apply
  Proposition 2.3; the conversion is polynomial in the input length. For
  hardness, the reductions just proved output k = r = 2(p(m)+2), which is
  polynomial in the source input length m. Writing this dimension in unary
  therefore preserves their polynomial running time. The output graphs and
  the equivalence condition are unchanged. QED

This completes the proof of Theorem A.

A.3 Where each hypothesis is used.

  Equal positive order.
    Used in 2.2 (capping), 2.4 (the game needs a bijection between two
  vertex sets), and in every realization (equal block-part sizes give
  equal graph orders).

  k >= 2.
    Used in 2.4: two occupied coordinates always occur together in some
  k-projection.

  Dimension capped at the order.
    Only in 2.3, to bound the number of tuple entries.

  Consistency of the scalar-image instance.
    Used only to produce the nonempty families H_U via 5.4, hence to
  build Duplicator's strategy in one direction of 6.2.

  A winning strategy.
    Used only in the converse direction of 6.2: the attained image sets
  are built from one fixed winning strategy.

  K = r + 1.
    Used as the number of slots throughout. The general realization
  needs r >= 3 (so K >= 4, enough for the ring transfer and one spare
  slot). The subcubic recognition tests need K >= 9, i.e. r >= 8, and
  the machine construction gives exactly r = 2(p(m)+2) with p(m) >= 2.

  The reached-history principle.
    Used only in 7.5, to read the auxiliary data off a winning strategy
  even when all slots are occupied.

  No extra hypotheses.
    There is no degree-regularity hypothesis, no connectivity hypothesis
  in the general case, no fixed-dimension hypothesis, and no graph-switch
  theorem imported from elsewhere.
