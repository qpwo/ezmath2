UNIVERSALITY OF CRITICAL QUENCH AUTOCORRELATIONS
IN THE SHERRINGTON-KIRKPATRICK MODEL

version 2.  a self-contained, tightened, ascii account.
read the PICTURE sections for the idea, the THEOREM sections
for the precise claim, the PROOF sections for the machinery.

====================================================================
PREFACE: what this is and what it is not
====================================================================

This is a research paper about how a physical system -- a spin
glass -- is driven far from equilibrium ("quenched") and then watched
as it relaxes. The precise object is the two-time autocorrelation of
the spins at the critical temperature. The result says the
correlation, when zoomed in by the right powers of the system size,
converges to a random function. The randomness is NOT the disorder
of the couplings; it is the random edge of the interaction matrix,
and the same random object controls both the equilibrium and the
quench dynamics.

There are two honest audiences.

  A curious reader with high-school math can follow the PICTURE
  parts and the statements. They describe a real phenomenon
  (a "slow," system-size-dependent relaxation that remembers a
  random spectral edge) and say, in words, why the answer is what
  it is.

  A mathematics student can follow the THEOREM and PROOF parts.
  These need background in probability (heat-bath Markov chains,
  relative entropy, concentration), a little random-matrix theory
  (GOE, the edge scaling n^{-2/3}), and, for one section,
  Dirichlet forms and stochastic calculus.

We cannot make the proof "readable in an afternoon with no
background" without either lying or deleting the proof. So we do the
next best thing: we tell the whole story cleanly, state every result
exactly, and in the proofs keep only the steps that actually carry
the argument. Each mechanism is named, so a reader can place it.

One-word map of the document:

  1  PICTURE                  the phenomenon, no symbols
  2  THE MODEL                the definitions, exactly
  3  THE RESULT               the main theorem, exactly
  4  THE ENGINE               the four mechanisms, in words
  5  EDGE ESTIMATES           how much of the spectrum matters
  6  WARMING                  the system forgets its initial law fast
  7  GAUSSIAN LIMIT           positive-time limit, random family
  8  ENTRANCE SELECTION       why the family is determined by g
  9  REPLACEMENT              Gaussian and Rademacher agree
  10 COMPLETION               glue, and which hypotheses are load-bearing


====================================================================
1.  PICTURE
====================================================================

Imagine n tiny magnets ("spins") at the corners of a complete graph.
Every pair pulls on every other pair with a random strength. At high
temperature the magnets flip around and forget each other quickly;
at low temperature they freeze into a glass with many valleys. Right
at the critical temperature between the two, something special
happens: the time it takes to relax grows with n.

  Relaxation time  ~  n^{2/3}      (this is the famous "critical
                                    window;" a random matrix
                                    eigenvalue gap sits here)

Because of this, if you watch the ordinary autocorrelation -- how
much a spin resembles its past self after some real time t -- you
see essentially zero for any fixed t, as n grows. To see something,
you zoom: you multiply the observed correlation by n^{2/3}... but
the correlation is defined perspin as an inner product, and after
summing over the n spins it is scaled by n^{-2/3}... let us not fudge
this in prose. The exact rescaling is in Section 2. The point is: a
specific zoom level makes the correlation converge to a nontrivial
random function of the rescaled time.

Now do TWO experiments at once.

  (equilibrium) start the system already in the critical Gibbs
  measure and watch. This gives A(t).

  (quench) start the system in a completely random state (every
  spin fair and independent; the "uniform" state, i.e. infinite
  temperature) and suddenly place it at the critical temperature.
  Then watch. This gives B(s,t): a correlation between the spin at
  waiting time s and the spin at a later time s+t.

The main result: both A and B converge, jointly, to the SAME kind
of object, living on the random GOE edge. The quench limit is more
than the equilibrium limit only at very short waiting times s. As
s -> infinity the quench correlation relaxes exactly to the
equilibrium one. And -- this is the surprising part -- the answer
does not care whether the couplings were Gaussian or +-1
("Rademacher"). The disorder is "universal."


====================================================================
2.  THE MODEL  (everything is here; no prose)
====================================================================

Let D be either the standard Gaussian law N(0,1) or the uniform law
on {-1,+1}.

For n >= 2 let J_ij, i < j, be independent with law D, and set
J_ji = J_ij and J_ii = 0.

The critical zero-field Gibbs measure on spins sigma in {-1,+1}^n is

    pi_{n,J}(sigma) = Z_{n,J}^{-1} exp( n^{-1/2} sum_{i<j} J_ij sigma_i sigma_j ).

This is the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick (SK) model at inverse temperature
beta = 1, which is its critical point.

Dynamics.  Every site has an independent rate-one clock. When the
clock at site i rings, the spin sigma_i is replaced by a in {-1,+1}
with conditional probability

    exp(a h_i(sigma)) / (2 cosh h_i(sigma)),     h_i(sigma) = n^{-1/2} sum_{j != i} J_ij sigma_j .

This is heat-bath (Glauber) dynamics reversible with respect to
pi_{n,J}. Write P_t^J for its semigroup. In one unit of (unscaled)
time there are about n spin flips.

Let nu_n be the uniform law on the cube {-1,+1}^n, chosen independent
of J. This is the quench initial law (all spins fair and independent).

Set the critical time unit

    T_n = n^{2/3} .

Stationary autocorrelation (in critical units):

    A_{n,J}(t) = n^{-2/3} sum_{i=1}^n < sigma_i , P_{t T_n}^J sigma_i >_{pi_{n,J}} ,   t > 0.

Quench autocorrelation (waiting time s, then lag t):

    B_{n,J}(s,t) = n^{-2/3} sum_{i=1}^n E_{nu_n}[ sigma_i(s T_n) sigma_i((s+t) T_n) ],   s,t > 0.

Thermal and clock randomness are averaged inside A and B. The
disorder J stays random. Both A and B are random functions; the
theorem is about their law.


====================================================================
3.  THE RESULT
====================================================================

We must first name the random object on the right side. It is built
from the random matrix edge.

Edge coordinates. Let lambda_1 >= lambda_2 >= ... be the eigenvalues
of a GOE matrix with off-diagonal variance 1/n. The top of the
spectrum sits at 2, and the gaps close on the n^{-2/3} scale:

    ( n^{2/3} ( 2 - lambda_a ) )_{a=1..m}  ==>  ( g_a )_{a=1..m}          (fixed m)

in law, where g = (g_a) is a strictly increasing "edge process"
(Ramirez-Rider-Virag 2011).

Fix an admissible shift: b with b + g_1 > 0 (the shift keeps the
edge away from zero). Put ell_a = (g_a + b)^{-1} and define the
renormalized edge constant

    D_g(b) = lim_{L -> inf} [ sqrt(L) - sum_{a>=1} ( 1/(g_a + b) - 1/(g_a + L) ) ].

This limit exists in probability over g. Let Z_a be independent
N(0, ell_a). Their centered square sum sum_a (Z_a^2 - ell_a) has
continuous positive density. The conditioned edge measure is

    Pi_g = Law( (Z_a)_{a>=1}  |  sum_a (Z_a^2 - ell_a) = D_g(b) ).

It does not depend on the admissible b. On R^N (product topology) a
coordinate is x_a; gradients are the plain Euclidean gradients. The
"energy" of a function F is

    E_g(F) = int  sum_{a>=1} | partial_a F |^2  dPi_g .

This closed form is Dirichlet; its generator H_g has kernel equal to
the constants; S_t^g = exp( - t c_* H_g ) is a symmetric Markov
semigroup. Here c_* > 0 is a fixed microscopic coefficient (the
"relaxed energy" constant, imported from the stationary theory).

The stationary limit function is

    A_g(t) = sum_{a>=1} < x_a , S_t^g x_a >_{Pi_g} ,   t > 0.


MAIN THEOREM (critical quench universality).
There is a measurable family ( h_s^g )_{s>0}, with h_s^g in
L^2(Pi_g), such that for almost every g:

 (1) h_s^g >= 0 and Pi_g[h_s^g] = 1. For s,t > 0,
         h_{s+t}^g = S_t^g h_s^g .
     As s -> 0,   h_s^g Pi_g  ==>  delta_0   (product topology on R^N).
     As s -> inf, || h_s^g - 1 ||_{L^1(Pi_g)} -> 0.

 (2) The series
         B_g(s,t) = sum_{a>=1} int h_s^g(x) x_a (S_t^g x_a)(x)  dPi_g(x)
     converges locally uniformly on (0,inf)^2 and is continuous.

 (3) For BOTH choices of D (Gaussian and Rademacher),
         Law_D( A_{n,J}, B_{n,J} )  ==>  Law_g( A_g, B_g )
     in  C_loc((0,inf)) x C_loc((0,inf)^2).
     The two coupling laws use the SAME edge path g, the SAME family
     h^g, and the SAME coefficient c_*.

 (4) For every 0 < a < b < inf,
         sup_{a <= t <= b} | B_g(s,t) - A_g(t) |  ->  0
     in probability over g, as s -> inf.

One sends the reader to Theorem 8.1 (entrance selection) for the
precise "these are the densities selected by the uniform start"
statement; the main theorem quotes it.


====================================================================
4.  THE ENGINE  (the whole proof in words)
====================================================================

Four mechanisms do the work. Everything in Sections 5-9 is one of
these four, made quantitative.

(5) EDGE ESTIMATES. Only a thin block of eigenvalues near 2 matters:
about k_p = n p^3 of them sit below 2 - p^2. A careful Bayesian
"cap observation" bounds how much of a bounded spin function stays
unresolved after we look at that block. The bound is (1/p^2) times
the energy, with a remainder e^{-c k_p^a}. This is what turns a
question about n spins into a question about a finite Gaussian
observation, coordinate by coordinate.

(6) WARMING. However you start (even fully random), after a positive
critical time s T_n the law has a bounded density relative to the
Gibbs measure, and its entropy is controlled by c (1 + s^{-1.54}).
So a quench "warms up" fast: the system forgets the initial state
exponentially fast in the rescaled time s, at the quantitative rate
in Theorem 6.1. This is the source of all uniformity in the initial
law.

(7) GAUSSIAN LIMIT. In Gaussian disorder, adding a GOE diagonal does
not change the spin law. Diagonalize: the spin projects onto
eigenvectors, and orthogonality rewrites the autocorrelation as a
sum over edge coordinates x_a. Warming bounds the densities; a
variational/resolvent argument identifies the evolution operator as
S_t^g. The result is a positive-time family h_s, but at this stage
it might still depend on randomness beyond g.

(8) ENTRANCE SELECTION. The uniform start leaves a fingerprint: at
short times, the law puts its mass on eigenmodes whose index runs to
infinity, so every fixed coordinate x_i becomes negligible relative
to the total weighted size M_g(x) = (1/2) sum_a x_a^2 / r_a. That
"escape" condition is enough to UNIQUELY determine the family h_s
from g alone, via a scalar Volterra (constraint) equation for a
single common diffusion factor. This is where the random family stops
depending on extra randomness and becomes a function of the GOE edge.

(9) REPLACEMENT. To move from Gaussian to +-1 couplings, use the
Lindeberg replacement idea: the two laws match through third
moments, so the averaged fourth derivative of any bounded test of the
truncated histories must vanish. The bookkeeping is a Taylor
expansion in each coupling entry; a "clock score" (derivative of the
path likelihood with respect to one entry) is projected and shown to
be small on average. The finite heat-bath kernel and a smooth
truncation of the long clock histories make this rigorous, uniformly
over the critical time window.

(10) COMPLETION. Assemble: edge estimates feed warming; warming feeds
the gain in Section 7; 7 gives positive-time limits; 8 pins them to
g; 9 transfers to +-1; the subsequence criterion turns all of it into
one limit.


====================================================================
5.  EDGE ESTIMATES  (only a thin spectral block matters)
====================================================================

Setup. Gaussian disorder, completed GOE matrix W = J / sqrt(n)
(adding an independent GOE diagonal changes the spin law by a
constant only). Write the eigenvalues lambda_a and the gap
d_a = 2 - lambda_a. The edge coordinates are

    g_{a,n} = T_n d_a = n^{2/3} d_a ,     x_a(sigma) = n^{-1/3} < u_a , sigma > ,

where u_a are orthonormal eigenvectors (Haar-distributed conditionally
on the spectrum).

The exact identity that makes everything run:

    n^{-2/3} (sigma . sigma') = sum_{a=1}^n x_a(sigma) x_a(sigma') .   (replica identity)

It converts "the two-time spin autocorrelation" into "a sum over
edge coordinates." That is the whole reason the edge controls the
answer.

The block that matters. At scale p in (0,1], keep the eigenvalues with
d_a <= p^2. Their number is comparable to

    k_p = n p^3 .

(Heuristic: the eigenvalue density near the edge is ~ sqrt(d_a), so
the count below p^2 is n * int_0^{p^2} sqrt(t) dt ~ n p^3.) We only
ever use scales with k_p above a tight threshold K, i.e. p not too
small; the theorem is uniform over compact positive times, so this
is allowed.

The cap observation. Form the Gaussian "observation" of the spin,
projected onto the kept block, plus Gaussian noise whose covariance
is exactly the kept projection. Bayes' formula says: the conditional
law of the spin given this observation is a Gibbs measure with the
kept block "capped" (its eigenvalues pushed to 2 - p^2). Precisely,
writing mu for pi_{n,J}, mu_p^h for the capped posterior at field h,
the posterior F_p(h) = mu_p^h[f] and its variance V_p(f) satisfy the
central estimate.

KEY PROPOSITION (edge variance; source Prop 3.1-3.2 + covariance
iteration). Fix tolerances. On Gaussian disorder events Cn with
liminf Prob(Cn) >= 1 - eps, simultaneously for every scale with
k_p >= K (K fixed, tight) and p <= r_n = n^{-1/3+1e-4}, and every
0 <= f <= 1,

    V_p(f) <= C p^{-2} E(f) + C e^{-c k_p^a} ,                     (variance)
    p^2 Q || grad_{H_p} F_p(h_p) ||^2 <= C V_p(f) + C e^{-c k_p^a} . (bracket)

The constants and the event are independent of f. The exponent 2 in
p^{-2} is essential: a fixed relative error at every scale would
accumulate to a power loss under the d(log p) integration.

Why. In time t = p^2 the posterior F_p is a bounded martingale in the
observation filtration; its predictable bracket is the squared
gradient. So

    -d V_p(f) / d(p^2) = Q || grad F_p ||^2 ,

the "localization variance" identity. If p^2 K_p(h) <= (1+eta)I off a
bad set with common cost rho_p, then

    -d V_p / d(log p) <= 2(1+eta) V_p + 2 rho_p .

With eta = C k_p^{-a} the integrating factor is at most C(q/p)^2 on
[p,q], and the small eta is summable in d(log p): that is exactly the
p^{-2} gain. A positive-curvature lemma (a parent/child cap comparison
with a Haar rotation diluting any positive error) delivers the needed
eta, i.e. p^2 K_p <= (1 + C k_p^{-a}) I, on events of probability
1 - C e^{-c k_p^a}. The remainder integrates against the source
variance estimate at the terminal scale r_n.

What to remember: a bounded spin function that the kept block fails
to resolve has variance controlled by (1/p^2) times its energy, and
the error is exponentially small above the threshold. This is the
entire content of the section. Everything else (saddles, norm-density
bounds, sphere-vs-cube cell comparison, the score martingales) is
technical machinery to prove that one proposition.


====================================================================
6.  WARMING  (the system forgets its initial law, quantitatively)
====================================================================

The point. We will start the quench from the completely random law
nu_n, and later from ANY law rho (possibly chosen after the disorder).
Warming says the first positive critical time washes the initial law
out, with explicit rates. It turns all the finite-time objects into
objects that have bounded densities relative to the Gibbs measure.

Notation. For a probability law rho, w_s^rho = d(rho P_{s T_n})/d mu,
the relative density. For the uniform start w_s = w_s^{nu_n}.
p_t(sigma,tau) = P_t(sigma,tau)/mu(tau), the heat-kernel density.
Ent_mu(z) is the entropy of z relative to mu.

THEOREM (warming from arbitrary initial laws; source Thm 4.1).
Gaussian couplings. Fix a tolerance, an integer m >= 1, and
0 < xi < 1/100. There are disorder events Gn with
liminf Prob(Gn) >= 1 - theta, a constant C, and eps_n -> 0, such
that on Gn, for all large n and for EVERY initial law rho (chosen
after J):

  (entropy)   Ent_mu( w_s^rho ) <= C(1 + s^{-1.54}) ,   s > 0;
  (kernel)    || P_{s T_n} ||_{L^1(mu) -> L^inf(mu)} <= C exp(C s^{-1.54}) ,   s >= eps_n;
  (log-mom)   ( 1 + mu[ w_s^rho (log_+ w_s^rho)^m ] )^{1/m}
                    <= C( 1 + s^{-1/(2/3 - xi)} ) ,   s > 0.

Read this as: entropy is controlled at every positive time; the
stronger pointwise heat-kernel bound begins at a time eps_n -> 0 in
the rescaled unit; the log-moment bound (bigger m, weaker rate)
controls the very shortest times used in the replacement argument.

The chain of ideas:

(mLSI) Modified log-Sobolev. On a two-point edge,
(a^2 - b^2)(log a^2 - log b^2) >= 4(a-b)^2; summing over edges gives
    I(f^2) >= 4 E(f) ,   I(z) = E(z, log z).

(restricted profile) For 0 <= f <= 1 with N = mu[f^2] = e^{-L},
K0 <= L <= C_lev n, and D(f^2 mu / N || mu) >= L/2,

    T_n I(f^2) / N >= c L^{5/3 - xi} ,                            (modified profile)
    T_n E(f)  / N  >= c L^{1.662}  (L <= n^delta) or c L^{1.162} (L > n^delta).  (classical)

This is proved in three entropy ranges:
  - L <= n^delta: the "two observation scales" argument. Assume the
    energy is too small; then the posterior variance at a large scale
    R (with k_R = L^M) is tiny, so the observation-tilt density is
    close to the spin-tilt density; a common diffusion argument shows
    the surviving entropy H(p) obeys dH/d log p >= -C H, so it cannot
    drop too fast, contradicting the assumed small energy. This is
    where Proposition 5 (variance) is spent.
  - n^delta < L <= lambda n: the proportional branch (source Sec 19.4).
  - lambda n <= L: a product-posterior endpoint plus the "reweighted
    posterior mean" lemma below.

(posterior mean, the new input; source Lemma 4.3 refining SG Prop 7.3).
At a subcritical posterior interaction, for a field h in the stability
regime and every f with N_h = mu_h[f^2] > 0,

    || nu_h[sigma] - m_* || / sqrt(n) <= C eps + (C_eps/sqrt(n))( 1 + sqrt( D_h(f)/N_h ) ),

where nu_h = f^2 mu_h/N_h and D_h(f) is the heat-bath energy. The
square-root energy dependence (instead of linear) is what lets the
large-entropy range reach order-n. It comes from a strengthened
"square-density"/residual estimate: every finite recipe residual is
bounded by N_h + sqrt( N_h D_h(f) ), proved by induction with
weighted Cauchy-Schwarz and the exact one-site integration by parts.

(passage to arbitrary rho). Log levels of any density z are cut into
layers [d, 1.1 d] with bounded test U_d(z) (a flat-top transform with
I(U_d) <= 4 I(z)). Each layer either carries enough mass and is
handled by the restricted profile, or the layers cannot sum to the
total entropy/log-moment -- a pigeonhole. Integrating the resulting
differential inequalities from an earlier positive time and letting
that time go to zero gives the three displayed bounds, uniformly in
the initial law.

Why we need all three. (entropy)+(mLSI) drive the entropy down like
H' <= -c H^{1.662}, giving the 1.54 exponent (1/0.662 ~ 1.51, rounded
up). (kernel) is the pointwise two-time bound that makes every density
appearing later uniformly bounded on compact positive time windows.
(log-mom) gives the polynomial-tail control used in the replacement
step at very short times.

COROLLARY (kernel-log tail; source Cor 4.4). For fixed beta, A,
C_star, on events of probability -> 1,

    sup_sigma sup_{1<=d<=C_star T_n} sum_tau P_d(sigma,tau)
        1{ log p_d(sigma,tau) > (1/2) n^beta (T_n/d)^{3/2} } <= C n^{-A}.

This is the quantitative form used later.


====================================================================
7.  GAUSSIAN LIMIT  (positive-time limit, still possibly extra random)
====================================================================

Now specialize to Gaussian J, use the representation from Section 5,
and push n -> inf at positive times.

Setup. Lambda = diag(lambda_1..lambda_n), U Haar orthogonal. The spin
projection x_a(sigma) = n^{-1/3}<u_a,sigma>. The finite quantities are

    A_{n,J}(t) = sum_{a=1}^n mu[ x_a P_{t T_n} x_a ] ,
    B_{n,J}(s,t) = sum_{a=1}^n mu[ w_{n,s} x_a P_{t T_n} x_a ] ,

where w_{n,s} = d(nu_n P_{s T_n})/d mu. We use a "represented
subsequence": keep a countable family of coordinates, densities against
cylinders, and the warming constants all at once, then extract a
subsequence on which everything converges.

PROPOSITION (positive-time Gaussian subsequential limits; source 5.1).
Every sequence n -> inf has a further represented subsequence on which,
almost surely, the edge coordinates converge to g and there exists a
measurable family h_s in L^2(Pi_g), s > 0 (possibly depending on extra
represented randomness, not only g at this stage), a finite random
C >= 1, with

   0 <= h_s <= K(s) := C exp(C s^{-77/50}),   Pi_g[h_s] = 1,
   h_{s+t} = S_t^g h_s ,   s,t > 0,   s -> h_s continuous in L^2(Pi_g),

a nonnegative symmetric kernel k_t^g for S_t^g with
0 <= k_t^g(x,y) <= K(t) a.e., and

   A_{n,J} -> A_g in C_loc((0,inf)) ,   B_{n,J} -> B_h in C_loc((0,inf)^2),

   B_h(s,t) = sum_a Pi_g[ h_s x_a S_t x_a ]   (locally uniformly), and

   || h_s - 1 ||_{L^2(Pi_g)} -> 0,   B_h(s,.) -> A_g(.) in C_loc  (s -> inf).

The exponent 77/50 is the 1.54 from warming. The kernel bound comes
from the warming kernel bound passed to the limit (a positive bounded
functional on L^1(Pi_g x Pi_g) defines a bounded density).

How. (i) Bounded cylindrical sources: the finite resolvent
(z + T_n H_n)^{-1} has sup-norm bound z^{-1}||.||_inf by the Markov
property, so its optimizers are polynomially bounded and the stationary
relaxed-energy theorem applies. A variational (min-max) argument with
energy lower-semicontinuity and recovery gives strong convergence of
resolvents and then of the semigroup, with the coefficient c_*.
(ii) Densities: warming bounds w_{n,s} in L^inf and L^2; their weak
limits are positive L^1 functionals, hence densities h_s. (iii) Kernel:
the finite bound 0 <= mu[phi P_{t T_n} psi] <= K(t) mu[phi] mu[psi]
passed to the limit defines k_t^g. (iv) Summation over all edge
coordinates: a two-copy tail. With Q_{n,m}(sigma,sigma') =
sum_{a>m} x_a x_a', its mean square is controlled by the squared
resolvent tail sum_{a>m} (ell_a)^2 -> 0; the quench two-time density
d_{n,s,t} = w_{n,s} p_{n,t} is L^2-uniformly bounded on compact sets by
warming, so the tail of the sum vanishes uniformly.

At this point h_s may still remember randomness beyond g. The next
two results kill that.

PROPOSITION (escape estimate from the uniform start; source 5.2).
There is a measurable admissible b = b(g) with r_a = g_a + b
satisfying c(1+a)^{2/3} <= r_a <= C(1+a)^{2/3} and D_g(b) > 0. Put
M_g(x) = (1/2) sum_a x_a^2 / r_a. Then 0 < M_g < inf Pi_g-a.s., and for
every represented family, every fixed i >= 1, every eps > 0,

    lim_{s->0}  Ehat[ Pi_g( h_s 1{ x_i^2 / M_g(x) > eps } ) ] = 0.

(Here Ehat averages the represented environment.) So along the
quench, each FIXED coordinate becomes negligible relative to the total
weighted size. The uniform start pushes its mass onto high indices.

How (sketch). Two halves.
  Numerator: x_i^2 is small in probability at time s. Use a stopped
  Haar rotation of the first m = ceil(s^{-5/4}) eigenvector columns:
  averaging over the rotation and using the exact path likelihood of a
  marked heat-bath path (no initial score, because the start is
  uniform), a martingale decomposition gives
  E x_i(s)^2 <= C s^{9/100}-ish for fixed i, rigorously via a stopped
  rotation and a surviving-mass event G with Prob(G^c) -> 0.
  Denominator: M_g is not small in probability. Under Pi_g the block
  of indices [N, 2N] with N = ceil(s^{-8/5}) carries weight
  >= c N^{-1/3} except with probability e^{-cN} (chi-square lower tail),
  and the conditioning density is bounded, so
  Pi_g{ M_g < s^{14/25} } <= C e^{-c s^{-8/5}}, which beats the
  h_s <= K(s) = e^{C s^{-77/50}} factor since 8/5 > 77/50.
Combining, x_i^2/M_g -> 0 in the averaged law; transfer to h_s Pi_g by
cylinder convergence.

Averaging over the environment turns the s-dependent conclusion into a
deterministic rational sequence s_k -> 0 along which, almost surely,
Pi_g(h_{s_k} 1{x_i^2/M_g > eps}) -> 0 simultaneously for all i, eps.
That sequence is the "escape" input to Section 8.

Intuition to keep: the fine n^{1/3}-scale structure of the initial
state is destroyed, but the total spectral weight is conserved and
lives at index -> infinity; the ratio is what pins the entrance.


====================================================================
8.  ENTRANCE SELECTION  (why the family is determined by g)
====================================================================

The question. Section 7 produced a family h_s, but it could depend on
extra randomness in the representation. We want: a.e. g, there is AT
MOST ONE family with the three structural properties, so the family is
a function of g. Uniqueness is not obvious, because the semigroup is
infinite-dimensional and h_s can wander.

THEOREM (entrance selection; source 6.1). Fix g in a full-measure set.
r_i = g_i + b(g), D = D_g(b(g)), Pi = Pi_g, H = H_g,
c(1+i)^{2/3} <= r_i <= C(1+i)^{2/3}, M(x) = sum_i x_i^2/(2 r_i). There is
AT MOST ONE family (h_s)_{s>0} of densities w.r.t. Pi with:

 (1) h_s >= 0, Pi[h_s] = 1, sup_{s0<=s<=s1} ||h_s||_inf < inf for every
     0 < s0 < s1 < inf;
 (2) h_{s+t} = S_t h_s in L^2(Pi), S_t = exp(-t c_* H);
 (3) there is a sequence s_k -> 0 such that, for every i and eps > 0,
     Pi[ h_{s_k} 1{ x_i^2 / M(x) > eps } ] -> 0.        (escape)

Any such family satisfies h_s Pi ==> delta_0 (product topology) as
s -> 0. For a.e. g such a family EXISTS; it can be chosen measurably in
g (L^2 classes), with jointly measurable representatives in (g,s,x).
Every represented Gaussian limit of Section 7 IS this family a.s.

Proof skeleton (the only place a genuine PDE enters).

(a) The form's diffusion. The closed gradient form E_g(F) =
int |grad F|^2 dPi is Dirichlet; its associated symmetric process X(t)
is a real diffusion with coordinate martingales M^i having bracket
<M^i,M^i>_t = 2t (the "unit speed" of the form) and M^i = sqrt(2) W_i
for independent Brownian motions. Set m = 12, R(x) =
(sum_{j<=m} x_j^2)^{1/2}, theta_i = x_i/R, rbar(x) = sum_{j<=m}
r_j theta_j^2. The angular coordinates theta_i solve, pathwise,

  d theta_i = (sqrt2/R)( dW_i - theta_i sum_{j<=m} theta_j dW_j )
              + theta_i ( (3 - m - 2 1{i<=m})/R^2 - r_i + rbar ) dt.   (angular SDE)

The cylinder density of Pi has a radial-square-constraint factor whose
gradient is radial, so it kills the head projection and gives the
drift; R = 0 is polar (zero capacity) so the SDE holds with R > 0.

(b) One common diffusion factor. Cancel the head projection and the
common angular drift with a single positive scalar U(t):

   dU = U ( (sqrt2/R) sum_{j<=m} theta_j dW_j + ((m-1)/R^2 - rbar) dt ),
   Q = U/R,  f = Q^2,  Y_i = U theta_i = Q X_i .

Then Y_i obeys the SIMPLE linear SDE

   dY_i = - r_i Y_i dt + sqrt(2) Q dW_i ,

driven by ONE common factor Q. The initial weights
p_i = y_i^2/(2 r_i) satisfy sum_i p_i = 1 and y_i^2 = X_i(0)^2/M(X(0));
the escape condition (3) says these weights leave every finite set as
the starting time decreases along s_k.

(c) The scalar constraint (Volterra). The coordinate SDEs plus the
quadratic constraint sum_i X_i^2 = n (encoded in Pi) determine Q
through a scalar Volterra equation. With L(t) = sum_i r_i^{-1} e^{-2 r_i t}
and the causal (zero on t<0) inverse kernel K solving
K * (D delta_0 + L') = delta_0, one gets, for 0 < t <= 1,

   f(t) = A_y(t) + 2 sqrt(2) sum_i int_0^t K_i(t-v) Q(v) Y_i(v) dW_i(v),

where K_i = K * e^{-2 r_i .} and A_y(t) = sum_i K_i(t) y_i^2 is the
"seed response." The kernel obeys c_g t^{-1/2} <= K(t) <= C_g t^{-1/2},
|K'| <= C_g t^{-3/2}, and the cross bounds that make every stochastic
series converge in C([0,1]) with all moments finite.

The key scalar fact: if the seed weights escape (p_i -> 0 in
probability for every fixed i), then

   A_y -> K   in probability, uniformly on compact subintervals of (0,1],

and the seed stochastic term built from y_i tends to 0 in L^2. That is
why two different entrance laws, which differ only in their seeds,
must actually coincide: both seed responses become the SAME function K.

(d) Uniqueness by coupling. Take two candidate families with starting
sequences u_{1,k}, u_{2,k} downarrow 0. Couple their diffusions on a
common Brownian path (conditional on W, sample the rest independently;
Brownian motion stays Brownian in the joint filtration). Stop both when
sqrt(t) f leaves a window [c, C_0^2] and when any coordinate leaves a
tolerance (1+r_i)^xi. The seed contributions both converge to the
common K; the stochastic convolutions differ only through their drivers
q_1, q_2, and the difference d(t) = sqrt(t) E|q_1 - q_2|^2 obeys a
Volterra contraction with a small coefficient on a short time interval.
Iterating and then letting the starting index k -> inf bounds the total
variation distance of the two marginals at time delta by C delta^b for
a fixed b > 0; letting delta -> 0 makes the marginals equal at every
positive time, hence the density classes equal. To get the boundary:
on the surviving event, each coordinate is at most
(sqrt(2 r_i) + (1+r_i)^xi) delta^{1/4}/sqrt(c) -> 0, so h_delta Pi
==> delta_0. This proves both uniqueness and the delta_0 boundary.

(e) Measurability. Encode a candidate family by the countable vector of
its integrals against bounded continuous cylinders at rational times.
The escape tests are Borel in the encoded pair (g, mu). The annealed
escape estimate lets one pick a deterministic rational s_k -> 0 with
sum_k E[ sum_{i<=k} mu_{s_k}( x_i^2/M > 1/k ) ] < inf, so a.s. all
escape tests hold. Disintegrate the law of (g, family) over g: two
independent conditional samples both satisfy the criterion, so
uniqueness makes them equal a.s.; a random probability measure on a
standard Borel space whose two independent draws agree a.s. is a point
mass. Hence the family depends measurably on g. Every Gaussian
subsequential limit is therefore the same function of g.

This is the heart of the paper: the semigroup is infinite-dimensional,
but the uniform start selects, through a scalar Volterra equation and a
single common diffusion factor Q, a UNIQUE entrance law, and the whole
limit becomes a deterministic functional of the GOE edge.


====================================================================
9.  REPLACEMENT  (Gaussian and +-1 give the same answer)
====================================================================

What must be shown. The limit in Section 8 is stated for Gaussian
couplings. We must show it survives replacing the Gaussian law D by
the Rademacher law (uniform on +-1). This is disorder universality.

The tool: Lindeberg replacement. Put an interpolation law on the
couplings,

   nu_lambda = (1 - lambda) N(0,1) + lambda (delta_{-1}+delta_{+1})/2 ,

on each entry independently, and differentiate the trace's expectation
in lambda. The derivative is a sum over entries of a difference of
expectations (one of the replaced entry sampled from the sign law, one
from the Gaussian),

   (d/d lambda) E[ Phi ] = sum_{e} E_{lambda,-e}[ E_xi Phi(.,xi) - E_Z Phi(.,Z) ] .

Because the Gaussian and the sign law agree in their first (and here,
all odd-order) moments through order 3, a Taylor expansion of Phi in
the single entry, averaged at a completed matrix, cancels orders 0
through 3 exactly. What is left is orders 4 through K-1 and a
remainder; the order-k term comes with n^{2 - k/2} (there are ~n^2
entries) and the kth averaged derivative of Phi. So to get a vanishing
difference we need the AVERAGED FOURTH derivative to tend to zero, and
the higher averaged or supremum derivatives to grow no faster than
n^{delta} T^{(k-4)/2}.

Choose the Taylor order K = 24. Then for k>=5 the contribution is
n^{-(k-4)/6 + delta} -> 0 and the remainder is n^{2 - K/6 + delta} =
n^{-2+delta} -> 0. The comparison is a single master inequality
uniform in lambda; integrating over lambda gives the transfer. The one
nonzero burden is: average of | fourth derivative of the truncated
observable | is at most n^{-a0}, some a0 > 0.

The observable and its truncation. The traced quantity is a smooth
function of finitely many query traces

   zeta_q = n^{-2/3} sum_i X_i Y_i ,

with |zeta_q| <= n^{1/3}. But a single trace is a two-time heat-bath
path of length of order T = n^{2/3}, and a derivative of its likelihood
in one entry is a sum over the clock rings -- potentially large. So we
truncate the clock history on every scale (so that no single ring can
carry too much weight), and only differentiate the resulting smooth
truncation. The truncation loses negligible Gaussian mass; we transfer
that negligibility to Rademacher using derivative estimates that hold
for EVERY matrix on their deterministic supports.

The clock tree. Bisect each path leg until pieces have length in
[1,2]; the "nodes" of the binary tree carry a check level

   N_I = N(d) = n^{beta} (T/d)^{3/2} ,   d = |I| .

A smooth weight W_N(.) caps the density of each node from above, with
plateau 1 below level N/2 and exponential decay above level ~N, and
with controlled derivative ratios. The truncated transition across a
node is the true transition times W_N(log density). The product of
these weights is the "survival factor"; a capped path is an ordinary
path resampled according to these factors. The mass lost by all caps
in one query is at most n^{-A} for any A, on events of probability
tending to one (this uses the warming log-moment bound to control
log p_d uniformly in the starting state and in the scale).

Static support. There is also a static cutoff Gamma = chi_n(J), a
smooth function that is 1 whenever certain four-replica overlap events
have small probability, and whose every derivative of order <= K in
one entry is O(n^{-b}). It uses the fact that a large two- or
four-point overlap has exponentially small Gibbs probability, and that
this can be certified uniformly over the (polynomial-size) matrix
support without Gaussian averages; the Gaussian verification of
"chi_n = 1 w.h.p." uses the capped replica cell comparison. The outer
cutoff chi(n^{10} sum_q loss_q) is added to control the mass lost in
capped paths. On the support of the full truncation the test is
smooth, bounded, and has derivatives in the Lebesgue class (bounded
sup, bounded sum over coordinates), which is what the Taylor
comparison needs.

Differentiating the truncation: the clock score. For an entry e = ij,
perturb theta_e = J_e/sqrt(n) by vartheta. Both the stationary measure
and the path likelihood change. The exact path likelihood is a product
over rings of single-ring factors M_{e,i,zeta}(u) = (1 + u zeta x_j)/(1
+ u x_j t_i(x)); the stationary measure picks up (1 + u q_e(x)) /
(1 + u q_e-bar). A "clock score" is the u-coefficient of the log
likelihood; each single-ring coefficient is centered under the dynamics
(sum over marks vanishes), so the score is a martingale and can be
compensated.

The one genuinely new estimate (clock scores) projects the LAST
compensated score against coordinate averages and shows the projected
object is small. Two ingredients:
  (a) an overlap bound along a truncated history: for m the minimum of
      consecutive gaps, |rho_2| <~ (1+m)^{-1/2} and |rho_4| <~
      (1+m)^{-3/4}, with probability 1 - n^{-A}. It follows because each
      gap contains a sub-node of length comparable to m, whose cap
      dominates the joint law of the endpoint states by mu^{x mu} times
      a controlled factor, and the static support bounds the overlaps.
  (b) the "gain from the last ring": compensate the last ring before
      taking absolute values; then the coordinate average of the
      remaining predicted prefix is a matrix-vector product against a
      bounded vector, and Cauchy-Schwarz with the overlap kernel gives
      the exponent 1/4 (L^2) and 5/16 (L^4). For earlier rings, average
      the raw block conditional on its endpoints (still in the past of
      the later blocks), which supplies a predictable conditional
      coefficient with the uniform coarse bound.

The conditional (bridge) coefficients R_l(I) are the interior averages
of the path score with the descendant weights fixed. They obey a
midpoint recursion (because the interior splits at the midpoint into
two independent bridges with a fixed base midpoint weight), and the
bounds |R_l|_inf <~ D^{l/2}, |R_l|_2 <~ D^{l/2-1}, |R_l|_4 <~
D^{l/2-1/2}, with extra late-time gains for l near the top of the
range. These come by induction on l: raw terms are projected (with the
gain) and paired with conditional terms (bounded), and the recursion
is closed. All bounds hold on events of probability 1 - n^{-A}.

Assembling the fourth derivative. Each |partial_e^k Phi| is a sum of
products of at most K clock coefficients, each bounded via the bridge
estimates after the projections. Chronologically earlier roots are
projected onto their endpoints before the last root is compensated;
their coefficient bounds feed forward. The power count: the fourth
averaged derivative is at most n^{-a0}, and the supremum and higher
averaged derivatives are as above. This delivers the master inequality
with a positive a0, so the interpolation derivative tends to zero and
the Gaussian and Rademacher laws of the truncated observables agree in
the large-n limit.

Tightness transfer (so we get the topology, not just f.d.d.). The
finite heat-bath generator has ||L_J||_{inf->inf} <= 2n and the
semigroup is a sup-norm contraction, so on the fixed time rectangles
|partial_t A|, |partial_s B|, |partial_t B| <= C n^2. Choose a grid of
mesh n^{-4}; interpolation error is O(n^{-2}). Testing a supremum or a
modulus of continuity uses signed linear forms with coefficient l^1 norm
at most 2. A softmax smax_lambda(a) = lambda^{-1} log sum_r e^{lambda
a_r} with lambda = (log n)^2 has its derivatives bounded exactly like
the Lebesgue class, so comparison at the grid transfers. The modulus
of continuity of the Rademacher law is then bounded by that of the
Gaussian law at twice the radius, which tends to zero by the Gaussian
tightness from Section 8. Arzela-Ascoli gives tightness in C_loc x
C_loc, every subsequential limit has the Gaussian finite-dimensional
laws, and hence the limit is the desired Law_g(A_g, B_g).

PROPOSITION (transfer to Rademacher; source 9.2). For the Rademacher
coupling law,

   Law_Rad( A_{n,J}, B_{n,J} )  ==>  Law_g( A_g, B_g )

in C_loc((0,inf)) x C_loc((0,inf)^2), with the SAME edge path g, SAME
family h^g, and SAME coefficient c_* as the Gaussian law.

The T, s, t in the comparison are the original critical times and the
original uniform initialization for B; the replacement changes only the
coupling law. So the entrance h_s^g and the coefficient c_* pass
through unchanged.


====================================================================
10.  COMPLETION  (glue, and which hypotheses are load-bearing)
====================================================================

Assemble.

  1. (Section 5) On Gaussian events of probability -> 1, the cap
     variance at every scale with k_p above a tight threshold controls
     energy unresolved by the leading spectral block, with the p^{-2}
     gain.
  2. (Section 6) Warming: applied to the quench's uniform start, gives
     a bounded-density regime at every positive critical time, with the
     1.54 entropy rate, uniformly in the initial law.
  3. (Section 7) Positive-time subsequential limit: A_{n,J} -> A_g,
     B_{n,J} -> B_h on a represented subsequence, with the finite
     bounds (density K(s), kernel K(t), relaxation h_s -> 1) passed to
     the limit. The uniform-start escape estimate is the extra
     fingerprint of a quench.
  4. (Section 8) Entrance selection: from the escape condition,
     uniqueness of the family and the delta_0 boundary; measurability in
     g; so every Gaussian limit is the same function h^g of the edge.
  5. (Section 9) Replacement transfers the whole joint finite-time and
     uniform-in-time conclusion from Gaussian to Rademacher couplings,
     preserving g, h^g, c_*.

The main theorem's four items then follow by reading off:
 (1) the structural facts are the conclusions of Section 8 for h^g;
 (2) locally uniform convergence of the B series is in Section 7;
 (3) the joint convergence in law, both couplings, is Sections 7-9;
 (4) B_g(s,.) -> A_g(.) in probability over g as s -> inf is the
     relaxation part of Section 7 with the entrance identification of
     Section 8.

Which hypotheses are load-bearing.

 - Criticality of beta = 1 is not decorative. It puts the relevant
   eigenvalue gap at order n^{-2/3}; the block count k_p = n p^3 and
   the whole edge scaling are consequences. Off criticality the scaling
   and the limit differ.

 - The rate-one clocks. Equal site rates make the finite heat-bath
   operator symmetric in the right measure and make N(d) =
   n^beta (T/d)^{3/2} the natural cap. Changing rates breaks the
   clean normalization c_* but the mechanism survives.

 - The uniform start. Its lack of an initial score is used twice: in
   the escape estimate (no initial likelihood factor when rotating
   columns) and in the fact that a quench's extra randomness lives
   only in the seed weights p_i, which escape. This is exactly why the
   quench limit is the SAME h^g as the equilibrium limit and relaxes to
   it; a different start would select a different (still unique)
   entrance.

 - The microscopic coefficient c_* is a genuine physical constant, not
   a normalization: it is the ratio between the spin energy and the
   edge gradient energy at the critical scale. It appears identically in
   the stationary and the quench limits.

 - The randomness that survives is the GOE edge and nothing else.
   Warming kills initial-law dependence; entrance selection kills extra
   representation randomness; replacement kills the choice of entry law.
   What remains is g.

That is the theorem: near the critical point of a mean-field spin
glass, the way the system remembers itself after a quench is governed,
up to the one random edge of the interaction matrix and one
microscopic constant, by the same infinite-dimensional diffusion that
governs its equilibrium, and the answer does not depend on whether the
disorder was continuous or +-1.
