Write $p_n$ for the $n$-th prime and $d_n=p_{n+1}-p_n$ for the gap to its successor. By the prime number theorem the average of $d_n$ near $p_n$ is asymptotic to $log p_n$, so a gap exceeding $Clog p_n$ is a large deviation for any fixed $C>0$. Such gaps have long been known to occur. Westzynthius showed in 1931 that $d_n/log p_n$ is unbounded; Erdos in 1935 and Rankin in 1938 supplied quantitative lower bounds of increasing strength; Ford, Green, Konyagin and Tao, and independently Maynard in 2016, improved these substantially; and Ford, Green, Konyagin, Maynard and Tao obtained a further gain in 2018.

The object of interest is not the largest gap but its frequency. A lower bound for the maximal gap does not prevent the exceptional gaps from forming a sparse set; a positive proportion of large gaps is a strictly stronger statement. For a set $A$ of positive integers write $d(A)=liminf_{Nto∞}|Acap[1,N]|/N$ for its lower asymptotic density.

Theorem. For every fixed real $C>0$ there are constants $c(C)>0$ and $N_0(C)$ such that, for every integer $Nge N_0(C)$,
$$#{1le nle N: p_{n+1}-p_n>Clog p_n} ge c(C),N.$$
The density is ordinary lower density: the inequality holds for every sufficiently long initial segment, and $c(C)$, $N_0(C)$ depend only on $C$.

Corollary (Erdos--Prachar). The set ${nge1: p_n/n<p_{n+1}/(n+1)}$ has positive lower asymptotic density.

./pdf/erdos-prachar-1961-21-1962-saetze-probleme-pk-k.pdf

The corollary follows at once from the theorem. The inequality $p_n/n<p_{n+1}/(n+1)$ is equivalent to $d_n>p_n/n$, and the prime number theorem gives $p_n/nsimlog p_n$. The theorem with $C=2$ then supplies a positive proportion of indices, after the finitely many indices with $p_n/nge2log p_n$ are removed. The corollary therefore answers the question of Erdos and Prachar affirmatively.

Two counting measures must be kept apart. Every integer start inside a prime-free interval yields such an interval, so a sparse set of very long gaps can inflate the count of empty intervals without contributing proportionally to the count of gaps. Bazzanella, Languasco and Zaccagnini isolate the two measures: one theorem there gives positive density for prime starts below a fixed threshold of roughly $0.579log p$, while the count of integer starts reaches longer intervals. Tao's analysis of empty intervals likewise exhibits the extra control needed to pass from interval measure to a genuine count of consecutive gaps.

Conditional results are quantitatively stronger. Gallagher derived Poisson statistics for the number of primes in integer-start intervals of length $lambdalog X$ from a uniform Hardy--Littlewood tuple hypothesis, and Jha extended the Poisson-tail account to growing parameter ranges under a stronger uniformity assumption. Both conclusions assume far more than the unconditional theorem and are quoted only for context.

The theorem reduces to a single weight construction over two adjacent blocks. Fix $lambda>0$ and write $L=log X$, $h=lfloorlambda Lrfloor$, with the integer $Xto∞$. For each integer start $min(X,2X]$ split the window above $m$ into
$$qquad J={1,...,h},qquad I={h+1,...,2h},$$
and put $θ(n)=(log n),1_{{n prime}}$ together with
$$V_B(m)=frac1Lsum_{bin B}θ(m+b)$$
for a block $B$ equal to $I$ or $J$. The quantity $V_B(m)$ is positive exactly when the block $m+B$ contains a prime, and then $V_B(m)ge1$ because every prime argument exceeds $X$. The symbol $E_X$ denotes the average over the integers $X<mle2X$.

Everything rests on the following family of weights.

Proposition (weights for adjacent blocks). For each fixed $lambda>0$ there is a finite $K_lambda>0$ with the following property. For every $ε>0$ there are weights $W_X(m)ge0$, defined for integers $X<mle2X$, such that
$$E_XW_Xto1,qquad E_X(W_XV_J)tolambda,$$
$$limsup_{Xto∞}E_X(W_XV_I)leε,qquad limsup_{Xto∞}E_X(W_XV_J^2)le K_lambda,$$
$$E_XW_X^2=O_{lambda,ε}(1).$$
The constant $K_lambda$ is independent of $ε$, while the $O_{lambda,ε}(1)$ bound is allowed to deteriorate as $ε$ shrinks.

The estimate on $E_X(W_XV_J^2)$ stops the detected prime mass from sitting on too little weighted mass: a second moment controls how far a nonnegative weight can concentrate. After the starts carrying a prime in $I$ are removed, the separate estimate on $E_XW_X^2$ converts the surviving weighted mass into a positive proportion of ordinary integer starts. This is why only the constant governing detection must be chosen before the suppression tolerance, and the mass bound may depend on it.

The bridge from blocks to ordinary gaps is a counting lemma. It bounds how many starts can be charged to the same prime, independently of how far the next prime lies.

Lemma. Suppose $hge1$ and that for at least $delta X$ integers $min(X,2X]$ the block $(m,m+h]$ contains a prime while $(m+h,m+2h]$ contains none. Then at least $delta X/h$ distinct primes $pin(X,2X+h]$ have a successor $p^+$ with $p^+-p>h$.

Proof. For each such $m$ let $p(m)$ be the last prime in $(m,m+h]$. The second block is empty, so $p(m)^+>m+2hge p(m)+h$. For a fixed prime $p$ counted this way the condition $m<ple m+h$ forces $p-hle mle p-1$, an interval of exactly $h$ integers. Thus each prime is selected at most $h$ times, and the number of distinct primes is at least $delta X/h$.

Lemma (singular series in boxes). Fix an integer $sge0$ and a constant $Kge1$. For each positive integer $h$ let $I_1,...,I_s$ be intervals of $h$ consecutive integers, all contained in an interval of diameter at most $Kh$. Then, as $hto∞$,
$$sum_{substack{a_iin I_i (1le ile s)a_1,...,a_s distinct}}mathfrak S(frakS({a_1,...,a_s})=h^s(1+o(1)),$$

For a distinct tuple put $Delta=prod_{i<j}|a_i-a_j|$, a positive integer. For $y>2s$ every factor of the tail $T_y(mathbf a)=bfa)=prod_{p>y}(1-nu_p/p)(1-1/p)^{-s}$ is positive. First the smoothness and support. Extend each $f_j$ by zero to $mathbb R^j$.bbR^j$. Building such a family uses product profiles cut to a simplex, with truncation controlled by moments of the squared density; such profiles occur in Maynard's sieve construction. The extra ingredient is to place the profiles at a short range of dimensions near an alternating boundary, so that neighbouring levels nearly cancel.

Lemma (one-dimensional profile). For every $lambda>0$ and $beta>0$ there is a nonnegative $gin C_c^∞((0,∞))$ with

Proposition (cancellation between high dimensions). For every fixed $lambda>0$ there are families of the type used in the weight construction, with increasing finite maximum dimension $k$, for which $wto1$ and $vto0$.

Proof of the weight proposition. Fix $lambda>0$ and one function $G$ with the stated properties, and set $K_lambda=C_*=lambda+lambda^2c_G^2$, a value independent of $ε$. Given $ε>0$, choose $0<beta<gamma<tau$, a profile $g$ from the profile lemma and a matching cutoff $chi$; by the cancellation proposition a single finite $k$ and its family satisfy $w>0$ and $v/wleε$. Freezing this family and setting $W_X=Z^2/w$, all conclusions follow from the moment proposition by dividing by $w$, or by $w^2$ for the second moment. Once the family is chosen in terms of $lambda$ and $ε$, the fixed-family second-moment bound reads $O_{lambda,ε}(1)$.

The order of the two limits is essential: the auxiliary limit $kto∞$ produces one family, and only afterwards does $Xto∞$. No divisor-sum asymptotic uniform in $k$ is needed. For a fixed threshold $C>0$, $lambda$ was fixed with $lambda>max{1,C}$ before any of these choices were made. This completes the proof of the theorem and, with it, of the corollary.
