Fix a binary string x=x_1... x_nin{0,1}^n. A deletion trace
is the random string produced by the following experiment: walk through
the positions 1,2,...,n; keep position i with probability p
and drop it with probability q=1-p, independently at each i; the
kept bits, in their original order, form the trace. For example, if
x=1011 and the deletion removes positions 2 and 4, the resulting
trace is 11. The trace reports
its own length and its bits, but nothing about which original positions
survived. An algorithm is handed n, the value p (as a rational
number), and a stream of independent traces of x, and must output
x. The goal is an algorithm that succeeds with probability at least 2/3
for every x (a worst-case guarantee), while minimizing the
two resources: how many traces it reads, and how many bit operations it
performs.

If the trace were the input up to independent bit flips, one could
denoise one coordinate at a time. Here the trouble is different: the
same trace rank can come from many different input positions as the
random deletions shift everything. So the desired positions are
``smeared out'', and local statistics do not suffice. What rescues the problem is
a family of carefully chosen nonlocal statistics (products of
bits at ranks that may be far apart) together with a way to test, bit
by bit, whether a partial guess is consistent with the data.

Let nge 2 and let p=a/bin(0,1] be a rational in lowest terms.
Write L for the total number of bits in a and b when written in
binary, so L is the length of the honest input to an algorithm.
Write q=1-p. All logarithms are natural unless a base is shown.
Define the budget proxy


  X(n,p)sfX(n,p) &=
  
    1+(log n)/(1+log(1/q)), & q>0,[6pt]
    1, & q=0,
  [4pt]
  B(n,p) &= p^{-1},(log n),X(n,p)^2sfX(n,p)^2,
            (1+log X(n,p))^6sfX(n,p))^6 .



[Uniform trace reconstruction]
There are absolute constants C,cge 1 and one randomized multitape
Turing machine A with the following property. Take any
nge 2, any rational pin(0,1], and any xin{0,1}^n. Given
n and p in binary and access to independent deletion traces of x
with retention probability p, the machine
[label=(roman*)]
 reads at most M_C(n,p)=bigllceil exp(C,B(n,p))bigrrceil
      traces,
 outputs a string in {0,1}^n, and
 returns x with probability at least 2/3.

Moreover, for every internal sequence of random bits and
every stream of well-formed traces of strings of length at most
n, the machine halts within
[
  (n+L+M_C(n,p))^c
]
bit operations. This count already includes reading every trace bit
and every delimiter, all preprocessing, all finite-precision arithmetic,
and writing the output. The only thing not counted is the external
generation of the traces.


For a fixed p, plugging q>0 constant into (eq:budget) gives
X(n,p)=O(sfX(n,p)=O(log n) and hence
B(n,p)=O_p((log n)^3 (1+loglog n)^6). Since
M_C=exp(CB), the number of traces is
exp(O_p((log n)^3(1+loglog n)^6)), which is
quasipolynomial in n. The running time bound
(n+L+M_C)^c is then also quasipolynomial. So both resources are
quasipolynomial for fixed p. When instead qle n^{-kappa} for a
fixed kappa>0, one has 1+log(1/q)ge kappalog n, so
X(n,p)sfX(n,p)le 1+1/kappa is bounded and p^{-1} is bounded by a
constant depending only on kappa; then B(n,p)=O_kappa(log n)
and everything is polynomial in n.

The proof is one long chain. The decoder is tested against a family of
positive moment functionals, a convex relaxation of the set of genuine
probability laws on strings. The heart of the argument is a
separation theorem: any positive moment functional that insists
on a wrong bit at some position must disagree, by an exponentially
small but nonzero amount, with one of the observable trace statistics.
That is an analytic fact, proved by moving a local signal out to large
distance scales and then rotating it into the trace generating
function. Turning separation into an algorithm then tests one bit at a
time using exact rational feasibility, with a bound on the cost. The
lineage of these ideas comes next.

Reconstructing a sequence from corrupted copies has a long
information-theoretic history. Levenshtein studied reconstruction under
several combinatorial and probabilistic channel models. Batu, Kannan,
Khanna, and McGregor brought reconstruction from independent random
deletion traces into theoretical computer science, motivated partly by
sequence alignment. What separates the deletion channel from
coordinatewise denoising is that a single trace rank can originate at
many input positions.

doi.org/10.1109/18.904499

For a fixed deletion probability, Holenstein, Mitzenmacher, Panigrahy,
and Wieder proved a worst-case sample bound of exp(widetilde
O(sqrt n)). In independent STOC 2017 papers, De, O'Donnell, and
Servedio, and separately Nazarov and Peres, proved
exp(O(n^{1/3})) bounds together with matching limitations for
methods that use only trace means. Their complex-analytic approach ties
deletion statistics to bounded-coefficient polynomials, with analytic
ineualities of Borwein and Erd'elyi in the background. That
mean-based lower bound restricts a method, not arbitrary trace
algorithms.

Sample complexity and reconstruction time need different arguments. At
fixed confidence, maximum likelihood reconstructs worst-case using at
most O(n) times the optimal sample count, but evaluating it over all
candidate strings requires a large search. Burudgunte, Valiant, and
Wang explicitly separate testing two known strings from reconstructing
an unknown string, and leave quasipolynomial-time reconstruction open
for their approach. The extra separation proved later concerns every
positive semidefinite moment completion of a wrong prefix. It enables
constructive feasibility tests in place of a search over complete
strings. Earlier polynomial-time results already cover low-deletion
regimes.

The positive moment relaxation is a Boolean instance of the semidefinite
moment and sum-of-squares framework. The finite feasibility iteration is
related to the classical relaxation methods of Agmon and
Motzkin--Schoenberg. The analytic separation and the rational
implementation are proved directly; no external trace-reconstruction or
optimization theorem is imported.

The decoder measures expected products of a few trace ranks and tests them
against a convex family. Everything the algorithm ever sees is a
trace statistic: an expected product of a few trace ranks. Because
the input positions behind those ranks are unknown, the algorithm
treats these statistics as a system of linear equations with a
semidefinite (positivity) condition, and solves for a completion
of a partial guess. That relaxation is set up first, along with the
separation theorem that makes it useful.

Throughout, [n]={1,...,n} and X_1,...,X_n are formal Boolean
variables standing for the unknown bits. Products are always reduced by
X_i^2=X_i (a bit times itself is itself), so every polynomial has
degree at most the number of distinct variables it mentions.

Fix a nonempty set of ranks I={ell_1<...<ell_k}⊆[n]. The
corresponding trace observable is the product of the bits that sit
at those ranks in a single trace; if the trace is shorter than ell_k,
the observable is 0. Let T_I(X) be its expectation over the
deletion channel, viewed as a polynomial in the input bits:

  T_I(X)=sum_{i_1<...<i_k} c_{I;,i_1,...,i_k},X_{i_1}... X_{i_k}.

The coefficient c_{I;,i_1,...,i_k} is the probability that the
input positions i_1,...,i_k land at trace ranks
ell_1,...,ell_k. As the input tuple varies these events are
disjoint, so

  c_{I;,i_1,...,i_k}ge 0,
  sum_{i_1<...<i_k} c_{I;,i_1,...,i_k}le 1.

Thus T_I has degree at most k, nonnegative coefficients, and
coefficient sum at most one; its coefficients are rational when p is
rational. The algorithm estimates T_I(x) by averaging the observable
over independent traces. It never learns the coefficients' input
positions from the data, only the numeric value.

The following Boolean moment relaxation is a special case of the
semidefinite moment and sum-of-squares frameworks of
Lasserre and Parrilo. Only the defining positivity condition and its
elementary consequences are used, all proved below.

Fix a proposed prefix uin{0,1}^d and a degree bound Dge 1. On the
remaining positions U={d+1,...,n} introduce sign variables
Y_i=2X_i-1, so that Y_i^2=1 and X_i=(1+Y_i)/2. A moment
array assigns real numbers m_J to subsets J⊆ U with
|J|le 2D, with m_∅=1, subject to the single
positive-semidefiniteness condition

  M_D(m)=(m_{I△ J})_{substack{I,J⊆ U
     |I|,|J|le D}}succeq 0,

where △ is symmetric difference. Only subsets that actually
exist are indexed, so the definition still makes sense when D>|U| or
U is empty.

The array defines a functional EE on polynomials of degree at most
2D as follows: substitute X_i=u_i for ile d; expand each remaining
bit as (1+Y_i)/2; reduce Y_i^2=1; then set
EE[prod_{iin J}Y_i]=m_J and extend complex-linearly. Call
u|->EE a positive moment functional of order 2D fixing
u. The word ``positive'' asserts (eq:moment-matrix), not the
existence of an actual probability law realising all these moments; any
feasible array, even a fictitious one, is allowed in the test.

[Elementary moment bounds]
For every array obeying (eq:moment-matrix):
[label=(alph*)]
 abs{m_J}le 1 for every |J|le 2D;
 under EE, the value of any product of at most 2D distinct
      input bits has magnitude at most one;
 on polynomials of degree at most D, the form
      < P,R>_{EE}=EE[P,overlineoverline R]R] is a positive
      semidefinite Hermitian form, linear in its first argument; it
      satisfies Cauchy--Schwarz and becomes an inner product after
      quotienting by its zero-norm subspace.



Proof. 
The empty moment equals one. Any nonempty J with |J|le 2D splits as
J=Isqcup K with |I|,|K|le D. In (eq:moment-matrix) the
I,K principal submatrix has diagonal entries one and off-diagonal
entry m_J; positive semidefiniteness of a real symmetric matrix with
unit diagonal forces |m_J|le 1. This is (a). For (b), write each
unfixed bit as (1+Y_i)/2; a product of t unfixed bits becomes
2^{-t}prod(1+Y_i), a convex combination of sign monomials of the
Y_i, each of modulus at most one by (a); fixed bits simply remove
factors or send the product to zero. For (c), the Gram-form reading of
the real symmetric positive semidefinite matrix (eq:moment-matrix),
extended to complex coefficient vectors, gives the stated form;
Cauchy--Schwarz follows by applying positivity to P+zR for zinCC,
and it shows every zero-norm vector is orthogonal to every vector, so
the quotient is well defined.

  > exp(-C_{sep},B(n,p)).

The theorem holds for every feasible array, including arrays that
no distribution realises. Its proof runs through the construction of a slowly decaying signal, the
transfer of that signal to trace statistics, and the decoder that turns
the resulting separation into an algorithm. Once separation holds,
accurate empirical trace moments rule out every wrong extension of any
prefix, because a wrong prefix would force a feasible array that violates
such a statistic. A bounded rational computation then finds the feasible
extension.

The separation proof is analytic, so the objects it uses come first.
Fix x,d,EE as above. For 1le kle 2D an order-k feature is
a family of polynomials indexed by the last position j,

  f_j=sum_{i_1<...<i_k=j}
      X_{i_1}... X_{i_k}
      prod_{h=1}^{k-1} z_h^{,i_{h+1}-i_h},
   jin[n],

where z_h=exp(-a_h+iitheta_h) with a_h>0 and
theta_hinRR. For k=1 this is just f_j=X_j. Each f_j sums over
increasing tuples of input positions with exponential weights on the
gaps. Put

  H_f=prod_{h=1}^{k-1}(1+frac1{a_h}).

Summing geometric series over the positive gaps and using
(1-e^{-a})^{-1}le 1+1/a shows that H_fge 1 bounds the sum of the
absolute coefficients of every f_j.

For s>0 and omegainRR, the signal of the feature is the
exponentially damped Fourier sum

  S_f(s,omega)
  =sum_{j=1}^n Delta[f_j],e^{(-s+iiomega)(j-d)}.

Every monomial in f_j uses positions at most j, so
Delta[f_j]=0 for j<d; hence, despite the exponent being centred at
d, only jge d contribute. For the base case k=1, where f_j=X_j,
this reads S_f(s,0)=S_f(s,0)=sum_{jge d}Delta[X_j],e^{-s(j-d)}, whose
first term is Delta[X_d]=pm1; that single term is what makes the
starting signal about 1/2 at decay s=4, as made precise in
(eq:initial-signal). The whole multiscale argument has one goal: to preserve a detectable signal
S_f(s,omega) while shrinking both s and |omega| to
O(1/n), at the cost of raising the order k by at most a factor of
four per step.

The signal (eq:signal) is a damped sum: it heavily discounts
positions more than about 1/s beyond the first disagreement. The next
proposition moves a detectable signal to a much smaller decay parameter
S', at the price of letting the order grow by at most a factor of four.
The overall loss in the logarithm of the signal is kept below a factor
of eight. Controlling the angles of the new gap parameters is
equally important: those angle bounds later allow passage from input
coordinates to trace coordinates. Enlarging distance scales while
raising the order follows the strategy of Burudgunte, Valiant, and Wang, but here the comparison functional may be a truncated
positive moment functional, and the scale step uses Gram matrices of
polynomial vectors.

[Cauchy matching]
Let w(v)=1/(pi(1+v^2)). For every rge0 there is a finite positive
measure mu_r on RR^2, supported on v-v'=r, whose two marginals
sum to at most w(v),dv. For r>0 its total mass is

 2mu_r(RR^2)=1-frac4piarctan(e^{-pi/r});

for r=0 its total mass is 1/2. In particular
(eq:cauchy-matching-mass) exceeds 0.737 for 0<rle2.


Proof.

partition the line into chains v+jr, jinZ, with
-r/2le v<r/2. Write w_j=w(v+jr). On the edge from j to j-1, for
jge1, put weight
[

]
These weights are nonnegative because the summands decrease along the
tail, and e_j+e_{j+1}=w_j, so the edges exhaust the weight at every
positive vertex. Build the negative-side edges the same way, from the
negative tail toward zero. The only possibly unused weight sits at the
central vertex, with value
[
 R(v)=sum_{jinZ}-1)^j w(v+jr).
]

subtract its translate by r. By (eq:cauchy-transform), with

[

One old inner edge can cross several gaps of the merged tuple, so

For the fixed interleaving write its distinct positions as
i_1<...<i_v with 1le vle mk. Form a multigraph on these
vertices: include the inner edges of each old tuple and the links
between consecutive endpoints in (eq:endpoint-link), each edge

tuple is a path to its endpoint and the endpoint links join those paths.

For each merged gap i_{a+1}-i_a, multiply the parameters of all edges
crossing it. Each edge length is the sum of the merged gaps it crosses,
so the new parameters reproduce the original weight exactly. Every
merged gap is crossed by some edge, by connectivity, so its damping is
positive. Within one old tuple the inner-edge intervals have disjoint
interiors, and the same holds for the consecutive endpoint links. It
follows that each new parameter uses at most one old parameter from each
copy and at most one added endpoint parameter. This is exactly the
factor-count assertion of Proposition~prop:multiscale.

Theorem~thm:separation gives a test for the first wrong bit of a
prefix: no moment system that fixes that prefix can match all the
low-order trace statistics. To turn this into an algorithm, the construction must also
recognise a correct prefix in bounded time. The extra fact that
makes this possible is that a correct prefix admits a moment system with
strict slack, both in its statistical constraints and in its positivity
condition. That slack is exhibited first, then a rational procedure is given
that is guaranteed to find a feasible system whenever slack exists.

The next lemma gives the bounded procedure for
(eq:prefix-feasibility). It only promises to succeed when there is
slack, and every reported solution satisfies the exact ineualities. This
sidesteps deciding arbitrary semidefinite feasibility. The procedure is a
projected relaxation method in the tradition of Agmon and
Motzkin--Schoenberg; the iteration and its rounding analysis are given
explicitly.

[Rational feasibility with slack]
Let rge0 and Dge1 be integers, and let m consist of the
[
 l=sum_{j=1}^{min(2D,r)}binom rj
]
nonempty sign moments. Let M(m) be their moment matrix, of dimension
[
 G=sum_{j=0}^{min(D,r)}binom rj.
]
Suppose a finite list of rational affine ineualities ell_t(m)le b_t
is given, each with nonconstant coefficient vector of ell^1-norm at
most one, together with a dyadic 0<zetale1/4. Then a deterministic
rational procedure either returns a vector with
[
 min[-1,1]^l, M(m)succeq0, ell_t(m)le b_t for all t,
]
or reports failure. It returns a vector whenever some m^*in[-1,1]^l
satisfies
[
 M(m^*)succeq(zeta/8)Id_G,
 ell_t(m^*)le b_t-zeta/2 for all t.
]
Put lambda=zeta/(16G). The procedure performs at most
lceil10(1+l)/lambda^2rceil feasibility checks, each taking polynomially
many bit operations in the list dimensions, the input bit lengths, and
log(1/zeta). These time bounds hold without the slack assumption.


Proof. 
If l=0 the matrix is [1] and all ineualities are constant, so direct
rational comparisons suffice. Suppose l>0. Exact separating
directions, then an iteration with bounded precision.

{itshape Directions from violated constraints.}
At a current point m^{cur}in[-1,1]^l first check the affine
ineualities and the matrix condition exactly. If all hold, return the
current vector. A violated ineuality ell_t(m^{cur})>b_t gives
the direction a equal to minus its nonconstant coefficient vector. Then
norm a_2le1 and, under the slack assumption,
< m^*-m^{cur},a>gezeta/2gelambda.

If instead the matrix condition fails, find a nonzero rational vector v
with v^top M(m^{cur})v<0 (constructed just below). Write the
associated affine form as v^top M(m)v=c+h· m and set
a=h/(G,v^top v). Each matrix entry is the constant 1 or one moment
coordinate, so norm h_1lenorm v_1^2le Gnorm v_2^2 and
norm a_2le1. The slack matrix bound gives
[
 < m^*-m^{cur},a>
 =frac{v^top(M(m^*)-M(m^{cur}))v}{G,v^top v}
 ge(zeta)/(8G)gelambda.
]
Thus either violated condition yields a rational direction with

 norm a_2le1, < m^*-m^{cur},a>gelambda

whenever a slack solution exists. A violated constant ineuality has zero
direction; that cannot happen in the slack case, and otherwise the
iteration is still bounded.

{itshape An exact matrix check.}
For a rational symmetric matrix, eliminate a positive diagonal pivot via
its Schur complement. Congruence by an invertible triangular matrix shows
the original is positive semidefinite exactly when the positive pivot and
this Schur complement are. A negative diagonal gives a negative unit
vector. If a remaining diagonal at i is zero and its entry b in column
j is nonzero, let c be the diagonal at j; assume cge0,
else a negative diagonal already exists. Then
[
 y=e_i-(b)/(1+c+|b|)e_j
]
has quadratic form
(b^2)/((1+c+|b|)^2)[-2(1+c+|b|)+c]<0. Otherwise continue
with a positive diagonal pivot; when none remain, all entries are zero and
the remainder is positive semidefinite. A negative vector of a Schur
complement lifts to one of the original matrix using only rational
arithmetic: with P the eliminated block and B its block against the
remaining indices, the lift of y is v=(-P^{-1}By,,y), whose quadratic
form equals that of y for the Schur complement.

These operations have polynomial bit complexity. Clear denominators in a
rational matrix with t-bit entries; the product of all denominators has
at most G^2t bits. If H is the resulting integer matrix with s-bit
entries, the determinant expansion bounds every j-row minor by
j!,2^{js}, of bit length O(G(s+log G)). Schur-complement entries are
ratios of minors, and the inverse formula for P gives the same bound for
the lift. Fraction-reduced elimination with rational comparisons computes
the check and the negative vector in polynomially many bit operations,
however small a positive pivot or negative quadratic form may be.

{itshape Iteration and rounding.}
Start with m^{cur}=0. Choose the dyadic grid with mesh
[
 eta=2^{-g},
 g=Bigllceillog_2(100(1+l))/(lambda^2)Bigrrceil.
]
The integer g is computed by rational comparisons with powers of two, so
etalelambda^2/(100(1+l)), with O(1+log((1+l)/lambda)) fractional
bits. After an unsuccessful check, add lambda a, clip every coordinate
to [-1,1], and round to the nearest grid point in the box (both box
endpoints lie on the grid). Repeat up to the stated number of checks,
reporting failure if none accepts.

Under the slack assumption (eq:feasibility-direction) gives
[
 norm{m^{cur}+lambda a-m^*}_2^2
 lenorm{m^{cur}-m^*}_2^2-lambda^2.
]
Clipping cannot increase distance to m^*; since both the clipped vector
and m^* lie in the box, rounding changes the squared distance by at most
4leta+leta^2<lambda^2/2. Each failed check thus decreases squared
distance by at least lambda^2/2, whose initial value is at most l, so
acceptance occurs before the cap. Finally, rounding keeps every iterate on
a single grid of the indicated bit length; the exact matrix check, the
affine comparisons, forming and normalizing h, and one update involve
polynomially many rational operations on numbers of polynomial bit length,
and temporary denominators do not accumulate across updates. The fixed cap
and these bounds apply to every rational input list, with or without a
slack solution.


For a prefix test, write each absolute-value constraint of

verifies exactly the slack hypotheses for a correct prefix, and every
acceptance, on any data, gives an actual moment system satisfying
(eq:prefix-feasibility).

The decoder starts with the empty prefix and tests both one-bit
extensions. If precisely one accepts, it appends that bit; otherwise it
appends 0. It repeats until the prefix has length n. The latter rule
specifies a length-n output even on data where the statistical estimates
fail. There are at most 2n tests.

If the input representation is p=a/b, every denominator in
(eq:explicit-channel-coefficient) divides b^n. Binomial
coefficients have O(n) bits. Sign expansion adds a denominator dividing
2^D, and empirical means have denominator T. Hence the constraint
coefficients have polynomial bit length in n+L+D+log T. Enumerating the
rank and input tuples, expanding the resulting bit monomials, and
substituting the prefix constructs them directly; no list of complete
trace masks is needed.

These enumerations fit the resource scale. From D=O(Y^2),
YasympX(n,p),sfX(n,p), and (eq:budget),

 Dlog n=O(B(n,p)).

The rank list, every matrix basis, and every moment list have size at most
(n+1)^{2D}=exp(O(B(n,p))), and so do input tuple lists. A sign expansion
has at most 2^D terms; products of these enumerations remain
exp(O(B(n,p))). Subsets larger than the available positions are simply
absent, so this includes D>n and prefixes with very few remaining
positions.

By (eq:decoder-parameters), T is exp(O(B(n,p))). For every
prefix the iteration cap of Lemma~lem:rational-feasibility is at most
[
 2560(1+l)G^2zeta^{-2}+1=exp(O(B(n,p))),
]
and the bit length of its grid is O(b_zeta+log G+log(1+l))=O(B(n,p)).
The coefficient bounds and that lemma bound all computation by a fixed
polynomial in

 n+L+exp(O(B(n,p))),

including parameter and constraint construction, exact rational
arithmetic, and the at most 2n prefix tests.

Proof. [Proof of Theorem~thm:main]
If p=1, request one trace and output it. For the universal halting
specification, output 0^n if the supplied well-formed trace has a
different length; the actual channel always supplies the input itself,
and this branch takes polynomially many bit operations in n+L.

For p<1, use (eq:integer-decoder-parameters), request the T
traces, and compute the empirical statistics. Run the prefix rule. This
defines the machine on every stream of well-formed traces.

On G, induction on prefix length shows precisely the correct
extension accepts: the incorrect extension is infeasible by
Lemma~lem:prefix-slack, and the correct one has the slack that
guarantees acceptance. Thus the output is x on G, whose
probability is at least 31/32>2/3 for each fixed x.

Each trace has at most n bits and one delimiter. Reading the traces and
computing each of the finitely many rank observables costs polynomially
many bit operations in n+T+N; the empirical counters have O(log T)
bits. All loops, including all feasibility tests, have the input-dependent
caps already given. Thus (eq:decoder-total-resource) bounds every
well-formed trace stream, independently of the success event, and the
machine can ignore its internal random bits.

To pin the computational model, the finite lists are stored sequentially
on tapes; replacing an indexed lookup by a scan costs at most a polynomial
factor in their total encoded length. Integer addition, multiplication,
division, and fraction reduction have elementary polynomial-time multitape
implementations. The bit-length and loop bounds thus give absolute
constants K,d with trace count at most exp(KB(n,p)) and total bit cost
at most (n+L+exp(KB(n,p)))^d, after enlarging K,d to absorb fixed
factors. This enlargement is uniform because B(n,p)gelog2. Choose
absolute C,c with Cge K and cge d; both the sample bound M_C(n,p)
and the runtime (n+L+M_C(n,p))^c follow. All constants in parameter
selection are fixed integers in one finite program.
