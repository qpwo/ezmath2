# Parity lifts and bounded-treewidth witnesses
       for Weisfeiler--Leman equivalence




maketitle

Abstract: 
For $k ge 4$, we construct two uncolored graphs that are
$k$-dimensional Weisfeiler--Leman equivalent if and only if a
prescribed finite-domain choice system has no compatible choice. The
system uses $k+1$ domains under joint refinement and $k$ under
separate-coordinate refinement. A successful choice is detected
after two joint rounds or one separate round. Applied to sparse
satisfiability, the reduction gives fixed-dimension $n^{Ω(k)}$
time lower bounds under positive-rate ETH, even when the algorithm
is only required to decide equality of these early histograms.


# Introduction

## WL refinement: two conventions

Fix $k ge 4$ and a finite simple graph $X$. For an ordered tuple
$x = (x_1, ..., x_k) in V(X)^k$, the atomic type is the
complete list of equalities among coordinates and of adjacencies
between distinct equal coordinates. Every tuple starts at round $0$
with its atomic type as color. If $C_r$ is the color assigned at
round $r$ to every $k$-tuple, write $x[i ← y]$ for the
tuple in which position $i$ holds $y$ and every other position is
unchanged.

Two refinement rules are in use.

Convention J (joint).
[
C_{r+1}(x) = ( C_r(x), ;
bigl{!bigl{,
( C_r(x[1← y]), ..., C_r(x[k← y]) ) :
y in V(X) ,bigr}!bigr} ).
]
Every possible $y$ contributes one whole vector of replacement
colors; the multiset of all such vectors is recorded.

Convention S (separate).
[
C_{r+1}(x) = ( C_r(x), ;
{!{ C_r(x[1← y]) : y in V(X) }!}, ;...,;
{!{ C_r(x[k← y]) : y in V(X) }!} ).
]
Every possible $y$ contributes one color to each of $k$ separate
multisets.

In both cases $C_{r+1}(x)$ contains $C_r(x)$, so the tuple partition
only refines; it stabilizes after finitely many rounds. Two graphs
$X_0, X_1$ are $k$-WL equivalent if, refining the disjoint
union $X_0 sqcup X_1$, the pure histograms on $V(X_0)^k$ and
$V(X_1)^k$ match at every round.

The compatibility parameter is
[
t =  k+1, & (J), k, & (S). 
]
The whole construction is written in terms of $t$; readers only have
to check at the end of Section~sec:wl-tests which convention
gives which $t$.

## Choice systems

[Choice system]
A choice system of arity $t$ consists of finite domains
$D_1, ..., D_t$ (possibly empty), finite label sets $L_{ij}$ for
$1 le i < j le t$, and maps
[
lambda_{ij}^{i} colon D_i to L_{ij}, qquad
lambda_{ij}^{j} colon D_j to L_{ij}.
]
A successful choice is a tuple
$(d_1, ..., d_t) in prod_{i=1}^{t} D_i$ with
$lambda_{ij}^i(d_i) = lambda_{ij}^j(d_j)$ for all $i<j$.


The prototype: each $D_i$ is a set of finite Boolean functions on a
variable scope $V_i$, and both pair maps are restriction to
$V_i cap V_j$. Then a successful choice is exactly a family of local
assignments that glues into one global assignment, so identifying a
successful choice is a finite constraint satisfaction problem.

## Main theorem

[Parity reduction]
Fix $k ge 4$ and either convention, with $t$ as above. From every
choice system of arity $t$ one can explicitly construct two uncolored
simple graphs $X_0$, $X_star$ of equal order such that
[
X_0 equiv_k X_star
;;⇔;;
no successful choice exists.
]
The construction satisfies both of the following.
[nosep]
item Equivalence holds separately under common color names on the two
  graphs and under pure-tuple comparison in the disjoint union
  $X_0 sqcup X_star$.
item If every domain and label set has size at most $A$, the common
  order is at most $A M_t$ with
[
M_t = t,2^{,t-2+3binom{t-1}{2}} + 12binom{t}{3}.
]

When a successful choice exists, the histograms already differ after
at most two joint rounds, or after one separate round.


The order bound is on the actual output as uncolored graphs; the
decider receives no distinguished fibers and no residual colors.

## Construction, three layers

The graphs are built in three stages; the layers correspond to
Sections~sec:wl-tests, sec:lifts, sec:separator.

Template. Let $T = T_t$ have main vertices
$[t] = {1,...,t}$ forming a clique, plus helpers
$h_{ij;ell}$ for each $i<j$ and $ellnotin{i,j}$, each $h_{ij;ell}$
adjacent exactly to $i, j, ell$. Its bags
${i,j,ell,h_{ij;ell}}$ give bag size $t$ (so treewidth $t-1$).
It has
[
|V(T)| = t + 3binom{t}{3},qquad
deg_T(i) = t - 1 + 3binom{t-1}{2},qquad
deg_T(h_{ij;ell}) = 3.
]
In particular every vertex of $T$ has positive degree and $T$ is
connected.

Base graph. Build $G$ on type-labeled vertices
[
{(i,d) : i in [t], , d in D_i}
;cup;
{(h_{ij;ell}, q) : 1 le i < j le t,, ellnotin{i,j},, q in L_{ij}}.
]
Type map $tau colon V(G) to V(T)$ sends the $i$-layer to main
type $i$ and the $(h_{ij;ell},cdot)$-layer to helper $h_{ij;ell}$.

Edges of $G$:
[nosep]
item $(i,d) sim (j,e)$ iff $lambda_{ij}^i(d) = lambda_{ij}^j(e)$;
item $(h_{ij;ell}, q) sim (i,d)$ iff $q = lambda_{ij}^i(d)$;
item $(h_{ij;ell}, q) sim (j,e)$ iff $q = lambda_{ij}^j(e)$;
item $(h_{ij;ell}, q) sim (ell,f)$ for every $f in D_ell$ (no
  condition).

Then $G$ is simple and $tau$ is a graph homomorphism $G to T$.

Parity lift. For $b colon V(T) to FF$, the graph
$X_b$ has vertices $(u, z)$ with $u in V(G)$ and
$z colon N_T(tau(u)) to FF$ satisfying $sum z = b(tau(u))$.
Two vertices $(u,z)$ and $(v,w)$ are adjacent iff $uv in E(G)$ and
$z(tau(v)) = w(tau(u))$. Set $X_0 := X_b$ with $b equiv 0$ and
$X_star := X_b$ with $b = e_1$, the unit vector at main type 1.

The rest of the proof has two directions, each of which occupies its
own section.
[nosep]
item Section~sec:wl-tests: WL equivalence is the same as
  agreement on homomorphism counts from all sources of bag size
  $le t$ (Lemma~lem:hom-implies-wl), and the template $T$
  alone is a sufficient detector (Lemma~lem:test-detected).
item Section~sec:lifts: $hom(F,X_0) ge hom(F,X_star)$ for
  every source $F$, with strict inequality iff a primal-dual
  certificate exists; a successful choice produces such a certificate
  (Lemmas~lem:lift-dual and lem:choice-witness).
item Section~sec:separator: a certificate for a bag-$le t$
  source forces a successful choice. Contrapositive: no successful
  choice implies identical counts from every small-bag source,
  hence WL equivalence of $X_0$ and $X_star$.


# Weisfeiler--Leman and homomorphism counts

The main result has two halves. One is a statement of WL equivalence in
the language of homomorphism counts from small-treewidth sources. The
other is a detection claim: a single template $T$ suffices to see any
count difference in two joint rounds (or one separate round). We prove
both here.

Throughout $k ge 4$ and $t$ is as in Section~sec:wl-def.

## Bag size and the $k$-WL characterization

Fix a graph $F$. A tree decomposition of $F$ consists of a
finite tree $D$ and a function bag $colon V(D) to 2^{V(F)}$ with:
[nosep]
item for every $x in V(F)$, the set ${c : x in bag(c)}$
  is a nonempty subtree of $D$;
item for every edge $xy in E(F)$, some bag contains both $x$ and $y$.

The bag size is the maximum bag cardinality over $c in V(D)$.
Equivalently, the treewidth of $F$ is bag size minus one.

We use $hom(F, X)$ for the number of graph homomorphisms
$phi colon V(F) to V(X)$, i.e. vertex maps sending every edge of
$F$ to an edge of $X$; nonedges may be mapped to edges or nonedges.


If $hom(F, X_0) = hom(F, X_1)$ for every finite simple graph $F$
with a tree decomposition of bag size at most $t$, then $X_0$ and
$X_1$ are $k$-WL equivalent in the corresponding convention
(J: $t = k+1$; S: $t = k$). The assertion also holds when WL is run
on the disjoint union $X_0 sqcup X_1$ and only the two pure-tuple
histograms are compared.


The proof is a self-contained adaptation of the rooted-count argument
of Dvov{r}'ak (2010) and Dell--Grohe--Rattan (2018). We spell it out
because we also need the disjoint-union version.

Proof. 
For each finite graph $F$, each list of $k$ (not necessarily distinct)
roots $boldsymbolell = (ell_1, ..., ell_k) in V(F)^k$, and each
target graph $X$, define
[
h_F(X; mathbf x)bfx) ;=; #{phi colon V(F) to V(X) : phi  homomorphism,  phi(ell_i) = x_i}.
]
This is a function $V(X)^k to ℕ$. Fix a finite set
$mathcal F_t$calF_t$ of bag-$le t$ graphs together with root lists of
length $k$ whose roots are jointly inside one bag. Let
$mathcal A_t$calA_t$ be the rational span of the corresponding functions
${ h_F(cdot; mathbf x)bfx) : F in mathcal F_tcalF_t }$ on the two
fixed targets $X_0, X_1$.
We prove two facts about $mathcal A_t$.calA_t$.

(A1) $mathcal A_t$calA_t$ contains every atomic-type indicator on
$X_j$-tuples. Three elementary sources:
[nosep]
item distinct isolated roots give the constant $1$;
item two equal roots give the indicator of the equality $x_i = x_k$;
item joining two roots by an edge of $F$ gives the indicator of
  adjacency $x_i x_k$. Other adjacencies are recovered from these by
  products and complements.

Products and complements stay in $mathcal A_t$:calA_t$: any two rooted
sources can be glued by identifying corresponding roots (formal
central bag of size $k$; never exceeds $t$ when $t ge k$) and the
result has bag size at most $max(bag_1, bag_2, k) le t$.

(A2) $mathcal A_t$calA_t$ is closed under both refinement operations.
Consider a tuple $(f_1, ..., f_k)$ of elements of $mathcal A_t$,calA_t$,
each represented by some rooted source. Define
[
Phi_{mathrm J}rmJ (f_1, ..., f_k)(X; mathbf x)bfx)
  = sum_{y in V(X)} , prod_{i=1}^k f_i(X; mathbf x[ibfx[i ← y]).
]
We produce a rooted source for this: take a formal central bag
${u_1, ..., u_k, y}$ of size $k+1 = t$. Attach operand source
$i$ at its root list, sending $ell_j mapsto u_j$ for $j ne i$ and
$ell_i mapsto y$. The identifications force repeated roots to
become single vertices. A homomorphism out of the glued source
uniquely picks $y$'s image once and then independently a
homomorphism from each operand, restricted to reproduce
$mathbf x[ibfx[i ← y]$ on the appropriate side. Multilinearity
gives the general case.
For convention S, use instead two adjacent bags
$O = {u_1, ..., u_k}$ and $R = {y, u_j : j ne i}$, each of
size $k = t$; attach operand $i$'s root bag to $R$. A check at a
merged vertex shows the resulting function is
[
Phi_{mathrm S}rmS^i (f)(X; mathbf x)bfx)
  = sum_{y in V(X)} f(X; mathbf x[ibfx[i ← y]).
]

Induction. Fix $r ge 0$. Suppose that, in the round-$r$
refinement, each color $C_r(mathbf x)$bfx)$ restricted to tuples of each
fixed $X_j$ is an element of $mathcal A_t$calA_t$ (for convention J the
"color" is understood as the whole hash value, which we think of as a
tuple of independent atomic-type indicators plus the recursively
produced higher-level indicators).

If a color multiplicity $M$ has previously attained values
$S subset ℕ$ on the two targets, then the indicator of
"$M = q$" for any $q in S$ is the polynomial
[
1_{M = q} ;=; prod_{s in S ∖ {q}} (M - s)/(q - s),
]
which involves only the values $s in S$. These coefficients are
identical for $X_0$ and $X_1$. Now, the $(r+1)$-st color of $mathbf x$bfx$
records:
- in J, the multiset of vectors $(C_r(mathbf x[1bfx[1 ← y]),
  ..., C_r(mathbf x[kbfx[k ← y]))$ over all $y$; equivalently
  the count
  $sum_{y} prod_{i} 1_{C_r(mathbf x[ibfx[i ← y]) = c_i}$
  for each candidate color vector $(c_1, ..., c_k)$;
- in S, the $k$ independent multiplicities
  $#{y : C_r(mathbf x[ibfx[i ← y]) = c_i}$.
Both are produced from elements of $mathcal A_t$calA_t$ by the closures
(A1) and (A2) -- interpolation identifies the indicators, and the
$Phi$ operators sum them over $y$. Then multiply by the old-color
indicator (which stays in $mathcal A_t$calA_t$ by (A1) applied to $C_r$).
Coefficients are common to $X_0$ and $X_1$.

Consequently each round-$r$, tuple-level color indicator on $V(X_j)^k$
lies in $mathcal A_t$,calA_t$, with coefficients independent of $j$.

Summing over tuples. For any rooted source $F$,
$sum_{x in V(X_j)^k} h_F(X_j; mathbf x)bfx) = hom(F, X_j)$:
each homomorphism contributes exactly one root-image tuple. Applying
this to every term of a color indicator yields
$#{mathbf xbfx : C_r(mathbf x)bfx) = c} = $ (a linear combination of
$hom(F, X_j)$ for $F in mathcal F_t$).calF_t$). Equality of the hypotheses
$hom(F, X_0) = hom(F, X_1)$ for all $F$ then gives equality of
histograms at every round $r$, hence $k$-WL equivalence.

Disjoint union. For $U = X_0 sqcup X_1$, the same induction
runs on all tuples of $U$, mixed tuples included. Interpolation is
performed once. At the end, for each remaining source $F$, split its
connected components into two classes: $mathcal L$calL$ (containing at
least one root) and $mathcal R$calR$ (rootless). Then
[
sum_{mathbf xbfx in V(X_j)^k} h_F(U; mathbf x)bfx)
  = prod_{Q in mathcal L}calL hom(Q, X_j) ;cdot
    prod_{Q in mathcal R}calR hom(Q, U).
]
A connected graph with a root must land in one component of $U$, and
if $Q$ is rooted and the root image is in $X_j$, the whole image lies
in $X_j$. Rootless components are free. Components inherit the bag
bound. Thus the first product is the same for $j = 0$ and $j = 1$
under the hypothesis, and the second is common by definition.


## The template is a sufficient detector


The template $T = T_t$ has main vertices $[t]$ forming a
clique, and a helper $h_{ij;ell}$ for each $i < j$ and
$ell notin {i, j}$, adjacent precisely to $i, j, ell$.


Its main-clique bag with one attached bag ${i,j,ell,h_{ij;ell}}$
per helper has bag size $t$. The vertex count and degrees are as in
Section~sec:layers.


The round-two joint-$k$-WL color histogram determines
$hom(T_{k+1}, X)$ for every finite simple graph $X$. The round-one
separate-$k$-WL color histogram determines $hom(T_k, X)$. The same
claims hold for the pure histograms obtained by refining a disjoint
union $X_0 sqcup X_1$.


Proof. 
Let $A_X(v, w) = ind{vw in E(X)} in {0, 1}$. For main images
$mathbf zbfz = (z_1, ..., z_t) in V(X)^t$ and triples
$B in binom{[t]}{3}$, put
[
q_B(mathbf z)bfz) = |∩_{b in B} N_X(z_b)|.
]
Then the number of extensions of $mathbf z$bfz$ to a homomorphism
$T to X$ is

E_X(mathbf z)bfz) = prod_{a<b} A_X(z_a, z_b) cdot
                 prod_{B in binom{[t]}{3}} q_B(mathbf z)^3.bfz)^3.

The clique factor rejects repeated main images (a loop is not an
edge and $A_X(v,v) = 0$). Each helper has three neighbors in $T$:
$i$, $j$, $ell$ for the helper $h_{ij;ell}$. A helper image can be
any common neighbor of $z_i, z_j, z_ell$, and these three coordinates
are chosen independently over all copies of the same triple, giving
$q_B^3$.

Joint round 2. Fix $mathbf xbfx = (z_1, ..., z_k) in V(X)^k$
and $y in V(X)$, and put $z_{k+1} = y$. Consider its round-one color:
- the old color $C_1(mathbf x)$bfx)$ of the $k$-tuple;
- the multiset of replacement-color $k$-vectors
  $(C_1(mathbf x[1←bfx[1← y]), ..., C_1(mathbf x[k←bfx[k← y]))$
  over all $y$.

Each replacement $k$-vector is the round-one color of $k$ vertices
$z_1, ..., z_{i-1}, y, z_{i+1}, ..., z_k$. So the round-one old
color and all faces together cover the round-one colors of all
$(k+1)$-vertex tuples with at most one coordinate equal to $y$ and the
rest as in $mathbf x$.bfx$.

Key observation: for any triple $B ⊆ [k+1]$, some face
$i notin B$ omits the position that is not used, and the round-one
color of that face determines $q_B(mathbf z)$.bfz)$. Indeed, at round one,
a $k$-tuple's color already determines the common-neighbor count of
any triple of its positions: for the triple $(p, q, r)$ inside the
face, the atomic replacement color of the $k$-tuple reveals
simultaneous adjacency to $p, q, r$, and summing multiplicities gives
the count. (Repeated positions are handled by the equality bits
inside the atomic type.)

Letting $i$ range over $[k+1]$, all pairs and triples are covered
when $k ge 4$: a set of size at most three is missed by some
position. Therefore the round-two multiset determines
$sum_y E_X(mathbf x,bfx, y)$ for each $mathbf x$.bfx$. Summing over
$mathbf x$bfx$ gives $hom(T_{k+1}, X)$.

Separate round 1. All $t = k$ main images are already fixed
in $mathbf x$.bfx$. For a triple $B$, choose any $i notin B$. Let
$M_i(sigma)$ be the multiplicity of atomic type $sigma$ in the
$i$-th coordinate's replacement multiset at round one. Then
[
q_B(mathbf x)bfx) = sum_sigma M_i(sigma) prod_{p in B} A_sigma(i, p),
]
where $A_sigma(i, p)$ is the $i$-$p$ adjacency bit recorded in $sigma$.
Thus the round-one color determines $E_X(mathbf x)$,bfx)$, and summing over
$mathbf x$bfx$ gives $hom(T_k, X)$.

Disjoint union. Take a pure tuple $mathbf xbfx in V(X_j)^k$
inside $U = X_0 sqcup X_1$. In the joint case, if some main image
$z_a$ is in the other component, then $A_U(z_a, z_b) = 0$ for any
other main image $z_b$, and (eq:extend) is zero; helper images
do not rescue this. So the nonzero summands have all main images
inside $X_j$, and common neighbors of triples lie in $X_j$ as well.
The same formulas, evaluated in $U$, thus count exactly
$hom(T_{k+1}, X_j)$. The separate case is identical.


# Parity lifts and F$_2$ duality

We now build the two uncolored graphs $X_0$ and $X_star$ from a choice
system. The construction has two parts: a base graph $G$ whose type
map $tau$ targets the template $T$, and a parity lift that replaces
each base vertex by a finite set of bit-vectors.

## The base graph

Let $t ge 4$ be the parameter from Section~sec:wl-def. Given a
choice system of arity $t$ (Definition~def:choice), build $G$ on
disjoint vertex sets
[
{(i, d) : d in D_i}  (i in [t]),
qquad
{(h_{ij;ell}, q) : q in L_{ij}}  (i<j, ell notin {i,j}).
]
The type map $taucolon V(G) to V(T)$ sends $(i, d) mapsto i$ and
$(h_{ij;ell}, q) mapsto h_{ij;ell}$.

Edges of $G$:
[nosep]
item $(i, d) sim (j, e)$ iff $lambda_{ij}^{i}(d) = lambda_{ij}^{j}(e)$;
item $(h_{ij;ell}, q) sim (i, d)$ iff $q = lambda_{ij}^{i}(d)$;
item $(h_{ij;ell}, q) sim (j, e)$ iff $q = lambda_{ij}^{j}(e)$;
item $(h_{ij;ell}, q) sim (ell, f)$ for every $f in D_ell$
  (unconditional).

$G$ is simple. $tau$ is a graph homomorphism $G to T$: main-main
edges match the clique edges, and helper edges match the three
neighbor relations in $T$.

Interpretation: a successful choice $(..., d_i, ...)$ with
common pair-label $lambda_{ij}^i(d_i) = lambda_{ij}^j(d_j)$ yields a
homomorphism $T to G$ by sending $i mapsto (i, d_i)$ and
$h_{ij;ell} mapsto (h_{ij;ell}, lambda_{ij}^i(d_i))$. This is the
"witness" alluded to in Section~sec:layers.

## The parity lift

Recall that $FF = {0, 1}$, the two-element field. For integers
$r, c ge 0$, a binary linear system is a matrix
$M in FF^{r times c}$ together with a right-hand side
$c in FF^r$; we ask whether there exists $z in FF^c$ with
$Mz = c$. Its solution set is either empty or a coset of the kernel
of $M$; when nonempty all cosets have the same size, and the row-echelon
form computes the rank in polynomial time. We use exactly one
structural fact about such systems. A binary linear system $Mz = c$
is inconsistent if and only if there is a vector $y$ of coefficients
on the rows of $M$ with $y^{mathsf T}sfT M = 0$ and
$y^{mathsf T}sfT c = 1$. The proof is the field-theoretic case of the
row-rank formula: if $c notin im M$, extend a basis of
$im M$ by the vector $c$, and read $y$ off in the row
basis. See the "Binary duality" paragraph inside the proof of
Lemma~lem:lift-dual for the explicit verification.

[Parity lift]
For $b in FF^{V(T)}$, the graph $X_b$ has vertices
[
(u, z), qquad
u in V(G),quad
z in FF^{N_T(tau(u))},quad
sum_{a' in N_T(tau(u))} z(a') = b(tau(u)).
]
Vertices $(u, z)$ and $(v, w)$ are adjacent precisely when $uv in E(G)$
and $z(tau(v)) = w(tau(u))$.
The two comparison graphs are $X_0 := X_{b equiv 0}$ and
$X_star := X_{e_1}$, where $e_1(a) = ind{a = 1}$.


Two quick sanity checks.

Symmetry and simplicity. The adjacency condition is symmetric
in $(u, z) leftrightarrow (v, w)$: $uv in E(G)$ is symmetric and
$z(tau(v)) = w(tau(u))$ is symmetric under the swap. A loop at
$(u, z)$ would require $u sim u$ in $G$, impossible in a simple
graph. Both graphs are uncolored.

Order bound. For each type $a in V(T)$, the number of
bit-vectors $z in FF^{N_T(a)}$ with prescribed parity
$sum z = ε$ is $2^{deg_T(a) - 1}$. Therefore both lifts have
the same order
[
|V(X_0)| = |V(X_star)|
= sum_{u in V(G)} 2^{deg_T(tau(u)) - 1}.
]
The base vertices of main type $i$ contribute a factor
$2^{deg_T(i) - 1} = 2^{t - 2 + 3binom{t-1}{2}}$, and the
$3binom{t}{3}$ helpers each contribute a factor
$2^{3 - 1} = 4$. If each of $t$ main fibers has at most $A$ base
vertices and each of $3binom{t}{3}$ helper fibers has at most $A$
base vertices, the common order is at most
[
A cdot t cdot 2^{t - 2 + 3binom{t-1}{2}}
+ A cdot 4 cdot 3 binom{t}{3}
= A M_t.
]
This is exactly the order bound of Theorem~thm:main.

The map $pi_bcolon X_b to G$, $(u, z) mapsto u$, is a graph
homomorphism: every adjacency in $X_b$ projects to an adjacency in
$G$.

## Lift counts and their dual certificate

Fix a finite simple graph $F$ with a homomorphism $phi colon F to G$
and put $a = tau circ phicolon F to T$. A lift of $phi$ to
$X_b$ is a homomorphism $psi colon F to X_b$ with $pi_b circ psi
= phi$; equivalently, a choice of tag $z_x in FF^{N_T(a(x))}$ for
each source vertex $x in V(F)$ such that $sum z_x = b(a(x))$ for
every $x$ and $z_{x}(a(y)) + z_{y}(a(x)) = 0$ for every $xy in E(F)$.

[Lift counts and their dual obstruction]
For every finite simple graph $F$,
$hom(F, X_0) ge hom(F, X_star)$.
The inequality is strict if and only if there exist a homomorphism
$phicolon F to G$ and weights $scolon V(F) to FF$ and
$rcolon E(F) to FF$ such that, with $a = tau circ phi$,

sum_{substack{y : xy in E(F) a(y) = a'}} r(xy)
  = s(x)
  qquad (x in V(F), a' in N_T(a(x))),

and

sum_{substack{x in V(F) a(x) = 1}} s(x) = 1.

Whenever (eq:dual)--(eq:dual-sum) hold, the type masses

m_u(Y) := sum_{substack{x in Y a(x) = u}} s(x),
qquad m_u(V(F)) = 1

satisfy $m_u(V(F)) = 1$ for every $u in V(T)$ and every
$Y ⊆ V(F)$ inherits the definition. All sums are in $FF$.


Proof. 
Fix $phi colon F to G$ once and for all. Lifts of $phi$ to $X_b$
are in bijection with solutions in variables
$(z_{x, a'})_{x in V(F), a' in N_T(a(x))}$ of the binary linear
system

sum_{a' in N_T(a(x))} z_{x, a'} = b(a(x))
quad(x in V(F)),
qquad
z_{x, a(y)} + z_{y, a(x)} = 0
quad(xy in E(F)).

A solution $(z_x)$ gives the map $x mapsto (phi(x), z_x)$, and any
lift is decoded uniquely in this form.

The coefficient matrix of (eq:system) depends on $phi$ (through
$a$) but not on $b$. At $b = 0$ the system is always solvable ($z = 0$
is a solution); its solution set $K$ is a vector subspace of
$FF^{d}$ where $d = sum_{x} |N_T(a(x))|$. For general $b$, the
system is either inconsistent (no solutions) or a translate of $K$;
in particular either $|K|$ or $0$ lifts.

Consequently, for each $phi$,
[
#{lifts of  phi  to  X_0}
= |K|,
qquad
#{lifts of  phi  to  X_star}
in {0, |K|}.
]
Projections to $G$ partition $hom(F, X_b)$ into disjoint fibers, so
$hom(F, X_0) - hom(F, X_star)$ is a sum over $phi$ of $|K| - 0$
or $|K| - |K|$. Each term is nonnegative, so the total is nonnegative,
and strictness is exactly: at least one $phi$ has the $X_star$
projected system inconsistent.

Binary duality. A binary linear system $Mz = c$ is
inconsistent exactly when some linear combination of its rows is zero
on the left and one on the right: there exists $y$ with $y^{mathsf T}sfT
M = 0$ and $y^{mathsf T}sfT c = 1$. The easy direction is
$y^{mathsf T}sfT M z = 0 = y^{mathsf T}sfT c$ for any solution, a
contradiction. For the reverse, suppose $c notin im M$;
extend a basis of $im M$ to a basis of
$FF^{#rows}$ by adding $c$, and let $ell$ be the dual
functional, $ell(im M) = 0$, $ell(c) = 1$. Reading
$ell$ in the row basis gives $y$.

Apply this to (eq:system) with $b = e_1$. The rows split into
vertex rows ($x in V(F)$) and edge rows ($xy in E(F)$). Let $y$'s
coordinates on vertex rows be $s(x)$ and on edge rows be $r(xy)$.
Then the coefficient of $z_{x, a'}$ in $y^{mathsf T}sfT M$ is
[
s(x) + sum_{substack{y : xy in E(F) a(y) = a'}} r(xy).
]
(Vertex row $x$ contributes $1$; edge row $xy$ contributes $1$ to
$z_{x, a(y)}$ when $a(y) = a'$.) Setting this to zero gives
(eq:dual). Right side: $y^{mathsf T}sfT c$ where
$c = (e_1(a(x)))_{x}$, so the contribution is $sum_{x : a(x) = 1}
s(x) = 1$, which is (eq:dual-sum). No independence assumption
on the equation rows is used: duality handles all ranks uniformly.

Type masses. Fix adjacent types $u, v$ in $T$. Sum
(eq:dual) over all $x in V(F)$ with $a(x) = u$, using the
neighbor type $a' = v$. Right side: $m_u(V(F))$. Left side: the total
$r$-weight of edges whose $u$-endpoint is in $F$ and $v$-endpoint is
in $F$ -- a symmetric quantity. Summing from type $v$ gives
$m_v(V(F))$, so $m_u(V(F)) = m_v(V(F))$. Since $T$ is connected and
$m_1(V(F)) = 1$ by (eq:dual-sum), we get $m_u(V(F)) = 1$ for
every $u$.


## A successful choice supplies the witness

[A choice supplies a witness]
If the choice system has a successful choice, then
$hom(T, X_0) > hom(T, X_star)$, and consequently $X_0$ and
$X_star$ are not $k$-WL equivalent.


Proof. 
Fix a successful choice $(d_1, ..., d_t) in prod_i D_i$. Build
$phi colon T to G$ by
[
phi(i) = (i, d_i) quad (i in [t]),
qquad
phi(h_{ij;ell}) = (h_{ij;ell}, lambda_{ij}^i(d_i)).
]
Since $lambda_{ij}^i(d_i) = lambda_{ij}^j(d_j)$ by success, this
assignment is consistent on both helper-to-main incidences. Edges:
[nosep]
item main-main $(i,j)$: $lambda_{ij}^i(d_i) = lambda_{ij}^j(d_j)$,
  so $(i, d_i) sim (j, d_j)$ in $G$;
item helper-to-$i$: by construction $q = lambda_{ij}^i(d_i)$;
item helper-to-$j$: by success $q = lambda_{ij}^j(d_j)$;
item helper-to-$ell$: unconditional.

So $phi$ is a graph homomorphism, and $tau circ phi = id_T$.

Now define weights $s(x) = 1$ for every $x in V(T)$ and
$r(xy) = 1$ for every $xy in E(T)$. With $a = tau circ phi =
id$, we have $a(y) = y$ for every $y in V(T)$.

Check (eq:dual). Fix $x in V(T)$ and $a' in N_T(x)$. The sum
on the left side is
[
sum_{y in N_T(x),  y = a'} r(xy) ;=; ind{a' in N_T(x)} ;=; 1,
]
because $T$ is simple: for any neighbor type $a'$ of $x$, there is
exactly one edge in $T$ with endpoint types $x$ and $a'$. And
$s(x) = 1$. So (eq:dual) holds.

Check (eq:dual-sum): the vertices of $T$ of main type $1$ form a
single vertex, so $sum_{x : a(x) = 1} s(x) = s(1) = 1$. So
(eq:dual-sum) holds.

Apply Lemma~lem:lift-dual to deduce strictness:
$hom(T, X_0) > hom(T, X_star)$.

Finally, Lemma~lem:test-detected turns this strict relation into
a difference of WL round histograms: for the joint convention, at
round two, the histogram of $X_0$ determines $hom(T_{k+1}, X_0) =
hom(T, X_0)$, and similarly the histogram of $X_star$ at round two
determines $hom(T, X_star)$. The two histograms therefore differ.
The separate case is analogous at round one.


This completes the "positive" direction: a successful choice forces
a visible WL difference. The reverse is the content of
Section~sec:separator.

# Extracting a choice from a bounded-bag witness

The final ingredient is a converse to Lemma~lem:choice-witness:
if any small-bag source distinguishes $X_0$ from $X_star$, the choice
system must already admit a successful choice. This is where the
specific helper structure of $G$ and $T$ is used. The technique is
the oddomorphism separator argument of Neuen and Seppelt; we adapt
it locally and prove every step.

Throughout this section $F$ is a finite simple graph with a tree
decomposition of bag size at most $t$, and $(phi, s, r)$ is a dual
certificate as in Lemma~lem:lift-dual with $a = tau circ
phi$. From (eq:type-mass), $m_u(V(F)) = 1$ for all $u in V(T)$.

## Orienting the decomposition

Recall the type mass $m_u(Y) = sum_{x in Y, a(x) = u} s(x)$
from (eq:type-mass).

Fix a tree decomposition $(D, bag)$ of $F$ with bags of size
at most $t$. The set ${c in V(D) : v in bag(c)}$ for a
vertex $v in V(F)$ is a nonempty subtree of $D$, the
occurrence subtree of $v$.

Contracting equal bags. Suppose an edge $(c, c')$ of $D$
joins two bags with $bag(c) = bag(c')$. Contract this
edge. Every remaining edge $(c, c')$ has
$|bag(c) cap bag(c')| le t - 1$, because having
$|bag(c) cap bag(c')| = t$ would require
$|bag(c)| = |bag(c')| = t$ and hence
$bag(c) = bag(c')$.

Splitting along an edge. Delete a remaining tree edge
$(c, c')$; the tree splits into two components. Put
$S = bag(c) cap bag(c')$ and
$A^nu = V(F) ∖ S$ intersected with the vertex set of the
$nu$-th component ($nu = 0, 1$). Then
$V(F) = A^0 sqcup S sqcup A^1$ and $|S| le t - 1$.
Furthermore no edge of $F$ joins $A^0$ to $A^1$: an edge has both
endpoints in some bag, and bags in different components after the
split are disjoint outside $S$.

Absent main type. Since $S$ has at most $t - 1$ vertices
and $T$ has $t$ main types, at least one main type is absent from $S$.
Call it $u$. By $m_u(V(F)) = 1$ and $m_u(A^0) + m_u(A^1) = 1$
(no type-$u$ vertex is in $S$), one of $A^0, A^1$ has mass $1$.

Two absent types. Suppose two distinct main types $u, v$
are absent from $S$. All type-$uv$ edges lie entirely inside $A^0$
or inside $A^1$ (any ``crossing'' endpoint would need to be in $S$).
Summing (eq:dual) over all type-$u$ vertices in $A^nu$ with
$a' = v$ gives $m_u(A^nu) = m_v(A^nu)$, since both sides equal the
total $r$-weight of $uv$-edges inside $A^nu$. Hence the mass-$1$
side is the same for $u$ and $v$.

Orientation. Orient $(c, c')$ toward the side
$A^nu$ whose mass is $1$. By the previous two paragraphs this
orientation is well-defined, independent of which absent type was
used to define it, and of whether one or several types are absent
from $S$.

## Choosing a sink bag

Direct every edge of the decomposition tree by the rule just stated.
Since a finite tree has a sink, fix a sink node and call its bag $B$.

The outer sides of $B$ partition $V(F) ∖ B$.
For each node $c$ adjacent to the sink, let $A_c$ be the side away
from the sink and $S_c = bag(sink) cap bag(c)$
the intersection. Since $B = bag(sink)$, we have
$S_c ⊆ B$, and $A_c ⊆ V(F) ∖ S_c$.
Every $v in V(F) ∖ B$ has an occurrence subtree disjoint
from the sink, so it lies inside exactly one component of
$D ∖ {sink}$; if $v$ were in the branch's
intersection $S_c$, it would be in $B$, impossible. Thus $v in A_c$
for that unique $c$, and the outer sides are disjoint and cover
$V(F) ∖ B$.

Absent types vanish outside. The sink is on the mass-$1$
side of every incident edge (that is what ``sink'' means), so
whenever a main type $ell$ is absent from $S_c$, we have
$m_ell(A_c) = 0$.

Every main type is present in $B$. Suppose a main type
$ell$ is absent from $B$. Then $ell$ is absent from every
$S_c ⊆ B$, so $m_ell(A_c) = 0$ for every incident $c$,
and $m_ell(B) = 0$ (no $ell$-vertex in $B$). Hence
$m_ell(V(F)) = 0$, contradicting $m_ell(V(F)) = 1$.

Bag contents. Since $|B| le t$ and $B$ contains at least
one vertex of each of the $t$ main types, $B$ contains exactly one
vertex of each main type and no helper vertex. Write
$x_i in B$ for the unique vertex of type $i$, and
$phi(x_i) = (i, d_i)$.

## The mismatch test

We prove that $(d_1, ..., d_t)$ is a successful choice. Suppose
not: for some pair $i < j$,
[
alpha := lambda_{ij}^{i}(d_i) ;ne; lambda_{ij}^{j}(d_j).
]
For each vertex $x$ of type $i$, define
$p_i(x) = ind{lambda_{ij}^i(phi(x)) ne alpha}$, and for each
vertex $y$ of type $j$, define
$p_j(y) = ind{lambda_{ij}^j(phi(y)) = alpha}$; other vertices
have $p = 0$. For every edge $xy in E(F)$ with $x$ of type $i$ and
$y$ of type $j$, we have $p_i(x) + p_j(y) = 1$ in $FF$, because
$phi$ is a homomorphism and
$uv in E(G)$ with $u$ of type $i$, $v$ of type $j$ is exactly the
condition $lambda_{ij}^i(u) = lambda_{ij}^j(v)$.

Define, for any $Y ⊆ V(F)$,
[
P(Y) := sum_{d in {i, j}}  sum_{substack{x in Y a(x) = d}}
        s(x), p_d(x).
]
Using (eq:dual) at each type-$i$ vertex $x$ with $a' = j$ and
at each type-$j$ vertex $y$ with $a' = i$, and the identity
$p_i(x) + p_j(y) = 1$ on every $ij$-edge,

P(V(F))
 = sum_{substack{xy in E(F)a(x)=i, a(y)=j}}
     r(xy),(p_i(x) + p_j(y))
 = sum_{substack{xy in E(F)a(x)=i, a(y)=j}} r(xy)
 = m_i(V(F)) = 1.


On the bag $B$ itself,
[
P(B) = s(x_i) p_i(x_i) + s(x_j) p_j(x_j) = 0,
]
because $p_i(x_i) = ind{alpha ne alpha} = 0$ and
$p_j(x_j) = ind{lambda_{ij}^j(d_j) = alpha} = 0$ (we assumed
$lambda_{ij}^j(d_j) ne alpha$).

## Vanishing of $P$ on every outer side

Fix an outer side $A = A_c$ with intersection $S = S_c ⊆ B$.
Choose a main type $ell$ absent from $S$; by the sink property,
$m_ell(A) = 0$. We will show $P(A) = 0$, retaining boundary
contributions.

Case $ell = i$ or $ell = j$. By symmetry consider
$ell = i$: no type-$i$ vertex lies in $S$.
- For each type-$j$ vertex $y in A$, every type-$i$ neighbor of
  $y$ lies in $A$ (an outside neighbor would have to lie in $S$,
  which has no type-$i$ vertices).
- For a type-$i$ vertex $x in A$, a type-$j$ neighbor $y$ outside
  $A$ must lie in $S$ and be the bag vertex $x_j$ (since
  $S ⊆ B$ has only one type-$j$ vertex); on such $y$,
  $p_j(y) = 0$.

Expanding (eq:dual) at every type-$i$ vertex of $A$ with
$a' = j$, and at every type-$j$ vertex of $A$ with $a' = i$:
[

P(A)
 &= sum_{substack{x in Aa(x)=i}}
       sum_{substack{y in N_F(x)a(y)=j}}
        r(xy),(p_i(x) + p_j(y))
 &= sum_{substack{x in Aa(x)=i}}
       sum_{substack{y in N_F(x)a(y)=j}} r(xy)
  = m_i(A) = 0.

]

Case $ell notin {i,j$.} Set $h = h_{ij;ell}$.
Because $S ⊆ B$ has no helper vertices (Section~sec:separator
) and every external neighbor of $A$ lies in $S$, every type-$h$
neighbor of an $i$- or $j$-vertex in $A$ lies in $A$.

Let $H_A = {w in A : a(w) = h}$. Expanding
(eq:dual) first at each type-$i$ or type-$j$ vertex of $A$
with $a' = h$ (all $h$-neighbors are inside $A$), and then at each
$w in H_A$ with $a' in {i, j}$,
[

P(A)
 &= sum_{w in H_A} Bigg( sum_{substack{x in N_F(w)a(x)=i}}
          r(wx), p_i(x)
        + sum_{substack{y in N_F(w)a(y)=j}} r(wy), p_j(y) Bigg).

]
Here the first inner sum only has terms for $x in A$; external
type-$i$ neighbors in $S$ contribute $p_i(x_i) = 0$. Similarly for
the second. Fix $w in H_A$ with $phi(w) = (h_{ij;ell}, q_w)$.
For a type-$i$ neighbor $x$, $phi(x) = (i, d_x)$ must satisfy
$lambda_{ij}^i(d_x) = q_w$ (the incidence in $G$), so
$p_i(x) = ind{q_w ne alpha}$, constant in $x$. Symmetrically,
for a type-$j$ neighbor $y$, $p_j(y) = ind{q_w = alpha}$.
Using (eq:dual) at $w$ with $a' = i$ and $a' = j$,
[
P(A) = sum_{w in H_A}
         ( ind{q_w ne alpha}, s(w)
              + ind{q_w = alpha}, s(w) )
     = sum_{w in H_A} s(w) = m_h(A).
]
Since $h$ and $ell$ are both absent from $S$, all $hell$-edges
incident to either type on $A$ remain in $A$; summing (eq:dual)
over the two endpoint classes gives $m_h(A) = m_ell(A) = 0$. So
$P(A) = 0$.

## Contradiction

The sets $B$ and ${A_c}_c$ form a disjoint partition of $V(F)$.
Thus
[
P(V(F)) = P(B) + sum_c P(A_c) = 0 + 0 = 0,
]
contradicting (eq:P-global). Therefore every pair $i < j$
satisfies $lambda_{ij}^i(d_i) = lambda_{ij}^j(d_j)$, i.e.,
$(d_1, ..., d_t)$ is a successful choice.

[Choice extraction]
Let $F$ be a finite simple graph with a tree decomposition of bag
size at most $t$. Suppose a homomorphism $phi colon F to G$ and
weights $s colon V(F) to FF$, $r colon E(F) to FF$ satisfy
(eq:dual)--(eq:dual-sum) with $a = tau circ phi$.
Then the choice system has a successful choice.


## Proof of the main theorem

Proof. [Proof of Theorem~thm:main]
The graphs $X_0, X_star$ are simple, uncolored, of equal order at
most $A M_t$ (Section~sec:lifts).

Forward direction. Suppose a successful choice exists. By
Lemma~lem:choice-witness,
$hom(T, X_0) > hom(T, X_star)$. The template $T$ is $T_{k+1}$ in
the joint case and $T_k$ in the separate case, so
Lemma~lem:test-detected shows that the WL round histograms
already differ: after two joint rounds, or after one separate round.
Hence $X_0$ and $X_star$ are not $k$-WL equivalent.

Converse direction. Suppose no successful choice exists. Let
$F$ be any finite simple graph with a tree decomposition of bag size
at most $t$ and suppose for contradiction that
$hom(F, X_0) ne hom(F, X_star)$. Since
Lemma~lem:lift-dual gives the non-strict inequality
$hom(F, X_0) ge hom(F, X_star)$ in general, we have strict
inequality here, so we get a dual certificate $(phi, s, r)$
satisfying (eq:dual)--(eq:dual-sum). But then
Lemma~lem:extract produces a successful choice, contradiction.
Therefore $hom(F, X_0) = hom(F, X_star)$ for every such $F$, and
Lemma~lem:hom-implies-wl yields $X_0 equiv_k X_star$ in the
corresponding convention, including the disjoint-union
implementation.


# Complexity consequences

The parity reduction is unconditional. To turn it into a time lower
bound, we first control the size of the choice system obtained from a
3-CNF, then account for the cost of sparsification and of writing out
the two graphs. Everything in this section is independent of the proof
in Sections~sec:wl-tests--sec:separator except through
Theorem~thm:main.

## Sparsification

[Sparsification, Impagliazzo--Paturi--Zane]
For every $ε > 0$ there are a constant $C_ε ge 1$ and a
deterministic algorithm that expresses any 3-CNF $F$ on $N ge 1$
variables as a disjunction of at most $2^{ε N}$ 3-CNFs on the
same variables, each with at most $C_ε N$ clauses. The running
time is $2^{ε N}poly(|F|)$, where $|F|$ is the
explicit encoding length of $F$. For positive rational $ε$, the
procedure and a valid density bound can be chosen effectively.


This is the imported sparsification theorem, not a consequence of the
parity construction. Its constructive set-system proof has a universe
of the $2N$ literals: choosing the rate $ε/2$ there gives the
variable-based rate $ε$ displayed above. The integer threshold
selection and fixed branching give the effective rational-rate
selection used below.

The stated input-length bound permits a polynomial-time normalization
before sparsification: rename variables and remove tautologies and
repeated clauses. There are only $O(N^3)$ distinct clauses of width at
most 3, so the normalized encoding has length polynomial in $N$.

## From 3-CNF to a choice system

[Grouping]
A 3-CNF with $m$ clauses yields a $t$-ary choice system (same $t$ as
the WL convention) whose domains and pair-label sets all have size at
most
[
A = 2^{3lceil m/trceil}.
]
The choice system has a successful choice if and only if the formula is
satisfiable.


Proof. 
Partition the clauses into $t$ groups, each with at most
$lceil m/trceil$ clauses. For each group $i$, let $V_i$ be the set of
variables that appear in it, and let $D_i$ be the set of assignments on
$V_i$ that satisfy every clause in group $i$. Pair labels are
restrictions:
[
L_{ij} = {0, 1}^{V_i cap V_j}, qquad
lambda_{ij}^{i}(d) = lambda_{ij}^{j}(d) = d|_{V_i cap V_j}.
]
Each scope $V_i$ has at most $3lceil m/trceil$ variables, so
$|D_i| le 2^{3lceil m/trceil} = A$ and $|L_{ij}| le A$.

If a successful choice exists, its components agree on every shared
variable, so their union is a well-defined assignment on
$∪_i V_i$; unused variables are free. It satisfies every clause
because each clause lies inside its group and is satisfied by that
group's assignment. Conversely, restrict a satisfying assignment to
each $V_i$ to obtain a successful choice. An empty group has its unique
empty assignment; an unsatisfiable group has empty domain; an empty
overlap has the unique empty label. None of these degenerate cases
requires special handling.


## Fixed-dimension ETH lower bound

[Fixed-dimension ETH consequence]
Assume positive exponential-rate ETH: some $delta > 0$ rules out a
deterministic 3-SAT algorithm with running time
$2^{delta N}poly(|F|)$, where $N$ is the number of
variables and $|F|$ the explicit encoding length. Then there are
constants $c > 0$ and $K ge 4$ such that, for every fixed integer
$k ge K$, no deterministic algorithm decides $k$-WL equivalence of
two $n$-vertex graphs in time $O(n^{ck})$. This holds for both joint
and separate refinement, with explicit adjacency-matrix inputs of
uncolored simple undirected graphs, and time measured on a multitape
Turing machine.


Proof. 
Fix $delta > 0$ from positive-rate ETH. Choose $ε < delta/4$
and let $C = C_ε$ be the constant of Lemma~lem:sparsify.

For the Turing-machine model set $q = 1$. More generally, suppose the
chosen machine model has a simulation
$A_k (T + ell + 1)^q$ steps, where $q ge 1$ is fixed by the model,
$T$ is the program's running time, $ell$ its input length, and $A_k$
may depend on $k$. Choose
[
c = (delta)/(24,C,q), qquad
K > (24,C,q)/(delta), qquad
K ge 4.
]

Fix any $k ge K$ and suppose a solver $mathcal S$calS$ decides $k$-WL
equivalence of two $n$-vertex graphs in time $O(n^{ck})$.

Reduction from 3-SAT. Given a 3-CNF $F$ on $N$ variables:
[nosep]
item Sparsify $F$ into at most $2^{ε N}$ sparse disjuncts
  $F_1, ..., F_D$, each with at most $C N$ clauses.
item For each $F_j$, build the choice system of
  Lemma~lem:sat-choice with $t = k + 1$ (joint convention) or
  $t = k$ (separate), and build the two graphs $X_0, X_star$ of
  Theorem~thm:main. Let $n_j$ be their common order.
item Run $mathcal S$calS$ on $(X_0, X_star)$. Accept the pair if
  inequivalent.
item Accept $F$ if any pair is inequivalent.

Correctness follows from Lemma~lem:sparsify (a disjunction of
satisfiable equals satisfiable) and Lemmas~lem:sat-choice and
Theorem~thm:main (inequivalence iff the choice system admits a
successful choice).

Size. With $m le CN$ clauses in a sparse disjunct, the
choice system has $A = 2^{3lceil m/trceil} le 2^{3(CN/t + 1)}
= 8 cdot 2^{3CN/t}$ domain size. So
[
n_j ;le; A M_t ;le; b_k,2^{3CN/t},
qquad
b_k := 8 M_t = 2^{O(k^2)}.
]

Construction time. Write out the two adjacency matrices. The
vertex records have length $O(N + k^2)$, and each entry of the matrix
is determined in $O(1)$ comparisons of records and tags, so a
multitape implementation costs at most
$b'_k,(N + |F|)^d,2^{B_0 CN/t}$ steps for an absolute $d$ and a
$k$-only constant $b'_k$, with $B_0 = 6$ (the factor comes from
squaring the $2^{3CN/t}$ bound on $n_j$).

Solver input. The adjacency matrix has length $ell = O(n_j^2)$,
so the simulated solver takes
$O_k(n_j^{ck} + n_j^{2} + 1)$ steps ($q = 1$; both terms matter, even
when $ck < 2$).

Total time for one disjunct.
Up to $k$-only constants and polynomial factors in $N$ and $|F|$:
[
2^{B_0 CN/t} + 2^{6q CN/t} + 2^{3CqckN/t}.
]
Since $t ge k ge K$:
[nosep]
item $B_0 C/t = 6C/t le 6C/k le 6C/K < 6C cdot delta/(24 C q) = delta/(4q) le delta/4$.
item $6 q C/t le 6q C/K < 6qC cdot delta/(24 C q) = delta/4$.
item $3 C q c k/t le 3C q c = 3C q cdot delta/(24 C q) = delta/8$.

So the total exponent over all $2^{ε N}$ disjuncts is at most
$ε + max(delta/4, delta/8) < delta/4 + delta/4 = delta/2 < delta$.

Under positive-rate ETH, no such algorithm exists. The parameters
of the supposed solver (its description, its running-time constant,
$A_k$, $b_k$, the eventual threshold in $N$) may all depend on this
fixed $k$; the constants $c$ and $K$ do not. Thus the contradiction
applies to every fixed $k ge K$, even without a uniform way of
selecting the alleged solvers.


## Uniform subexponential form

[Uniform lower bound]
If no deterministic 3-SAT algorithm has running time
$2^{o(N)}poly(|F|)$, then there is no uniform
deterministic algorithm for $k$-WL equivalence with running time
$f(k) , n^{g(k)}$ where $g(k)/k to 0$. Here a uniform algorithm
takes $k$ and the two graphs as input.


Proof. 
The constructive sparsification proof chooses integer thresholds by a
computable search from any positive rational rate and then runs a
fixed branching procedure. We may thus fix an effectively indexed
family $S_j$ with rate $1/j$ and sparsity constant $C_j$, for each
$j ge 1$.

Suppose the uniform WL algorithm exists. Build one fixed machine
$U(j, k, F)$ that: runs $S_j$; constructs the graphs; calls the WL
algorithm. Its running time is $T_{j,k}(F) le A_{j,k},N^{d_j},
2^{rho_{j,k} N}$ after normalization, where
[
rho_{j,k} = (1)/(j) + max{(B_0 C_j)/(t),
         (3 C_j g(k))/(t)},
qquad t =  k+1, & (J),  k, & (S).
]
Run doubling rounds $R = 1, 2, 4, ...$. In each round, simulate
$U(j, k, F)$ from scratch for at most $R$ steps, for every
$1 le j le R$ and $4 le k le R$; stop at the first completed
decision. All computations so produced are correct. If $L$ is the
normalized input length, a fixed tape simulator costs at most
$O((R + L + 1)^3)$ per trial, and there are at most $R^2$ trials. So
the racing machine runs in time
[
D(F) le C_U (T_{j,k}(F) + L + j + k + 4)^5
]
for every fixed pair $(j, k)$, with a constant $C_U$ independent of
the pair.

Given $eta > 0$: first fix $j$ with $1/j < eta/20$. Then fix $k$
with both terms in the maximum defining $rho_{j,k}$ below
$eta/20$; this is possible since $t ge k$ and $g(k)/k to 0$.
Then $rho_{j,k} < eta/10$, and the fifth-power bound is
$2^{eta N}$ for all sufficiently large $N$, after absorbing its
fixed constants and polynomial factors. The same pair $(j, k)$ works
for all sufficiently large $N$. The racing machine need not know a
convergence modulus for $g(k)/k$ and need not recognize a good pair.

For precision, normalize first to a canonical formula on its occurring
variables. If none occur, decide the constant formula directly without
sparsification. Otherwise, with $1 le N' le N$ variables, the
canonical length is polynomial in $N'$. For every desired rate, the
same fixed pair $(j, k)$ bounds all sufficiently large $N'$; the
finitely many canonical formulas with smaller $N'$ have a common
finite maximum running time. So the normalized solver has a uniform
$2^{o(N)}$ bound even when the input names many unused variables.
Parsing and normalization cost one fixed polynomial, so the full
algorithm contradicts the assumed uniform lower bound.


The same model qualification applies: at cost $q$ per simulated
step, the effective $g(k)$ becomes at most
$max{q g(k), 2q} + 1 = o(k)$ when $q$ is fixed. For the uniform
transfer, the simulation must itself be uniform in $k$; an
ineffective family of separately supplied fixed-$k$ solvers does not
define $U$, and Proposition~prop:uniform makes no claim about
such a family under its weaker premise.

## Small dimensions on matrix inputs

[Small dimensions]
On a comparison of two edgeless $n$-vertex graphs, a deterministic
algorithm must inspect every potential edge: changing an unseen edge
in one graph destroys equivalence, either by degrees (ordinary 1-WL)
or by atomic tuple types ($k ge 2$). This is an $Ω(n^2)$
bit-query bound.
Decreasing $c$ so that $ck < 2$ for the finitely many $2 le k < K$
covers those dimensions for both tuple conventions. Ordinary neighbor
color refinement at dimension one is covered separately by the
degree argument. The literal one-coordinate replacement rules above,
whose atomic types see no edges, are not this ordinary 1-WL convention.


## Fixed-round decision corollary

Given two $n$-vertex graphs, consider deciding whether their
$k$-tuple color histograms agree after two joint rounds, or after one
separate round, starting from atomic types. This includes separate
runs with common color names and pure-tuple comparison in the
disjoint-union run.

On the graph pairs produced by the reduction, Theorem~thm:main
gives equality at every round when no successful choice exists, and
inequality by the specified round when a choice exists. The inequality
persists because each update retains the old color. Thus the reductions
proving Theorem~thm:eth and Proposition~prop:uniform give
the corresponding deterministic time exclusions for these fixed-round
decisions, with their respective hypotheses and parameter ranges. The
difficulty is already present in comparing these early histograms; it
does not require many rounds of stabilization.
