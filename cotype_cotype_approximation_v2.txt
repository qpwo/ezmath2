Random signed sums measure the curvature of a Banach space. Two questions turn that measurement into geometry: when is a space bounded away from Hilbert space, and when does it fail to admit a basis? Finite Rademacher cotype of the space and its dual decides both questions, under one axiom of approximation. Pisier conjectured that finite cotype of the space and its dual, in two arbitrary exponents, already decides both; the ordinary approximation property turns out to be the axiom that settles the question.

A Banach space $X$ has the approximation property (AP) if, for every compact $M\subset X$ and every $\delta>0$, there is a bounded finite-rank linear map $S:X\to X$ with $\sup_{x\in M}\|Sx-x\|<\delta$. The operator norms $\|S\|$ need not be bounded. The bounded approximation property (BAP) requires a single $\lambda<\infty$ with $\|S\|\le\lambda$ for every such approximant. Enflo produced a separable reflexive space without AP, Figiel and Johnson showed that AP does not force BAP, and Szarek constructed a superreflexive space with BAP and without a Schauder basis. A Schauder basis therefore implies BAP, BAP implies AP, and neither arrow reverses. An argument resting on AP alone must tolerate approximants of unbounded norm.

./figiel-johnson-1973-ap-without-bap-pams.pdf

For $2\le q<\infty$, the cotype-$q$ constant $C_q(Y)$ of a Banach space $Y$ is the least $C$ with
$$\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^s\|y_i\|^q\Bigr)^{1/q}\le C \Bigl(\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^s\varepsilon_i y_i\Bigr\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}$$
for every finite family in $Y$, the signs $\varepsilon_i$ independent and uniform. Finite Rademacher cotype means $C_q(Y)<\infty$ for some finite $q$. A Banach space $X$ has Rademacher type $p$, $1<p\le 2$, if for some $D<\infty$
$$\Bigl(\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^s\varepsilon_i x_i\Bigr\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}\le D\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^s\|x_i\|^p\Bigr)^{1/p}$$
for every finite family. The constants $C_q$ and the type exponent thus capture the size of random signed sums and thereby the uniform finite-dimensional structure.

The Rademacher projection on $X$-valued functions on the $s$-cube retains first-order coefficients:
$$(R_s f)(\varepsilon)=\sum_{i=1}^s\varepsilon_i\,\mathbb{E}_\eta[\eta_i f(\eta)].$$
The Rademacher cotype of a vector-valued function of bounded Walsh degree turns out to be comparable to that of a single variable, a comparison due to Borell; Kahane's inequality handles degree one, and the higher-degree statement follows.
./borell-1979-banach-walsh-polynomials-numdam-SPS1979.pdf

Define
$$K(X)=\sup_{s\ge 1}\|R_s:L_2(\{-1,1\}^s;X)\to L_2(\{-1,1\}^s;X)\|,$$
with $L_2$ carrying the probability-uniform norm. The space $X$ is $K$-convex if $K(X)<\infty$. On each cube the adjoint of $R_s$ is the Rademacher projection on $L_2(\{-1,1\}^s;X^*)$, so $K(X^*)=K(X)$.

Maurey and Pisier identified $K$-convexity with nontrivial type, and Pisier's holomorphic-semigroup theorem completed the equivalence. The statement is
$$K(X)<\infty\ \Longleftrightarrow\ X\text{ has Rademacher type }p\text{ for some }p>1\ \Longleftrightarrow\ X\text{ does not contain the }\ell_1^s\text{ uniformly.}$$
The three properties hold without approximation hypotheses. The finite-cube definition of $K(X)$ agrees with the infinite-product definition, because functions depending on finitely many coordinates are dense.

For cotype two on both sides and AP, Pisier proved a stronger conclusion: such an $X$ is isomorphic to a Hilbert space. Pisier then obtained $K$-convexity from finite cotype of $X$ and $X^*$ under one of three extra hypotheses: a Schauder basis, a dense increasing family of uniformly complemented finite-dimensional subspaces, or BAP. Each of those hypotheses comes with a restriction on the cotype exponents $q$ and $r$, and Pisier asked whether the restriction is necessary. The BAP formulation reappears as the cotype--cotype conjecture in work of Szarek and Tomczak-Jaegermann, and later as a question about uniform tensor estimates.
./pisier-1980-81-seminaire-exp7-kseminar-suite.pdf

The characterisation under AP has a uniform finite-dimensional form, and that form feeds metric entropy. For an origin-symmetric convex body $B\subset\mathbb{R}^n$, let $X_B$ denote $\mathbb{R}^n$ with unit ball $B$, and let $B^\circ$ be the polar. Write $N(S,T)$ for the least number of translates of $T$ covering $S$. Artstein, Milman, Szarek and Tomczak-Jaegermann proved that a bound on the $K$-convexity constant of either gauge space, in one direction, gives a two-sided comparison of $\log N(K,tL)$ with $\log N(L^\circ,atK^\circ)$; Milman's note restates the theorem with explicit covering-number constants. A uniform Cotype-to-$K$ bound turns this into a dimension-free two-sided duality for gauge spaces whose bodies obey uniform cotype assumptions.

The main result is the following.

If $X$ is a nonzero real Banach space with AP, then $X$ is $K$-convex if and only if $C_q(X)<\infty$ and $C_r(X^*)<\infty$ for some $q,r\in[2,\infty)$, with $q$ and $r$ independent of each other. The forward direction holds for every real Banach space, without any approximation hypothesis.

The result settles the cotype--cotype conjecture of Pisier in greater generality than the BAP formulation: finite cotype of $X$ and $X^*$ forces $K$-convexity whenever $X$ has AP, with no relation between the two cotype exponents. The reverse direction fails without AP. Starting from a cotype-two space such as $\ell_1$ and applying Pisier's embedding produces a space whose primal and dual both have cotype two and which fails $K$-convexity; such examples lack AP. The theorem isolates approximation as the precise additional hypothesis.

The engine of the proof is the Walsh-matrix norm. Put $V_n=\mathbb{F}_2^n$ with its standard dot product and $\chi(u,v)=(-1)^{u\cdot v}$. For a bounded operator $T:G\to F$ between Banach spaces, define
$$(W_n^Tx)(v)=\mathbb{E}_{u\in V_n}\,\chi(u,v)\,T x(u),\qquad w_n(T)=\|W_n^T:L_2(V_n;G)\to L_2(V_n;F)\|,$$
and set $w_0(T)=\|T\|$. Finite-sum duality rewrites $w_n(T)$ as
$$w_n(T)=\sup\Bigl|\mathbb{E}_{u,v\in V_n}\,\chi(u,v)y_v(Tx_u)\Bigr|,\qquad \mathbb{E}_u\|x_u\|^2\le1,\quad \mathbb{E}_v\|y_v\|^2\le1.$$

Basic Walsh estimates. For every bounded $T:G\to F$:
(i) $2^{-n/2}\|T\|\le w_n(T)\le\|T\|$, and $n\mapsto w_n(T)$ is nonincreasing;
(ii) if $T_\Omega:L_2(\Omega;G)\to L_2(\Omega;F)$ acts pointwise as $T$, with $\Omega$ finite, then $w_n(T_\Omega)=w_n(T)$;
(iii) if $T$ has finite rank, there is $D_T<\infty$ with $w_n(T)\le D_T\,2^{-n/2}$ for every $n\ge0$.
Moreover, for bounded $A,B:G\to F$,
$$|w_n(A)-w_n(B)|\le\|A-B\|.$$

Proof. For the upper bound, interchange the expectation with the operator and apply Cauchy--Schwarz. For the lower bound, take an input supported at a single $u_0$, scaled so that $\mathbb{E}_u\|x_u\|^2=1$; the output norm is then $2^{-n/2}\|Tx_{u_0}\|$ and the supremum over $u_0$ with unit norm recovers $\|T\|$ up to $2^{-n/2}$. For monotonicity, identify $V_{n+j}=V_n\oplus V_j$: first apply $W_n^T$ in the first coordinate, then the normalised Walsh transform on $V_j$ with the identity of $F$ in the second coordinate. The latter is orthonormal on $L_2(V_j;F)$ and hence has norm one; averaging squared estimates in the unused coordinates gives $w_{n+j}(T)\le w_n(T)$.

For (ii), interchange the two finite coordinate systems. For (iii), factor the finite-rank $T$ through a finite-dimensional Hilbert space $H$ as $T=BA$ with $A:G\to H$ and $B:H\to F$. Orthogonality of the Walsh matrix gives $\|W_n^{\mathrm{Id}_H}\|=2^{-n/2}$, and $W_n^T=B_\ast W_n^A$, where $B_\ast$ acts pointwise through $B$. Hence $w_n(T)\le\|B\|\|A\|\,2^{-n/2}$. For the continuity estimate, $W_n^A-W_n^B=W_n^{A-B}$, so the upper bound together with the reverse triangle inequality gives $|w_n(A)-w_n(B)|\le\|A-B\|$.

Fix the length $m$ and define $c_m(Y)$ as the least $C$ in
$$\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^m\|y_i\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}\le C\,\Bigl(\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^m\varepsilon_i y_i\Bigr\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}.$$
The cotype bound reads $c_m(Y)\le C_q(Y)m^{1/2-1/q}$. Squaring and averaging inside a finite probability space $\Omega$ gives
$$c_m(L_2(\Omega;Y))\le c_m(Y),$$
and the same inequality holds after passing to finite-sum duals, so the constants below survive any amplification that appears later.

The one estimate the moment lifts need is the $L_1$--$L_2$ case of Kahane's inequality, with an explicit constant.

Kahane moment comparison. For every finite Rademacher sum $S=\sum_{i=1}^m\varepsilon_i z_i$ in a Banach space,
$$\bigl(\mathbb{E}\|S\|^2\bigr)^{1/2}\le L\,\mathbb{E}\|S\|,\qquad L=24.$$

Proof. Put $M=\mathbb{E}\|S\|$ and $b=\max_i\|z_i\|$. Since $z_i=\mathbb{E}(\varepsilon_i S)$, one has $b\le M$; the case $M=0$ is immediate.

Let $S_j=\sum_{i\le j}\varepsilon_i z_i$ and stop at the first $j$ with $\|S_j\|>u$. On a stopped prefix $z$, the remaining sum is symmetric, so at least one of $\|z+h\|$, $\|z-h\|$ exceeds $u$, and hence the probability that a stop occurs is at most $2\mathbb{P}(\|S\|>u)$. For any fixed $j$, symmetry of the independent prefix gives $\mathbb{P}\bigl(\|\sum_{i>j}\varepsilon_i z_i\|>v\bigr)\le 2\mathbb{P}(\|S\|>v)$. At the first stop $\|S_j\|\le u+b$; decomposing according to the stopping time and using independence of the remaining sum yields
$$\mathbb{P}(\|S\|>u+b+v)\le 4\,\mathbb{P}(\|S\|>u)\,\mathbb{P}(\|S\|>v).$$
Take $u=8M$, so that Markov's inequality together with $b\le M$ gives $\mathbb{P}(\|S\|>9M+v)\le\tfrac12\mathbb{P}(\|S\|>v)$. Then $\mathbb{P}(\|S\|>9jM)\le2^{-j}$, and integrating over intervals of length $9M$,
$$\mathbb{E}\|S\|^2\le81M^2\sum_{j\ge0}(2j+1)2^{-j}=486M^2<24^2M^2.$$

A simple consequence of quotient duality is used repeatedly. If $U:A\to B$ is a bounded surjection and $\|U^*b^*\|\ge D^{-1}\|b^*\|$ for every $b^*\in B^*$, then
$$\inf\bigl\{\|a\|:Ua=b\bigr\}\le D\|b\|.$$
Indeed, $A/\ker U$ is isometrically isomorphic to $B$ under $U$, the quotient norm of the class of $a$ equals $\sup\{|\langle b,b^*\rangle|:\|U^*b^*\|\le1\}$, and the assumed lower bound makes the outer supremum at most $D\|b\|$. Every application below has an explicit bounded right inverse, so surjectivity is never an issue.

Bounded moment lifts. Suppose $c_m(G^*)\le D_g$ and $c_m(F)\le D_f$, with $D_g,D_f\ge1$, and set
$$a=\frac{1}{2LD_g\sqrt m},\qquad b=\frac{1}{2LD_f\sqrt m},$$
where $L=24$ is the Kahane constant. Then:
(i) for $x_1,\ldots,x_m\in B_G$ there are $P_\varepsilon\in B_G$ with $\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\varepsilon_i P_\varepsilon=ax_i$, $1\le i\le m$;
(ii) for $y_1,\ldots,y_m\in B_{F^*}$ there are $Z_\delta\in B_{F^*}$ with $\mathbb{E}_\delta\delta_j Z_\delta=by_j$, $1\le j\le m$.
The conclusions and constants persist after replacing $G$ and $F$ by finite $L_2$ sums of their copies.

Proof. The moment map
$$U:L_\infty(\{-1,1\}^m;G)\longrightarrow\ell_2^m(G),\qquad UP=(\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\varepsilon_iP_\varepsilon)_{i=1}^m,$$
is onto, with right inverse $(x_i)\mapsto\sum_i\varepsilon_ix_i$. Its adjoint sends $(z_i^*)$ to the function $\varepsilon\mapsto\sum_i\varepsilon_i z_i^*$. By the Kahane comparison and the cotype bound,
$$\|U^*(z_i^*)\|_{L_1(G^*)}=\mathbb{E}\Bigl\|\sum_i\varepsilon_iz_i^*\Bigr\|\ge(LD_g)^{-1}\Bigl(\sum_i\|z_i^*\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}.$$
The quotient-lift inequality with $D=LD_g$, applied to the tuple $(ax_i)$, bounds its infimal lift norm by $LD_g\,a\sqrt m=\tfrac12$; a lift of norm at most $1$ therefore exists, and (i) follows.

For (ii) consider
$$V:\ell_2^m(F)\longrightarrow L_1(\{-1,1\}^m;F),\qquad V(w_1,\ldots,w_m)=\sum_j\delta_jw_j,$$
which satisfies $\|Vw\|_{L_1}\ge(LD_f)^{-1}\|w\|_{\ell_2^m(F)}$. The functional $Vw\mapsto b\sum_j y_j(w_j)$ is bounded by $b\sqrt m\,LD_f=\tfrac12$ on the range of $V$; Hahn--Banach extends it to $L_1(F)$ with the same norm, and identifying $L_1(F)^*=L_\infty(F^*)$ yields $Z\in L_\infty(\{-1,1\}^m;F^*)$ with the required moments. The amplification assertion is a consequence of $c_m(L_2(\Omega;Y))\le c_m(Y)$ and of finite-sum duality.

For a function on a sign cube define the odd part $\mathcal{O}h(z)=\tfrac12(h(z)-h(-z))$ and call $h$ odd when $\mathcal{O}h=h$. Fourier coefficients use uniform measure, so for $S\in\{-1,1\}^{m\times m}$ and $P\in\{0,1\}^{m\times m}$ one writes $S^P=\prod_{i,j}S_{ij}^{P_{ij}}$, $\widehat h(P)=\mathbb{E}_S h(S)S^P$. For vector cubes, indices $p\in\{0,1\}^m$ have $|p|$ ones and $e_i$ is the $i$-th unit vector; the products $\sigma S$ and $S\tau$ multiply row $i$ and column $j$ by signs $\sigma_i,\tau_j$. The binary rank used below is the rank over $\mathbb{F}_2$.

The binary rank controls the following feature. For $u_i,v_j\in\mathbb{F}_2^n$,
$$\prod_{i,j}\chi(u_i,v_j)^{P_{ij}}=(-1)^{\sum_{i,j}P_{ij}u_i\cdot v_j},$$
so an index $P$ of binary rank $k$ contains $k$ independent bilinear terms after a change of coordinates. A rank-one index $P=ph^t$ is supported on a rectangle.

Coefficient lemma. Let $M_0=4$, let $d=\lceil2\log_2 m\rceil$ satisfy $4\le d<m$, let $a,b>0$, put $A=ma$, $B=mb$, and assume
$$\min(A,B)\ge20M_0 d(d+1)^2.$$
There exists $c_*>0$, depending only on $m,a,b$, with the following property. Suppose
$$\Psi:\{-1,1\}^{m\times m}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad f,g:\{-1,1\}^m\to\mathbb{R}$$
are odd, have sup norms at most $M_0$, and satisfy
$$\widehat f(e_i)=a,\qquad \widehat g(e_j)=b,$$
together with the projections
$$\mathbb{E}_\sigma\sigma_i\Psi(\sigma S)=a\,g(S_{i,:}),\qquad \mathbb{E}_\tau\tau_j\Psi(S\tau)=b\,f(S_{:,j})$$
for all $i,j$ and all $S$. Then $|\widehat\Psi(P)|\ge c_*$ for some binary matrix $P$ of rank at least two.

Proof. The admissible triples $(\Psi,f,g)$ form a compact subset of a finite-dimensional space, and each $|\widehat\Psi(P)|$ is continuous there; the maximum $\max_{\operatorname{rank}P\ge2}|\widehat\Psi(P)|$ is therefore continuous. If the admissible set is empty, any positive $c_*$ applies. Otherwise it suffices to show that this maximum never vanishes.

Suppose, for contradiction, that the Fourier support of $\Psi$ has binary rank at most one. Oddness eliminates indices with an even number of ones, so every surviving index is a rectangle $ph^t$ with $|p|=k$, $|h|=l$ odd, and $\widehat\Psi(ph^t)$ enters only through $C_{kl}=\sum_{|p|=k,|h|=l}\widehat\Psi(ph^t)$. Parseval and Cauchy--Schwarz give
$$|C_{kl}|\le M_0\Bigl(\binom{m}{k}\binom{m}{l}\Bigr)^{1/2}.$$

Damping large rectangles gives a small analytic perturbation. Consider
$$H(s,u)=\sum_{\substack{1\le k,l\le m\\ k,l\text{ odd}}}C_{kl}\,s^k u^l\,2^{-kl}.$$
On $[-1,1]^2$ this has sup norm at most $M_0$: with independent signs $D_i,E_j,R_{ij}$ of mean $s,u,\tfrac12$ respectively and $S_{ij}=D_iE_jR_{ij}$, $\mathbb{E}S^{ph^t}=s^{|p|}u^{|h|}2^{-|p||h|}$, whence $H(s,u)=\mathbb{E}\Psi(S)$ and $|H|\le\|\Psi\|_\infty\le M_0$.

Delete from $H$ the terms with $k>d$ and $l>d$, obtaining $H_0$. When $k,l>d$, the estimates $(k+l)\log_2m\le kl$ and $kl/2\ge k+l$ combined with the bound on $C_{kl}$ show that the deleted part has sup norm at most
$$M_0\sum_{k,l>d}m^{(k+l)/2}2^{-kl}\le M_0\sum_{k,l>d}2^{-kl/2}\le M_0\sum_{k,l>d}2^{-k-l}\le M_0.$$
Consequently $\|H_0\|_\infty\le2M_0$.

The two projection identities detect the first row and first column of the Fourier support. For an odd rectangle $ph^t$, changing variables in $\mathbb{E}_\sigma\sigma_i\Psi(\sigma S)$ isolates $\widehat\Psi(e_i h^t)=a\widehat g(h)$, so
$$C_{1l}=A\sum_{|h|=l}\widehat g(h),\qquad C_{k1}=B\sum_{|p|=k}\widehat f(p),$$
and the deletion defining $H_0$ leaves these untouched. Therefore
$$\partial_sH_0(0,u)=A\sum_h\widehat g(h)\,(u/2)^{|h|},\quad\partial_uH_0(s,0)=B\sum_p\widehat f(p)\,(s/2)^{|p|},\quad\partial_s\partial_uH_0(0,0)=AB/2,$$
and the first two expressions have sup norms at most $AM_0$ and $BM_0$.

Every retained monomial has low degree in at least one variable. Expand $H_0$ in the Chebyshev basis $T_j(s)=\cos(j\arccos s)$; let $H_1$ be the part of $H_0$ with $s$-degree at most $d$, and put $H_2=H_0-H_1$. Then
$$\deg_sH_1\le d,\qquad \deg_uH_2\le d,$$
because every monomial of $H_0$ has $s$-degree at most $d$ or $u$-degree at most $d$, and truncating in the Chebyshev basis cancels the terms of the second kind in $H_2$.

Each Chebyshev coefficient of a polynomial on $[-1,1]$ is at most twice its sup norm, and $|T_j|\le1$, so
$$\|H_1\|_\infty\le4M_0(d+1),\qquad \|H_2\|_\infty\le6M_0(d+1).$$
The Markov-type bound $|T_j'(0)|\le j^2$ together with $|T_j'(0)|\le j$ for degree at most $d$ yields
$$|Q'(0)|\le d(d+1)\|Q\|_\infty$$
for every polynomial $Q$ of degree at most $d$ on $[-1,1]$. Applying this in $s$ to $H_1$ and in $u$ to $H_2$ gives
$$\|\partial_sH_2(0,\cdot)\|_\infty\le AM_0+4M_0d(d+1)^2,\qquad\|\partial_uH_1(\cdot,0)\|_\infty\le BM_0+6M_0d(d+1)^2.$$
By the degree bounds, these are polynomials of degree at most $d$ in $u$, respectively $s$; applying the derivative bound once more and adding gives
$$|\partial_s\partial_uH_0(0,0)|\le M_0d(d+1)\bigl(A+B+10d(d+1)^2\bigr).$$

On the other hand, $\min(A,B)\ge20M_0d(d+1)^2$ together with $A+B\ge2\min(A,B)$ gives
$$\frac{AB}{2}\ge5M_0d(d+1)^2(A+B),\qquad 10d(d+1)^2\le\frac{A+B}{4M_0},$$
and, since $5(d+1)>1+1/(4M_0)$, the two displayed inequalities above force $AB/2>M_0d(d+1)(A+B+10d(d+1)^2)$, contradicting $\partial_s\partial_uH_0(0,0)=AB/2$. This contradiction completes the proof.

The coefficient lemma is used in the growth lemma below, where a Walsh test on $V_n$ is repackaged into functions on sign cubes. The repackaging uses the following transitivity of the general linear group on pairs of frames.

Frame lemma. The action $(U,V)\mapsto(HU,H^{-t}V)$, $H\in\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_2)$, is transitive on pairs of frames of fixed lengths with a prescribed matrix of pairings $u_i\cdot v_j$. Every binary $r\times s$ matrix is such a pairing matrix when $n\ge r+s$.

Proof. Let $(U,V)$ and $(U',V')$ be two pairs with the same pairing matrix. The covector frames define surjections $\pi,\pi':V_n\to\mathbb{F}_2^s$. The linear map $u_i\mapsto u_i'$ between the primal spans preserves evaluation, hence carries $\operatorname{span}(U)\cap\ker\pi$ onto $\operatorname{span}(U')\cap\ker\pi'$. Extending this restriction to an isomorphism of the two kernels and adjoining the map on the primal spans produces an isomorphism
$$\operatorname{span}(U)+\ker\pi\longrightarrow\operatorname{span}(U')+\ker\pi'$$
satisfying $\pi'H=\pi$ on its domain. To complete $H$ to an invertible map of $V_n$, choose a basis of the common evaluation image $\pi(\operatorname{span}U)=\pi'(\operatorname{span}U')$ in $\mathbb{F}_2^s$ and match lifts on both sides. Then $HU=U'$, and $\pi'H=\pi$ is equivalent to $H^{-t}V=V'$.

For feasibility in dimension $r+s$: given a binary matrix $D$, take $u_i=(e_i,0)$ and $v_j=(D_{:,j},e_j)$. These have the same length as $U$ and $V$, are linearly independent on each side, and reproduce the matrix of pairings.

Growth lemma. Fix an integer $m\ge1$ and constants $D_g,D_f\ge1$. Suppose $m$ and the parameters $a,b$ satisfy the hypothesis of the coefficient lemma. There is $c_1\in(0,\tfrac12]$, depending only on these parameters, with the following property. If
$$c_m(G^*)\le D_g,\qquad c_m(F)\le D_f,\qquad 0\ne T:G\to F,$$
and $n>4m$ satisfies
$$w_{n-2m}(T)\le2w_n(T),$$
then
$$w_{2(n-2m)}(T)\ge c_1\,w_n(T).$$
The operator $T$ need not have finite rank.

Proof. Put $n'=n-2m$. A Walsh test at index $n$ becomes a family of bounded scalar functions satisfying the coefficient lemma, and a coefficient of binary rank $k\ge2$ produces a Walsh test at index $kn'$.

Step 1. Exact and equivariant sign realisation. Choose families $p_u\in G$, $q_v\in F^*$ admissible in the bilinear formula for $w_n$, and change a global sign so that
$$\gamma=\mathbb{E}_{u,v}\,\chi(u,v)\,q_v(Tp_u)\ge w_n(T)/2>0.$$
Amplify $T$ over $V_n\times V_n$ and set
$$x'_u(\alpha,\beta)=(-1)^{u\cdot\beta+\alpha\cdot\beta}p_{u+\alpha},\qquad y'_v(\alpha,\beta)=(-1)^{\alpha\cdot v}q_{v+\beta}.$$
Translation invariance of the uniform law on $V_n$ shows that every $x'_u$ and $y'_v$ has norm at most one in the corresponding finite $L_2$ space. Writing $T'$ for the amplified operator, the phase computation
$$u\cdot\beta+\alpha\cdot\beta+\alpha\cdot v=(u+\alpha)\cdot(v+\beta)+u\cdot v$$
gives $y'_v(T'x'_u)=\gamma\chi(u,v)$.

Amplify once more over $\mathcal{G}=\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F}_2)$. Let $\widetilde T$ denote the pointwise amplified operator and set
$$x_u(R)=x'_{Ru},\qquad y_v(R)=y'_{R^{-t}v}.$$
For the bilinear form $B(x,y)=y(\widetilde T x)$ this gives
$$x_u\in B_{\widetilde G},\qquad y_v\in B_{\widetilde F^*},\qquad B(x_u,y_v)=\gamma\chi(u,v).$$
The right translations $\Lambda(H)z(R)=z(RH)$ on both sides are isometries, preserve $B$ under simultaneous application, and satisfy $\Lambda(H)x_u=x_{Hu}$, $\Lambda(H)y_v=y_{H^{-t}v}$, $\Lambda(H_1)\Lambda(H_2)=\Lambda(H_1H_2)$. The basic Walsh estimates give $w_j(\widetilde T)=w_j(T)$ for every $j$, and the amplified cotype bounds keep the moment lifts available with the same $a,b$.

Step 2. Lift frames while preserving the symmetry. For every primal $m$-frame $U=(u_i)_{i=1}^m$ choose $P_{U,\varepsilon}\in B_{\widetilde G}$ with
$$\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\varepsilon_iP_{U,\varepsilon}=ax_{u_i}.$$
These choices can be made equivariant. On one reference frame, take the lifts from the moment-lift lemma and average each lift over its stabiliser under $\Lambda$. The prescribed moments are fixed by this stabiliser, so each average is stabiliser-invariant; transporting to any other frame by $\Lambda(H)$ gives a well-defined equivariant family, because two transporters differ on the right by a stabiliser element.

The same construction for covector frames, with the action $H^{-t}$, produces $Z_{V,\delta}\in B_{\widetilde F^*}$ with
$$\mathbb{E}_\delta\delta_jZ_{V,\delta}=by_{v_j}.$$
The primal and covector lifts can be chosen independently. Their equivariance, the invariance of $B$ under simultaneous right translation, and the frame lemma show that the following pairings depend only on the displayed sign patterns and lift labels:
$$\Phi(S,\varepsilon,\delta)=B(P_{U,\varepsilon},Z_{V,\delta}),\qquad S_{ij}=\chi(u_i,v_j);$$
$$\phi(s,\varepsilon)=B(P_{U,\varepsilon},y_v),\qquad s_i=\chi(u_i,v),\quad v\ne0;$$
$$\psi(t,\delta)=B(x_u,Z_{V,\delta}),\qquad t_j=\chi(u,v_j),\quad u\ne0.$$
Every sign pattern is feasible because $n>4m$. The moment identities and the exact pairing give
$$\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\varepsilon_i\Phi(S,\varepsilon,\delta)=a\,\psi(S_{i,:},\delta),\qquad\mathbb{E}_\delta\delta_j\Phi(S,\varepsilon,\delta)=b\,\phi(S_{:,j},\varepsilon),$$
$$\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\varepsilon_i\phi(s,\varepsilon)=a\gamma\,s_i,\qquad\mathbb{E}_\delta\delta_j\psi(t,\delta)=b\gamma\,t_j.$$

Step 3. Control the odd parts on a smaller Walsh cube. For every fixed choice of lift labels,
$$\|\mathcal{O}_S\Phi(\cdot,\varepsilon,\delta)\|_\infty,\quad\|\mathcal{O}_s\phi(\cdot,\varepsilon)\|_\infty,\quad\|\mathcal{O}_t\psi(\cdot,\delta)\|_\infty\le w_{n'}(T).$$
Decompose $V_n=V_{n'}\oplus\mathbb{F}_2^m\oplus\mathbb{F}_2^m$ and fix a sign matrix $S_0=((-1)^{D_{ij}})$ with $D\in\{0,1\}^{m\times m}$. For $u,v\in V_{n'}$, take the primal frame $U(u)_i=(u,e_i,0)$ and the two covector frames
$$V^+(v)_j=(v,D_{:,j},e_j),\qquad V^-(v)_j=(v,D_{:,j}+\mathbf{1},e_j),$$
where $\mathbf{1}$ is the all-ones vector. The tags $e_i,e_j$ make both sides independent for every $u,v$; their sign matrices are $\chi(u,v)S_0$ and $-\chi(u,v)S_0$. Therefore
$$B\Bigl(P_{U(u),\varepsilon},\frac{Z_{V^+(v),\delta}-Z_{V^-(v),\delta}}2\Bigr)=\chi(u,v)(\mathcal{O}_S\Phi)(S_0,\varepsilon,\delta).$$
Both families lie in their unit balls and each depends only on its own Walsh index. Testing the bilinear formula for $w_{n'}(T)$ at index $n'$, and using amplification invariance, proves the first bound.

For $\phi$, retain $U(u)$ and use the covectors $(v,h,e_1),(v,h+\mathbf{1},e_1)$ with $s_i=(-1)^{h_i}$; the half-difference of the corresponding $y$'s gives the same calculation. For $\psi$, retain the covector frame $(v,0,e_j)$ and take half the difference of the primal vectors at $(u,e_1,h)$ and $(u,e_1,h+\mathbf{1})$. Their pairings are $\chi(u,v)(-1)^{h_j}$ and its negative, with the $e_1$ tags keeping the singleton vectors nonzero. This proves the remaining bounds.

Step 4. Find a Fourier coefficient of binary rank at least two. Define
$$f(s)=\gamma^{-1}\mathcal{O}_s\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\phi(\varepsilon s,\varepsilon),\quad g(t)=\gamma^{-1}\mathcal{O}_t\mathbb{E}_\delta\psi(\delta t,\delta),\quad \Psi(S)=\gamma^{-1}\mathcal{O}_S\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\Phi(\varepsilon S\delta,\varepsilon,\delta).$$
Each is odd; the slow-decrease hypothesis and the odd bound of Step 3 give
$$\max(\|f\|_\infty,\|g\|_\infty,\|\Psi\|_\infty)\le w_{n-2m}(T)/\gamma\le4.$$
Changing variables $s'=\varepsilon s$ in the first coefficient and using the moment identities,
$$\widehat f(e_i)=\gamma^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{s',\varepsilon}s'_i\varepsilon_i\phi(s',\varepsilon)=a,$$
and the odd projection leaves the coefficient unchanged; likewise $\widehat g(e_j)=b$.

For the row projection, the global odd projection does not change a first-row projection, since $\mathbb{E}_\sigma\sigma_ih(-\sigma S)=-\mathbb{E}_\sigma\sigma_ih(\sigma S)$. Substitute $\xi=\varepsilon\sigma$; the vectors $\xi,\varepsilon,\delta$ stay independent uniform. By the moment identities,
$$\gamma\mathbb{E}_\sigma\sigma_i\Psi(\sigma S)=\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\varepsilon,\delta}\xi_i\varepsilon_i\Phi(\xi S\delta,\varepsilon,\delta)$$
$$=a\,\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\delta}\xi_i\psi(\xi_i(\delta S_{i,:}),\delta)=a\,\mathbb{E}_\delta\frac{\psi(\delta S_{i,:},\delta)-\psi(-\delta S_{i,:},\delta)}2=a\gamma\,g(S_{i,:}).$$
Interchanging rows and columns, and simultaneously $(a,\varepsilon,\phi)$ with $(b,\delta,\psi)$, gives $\mathbb{E}_\tau\tau_j\Psi(S\tau)=bf(S_{:,j})$. Thus the coefficient lemma applies with the fixed parameters $m,a,b$.

Choose a binary matrix $P$ of rank $k\ge2$ with $|\widehat\Psi(P)|\ge c_*$. Since $\Psi$ is odd and this coefficient is nonzero, $P$ has an odd number of ones; the coefficient of $\Psi$ is then unchanged when the odd projection is removed. Writing $\Phi_{\varepsilon,\delta}(S)=\Phi(S,\varepsilon,\delta)$, row and column sign multiplication gives the exact identity
$$\gamma\widehat\Psi(P)=\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\Bigl[\Bigl(\prod_{i,j}(\varepsilon_i\delta_j)^{P_{ij}}\Bigr)\widehat{\Phi_{\varepsilon,\delta}}(P)\Bigr].$$
For some fixed $\varepsilon,\delta$, therefore, the function $h=\Phi_{\varepsilon,\delta}$ satisfies $|\widehat h(P)|\ge c_*\gamma$. No oddness of $h$ is required.

Step 5. Extract the independent Walsh factors. Keep $P,\varepsilon,\delta$ fixed. For tuples $(u'_i)_{i=1}^m,(v'_j)_{j=1}^m$ in $V_{n'}$, set
$$U_i=(u'_i,e_i,0),\qquad V(D)_j=(v'_j,D_{:,j},e_j),$$
with $D$ ranging over binary $m\times m$ matrices. The tagged tuples are frames even when the original tuples repeat or vanish. The Fourier coefficient formula gives
$$B\Bigl(P_{U,\varepsilon},\mathbb{E}_D(-1)^{\langle P,D\rangle}Z_{V(D),\delta}\Bigr)=\widehat h(P)\,(-1)^{\sum_{i,j}P_{ij}u'_i\cdot v'_j},$$
where $\langle P,D\rangle=\sum_{i,j}P_{ij}D_{ij}$ modulo two; the identity follows by translating the uniform law for $D$ by the matrix $(u'_i\cdot v'_j)$ inside the defining Fourier average. Both vectors have norm at most one, the first depending only on $u'$ and the second only on $v'$.

Since $\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_2}P=k$, there are binary $m\times k$ matrices $I,J$ with $I^tPJ=\mathrm{Id}_k$. For $\zeta,\eta\in(V_{n'})^k$ substitute
$$u'_i=\sum_{\ell=1}^kI_{i\ell}\zeta_\ell,\qquad v'_j=\sum_{\ell=1}^kJ_{j\ell}\eta_\ell.$$
The exponent becomes $\sum_\ell\zeta_\ell\cdot\eta_\ell$, which is the Walsh phase on $V_{kn'}$. Thus the identity produces a Walsh test on $V_{kn'}$ of amplitude $|\widehat h(P)|$, with separate unit-ball families on its two sides. Monotonicity and amplification invariance now give
$$w_{2n'}(T)\ge w_{kn'}(T)=w_{kn'}(\widetilde T)\ge|\widehat h(P)|\ge(c_*/2)\,w_n(T).$$
Taking $c_1=\min(1/2,\,c_*/2)$ proves the growth lemma.

Uniform finite-rank decay. Fix $m,D_g,D_f$ satisfying the growth-lemma hypothesis. There is an integer $n_0$, depending only on these parameters, such that every finite-rank $T:G\to F$ with $c_m(G^*)\le D_g$ and $c_m(F)\le D_f$ satisfies
$$w_n(T)\le\tfrac12\,w_{n-2m}(T)\qquad(n\ge n_0).$$
Consequently, with numbers $\rho_N\to0$ independent of $G,F,T$,
$$w_N(T)\le\rho_N\,\|T\|.$$
Both bounds extend to every $T:G\to F$ that is an operator-norm limit of finite-rank maps, under the same moment hypotheses on $G^*$ and $F$.

Proof. Let $c_1$ be the growth-lemma constant, choose an integer $K>\log_2(1/c_1)$, and put $n_0=4m(K+2)+2$. Fix a nonzero finite-rank $T$, and suppose the slow decrease holds at some $n\ge n_0$. The growth lemma forces $w_{2n-4m}(T)\ge c_1w_n(T)$, and by monotonicity this lower bound persists throughout $[n,2n-4m]$.

Set $a_n=\lceil 3n/2\rceil$. The $K$ consecutive blocks $[a_n+2m(j-1),\,a_n+2mj]$, $1\le j\le K$, lie inside $[n,2n-4m]$ because $2n-a_n=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\ge 2mK+4m+1$. The endpoint ratios telescope:
$$\prod_{j=1}^K\frac{w_{a_n+2m(j-1)}(T)}{w_{a_n+2mj}(T)}=\frac{w_{a_n}(T)}{w_{a_n+2mK}(T)}\le\frac1{c_1}.$$
Since $2^K>1/c_1$, at least one right endpoint $t$ satisfies $w_{t-2m}(T)\le2w_t(T)$, $t\ge3n/2$, and $w_t(T)\ge c_1w_n(T)$.

Repeat the argument at $t$. Every use of the growth lemma returns an inequality for the original $T$, so this iteration produces $n_j\ge(3/2)^j n$ with $w_{n_j}(T)\ge c_1^jw_n(T)>0$. The rank-dependent estimate $w_N(T)\le D_T2^{-N/2}$ for the single operator $T$ then gives $0<w_n(T)\le D_Tc_1^{-j}2^{-n_j/2}\to0$, a contradiction. Hence the slow decrease is impossible for $n\ge n_0$, giving the strict inequality above; the zero operator is immediate.

Iterating proves $w_N(T)\le\rho_N\|T\|$ with, for example, $\rho_N=1$ for $N<n_0$ and $\rho_N=2^{-\lfloor(N-n_0)/(2m)\rfloor}$ for $N\ge n_0$. Finally, if finite-rank $T_j$ converge in operator norm to $T$, the basic Walsh estimate gives $w_s(T_j)\to w_s(T)$ and $\|T_j\|\to\|T\|$; passing to the limit preserves both bounds.

Compact domain lemma. Let $m\ge1$ be an integer, $\Delta\ge1$, and $X$ a Banach space with $c_m(X^*)\le\Delta$. For every nonzero finite-dimensional subspace $E\subset X$, there is a Banach space $G$, an injective compact operator $J:G\to X$, and a contraction $\iota:E\to G$, such that
$$J\iota x=x\ \ (x\in E),\qquad \|J\|\le2,\qquad c_m(G^*)\le12\Delta.$$

Proof. Nested finite-dimensional enlargements come first. Construct nested finite-dimensional subspaces $E=E_0\subset E_1\subset E_2\subset\cdots\subset X$ so that every $z=(z_i)_{i=1}^m\in\ell_2^m(E_l)$ has a lift $H_l:\{-1,1\}^m\to E_{l+1}$ with
$$\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\varepsilon_iH_l(\varepsilon)=z_i,\qquad \|H_l\|_{L_2(E_{l+1})}\le3\Delta\|z\|_{\ell_2^m(E_l)}.$$
Take moments in the whole space. The map $U:L_2(\{-1,1\}^m;X)\to\ell_2^m(X)$, $UH=(\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\varepsilon_iH(\varepsilon))_i$ has the bounded right inverse $(z_i)\mapsto\sum_i\varepsilon_i z_i$, and its adjoint satisfies
$$\|U^*(\theta_i)\|_{L_2(X^*)}=\Bigl(\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\Bigl\|\sum_i\varepsilon_i\theta_i\Bigr\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}\ge\Delta^{-1}\|(\theta_i)\|_{\ell_2^m(X^*)}.$$
By the quotient-lift inequality the infimal lift norm of a unit tuple is at most $\Delta$, so a lift of norm below $2\Delta$ exists.

Take a finite $\eta$-net on the unit sphere of $\ell_2^m(E_l)$, with $0<\eta\le\min(1/2,\Delta/\sqrt m)$, and choose such a lift for each net point. Define $E_{l+1}$ as the span of $E_l$ and all finitely many values of these lifts. For a unit tuple $z\in\ell_2^m(E_l)$, choose a net point $v$ with $\|z-v\|_{\ell_2^m(X)}\le\eta$; the correction $\sum_i\varepsilon_i(z_i-v_i)$ makes the moments of its lift exactly $z$, and changes the norm by at most
$$\Bigl\|\sum_i\varepsilon_i(z_i-v_i)\Bigr\|_{L_2(X)}\le\sqrt m\,\|z-v\|_{\ell_2^m(X)}\le\Delta.$$
This proves the finite-level estimate for unit tuples; scaling and the zero lift cover the remaining cases.

The compact map comes next. Put
$$\mathcal H=\Bigl(\bigoplus_{l\ge0}E_l\Bigr)_{\ell_2},\qquad j:\mathcal H\to X,\qquad j(z)=\sum_{l\ge0}2^{-l}z_l.$$
Cauchy--Schwarz gives absolute convergence and $\|j\|\le(\sum_{l\ge0}4^{-l})^{1/2}<2$. The same estimate applied to the tails shows that the finite-rank truncations of $j$ converge to $j$ in operator norm, so $j$ is compact.

Let $G=\mathcal H/\ker j$ with quotient map $Q$, and define $J$ by $JQ=j$. The kernel is closed so $G$ is a Banach space; the quotient norm gives $\|J\|\le\|j\|<2$, and $J$ is injective. Every element of $B_G$ has a representative in $2B_{\mathcal H}$, hence $J(B_G)\subset j(2B_{\mathcal H})$ is relatively compact, proving compactness of $J$. Finally $\iota(x)=Q(x,0,0,\ldots)$ is a contraction and $J\iota x=x$.

The truncations of $j$ need not annihilate $\ker j$ and so need not induce operators on $G$; compactness of $J$ alone is what the construction yields, and the passage to finite-rank approximants will use AP of $X$.

Dual moments come from the weighted shift. The nesting of the $E_l$ makes
$$R:\mathcal H\to\mathcal H,\qquad R(z_0,z_1,\ldots)=(0,2z_0,2z_1,\ldots)$$
bounded. The factor $2$ compensates the next dyadic weight, so $jR=j$ and therefore $QR=Q$.

For $g_1,\ldots,g_m\in G$, choose representatives $z^i=(z_l^i)_l\in\mathcal H$ with $Qz^i=g_i$ and $\|z^i\|\le2\|g_i\|$, taking $z^i=0$ when $g_i=0$. Apply the finite-level estimate to each tuple $(z_l^i)_i$ and set
$$\widetilde H(\varepsilon)=(0,2H_0(\varepsilon),2H_1(\varepsilon),\ldots).$$
Summing squared estimates gives
$$\|\widetilde H\|_{L_2(\mathcal H)}\le6\Delta\Bigl(\sum_i\|z^i\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}\le12\Delta\Bigl(\sum_i\|g_i\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}.$$
Every atom of the sign cube has positive mass, so the same estimate shows $\widetilde H(\varepsilon)\in\mathcal H$ for every $\varepsilon$. The $i$-th moment of $\widetilde H$ is $Rz^i$, and $QR=Q$, so
$$\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\varepsilon_iQ\widetilde H(\varepsilon)=QRz^i=Qz^i=g_i.$$
For $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_m\in G^*$ this yields
$$\Bigl|\sum_i\theta_i(g_i)\Bigr|=\Bigl|\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\Bigl\langle Q\widetilde H(\varepsilon),\sum_i\varepsilon_i\theta_i\Bigr\rangle\Bigr|\le12\Delta\Bigl(\sum_i\|g_i\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}\Bigl(\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\Bigl\|\sum_i\varepsilon_i\theta_i\Bigr\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}.$$
Taking the supremum over $\sum_i\|g_i\|^2\le1$ gives $c_m(G^*)\le12\Delta$ by finite $\ell_2$ duality.

The main theorem now follows from the compact domain lemma together with the uniform decay theorem.

Forward direction. Suppose $K(X)<\infty$. On each finite cube the adjoint of the Rademacher projection on $L_2(X)$ is the corresponding projection on $L_2(X^*)$, so $K(X^*)=K(X)$. By the classical characterisation both $X$ and $X^*$ have Rademacher type $p>1$.

Type passes to cotype on the dual. If a space $Y$ has type $p>1$ with constant $D$, then $Y^*$ has cotype $p'=p/(p-1)$; for finite tuples $y_i\in Y$, $y_i^*\in Y^*$,
$$\Bigl|\sum_iy_i^*(y_i)\Bigr|=\Bigl|\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\Bigl\langle\sum_i\varepsilon_iy_i,\sum_i\varepsilon_iy_i^*\Bigr\rangle\Bigr|\le D\Bigl(\sum_i\|y_i\|^p\Bigr)^{1/p}\Bigl(\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\Bigl\|\sum_i\varepsilon_iy_i^*\Bigr\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}.$$
Taking the supremum over the unit ball of $\ell_p^m(Y)$ proves the claim. Applying it to $Y=X$ and $Y=X^*$, and restricting the resulting cotype inequality on $X^{**}$ to $X$, proves that $C_q(X)<\infty$ and $C_r(X^*)<\infty$ with $q=p'$, $r=p'$ after interchanging roles. This direction uses no approximation hypothesis.

Reverse direction. Suppose $X$ has AP and $C_q(X)\le C_F$, $C_r(X^*)\le C_Y$ for some $q,r\in[2,\infty)$ and constants $C_F,C_Y\ge1$. For an integer $m$, set
$$D_f=C_F\,m^{1/2-1/q},\qquad \Delta=C_Y\,m^{1/2-1/r},\qquad D_g=12\Delta.$$
The parameters of the moment-lift lemma then satisfy
$$ma=\frac{m^{1/r}}{24LC_Y},\qquad mb=\frac{m^{1/q}}{2LC_F}.$$
These positive powers of $m$ eventually exceed any fixed multiple of $(\log m)^3$, so a choice of $m$ makes the coefficient-lemma hypothesis hold. Fix such an $m$ and fix the decay sequence $\rho_N$ from the uniform decay theorem applied to $m,D_g,D_f$.

The first step is
$$w_N(\mathrm{Id}_X)\le2\rho_N\qquad(N\ge0).$$
For a nonzero finite-dimensional $E\subset X$, take $J:G\to X$ and $\iota:E\to G$ from the compact domain lemma, and apply AP of $X$ to the compact set $M=\overline{J(B_G)}$. For each $j\ge1$ there is a bounded finite-rank $S_j:X\to X$ with
$$\|S_jJ-J\|\le\sup_{x\in M}\|S_jx-x\|<1/j.$$
Thus $J$ is an operator-norm limit of finite-rank maps from $G$ to $X$. Since $c_m(G^*)\le D_g$ and $c_m(X)\le D_f$, the norm-limit assertion of the uniform decay theorem gives
$$w_N(J)\le\rho_N\|J\|\le2\rho_N.$$
Only the composites $S_jJ$ converge in operator norm; no common bound on $\|S_j\|$ is required.

Now fix any input $x=(x_u)_{u\in V_N}\in L_2(V_N;X)$. If $x=0$ there is nothing to prove. Otherwise set $E=\operatorname{span}\{x_u:u\in V_N\}$, take the corresponding $J,\iota$, and let $z_u=\iota(x_u)$. Then $\|z\|_{L_2(G)}\le\|x\|_{L_2(X)}$ and $Jz_u=x_u$, so
$$\|W_N^{\mathrm{Id}_X}x\|_{L_2(X)}=\|W_N^Jz\|_{L_2(X)}\le2\rho_N\|x\|_{L_2(X)},$$
which is $w_N(\mathrm{Id}_X)\le2\rho_N$.

If $K(X)=\infty$, the classical characterisation supplies $\lambda\ge1$ such that, for every $N\ge1$, there are $x_u\in B_X$, $u\in V_N$, with
$$\Bigl\|\sum_{u\in V_N}\alpha_u x_u\Bigr\|\ge\lambda^{-1}\sum_{u\in V_N}|\alpha_u|$$
for all real coefficients. These vectors form an input of $L_2$ norm at most one, while every Walsh output satisfies
$$\bigl\|(W_N^{\mathrm{Id}_X}x)(v)\bigr\|=\Bigl\|2^{-N}\sum_{u\in V_N}\chi(u,v)x_u\Bigr\|\ge\lambda^{-1}.$$
Thus $w_N(\mathrm{Id}_X)\ge\lambda^{-1}$ for every $N$, contradicting $w_N(\mathrm{Id}_X)\le2\rho_N$ and $\rho_N\to0$. This proves $K(X)<\infty$ and completes the proof of the main theorem.

The main theorem is uniform in the cotype data. Fix $q,r\in[2,\infty)$ and $C,D\in[1,\infty)$. There is a finite constant $\kappa=\kappa(q,r,C,D)$ such that every nonzero real Banach space $X$ with AP and
$$C_q(X)\le C,\qquad C_r(X^*)\le D$$
satisfies $K(X)\le\kappa$. Consequently there are constants $a,b\ge1$, depending only on $q,r,C,D$, such that for every $n\ge1$, all origin-symmetric convex bodies $K,L\subset\mathbb{R}^n$, and every $t>0$,
$$\frac1b\log N(L^\circ,atK^\circ)\le\log N(K,tL)\le b\log N(L^\circ,a^{-1}tK^\circ),$$
provided either gauge space $X_K$ or $X_L$ satisfies the displayed cotype bounds.

For the uniform bound, suppose for contradiction that a sequence $X_j$ with the fixed cotype bounds and AP has $K(X_j)\to\infty$, and set $Z=(\bigoplus_{j\ge1}X_j)_{\ell_2}$. Finite-coordinate truncations converge uniformly on each compact subset of $Z$, and approximating their finitely many compact coordinate images by finite-rank maps proves AP for $Z$; no uniform bound on the norms of those maps is needed. The identity $Z^*=(\bigoplus_{j\ge1}X_j^*)_{\ell_2}$ holds isometrically. For any finite tuple $z_i=(z_{ij})_j$ in $Z$, Minkowski's inequality in $\ell_{q/2}$ and the coordinate cotype bounds give
$$\Bigl(\sum_i\|z_i\|_Z^q\Bigr)^{2/q}\le\sum_j\Bigl(\sum_i\|z_{ij}\|^q\Bigr)^{2/q}\le C^2\,\mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\Bigl\|\sum_i\varepsilon_i z_i\Bigr\|_Z^2,$$
so $C_q(Z)\le C$; the same argument on the dual gives $C_r(Z^*)\le D$. The main theorem makes $K(Z)<\infty$. Restriction of each finite-cube Rademacher projection to a coordinate summand gives $K(X_j)\le K(Z)$, contradicting $K(X_j)\to\infty$.

The entropy duality uses the bounded-$K$ covering theorem of Artstein, Milman, Szarek and Tomczak-Jaegermann (their Theorem 5), in the covering-number formulation of Milman (Theorem 1.1, equation (1.6), and Section 2). That theorem supplies $a,b\ge1$ depending only on an upper bound for $K$-convexity of whichever gauge space carries the bound, and asserts
$$\frac1b\log N(L^\circ,atK^\circ)\le\log N(K,tL)\le b\log N(L^\circ,a^{-1}tK^\circ)$$
for origin-symmetric convex bodies $K,L$ under that bound.

Finite-dimensional gauge spaces have AP, so the previous paragraph bounds their $K$-convexity constants by $\kappa$. The polar identity $X_{B^\circ}=X_B^*$ and $K(X_B^*)=K(X_B)$ provide the same bound under polarity, so the reverse inequality is available. Also $X_{tL}$ is linearly isometric to $X_L$. Apply the covering theorem to the pair $K,tL$: from $(tL)^\circ=t^{-1}L^\circ$ and $N(t^{-1}L^\circ,cK^\circ)=N(L^\circ,ctK^\circ)$ for $c>0$, both inequalities of the corollary follow for every $t>0$, with constants depending only on $q,r,C,D$.

The characterisation also settles Pisier's conjecture on Grothendieck pairs, in the form stated by Gupta, Misra and Ray. For an algebraic tensor $t\in E\otimes F$ the injective and projective norms are
$$\|t\|_\varepsilon=\sup_{\substack{x^*\in B_{E^*}\\y^*\in B_{F^*}}}|(x^*\otimes y^*)(t)|,\qquad\|t\|_\pi=\inf_{t=\sum_ix_i\otimes y_i}\sum_i\|x_i\|\|y_i\|.$$
Their completions are $E\widehat\otimes_\varepsilon F$ and $E\widehat\otimes_\pi F$. Both norms obey the metric mapping property, so tensoring bounded maps multiplies the norm by at most the product of their operator norms; this follows directly from the two definitions.

Let $\mathcal E=(E_n)_{n\ge1}$ be a sequence of real Banach spaces with $\dim E_n=n$. Following Gupta, Misra and Ray (Definition 1.2), a pair $(\mathcal E,F)$ is a Grothendieck pair if there is $C<\infty$ with
$$\|A\otimes\mathrm{Id}_F:E_m\widehat\otimes_\varepsilon F\to E_n^*\widehat\otimes_\pi F\|\le C\|A\|$$
for all $m,n\ge1$ and every $A:E_m\to E_n^*$. The existence of the bounded extension is part of the condition.

The main theorem gives the following consequence. If $(\mathcal E,F)$ is a Grothendieck pair and $F$ is infinite-dimensional with AP, then $(\mathcal E,\ell_2)$ is a Grothendieck pair. In particular Pisier's conjecture (Gupta--Misra--Ray, Conjecture 1.4) holds, with AP in place of BAP.

./gupta-misra-ray-2025-grothendieck-variants-2305.13270.pdf

Proof. By Gupta--Misra--Ray (Theorem 1.3) the pair condition forces finite cotype of $F$ and $F^*$, so the main theorem makes $F$ $K$-convex. The complemented Euclidean subspace theorem of Figiel and Tomczak-Jaegermann, in the formulation of Pisier's ICM text (Theorem 4), now applies: for infinite-dimensional $K$-convex $F$ there is $D<\infty$ such that, for every $d\ge1$, there are bounded maps
$$U_d:\ell_2^d\to F,\qquad V_d:F\to\ell_2^d,\qquad V_dU_d=\mathrm{Id}_{\ell_2^d},\qquad\|U_d\|\|V_d\|\le D.$$

The metric mapping property and the factorisation
$$A\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\ell_2^d}=(\mathrm{Id}_{E_n^*}\otimes V_d)\,(A\otimes\mathrm{Id}_F)\,(\mathrm{Id}_{E_m}\otimes U_d)$$
show that the tensor operator with $\ell_2^d$ in place of $F$ has norm at most $CD\|A\|$, uniformly in $d,m,n,A$. Every algebraic tensor in $E_m\otimes\ell_2$ has its second coordinates in a finite-dimensional Hilbert subspace $H\subset\ell_2$. The inclusion of $H$ and the orthogonal projection onto $H$ both have norm one, so the metric mapping property shows that both tensor norms on tensors supported in $H$ agree with the corresponding norms computed in $\ell_2$. The uniform estimate therefore holds on all of $E_m\otimes\ell_2$, and extends to its injective completion, which is the Grothendieck-pair condition for $\ell_2$ with constant $CD$.

./pisier-1984-icm-finite-rank-projections.pdf

This uniform tensor estimate differs from Grothendieck's original injective/projective tensor-product conjecture, which Pisier's counterexamples refuted, and from the GT-pairs surveyed in Pisier's account of Grothendieck's theorem. The present statement concerns only the operator norm of $A\otimes\mathrm{Id}_F$ between the injective and projective norms, in the sharp form where $\ell_2$ substitutes for $F$.
