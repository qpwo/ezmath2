Random signed sums measure the curvature of a Banach space. Two questions turn that measurement into geometry: when is a space bounded away from Hilbert space, and when does it fail to admit a basis? Finite Rademacher cotype of the space and its dual decides both questions, under one axiom of approximation. Pisier conjectured that finite cotype of the space and its dual, in two arbitrary exponents, already decides both; the ordinary approximation property turns out to be the axiom that settles the question.

A Banach space X has the approximation property (AP) if, for every compact Msubset X and every delta>0, there is a bounded finite-rank linear map S:Xto X with sup_{xin M}|Sx-x|<delta. The operator norms |S| need not be bounded. The bounded approximation property (BAP) requires a single lambda<∞ with |S|lelambda for every such approximant. Enflo produced a separable reflexive space without AP, Figiel and Johnson showed that AP does not force BAP, and Szarek constructed a superreflexive space with BAP and without a Schauder basis. A Schauder basis therefore implies BAP, BAP implies AP, and neither arrow reverses. An argument resting on AP alone must tolerate approximants of unbounded norm.

"There is a Banach space which has the approximation property but fails the bounded approximation property." Figiel and Johnson.

For 2le q<∞, the cotype-q constant C_q(Y) of a Banach space Y is the least C with
(sum_{i=1}^s|y_i|^q)^{1/q}le C (𝔼_εBigl|sum_{i=1}^sε_i y_iBigr|^2)^{1/2}
for every finite family in Y, the signs ε_i independent and uniform. Finite Rademacher cotype means C_q(Y)<∞ for some finite q. A Banach space X has Rademacher type p, 1<ple 2, if for some D<∞
(𝔼_εBigl|sum_{i=1}^sε_i x_iBigr|^2)^{1/2}le D(sum_{i=1}^s|x_i|^p)^{1/p}
for every finite family. The constants C_q and the type exponent thus capture the size of random signed sums and thereby the uniform finite-dimensional structure.

The Rademacher projection on X-valued functions on the s-cube retains first-order coefficients:
(R_s f)(ε)=sum_{i=1}^sε_i,𝔼_eta[eta_i f(eta)].
The Rademacher cotype of a vector-valued function of bounded Walsh degree turns out to be comparable to that of a single variable, a comparison due to Borell; Kahane's inequality handles degree one, and the higher-degree statement follows.

Define
K(X)=sup_{sge 1}|R_s:L_2({-1,1}^s;X)to L_2({-1,1}^s;X)|,
with L_2 carrying the probability-uniform norm. The space X is K-convex if K(X)<∞. On each cube the adjoint of R_s is the Rademacher projection on L_2({-1,1}^s;X^*), so K(X^*)=K(X).

Maurey and Pisier identified K-convexity with nontrivial type, and Pisier's holomorphic-semigroup theorem completed the equivalence. The statement is
K(X)<∞ ⇔ X has Rademacher type p for some p>1 ⇔ X does not contain the ell_1^s uniformly.
The three properties hold without approximation hypotheses. The finite-cube definition of K(X) agrees with the infinite-product definition, because functions depending on finitely many coordinates are dense.

For cotype two on both sides and AP, Pisier proved a stronger conclusion: such an X is isomorphic to a Hilbert space. Pisier then obtained K-convexity from finite cotype of X and X^* under one of three extra hypotheses: a Schauder basis, a dense increasing family of uniformly complemented finite-dimensional subspaces, or BAP. Each of those hypotheses comes with a restriction on the cotype exponents q and r, and Pisier asked whether the restriction is necessary. The BAP formulation reappears as the cotype--cotype conjecture in work of Szarek and Tomczak-Jaegermann, and later as a question about uniform tensor estimates.

The characterisation under AP has a uniform finite-dimensional form, and that form feeds metric entropy. For an origin-symmetric convex body Bsubsetℝ^n, let X_B denote ℝ^n with unit ball B, and let B^circ be the polar. Write N(S,T) for the least number of translates of T covering S. Artstein, Milman, Szarek and Tomczak-Jaegermann proved that a bound on the K-convexity constant of either gauge space, in one direction, gives a two-sided comparison of log N(K,tL) with log N(L^circ,atK^circ); Milman's note restates the theorem with explicit covering-number constants. A uniform Cotype-to-K bound turns this into a dimension-free two-sided duality for gauge spaces whose bodies obey uniform cotype assumptions.

The main result is the following.

If X is a nonzero real Banach space with AP, then X is K-convex if and only if C_q(X)<∞ and C_r(X^*)<∞ for some q,rin[2,∞), with q and r independent of each other. The forward direction holds for every real Banach space, without any approximation hypothesis.

The result settles the cotype--cotype conjecture of Pisier in greater generality than the BAP formulation: finite cotype of X and X^* forces K-convexity whenever X has AP, with no relation between the two cotype exponents. The reverse direction fails without AP. Starting from a cotype-two space such as ell_1 and applying Pisier's embedding produces a space whose primal and dual both have cotype two and which fails K-convexity; such examples lack AP. The theorem isolates approximation as the precise additional hypothesis.

The engine of the proof is the Walsh-matrix norm. Put V_n=𝔽_2^n with its standard dot product and chi(u,v)=(-1)^{u· v}. For a bounded operator T:Gto F between Banach spaces, define
(W_n^Tx)(v)=𝔼_{uin V_n},chi(u,v),T x(u), w_n(T)=|W_n^T:L_2(V_n;G)to L_2(V_n;F)|,
and set w_0(T)=|T|. Finite-sum duality rewrites w_n(T) as
w_n(T)=sup|𝔼_{u,vin V_n},chi(u,v)y_v(Tx_u)|, 𝔼_u|x_u|^2le1, 𝔼_v|y_v|^2le1.

Basic Walsh estimates. For every bounded T:Gto F:
(i) 2^{-n/2}|T|le w_n(T)le|T|, and n|-> w_n(T) is nonincreasing;
(ii) if T_Ω:L_2(Ω;G)to L_2(Ω;F) acts pointwise as T, with Ω finite, then w_n(T_Ω)=w_n(T);
(iii) if T has finite rank, there is D_T<∞ with w_n(T)le D_T,2^{-n/2} for every nge0.
Moreover, for bounded A,B:Gto F,
|w_n(A)-w_n(B)|le|A-B|.

Proof. For the upper bound, interchange the expectation with the operator and apply Cauchy--Schwarz. For the lower bound, take an input supported at a single u_0, scaled so that 𝔼_u|x_u|^2=1; the output norm is then 2^{-n/2}|Tx_{u_0}| and the supremum over u_0 with unit norm recovers |T| up to 2^{-n/2}. For monotonicity, identify V_{n+j}=V_n ⊕  V_j: first apply W_n^T in the first coordinate, then the normalised Walsh transform on V_j with the identity of F in the second coordinate. The latter is orthonormal on L_2(V_j;F) and hence has norm one; averaging squared estimates in the unused coordinates gives w_{n+j}(T)le w_n(T).

For (ii), interchange the two finite coordinate systems. For (iii), factor the finite-rank T through a finite-dimensional Hilbert space H as T=BA with A:Gto H and B:Hto F. Orthogonality of the Walsh matrix gives |W_n^{Id_H}|=2^{-n/2}, and W_n^T=B_ast W_n^A, where B_ast acts pointwise through B. Hence w_n(T)le|B||A|,2^{-n/2}. For the continuity estimate, W_n^A-W_n^B=W_n^{A-B}, so the upper bound together with the reverse triangle inequality gives |w_n(A)-w_n(B)|le|A-B|.

Fix the length m and define c_m(Y) as the least C in
(sum_{i=1}^m|y_i|^2)^{1/2}le C,(𝔼_εBigl|sum_{i=1}^mε_i y_iBigr|^2)^{1/2}.
The cotype bound reads c_m(Y)le C_q(Y)m^{1/2-1/q}. Squaring and averaging inside a finite probability space Ω gives

and the same inequality holds after passing to finite-sum duals, so the constants survive any amplification that appears later.

The one estimate the moment lifts need is the L_1--L_2 case of Kahane's inequality, with an explicit constant.

Kahane moment comparison. For every finite Rademacher sum S=sum_{i=1}^mε_i z_i in a Banach space,
(𝔼|S|^2)^{1/2}le L,𝔼|S|, L=24.

Proof. Put M=𝔼|S| and b=max_i|z_i|. Since z_i=𝔼(ε_i S), one has ble M; the case M=0 is immediate.

Let S_j=sum_{ile j}ε_i z_i and stop at the first j with |S_j|>u. On a stopped prefix z, the remaining sum is symmetric, so at least one of |z+h|, |z-h| exceeds u, and hence the probability that a stop occurs is at most 2ℙ(|S|>u). For any fixed j, symmetry of the independent prefix gives ℙ(|sum_{i>j}ε_i z_i|>v)le 2ℙ(|S|>v). At the first stop |S_j|le u+b; decomposing according to the stopping time and using independence of the remaining sum yields
ℙ(|S|>u+b+v)le 4,ℙ(|S|>u),ℙ(|S|>v).
Take u=8M, so that Markov's inequality together with ble M gives ℙ(|S|>9M+v)letfrac12ℙ(|S|>v). Then ℙ(|S|>9jM)le2^{-j}, and integrating over intervals of length 9M,
𝔼|S|^2le81M^2sum_{jge0}(2j+1)2^{-j}=486M^2<24^2M^2.

A simple consequence of quotient duality is used repeatedly. If U:Ato B is a bounded surjection and |U^*b^*|ge D^{-1}|b^*| for every b^*in B^*, then
infbigl{|a|:Ua=bbigr}le D|b|.
Indeed, A/ker U is isometrically isomorphic to B under U, the quotient norm of the class of a equals sup{|< b,b^*>|:|U^*b^*|le1}, and the assumed lower bound makes the outer supremum at most D|b|. Every application has an explicit bounded right inverse, so surjectivity is never an issue.
