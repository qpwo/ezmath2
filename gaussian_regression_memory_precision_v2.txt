# Memory and precision in noiseless Gaussian regression




maketitle

Abstract: 
For every fixed A>0, a learner that keeps at most Ad^2 bits between
fresh exact Gaussian linear measurements needs
Ω_A(dlog(1/ε)) measurements to estimate a uniformly random
unit vector to angular error at most ε, for any
0<εle 1/10, with probability at least 2/3. The constant is
absolute when the memory is o(d^2) bits.

# Introduction and main result

A single exact linear equation y=ip{x}{s} pins the unknown vector s
onto a hyperplane. With independent Gaussian rows x_tsim N(0,I_d) and
exact labels y_t=ip{x_t}{s}, a handful of equations already carry
arbitrary precision: if all of them could be kept, d rows would
determine s almost surely. The question here is what happens to a
learner that may read each pair only once, keep a bounded number of
bits, and must eventually output an estimate. We show that exact
observations do not rescue finite memory: sharpening ε forces a
logarithmic-in-precision increase in the number of samples.

## The model

[Finite-state Gaussian regression]
Fix integers dge2, Mge0, Tge0. A learner is specified as
follows. A signal sin S^{d-1} is fixed before sampling. At index
tin{1,...,T} the learner observes
[
 x_tsim N(0,I_d), y_t=ip{x_t}{s},
]
where x_1,...,x_T are independent. The learner reads the pairs once,
in order. Its persistent state always belongs to a prescribed set of at
most 2^M elements. A transition may use the entire current pair
(x_t,y_t) and fresh randomness, but only the next finite state is
retained. The learner may stop at any index (including before the first
sample) but must stop by the deterministic horizon T. At a stopping
index its output widehatwidehat ssin S^{d-1} is generated from the terminal
state, the stopping index, and fresh randomness only. All transition,
stopping, and output rules may depend on d, on the target accuracy
ε, and on the index, but never on s.



The state bound counts bits, not reals. The horizon T is fixed in
advance. Accuracy means arccosip{widehatwidehat s}s{s}leε, i.e. the
angle between the estimate and the truth is at most ε.

Write sigma=sigma_d for the uniform probability measure on S^{d-1}.

## Main theorem and corollaries

[Memory and precision]
For every fixed finite A>0 there are constants c_A>0 and d_A such
that the following holds. Let dge d_A, 0le Mle Ad^2 an integer,
and 0<εle1/10. Draw Ssimsigma independently of the rows and
of the learner's data-independent randomness. If a learner as in
Definition~def:model has
[
 Prob{arccosip{widehatwidehat s}s{S}leε}getfrac23,
]
then its horizon satisfies Tge c_A,dlog(1/ε).


Throughout log is natural. The constant c_A depends on the fixed
number A; the statement makes no uniform claim as A=A(d)to∞.

[Subquadratic memory]
Let M(d)ge0 be integer-valued with M(d)=o(d^2), and let
0<ε(d)le1/10 be any accuracy sequence. A family of learners with
uniform-sphere average success at least 2/3 satisfies
T(d)ge c,dlog(1/ε(d)) for all sufficiently large d, where
c>0 is absolute.


[A guarantee for every signal]
The conclusions of Theorem~thm:main and Corollary~cor:sub
hold also when average success is replaced by the requirement that
Prob{arccosip{widehatwidehat s}s{s}leε}ge2/3 for every
sin S^{d-1}, the probability being over the rows and the learner's
randomness.


## Why the scale dlog(1/ε) is natural

Before proving that memory cannot help, recall why the answer
dlog(1/ε) is the right yardstick even with unlimited
memory. The Kaczmarz projection step
[cite] runs
[
 z_t=z_{t-1}+frac{y_t-ip{x_t}{z_{t-1}}}{norm{x_t}^2},x_t
 (x_tneq0),
]
and leaves z_t=z_{t-1} on the Gaussian-null event x_t=0. If
u_t=x_t/norm{x_t}, the error is multiplied by I-u_tu_t^{mathsf T}sfT.
Because the fresh direction u_t is uniform on the sphere,
E(u_tu_t^{mathsf T}sfT)=I_d/d, so conditionally
[
 E(norm{z_t-s}^2mid z_{t-1})
 =(1-1/d),norm{z_{t-1}-s}^2,
]
and induction gives
Enorm{z_t-s}^2=(1-1/d)^t. Hence
[
 Enorm{z_t-s}^2le e^{-t/d},
]
so tasymp dlog(1/ε) samples already bring the error down to
ε with real-valued registers. Theorem~thm:main says finite
memory cannot beat this scale either: it does not help to be clever, and
it does not help that the labels are exact.

## Relation to prior work

Memory-limited learning has an established lineage. Shamir's
finite-message framework [cite] and the minimax analysis of
Steinhardt--Duchi [cite] show that retained
information is itself a statistical resource. For discrete problems,
Steinhardt--Valiant--Wager [cite] isolated a quadratic-memory
versus exponential-sample question, and Raz [cite] proved
qualitative separations via a spectral method. For linear prediction,
Dagan--Kur--Shamir [cite] gave quadratic-space obstructions for a
null-vector task and for a correlated random system.

The closest continuous-regression comparator is
Sharan--Sidford--Valiant [cite], whose Theorem~1 uses
N(0,I_d) covariates, a fixed unit target, and independent
uniform noise of half-width 2^{-d/5}. With at most d^2/4 bits
and Euclidean accuracy d^{-r}, it yields an Ω(dlog r) lower
bound over a stated range of r. Its proof draws the target uniformly on
the sphere and averages over it and the samples. Our model follows that
finite-width layered program, with randomized rules and bounded stopping
handled directly; the decisive difference is the observation law. Noise
weakens a negative result---it cannot be used to conclude anything about
the more informative exact experiment. Theorem~thm:main
settles the exact-observation precision question for isotropic Gaussian
rows, and a short simulation (Section~sec:assembly) recovers the
Ω_A(d,rlog d) consequence for the noisy ``fine accuracy'' range
of [cite].

# The proof in one page

The proof never conditions on observed data. Conditioning the uniform
prior on one exact equation almost surely confines the signal to a
hyperplane section of the sphere, a law singular against sigma;
bounds would fight zero sets. We keep the original prior fixed instead and
study the probability of future success from an arbitrary starting
state.

Fix a state u at index t, reachable or not, and let h(s) be the
probability that, starting from (u,t) with signal s, the learner
succeeds using fresh future samples. The key object is a quantitative
statement about how concentrated h is. Set
[
 n=d-1, a=frac n2, q=leftlfloorfrac n8rightrfloor.
]
For d large, qge2, qge d/16, and age d/3. We say h:S^{d-1}to[0,1]
has local mass at scale R if

 int_{B(z,r)}h,dsigmale(Rr)^a
 qquadfor all zin S^{d-1}, r>0.

The scale R is an upper-bound parameter, not a posterior radius.

The whole argument is three moves.

[label=(roman*)]
 Terminal. From a terminal state the output is independent of
the signal. Success is confined to an ε-cap globally and to
a r-cap locally; taking the geometric mean gives
(eq:invariant) at R=ε (Lemma~lem:terminal).
 One block. A block of mle q rows routes to at most
Nle(q+2)2^M destinations. Two facts combine: the exact joint law of
(A,As) given a local restriction has a joint L^q density whose norm
exceeds the input by a factor of order exp(Cd)R^{a}r^{-m(1-1/q)}
(Theorem~thm:proj); and its common support is the projected
ellipsoid whose average volume is at most C_E^d r^m
(Lemma~lem:volume). H"older then restores the lost radius power and
charges only N^{1/q} for routing. Taking a-th roots, one block
multiplies R by at most
exp(Cd/a),N^{1/(aq)} (Lemma~lem:block).
 Streaming. Partition the stream into B=lceil T/qrceil
blocks. The per-block multiplier is at most e^{Λ} with
[
 Λ=C(1+(M)/(d^2)),
]
because N^{1/(aq)} divides log N by qasymp d, and log N carries
the memory budget. Chaining B blocks gives the invariant at scale
R=ε e^{Λ B} (Proposition~prop:stream). Since the ball
of radius two covers the sphere, total success is at most (2R)^a.


The memory enters only through the ratio M/d^2: quadratic memory
contributes a constant per block. At constant accuracy the block
inequality is too weak (the logarithm is not yet large), and a separate
symmetry argument supplies T>d/4 (Lemma~lem:reflect). The two
regimes join in Section~sec:assembly.

# Terminal states: how concentrated can success be?

A terminal estimate is chosen from a law independent of the signal. Its
success as a function of s is concentrated on a cap, and the cap has
small mass. We record both bounds and take their geometric mean.

[Cap mass]
For dge2, zin S^{d-1}, and 0<rle1/2,

 sigma_d(B(z,r))le r^{d-1}.



Proof. 
Rotate z to the north pole and write n=d-1. For sin S^{d-1}cap B(z,r),
the first n coordinates u=(s_1,...,s_n) satisfy
norm ulenorm{s-z}le r, and the last coordinate is
s_d=√(1-norm u^2)ge√(1-r^2)ge1/2, since rle1/2. Over the
disk norm ule r the graph area element 1/s_d is therefore at most two,
so the cap has area at most 2v_nr^n, where v_n is the volume of the unit
n-ball. Each hemisphere projects onto that ball with area element
1/s_dge1, so the whole sphere has area at least 2v_n. The ratio is at
most r^n.


[Terminal local mass]
Let Q be a probability law on S^{d-1} independent of the signal and
put
[
 h(s)=Q{v:arccosip{v}{s}leε}.
]
Then h has local mass at scale R=ε; that is,
int_{B(z,r)}h,dsigma_dle(ε r)^{(d-1)/2} for all
zin S^{d-1}, r>0.


Proof. 
Fix a unit output v with arccosip{v}{s}leε. Then
norm{v-s}=2sin(theta/2)lethetaleε, because sin tle t;
so the globally successful set for a fixed v lies in a cap of chordal
radius ε and has sigma-mass at most ε^n. Averaging
against Q gives int_{S^{d-1}}h,dsigmaleε^n, hence
int_{B(z,r)}h,dsigmaleε^n for every r. Now fix zin S^{d-1}
and r>0 (throughout the paper εle1/10<1/2). If rleε,
then 0le hle1 and Lemma~lem:cap give
int_{B(z,r)}h,dsigmalesigma(B(z,r))le r^nle(ε r)^{n/2}.
If r>ε, then int_{B(z,r)}h,dsigmaleε^nle(ε r)^{n/2}.
Either way the bound holds.


noindentWhat this says. Two constraints tug in opposite
directions: for a small ball success cannot exceed the ball's own mass, of
order r^n, and globally it cannot exceed the accuracy cap's mass
ε^n. The
geometric mean (ε r)^{n/2} has the same exponent in r and
ε, exactly the form preserved by one block.

# One block of exact equations: the projection theorem

This is the technical heart. One block of m exact rows turns a localized
part of successful mass into an exact joint law of (A,As); we must
control that law's density and its support. Throughout, gamma_m is the
law of an m-by-d standard Gaussian matrix on R^{mtimes d}, and
[
 lambda_m(dA,dy)=gamma_m(dA),dy
]
is the reference measure on R^{mtimes d}timesR^m. For a finite
nonnegative Borel measure mu on R^d, let eta_mu be the image of
gamma_m(dA),mu(ds) under (A,s)mapsto(A,As). The matrix is part of
the observed data; mu is not renormalized. Write v_m for the unit
m-ball volume. For a closed support ball B(z,R) set

 mathcal E_calE_{z,R}={(A,y):yin Az+AB(0,R)}.

This set is Borel (it is the zero set of a continuous function), and the
exact image is carried by it.

## Affine distance and the Gram determinant

[Tube and inverse-distance bounds]
Let 1le mle d, let pge2 be an integer, let
beta>m+p-2, H,R>0, zinR^d, and let mu be a finite nonnegative
Borel measure supported on B(z,R) with
mu(B(x,t))le H t^beta for all xinR^d, t>0. If L is an affine
j-plane with 0le jle p-2, then for 0<tle R

 mu{s:dist(s,L)le t}&le 3^j2^beta H R^j t^{beta-j},
 
 intdist(s,L)^{-m},dmu(s)&le J_j,H R^{beta-m},
  J_j=1+3^j2^beta(m)/(beta-j-m).
 



Proof. 
The projection of B(z,R) onto L lies in a j-dimensional ball of
radius R. A maximal t-separated set in it is a t-net with at most
(1+2R/t)^jle(3R/t)^j points: disjoint balls of radius t/2 fit inside
the ball of radius R+t/2. When j=0, use its single point. Any point of
B(z,R) within distance t of L is within 2t of a net point, so the
ambient ball bound gives
[
 mu{dist(s,L)le t}le(3R/t)^j,H(2t)^beta=3^j2^beta H R^j t^{beta-j},
]
which is (eq:tube). Since beta>j, letting t↓0 also
gives mu(L)=0.

The total mass is mu(R^d)le H R^beta (take the ball B(z,R)).
Using dist(s,L)^{-m}=R^{-m}+mint_{dist(s,L)}^{R}t^{-m-1},dt on the
support (where distle R) and Tonelli,

 intdist(s,L)^{-m},dmu
 &le R^{-m}mu(R^d)+mint_0^R t^{-m-1}mu{dist(s,L)le t},dt
 &le H R^{beta-m}+3^j2^beta mHR^jint_0^R t^{beta-j-m-1},dt
 &=(1+3^j2^beta(m)/(beta-j-m))HR^{beta-m}=J_j HR^{beta-m},

since beta-j-m>0.


The exponent beta-j-m>0 is exactly why Theorem~thm:proj below
requires beta>m+p-2.

[Gram product]
Under the hypotheses of Lemma~lem:tube, for the tuple
(s_1,...,s_p) and V=[s_2-s_1,...,s_p-s_1],

 intdet(V^{mathsf T}sfTV)^{-m/2},dmu^{ ⊗  p}
 le H^p R^{beta p-m(p-1)}prod_{j=0}^{p-2}J_j,

the integrand being assigned any fixed value on the
mu^{ ⊗  p}-null set of rank-deficient tuples.


Proof. 
Proper affine subspaces of dimension at most p-2 have mu-measure zero
(by Lemma~lem:tube in the top case L=R^d if p-1>d; otherwise
by slicing). Hence s_inotinaff(s_1,...,s_{i-1}) for almost every
tuple, so by Gram--Schmidt
[
 det(V^{mathsf T}sfTV)^{-1/2}
 =prod_{i=2}^pdist(s_i,aff(s_1,...,s_{i-1}))^{-1}.
]
Integrate first in s_p, then s_{p-1}, down to s_2. At the s_i step
the preceding span has dimension i-2 almost surely, so (2) contributes
J_{i-2}HR^{beta-m}. The final s_1 integral contributes at most
HR^beta. All integrands are nonnegative and Tonelli justifies the
order, giving (eq:gram).


## The Gaussian smoothing identity

[Gaussian difference identity]
Let d,mge1, kge2, delta>0, and t_1,...,t_kinR^d. Put
D=[t_2-t_1,...,t_k-t_1] and let phi_delta be the N(0,delta^2I_m)

 =(2pi)^{-m(k-1)/2}
 det!(D^{mathsf T}sfTD+delta^2(I_{k-1}+mathbf1bf1mathbf1^bf1^{mathsf T}sfT))^{-m/2}.

If D has full column rank, the right side is at most
(2pi)^{-m(k-1)/2}det(D^{mathsf T}sfTD)^{-m/2}.


Proof. 
Introduce independent N(0,delta^2I_m) vectors eta_i. For fixed A,
integrating prod_iphi_delta(y-At_i) over the common y gives the
density at zero of the k-1 differences
(At_i+eta_i)-(At_1+eta_1), 2le ile k; the change of variables from
k vectors to the first plus these differences has determinant one.
Averaging over A, each of the m rows of these differences is a centered
Gaussian vector with covariance
D^{mathsf T}sfTD+delta^2(I+mathbf1bf1mathbf1^bf1^{mathsf T}sfT), and the rows are
independent; evaluating the joint density at zero gives (eq:gaussid).
If G=D^{mathsf T}sfTD is positive definite and Q positive semidefinite,
det(G+Q)=det G,det(I+G^{-1/2}QG^{-1/2})gedet G. Apply with

## The exact projection density

[Exact projection density]
Let d,mge1 and pge2 be integers, let beta>m+p-2, H,R>0,
zinR^d, and let mu be a finite nonnegative Borel measure supported on
B(z,R) with mu(B(x,t))le Ht^beta for all x,t. Then eta_mu has
a nonnegative jointly Borel density F with respect to lambda_m, which
can be taken to vanish outside mathcal E_calE_{z,R}, and

 norm{F}_{L^p(lambda_m)}le mathcal BcalB
 :=(2pi)^{-m(p-1)/(2p)},
 H R^{beta-m(1-1/p)}
 (prod_{j=0}^{p-2}J_j)^{1/p},

with J_j as in Lemma~lem:tube.


Proof. 
Step 1 (smoothing moment). For delta>0 put
F_delta(A,y)=intphi_delta(y-As),dmu(s). Expanding the p-th power
and using Tonelli and Lemma~lem:gauss with k=p,

 int F_delta^p,dlambda_m
 =(2pi)^{-m(p-1)/2}
 intdet!(V^{mathsf T}sfTV+delta^2(I+mathbf1bf1mathbf1^bf1^{mathsf T}sfT))^{-m/2},dmu^{ ⊗  p}
 lemathcal B^p,calB^p,

where we used that adding the positive semidefinite covariance only
increases the determinant, that rank-deficient tuples are null for
mu^{ ⊗  p}, and Lemma~lem:gram. Note the exact
(2pi)^{-m(p-1)/2} under the root matches the prefactor in
(eq:projbound).

Step 2 (limit functional). For real psiin C_c(R^{md}timesR^m)
and independent Zsim N(0,I_m),
[

 =intE_Zpsi(A,As+delta Z),dgamma_m(A),dmu(s)
 →intpsi,deta_mu
]
by bounded convergence, since mu is finite and psi is bounded
continuous. By H"older and (eq:smooth),
|intpsi F_delta,dlambda_m|
lemathcal BcalB,norm{psi}_{L^{p/(p-1)}(lambda_m)}.

Step 3 (density by duality). The measure lambda_m is locally
finite, sigma-finite, Borel; eta_mu is finite, nonnegative, Borel.
The bounded functional psimapstointpsi,deta_mu on C_c extends to
L^{p/(p-1)}(lambda_m) with norm at most mathcal B.calB. Standard L^p
duality then gives fin L^p(lambda_m) with norm{f}_plemathcal BcalB and
eta_mu=flambda_m; taking a Borel representative and using

Step 4 (support). The image measure is carried by the Borel set

preserves the identity and makes F vanish almost everywhere outside it.



Theorem~thm:proj is the only place measure-theoretic care is needed.
The analytic content is entirely in Lemma~lem:tube and
Lemma~lem:gram; the passage from the smoothed bound
(eq:smooth) to the exact density is a standard Riesz-representation
argument, and we have included it so that the proof uses exact
labels, not a smoothed surrogate.


# How much room do the labels take?

The density bound of Theorem~thm:proj loses m(1-1/p) powers of the
support radius; the same powers must be recovered by the average volume of
the common support. The projected ellipsoid does exactly this.

[Projected volume]
Let 1le mle d, zinR^d, R>0, and let mathcal E_calE_{z,R} be as in
(eq:support). There is an absolute C_Ege1 with, for a standard
Gaussian m-by-d matrix,

 lambda_m(mathcal E_calE_{z,R})
 =v_mR^m,E_Asqrt{det(AA^{mathsf T}sfT)}
 le v_mR^md^{m/2}
 le R^m((2pi e,d)/(m))^{m/2}
 le C_E^d R^m.



Proof. 
Almost surely AA^{mathsf T}sfT is invertible; in singular-value
coordinates AB(0,R) is an ellipsoid with semiaxes R times the singular
values of A, so its volume is exactly v_mR^msqrt{det(AA^{mathsf T}sfT)}.
Hadamard's inequality bounds sqrt{det(AA^{mathsf T}sfT)} by the product of
the row norms, whose expectations are at most sqrt d each; independence
gives the first inequality. Also
(2pi)^{m/2}=int e^{-norm y^2/2},dyge e^{-m/2}v_mm^{m/2}, whence
v_mle(2pi e/m)^{m/2}. For t=m/din(0,1], since tlog(1/t)le1/e,
[

 =frac t2log(2pi e)+frac t2logtfrac1t

]
an absolute constant, so some C_E works.


# Routing among many destinations

All later estimates select among boundedly many continuation measures
supported on one common data domain. Selection costs only N^{1/p}.

# Putting one block together

[Backward block bound]
Let 1le mle q and Nge1. Suppose h_1,...,h_N:S^{d-1}to[0,1]
have local mass at a common scale R>0 in the sense of
(eq:invariant). Let g_j:R^{mtimes d}timesR^mto[0,1] be Borel
with sum_jg_jle1 and define
h(s)=sum_jh_j(s)int g_j(A,As),dgamma_m(A). Then for every
zin S^{d-1} and r>0,

 int_{B(z,r)}h,dsigmaleexp(Cd)N^{1/q}(Rr)^a.

Equivalently h has local mass at scale
R'=Rexp(Cd/a)N^{1/(aq)}.


Proof. 
Fix zin S^{d-1}, r>0, and mu_j=ind_{B(z,r)}h_jsigma, a finite
Borel measure on B(z,r). If an ambient ball B(x,t) has positive
mu_j-mass, choose a point p of its intersection with the sphere; every
other point of the intersection lies within 2t of p. The local bound
therefore gives

 mu_j(B(x,t))le(2Rt)^aqquad(xinR^d, t>0).

Apply Theorem~thm:proj with exponent beta=a, moment order p=q,
coefficient H=(2R)^a, and support radius r. The margin
J_ellleexp(Cd) holds for 0leellle q-2 by (eq:invariant)
parameters; absorbing 2^a, we obtain exact joint densities f_j,
supported on mathcal E_calE_{z,r}, with
[
 norm{f_j}_{L^q(lambda_m)}leexp(Cd)R^a r^{a-m(1-1/q)}.
]
These represent unnormalized successful mass, not posteriors.

Put q'=q/(q-1). By the exact image-measure identity, H"older on
matrix--label space, and H"older on the finite index set,

 int_{B(z,r)}h,dsigma
 &=sum_jint_{mathcal E_calE_{z,r}}f_jg_j,dlambda_m
 &leexp(Cd)R^a r^{a-m(1-1/q)}
   sum_j(int_{mathcal E_calE_{z,r}}g_j^{q'},dlambda_m)^{1/q'}
 &leexp(Cd)R^a r^{a-m(1-1/q)}N^{1/q}
   (int_{mathcal E_calE_{z,r}}sum_jg_j^{q'},dlambda_m)^{1/q'}.

Since 0le g_jle1 and sum_jg_jle1, the last integral is at most
lambda_m(mathcal E_calE_{z,r})le C_E^d r^m by Lemma~lem:volume. Its
1-1/q power restores the radius exponent:
r^{a-m(1-1/q)}(r^m)^{1-1/q}=r^a. Absorbing constants proves
(eq:block); the a-th root gives the scale update.


[Why the two radii cancel]
The localization radius r appears with opposite signs in the
density norm (r^{-m(1-1/q)}) and the support volume (r^{+m(1-1/q)}).
Their product leaves exactly r^a, so the block bound pays no price for
localizing. Only the destination count and absolute constants remain.
For a block of length mle q with Nle(q+2)2^M,
[
 log(R')/(R)le(Cd)/(a)+(Mlog2+log(q+2))/(aq)
 le C(1+(M)/(d^2))=:Λ,
]
since d/ale3 and 1/(aq)le48/d^2. Here is where quadratic memory
enters: qasymp d divides the log of the number of choices.


# Reading a whole stream

We now split the horizon into blocks of at most q rows and iterate
Lemma~lem:block. Two bookkeeping facts are needed: how many
destinations a block can have, and how to pass from completed-measurable
rules to Borel rules without changing the prior experiment.

## Destinations of a block

[Destinations]
For a fixed-seed Borel learner with state sets Q_t (|Q_t|le2^M), a
sample-free stopping check at each index, and the horizon treated as
terminal, let 0le t<T, uin Q_t, and 1le mle T-t. There are an
integer 1le Nle(m+2)2^M, Borel routes g_jge0 with sum_jg_jle1,
and Borel functions h_j:S^{d-1}to[0,1] such that, for every s,

 h_{t,u}(s)=sum_{j=1}^N h_j(s)int g_j(A,As),dgamma_m(A).

Each destination is either a terminal state (with output law depending
only on its state and stopping index) or a continuing state at index
t+m.


Proof. 
Pre-generate all m rows of the block. At each k=t,...,t+m-1 perform
the sample-free stopping check; if active, process the next pair into
the next state. A terminal tag from the last transition stops at t+m;
an active exit is passed to the continuation before the check at t+m,
so that check is performed exactly once. A terminal destination records
its local offset iin{0,...,m} and state in Q_{t+i} (with i=0
the immediate stop and i=m from the last transition); these outcomes
merge with same-state, same-index sample-free stops. Continuing
destinations form one extra copy of Q_{t+m}, distinguished from
terminal ones at that index. Thus
[
 Nlesum_{i=0}^m|Q_{t+i}|+|Q_{t+m}|le(m+2)2^M.
]
Because only local offsets appear, the count has no factor of T. For
arbitrary data (A,b), each g_j(A,b) is a finite sum of products of
Borel coordinate probabilities, hence Borel; conditioning on block data
then on the destination proves (eq:category) for every signal and
every starting state.


## Borel versions

[Borel reduction]
Let dge2, let pi be a Borel probability measure on S^{d-1}, and
let Xsimgamma_1 be independent of Ssimpi. Suppose

 mathcal L(X,calL(X,ip{X}{S})lllambda, lambda(dx,dy)=gamma_1(dx),dy.

Fix a horizon Tge0 and finite state sets. If every coordinate of the
finite transition/stopping vector is measurable in the lambda-completion
of the Borel sigma-field, then there are everywhere-defined Borel
transition and stopping kernels, with the same state sets and terminal
output laws, whose pi-prior joint law of the signal, state path,
stopping index and output is unchanged. The domination
(eq:domination) holds when pi=sigma is uniform, for every
dge2.


Proof. 
If f is lambda-completed-measurable, each dyadic superlevel set
agrees with a Borel set off a Borel lambda-null set; replacing these by
their Borel representatives in
f_n=2^{-n}sum_{k=1}^{2^n}ind{fge k2^{-n}} yields Borel f_n with a
Borel limit equal to f off the union of countably many null sets. Do
this simultaneously for all coordinates of all kernels; the (null) set
where a resulting vector fails to be a probability vector is repaired by a
fixed point mass. One Borel lambda-null set Z contains every altered
pair. Pre-generate all T rows; each pair (X_t,ip{X_t}{S}) has
lambda-null Z-preimage, and a finite union over t shows no altered
pair is ever visited almost surely. Coupling original and modified finite
choices by shared uniform randomness makes the state paths agree almost
surely; equal terminal state and index give equal output laws.

For the domination: condition on X=x, rotate to make x/norm{x} the
first axis, and write a uniform sphere point as G/norm G with
G=(G_1,G_perp). Then norm{G_perp}>0 almost surely and is independent
of G_1, and gmapsto g/√(g^2+R^2) (R=norm{G_perp}) is a smooth
increasing bijection of R with nonzero derivative. So ip{X}{S} is
absolutely continuous for every xneq0; Fubini over sections of a
lambda-null set proves (eq:domination).


The reduction averages original conditional success probabilities; it
never exchanges limits with a jointly measurable choice of Borel
replacements.

## The quantitative success bound

[Success after a finite stream]
There are absolute constants C,d_0>0 with the following property. For
dge d_0, every learner with Mge0 bits and integer horizon Tge0
has, for Ssimsigma independent of the fresh Gaussian rows,

 Prob{arccosip{widehatwidehat s}s{S}leε}
 leminBigl{1, [2εexp(Λlceil T/qrceil)]^aBigr},

where 0<εle1/10, a=(d-1)/2, q=lfloor(d-1)/8rfloor, and
Λ=C(1+M/d^2).


Proof. 
Use the angular score. Fix a prior-preserving Borel replacement
(Lemma~lem:borel) with prior sigma. If T=0, the output is a
signal-independent mixture of terminal laws, so total success is at most
(2ε)^a. Now assume Tge1; increase d_0 so qge2, qge d/16,
age d/3. Partition the T indices into B=lceil T/qrceil consecutive
blocks of length at most q. At the final boundary every continuation is
terminal and obeys Lemma~lem:terminal at scale ε.

Suppose at the end of one block all continuing functions obey
(eq:invariant) at a common scale Rgeε; terminal
destinations do so too by Lemma~lem:terminal and monotonicity in the
scale. For a block of length mle q, Lemma~lem:dest puts every
preceding continuation in the form required by Lemma~lem:block with
Nle(q+2)2^M. That lemma multiplies the scale by at most
exp(C_0d/a)N^{1/(aq)}, whose logarithm is at most
[
 (C_0d)/(a)+(Mlog2+log(q+2))/(aq)le C(1+(M)/(d^2))=Λ,
]
using d/ale3, 1/(aq)le48/d^2, and
log(q+2)/(aq)le48log(d+2)/d^2. Backward induction gives
(eq:invariant) at scale R=ε e^{Λ B} for every initial
state; averaging over the independent initial-state law preserves it. The
radius-two ball about any sphere point covers S^{d-1}, so total success is
at most (2R)^a. By Lemma~lem:borel this bounds the original
conditional success for almost every seed; averaging over seeds gives
(eq:streambound), and a probability is also at most one.


# Constant accuracy: a symmetry argument

For ε not small, the block inequality is too lossy; but a learner
that sees only T rows can never distinguish a signal from its reflection
across the row span. Even with unlimited memory and all data, accuracy
near 1/10 needs more than d/4 rows.

[Reflection]
Let dge1, Tge0, Ssimsigma. Let a T-by-d matrix A,
independent of S, have row span U with dim Ule T, and let
widehatwidehat SS be drawn from a kernel depending only on (A,AS). Put
u=P_US, v=(I-P_U)S. For any widehatwidehat SSinR^d and
0<ε<1,

 Prob{norm{widehatwidehat S-S}S-Sleε}
 lefrac12+(T)/(2d(1-ε^2)).

For a unit output, Eip{widehatwidehat S}S{v}=0 and
Eip{widehatwidehat S}S{S}le√(T/d).


Proof. 
Conditional on A, reflection across U sends S=u+v to u-v. It
preserves uniform spherical measure and the data (A,AS), because Av=0,
so the output kernel is the same at both signals. If norm v>ε,
the two reflected signals are more than 2ε apart; a fixed
estimate succeeds for at most one, so averaging the common kernel over the
pair gives success at most 1/2 on that event. Hence
[
 Prob{norm{widehatwidehat S-S}S-Sleε}
 lefrac12+frac12Prob{norm u^2ge1-ε^2}.
]
Spherical isotropy and independence of A give
E(norm u^2mid A)=dim U/dle T/d; Markov proves (eq:reflect).
For unit outputs reflection cancels Eip{widehatwidehat S}S{v}, while
ip{widehatwidehat S}S{u}lenorm u; Cauchy--Schwarz and the same second moment
give the expectation bound.



If Tle d/4 and εle1/10, then any learner (even with unlimited
memory and all pre-generated rows) has chordal, hence angular, success
probability less than 2/3.


Proof. 
By (eq:reflect), success is at most
tfrac12+(1)/(8(1-ε^2))letfrac12+tfrac1{7.92}<tfrac23.
Angular error leε implies norm{widehatwidehat S-S}S-Sleε since

horizon uses a kernel of the full data, so the bound applies.


# Proof of the main theorem

Proof. [Proof of Theorem~thm:main]
Fix A>0. Let d exceed the absolute threshold of
Proposition~prop:stream and satisfy age d/3, qge d/16. Put
[
 K_A=exp(C(1+A))>1, L=log(1/ε), B=lceil T/qrceil,
]
with C the absolute constant in Λ. Since Mle Ad^2 we have
Λle C(1+A)=log K_A. If prior success is at least 2/3, then by
(eq:streambound)
[
 tfrac23le(2ε K_A^{B})^a,
]
so ε K_A^{B}ge(1/2)(2/3)^{1/a}ge1/3 for large d (since
age d/3). Taking logarithms and using Ble T/q+1,

 Tge q((L-log3)/(log K_A)-1).

Tge qL/(2log K_A)ge dL/(32log K_A). If L<H_A, Corollary

[
 c_A=minBigl{frac1{32log K_A}, frac1{4H_A}Bigr}>0
]
we have Tge c_A,dlog(1/ε) throughout
0<εle1/10, Mle Ad^2.


Proof. [Proof of Corollary~cor:sub]
Since M(d)=o(d^2), there is d_M with M(d)le d^2 for dge d_M.
Apply Theorem~thm:main with A=1 for each dge d_M and each
accuracy 0<ε(d)le1/10; K_1=e^{2C} is absolute and c_1>0 is
absolute. The dimension threshold may depend on the memory sequence through
d_M. No relation between memory and accuracy is needed.


Proof. [Proof of Corollary~cor:worst]
Average the fixed-signal experiment over Ssimsigma before selecting
Borel versions; measurability makes the averaged success the integral of
the per-signal successes, so success ge2/3 for every signal gives
uniform success ge2/3. Theorem~thm:main and
Corollary~cor:sub then apply with unchanged constants and thresholds;
Lemma~lem:borel preserves the average even if a version changes
