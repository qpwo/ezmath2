e^{-V} on R^d, under a uniform second-order bound and a known
minimizer, when the only access to V is exact first-order queries.
For dge2 let V_d be the set of C^2 potentials
V:R^dtoR with

 V(0)=0, nabla V(0)=0,
 I_dpreceqnabla^2V(x)preceq2I_dquadfor every xinR^d .

The origin is therefore the unique minimizer and is known to the
algorithm.  The target law is
[
 pi_V(dx)=Z_V^{-1}e^{-V(x)},dx,
 Z_V=int_{R^d}e^{-V(x)},dx .
]

Oracle model.  An exact first-order query at a point x
returns the pair (V(x),nabla V(x)).  Queries may be adaptive.
Random real numbers may be generated.  Computation between queries is
unrestricted: only the number of queries is measured.  An algorithm
may depend on d but learns about V only through oracle answers.
All algorithms are measurable.

Write norm{mu-eta}_{TV}=sup_B|mu(B)-eta(B)| for total
variation.  Let Q(d) be the smallest integer q such that some
algorithm makes at most q queries on every execution and
returns X with norm{L(X)-calL(X)-pi_V}_{TV}le1/10 for every
VinV_d.  The budget is a uniform cap, not an expectation.
Define the optimal dimension exponent
[
 gamma_*=inf{gammage0:Q(d)le d^{gamma+o(1)}}
          =limsup_{dto∞}(log Q(d))/(log d).
]

[Main theorem]
In the oracle model above:
(a) for every ε>0 there is C_ε<∞ with
Q(d)le C_ε d^ε for all dge2;
(b) there are absolute constants c>0, d_0 with Q(d)ge clog d
for every dge d_0, even when inputs are restricted to quadratic
potentials V(x)=x^TAx/2 with Ipreceq Apreceq2I.
The lower bound holds for arbitrary randomized adaptive algorithms.
In particular, gamma_*=0.


The upper bound (a) is Theorem~thm:upper, the lower bound (b) is
Theorem~thm:lower.  Together they fix the dimension exponent but
not the finer growth of Q(d): the upper bound does not assert a
polylogarithmic query count.  The constants and the algorithmic
approximation orders may depend on ε.  No arithmetic or
bit-complexity bound is imposed or deduced.


Familiar samplers -- Langevin, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo, and their
discretizations -- are governed by a step-size analysis whose error,
at fixed conditioning and accuracy, typically grows like a positive
power of d.  Two recent lines of work break this pattern at fixed
accuracy: shifted composition (Altschuler, Chewi, and Zhang) and exact
diffusion simulation (Chen, Chewi, Rakhlin, and Zhang) achieve
widetilde O(d^{1/3}) queries; smoothed Picard methods (Chen, Chewi,
Lu, and Zhang) achieve widetilde O(d^{1/6}) expected gradient
queries and a Gaussian-cloud variant achieves widetilde O(d^{1/5})
at high accuracy.  All of these are positive powers.  The question is whether any positive power is necessary.

https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.23062
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.05022
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.06906
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.38710

The answer is no: the exponent can be driven to zero.  The cost of
this improvement is that the approximation order must grow with the
requested exponent ε; the algorithm is aimed at oracle
complexity, not practical running time.  The lower bound says that,
nevertheless, some logarithmic dependence on d is unavoidable.

Historical context for the individual tools is given where they are
used.  The Gaussian conditional-sampling framework is due to Lee, Shen,
and Tian and to Chen, Chewi, Salim, and Wibisono; the smoothed Picard
precedent to Chen, Chewi, Lu, and Zhang; the all-split tensor techniques
build on the classical Appell polynomials of Anshelevich, the Poincar'e
concentration bounds of Adamczak and Wolff, and the relaxed tensor norms
of Kothari and Steinhardt.  The lower-bound strategy adapts the
block-Krylov method of Chewi, de Dios Pont, Li, Lu, and Narayanan.

The proof has five movements.  Each is a construction plus an
estimate, and each estimates only one kind of error.

Movement 1: reduce to a near-quadratic problem.: 
Condition a target sample Xsimpi_V on X+sqrt h,G=y, where G
is a standard Gaussian and h>0 is small.  The conditional density is
proportional to exp(-V(x')-|x'-y|^2/(2h)).  Writing
x'=y+sqrt h,z, the normalized potential is |z|^2/2+F_y(z) with
F_y(z)=V(y+sqrt h,z), and Lip(nabla F_y)le2h.  Thus the
hard part is the following smaller, uniform problem.  For a C^2
perturbation F with Lip(nabla F)ledelta small, a center x,
and 0<rle1, sample or compute means of

 nu_{x,r}(dz)propto
 e^{-|z|^2/2-F(x+rz)},dz .

Both the sample and the conditional mean
u_{x,r}=E_{nu_{x,r}}nabla F(x+rZ) are constructed with an added
Gaussian, whose noise is later removed by conditioning.

Movement 2: Gaussian smoothing and dimension control.: 
A Gaussian convolution makes the conditional mean smooth although
F is only C^2.  Its derivatives are cumulant tensors of the
conditional law.  Two facts keep them in hand: a Poincar'e
inequality controls every tensor split, and keeping at least two free
tensor slots until the very end ensures each additional derivative
costs at most a polylogarithmic factor in d, never a fresh power.
Only the final passage from a tensor to a vector costs one sqrt d.

Movement 3: two flows.: 
The probability transport
Y_rho=rho Z+√(1-rho^2),N moves a Gaussian to xi_rho.
Its velocity is a conditional mean, hence another instance of the
auxiliary problem.  A complementary stationary flow in position
and momentum, built from a skew-matrix drift, turns a path integral of
gradient means into an exact Gaussian centered at the desired mean.
High-order quadrature of both flows uses the tensor bounds of Movement
2 and a singularity-cancellation at the endpoint.

Movement 4: finite evaluation with Gaussian noise.: 
Both flows become finite expressions in exact conditional means.
The difficulty: a mean is needed at a center that is itself built from
means.  A prescribed Gaussian is added to every computed center and
absorbed, via an exact covariance identity on the random seeds, into
the lower-level routine.  This restores the law of a call at the
intended center without changing any physical query.  Every routine
carries an error label and a size label; the labels make the recursion
finite and cap the number of queries on every execution.

Movement 5: return to the target and the lower bound.: 
A contracting Gaussian proximal chain, run for a prescribed number of
steps, approaches pi_V*N(0,hI_d).  The noised conditional sample is
then unnoised by variance-halving descent, with a mode-centered
Gaussian at the bottom.  The query count of the descent and of the
chain, multiplied by the per-call cap, gives d^{e(1+t)+o(1)}, and
t is chosen so e(1+t)<ε.  The lower bound is independent:
a random rotation hides the input, and a block-Krylov statistic forces
log d queries for quadratic potentials.



The conditional density is a Gaussian times a factor with small
Hessian.  A uniform guarantee is needed for that larger class; it is
stated below, together with the comparison and smoothing
facts used everywhere after.  The center is arbitrary: later routines
are called at centers produced by earlier random computations, so no
gradient bound at the center may be assumed.


Vectors use Euclidean norm; matrices the induced operator norm unless
stated otherwise.  For a random vector or tensor X,
norm{X}_2=(E|X|^2)^{1/2} with the chosen pointwise norm, and
norm{X}_{HS,2} specifies the Frobenius norm.  Wtwo is quadratic
Wasserstein distance, and
norm{mu-eta}_{TV}=sup_B|mu(B)-eta(B)|.  Laws and oracle
procedures are measurable.

Fix 0<e<1 and put delta=d^{-e}.  Let F:R^dtoR be C^2 with
g=nabla F Lipschitz with constant at most lambda=delta; values
of F need not be available.  For xinR^d and 0<rle1 define

 nu_{x,r}(dz)=
 frac{exp(-|z|^2/2-F(x+rz)),dz}
 {int_{R^d}exp(-|v|^2/2-F(x+rv)),dv},
 f(z)=g(x+rz), u=E_{nu_{x,r}}f .

Often nu=nu_{x,r}, and write

 L=lambda r, l=lambda r^2=rL,
 D(x)=sqrt d+|g(x)| .

The center x need not be a minimizer.  Since lambda is only a
declared bound, affine and constant F are included.  The full
potential in (eq:primitive-law) has Hessian between (1-l)I and
(1+l)I; as lledelta<1 for dge2 the normalizing integral is
finite even when F is not convex.  For all quantitative work it suffices to take
d large enough that lle1/2.

Throughout, polylog(d) is a factor bounded by C(1+log d)^C whose
constants may depend on the fixed parameters (e, accuracy orders,
and fixed orders introduced below) but never on F,x,r or the
answers.  Different occurrences may use different constants.  Every
threshold ``for large d'' is uniform.

[Noisy near-quadratic sampling]
Fix 0<e<1, an integer K_0ge2, and t>0 with

 40(K_0+2)t<tfrac14 .

There are constants and a threshold depending only on e,K_0,t such
that for every integer d above the threshold, with delta=d^{-e},
one measurable randomized oracle procedure works for every C^2
function F with Lip(nabla F)ledelta, every center x, and
every 0<rle1.  Given access to g=nabla F it makes at most
polylog(d)delta^{-t} evaluations of g on every execution and
returns
[
 X=X_{pre}+tfrac12G,
]
where Gsim N(0,I_d) is independent of X_{pre} and of all
queries, and

 Wtwo!(L(X_{pre}),
       nu_{x,r}*N(0,tfrac34I_d))
       le polylog(d)delta^{K_0}D(x).

Consequently both
[
 Wtwo!(L(X),nu_{x,r}*N(0,I_d))
 quadandquad
 norm{L(X)-calL(X)-nu_{x,r}*N(0,I_d)}_{TV}
]
are at most polylog(d)delta^{K_0}D(x).  All constants are uniform
in F,x,r.  The scalar parameters, computation shapes, and query cap
of the procedure do not depend on x or on F, and it uses no
evaluations of F itself.


The proof is carried out in the derivations below.  An
independent Gaussian is reserved until all queries are made: adding the
same reserve to a coupling keeps its transportation error, and smoothing
by the reserve gives the total-variation conclusion through
Lemma~lem:coupling-smoothing.  The target carries a full
Gaussian convolution; that convolution is removed later.

[Uniformly convex laws]
Let Hin C^2(R^n) satisfy mIpreceqnabla^2Hpreceq MI with
0<mle M<∞.  Write mu(dx)propto e^{-H(x)}dx and let z_0
be the unique mode.  Then
[
 E_mu|X-z_0|^2le n/m,
 Var_mu hle m^{-1}E_mu|nabla h|^2
]
for C^1 h with polynomial growth of value and gradient.  The
diffusion dX_t=-nabla H(X_t),dt+sqrt2,dB_t has invariant law
mu, and synchronous solutions contract in distance by e^{-mt}.
If barbar HH is another strongly convex C^2 potential with bounded
Hessian and law eta, then

 Wtwo(mu,eta)
 le frac1m(E_eta|nabla H-nablabarbar H|^2)^H|^2)^{1/2}.



Proof. 
Strong convexity yields Gaussian tails and a unique minimizer.
Integration by parts gives
E_mu(X-z_0)·nabla H(X)=n; strong monotonicity of nabla H
gives the moment bound.  A globally Lipschitz drift makes the diffusion
nonexplosive, and differentiating the squared distance of two
synchronous solutions against strong monotonicity gives the e^{-mt}
contraction.

Poincar'e.  First take H smooth with bounded derivatives of
order at least two.  For smooth compactly supported h let
P_th(x)=E[h(X_t)mid X_0=x].  It^o's formula and the semigroup
identity give ∂_tP_th=P_tAh=, where
A=-nabla H·nabla, and the second equality also
follows by differentiating P_{t+s}h=P_s(P_th) at s=0.  Then
intAP_th,dmu=0 by parts, so mu is invariant; coupling
to an invariant start and using contraction gives P_thtomu h in
L^2(mu).  Hence
Var_mu h=2int_0^∞!int|nabla P_th|^2,dmu,dt, the flow
derivative has norm at most e^{-mt}, and Jensen bounds the inner
integral, proving Poincar'e.

For C^2 potentials, convolve with smooth compactly supported
mollifiers of radii tending to zero: both Hessian bounds survive and all
higher derivatives are bounded for each mollification.  Normalized laws
converge in total variation by the quadratic tail bounds (subtract the
value at zero), and gradients converge uniformly by the global
Lipschitz bound.  Synchronous solutions of mollified and original
diffusions converge uniformly on bounded time intervals by Gronwall, so
invariance passes to the limit; Poincar'e first passes on smooth
compact tests, then by cutoff.

For (eq:synchronous-comparison), start the two diffusions from
their invariant marginals with common Brownian motion and let
a(t)=(E|X_t-Y_t|^2)^{1/2}, beta=(E_eta|nabla H-nablabarbar H|^2)^H|^2)^{1/2}.
Strong monotonicity and Cauchy--Schwarz give, in the upper-derivative
sense, a'(t)le-ma(t)+beta.  The marginals are fixed, so
Wtwo(mu,eta)le a(t); letting tto∞ proves it.


These apply to the auxiliary laws without convexity of F.  The mode
of nu_{x,r} satisfies z_0=-r g(x+rz_0); for lle1/2,

 |z_0|le(r|g(x)|)/(1-l),
 (E_nu|Z|^2)^{1/2}le C(sqrt d+r|g(x)|),
 |u|le CD(x).

Indeed |g(x+rz_0)-g(x)|le L|z_0| gives the first, the lemma gives
norm{Z-z_0}_2le√(d/(1-l)), and |u|le|g(x)|+Lnorm{Z}_2
gives the third.  Thus D(x) controls all moments uniformly in the
center, even where the gradient is huge.


Errors of a deterministic map on a fixed input law are measured in root
mean square; errors of randomized outputs in Wtwo.  The next rules
keep these two notions apart.

[Conditional comparisons and smoothing]
Let (mu_z),(eta_z) be probability kernels on Euclidean space and Z
have law zeta.  Whenever second moments are finite,
[
 Wtwo^2!(intmu_z,dzeta(z),inteta_z,dzeta(z))
       leintWtwo^2(mu_z,eta_z),dzeta(z).
]
For finite families, independent sums satisfy
[
 Wtwo!(L( a_iX_i),
             L( a_iY_i))
 lesum_i|a_i|Wtwo(L(X_i),L(Y_i)),
]
independence being required within each family.  For s>0,

 norm{mu*N(0,s^2I)-eta*N(0,s^2I)}_{TV}le s^{-1}Wtwo(mu,eta).

A common probability kernel cannot increase total variation, and
bigl|intmu_z,dzeta-inteta_z,dzetabigr|_{TV}
leintnorm{mu_z-eta_z}_{TV},dzeta.


Proof. 
Use conditional near-optimal couplings for the first inequality and
their independent product for the second, then the L^2 triangle
inequality.  Measurability of the kernels is obtained by quantizing each
output law on a finite grid inside a box, sending the complement to
zero, choosing a minimizing plan on the finite sets by a fixed ordering
of basic feasible solutions, lifting via the conditional cell measures,
and refining.  Finite second moments give the limit.

For centers x,y the relative entropy of N(x,s^2I) from
N(y,s^2I) is |x-y|^2/(2s^2), so Pinsker gives TV at most
|x-y|/(2s); integrate over a coupling and apply Cauchy--Schwarz for
(eq:gaussian-smoothing).  The total-variation rules follow by
testing functions valued in [0,1].


Deterministic root-mean-square errors are also combined using
absolute coefficient sums; this uses only the ordinary L^2 triangle
inequality and needs no independence.


A standard Gaussian is transported to the auxiliary law xi by
Gaussian interpolation, whose velocity is a conditional gradient mean.
The point of the Gaussian is that it smooths: although F is only
C^2, the conditional mean is smooth in the conditioning variable, and
every derivative tensor can be bounded.
The bounds are stated as matrix norms under every split of the tensor
slots, so later contractions never pay a dimension factor.  The
interpolation itself is the stochastic interpolant of
Albergo, Boffi, and Vanden-Eijnden (Section 5.1, equations (5.5)--(5.6)).

A tensor Tin(R^D)^{ ⊗  n} becomes a matrix by partitioning its
n slots into two nonempty sets A,B and contracting the A slots:
a linear map (R^D)^{ ⊗ |A|}to(R^D)^{ ⊗ |B|} between
Frobenius-normed spaces.  Its operator norm is the split norm
|T|_{split} for that partition; |T|_{split} means
the bound holds for every partition.  Permuting slots permutes these
norms, and |T|_F is the plain Frobenius norm.  The relevance: if
every factor in a contraction is bounded in split norm, the product is
bounded by matrix submultiplicativity with no dimension factor.

Multivariate Appell polynomials, introduced by Appell (1880), are used
(Anshelevich, Section 2.3); their vanishing expected lower derivatives
link Poincar'e to tensor
covariance (Adamczak and Wolff, Proposition 3.2 and Theorem 3.3;
Kothari and Steinhardt, Section 4.2).

[All-split cumulant bound]
Let S be a random vector in R^D with moment-generating function
finite near zero.  Suppose that for some betage0,
[
 Var(P(S))lebetaE|nabla P(S)|^2
]
for every polynomial P on R^D.  For nge2 let
kappa_n(S)=nabla_theta^nlogE e^{theta· S}|_{theta=0} be
the cumulant tensor.  Then for an absolute C,
[
 |kappa_n(S)|_{split}le C^n n^{Cn}beta^{n/2}
]
for every nontrivial split, independently of D and of the mean.


Proof. 
Covariances of centered polynomial tensors are first bounded without a
dimension factor, and cumulants are then recovered.  If beta=0, testing
coordinate functions shows S is almost surely constant and every
cumulant vanishes; assume beta>0.

Define symmetric Appell tensor polynomials
[
 P_j(s)=nabla_theta^j
 (exp(theta· s))/(Eexp(theta· S))Big|_{theta=0}.
]
For a coefficient tensor T of order j put Q_T(s)=T:P_j(s), colon
contracting all slots.  Differentiating the generating function in s
multiplies it by copies of theta, so
[
 Enabla^h Q_T(S)=0 (0le h<j),
 nabla^j Q_T=j!Sym(T),
]
with |Sym(T)|_Fle|T|_F.  Apply Poincar'e to each
component of nabla^hQ_T and sum, iterating h=0,...,j-1:

 E|T:P_j(S)|^2lebeta^j(j!)^2|T|_F^2 .

Hence for T,V of orders j,kge1,

 |E[(T:P_j(S))(V:P_k(S))]|
 lebeta^{(j+k)/2}j!k!,|T|_F|V|_F .

This controls arbitrary coefficient tensors, not just products of
one-dimensional test directions -- which is precisely what a matrix
split needs.

To recover cumulants while keeping a prescribed split, use the
two-variable generating function
[
 G(,omega)=
 frac{Eexp((theta+omega)· S)}
 {Eexp(theta· S),Eexp(omega· S)}.
]
Its derivative of orders j,kge1 at (0,0) is the cross-moment
tensor E[P_j(S) ⊗  P_k(S)]; across the split between the
theta and omega slots its operator norm is at most
beta^{(j+k)/2}j!k! by (eq:appell-covariance).  Since
G(,0)=G(0,omega)=1, every nonzero term of
G-1calG-1 contains both groups, and in
logG={mge1}frac{(-1)^{m+1}}m(G-1)^mcalG-1)^m only
mle n contributes at total order n.  Each differentiated product is
a tensor product of cross-moment operators up to permutations within a
side, so its norm is at most the product of theirs.  Distributing n
labeled slots among at most n ordered nonempty factors gives at most
n^{n+1} assignments, and the factorials inside any one multiply to at
most n!; enlarging the constant bounds the total by C^nn^{Cn}.

The mixed derivatives of logG equal those of
logEexp((theta+omega)· S), since the other two logarithms
involve a single group.  These are the cumulants, and the two groups
represent any prescribed nontrivial split.

[Gaussian jet bounds]
Under the above, in particular lle1/2, define for qge1
[
 U_q(rho,y)=rho^{-q}nabla^qM_rho(y)(rho>0).
]
These extend smoothly to rho=0.  With the vector-output slot counted
among the tensor slots, there is an absolute C such that for every
0lerho<1, yinR^d, and every split,

 |U_q(rho,y)|_{split}
 le C^q q^{Cq}L(sqrt a)^{-(q-1)},

uniformly in dimension, F, center, scale, and order q.  Moreover
DM_rho and U_1 are symmetric, and

 nabla H_rho(y)=y+rrho M_rho(y),
 |nabla^2H_rho|_{op}le C .

For jge1 every split of nabla^jnabla^2H_rho is bounded by

 C^{j+1}(j+1)^{C(j+1)}(sqrt a)^{-j}.



Proof. 
The conditional potential in (eq:conditional-tilt) has Hessian
between (a^{-1}-l)I and (a^{-1}+l)I, so by
Lemma~lem:convex-preliminaries its Poincar'e constant is at most
Ca.  Under this conditional law the map
z|-> S(z)=(z,f(z)/L) has derivative norm at most sqrt2 because
f is L-Lipschitz; pulling back an ambient test function shows the
possibly singular law of S satisfies Poincar'e with constant Ca.
It has exponential moments of every linear functional (f grows at
most linearly and the conditional law has Gaussian tails), so
Lemma~lem:appell-cumulants applies.  This repeated use of
Poincar'e never differentiates f repeatedly: each application pulls
back a fresh ambient polynomial and uses only Df.

Repeated differentiation of the expectation in the linear tilt
rho y/a gives joint cumulants, i.e.

 (U_q)_{i,k_1,...,k_q}
 =a^{-q}cum
 (f_i(Z),Z_{k_1},...,Z_{k_q}mid Y=y).

Restricting the cumulant of S to one f/L coordinate and q ordinary
coordinates, Lemma~lem:appell-cumulants gives a factor
L a^{(q+1)/2}, and the prefactor a^{-q} yields (eq:jets).

All conditional integrals are smooth in (rho,y) for rho<1:
differentiating densities inserts polynomials with integrable Gaussian
majorants on compact parameter sets, and (eq:jet-cumulant) gives
the extension at zero without dividing by rho.  For the integrations
by parts below, polynomial growth is also recorded: the conditional mode
satisfies z_0=rho y-ar g(x+rz_0), so |z_0|le C(|y|+r|g(x)|), and
comparing the density and its normalizing integral to Gaussian integrals
around the mode bounds moments.  The same holds for time derivatives;
dimension-dependent constants are harmless.

Conditional integration by parts gives
E[Zmid Y=y]=rho y-arM_rho(y); differentiating the marginal density
gives the first identity in (eq:interpolation-score).  For
rho>0 it implies symmetry of DM_rho, hence of U_1, and continuity
handles rho=0.  Its derivative with (eq:jets) gives the bounded
Hessian and (eq:score-derivatives).  All of this uses only the
C^2 regularity of F.



[Probability transport]
The ODE

 (dY_rho)/(drho)=-rM_rho(Y_rho)

transports xi_0=N(0,I_d) to xi_rho for every rho<1.  Its velocity
is globally Cl-Lipschitz in space, and on every compact probability-time
interval it has a smooth flow in both directions.


Proof. 
Along the random interpolation,
(d)/(drho)(rho Z+sqrt a,N)=(Z-rho Y)/a, whose conditional
mean is -rM_rho(Y) by the identity above.  Thus for smooth compactly
supported phi,
[
 (d)/(drho)intphi,dxi_rho=-rint M_rho·nablaphi,dxi_rho .
]
By (eq:jets) with q=1, r|DM_rho|_{op}le Cl, so the
velocity has at most linear growth and its smooth ODE has a
global-in-space flow and inverse on every compact interval in rho<1.
To identify the pushforward, fix a smooth compactly supported terminal
test and transport it backward; it has zero material derivative and its
support stays bounded by linear growth, and the display shows its
xi_rho-expectation is constant.


The remaining difficulty is not spatial smoothness (supplied by the
Gaussian) but control of derivatives along the transport while
paying only one sqrt d for a vector output.  That control is the next task.


Gaussian interpolation gave spatial derivatives of all orders from a
C^2 potential.  Differentiation now proceeds along the
transporting trajectories, since that controls the error when a path
integral is replaced by quadrature.  A naive Euclidean norm after each
differentiation loses a power of d each time.  The fix: keep at least
two tensor slots free throughout, convert to a vector norm only at the
last step, and bound every matrix split.

Keep L=lambda r, l=rLle1/2, and the interpolation
xi_rho=L( Z+sqrt a,N), a=1-rho^2, 0lerho<1.
Expectations and L^p norms use xi_rho at the displayed time.
Along Y'_rho=-rM_rho(Y_rho) the chain rule gives

Before iterating, the estimate making product terms
harmless is isolated.  Contracting one slot of each of two tensors means summing
over one common coordinate, keeping all other slots free; restricting
to one slot is what makes it matrix composition.

[A single contraction]
Let A,B be tensors, contract one slot of each leaving at least one
free slot in each.  For any nontrivial split of the resulting free
slots there are nontrivial splits of A and of B with
|A*B|_{split}le|A|_{split}|B|_{split}.


Proof. 
Call the two output sides input and output.  Orient the contracted
index from one factor to the other.  If all free slots of one factor
lie on the same side, its contracted slot must sit on the opposite side
of that factor's unfolding; these requirements are compatible, since
incompatibility would put every output free slot on one side.  If a
factor already has free slots on both sides it imposes none.  With this
orientation the contraction is composition of the two matrix actions,
plus identities on unused coordinates and coordinate permutations, all
of operator norm one; submultiplicativity proves it.


The following records exactly which tensor formulas are estimated and
which contractions are allowed (contracting two slots of one tensor is
not).

[Tensor expressions and weights]
A tensor expression is a finite formula built by:
(1) for each qge1, U_q/L is an expression with q+1 free slots and
weight q-1; (2) permuting free slots preserves weight; (3) contracting
one chosen slot of two expressions, each keeping a free slot, adds the
weights; (4) an adjoint divergence on a chosen free slot is allowed
while at least two slots remain and raises the weight by one.  The
formula fixes its leaves, orders, and slots, a finite syntactic datum
independent of d,rho,x,r and F.  Repeated occurrences are separate
factors even when equal in value.


For instance nabla^*(U_2/L) has two slots and weight two, while
nabla^*(U_1/L) is not an expression (vector output) -- the final
sqrt d.  If E is an expression, nabla^jE has j more slots, a
split placing any on either side.  The next theorem bounds all splits at
once, with explicit dependence on j and p because a later proof
takes a moment order growing with log d.

[Tensor divergence bound]
Let E be a tensor expression of weight k.  There is C_Ege1,
depending only on the finite formula, such that for every integer
jge0, every real 2le p<∞, every 0lerho<1, and every
nontrivial split,

 bigl|,|nabla^jE|_{split},bigr|_{L^p(xi_rho)}
 le C_E^{j+1}
   (p+j+log(d+1))^{C_E(j+1)}(sqrt a)^{-(k+j)} .

The same C_E works for all j,p,d,rho,x,r.


Proof. 
Induct on the finite construction of E, proving all derivative and
moment orders at once.  Permutations and single contractions follow from
Lemma~lem:split-contraction.  A normalized jet has
nabla^jU_q=rho^jU_{q+j}, so Proposition~prop:gaussian-jets
handles leaves after enlarging a constant in q.  For a contraction,
distribute derivatives by Leibniz (at most 2^j summands), apply
Lemma~lem:split-contraction to each, and use the inductive
estimates at exponent 2p, giving (eq:network) with an enlarged
constant.

It remains to consider E=nabla^*V, V of weight k_V, E with at
least two slots.  Commuting j derivatives past the divergence leaves a
main term T=nabla^*(nabla^jV); the others are single contractions of
nabla^{j-h}V with an order-h score derivative, 1le hle j, each
bounded pointwise by C^hh^{Ch}(sqrt a)^{-(h-1)} via
(eq:interpolation-score)--(eq:score-derivatives), so
Lemma~lem:split-contraction, the inductive hypothesis, and
Leibniz multiplicities bound them by the required power.  Only T
remains.

Removing the adjoints.  Fix an unfolding of T (both sides
nonempty) and sge1.  Pointwise |T|_{op}^{2s}le
tr((TT^T)^s); expand the trace by indices, giving 2s labeled primary
factors, each an adjoint divergence of nabla^jV, joined in the
alternating trace cycle with nonempty incident bundles.  Remove adjoints
one at a time by parts; a chosen primary differentiates the product of
the others.  If a derivative hits a remaining adjoint, use
∂_i(nabla^*W)=nabla^*(∂_iW)
+(∂_i∂_kH_rho)W_k: the first term leaves that adjoint,
the second consumes it and creates a Hessian factor joining the two
divergence indices.  After all removals a typical term has primary
factors nabla^{j+e_i}V, 1le ile2s, and n_H factors
nabla^{h_v}nabla^2H_rho, with

 sum_{i=1}^{2s}e_i+sum_{v=1}^{n_H}h_v+2n_H=2s,

since a removed adjoint either adds one derivative or pairs with another
to create a Hessian.  Each step has at most Cs targets and two
commutator terms, so at most (Cs)^{Cs} terms result.

Use a graph only to describe these sums: primary vertices are the 2s
labeled tensor factors, a Hessian vertex is one factor
nabla^{h_v}nabla^2H_rho, an edge is a summed slot pair, a bundle
several such edges.  Three properties control contraction: all original
trace bundles remain; every new direct edge joins distinct primaries (an
adjoint never differentiates its own field); every Hessian has at least
two distinct primary neighbors (its creators) and no Hessian-Hessian
edges.

Opening the trace.  Cut one bundle of the original trace cycle,
leaving bge1 indices unsummed at its ends; the rest form a chain
through the primaries.  Order primaries along it.  Each Hessian has two
distinct creating primaries at different positions; place it strictly
between its earliest and latest neighbors (possible because no
Hessian-Hessian edge).  Order Hessians in the same gap arbitrarily and
orient every uncut edge from earlier to later.  The b cut indices
enter the first primary and leave the last.

Every tensor now has an input and an output.  An interior primary
receives one original chain bundle and sends the next, with the cut
bundle supplying the missing side at the endpoints; a Hessian receives
from an earlier and sends to a later neighbor.  Thus all matrix
unfoldings are nontrivial splits, as required.  Concretely, retain one
copy of R^d per index whose earlier endpoint is processed but later is
not, start from (R^d)^{ ⊗  b} on the cut inputs, permute the
incoming indices of the next factor adjacent, apply that factor's
unfolding and the identity on the rest, then permute the outputs; matrix
multiplication performs the sums.  If Q maps
(R^d)^{ ⊗  b}to(R^d)^{ ⊗  b} is the induced map,
[
 |Q|_{op}leprod_{i=1}^{2s}|nabla^{j+e_i}V|_{split}
   prod_{v=1}^{n_H}|nabla^{h_v}nabla^2H_rho|_{split},
]
permutations and identities having norm one, and closing the cut is one
matrix trace:

 |tr Q|le d^b|Q|_{op}.

That is why other cycles add no dimension factor -- their sums are
already done by the compositions.

Uniform high-moment bookkeeping.  Apply the inductive hypothesis to
the 2s primaries at exponent 2s and the pointwise Hessian estimates
elsewhere (H"older uses no larger exponent).  The power of
1/sqrt a before the 2s-th root is at most
2s(k_V+j)+sum_ie_i+sum_vh_vle2s(k_V+j+1); polynomial bases are at
most C(s+j+log(d+1)), with total exponent at most
[

]
by (eq:network-degree-count), absorbing (Cs)^{Cs}.  The cut

[
 E|T|_{op}^{2s}le d^{j+C_V}
 [C(s+j+log(d+1))]^{Cs(j+1)}(sqrt a)^{-2s(k_V+j+1)}.

Take s smallest with 2sge p and sge(j+C_V)log(d+1); then
d^{(j+C_V)/(2s)}le e^{1/2} and

The probability transport and the stationary centering flow are now turned
into finite formulas in conditional means, meeting two requirements at
once: small error on the prescribed input law, and each new mean
evaluated at a center that is an affine expression in earlier means with
scalar coefficients and controlled size.  The latter is what permits the
recursive implementation described later.

The main conclusion is Theorem~thm:numerical-interface.  Three
numerical steps: global Picard iteration discretizes probability
transport without dependency depth proportional to the number of time
cells; short harmonic trajectories give a formula for K(y)^TG; Picard
iteration discretizes the stationary flow.  Two cancellations matter: a
zero derivative removes the singularity at the lower endpoint of the
kernel integral, and keeping only a forward transport correction
cancels an apparent inverse power of r.  Polynomial collocation of
Picard iteration is due to Lee, Song, and Vempala (Algorithm 2 and
Theorem 2.3); Gaussian smoothing and high-order Picard to Chen, Chewi,
Lu, and Zhang (Appendix E, Algorithm E.1, and Theorem E.2).

A deterministic formula that calls for a conditional mean at a center
built from other conditional means is awkward: its error changes the next
center, and the next call is analyzed at a random center.  A prescribed
Gaussian is instead added to every computed center.  A translation
identity of the lower-level routine absorbs that Gaussian into its random
seeds, restoring the law of a call at the intended center, which is fixed
after conditioning on the parent's random anchors.

The identity is proved first, then the full recursive construction
(termination, per-execution query cap, distributional error).  The
analytical input is the family of affine formulas in exact means supplied
by Theorem~thm:numerical-interface.  These are implemented below,
proving Theorem~thm:primitive.

The remainder satisfies
|R_{d,q}|/(dρ^n/sqrt2)le2ρ^{2d-2n}/(1-ρ)to0
uniformly since nle1+2c_0log d, hence is negligible; with
T_{d,q}=160(q+1)^2sqrt d, |mu_{d,q}|>T_{d,q} holds for large d,
and by Chebyshev
[
 P_{target}(E_{d,q}^c)
 lefrac2d+frac{2d}{(|mu_{d,q}|-T_{d,q})^2}=o(1)
]
uniformly.  Thus the two joint experiments' probabilities of
E_{d,q} differ by at least 0.99-o(1).

To compare with the guarantee on each fixed input, let K_O be the
output law on A=OΛ O^T and G_O=N(0,A^{-1}); for a joint event
E write E_O={x:(O,x)inE}.  Both laws use
the same Haar prior, so

 |P_{alg}(E)-calE)-P_{target}(E)|calE)|
 &=|int[K_O(E_O)-G_O(calE_O)-G_O(E_O)]calE_O)],dHaar(O)|
 &leint|K_O-G_O|_{TV},dHaar(O).

The pointwise guarantee would bound this by 1/10, and the event testing
the joint laws need not be computable by the algorithm.  This is impossible
for (eq:lower-separating-event).  So no algorithm with
qle c_0log d meets the accuracy for all inputs; decreasing the constant
proves the theorem.
