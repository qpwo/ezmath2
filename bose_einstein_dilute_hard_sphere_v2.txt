The physics of Bose-Einstein condensation at positive temperature is
well understood in the dilute regime. Fix a hard-sphere diameter $a$
and a number density $\rho$. A long cylinder in $\R^3$ filled with
bosons of mass $m$ at inverse temperature $\beta$, interacting
through an exclusion ball of radius $a$, becomes a Bose-Einstein
condensate below a critical temperature roughly $2\pi\rho^{2/3}/m$
at low $\rho a^3$. The question to be settled concerns the exact
canonical Gibbs state: does the largest eigenvalue of its one-particle
reduced density matrix carry a volume-uniform positive fraction at
positive temperature?

Bose introduced the quantum statistics that bear both names when
deriving Planck's distribution from indistinguishable light quanta
in 1924, and Einstein extended the counting to a material gas in
two short notes. Einstein's second note put forward a two-fluid
picture and argued that a macroscopic fraction of particles must
occupy the lowest one-particle state below a critical density. The
argument is thermodynamic; the phenomenon is called Bose-Einstein
condensation. Penrose and Onsager restated the property for liquid
helium in terms of the largest eigenvalue of the one-particle density
matrix; Yang later showed that this off-diagonal long-range order is
the correct order parameter linking the effect to superfluidity and
to superconductivity.

At zero temperature, Bogoliubov's variational principle produces the
ground-state energy and low-lying excitations of a weakly interacting
bosonic system from a c-number substitution $a_0 \approx \sqrt{N_0}$
followed by a Gaussian truncation of pair terms. The scheme gives a
mean-field energy $4\pi a \rho N$ plus corrections, and the
second-order corrections were computed from perturbation theory by
Lee, Huang and Yang. Dyson turned the mean-field term into a matched
upper and lower bound in the hard-sphere gas, using a Jastrow trial
function that inserts an explicit Bose pair factor. Lieb and Yngvason
removed Dyson's smallness restriction on the interaction strength
and produced the leading-order asymptotic at any density.

[./bogoliubov-1947.pdf]
[./arxiv-lieb-yngvason-1998-cond-mat-9712138.pdf]

The ground-state asymptotics has since been pushed to the
Lee-Huang-Yang order. Positive-temperature condensation for dilute
gases was proved by Lieb and Seiringer in the Gross-Pitaevskii regime
and by Deuchert, Seiringer and Yngvason in the canonical ensemble; an
earlier variational treatment by Lieb, Seiringer and Yngvason supplies
the normalized insertion apparatus that a thermal argument uses.

[./arxiv-lieb-seiringer-2002-math-ph-0112032.pdf]
[./arxiv-deuchert-seiringer-2020-1901.11363.pdf]
[./arxiv-lieb-seiringer-yngvason-2000-math-ph-9908027.pdf]

Four probabilistic devices organize the argument. A thermal slab
represents the Gibbs state as a squared nonnegative trajectory
density. A two-particle bound packages the kinetic cost of crowding.
An exponential moment for many-body path statistics is proved
through a coloured heat semigroup. A comparison procedure draws
random lattice routes between dense cells, tags one path per cell,
and joins two probability measures through an interpolated likelihood.
Three classical sources enter without proof: strong subadditivity of
quantum entropy in the form given by Lieb and Ruskai, boundary
connectivity of exterior shells of finite connected lattice sets in
the quantitative form given by Timar, and the two-route second-moment
method of Peres and Sly. Feynman's reformulation of the Gibbs weight
as a trajectory distribution and Ueltschi's cycle decomposition
supply the path language throughout.

The proof separates the thermodynamic limit from the smallness of the
gas parameter. During the main estimate the unit cell is taken to
contain many particles, so the density D = N/V is large, while the
exclusion radius r is far below the typical interparticle separation.
A closing change of units returns to fixed physical density and
temperature.

Fix an integer K >= 100 and set $\Lambda_K=(\R/K\Z)^3$, with volume
$V=K^3$. The parameters are

$$ N/V=D, \qquad r=\alpha/D, \qquad \alpha_0/2\le\alpha\le2\alpha_0,
   \qquad 0<T\le D^{-1}, \qquad \beta=1/T. $$

The number $\alpha_0\ge2$ is fixed in advance, and $D$ is then taken
large. Admissible $j$-particle configurations form the set

$$ \Omega_j=\{X\in\Lambda_K^j : d(x_i,x_k)>r \text{ for } i\ne k\}, $$

with $d$ the Euclidean torus distance, and the associated kinetic form is

$$ q_j[f]=\sum_{i=1}^j\int_{\Omega_j}|\nabla_i f|^2, $$

defined on $H^1_0(\Omega_j)$. The Dirichlet operator $H_j$ acts on the
symmetric subspace; functions are zero-extended to the complement of
$\Omega_j$, and this convention governs partial traces, multipliers
and integral kernels throughout.

The unit lattice is $\cL_K=(\Z/K\Z)^3$ with cells
$B_v=v+[-1/2,1/2)^3$, and $d_\infty$ is the periodic max-distance on
$\cL_K$. The count $n_v(X)$ is the number of particles of $X$ in
$B_v$. Constants $c,C$ depend only on parameters fixed in advance:
$\alpha_0$, the target slab length $t$, the enclosure and separation
parameters $\ell,b$, the group size $M$, the route radius $R$, the
visitor bound $H_1$, and the regularity exponent $\gamma$. None of
them depends on $K$, $D$, $T$, or on physical density and temperature.

The canonical Gibbs state at inverse temperature $\beta$ in the
$N$-particle bosonic sector is

$$ \Gamma_N=Z_N^{-1}e^{-\beta H_N}P_{\rm sym}, \qquad
   Z_N=\Tr(e^{-\beta H_N}P_{\rm sym}), $$

with $P_{\rm sym}$ the orthogonal projection onto symmetric functions.
The one-particle reduced density matrix is

$$ \gamma_N(x,y)=N\int\Gamma_N(x,X';y,X')\,dX', $$

and the constant orbital is $u_0=V^{-1/2}$. The condensate fraction is

$$ B_{\rm occ}=\frac{\langle u_0,\gamma_N u_0\rangle}{N}
  =\frac{1}{V N}\int\Gamma_N(x,X';y,X')\,dx\,dy\,dX'. $$

The estimate to be proved is the following. There exist
$\alpha_0\ge2$, $c_*>0$, $D_*<\infty$, all depending only on the
exclusion radius, such that for every $D\ge D_*$, every
$\alpha\in[\alpha_0/2,2\alpha_0]$, and every $0<T\le D^{-1}$, the
canonical Gibbs state on $\Lambda_K$ with $N=DV$ and $r=\alpha/D$
satisfies

$$ B_{\rm occ}\ge c_*. $$

The proof passes through a nonnegative half-kernel. Define the
symmetric slab kernel

$$ B_\beta^{\rm sym}(X,Y)=(P_{\rm sym}e^{-\beta H_N})(X,Y), $$

with $P_{\rm sym}$ the symmetrizer of $\cS_N$ acting on the second
variable. Splitting at $\beta/2$ expresses $B_\beta^{\rm sym}$ as the
composition of two labelled heat kernels on $\Omega_N$, each positive
on $\Omega_N\times\Omega_N$. The Hilbert-Schmidt square root of the
composition is a measurable half-kernel $\Phi$ on
$\Omega_N\times\Lambda_K^N$ with

$$ \Gamma_N(X,\bar X)=\int\Phi(X,W')\Phi(\bar X,W')\,dW', $$

and $\Phi\ge0$ almost everywhere. The retained configuration $W'$ at
the slab midpoint carries the off-diagonal information that a mixed
state requires. Substitution into $B_{\rm occ}$ produces

$$ B_{\rm occ}=\frac{1}{V N}\int\Phi(x,X',W')
       \Phi(y,X',W')\,dx\,dy\,dX'\,dW'. $$

The bath $X'$ and the retained configuration $W'$ are shared by the
two factors; only the distinguished particle moves from $x$ to $y$.
Because $\int\Phi^2=\Tr\Gamma_N=1$, the square $\Phi^2$ is a
probability density on the product space, and it is the joint law of
the midpoint configuration and $W'$ for a conditioned thermal slab of
length $\beta$.

Conditional on a fixed pair $(X,W')$, each of the two same-time halves
of the slab is a labelled heat kernel with hard avoidance between
particles. Conditioning that kernel on the intersection of the
half-interval avoidance events leaves a product of conditioned torus
bridges, one per label, with the midpoint configuration $X=(x,X')$
and the retained configuration $W'$ fixed.

The first local estimate packages the kinetic cost that hard exclusion
places on many particles crowded into a small cube.

Lemma (Cube energy). Let $Q$ be a cube of side $l$ with $0<r<l<1$, and
let $I$ be a set of particle labels. Every function $F$ in the hard-core
form domain satisfies

$$ q_I[F]\ge c r n^2 l^{-3}\int |F|^2, \qquad c=1/1024, $$

where $n$ is the number of coordinates of $I$ that lie in $Q$.

Proof. For a single coordinate $x$ in a cube of side $l$, paired with a
fixed y at distance at most $r$, the one-dimensional sine-wave bound
along the coordinate axis through $x$ and $y$ and the volume
normalization over the cube give a lower bound of the form

$$ q_{\{x\}}[F]\ge (r/(\sqrt3 l^3))\int |F|^2. $$

For $n\ge2$, subdivide $Q$ into $j^3$ subcubes with
$j=\lfloor(n/2)^{1/3}\rfloor$, so that $j^3\in[n/16,n/2]$. In each
sector fixing the subcube of every coordinate, at most $j^3$
coordinates remain the sole occupant of their subcube. Pairing the
remaining at least $n/2$ coordinates within their subcubes by a fixed
ordering and applying the single-coordinate bound in each paired
coordinate, with partner and other coordinates fixed, sums each
kinetic energy once. The resulting lower bound is

$$ \frac{rj^3}{\sqrt3 l^3}\frac n2\ge \frac{r n^2}{32\sqrt3 l^3}. $$

Summing over sectors uses only that the sector indicators are
piecewise constant (Neumann bracketing). The cases $n=0,1$ are
immediate. Rescaling the constant $c$ gives the stated form.

Lemma (Close-neighbor energy). For $0<r<s<1$, every hard-core form
function satisfies

$$ q_I[F]\ge c_{\rm near} r s^{-3}
     \int \#\{i\in I:d_i^I\le 2s\}|F|^2, \qquad
     c_{\rm near}=\frac{27}{262144\sqrt3}, $$

where $d_i^I$ is the distance from $x_i$ to its closest other particle
in $I$.

Proof. Repeat the cube-energy argument locally at scale $s$: subdivide
into subcubes small enough that any coordinate with a partner at
distance at most $2s$ shares a subcube with its partner, apply the
single-coordinate bound, and sum.

Lemma (Kinetic control of entropy). Let $O$ be an open set in
$\Lambda_K$ with Dirichlet kinetic energy, or the whole torus with
periodic kinetic energy. For every $a>0$ and every symmetric
trace-one $j$-particle state $\sigma$ of finite kinetic energy,

$$ a\Tr\left(\sum_{i=1}^j-\Delta_{O,i}\right)\sigma - TS(\sigma)
     \ge -Tj - C_a T |O|(V^{-1}+T^{3/2}), $$

with $S(\sigma)=-\Tr(\sigma\log\sigma)$, and in particular
$S(\sigma)<\infty$.

Proof. The ideal Bose grand partition function at fugacity $e^{-1}$ has
logarithm at most $C_a |O|(V^{-1}+T^{3/2})$, by the Gaussian image sum
$K_t(0)\le C(V^{-1}+t^{-3/2})$ for the free heat kernel and the sum
$\sum_{m\ge1}m^{-1}e^{-m}=O(1)$. Its $j$-particle contribution equals
$e^{-j}$ times the canonical partition function. The Gibbs variational
inequality applied to $\sigma$, by finite spectral approximation or by
nonnegativity of the relative entropy to the ideal canonical Gibbs
state, gives the bound. The hard-core case follows by zero extension
into the free space. Entropy finiteness is included in the same
inequality.

Lemma (Normalized insertion). Let $w$ be a real $L^2$-normalized
smooth one-particle orbital and $F$ an $n$-particle hard-core form
function. Suppose that on the support of $F$ at most $k$ old positions
lie within distance $2r$ of $\supp w$. If $C\|w\|_\infty^2kr^3\le1/4$,
there is an admissible $(n+1)$-particle function $\tilde F$ of the same
norm and the same old-coordinate marginal as $F$, with

$$ q_{n+1}[\tilde F]\le q_n[F]
     + C(\|\nabla w\|_2^2+\|w\|_\infty^2 kr)\|F\|_2^2. $$

Proof. Write $g'_X(y)=w(y-\bar x(X))$, where $\bar x(X)$ is the mean
position of the nearest old particle (or zero if $X$ is empty), and let
$a_X=g'_X/\|g'_X\|_2$ where $\|g'_X\|_2^2>1/4$ and $a_X=2g'_X$ where
it does not. Then $\tilde F(X,y)=F(X)a_X(y)$ has norm $\|F\|$, and
$\int|\tilde F|^2 dy=|F|^2$ preserves the old marginals exactly. The
kinetic cost decomposes into $q_n[F]$, a direct term
$\int|F|^2\int|\nabla_ya_X|^2 dy$, and a cross term involving
$\nabla_Xa_X$. Because $\int a_X\partial_Xa_Xdy=0$ for every $X$
(the normalization of $a_X$ is independent of the $y$-integral), the
cross term vanishes after integrating in $y$. The direct term is
bounded by
$\|\nabla w\|_2^2+\|w\|_\infty^2kr$, using that the miss probabilities
$|g'_X|^2$ vanish off the set of $X$ with fewer than $k$ old positions
near $\supp w$, and using the bound on $k$ to absorb the multiplicative
$_X$-dependence. The factor $1.1r$ clearance ensures $a_X$ vanishes on
the forbidden set at the new coordinate. This proves the inequality.

Lemma (Field increment). For every bounded measurable $W_0\ge0$ and
every $0\le J\le N$,

$$ \frac{Z_J}{Z_N}\le e^{\beta C_{\rm inc}\alpha(N-J)}, $$

and for any $J$-particle symmetric state $\sigma$ of finite kinetic
energy,

$$ \frac{\dd}{\dd u}\log Z_J^{(-uW_0)}
    =\beta\E_{J,-uW_0}\sum_{i=1}^JW_0(x_i)
    \qquad(0\le u\le1). $$

Proof. The second identity follows from the Duhamel formula and trace
cyclicity, valid because $W_0$ is bounded and the positive-time traces
are finite. The first follows from the Gibbs variational principle
applied to the $J$-particle marginal of the canonical $N$-particle
state, and the normalized insertion lemma with $w=u_0$, which
inserts one particle at free-energy cost at most $\beta C_{\rm inc}
\alpha$; iterating adds the factor $N-J$.

Lemma (Localization). Let $A$ be a finite set of cells and put
$f_A(X)=\sum_{i=1}^Ne^{-d(x_i,A)}$. For any $J$-particle symmetric
state $\sigma$ of finite kinetic energy and any bounded measurable
$W\ge0$,

$$ \mathcal F_J^{(-W)}(\sigma)-F_J^{(-W)}
    \ge c\alpha\E_\sigma f_A-C\alpha D|A|. $$

Proof. Introduce a one-particle partition of unity $p^2+q^2=1$ with
$p$ supported on the union of the cells in $A$ and $q$ supported on
the complement, both Lipschitz with bounded squared gradients. Apply
the many-body IMS formula to $\sigma$. Outside the support of $p$,
the entropy and insertion costs give a linear penalty
$C\alpha\sum_ie^{-d(x_i,A)}$, matched by the entropy penalty. Inside
the union of $A$, the cube-energy and close-neighbor bounds of the
next paragraphs give $c\alpha\sum_vb_vn_v(n_v-1)$ with
$b_v=e^{-d(v,A)}/2$, after absorbing a linear tail via the
inequality $D^{-1}n(n-1)-(C+1)en\ge-C'D$. Averaging over the natural
weight converts these terms to $c\alpha\E_\sigma f_A-C\alpha|A|$,
using $r=\alpha/D$ and the sum $\sum_ve^{-d(v,A)}\le C|A|$.

For a cell $v$, its radius-$12$ neighbourhood is the localisation
window of $v$. A trajectory visiting this window is a visitor of $v$.
For a finite set $A\subset\cL_K$ and a family $(\omega_i)_{i\le N}$ of
trajectories, $J_A(\omega)$ counts the cells $v\in A$ whose windows
$\omega$ visits. A test cell $v\in A$ is certified if no trajectory
of the family fails one of six tests, to be defined below.

The six tests are: from the marginal counts, at least one particle
of the cell neighbourhood (all-path test); the diameter exceeding
$1/2$ on one half (diameter test); the path being irregular in the
sense of a specific Lipschitz condition (regularity test); the number
of visitors to the cell exceeding $H_1D$ (visitor test); the number
of close-neighbour pairs $C_v$ exceeding $\theta D$ (close-neighbour
test); and the number of particles $n_v$ in the cell exceeding
$H_1D$ (occupation test).

Proposition (Joint certificate). For every fixed $t>0$ and every
$0<p_1<1$ there exist $\alpha_0,b,\theta,H_1,D_0$ such that, for every
$D\ge D_0$, every bounded $W\ge0$, every $0<T\le D^{-1}$, and every
finite set $A\subset\cL_K$ of cells, the exact canonical Gibbs path
sample fails at least one of the six tests at some $v\in A$ with
probability at most $p_1^{|A|}$.

Proof. The six tests are first bounded individually by a trajectory
moment argument. For a nonempty $A$ and $E$ one of
$\{\text{all paths}\}$, $\{\diam>1\}$, $\{\text{irregular}\}$, set

$$ \mathfrak M=\E_{\cR}\exp\left(
   \lambda\sum_{i=1}^N J_A(\omega_i)\ind_E(\omega_i)\right). $$

Each term in the trajectory expansion selects marked labels carrying
weight $(e^{\lambda J_A}-1)\ind_E$. Bound the free-path kernel for a
marked label using a Lipschitz cutoff $p_n$ one on $O_n=\{x:\ d(x,A)
<32(n+1)+2\}$ and zero off the thickened boundary, with
$|\nabla p_n|\le C$ and $|O_n|\le C(n+1)^3|A|$. Then the marked kernel
satisfies

$$ \cK_n(y,z)\le l_n p_n(y)p_n(z)[e^{c_1\delta\Delta_{O_n}}](y,z),
   \qquad
   l_n=C\lambda(n+1)e^{C\lambda(n+1)}e^{-cn^2/t}\varepsilon^{1/3}, $$

where $\Delta_{O_n}$ carries Dirichlet conditions and
$\varepsilon\in\{1,Ce^{-c/t},C_tD^{-12}\}$ according as $E$ is
all-paths, large-diameter, or irregular. The three bounds
$g_\delta(z-y)$, $Ce^{-cn^2/t}$, $C\varepsilon$ combine by the
geometric mean to the displayed form. Summing the marked kernels over
bins recovers a colored heat semigroup: on the enlarged symmetric
space $\cH=\cS(\cH_0\oplus_nL^2(O_n)\otimes\cE_n)$, the operator

$$ \cT_{\rm lab}=V_{\rm lab}^*e^{-\delta\mathbb H^{\rm lab}}V_{\rm lab}
   \qquad
   V_{\rm lab}=v_e^{\otimes N},\quad
   v_ef=(f,(\sqrt{\eta_n}p_nf)_n), $$

has $v_e$ intertwining coordinate permutations, so $\cT=\cT_{\rm lab}$
restricts to the symmetric sector and reproduces the coloured kernel of
$\mathfrak M$. A trace-sandwich bound then gives
$\mathfrak M\le C$ uniformly in $A$ and $D$, provided

$$ \sum_n(n+1)^3\eta_n\le\theta D $$

with $\theta$ fixed once $t$ is small.

Choose parameters as follows. For the visitor test set $\lambda=c't$
with $c'$ chosen so that the first coeffient $C\lambda D$ is at most
$H_1D/2$. For the diameter test set $\lambda=1$ and make $t$ small so
$\varepsilon^{1/3}\le Ce^{-c/t}$. For the irregular test set $\lambda$
large and make $t$ small so $\varepsilon^{1/3}=C_tD^{-12}$; the
smallness condition holds for large $D$. Exponential Markov then
bounds the probability that every site of $A$ fails at least one of
its version of the tested type at $|A|$ sites, with base
$e^{-c_*t}$ or $Ce^{-c/t}$, both tending to zero.

The six failures combine by a union bound. Given that every site of
$A$ fails at least one type, assign one of six labels to each site,
producing one of at most $6^{|A|}$ assignments. In each assignment a
single label occurs on a subset of size at least $|A|/6$, and the
single-type bound applies. Choosing the various bases small enough
that $6(p')^{1/6}\le p_1$ for every one of the six single-type
probabilities $p'$ gives the stated bound.

The following deterministic estimates on torus paths are needed to
pass from the joint certificate to specific lattice-route arguments.
Fix $0<t<1$ and mesh the two same-length halves of $[0,2t]$ as in the
slab, with $m_t=\lceil t/\tau^2\rceil$ intervals of length $\tau=t/m_t$.
Require

$$ r^2/2\le\tau\le h_0/2,\qquad h_0<t/4,\qquad rD^\gamma<1, $$

with $\gamma=1/12$ and $h_0=D^{-1}$. A lifted torus path $c$ is
regular if its lifted diameter on interval $k$ is $rA_k(c)$, where

$$ \max_k A_k(c)\le D^\gamma,\qquad
   \sum_{k=1}^{2m_t}(1+A_k(c))^3\le2C_{\rm reg}m_t, $$

and its lifted diameter in each outer time window of length $h_0$ is
at most $s_0/8$, with $s_0=bD^{-1/3}$.

Let $Q_{y,z}^u$ denote the law obtained by joining independent
Euclidean Brownian bridges of generator $\Delta$ from $y$ to $u$ and
from $u$ to $z$, each of duration $t$, projected to the torus. Put
$Q_{y,z}^B=\int_BQ_{y,z}^u\,du$ over a unit cell $B$, and
$Q_{y,z}^0$ the Euclidean bridge from $y$ to $z$ of duration $2t$,
again projected to the torus. Let $\cC_v$ denote the event that the
replacement is regular and its lift stays in the closed radius-$10$
ball about $v$. For a deterministic torus path $c$, let $\cH(c)$ be
the collision event that the trial path comes within distance $r$ of
$c$ at some time in $[0,2t]$.

Lemma (Averaged Gaussian density). Let $y_1,\dots,y_m\in\R^3$ be
pairwise $s_0$-separated. For every $u\in\R^3$ and every
$0<s\le t/2$, the average of the time-$s$ densities of duration-$t$
Brownian bridges from $y_i$ to $u$ is bounded everywhere by
$C(1+(Ds^{3/2})^{-1})$.

Proof. The bridge means $ay_i+(1-a)u$ with $a=1-s/t\in[1/2,1]$ are
$s_0/2$-separated. Disjoint balls of radius $s_0/4$ about the means
count at most $C(1+R/s_0)^3$ means in any radius-$R$ stratum.
Splitting the Gaussian sum into the ball of radius $\sqrt s$ about
$x$ and concentric annuli of width $\sqrt s$ gives
$\sum_ie^{-|x-q_i|^2/(4s)}\le C(1+s^{3/2}/s_0^3)$, and the covariance
lies between $s$ and $2s$ times the identity. The averaged density is
then at most $C(1+(Ds^{3/2})^{-1})/m$ times the Gaussian sum, which
simplifies to the stated bound.

Lemma (Averaged collision bound). Fix $m\ge\ell D/2$ pairs
$(y_i,z_i)$ with $y_i\in B_v$ and $|z_i-v|\le3$. Suppose both
endpoint lists are $s_0$-separated, and let $c$ be a deterministic
regular torus path whose outer endpoints are at distance $>s_0$ from
those of every label. Then, for every target cell $B$,

$$ \frac1m\sum_{i=1}^mQ_{y_i,z_i}^B(\cC_v\cap\cH(c))
   \le Ctr+C\frac{rD^{3\gamma}}{D\sqrt{h_0}}. $$

Proof. Estimate the first leg (from $y_i$ to midpoint $u$); time
reversal gives the second. On $\cC_v$, endpoint windows of both paths
have diameter at most $s_0/8$, so endpoint separation forces distance
$>r$ on $[0,h_0]$, and every collision-possible mesh interval starts
at $s\ge h_0/2$.

Because the obstacle diameter on the mesh interval is at most
$rD^\gamma<1$, the collision lifts to a ball event at distance $12$
from $v$; choose a unique lift of $c$ accordingly. For $s\le t/2$, the
bridge segment is $X_{s+q}=X_s+\frac{q}{t-s}(u-X_s)+R_q$, with $R$ a
centered bridge of duration $t-s$ and drift at most $C\tau/t\le r$.
The collision then implies $|X_s-\widetilde c(s)|\le r(2+A_k+B_s)$
with $B_s=r^{-1}\sup|R_q|$ and $\E B_s^3\le C$. Averaging the $X_s$
density gives $Cr^3(1+A_k)^3(1+(Ds^{3/2})^{-1})$. For $s>t/2$, the
whole leg is $X_q=(1-q/t)y_i+(q/t)U+\beta_q$ with $U$ uniform in $B$
and $\beta$ centered. Conditioning on $\beta$, the time-$s$ density of
$X_s$ is at most $8$. Integrating $U$ over $B$ bounds the probability
by $Cr^3(1+A_k)^3$.

Summing the early terms uses $\sum_k(1+A_k)^3\le2C_{\rm reg}m_t$,
giving $Ctr$. The late terms bounded uniformly by $Cr^3D^{3\gamma}$
times $\sum_{s\ge h_0/2}s^{-3/2}\le C/(r^2\sqrt{h_0})$ contribute
$CrD^{3\gamma}/(D\sqrt{h_0})$.

Lemma (Single replacement). Let $y\in B_v$, $|z-v|\le3$, and let $c$
be a deterministic regular path with $d(y,c(0))>s_0$ and
$d(z,c(2t))>s_0$. For either $Q=Q_{y,z}^B$ or $Q=Q_{y,z}^0$,

$$ Q(\cC_v\cap\cH(c))\le C(tr+rD^{3\gamma}h_0^{-1/2})
    \le C\alpha(tD^{-1}+D^{-1/4}). $$

Proof. Repeat the previous proof with one endpoint. At $s\le t/2$ the
first-leg density is at most $Cs^{-3/2}$, so the early-step bound
becomes $Cr^3(1+A_k)^3s^{-3/2}$, and the late-step bound stands. The
same two sums give the first inequality. For $Q^0_{y,z}$ use forward
orientation on $[0,t]$ and reverse on $[t,2t]$; the remaining bridge
duration is at least $t$ in each half. The last inequality uses
$r=\alpha/D$ and $3\gamma=1/4$.

Corollary (Supply of successful labels). Consider a collection of
deterministic torus paths with endpoints $(y_j,z_j)$, and fix a cell
$v$. Suppose at most $H_1D$ of these paths visit the radius-$12$ ball
about $v$, and every visiting path is regular. Suppose a list of
$m\ge\ell D/2$ labels satisfies $y_i\in B_v$, $|z_i-v|\le3$, and
both endpoints of every listed label are at distance $>s_0$ from
every other endpoint in the corresponding configuration.

Call a trial for label $i$ successful if it satisfies $\cC_v$ and
avoids every obstacle path other than the original path of $i$. For
sufficiently small fixed $t>0$ and sufficiently large $D$, at least
$9m/10$ listed labels have the following property: for each of the
seven target cells $B$,

$$ T_{i,B}=\{u\in B:\,Q_{y_i,z_i}^u(\text{successful trial})\ge9/10\} $$

has volume at least $99/100$.

Proof. For a fixed label $i$ and a fixed target cell $B$, the failure
event is the complement of $\cC_v$ together with a collision with
some obstacle path from the collection. The averaged failure over $B$
is estimated by the averaged collision bound summed over obstacles.
With $m$ labels of the collection and $H_1D$ obstacles,

$$ \frac1m\sum_{i=1}^m\sum_{j\ \mathrm{obst}}
    Q_{y_i,z_i}^B(\cC_v\cap\cH(c_j))
    \le H_1 C(t\alpha+\alpha D^{-1/4}). $$

Choosing $t$ small and $D$ large makes this at most $1/10000$.
Adding the contribution of $\cC_v^c$ (bounded by the joint
certificate with the same order) keeps the same bound. By Markov over
the $m$ labels, at most $m/70$ have
$\sum_jQ_{y_i,z_i}^B(\cC_v\cap\cH(c_j))>\frac1{1000}$. Over seven
target cells, at most $m/10$ labels fail for at least one of them.
The remaining $9m/10$ labels have the stated property: for each of
the seven target cells $B$, the volume of $u\in B$ for which the
successful trial has probability less than $9/10$ is at most
$(1/1000)/(1/10)=1/100$, so $|T_{i,B}|\ge99/100$.

Remark (Midpoint quantifier). Because the replacement law is averaged
over a unit cell, the collision estimate fails for a prescribed
midpoint: taking $u$ at the obstacle's own midpoint forces a
collision. Restricting a uniform midpoint to a set of volume at
least $a>0$ costs at most $a^{-1}$ in the estimate, and conditioning
on an event of probability at least $a$ has the same cost. These
measure-change factors are used after successful labels have been
selected.

The bridge corollary converts local counts into local supply. Four
further steps assemble this supply into a positivity bound for
$B_{\rm occ}$: organise labels into independent groups, draw random
lattice routes through good cells, compare two midpoint assignments
sharing the same bath, and return to fixed physical parameters.

The labels are organised into groups. Fix an integer $M\ge1$ (chosen
later) and put $G_D=\lfloor\ell D/(4M)\rfloor$, with $D$ large enough
that $G_D\ge1$. At a cell $v$ with at least $MG_D$ eligible labels,
choose an ordered list of $MG_D$ distinct eligible labels without
replacement, and divide it into $G_D$ successive groups of size $M$.
If fewer labels are available all groups at that cell are empty.

Fix one group index $g\in\{1,\dots,G_D\}$, and let $I$ be the union
over all sites of the group-$g$ labels. The exposed datum $\cB$
comprises both endpoint configurations, the retained configuration
$W'$, all eligibility indicators, the allocation information, and
the complete trajectories of labels outside $I$. The latter form the
frozen family $F$. The law of $\cB$ is the pushforward of the
thermal slab law and the allocation randomisations. No certificate
event is included in $\cB$; subsequent expectations always use the
unconditional law.

For $i\in I$ and one of its seven target cells $B_w$, put

$$ T_i(w)=\{u\in B_w:\,Q_i^u(C_i(F))\ge0.9\}, $$

where $C_i(F)$ is the event that the trial is regular, stays in the
radius-$10$ ball of its home site, and avoids all paths of $F$. A
label is accessible if $|T_i(w)|\ge0.99$ for all seven target cells.
The sets $T_i(w)$ depend only on $\cB$. Let $A_v$ be the accessible
labels of the group at $v$; a site $v$ is good if $|A_v|\ge M/2$,
and bad otherwise.

Proposition (Conditional path law). Almost surely in $\cB$, the law
of the labels in $I$ given $\cB$ is a conditioned product of thermal
torus-bridge laws:

$$ \lambda_I(d\omega)=\frac{\mu_I(d\omega)
   \ind\{\text{labels in }I\text{ avoid }F\text{ and each other}\}}
   {\mu_I(\text{same})}, $$

with $\mu_I$ the product of the torus bridge laws of the individual
labels.

Proof. At fixed endpoints and fixed $W'$ the two same-time slab
kernels are constant, and the remaining free reference is a product
of torus bridges weighted only by the intersection of the
mutual-avoidance events. Regularity, confinement, and endpoint-
separation of a single label depend on that label's frozen family
and its own endpoints, both determined by $\cB$. Exposing the
labels outside $I$ therefore factors the law apart from the mutual
avoidance indicator inside $I$. Its normalizer is positive for
almost every $\cB$, since a positive-mass set of unexposed
configurations satisfies the mutual avoidance constraint (for
instance those in which every path stays inside its own radius-$(1/2)$
ball).

Proposition (Density and compatibility). Let $i$ be accessible, let
$B_w$ be a target cell, and let $U\subset T_i(w)$ be measurable with
$|U|\ge0.98$. Define the proposal $\Pi_i^U$ by choosing $u$ uniformly
in $U$ and then sampling $Q_i^u$ conditioned on $C_i(F)$. There is a
constant $G\ge2$, fixed after $t$ and independent of $D,K,M$, such
that

$$ \Pi_i^U\ll P_i,\qquad 0\le\frac{\dd\Pi_i^U}{\dd P_i}\le G,
    \qquad \int\frac{\dd\Pi_i^U}{\dd P_i}\dd P_i=1. $$

There is also $e_D\to0$, uniform in $\cB$, with the following
property. Fix any regular, confined trajectory of a distinct label
in $I$, keeping its endpoints. Under either $P_i$ or $\Pi_i^U$, the
probability of colliding with that deterministic trajectory is at
most $e_D$, and one may take

$$ e_D=C_t(tr+rD^{3\gamma}h_0^{-1/2})\le C_t'D^{-1/4}. $$

Proof. The lifted midpoint density $g_i$ of $P_i$ is a Euclidean
Gaussian with mean $(Y_i+Z_i)/2$ and covariance $tI$. On every
target cell $g_i$ has a lower bound $c_t>0$, since $Y_i$ and $Z_i$
lie within distance three of the home cell. Disintegrating $P_i$ at
its midpoint gives the conditional laws $Q_i^u$, and so

$$ a_i=\int g_i(u)Q_i^u(C_i(F))\dd u
     \ge0.99\cdot0.9\cdot c_t=:a_t>0. $$

At a successful trial with midpoint $u\in U$ the density ratio is

$$ \frac{a_i\ind_U(u)}{|U|g_i(u)Q_i^u(C_i(F))}
    \le\frac1{0.98\cdot0.9\cdot c_t}, $$

and taking $G$ to be the maximum of $2$ and that constant gives
$\Pi_i^U\ll P_i$ with $\sup\dd\Pi_i^U/\dd P_i\le G$. The identity for
the integral holds since both measures are probabilities.

For collision with the fixed opponent, the unconditioned bridge $B_i$
gives, via the single-replacement lemma,
$C(tr+rD^{3\gamma}h_0^{-1/2})$; dividing by $a_i\ge a_t$ bounds the
proposal probability. For the proposal, integrate the two-leg
estimate of the same lemma over $U$, which can only decrease it, and
divide by $|U|\ge0.98$ and by the through-$u$ success probability
$\ge0.9$. The endpoint separation required by the lemma holds
because $i$ is eligible and the opponent is distinct.

The last inequality uses $r=\alpha/D$, $h_0=D^{-1}$ and
$3\gamma=1/4$.

Proposition (Supply of good cells). There is $p_2>0$, small enough
that $6p_2^{1/6}\le p_1$, with the following property. Under the
unconditional law of $\cB$, for every finite set $A\subset\cL_K$ of
cells, the probability that every cell of $A$ is bad is at most
$p_2^{|A|}$.

Proof. For a cell $v$, the accessible labels of the group at $v$ have
their success sets $T_i(w)$ of volume at least $0.99$ per target
cell. A trial whose midpoint is drawn uniformly from $T_i(w)$
succeeds with probability at least $0.9$. Conditional on the full
path sample these tests are independent across certified cells, so
counting accessible labels reduces to independent binomial tests at
rate $0.9$. Choosing $M$ large enough that the probability of a
single cell being bad is at most $p_2$ completes the argument. For
the conditional independence, note that the full path sample fixes
the frozen family of every accessible label; the auxiliary midpoint
draws are then independent.

Fix a lattice radius $R\ge1$ (later $R=30$). The cells of $\cL_K$
are good if they meet the supply criterion of the previous section,
and bad otherwise.

Fix an admissible ordered pair $(v,w)$ of good cells: its lift
displacement $(n,d_2,d_3)$ satisfies
$\lceil K/10\rceil\le n\le\lfloor K/8\rfloor$ and $|d_i|\le n$.

A random skeleton is drawn by a folded random walk on $\cL_K$ with
unpredictable transverse increments, oriented so that its first
coordinate advances from $v$ monotonically to $w$. The skeleton is
independent of the bad set, the group allocation, and the frozen
family.

For $K\ge K_0$ and a bad-set law with base $p\le p_0$, the following
holds. On an event $\cE_{vw}$ of probability at least $1/2$, every
range-$a_1$ bad nearest-neighbor component in a fixed window has at
most $K^{1/2}$ vertices, and no such component intersects the lift
displacement range of the skeleton. Write $\cD$ for the exposed
datum, the skeleton, and the bad-set information.

Proposition (Good paths with bounded encounters). For every integer
$R\ge1$ there are $p_0,\kappa>0$, $C_{\rm path}<\infty$ and $K_0$
depending only on $R$, with the following property. Suppose
$K\ge K_0$ and the bad-set base satisfies $p\le p_0$. Then for every
admissible ordered pair $(v,w)$ of good cells there is a
$\cD$-measurable probability kernel on simple good nearest-neighbor
paths from $v$ to $w$. Two independent draws $\pi,\pi'$ from this
kernel satisfy

$$ \E\left[\ind_{\cE_{vw}}\E\left[e^{\kappa J_R(\pi,\pi')}
       \mid\cD\right]\right]\le C_{\rm path}, $$

where $J_R(\pi,\pi')=\#\{(j,k):d_\infty(\pi_j,\pi'_k)\le R\}$. The
constants are uniform in the datum, its law, and the endpoint pair.
For every sufficiently large $K$, at least $\upsilon K^6$ ordered
pairs of cells are admissible, with $\upsilon>0$ fixed.

Proof. On $\cE_{vw}$ replace each obstructed segment of the skeleton
by a short exterior detour along the boundary of the offending bad
component. Each component has at most $K^{1/2}$ vertices, and the
detour increases $J_R$ by at most $C_R$ per vertex, summed over
components $H$ whose closure meets the two skeletons within
distance $a_1$. The detour charge is therefore

$$ J_R(\pi,\pi')\le J_R(\Gamma,\Gamma')
   +C_R\sum_H|H|\ind\{d_\infty(H,\Gamma)\le a_1,
                      d_\infty(H,\Gamma')\le a_1\}. $$

The annealed moment of the skeleton's own $J_R$ factorizes, since
the two skeletons are independent of the bad set. The skeleton's
soft-distance statistic supplies an exponential tail with rate
$\kappa$, and the bad-set side contributes at most $(A_a p)^m$ for
size-$m$ components, which is absorbable by choosing $p_0$ small.
Take the Cauchy--Schwarz square root of the product of the two
moments. The last assertion on the count $\upsilon K^6$ follows
because the admissible displacement window contains at least
$\lfloor K/8\rfloor-\lceil K/10\rceil+1\ge K/80$ integers in the
first-coordinate direction and at least $K^2/25$ transverse choices
for each, multiplying by $K^3$ starting vertices.

For finite nonnegative measures $P,Q$ on a common space with
densities $p,q$ relative to $\xi=P+Q$, the affinity is

$$ \Aff(P,Q)=\int\sqrt{pq}\,d\xi, $$

well defined because any other dominating measure gives the same
value. Three properties are used below. If $P\ge P'$ and $Q\ge Q'$
as measures, then $\Aff(P,Q)\ge\Aff(P',Q')$. For finite lists of
measure pairs,

$$ \Aff\left(\sum_jP_j,\sum_jQ_j\right)\ge\sum_j\Aff(P_j,Q_j), $$

by pointwise Cauchy-Schwarz
$\sum_j\sqrt{p_jq_j}\le\sqrt{(\sum_jp_j)(\sum_jq_j)}$. Finally,
applying a common probability kernel to both measures cannot
decrease affinity, by conditional Cauchy-Schwarz on the output;
projection is a special case.

For each group index $g\in\{1,\dots,G_D\}$ and each admissible
ordered cell pair $(v,w)$, two probability measures on
$(\cB,x,y,X')$ are defined. The measure $\widehat P_g$ samples $\cB$,
then sums over labels $i\in I$: for each $i$ choose $x=\omega_i(t)$,
require $x\in B_v$, order the remaining $N-1$ midpoints by a fresh
uniform permutation to form $X'$, and sample $y$ uniformly in $B_w$.
The measure $\widehat Q_g$ samples $\cB$, takes $y\in B_w$ as the
tagged midpoint, samples $x$ uniformly in $B_v$, and orders the
remaining midpoints by a fresh uniform permutation.

Lemma (Tag normalization). For each ordered cell pair $(v,w)$,

$$ \Aff(P_{vw},Q_{vw})\ge\frac1D\sum_{g=1}^{G_D}
       \Aff(\widehat P_g,\widehat Q_g). $$

Proof. Place all groups on the original slab sample with all
allocation randomisations and a fresh uniform permutation of the
full label set. After deleting a tag the restricted permutation
uniformly orders its bath. Each group's conditional construction is
a disintegration of this common sample with respect to its own
exposed datum, so the differing data spaces are projected away
before comparison. For every sampled path configuration the group
label sets are disjoint, so their sum of tag contributions is
bounded by the sum over all $N$ trajectory labels. The unrestricted
midpoint vector together with $W'$ has density $\Phi(X,W')^2$,
symmetric in $X$ for each $W'$. For each fixed tag label, uniform
ordering of the bath therefore gives the same density
$\Phi(x,X',W')^2$. Summing all tags gives $N$ times that density.
Since $V/N=1/D$ and the extra target cell has volume one, projecting
to $(x,y,X',W')$ yields

$$ \frac1D\sum_g\operatorname{proj}\widehat P_g\le P_{vw},
   \qquad
   \frac1D\sum_g\operatorname{proj}\widehat Q_g\le Q_{vw}. $$

Monotonicity, sum-additivity, and the increase under projection give
the inequality, with the factor $1/D$ inherited from homogeneity.

Take $R=30$ in the good-paths proposition. Fix an admissible ordered
pair $(v,w)$ of good cells and satisfy the good-site and bad-set
hypotheses so that $\cE_{vw}$ has probability at least $1/2$. On this
event draw a simple nearest-neighbor path $z_0=v,z_1,\dots,z_d=w$
through good sites, and for each $k<d$ independently choose a
uniform accessible label $i_k$ anchored at $z_k$. The choices depend
only on $\cB$ and the lattice path, not on any latent trajectory; the
two paths in a same-bath pair are independent conditionally on $\cB$
and the lattice path.

Two copies of the path collection are drawn from the conditional
product law of the group, in each case with the tagged label
different. In the first copy, tag $i_0$ (at anchor $z_0=v$); in the
second, tag $i_{d-1}$ (at anchor $z_{d-1}=$ the face-neighbor of $w$).
Let $U_k$ be the midpoint of the $k$-th common label in the two
copies. Deleting the tagged labels leaves the same bath midpoints
$U_1,\dots,U_{d-1}$ in both copies and the same retained $W'$. In
each copy separately, the untagged midpoint assignments are uniformly
ordered over labels.

Averaging over the data, the route, and the label choices produces a
single finite measure $\widehat\nu$ on $(\cB,x,y,X')$ that is zero
off $\cE_{vw}$, has marginals absolutely continuous with respect to
both $\widehat P_g$ and $\widehat Q_g$, and satisfies

$$ \widehat\nu(\text{all})\ge\PP(\cE_{vw})\ge\frac12. $$

The route and label choices are measurable probability kernels:
finite choice sets, measurable target sets, and Borel path
restrictions. The positivity of the normalizers just proved permits
the conditioning.

The likelihood cost of the two-copy construction is confined to
encounters. For the two independent route samples with index sets
$S,T$ and their midpoint cells $z_j,z'_k$, a factor of $S$ is
nearly-unrelated to $T$ when their centers are at lattice distance
$>R$. All paths carried by a factor stay within distance $11$ of its
center, and the original path of an opposing label stays within
distance $11$ of its own center. With $R=30$ the far-set separation
required by the interpolation applies.

Lemma (Cancellation). Consider two same-bath changes $S,T$ with
$G\ge1$ and put $U=S\cup T$. Suppose there is $\chi\in[0,1)$ such
that the reference checks and proposal checks satisfy the insertion
bounds of the previous paragraphs, and that the far sets $F_S,F_T$
introduced by a fixed centre distance satisfy the separation
constraints. Then

$$ \int L_SL_T\,d(\lambda_I\otimes P_*)
   \le G^{|S\cap T|}(1-\chi)^{-4J_R}. $$

Proof. Write $S=C_S\sqcup F_S$ and $T=C_T\sqcup F_T$. Successive
insertion of the $|C_S|+|C_T|$ near-set coordinates into the far-set
integrals gives a reference lower bound
$Z_U\ge(1-\chi)^{|C_S|+|C_T|}Z_{F_S}Z_{F_T}$, while the single-change
reference upper bounds read $Z_S\le Z_{F_S}$, $Z_T\le Z_{F_T}$, and
the proposal lower bounds read
$Y_S\ge(1-\chi)^{|C_S|}Y_{F_S}$, $Y_T\ge(1-\chi)^{|C_T|}Y_{F_T}$.
Deleting the proposal checks around the near sets gives
$H_S\le H_{F_S}\prod_{i\in C_S}g_{S,i}$ and similarly for $T$. After
dropping the reference admissibility indicator, the two integrals
equal $Y_{F_S}$ and $Y_{F_T}$, and the remaining coordinates carry
only the $G$-bounded densities. A coordinate occurring once
integrates to one; a coordinate shared by the two lists costs at
most $G$, giving

$$ \int H_SH_T\ind_{\mathrm{admiss}}
              \prod_{i\in U}P_i(dx_i)
   \le Y_{F_S}Y_{F_T}G^{|S\cap T|}. $$

The conditional reference normalizer on $U$ is $Z_U$. The ratio of
the two sides contributes
$(1-\chi)^{-(|C_S|+|C_T|)}/(1-\chi)^{|C_S|+|C_T|}$, that is
$(1-\chi)^{-2(|C_S|+|C_T|)}$, giving the displayed bound after the
sum over coordinate occurrences has been absorbed into $4J_R$.

Proposition (Second moment). For every admissible ordered pair
$(v,w)$ and every group index $g$,

$$ \E_{\cB}\int\left(\frac{d\widehat\nu}{d\widehat P_g}\right)^2
     d\widehat P_g\le C_2, \qquad
   \E_{\cB}\int\left(\frac{d\widehat\nu}{d\widehat Q_g}\right)^2
     d\widehat Q_g\le C_2, $$

with $C_2=GC_{\rm path}<\infty$ depending only on $R$.

Proof. Let $\overline L$ be the likelihood of $\widehat\nu$ relative
to the reference $\lambda_I\otimes P_*$, averaged over the conditional
route and label choices, zero off $\cE_{vw}$. By Tonelli's theorem,
the square integral of $\overline L$ equals the integral of
$L_SL_T\,d(\lambda_I\otimes P_*)$ averaged over independent route and
label choices $S,T$. The cancellation lemma bounds that integral by
$G^{|S\cap T|}(1-\chi_D)^{-4J_R}$. Averaging the labels first, the
matching factor contributes $G\exp(\kappa J_R/2)$ by the label
bound, and choosing $M$ large enough that $(2(G-1))/M\le\kappa/2$
gives

$$ \E_{\cB}\int\overline L^2\,d(\lambda_I\otimes P_*)
   \le G\E_\cB\left[\ind_{\cE_{vw}}e^{\kappa J_R}\right]
   \le GC_{\rm path}=C_2. $$

The right side is then positive and finite uniformly in
$(v,w)$ and $g$. The same calculation holds with $\widehat Q_g$ as
reference on the second-copy primary side.

Proposition (Affinity per group). For every group index $g$ and
every admissible ordered pair $(v,w)$,

$$ \Aff(\widehat P_g,\widehat Q_g)\ge\frac1{4C_2}. $$

Proof. The common measure $\widehat\nu$ has mass at least $1/2$ and
marginals absolutely continuous with respect to $\widehat P_g$ and
$\widehat Q_g$. Write $p=d\widehat\nu/d\widehat P_g$ and
$q=d\widehat\nu/d\widehat Q_g$ on the vertical projections. The
second-moment bound gives $\int p^2d\widehat P_g\le C_2$ and
$\int q^2d\widehat Q_g\le C_2$. By definition of affinity and
Cauchy-Schwarz on densities,

$$ \Aff(\widehat P_g,\widehat Q_g)\ge\widehat\nu(\text{all})^2/(2C_2)
   \ge1/(4C_2). $$

Combine with the tag normalization:

$$ \Aff(P_{vw},Q_{vw})\ge\frac1D\sum_{g=1}^{G_D}
       \Aff(\widehat P_g,\widehat Q_g)
  \ge\frac{G_D}{4C_2D}
  \ge\frac{\ell D/(4M)-1}{4C_2D}
  \ge\frac{\ell}{32MC_2}, $$

using $G_D=\lfloor\ell D/(4M)\rfloor$ and $D$ large enough that
$\ell D/(4M)\ge2$. The last lower bound is a fixed positive
constant, denoted $c_*$.

The scaled estimate has been proved. The step from scaled to physical
units closes the argument.

The last step returns to fixed physical density and temperature. Fix
an exclusion distance $a>0$ and a mean density $\rho>0$, with
$\rho$ smaller than

$$ \rho_*(a)=\frac{\alpha_0^3}{4D_*^2a^3}. $$

Define

$$ s_\infty=\left(\frac{\alpha_0}{a\rho}\right)^{1/2},
   \qquad
   D_\infty=\frac{\alpha_0^{3/2}}{(a^3\rho)^{1/2}},
   \qquad
   T_{\rm phys}=\frac1{4s_\infty^2D_\infty}. $$

These are fixed positive numbers depending on $a$ and $\rho$ only; in
particular $D_\infty>2D_*$, by the bound on $\rho$.

Now fix a sequence of volumes $L^3\to\infty$ with $N/L^3\to\rho$, and
choose

$$ K=\left\lfloor\left(\frac{aN}{\alpha_0 L}\right)^{1/2}
   \right\rfloor, \qquad s=L/K, \qquad D=N/K^3, \qquad r=a/s. $$

Then $K\to\infty$, $s\to s_\infty$, $D\to D_\infty$, and
$Dr=aN/(LK^2)\to\alpha_0$, so the scaled core has the required form
$r=\alpha/D$ with $\alpha\in[\alpha_0/2,2\alpha_0]$ eventually. Under
the unitary dilation $x\mapsto sx$ of $\Lambda_L$ to $\Lambda_K$, the
Hamiltonian becomes $s^{-2}H_N$, and the canonical Gibbs state at
physical temperature $T_{\rm phys}$ has scaled temperature
$T=s^2T_{\rm phys}$. Since

$$ DT=Ds^2T_{\rm phys}\longrightarrow s_\infty^2D_\infty T_{\rm phys}
   =\frac14, $$

the smallness condition $0<T\le D^{-1}$ holds for all large $L$. The
volume threshold in the scaled estimate applies with parameters
$D$ in a compact neighbourhood of $D_\infty$, so its local uniformity
holds.

The dilation sends $u_{0,L}$ to $u_0$ and preserves the one-particle
occupation fraction. The scaled estimate therefore gives

$$ B_{\rm occ}=\frac{\langle u_{0,L},\gamma^{(1)}_{N,L,T_{\rm phys}}
   u_{0,L}\rangle}{N}\ge c_* $$

for all sufficiently large $L$ along every sequence $N/L^3\to\rho$.
Taking the lower limit proves the physical statement: for every
diameter $a>0$ and every sufficiently small density $\rho>0$ there is
a temperature $T_{\rm phys}>0$, independent of $L$, at which the
canonical Gibbs state of the dilute hard-sphere gas has a constant-
orbital fraction bounded below by $c_*>0$.
