# Equidistribution of Primitive Sextic Torus Packets



maketitle

Abstract: 
Let K range over totally real sextic fields with no proper
intermediate subfield, and let D_K be the discriminant. For each
K, take the complete finite packet of compact diagonal orbits attached
to the ideal classes of mathcal O_K,calO_K, with all coordinate-sign
translates included and each orbit weighted by its diagonal-orbit
volume. We prove that these packets become equidistributed with respect
to Haar probability on SL_6(Z)backslashSL_6(R) as |D_K|to∞,
with no escape of mass. The obstruction special to composite degree~6
is that the measure-classification theorem leaves, as candidates for a
limit, properly embedded block-diagonal orbits of type 3+3 and
2+2+2. The paper excludes these using a conditioning scheme at
intermediate scales: it produces leafwise plaque probabilities inside
each limiting state that retain arithmetic information from the growing
discriminant. A product rule for these probabilities exhibits enough
root-activity to force every homogeneous component to be full.

## Packets and the main theorem

Let
[
 X_6=SL_6(Z)backslashSL_6(R),
 A_6={diag(e^{u_1},...,e^{u_6}):textstylesum_j u_j=0}.
]
Rows act on the right: xg denotes right multiplication of the coset
representative by ginSL_6(R). We denote by m_6 the
SL_6(R)-invariant probability on X_6. Haar measure on A_6 is
dd u_1...dd u_5 with u_6=-sum_{j=1}^5u_j.

A degree-six number field K is primitive if it has no proper
intermediate field. Fix an ordering sigma=(sigma_1,...,sigma_6)
of the real embeddings and let mathcal O_KcalO_K denote the ring of
integers. For a nonzero fractional ideal I, put
[
 sigma(I)={(sigma_1(d),...,sigma_6(d)):din I},
 Λ_{I,sigma}=covol(sigma(I))^{-1/6}sigma(I).
]
This is a unimodular lattice in R^6, hence determines a point of
X_6.

Choose one representative I for each ordinary ideal class. For each
sign matrix w=diag(ε_1,...,ε_6) with
ε_jin{pm1}, form the orbit Λ_{I,sigma}wA_6. By
Dirichlet's unit theorem the orbit is compact. Two such orbits (with the
same I) coincide as subsets of X_6 exactly when the two sign
matrices differ by the signature of a unit; see
Section~sec:arithmetic. The sextic packet
mathcal P_calP_{K,sigma} is the set of distinct compact orbits obtained
this way. Replacing the ideal-class representatives produces the same
packet, so the packet depends only on K and the ordering sigma of
the embeddings.

For mathcal OcalOinmathcal P_calP_{K,sigma}, let m_{mathcal O}calO be the
invariant probability on mathcal OcalO and vol_A(mathcal O)calO) its
A_6-volume. The packet probability is

 mu_{K,sigma}=
 frac{sum_{mathcal OcalOinmathcal P_calP_{K,sigma}}
       vol_A(mathcal O)calO),m_{mathcal O}calO}
      {sum_{mathcal OcalOinmathcal P_calP_{K,sigma}}
       vol_A(mathcal O)}calO).



Let K_i be primitive totally real sextic fields with
|D_{K_i}|to∞, and let sigma_i be any ordering of the
embeddings of K_i. Then
[
 int_{X_6}f,ddmu_{K_i,sigma_i}→int_{X_6}f,dd m_6
 qquadfor all fin C_c(X_6).
]
Additionally, there is no escape of mass: for every ε>0 there
is a compact Csubset X_6 with
mu_{K_i,sigma_i}(C)ge 1-ε for all sufficiently large i.


## History and the composite-degree obstruction

In degree two, quadratic packets reduce to packets of closed geodesics
on the modular surface. Linnik [cite] proved
equidistribution under an auxiliary splitting condition, which Duke
[cite] removed for fundamental discriminants by analytic
methods. Einsiedler, Lindenstrauss, Michel, and Venkatesh
[cite] gave a dynamical proof valid for all positive nonsquare
discriminants. In degree three, the same four authors proved
equidistribution of cubic packets [cite] by combining measure
classification, subconvexity, and local harmonic analysis.

Two of the general tools used here are due to Einsiedler, Katok, and
Lindenstrauss. First, the EKL measure-classification theorem
cite[Theorem~1.3]{EKL2006}: on SL_d(Z)backslashSL_d(R) for
dge3, any A-invariant, A-ergodic probability of positive entropy
for some diagonal one-parameter subgroup is the invariant probability
on a closed finite-volume orbit xJ for some closed connected subgroup
J containing A. Second, the Einsiedler--Lindenstrauss--Michel--
Venkatesh criterion cite[Corollary~3.3]{ELMV2009} for positive entropy,
which we use as a black box in Section~sec:homogeneous.

The classification does not, in composite degree, reduce the
possibility of limits to Haar. In degree six, there are proper block
subgroups J⊊ G that contain the full diagonal A_6. Their
orbits xJ carry finite J-invariant measure. These are the residuals
of the classification that must be ruled out, using arithmetic that the
measure-classification theorem alone cannot see. Primitivity of K is
what makes that arithmetic available.

Subsequent work has sharpened entropy estimates in higher degree:
Khayutin cite[Theorem~1.1]{Khayutin2019} treats maximal-order packets
whose Galois closures act two-transitively on the embeddings, and
Wieser--Yang [cite] treat quartic packets of maximal type
with quadratic subfields. Neither result covers the degree-six
primitive case here.

The arithmetic estimates adapt the ordinary-vector method of
cite[Section~2--6]{PrimeDegreePackets} and cite[Section~3]{QuarticPackets},
with novel uses of Stark's effective zero-free region [cite]
and the short-interval multiplicative-function theorem of Shiu
[cite] in the form given by Pollack [cite].
Primitivity ensures that every nonrational din K generates the whole
field, and hence the order Z[d] has discriminant at least D_K.
This yields the uniform separation of rational lattice vectors used in
Proposition~prop:bowen.

## The intermediate-resolution argument

The core of the paper builds leafwise measures at the limit that retain
information about mu_{K_i,sigma_i} at scales that tend to infinity
with the field.

Ordinary-vector estimates prove tightness and give every probability
limit mu an O(r^6) bound on balls of radius r, uniformly when
the centers range over a compact set (Proposition~prop:ball).
Since A_6 has dimension five, this bound forces positive entropy in
every positive-weight A_6-ergodic component
(Proposition~prop:positive-entropy). The EKL classification then
makes each such component homogeneous (Proposition~prop:positive-entropy).
Every proper homogeneous component is supported on points fixed by a
nonidentity diagonal matrix with algebraic entries. There are only
countably many such matrices, so any such component is confined to a
countable union of smooth pieces (Lemma~lem:fixed-pieces).

The arithmetic tube estimate used to detect and exclude those pieces is
Proposition~prop:bowen. It bounds the mass of a two-sided tube
[
 x(a(tb)Oa(-tb)cap a(-tb)Oa(tb))
]
in a packet by e^{-t/3}, for times
tin[T_{-,i},T_{+,i}] with T_{-,i}to∞ and
T_{+,i}/T_{-,i}to∞. The breadth of this time interval is used
throughout.

For jne k, let U_{jk}={1+sE_{jk}:sinR}. Exact conditional
measures of a finite packet along U_{jk} are Dirac masses. To capture
the spread detected by the tube bound, we condition not exact measures
but plaque probabilities at intermediate resolutions: in a local
chart (q,h) for a unipotent group, condition the plaque coordinate
h on a transverse cell of side 2^{-l} in q. At depths
l_{-,i}prec lprec l_{+,i}, the packet's conditional probabilities
change negligibly over a wide internal range
(Lemma~lem:plateau). The resulting limiting state retains the
plaque probabilities together with the lattice point
(Section~sec:conditioning), and the sampling identity
(Lemma~lem:sampling) says the sampled plaque coordinate is
distributed according to its own recorded probability.

The product rule (Theorem~thm:product) reduces these plaque
probabilities to product factors across commuting root subgroups.
Call a root active at a limiting state omega when
m^{U_{jk}}_omega is supported beyond the identity
(Section~sec:activity). Product structure makes
activity transitive under composed roots
(Proposition~prop:activity). Activity is preserved by
diagonal motions (Lemma~lem:activity-indicators) and forms a
directed graph that must be strongly connected on cut-crossing
indicators (Section~sec:combination).

The entropy argument on long segments
(Proposition~prop:segment-activity) uses the tube bound to prove
that crossing-root activity on any long segment of a dominated measure
has positive mean. Composing this with two-edge cycles upgrades the
cut-crossing activity to full activity at almost every state of the
augmented limit (Theorem~thm:full-activity). Full activity plus
support on Homogeneous fixed-point pieces gives a contradiction unless
the limiting component is Haar.

## Conventions

For the rest of the paper, n=6, G=SL_n(R), Gamma=SL_n(Z),
X=Gammabackslash G, and A=A_n. Let
L={binR^n:sum_j b_j=0} be the Lie algebra of A and
a(b)=diag(e^{b_1},...,e^{b_n}). The right action is used
throughout: right multiplication by a(b) sends the group element h
to a(-b)ha(b). Matrix neighborhoods of the identity and smooth
metrics are interchangeable on fixed compact sets. When a metric on
X is needed it is the quotient of a left-invariant Riemannian metric
on G. The notation B(r) denotes a sufficiently small identity
neighborhood of radius r. Implicit constants may depend on compact
sets, chosen charts, and n, but not on the varying field. All passages
to subsequences preserve the probability limit under consideration.

# Arithmetic estimates for the packets

This section proves the three estimates on which the rest of the paper
relies:
 uniform control of the cusp (Proposition~prop:cusp);
 a dimension bound for limits of packet measures: balls of radius
 r have mass O(r^6) (Proposition~prop:ball);
 exponential decay on two-sided diagonal tubes
 (Proposition~prop:bowen).

The first two follow by counting ordinary lattice vectors. The third
counts a vector together with a vector in the dual lattice; their
coordinatewise product lands in the codifferent of K, and primitivity
of K supplies the separation needed to count the thin slices that
such products visit.

Throughout, let K be a totally real sextic field with no proper
intermediate field, and let sigma be an ordering of its real
embeddings. Write
[
 D=|Disc(K)|, Q=D^{1/2},
 kappa=Res_{s=1}zeta_K(s),
 d_K(l)=#{mathfrak bfrakbsubsetmathcal O_K:NcalO_K:Nmathfrak b=lfrakb=l}.
]
All ideals counted here are nonzero. For a sequence of fields and
embedding orderings, use the corresponding decorated notation
D_i,Q_i,kappa_i,mu_i. Unless another dependence is indicated,
constants below are absolute for this fixed degree; in particular, they
do not depend on the field or on the ordering of its embeddings. The
arguments are adapted from the ordinary-vector treatment in
cite[Section~2--6]{PrimeDegreePackets} and cite[Section~3]{QuarticPackets},
reproduced in the present normalizations.

## Volume and unfolding

We first fix the sign convention in the definition of the packet. The
space X=SL_6(Z)backslashSL_6(R) parametrizes ordinary unimodular
lattices. A diagonal sign matrix acts on lattices even when its
determinant is -1; the resulting lattice again represents a point of
X (via any positively oriented basis). There is no additional
orientation coordinate, and the packet definition already allows both
determinant signs.

Let h_K and R_K denote the ordinary class number and regulator, with
R_K the covolume of the absolute logarithms of the units after
deleting the sixth coordinate. Put
[
 U=mathcal O_K^calO_K^times,
 U^+={εin U:sigma_j(ε)>0,,(1le jle6)},
 s=[U:U^+].
]
For a nonnegative Borel f on R^6 and a lattice Λ, define
widehatwidehat f(Λ)=f(Λ)=sum_{vinΛ∖{0}}f(v). For p>0
define a logarithmic fiber integral

 V_f(p)
  =sum_{εin{pm1}^6}int_{R^5}
  f(ε_1e^{u_1},...,ε_5e^{u_5},
         ε_6p e^{-u_1-...-u_5})
   ,dd u_1...dd u_5.

Thus V_f(p) integrates over the set on which the absolute product of
the six coordinates is p, with the Haar normalization for A.

[Packet volume and vector unfolding]

Every orbit in mathcal P_calP_{K,sigma} has A-volume (s/2)R_K, and
there are h_K2^6/s distinct orbits. In particular, the total packet
volume is Qkappa. For every nonnegative Borel f,

 int_Xwidehatwidehat ff,ddmu_{K,sigma}
   =frac1{Qkappa}sum_{lge1}d_K(l)V_f(l/Q),

with both sides allowed to be infinite.


Proof. 
A totally real field has only pm1 as roots of unity, so the kernel
of the absolute-logarithmic map on U has order two. Consequently the
image of U^+ has index s/2 in the image of U. The unit theorem
gives covolume (s/2)R_K for the positive-unit logarithms in the
coordinates u_1,...,u_5.

Suppose a real diagonal map sends sigma(I) onto sigma(J) for two
nonzero fractional ideals. Pick 0ne din I with image sigma(e),
ein J. Then all embeddings of d are nonzero, and the diagonal
entries of the map are sigma_j(e/d), so the map is multiplication by
e/din K^times. Hence J=(e/d)I. Consequently, different ordinary
ideal classes give disjoint packet orbits. For a fixed ideal I, its
diagonal self-automorphisms are its units, because (I:I)=mathcal O_K.calO_K.
The stabilizer in A is the closure of the image of U^+.

Two sign translates lie on the same A-orbit exactly when their sign
patterns differ by a unit signature. The signature map on units has
image of cardinality s, giving 2^6/s orbits per ordinary class and
the stated orbit volume. The analytic class-number formula, in this
normalization, gives Qkappa=2^5h_KR_K; this matches the product of
orbit count and orbit volume.

For unfolding, let mathcal FcalF be a fundamental domain for the
positive-unit logarithms in the traceless diagonal algebra. Summing over
ordinary class representatives and all sign matrices counts each orbit
s times, so

 int_XF,ddmu_{K,sigma}
 =frac1{sQkappa}sum_{[I]}sum_w
   int_{mathcal F}calFF(Λ_{I,sigma}w a(u)),dd u

for any nonnegative F on X, with dd u=dd u_1...dd u_5 and
the sixth coordinate the negative sum of the others.

Apply this to F=widehatwidehat f.f. A nonzero din I determines the integral
ideal mathfrak b=(d)I^frakb=(d)I^{-1}, and
[
 prod_{j=1}^6
 |covol(sigma(I))^{-1/6}sigma_j(d)|
 =(Nmathfrak b)/(Q),frakb)/(Q),
]
since covol(sigma(I))=QNI. The ideal mathfrak bfrakb determines
[I]=[mathfrak b]^frakb]^{-1} and every integral mathfrak bfrakb occurs. For
fixed mathfrak b,frakb, the possible d form a single U-orbit, or s
orbits under U^+. Summing a positive-unit orbit and integrating over
mathcal FcalF unfolds to the full traceless diagonal algebra.
Multiplication by a representative of U/U^+ translates the absolute
logarithms and permutes the signs. After the sum over w, each of
these s orbits contributes V_f(Nmathfrak b/Q),frakb/Q), and their number
cancels the factor s in (eq:packet-integral). The conclusion
follows by nonnegativity.


## Uniform counts of ideals

The normalization by kappa in (eq:vector-unfolding) makes it
important to keep this factor in upper bounds for ideal counts. The key
estimate is standard and comes from a zero-free neighborhood of 1
plus Shiu's short-interval estimate.

[Uniform ideal estimates]
For all sufficiently large discriminants D in the present family,

 kappa&gg(log D)^{-1}, 
 sum_{ple D}frac{d_K(p)}p
   &lelog(kappalog D)+O(1), 
 sum_{x-y<lle x}d_K(l)
   &ll ykappa
   (D^{1/4}le xle D, x^{1/2}le yle x).
   

In (eq:prime-sum), p ranges over rational primes. Constants and
the discriminant threshold are uniform in K.


Proof. 
Stark's Lemma~3 in [cite] shows that the rectangle
[
 Re sge1-frac1{4log D}, |Im s|lefrac1{4log D}
]
contains at most one nontrivial zero of zeta_K, and that such a zero
is real and simple. The same paper's Lemma~8 shows that a real zero
beta with
[
 1-frac1{4cdot 6!log D}lebeta<1
]
would be a zero of a quadratic subfield zeta function
cite[Lemmas~3 and~8]{Stark1974}. Since K has no quadratic subfield,
there is an absolute c_0>0 with

 |1-rho|ge c_0/log D

for every nontrivial zero rho of zeta_K. One may take
c_0=1/2880.

Set s_0=1+1/log D. The logarithmic derivative of the completed
Dedekind zeta gives, for real 1<sle s_0,

 S(s):=sum_rhoRefrac1{s-rho}
   =frac1{s-1}+frac12log D
   +(zeta'_K(s))/(zeta_K(s))+O(1).

Zeros are counted with multiplicity, and the gamma-factor contributes
O(1) because degree and signature are fixed. The left side is a sum
of nonnegative terms, and zeta'_K(s_0)/zeta_K(s_0)le0 by the
Dirichlet series. Hence S(s_0)lllog D.

The same bound holds uniformly for 1<sle s_0. Write
u=s-1, a=1-Rerhoge0, v=Imrho. The summand is
(a+u)/((a+u)^2+v^2). For 0<ule1/log D, the numerator is at most
its value at s_0, and (eq:zero-exclusion) bounds
a^2+v^2ge c_0^2/(log D)^2. Termwise comparison therefore gives the
uniform estimate for S(s) on (1,s_0]. Integrating
(eq:zeta-derivative) after moving 1/(s-1) gives

 zeta_K(s_0)asympkappalog D.

Explicitly, the logarithmic derivative of (s-1)zeta_K(s) is
S(s)-tfrac12log D+O(1), whose integral over this interval is O(1)
and whose limit at s=1 is 1. Since zeta_K(s_0)ge1, this proves
(eq:residue-lower).

The Euler product gives sum_{ple D}d_K(p)/p^{s_0}lelogzeta_K(s_0).
Also d_K(p)le6 and
[
 sum_{ple D}d_K(p)(frac1p-frac1{p^{s_0}})
 lefrac6{log D}sum_{ple D}(log p)/(p)ll1,
]
by Chebyshev's bound and partial summation. Equation
(eq:zeta-residue-comparison) then gives (eq:prime-sum).

For the interval estimate, we use Shiu's theorem [cite] in the
form of cite[Theorem~1.1]{Pollack2020} with empty excluded residue
sets: for fge0 multiplicative with f(p^a)le A_1^a and
f(m)le A_2(ε)m^ε, and for each fixed
0<beta<1/2,

 sum_{x-y<mle x}f(m)
 llfrac y{log x}exp(sum_{ple x}frac{f(p)}p)
 (x^beta<yle x).

Constants and thresholds depend only on beta,A_1, and A_2. The
function d_K is multiplicative and bounded by d_6, since
d_K(p^a)lebinom{a+5}{5}le 6^a and d_K(m)le d_6(m)ll_ε

Take beta=1/3; the assumption yge x^{1/2} lies in range. Since
xle D, equation (eq:prime-sum) bounds the exponential factor
by O(kappalog D), and xge D^{1/4} gives
log D/log xle 4. This proves (eq:ideal-interval).


## The cusp and balls in the space of lattices

For xin X, let m(x) be the length of its shortest nonzero vector
in the sup norm. The sup norm is only used to simplify the fiber
integral.

[Uniform cusp bound]
There exist C,c>0 such that, for all sufficiently large D,
[
 mu_{K,sigma}{x:m(x)le r}le Cr^c
 (0<r<1).
]
Consequently the packet probabilities form a tight family as
Dto∞, and every subsequential limit is an A-invariant
probability measure on X.


Proof. 
Take f=one_{[-r,r]^6} and put X_r=Qr^6. For 0<ple r^6, substitute
t_j=log r-u_j in (eq:fiber-integral). The positive-sign region

[

       (0<ple r^6),
  V_f(p)=0quad(p>r^6).
]

vectors, (eq:vector-unfolding) gives

 mu_{K,sigma}{mle r}
 llfrac1{Qkappa}sum_{lle X_r}d_K(l)
     (1+log(X_r/l))^5.

The sum is empty for X_r<1.

Suppose first rge D^{-1/48}, so X_rge D^{3/8}. Split the range
lge D^{1/4} into dyadic intervals (2^{-j-1}X_r,2^{-j}X_r]. Any
such interval with lge D^{1/4} has upper endpoint between D^{1/4}
and Q, and its ideal count is O(kappa2^{-j}X_r) by

Now let r<D^{-1/48} and X_rge1. Summing the same divisor estimate

## Trace slices of the codifferent

For a lattice vector v and dual vector y, the coordinatewise product
v_jy_j is nearly preserved under block-diagonal motion. The useful
arithmetic quantity is a partial sum of these products, which is

# Homogeneous limits and their fixed points

The arithmetic estimates of Section~sec:arithmetic already imply
that any weak limit of packet probabilities is an A_6-invariant
probability. This section shows that any such limit has positive entropy
in every positive-weight ergodic component, and that every positive-entropy
ergodic component is algebraic. The algebraic components are then
classified; the proper ones are the block-diagonal orbits of types
3+3 and 2+2+2. Every such orbit lies in a fixed-point set of a
nonidentity diagonal with algebraic entries.

## The algebraic alternatives

We use the EKL measure-classification theorem.

[EKL]

preserves entropy.

Proof.

ine k.

modular character to A gives
[

the purpose of the rest of the paper.

# Conditional probabilities at intermediate resolutions


This is the technical core of the paper. We must extract, from each
packet measure mu_i, conditional information along unipotent plaques
that survives the limit. Exact conditional measures give only Dirac
masses because the orbits are compact; instead we condition small
transverse cells before taking the limit, at depths that are chosen so
that the packet data change little over a broad range of depths.

the defining grids. None of these conventions requires absolute
continuity.

# A product rule for the plaque probabilities

The projective measures constructed in Section~sec:conditioning
retain information from conditioning the packet measures at depths
tending to infinity. We now prove a product rule for them, into
one-dimensional root group factors attached to the same augmented state.
That property is what lets root orientations later compose.

Product decompositions for genuine leafwise conditional measures were
developed by Einsiedler and Katok cite[Proposition~8.3]{EK2003}
cite[Theorems~8.4--8.5]{EK2005}; see also cite[Corollary~8.8 and
Theorem~9.8]{EL2010}. Here the underlying measures are the
augmented-state data from Section~sec:conditioning, built by
conditioning at intermediate depths and passing to the limit; the cited
leafwise theorems do not directly apply. We prove the product identity
for the data by the chart-comparison machinery of
Section~sec:conditioning.

Throughout, use pprec q for p_i/q_ito0. Every scale used below
lies in the plateau [l_{-,i},l_{+,i}], with room for the displayed
separations and additional separations at both ends.

[Product rule]
Let V and W be pattern groups, let U=U_alpha be a one-dimensional
root group, and suppose that multiplication gives a coordinate
decomposition
[

]
Suppose also that some a_0in A fixes U and that the automorphism

 Delta(w)=a_0^{-1}wa_0

 m^V_omega
   propto
  ((w,u)⟼ wu)_*
      (m^W_omega ⊗  m^U_omega).

The constant of proportionality is positive, and may depend on omega.


The case W={1} is immediate. Henceforth Wne{1}. The root
constraints defining a contracting logarithm are rational linear

in matrix-entry coordinates of W,

    d_{jk}inN, d_{jk}>0.

We first show that Theorem~thm:product factors the measure of any
pattern group into its one-dimensional root measures, in a suitable
order. The cone generated by its roots is pointed. Choose a root

proportional to alpha, so a diagonal logarithm can vanish on
alpha and be strictly negative on every other root. Removing
alpha leaves a normal pattern subgroup W: a sum of two roots in
the pattern cannot equal alpha, since extremality would force both
summands onto its ray. Thus Theorem~thm:product applies with
V=WU_alpha. Iterate, allowing W={1} at the last step.

For transitivity, fix distinct p,q,r and consider the three-root
Heisenberg group generated by U_{pq},U_{qr},U_{pr}. If a_{pr}=0,
factor first with U_{pq} as the final factor and then with U_{qr}

[

[

positive mass to matrices with nonzero pr-entry, contradicting the
first description. The projective identities hold on compact parameter
ranges, so all masses are finite. Thus transitivity holds.

For the support assertion, iterate the successive factorization.
Every inactive root factor is a point mass at the identity; each
remaining factor lies on its own active root group. The product lies
in the subgroup generated by the active roots. Transitivity shows
exactly that the active positive roots are closed under root addition,
so that subgroup is a pattern group and is closed. There are finitely
many positive-root patterns, so the simultaneous assertion holds.

# Combining the root orientations

The previous section produced activity somewhere on every long
segment across each oriented cut. This section shows that actually
every root is active at almost every state of the augmented limit.
The bridge is that, on the scale R_i, the plaque activity of a root
jk is nearly a function of the value of the root coordinate alone.
Transitivity then becomes an approximate addition rule on real
functions, and two-edge cycles force the inactive sets to have measure
zero.

law.

# Proof of the equidistribution theorem

We now return to the packet sequence of Theorem~thm:main.
Proposition~prop:cusp and Mahler's compactness criterion give
tightness. Take any subsequence with a weak probability limit mu;
it is A-invariant, and Proposition~prop:ball gives the
ordinary-ball estimate required by
Proposition~prop:positive-entropy. Theorem~thm:ekl
therefore makes almost every A-ergodic component of mu
homogeneous.

Apply the intermediate-conditioning construction to this subsequence,
passing further to a subsequence wherever necessary. The augmented
limiting state projects to mu on X. Proposition~prop:bowen
supplies the tube estimate throughout the required time interval, and
Theorem~thm:full-activity makes every root active almost surely
in this state.

If proper homogeneous components had positive total weight,
Lemma~lem:fixed-pieces would place positive mu-mass on the
countable union of their fixed-point pieces. At least one piece would
then have positive mass. Its fixing diagonal is nonidentity, so it has
two unequal diagonal entries, and the corresponding root plaques meet
that piece in at most countably many points. By
Lemma~lem:transverse-inactive, that root is inactive almost surely
on states projecting to the piece, a contradiction with the previous
paragraph. Thus almost every component is the G-invariant
probability, and mu=m_6.

Every weakly convergent subsequence supplied by tightness has the same
limit. It follows that the original sequence converges to m_6 on
C_c(X_6). Proposition~prop:cusp already gives the compact sets
required for the nonescape assertion. The estimates are uniform in the
embedding ordering, so the conclusion holds for the arbitrary choices
in Theorem~thm:main.
