Fix an integer $dge1$ and let $w_d=exp(2pi i/d)$ be the primitive $d$-th root of unity. The unnormalized Fourier matrix $F_d$ has entry $w_d^{jk}$ in row $j$ and column $k$, with $0le j,k<d$, and the Fourier transform of a complex vector $x$ of length $n$ is the product $F_n x$. Direct multiplication costs on the order of $n^2$ operations; the aim is a faster exact procedure at every length $n$, counting also every block of scalar preparation and every movement of data.

The arithmetic model charges one unit per complex field operation on the input (addition, subtraction, multiplication by a prepared scalar) and one unit per integer operation or random access on $O(log(n+2))$-bit words, a fixed number of which may hold an address. Division occurs only in scalar preparation and always with a justified nonzero divisor. The algorithm receives one explicitly specified root $w_D$ of a stated order $D$, as an oracle of unit cost, and only one root of polynomially bounded order is ever used; every other scalar, schedule, and address map is built from it inside the budget. No bound is placed on coefficients or on intermediate magnitudes, so the statements concern exact deterministic arithmetic and claim nothing about finite precision, numerical stability, or bit complexity.

Fix the exact integers
$$ m=10^6,quad W_*=2^{71},quad Delta=6871402692000000,quad lambda=m-Delta/W_*,quad theta=log_mlambda . $$
Since $1<lambda<m$, one has $0<theta<1$, and $theta=0.99999999999978935615699598...$; the decimal only gauges the size of the saving, while the definition through $m$ and $Delta$ is exact.

The main result is this. There is a single deterministic algorithm which, for every positive integer $n$ and every $xinℂ^n$, computes $F_n x$ exactly, and which, in the stated model including all scalar preparation, schedule construction, and address work, runs in
$$ O!(n(log n)^theta(loglog n)^{4-theta}) $$
field and address operations as $nto∞$. A single supplied root $w_{D_*}$, for an explicitly computable order $D_*<1024,n^3$, suffices. Consequently the transform is computable at every length in $O!(n(log n)^{1-10^{-13}})=o(nlog n)$ time; a finite initial range of $n$ is handled by the direct formula. The constants are enormous, so this is an asymptotic existence and explicitness statement with no practical crossover, conditional on exact arithmetic and the specified root.

A companion result settles exact convolution. There is a single deterministic algorithm which, for every $n$ and all $a,binℂ^n$, computes the linear convolution $c_k=sum_{r+s=k}a_r b_s$, $0le kle 2n-2$, equivalently multiplies two complex polynomials of degree below $n$, and which, with general field multiplication allowed for the pointwise products, runs in the same time $O!(n(log n)^theta(loglog n)^{4-theta})$, hence eventually $O!(n(log n)^{1-10^{-13}})$, including preparation, padding, and indexing. This uses three transforms; it is bilinear in the two inputs, not a linear circuit.

The whole estimate rides on a saving inside a single small tensor factor. The classical mixed-radix factorization of Cooley and Tukey computes transforms at highly composite lengths in $O(nlog n)$ arithmetic operations, and its own account of the cost is worth quoting:
The algorithm iterates on the array of given complex Fourier amplitudes and yields the result in less than $2Nlog_2 N$ operations without requiring more data storage than is required for the given array $A$.

Good's coprime-factor construction identifies certain one-dimensional transforms with tensor products having no intervening twiddle factor, and Cooley, Lewis, and Welch trace the same construction back to Thomas through the Chinese remainder problem, singling out the difference that matters:
the "twiddle factor" ... does not appear in (10) and the correspondence between one- and two-dimensional indexing is different.

Bluestein's chirp identity reduces an arbitrary Fourier length to a convolution, and with an ordinary power-of-two transform this yields the $O(nlog n)$ arithmetic bound at every length; Rabiner, Schafer, and Rader give the weighted-convolution derivation, the padding, and the operation count, and Harvey, van der Hoeven, and Lecerf restate the chirp reduction among the tools of fast multiplication.

Improving the constant or the logarithm inside the $nlog n$ scale is a different question from moving the scale. Morgenstern's determinant argument bounds the linear complexity of the unnormalized transform with bounded coefficients, and Ailon's matrix-entropy argument bounds the normalized transform computed from two-coordinate unitary gates, later sharpening the bound under conditioning restrictions:
./pdf/ailon-1305.4745v1-matrix-entropy.pdf
./pdf/ailon-1403.1307v5-conditioned-lb.pdf
Alman and Rao use matrix non-rigidity to speed up the Walsh-Hadamard transform and then to improve the leading constant of power-of-two discrete Fourier transforms in counts of real arithmetic operations with precomputed circuit structure:
./pdf/alman-rao-2211.06459v2-nonrigidity.pdf
The result is of the other kind: a power saving in the logarithm at every length, in an exact complex-arithmetic model that also charges scalar preparation and array organization. The unnormalized transform with unrestricted coefficients and exact intermediate values sits outside the hypotheses of the determinant and entropy bounds, and the logarithmic word size describes addresses only, never the precision of complex registers.

The three inputs to the argument can be stated at once. The first is a fixed finite network that applies tensor powers $C^{ ⊗  k}$ of one two-by-two kernel $C$ faster than the naive $O(k2^k)$; the kernel is
$$ C=frac121+i&1-i1-i&1+i $$
so that $C^2$ is the swap of two coordinates. The second is a compiler that writes each Fourier matrix $F_r$, at any primitive $r$-th root, as a short word on exactly $r$ coordinates whose layers are monomial or disjoint copies of $C$, borrowing no padding. The third runs a tensor product of such words across many coprime axes with linear-time reindexing; a chirp convolution then reaches every requested length. The saving in the top-level logarithm is the elementary fact that a good working length carries only $O(log n/loglog n)$ odd-prime factors.

First, the fixed network. Write the swap of two coordinates as the 2x2 matrix with ones on the antidiagonal, and let $C$ be the 2x2 matrix that has $a=(1+i)/2$ on the diagonal and $b=(1-i)/2$ off it. Since $a^2+b^2=0$ and $2ab=1$, one has $C^2$ equal to the swap, so $C$ is a square root of the swap, and $C$ is not diagonal.

Fix $h=100$, let $T$ run over the three-element subsets of ${1,...,h}$, put $v=binom{h}{3}=161700$, $N_0=v^3$, and $m=h^3=10^6$. Two triples are neighbours when the intersection has even size; neighbours are then distinct. There are two banks of scalar coordinates $X_d$ and $Y_d$, indexed by triples $d=(d_1,d_2,d_3)in T^3$. The network has three stages; at stage $j$ the two indices of $d$ other than $d_j$ are frozen and the two lists obtained by varying $d_j$ are processed. There are $I_0=3v^2$ such invocations, and each owns auxiliary coordinates used by no other: one side coordinate $A_{ST}$ for each ordered neighbouring pair $(S,T)$, and $h+1$ central coordinates $c_1,...,c_h,c_*$.

For one invocation name the logical source and target banks $x=(x_T)$ and $y=(y_S)$. The invocation rests on four fixed linear maps:
$$ (Vx)_{ST}=x_T,qquad (Gx)_j=sum_{Tni j}x_T,qquad (Gx)_*=sum_T x_T, $$
$$ (JA)_S=-sum_{T  neighbour  S}(|Scap T|-1)/(2),A_{ST},qquad (Rc)_S=frac{sum_{jin S}c_j-c_*}{2}. $$
The forward invocation performs the following eight rows in order: $y← y-JA$; $y← y-Rc$; $A← A+Vx$; $c← c+Gx$; $y← y+Rc$; $y← y+JA$; $c← c-Gx$; $A← A-Vx$. Each gate is the indicated finite linear map on its own coordinates, at fixed finite cost including all scalar work.

Lemma. For arbitrary initial auxiliary values the forward invocation sends $(x,y,A,c)$ to $(x,y+x,A,c)$; three such invocations, two forwards and the second reversed, send $(X_d,Y_d)$ to $(-Y_d,X_d)$ for every $din T^3$ and restore every auxiliary value.

Proof. The first two rows subtract $JA+Rc$ from $y$, the middle rows add $R(c+Gx)+J(A+Vx)$, and the last two restore $c$ and $A$; the net addition to $y$ is $(RG+JV)x$. The coefficient of $x_T$ in $(RG)_S$ is $(|Scap T|-1)/2$, which is $1$ when $S=T$, $0$ when $|Scap T|=1$, and is cancelled by $JV$ when $|Scap T|=0$ or $2$. Hence $RG+JV$ is the identity, and the auxiliary values cancel in each invocation on its own coordinates. The stage sequence $(x,y)mapsto(x,y+x)mapsto(-y,y+x)mapsto(-y,x)$ gives the stated bank exchange.

A triple has $d_{nbr}=binom{97}{3}+3cdot97=147731$ neighbours, the two terms counting intersections of size $0$ and $2$, and the number of physical wires is $W=2N_0+I_0(vd_{nbr}+101)=1873807244643542670000$, the term $vd_{nbr}$ counting ordered pairs.

The network alone would still cost $Wm$ pointwise operations to turn into tensor powers. The saving comes from changing frame along its wires. Work in the binary space $D=𝔽_2^m$ with its dot product; call a subspace nondegenerate when its bilinear form has zero radical. For $zin D$ write $R_z$ for translation of addresses, $(R_z f)(x)=f(x+z)$, and let $J_m=2^{-m/2},((-1)^{xcdot y})_{x,y}$ be the normalized Walsh matrix, with $J_m^2$ the identity. Conjugating the diagonal character $(-1)^{zcdot x}$ by $J_m$ returns $R_z$. For a nondegenerate label $U$ let $P_U$ be projection onto $U$ along $U^perp$ and set
$$ q_U(x)=wt(P_U x)bmod 4,qquad Phi_U=J_m,diag_x(i^{q_U(x)}),J_m, $$
where $wt$ is integer Hamming weight. These matrices are used only to prove identities; the algorithm never executes a Walsh transform. Plainly $Phi_0$ is the identity, and $Phi_D=C^{ ⊗  m}$, because $q_D(x)=wt(x)$ splits over the $m$ bits and each bit factor is the $2times 2$ kernel $C$.

Lemma. Let $U⊆ V$ be nested nondegenerate labels and let the residual $E=Vcap U^perp$ carry an orthonormal binary basis of size $rho$. Then $Phi_VPhi_U^{-1}$ is a product of exactly $rho$ directional kernels $C_z=a,Id+b,R_z$ or the inverse $C_z^{-1}$, with $zne0$.

Proof. For binary $x,y$ one has $wt(x+y)=wt(x)+wt(y)-2|supp(x)capsupp(y)|$, so weight is additive modulo four on orthogonal vectors. Decomposing $V=Uperp E$ over the orthonormal basis $z_1,...,z_rho$ gives $q_V(x)-q_U(x)=sum_j wt(z_j)[z_jcdot x]pmod 4$, with brackets the integer representative $0$ or $1$. Every $wt(z_j)$ is odd, so its residue is $pm1$; on a decreasing edge the whole expression is negated. The identity $i^{[zcdot x]}=a+b(-1)^{zcdot x}$ followed by Walsh conjugation produces $C_z$, and $R_z^2=Id$ with $C_z^2=R_z$ gives $C_z^{-1}=R_zC_z$.

To apply the lemma, replace each scalar wire by an array indexed by $D$, give every gate a nondegenerate label shared by all wires it touches, give each source and sink its own label, and insert $Phi_VPhi_U^{-1}$ along every edge from label $U$ to label $V$. Scalar gates mix the touched arrays with scalar coefficients, while the common frame acts only on the address coordinate, so the two commute; the intermediate frames cancel and the intermediate auxiliary roles are restored by the scalar lemma. Indexing every array instead by $f$ columns of $m$ bits and using $Phi_U^{ ⊗  f}$, the same cancellation gives the full identity
$$ diag_{sinks}(Phi_U^{ ⊗  f}),S,diag_{sources}((Phi_U^{-1})^{ ⊗  f}), $$
where $S$ is the pointwise signed bank exchange and the identity on auxiliaries. This holds on arbitrary values in every physical role.

The remaining choice is the labels. Identify $D$ with $D^{ ⊗ 3}$ for $D=𝔽_2^h$, and for $Tin{1,...,h}$ of size three let $t_T$ be its indicator, so $t_Tcdot t_T=1$ and neighbour indicators are orthogonal. Put $u_d=t_{d_1} ⊗  t_{d_2} ⊗  t_{d_3}$ and $U_d=< u_d>$, a nondegenerate line. Sources carry label $0$ except that $X_d$ carries $U_d$; sinks carry label $D$ except that $Y_d$ carries $U_d^perp$. A route from $0$ to $D$ then has frame ratio $C^{ ⊗  m}$, and the route $X_dto Y_d$ has the same ratio up to a translation, as the terminal computation confirms. The total increase of terminal dimensions is $Wm-2N_0$, leaving a margin of $2N_0$ below $Wm$ before any internal decrease is charged.

At an invocation in stage $j$ write $E=D^{ ⊗ (j-1)}$, $P=<⊗_{l<j}t_{d_l}>$, $B=P^perpsubset E$, and $Q=<⊗_{l>j}t_{d_l}>$, with an empty tensor product the whole one-dimensional space. Thus $E=Bperp P$, $B=0$ in stage $1$, and $Q$ is everything in stage $3$. Writing $t=t_{d_j}$ at the physical bank entry of a gate, the eight scalar rows carry the following labels, each further tensored with $Q$: row $0$ on $Y$ and its side wires, $B ⊗ < t>$; rows $1$ and $4$ on $Y$ and the centre, $B ⊗  D$; row $2$ on $X$ and the side, $(B ⊗  D)perp(P ⊗ < t>)$; rows $3$ and $6$ on $X$ and the centre, $E ⊗  D$; row $5$ on $Y$ and the side, $(B ⊗  D)perp(P ⊗  t^perp)$; row $7$ on $X$ and the side, $E ⊗  D$. The $X$ labels rise to $E ⊗  D$ while the $Y$ labels rise through $B ⊗  D$; the only decrease is the centre wire's return from $E ⊗  D$ in row $3$ to $B ⊗  D$ in row $4$, of dimension $h$. At stage $2$ the eight updates run reversed with logical source $Y$ and target $X$; the physical touch order is $Y$-side, $Y$-centre, $X$-side, $X$-centre, $Y$-centre, $Y$-side, $X$-centre, $X$-side, the ordered neighbouring side pairs stay neighbouring, and every displayed label stays nondegenerate.

Lemma. Every edge has nested nondegenerate endpoint labels, every nonzero orthogonal residual has an orthonormal binary basis, and the summed residual dimension is
$$ s=Wm-2N_0+2I_0(h+1)h=1873807244636671267308000000,qquad Delta:=Wm-s=2v^2(v-3h(h+1))=6871402692000000>0. $$

Proof of the residual lemma. The full list of nonzero segments is as follows, all but the last tensored with $Q$; the omitted edges have zero residual. On $X$: entering to row $2$ has $B ⊗  t_X^perp$, and row $2$ to $3$ has $P ⊗  t_X^perp$. On $Y$: row $0$ to $1$ has $B ⊗  t_Y^perp$, and row $4$ to $5$ has $P ⊗  t_Y^perp$. On the centre: source to row $1$ has $B ⊗  D$, while each of $1to3$, $3to4$, $4to6$ has $P ⊗  D$. On the side: source to row $0$ has $B ⊗ < t_Y>$; row $0$ to $2$ has $(B ⊗  t_Y^perp)perp(P ⊗ < t_X>)$; row $2$ to $5$ has $P ⊗ < t_X,t_Y>^perp$; row $5$ to $7$ has $P ⊗ < t_Y>$. Finally the auxiliary edge from its last use to the sink has $E ⊗  D ⊗  Q^perp$, with $Q^perp$ taken in the full future space.

The point of neighbouring triples is the side edge $2to5$. Since $t_Xcdot t_Y=0$ and both have norm one, $t_Y^perp=< t_X>perp< t_X,t_Y>^perp$, so the row-$2$ label $(B ⊗  D)perp(P ⊗ < t_X>)$ sits inside the row-$5$ label $(B ⊗  D)perp(P ⊗  t_Y^perp)$ with exactly the displayed residual. All other rows follow from $E=Bperp P$ and $D=< t>perp t^perp$. These decompositions confirm every inclusion and show that the only decreases are the central edges $3to4$, each of dimension $h$. The signed changes telescope along physical wires: sources sum to $N_0$ and sinks to $Wm-N_0$, a total of $Wm-2N_0$, and each of the $I_0(h+1)$ central wires loses $h$ once; replacing signed by absolute changes adds twice these losses, giving $s$. Since $v>3h(h+1)$, $Delta=2N_0-2I_0(h+1)h=2v^2(v-3h(h+1))>0$, and substitution yields the printed integers.

Nondegeneracy alone does not force an orthonormal basis over $𝔽_2$, because a nondegenerate form can be alternating. Every nonzero residual contains a norm-one vector: $t^perp$ and $< t_X,t_Y>^perp$ contain coordinate units outside supports of size at most three and six, a nonzero $B$ contains a coordinate unit outside a support of size $3^{j-1}<h^{j-1}$, a nonzero $Q^perp$ is handled the same way, and the full spaces and lines $P,Q,< t>$ plainly contain norm-one vectors; tensor products and a nonzero summand in an orthogonal sum preserve this. A nondegenerate binary symmetric space with a norm-one vector has an orthonormal basis: split off unit lines until the remainder is alternating, split a nonzero nondegenerate alternating space into orthogonal hyperbolic planes, and absorb each plane perpendicular to an already removed unit $w$ by replacing $(w,p,q)$ with $w+p$, $w+q$, $w+p+q$, which stay pairwise orthogonal unit vectors spanning the same three dimensions. All choices are finite binary linear algebra with a fixed pivot order.

Proposition (finite network interface). For every integer $fge1$ the explicit network applies $C^{ ⊗  mf}$ to each of the $W$ arrays indexed by $𝔽_2^{mf}$, using $s$ directional steps, each on one array and conjugate by address permutations to copies of $C^{ ⊗  f}$ on fibres, together with a fixed number of pointwise linear maps and address permutations; the assertion survives any extra spectator address bits.

Proof. Use the network labels with each address split into $f$ columns of $m$ bits. Except on the route $X_dto Y_d$ a source and its routed sink carry labels $0$ and $D$, so the frame ratio is $C^{ ⊗  mf}$. On the exceptional route put $u=u_d$; since $ucdot u=1$, $P_{U_d}x=u[ucdot x]$, and orthogonal weight additivity gives $q_{U_d^perp}(x)-q_{U_d}(x)=wt(x)-2,wt(u)[ucdot x]=wt(x)+2[ucdot x]pmod4$, because $wt(u_d)=27$ is odd. The exceptional frame ratio is therefore $R_uC^{ ⊗  m}$ in each column. Correct these translations at the sinks and undo the signed bank exchange; the result is $C^{ ⊗  mf}$ on every physical role, including each auxiliary array.

By the edge lemma an edge of residual dimension $rho$ costs $rho$ products $C_z^{ ⊗  f}$ or $(C_z^{-1})^{ ⊗  f}$. Extend $z$ to a binary basis and apply the corresponding invertible address change in each column; translation by $z$ becomes a flip of one selected bit, so the forward product is $C^{ ⊗  f}$ on those $f$ bits for each setting of all other bits, and an inverse product adds only a translation. Summing the residual dimensions gives exactly $s$ directional steps, and the network, its bases, and the count of remaining pointwise operations are fixed independently of $f$.

Lemma (linear-time address changes). Let $k=mf+r$ with $0le r<m$, and fix an invertible binary map on an $m$-bit column. Applying it in all $f$ columns, translating columns by prescribed fixed vectors, and gathering one chosen output bit from every column into a contiguous $f$-bit field all take $O(2^k)$ time, with a constant depending on $m$ and the fixed maps but not on $f$ or $k$.

Proof. An $m$-bit column is a digit of radix $2^m$. Since $m$ and the maps are fixed, a finite table gives for each column digit its old bits, its transformed bits, the selected output bit, and the remaining output bits; the table is built once at absolute cost. The $r$ spectator bits form one more digit of radix $2^r$, absent when $r=0$. For a given $k$ compute the input and output stride contributions of each column in $O(f+1)$ operations, then traverse the digit prefix tree keeping the running input address and the desired output address; extending a prefix by a digit adds its two tabulated contributions in constant time, and at each leaf one value is copied to its output address through a separate output array. Every internal node has at least two children, so there are fewer than $2^{k+1}$ nodes for $2^k$ leaves and the total work is $O(2^k)$, including preparation. Reversing the two addresses gives the inverse permutation at the same cost. Placing the chosen $f$ bits as the least significant output field makes each fibre contiguous and each consecutive block of $W_*$ fibres a contiguous block of $W_*2^f$ entries, needing no per-entry scan.

Theorem (tensor-power bound). There is a deterministic exact algorithm applying $C^{ ⊗  k}$ to an arbitrary complex array of length $2^k$ in $O(2^k(k+1)^theta)$ time for every $kge0$, including fixed-table construction, all scalar arithmetic, array initialization, index preparation, and data movement; only the rational constants and $i$ are needed.

Proof. Take the least power of two at least $W$, namely $W_*=2^{71}$, and set
$$ S=s+(W_*-W)m=W_*m-Delta,qquad lambda=(S)/(W_*)=m-frac{Delta}{2^{71}},qquad theta=log_mlambda, $$
so that $1<lambda<m$ and $0<theta<1$; the $W_*-W$ extra roles each use the $m$ ordinary coordinate directions, preserving the absolute saving $Delta$. Transform $W_*$ arbitrary arrays at once. With $K=m(71+1)$ fixed, for $k<K$ apply the $k$ factors of $C$ along the fibres one at a time; since $K$ is fixed the cost divided by $W_*2^k$ stays bounded, including $k=0$. For $kge K$ write $k=mf+r$ with $f=lfloor k/mrfloor$ and $0le r<m$, apply the $r$ leftover bit factors directly, use the finite interface with $f$ columns and $r$ spectator bits on $W$ arrays, and on each of the other $W_*-W$ arrays use one directional step for each of the $m$ coordinate directions; there are $S$ directional steps in all. Every step needs $2^{k-f}$ independent transforms $C^{ ⊗  f}$; since $fge72$ and $k-fge(m-1)fge71$, the number $2^{k-f}$ is divisible by $W_*=2^{71}$, so after the address permutation its fibres split into exactly $2^{k-f}/W_*$ batches, each handled by recursion, with no partial batch.

Let $T(k)$ be the cost for $W_*$ arrays and $t(k)=T(k)/(W_*2^k)$. The fixed pointwise gates, permutations, terminal corrections, and at most $m-1$ leftover bit layers cost at most $A W_*2^k$ outside recursive calls, so
$$ T(k)le A W_*2^k+S,frac{2^{k-f}}{W_*},T(lfloor k/mrfloor),qquad t(k)le A+lambda,t(lfloor k/mrfloor)quad(kge K). $$
Unrolling $dlelceillog_m(k+1)rceil$ steps, with successive arguments $lfloor k/m^jrfloor$ and base costs at most $B$, gives
$$ t(k)le Asum_{j=0}^{d-1}lambda^j+Blambda^dle((A)/(lambda-1)+B)lambda^d=O((k+1)^theta), $$
using $lambda^dlelambda(k+1)^theta$. Fixed tables are generated once at absolute cost. The recursion can reuse working arrays: its stack has $O(log(k+2))$ levels, consecutive active lengths drop by at least a factor two, and all counters and strides fit $O(k+1)$ bits. Initializing $W_*-1$ unused arrays, running the simultaneous procedure on the requested array in the last role, and keeping that role's output costs $O(W_*2^k)=O(2^k)$, and $W_*$ is an absolute constant. This proves the stated single-array bound.

Restoring arbitrary auxiliary values in the scalar lemma is exactly what makes the recursion work: after the frame changes those auxiliary roles must undergo the same tensor transform as the data banks, and only a network valid on arbitrary inputs lets arbitrary fibres serve as all $W_*$ roles.

Second, compiling a Fourier matrix on exactly its own coordinates. Padding a coordinate set is forbidden, because the sets later become tensor factors and separate padding would multiply the total dimension. The Fourier matrix is first factored into diagonal and triangular Toeplitz factors, the Toeplitz factors are realized as words on the original coordinates, with recorded workspace, and every two-coordinate shear is then compiled to the single kernel $C$. Call an update that adds a scalar multiple of one coordinate to a distinct coordinate a shear; a shear layer is a set of shears on disjoint pairs; a monomial layer is an invertible monomial matrix; a mixed round performs monomial layers or shear layers on disjoint coordinate regions, and splits into a monomial layer followed by a shear layer.

Lemma (Newton reduction). For every primitive $r$-th root $u$ and $rge1$ there is an invertible lower triangular $N$ with $F_r(u)=N D_N^{-1} N^{mathsf T}sfT$, where $D_N=diag(N_{00},...,N_{r-1,r-1})$; $N$ is a product of two invertible diagonal matrices and one invertible lower triangular Toeplitz matrix of width $r$, prepared as rational expressions in $u$ with every denominator nonzero.

Proof. For $r=1$ take $N=D_N=T=(1)$. Otherwise let the $k$-th column of $P$ be the coefficient vector of the monic Newton polynomial $prod_{j<k}(Z-u^j)$, so $P$ is unit upper triangular and $N=F_r(u)P$ is lower triangular. For $ige k$,
$$ N_{ik}=prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(u^i-u^j)=(-1)^k u^{k(k-1)/2}frac{H_i}{H_{i-k}},qquad H_j=prod_{s=1}^{j}(1-u^s), H_0=1, $$
each $H_j$, $0le j<r$, nonzero because $u$ is primitive. Hence $N=diag(H_i),T,diag((-1)^k u^{k(k-1)/2})$ with $T_{ik}=H_{i-k}^{-1}$ for $ige k$ and $0$ else, an invertible lower triangular Toeplitz matrix of diagonal one. Writing $L=ND_N^{-1}$ gives the unit-lower/diagonal/unit-upper factorization $F_r(u)=LD_NP^{-1}$; such a factorization is unique, since comparing two forces a unit lower triangular matrix to equal an upper one, hence the identity, and then fixes the diagonal. Since $F_r(u)=F_r(u)^{mathsf T}sfT$, transposition gives another such factorization, so $P^{-1}=L^{mathsf T}sfT$ and $F_r(u)=ND_N^{-1}N^{mathsf T}sfT$. Every division is by a product of nonzero $H_j$ and powers of $u$, and the argument covers composite $r$ and every primitive root.

Thus it suffices to compile an invertible triangular Toeplitz matrix on its own coordinates; its transpose needs the same number of layers, since transposition reverses factor order and preserves monomial and shear layers. The local convolutions are written as linear arithmetic directed acyclic graphs in which each gate forms a linear combination of at most two earlier values or sources, fan-out counting designated output uses. A convolution of variable data with a fixed vector, of total operand length $v$, has such a DAG with $O(vlog(2+v))$ gates and $O(log(2+v))$ depth and absolute bounded fan-out: pad to the least power of two at least the full length, use pointwise multiplication at roots of unity with the fixed vector's transform supplying scalars, and combine transformed entries by the binary recursion $a,bmapsto a+omega b,,a-omega b$. Reversals and truncations preserve the bounds. Topology is printed before any coefficient is evaluated, keeping even gates whose scalar happens to vanish.

The DAG may need more registers than the coordinates hold. A replay identity runs it inside coordinates that already carry other data, using the compute-copy-reverse pattern of Bennett's reversible simulation and the principle of restoring storage whose contents are arbitrary, as in clean transparent computation in catalytic space:
./pdf/buhrman-et-al-2014-catalytic-space.pdf

Lemma (replay with arbitrary borrowed coordinates). Suppose $M:ℂ^etoℂ^a$ has a linear DAG with $g$ gates, depth $H$, fan-in at most two, and fan-out at most $delta$ counting output uses, and each output is a source or gate read or zero. On disjoint coordinate sets $x,y,z$ of sizes $e,a,g$, a word of $O((delta+1)(H+1))$ shear layers and at most $8g+2a$ shears implements $(x,y,z)mapsto(x,y+Mx,z)$; the initial contents of $z$ are arbitrary, even correlated with $x$ and $y$.

Proof. Give each gate one coordinate $z_v$ in topological order and add its prescribed linear combination of predecessors into $z_v$, at most two shears per gate; the forward sweep is $zmapsto Uz+Tx$ with $U$ unit lower triangular, and the output reads are $Fz+Dx$ with $FT+D=M$ because a zero-gate-coordinate start reproduces the DAG. Run the sweep, add the output reads to $y$, and undo, restoring $z$ and adding $Mx+FUz$ to $y$. Run the same sweep with direct-source edges suppressed, omit $Dx$ at the readout, subtract the resulting $FUz$, and undo again; the net effect is the stated map, and it is an identity in independent $x,y,z$, hence valid for correlated initial $z$. To layer the sweeps, give each gate a longest-path depth level; within a level the undirected multigraph of shear edges has maximum degree at most $max{2,delta}$, so greedy edge colouring uses at most $2max{2,delta}-1$ colours and each colour has disjoint pairs, reordering being legal because no destination precedes a predecessor at the same level. Reverse sweeps reverse the layers and negate coefficients; four sweeps and two output rounds give the depth and the count $8g+2a$.

Lemma (exact-width Toeplitz layers). An invertible lower triangular Toeplitz matrix of width $r$ has a word on exactly $r$ coordinates in $O(1+log^4 r)$ mixed rounds, prepared together with its coefficients in $r^{O(1)}$ time from the Toeplitz entries and power-of-two roots of order at most $8r$; the topology depends only on $r$, and every division is justified by the nonzero diagonal entry.

Proof. The construction is the displacement-rank factorization of Kailath, Kung, and Morf, with an effective choice of chunk size that is measured on the printed graphs. Write $T_v=(h_{i-j})_{0le jle i<v}$, zero above the diagonal, $h_0ne0$. Compute reciprocal series $g_0=h_0^{-1}$ and $g_k=-h_0^{-1}sum_{j=1}^{k}h_jg_{k-j}$ for $1le k<r$; these invert every lower triangular Toeplitz block. For $v>1$ put $p=lfloor v/2rfloor$ and split
$$ T_v=T_p&0B&T_{v-p}=Id&0E&Iddiag(T_p,T_{v-p}),qquad E=BT_p^{-1}, $$
so two diagonal transforms run in parallel and then add into the second block from the transformed first. In original indices $E_{ij}=sum_{nu=j}^{p-1}h_{i-nu}g_{nu-j}$ for $ple i<v$, $0le j<p$.

Cut both intervals into consecutive chunks. For a target chunk of size $a$ and source chunk of size $e$ let $M$ be the corresponding $atimes e$ submatrix of $E$ and let $J_a,J_e$ be the lower shifts. Away from the first local row and column the entries telescope to $E_{ij}-E_{i-1,j-1}=-h_{i-p}g_{p-j}$, so, with $D=M-J_aMJ_e^{mathsf T}sfT$, one has without any rank test
$$ D=sum_{nu=1}^{3}v^{(nu)}(w^{(nu)})^{mathsf T}sfT, $$
formed by extending that outer product to the whole chunk, subtracting it from $D$ to get $R$, and adding the two corrective products $e_0R_{0,*}$ and $(R_{*,0}-R_{00}e_0)e_0^{mathsf T}sfT$; all three terms are kept even when coefficients vanish. Iterating $M=D+J_aMJ_e^{mathsf T}sfT$ gives, by nilpotence, $M=sum_{lge0}J_a^lD(J_e^{mathsf T}sfT)^l$. For one product $vw^{mathsf T}sfT$ its action on $x$ is two convolutions, $b_l=sum_{j=l}^{e-1}w_{j-l}x_j$ then $c_i=sum_{l=0}^{e-1}v_{i-l}b_l$; reversing $x$, the first is an ordinary convolution, the second a truncated one, and three such branches summed give a specific DAG with absolute constants $K_0,K_1,delta_0$ and
$$ gle K_0(a+e)log_2(2+a+e),qquad Hle K_1log_2(2+a+e),qquad deltaledelta_0, $$
counting output uses.

To fit this DAG in the parent block, reserve the $a+e$ source and target coordinates and one borrowed coordinate for each of the $g$ gates, each such coordinate staying assigned to its gate throughout the replay. Choose the chunk size by measurement, without knowing $K_0$: for each integer $bin{1,...,v}$ partition both intervals into chunks of size $b$ except the last, print the DAG skeleton of every resulting pair, count its gates, and call $b$ admissible when $g+a+ele v$ for every pair. Take the largest admissible $b$; this is an integer test on printed graphs, made before any data moves. If no $b$ is admissible, replace the entire recursive decomposition of $T_v$ by a direct word: process rows in decreasing order, multiply the running coordinate by $h_0$, and add $h_{i-j}$ times each lower coordinate $j<i$, whose original values are still in place.

The depth bound is now immediate to verify. Take an absolute $Dge16K_0+16$; for large $v$ the size $b_0=lfloor v/(Dlog_2 v)rfloorge v/(2Dlog_2 v)$ is admissible, because $a+ele 2v/(Dlog_2 v)le v/8$ and the gate bound gives $gle 4K_0v/Dle v/4$. So the fallback occurs only for bounded $v$, and the chosen $bge b_0$ leaves $O(log v)$ chunks per interval. For a chosen pair, borrow its $g$ coordinates outside its source and target chunks but inside the same $v$ coordinates; $g+a+ele v$ guarantees such coordinates exist. The replay runs in $O(1+log v)$ shear layers, the $O(log^2 v)$ pairs are processed serially, every borrowed coordinate is restored before the next pair, and the cross update takes $O(1+log^3 v)$ layers. From disjoint child blocks,
$$ d(v)lemax{d(lfloor v/2rfloor),d(lceil v/2rceil)}+O(1+log^3 v), $$
and summing along a balanced path gives $d(r)=O(1+log^4 r)$.

Preparation uses polynomially many scalar operations for the reciprocals, the entries $E_{ij}$, and the three outer products. Each recursion node tests at most $v$ sizes and at most $v^2$ chunk pairs per size, every printed graph has $O(vlog(2+v))$ gates, and there are fewer than $2r$ nodes, so even this deliberately redundant search is polynomial in integer cost; only the chosen graphs have coefficients evaluated, and computed scalars are stored as a shared arithmetic DAG. The first convolution has operand lengths $e,e$ and the second $a,e$, both padded to power-of-two lengths below $4r$, so one primitive root of order $L_r=2^{lceillog_2(4r)rceil}<8r$ supplies every needed root and its inverse by taking powers. All integer indices have $O(log(2+r))$ bits. This is the claimed preparation bound, and the finite threshold in the depth argument never has to be computed.

Lemma (Fourier layers at every primitive root). For every primitive $r$-th root $u$ the matrix $F_r(u)$ has a polynomially preparable exact-width word in $O(1+log^4 r)$ monomial and shear layers, whose coefficients are rational expressions in $u$ and the power-of-two roots just used, with all denominators nonzero.

Proof. Apply the Toeplitz lemma to the Toeplitz factor $T$ inside $N$ from the Newton reduction; the two diagonal factors of $N$ are monomial layers. Composing the words for $N^{mathsf T}sfT$, $D_N^{-1}$, and $N$, and noting that transposition preserves layer types while reversing factor order, gives the Fourier word. Splitting mixed rounds into monomial and shear layers gives $O(1+log^4 r)$ layers, and preparation and nonzero-denominator guarantees follow from the two lemmas.

It remains to compile a single shear into the kernel $C$. Put $H'$ the $2times2$ matrix with first row $1,1$ and second row $1,-1$, and $S=diag(1,i)$. Direct multiplication gives $H'=a^{-1}SCS$. Then
$$ K=H',diag(2,1),H',diag(-3,1),H'=-8&-100&-6, $$
and for every nonzero $t$
$$ 1&t0&1=D_t,diag(-1/8,-1/6),K,D_t^{-1},qquad D_t=diag(4t/5,1), $$
so an upper shear $y← y+tx$ with $tne0$ is three forward copies of $C$ framed by invertible diagonal factors. A coefficient $mu$ may vanish, but no test is needed: with
$$ kappa=1+mumubar,qquad t_1=kappa,qquad t_2=mu-kappa, $$
both $t_1$ and $t_2$ are nonzero because $1+|mu|^2>|mu|$, and the two shears compose to the shear with coefficient $mu$, including $mu=0$. Thus the same six-$C$ slot pattern serves every coefficient, and on disjoint pairs the words run in parallel with identity singletons elsewhere.

The conjugate in $kappa$ is a prepared scalar, not an operation on variable data: each coefficient $mu$ comes from an arithmetic DAG over rationals and the specified roots, and evaluating a second copy of that DAG with every root replaced by its inverse yields exactly $mubar$, since rationals are real, with denominators the conjugates of nonzero denominators; this covers the fixed $i$, whose conjugate is $-i$. Placing each layer of the Fourier word into a monomial slot followed by a shear slot, using identity for the idle type and the six-$C$ pattern for shears, and padding idle rounds turns every word into repeats of one constant-size pattern. Padding shorter words with idle rounds synchronizes any finite family without raising the $O(1+log^4 R)$ bound.

Proposition (uniform local compiler). Let $rge1$ and let $u$ be any primitive $r$-th root. The matrix $F_r(u)$ has a word on exactly $r$ coordinates in $O(1+log^4 r)$ slots, each a monomial layer or disjoint copies of $C$ on ordered pairs with identity on the remaining singletons; one fixed repeating pattern of slot types accepts all such words, so any finite family of widths at most $R$ synchronizes in $O(1+log^4 R)$ slots. Schedules and coefficients are prepared in $r^{O(1)}$ time, using besides $u$ and $i$ only power-of-two roots of order at most $8r$; coefficients are shared arithmetic DAGs over these roots and rationals, every division is by a proved nonzero scalar, every structural choice uses integers alone, and neither a zero test on a complex number nor conjugation of input data is needed.

Third, running many tensor factors at once. For radices $r_1,...,r_ellge2$ let $R=prod_j r_j$ and order coordinates by mixed-radix tuples with the first digit most significant. A depth-first traversal of the digit tuples visits fewer than $2R$ nodes, because
$$ sum_{j=0}^{ell}prod_{i=1}^{j}r_ile Rsum_{j=0}^{ell}2^{j-ell}<2R, $$
so any state updated in a fixed number of operations per prefix extension is available for all coordinates in $O(R)$ time using a stack rather than copying the whole prefix. A tensor monomial, mapping each digit $a_j$ to $pi_j(a_j)$ and multiplying by $c_j(a_j)$, extends the original and permuted mixed-radix addresses by multiply-and-add and a running coefficient by $c_j(a_j)$, then moves and scales one entry at a leaf; this is $O(R)$ including the scalar products.

A layer of disjoint copies of $C$ on an axis partitions its coordinates into ordered pairs and unused singletons. Order the blocks arbitrarily; for a selected block on axis $j$ let $q_jin{1,2}$ be its size and $p_j$ the number of coordinates in preceding blocks, and let $t_jin{0,...,q_j-1}$ be the position inside the block, all recorded once in the compiled layer. A sector picks one block per axis and has width $Q=prod_j q_j=2^k$, $k$ the number of chosen pairs; order sectors lexicographically by block choice and each sector internally by its $t_j$, and put $tau_j=prod_{i>j}r_i$.

Lemma (sector addressing). The start of the selected sector in the packed array is $S=sum_{j=1}^{ell}(prod_{i<j}q_i),p_j,tau_j$; the packing permutation, its inverse, and the lists of sector starts, widths, and pair counts are all generated in $O(R)$ operations once the local tables and suffix products are made.

Proof. Classify earlier sectors by the first axis at which the block choice differs: preceding chosen blocks contribute width $prod_{i<j}q_i$, earlier blocks at axis $j$ contribute $p_j$, and all later axes contribute $tau_j$, and these disjoint classes sum to the displayed start. During digit traversal keep a sector start $S$, a prefix width $Q$, a within-sector offset $o$, and an original address $I$, initially $0,1,0,0$; extending by the local digit $a_j$, whose table supplies $p_j,q_j,t_j$, updates
$$ S← S+Qp_jtau_j,quad Q← Qq_j,quad o← oq_j+t_j,quad I← Ir_j+a_j, $$
and the packed address of a leaf is $S+o$. To enumerate calls, traverse block choices instead of digits with the same update rule; axis $j$ has at most $r_j$ blocks, so block prefixes are at most digit prefixes at every depth and total $O(R)$, each leaf giving one contiguous sector with no scan through the axes.

Proposition (synchronized tensor transform). For integers $ellge1$ and $r_1,...,r_ellge2$, with the required specified roots given, put $R=prod_j r_j$ and $r_{max}=max_j r_j$. The map $⊗_j F_{r_j}$ is computable in
$$ O!(R(ell+1)^theta(1+log r_{max})^4)+poly(ell,r_{max},log(R+2)) $$
operations, including compilation, scalar preparation, and array work.

Proof. Run the local compiler on every axis and refine and pad the words to a common sequence of $O((1+log r_{max})^4)$ slot types, a monomial slot or a slot of disjoint copies of $C$ and singleton identities; padding a pair slot means using only singleton blocks on that axis. At each slot take the tensor product across axes; these multiply to the desired tensor because $(⊗_j A_j)(⊗_j B_j)=⊗_j(A_jB_j)$, and a local word is correct on arbitrary inputs, so another axis may rearrange coordinates in intermediate slots. Tensor monomials cost $O(R)$ by the prefix traversal. A tensor pair slot splits into the sectors of the addressing lemma, each width-$2^k$ sector carrying precisely $C^{ ⊗  k}$; the tensor-power bound gives $O(2^k(k+1)^theta)$ per sector, and since $kleell$ and the sector widths sum to $R$ the total is $O(R(ell+1)^theta)$, with $O(R)$ more for packing, unpacking, and the width-one sectors. The local compilers and integer preparation use polynomial work in $ell,r_{max},log(R+2)$, and all running scalar products were already charged in the traversal, so the polynomial term is local preparation only.

Now every length. To apply the synchronized transform at a requested $n$, choose a working length $L$ with $2nle L<4n$: the lower bound makes the chirp convolution correct and the upper bound keeps the array linear in $n$. The axes are a product of small odd primes, which must be polynomial in $log n$ so that local compilation stays polylogarithmic, and one power of two that fills the gap to $2n$.

Lemma (prime axes). Let $r_1<r_2<...$ be the odd primes and $R_ell=prod_{j=1}^{ell}r_j$, $R_0=1$. Then $r_j=O((j+1)^2)$ and $log R_ell=Θ(elllog(ell+2))$; for the largest $ell$ with $R_ellle2n$,
$$ ell=Θ!((log n)/(loglog n)),qquad logell=Θ(loglog n), $$
and these primes and this $ell$ are found in polynomial work in $log(n+2)$ on $O(log(n+2))$-bit integers.

Proof. With $pi(t)$ the count of primes at most $t$, for integer $uge1$,
$$ (4^u)/(2u+1)lebinom{2u}{u}le(2u)^{pi(2u)}. $$
The first bound holds because the central binomial coefficient is the largest; in the factorial formula the exponent of a prime $p$ in $binom{2u}{u}$ is $sum_{age1}(lfloor 2u/p^arfloor-2lfloor u/p^arfloor)lelfloorlog_p(2u)rfloor$, whose prime-power contribution is at most $2u$, giving the second. Taking $u=(j+1)^2$ gives $pi(2u)>j+1$ for all large $j$, so the $j$-th odd prime is $O((j+1)^2)$, and the upper bound on $log R_ell$ follows by summation. For the lower bound $r_jge j+2$, so $R_ellge(ell+2)!/2$ contributes a constant times $elllogell$ to the logarithm. Maximality gives $R_ellle2n<R_ell r_{ell+1}$, and the extra factor $r_{ell+1}$ contributes $O(log(ell+2))$, so $log n=Θ(elllogell)$ and the displayed $ell$ follows. Primes up to the first unused one are bounded by a fixed polynomial in $ell+1=O(log(n+2))$, testing divisors is polynomial work in $log(n+2)$, and maintaining the product up to the first value exceeding $2n$ keeps everything at $O(log(n+2))$ bits.

Choose this $ell$ and let $ege0$ be least with $L=R_ell 2^ege2n$, so $2nle L<4n$, $2^e<2r_{ell+1}$, and $e=O(log(ell+2))$; a bounded range is handled directly, so asymptotics may assume $ellge1$. For pairwise coprime factors $q$ of $L$, namely the odd primes and the factor $2^e$ when nontrivial, choose $d_q$ with $(L/q)d_qequiv1pmod q$ and $E_q=(L/q)d_q$. The $E_q$ are orthogonal idempotents modulo $L$, and the Chinese remainder indexing $(j_q)_qmapstosum_q E_qj_qpmod L$ is a bijection with
$$ w_L^{jk}=prod_q w_q^{d_q j_q k_q}, $$
so, after input and output permutations, $F_L$ is the tensor product of the $F_q$, the local output permutation multiplying a digit by the unit $d_q$ modulo $q$. Good's coprime factorization is the source of this reduction, and Cooley, Lewis, and Welch make explicit that no twiddle factor enters:

The permutations cost $O(L)$ array operations through the prefix traversal, with the inverses $d_q$ and local tables computed in time polynomial in $log n$. Apply the synchronized tensor transform on the odd-prime axes with each digit of the power-of-two axis fixed in turn, then a usual radix-two recursion on the fibers of that axis; the former costs $O(L(ell+1)^theta(1+log(ell+2))^4)+poly(log(n+2))$ since $r_ell=O((ell+1)^2)$, the latter $O(L(1+e))$, and $F_L^{-1}=L^{-1}J F_L$ with $J$ negating indices uses the geometric root sum $F_L^2=LJ$. $$ O!(L[(ell+1)^theta(1+log(ell+2))^4+1+e])+poly(log(n+2))+O(1). $$

Put $eta=w_{2n}$. For $0le j,k<n$,
$$ w_n^{jk}=eta^{k^2}eta^{-(k-j)^2}eta^{j^2}. $$
Prepare a length-$L$ vector with value $eta^{j^2}x_j$ at $0le j<n$ and zero elsewhere, and a fixed length-$L$ vector with value $eta^{-u^2}$ at the residues represented by $-(n-1)le ule n-1$ and zero elsewhere, distinct because $Lge2n$. The cyclic convolution of the two vectors gives, at each $0le k<n$, the sum in the chirp before multiplication by $eta^{k^2}$. Convolution proceeds by transforming both vectors, multiplying pointwise, and inverting; the transform of the fixed vector is part of scalar preparation and is charged as one full transform, while its pointwise use multiplies only variable data by prepared scalars. Chirp tables and zero-padded vectors take $O(L)=O(n)$ work, since successive chirp ratios form a geometric progression, so there are only three transforms.

The single supplied root is now fixed. With $L$ as chosen, set
$$ D_*=(2n),L,2^{lceillog_2(16L)rceil}. $$
The chirp root has order $2n$, the working factor roots have orders dividing $L$, and the local compiler needs power-of-two roots of orders at most $8r$ for a local width $rle L$, each dividing the last factor; the fixed kernel uses only $i$ and rationals. The main estimate follows. All maps are exact and deterministic, the local compiler and the finite network have explicit finite construction procedures, and on the input the algorithm is linear including the fixed-operand chirp. Since $e=O(log(ell+2))$ and $L=Θ(n)$, and $theta>0$,

A general route from a finite tensor saving to a uniform Fourier algorithm separates how usable coefficients are obtained from an existence theorem over the complex numbers from how effective small circuits with no useful compilation bound are used. The explicit network already gives the stronger quantitative conclusion; the route is the certificate interface that stands behind it.

The certificate is a pair of finite words for one invertible nonmonomial matrix $AinGL_q$ with integers $q,bge2$. The first word acts on exactly $q^b$ coordinates as $A^{ ⊗  b}$ with $g<bq^{b-1}$ forward calls to $A$ interleaved with invertible monomials, a call acting on an ordered $q$-tuple and fixing the rest; the second acts on $q$ coordinates as the shear $U$ that adds the second coordinate to the first, using a positive number of forward calls to the same $A$ and monomials.

Lemma (local certificate). For the kernel $C$ there are integers $bge2$ and $gge0$ and a word on exactly $2^b$ coordinates implementing $C^{ ⊗  b}$ with $g<b2^{b-1}$ forward calls to $C$ and invertible monomials; and on two coordinates the shear $U$ has a word with exactly three forward calls to the same $C$ and invertible diagonal factors.

Proof. Run the finite interface and the padding on $W_*=2^{71}$ arrays of length $2^{mf}$; the $S=W_*m-Delta$ directional steps need exactly $S f 2^{mf-1}$ forward $C$-calls when the tensor factors are applied separately, an inverse step adding only translations, and each scalar row is a composition of shears between existing distinct roles with nonzero rational coefficients, so the pointwise work is $H2^{mf}$ forward calls for a fixed integer $H$. $$ gle(Sf+71W_*+2H)2^{mf-1}=b2^{b-1}-(Delta f-2H)2^{mf-1}<b2^{b-1} $$
for any integer $f>2H/Delta$. Since $det C=i$ and both coefficients are nonzero, $C$ is invertible and nonmonomial, and the nonzero-shear identity with $t=1$ supplies the three-call word for $U$.

Lemma (effective extraction). Given finitely many polynomials in $a$ variables over the rationals, a deterministic rational algorithm either certifies that the ideal is the unit ideal or produces a nonzero finite-dimensional commutative unital rational algebra $E$ with a rational multiplication table and elements $xi_1,...,xi_ain E$ with every $f_i(xi)=0$; a complex solution forces the second outcome, and required units are imposed by adding inverse-witness equations.

Proof. The triangular substitution below is the standard proof of Noether normalization, and the obstruction argument is the determinant form of Nakayama's lemma (Stacks Project tags 051M, 051N, 00OX, and 00DV). Keep a finite generating list of the ideal $I=(f_i)$ and an integer $m$ initially equal to $a$, with witnesses for derived members. After invertible polynomial changes, maintain for each $j>m$ a member of $I$ monic in $x_j$ over the earlier variables, so $S=ℚ[x_1,...,x_a]/I$ is finite over $R=ℚ[x_1,...,x_m]$. The fiber $S/(x_1,...,x_m)S$ is computed by setting the first $m$ variables to zero and reducing monically in a rational basis; if it is nonzero that is the answer, and if $m=0$ and it is zero then $S=0$. If the fiber vanishes and $m>0$, localizing at the maximal ideal and applying the determinant form of Nakayama shows $S_{mathfrak m}frakm=mS_{mathfrak m}frakm$ forces $det(Id-M)$ a unit and nonzero, a contradiction unless $Icap Rne0$; a nonzero member $hin Icap R$ is found by searching increasing degree cutoffs with rational linear algebra. The triangular monic substitution $x_i=y_i+y_m^{(d+1)^i}$, $x_m=y_m$ (the earlier variables, with $d=deg h$) weights monomials so that the leading power of $y_m$ in the transformed $h$ has a unique monomial source and a nonzero rational coefficient, giving a monic relation in $y_m$ after dividing; the earlier relations stay monic, so $m$ decreases and the process terminates in at most $a$ steps of finite rational arithmetic. An inverse-witness equation $uv=1$ forces $u$ a unit in every output algebra.

Remark (faithful simulation). If $d=dim_{ℚ}E$, the algebra $E ⊗ _{ℚ}ℂ$ is the space $ℂ^d$ with the computed multiplication table, and $zmapsto z1_E$ is injective. Addition is coordinatewise and multiplication by a prepared algebra element is a $dtimes d$ complex matrix, so each linear operation costs $O(d^2)$ ordinary scalar operations. When a circuit acts as $F_r$ times the algebra identity, embedding inputs as multiples of $1_E$ and reading the first coordinate of each output is a faithful constant-factor simulation, needing neither a complex embedding nor extraction of algebraic roots; the integer $d$ is fixed before the input length is known.

The fixed identities suffice for an effective circuit printer. Put $Q=q^b$, $j=lfloor k/brfloor$, and tensor the first certificate word across $j$ groups; every call slot decomposes, after reindexing, as
$$ (A ⊕  I_{Q-q})^{ ⊗  j}cong⊕_{r=0}^{j}(A^{ ⊗  r})^{ ⊕ binom{j}{r}(Q-q)^{j-r}}, $$
and recursion on these smaller tensor orders plus direct calls on the fewer than $b$ remaining axes gives, writing $f(k)$ for the construction size divided by $q^k$, a normalized cost $f(k)le C_0+g,𝔼,f(K)$ with $K$ binomial on $lfloor k/brfloor$ trials and success probability $q/Q$ (the sector widths weight the recurrence by dimension). Since $gq/(bQ)<1$, choosing $0<alpha<1$ with $g(q/(bQ))^alpha<1$ and using concavity and $𝔼(K+1)^alphale(1+kq/(bQ))^alpha$ gives $f(k)=O((k+1)^alpha)$.

For successive $tge1$ let $r_1<...<r_t$ be the first $t$ primes above $2q$ and set $m_t=prod_{i=1}^{t}r_i$, $ell_t=m_t2^{lceillog_2(16r_t)rceil}$, $R_t=ℚ[z]/(Phi_{ell_t}(z))$. Cyclotomic polynomials are computed from $z^s-1=prod_{dmid s}Phi_d(z)$, arithmetic is polynomial division and the Euclidean algorithm, a polynomial is a unit modulo $Phi_s$ exactly when its gcd with $Phi_s$ is one, and identities in $R_t$ are checked at the primitive roots; roots of orders dividing $ell_t$ are compatible powers of $z$. Carry the constructions over $E ⊗ _{ℚ}R_t$ with Fourier coefficients in the central factor, use the Fourier layer construction on each width $r_i$ (its rational formulas hold in $R_t$, all denominators nonzero at every primitive specialization and every convolution root order dividing the power of two), and realize a shear of unit coefficient $v$ as the conjugate of $U$ by $diag(v,1,...,1)$, splitting a general coefficient $c$ as $d+(c-d)$ with a positive integer $d$ chosen until $c-d$ is a unit. Partition the disjoint pairs of an axis into at most $q$ batches of at most $lfloor r_i/qrfloor$ pairs, possible because $r_i>2q$, with spectators restored before the next batch. Synchronizing these fixed patterns across axes with identities in idle positions, each monomial slot having linear size and every other slot a direct sum of $A^{ ⊗  k}$ with $kle t$ and total width $m_t$, the amplification bound gives a circuit over $E ⊗ _{ℚ}ℂ$ of size
$$ s_t=O!(m_t(t+1)^alpha(1+log^4(t+2)))=o(m_tlog m_t), $$
because $r_t=O((t+1)^2)$ and $log m_tge tlog2$. Specializing $zmapsto w_{ell_t}$ and expanding only the fixed algebra $E$ yields a fully explicit complex circuit together with a fully expanded scalar preparation program, with every coefficient a rational polynomial evaluated at $w_{ell_t}$ and written as a finite Horner list.

Theorem (bounded-printer uniformization). Suppose a fixed bit machine prints complete records $(m_t,C_t)$ with strictly increasing $m_tto∞$, where $C_t$ computes $F_{m_t}$ by $s_t=o(m_tlog m_t)$ scalar linear gates, and each record explicitly lists its gates, output indices, and a scalar preparation program whose instructions use rationals, valid nonzero divisors, and canonical roots $w_a$ of positive integer orders written in binary. Then one deterministic algorithm computes every $F_n$ in $o(nlog n)$ time, including preparation and indexing, and a single specified root of order below $8n^3$ suffices.

Proof. Run the normalized printer from the start for $B=lfloorlog_2 nrfloor$ bit steps and take the last completed record with $2le m_tlesqrt B$, or the ordinary radix-two circuit if there is none; every fixed record eventually finishes with a width inside the bound, so the selected width tends to infinity and $s/(mlog m)to0$. The visible description has at most $B$ bits; decode the explicit lists, evaluate the preparation program once (cost $B^{O(1)}$), and then run its $m+s$ gates on each new input in $O(m+s)$ time without re-expansion. Put $X=2n-1$, let $m^H$ be the largest power of $m$ not exceeding $X$, and set $p=lceil X/m^Hrceil$, $P=m^Hp$, so $1le ple m$ and $Xle P<2X<4n$. The mixed-radix recursion uses $Hlelog(2n)/log m$ copies of radix $m$ and one radix $p$, the latter computed directly; at a split $ab$ using input index $bj_a+j_b$ and output index $k_a+ak_b$ the identity $(bj_a+j_b)(k_a+ak_b)equiv bj_ak_a+aj_bk_b+j_bk_apmod{ab}$ gives the two smaller transforms and the intervening twiddle, and copying, tabulating, and enumerating cost linear work per level, so one transform costs
$$ O!(P[1+p+H(1+s/m)])+B^{O(1)}. $$
Finally reduce $F_n$ to a convolution of length $P$: store $eta^{j^2}x_j$ with $eta=w_{2n}$ for $0le j<n$, use the fixed kernel $eta^{-h^2}$ at the residues of $-(n-1)le hle n-1$ (distinct since $Pge2n-1$), and multiply output $k$ by $eta^{k^2}$, which reproduces $F_nx$ through $k^2+j^2-(k-j)^2=2kj$; a constant number of transforms plus linear work suffices. For the root, let $R$ be the product of the listed root orders, with total binary length at most $B$, so $Rle2^Ble n$, and the single $w_D$ with $D=2nPR<8n^3$ supplies every listed root and the two outer roots by integer powering. Since $p=O(1+√(log n))$, dividing the arithmetic term by $nlog n$ gives
$$ O!((1+p)/(log n)+(1)/(log m)+(s)/(mlog m))=o(1), $$
and the polynomial logarithmic preparation term is also $o(nlog n)$, so the transform at every length is $o(nlog n)$, with every choice independent of the input values.
