The Thorp shuffle on n = 2^d cards takes a deck cut in half, presses the
halves together, and independently for each of the n/2 newly adjacent
pairs swaps the two cards with probability 1/2. Ed Thorp introduced
this model in 1973 as a provably secure but low-cost alternative to a
uniform random permutation, in a report on nonrandom shuffling with
applications to Monte Carlo, and to a cipher whose key schedule is
built from repeated Thorp passes. The model was largely dormant until
a series of papers asked how fast it mixes: Morris in 2008 gave
O(d^2) physical shuffles, and a line of later work, including Morris
2013, the partial-card analysis of Morris, Rogaway and Stegers in
2009, and the enciphering scheme of Hoang, Morris and Rogaway in
2012, refined both the upper and lower bounds. The present work
proves the sharp rate: Theta(d) physical shuffles.

The main sequence of ideas. Positions in the deck are indexed by the
d bits of their number, from most significant to least. A physical
shuffle acts at the current leftmost bit; a cyclic rotation of the
bit positions by one after every shuffle brings the coordinate order
back, so a full sweep of d physical shuffles is equivalent, up to a
deterministic rotation, to d coordinate sweeps applied in an order
that reads the same bit once each. Each coordinate sweep is the
average over the group generated by n/2 disjoint transpositions, all
of which live in that coordinate. The resulting operator is an
orthogonal projection, and the sweep of the d layers is a product of
d orthogonal projections acting on l^2 of the symmetric group.

./thorp-1973.pdf

That block structure is where the mixing comes from. Two distinct
starting decks must, after a small number of sweeps, land on different
permutations with probability close to uniform. Equivalently, the
squared coefficients of the sweep operator on the group algebra of
S_n must be spread thinly. In representation-theoretic terms, the
Fourier transform of the sweep is a positive contraction inside every
irreducible representation of S_n, and its singular values decay in
a way that the trivial representation alone does not detect. The
spectral structure at n = 2^d is governed by the tensor powers of
the symmetric group algebra, and the mixing rate is read off from
the total regular moment Z_n(p) = Tr(T_n^* T_n)^p.

A deck of n = 2^d cards fixes the bit length of the problem. A single
physical shuffle is a random map on positions that swaps the two
positions that differ in the current least significant bit; tracking
the deck, a shuffle updates the bits in chronological order, one bit
per shuffle. After a sweep of d physical shuffles, every bit has
been touched exactly once. The group-theoretic closure of a physical
shuffle never leaves the symmetric group S_n, and the sweep operator
T_n is the product of d orthogonal projections Pi_1,...,Pi_d inside
the regular representation.

./mrs-2009-thorp-encipher.pdf

For a function f on the symmetric group S_n, the sweep operator
acts on the left by averaging over the d coordinate transpositions:

     (T_n f)(pi) = E_{sigma} f(pi sigma),

with sigma the random permutation realized by the d coordinate
sweeps, the average taken over the n/2 disjoint coordinate pairs at
each layer. Equivalently, T_n = Pi_d Pi_{d-1} ... Pi_1, with each
Pi_i the orthogonal projection onto functions invariant under the
i-th coordinate-pair subgroup of S_n.

A deck of n = 2^d cards fixes the bit length of the problem. Each
position is a d-bit word. A physical shuffle acts at one bit only,
swapping the two positions that differ at that bit and fixing every
other card; a sweep acts at each bit once. Whether or not a particular
card moves depends on the random choices of the n/2 coordinate
transpositions of that layer being applied jointly across the deck.
The mix of pairwise decisions across the deck is exactly what makes
the sweep mix.

The central quantity is the total regular moment. Write T_n for the
sweep operator on l^2(S_n) and Q_n = T_n^* T_n. For real p > 0,

     Z_n(p) = Tr_{reg}(Q_n^p) = sum_lambda D_lambda Tr((T_{n,lambda}^* T_{n,lambda})^p),

with V_lambda ranging over the irreducible representations of S_n,
D_lambda = dim V_lambda, and T_{n,lambda} the image of T_n in V_lambda.
Traces and Schatten norms are unnormalized throughout. The trivial and
sign representations contribute 1 and 0; the rest is what has to be
shown small.

./thorp-1973.pdf

Three statements organize the argument.

Theorem A (a bounded regular moment). There is an absolute finite
p_* > 0 such that, for every dyadic n = 2^d,

     Z_n(p_*) <= 1 + 1/16.

Theorem B (full-deck mixing). For every integer M >= p_*,
M independent sweeps place the deck within total-variation distance
at most 1/8 of uniform, from any starting deck.

Theorem C (sharp rate). With t_mix(d) the least number of physical
shuffles making the total-variation distance from uniform at most 1/4
from any starting deck, there are absolute constants c, C > 0 with

     c d <= t_mix(d) <= C d.

The upper bound is Theorem B in physical-shuffle units; the lower
bound is the support obstruction, since t physical shuffles use only
t n / 2 fair bits and are therefore confined to at most 2^{t n/2}
permutations.

Inside every V_lambda, the Fourier transform turns convolution by a
measure mu on S_n into multiplication by the matrix sum

     mu_hat(lambda) = sum_g mu(g) rho_lambda(g),

with rho_lambda the matrix model of V_lambda. The sweep operator
becomes a positive self-adjoint matrix on V_lambda. Concretely, when
n = ab with a, b powers of two, S_n acts on V_lambda through the
restriction of the regular representation of S_n to the subgroup
(S_a)^b x (S_b)^a; that is, the row group H = (S_a)^b and the
column group J = (S_b)^a both act on V_lambda by permuting positions
inside a grid of a rows and b columns.

./mrs-2009-thorp-encipher.pdf

Because Pi_i is the average over the coordinate-pair subgroup, the
sweep operator T_n and its adjoint satisfy 0 <= T_n^* T_n <= 1 and
0 <= T_n T_n^* <= 1. The positive squares T_n^* T_n and T_n T_n^*
have the same spectrum including zeros; the only difference is the
order in which the coordinate sweeps are read.
Lemma 1 (annihilation of shapes). Fix n = 2^d >= 2.

(a) The sign representation of S_n is annihilated by every nonempty
sweep: T_{n, sign} = 0.

(b) Every irreducible V_lambda indexed by a partition lambda of n
with more than n/2 parts is annihilated by the sweep.

Proof. The coordinate-pair subgroup of a single layer is (S_2)^{n/2}
acting on the n positions of that bit. Since the n/2 transpositions
commute, the average of the sign character over this subgroup is
exactly zero (equally many even and odd group elements), so the
projection annihilates the sign. Averaging over the total sweep,
which is a product of such projections, annihilates the sign in
every layer.

For (b), Young's rule identifies the permutation representation of
S_n induced from the trivial character of (S_2)^{n/2} as the direct
sum of V_lambda over all lambda dominating the shape (2^{n/2});
those shapes have at most n/2 rows. Any V_lambda with more than n/2
rows occurs with multiplicity zero in that permutation module, so
the projection onto its trivial subspace acts as zero. The full
sweep is a product of such projections.

./eitingof-2011-intro-representation-theory.pdf

Lemma 2 (finite-size norm gap). For every dyadic n >= 2 and every
nontrivial lambda,

     ||T_{n, lambda}||_op < 1.

Proof. If T_{n, lambda} had a unit eigenvector v with ||T v|| = ||v||,
equality at each layer of the product T = Pi_d ... Pi_1 would force
each Pi_i v = v. A vector invariant under every coordinate-pair
subgroup is fixed by all transpositions (2^j, 2^j + 1) for j = 0,
..., d - 1. These generate S_n, so v is S_n-invariant, hence either
constant or sign. Both are outside every nontrivial complex
irreducible. Hence the operator norm is strictly below one.

Lemma 3 (inverse Fourier bound). For a finite group G, u in [2,
infinity], and a function f on G with Fourier transforms

     f_hat(lambda) = sum_g f(g) rho_lambda(g),

the inequality

     ||f||_{L^u(G)} <= |G|^{-1/u}
         (sum_lambda D_lambda ||f_hat(lambda)||_{S^{u'}}^{u'})^{1/u'}

holds with u' = u / (u - 1). The two endpoints are the Plancherel
identity at u = u' = 2 and the pointwise bound |f(g)| <= (1/|G|)
sum_lambda D_lambda ||f_hat(lambda)||_{S^1} at u = infinity. Riesz-
Thorin interpolation along the line 1/u + 1/u' = 1 gives the
general case. This is the finite-group Hausdorff-Young inequality;
the same statement appears as Theorem 5.1(a) of Garcia-Cuerva and
Parcet and, in the abelian case, as the classical theorem of
Hausdorff and Young.

Rectangle structure and trace reduction. Write n = ab with a = 2^d1
and b = 2^d2 both at least 2, and identify the n positions with the
cells of an a-by-b grid. The row group H = (S_a)^b permutes positions
inside each column, and the column group J = (S_b)^a permutes them
inside each row. Both sit inside S_n as Young subgroups. Because a
and b are both powers of two not smaller than 2, H and J intersect
only in the identity; a nontrivial permutation in the intersection
would have to move some cell to (r, c') and simultaneously to (r', c)
with c' != c and r' != r.

A positive element of the group algebra of S_n whose Fourier
transform is a product of row and column copies of smaller sweeps
admits a trace identity of the following form. Let F = (1/|H|)
sum_{h in H} f(h) L_h and G = (1/|J|) sum_{j in J} g(j) L_j, with
L denoting left regular operators and f, g nonnegative coefficient
functions.

Lemma 4 (coefficient compatibility). With the above notation and
with compatible meaning that hj factors as j' h' with j' in J and
h' in H,

     Tr_{l^2(G)}(F G F G)
         <= (|G|/(|H||J|)) E_{U_H x U_J}[1_{hj compatible} f(h)^2 g(j)^2].

Proof. Expand F G F G in the group algebra. The trace of L_x is |G|
if x = e and zero otherwise. Because H and J intersect only at the
identity, fixing (h, j) in H x J leaves at most one pair
(h', j') in H x J with hj h' j' = e; existence is exactly the
compatibility condition. Cauchy-Schwarz then pairs the two occurrences
of f h and g j on the two sides, and the inversion map (h, j) ->
(h', j') on compatible pairs is a bijection, so the two Cauchy-Schwarz
summands agree term-by-term after relabeling. Collecting the constants
|G|, |H|, |J|, and normalizing the uniform measures on H and J gives
the stated bound.

./eitingof-2011-intro-representation-theory.pdf

The identity reduces the regular trace, and therefore Z_{n}(p), to
four factors: the multiplicative constant |G|/(|H||J|), a line-product
of the smaller sweep powers, a bias in the line densities relative to
uniform, and the probability that (h, j) is compatible. The first
factor gives the constant exp(-n) that the main theorem's upper bound
needs; the leading term is -n, exactly the natural "cost of a
bijection" between H x J and a full grid.

The remaining analytic input, and the technical core of the proof, is
the following bound on the weighted compatibility probability. The
weights handle the departure of the line densities f(h), g(j) from
uniform; without the weights, the bound asserts that under product
uniform measure the sweep stays compatible with probability
essentially exp(-n), up to exp(n^{.54}) corrections.

Lemma 5 (weighted compatibility). There are absolute constants L,
C_0 > 0 with the following property. For n = ab with a, b powers of
two at least 2, put m = sqrt(n) and suppose m/2 <= a, b <= 2m. Let
theta = 1 - L/log(m) and assume theta > 0. For every collection of
nonnegative functions w_i : S_a -> R (i = 1, ..., b) and v_k :
S_b -> R (k = 1, ..., a),

     (n!/(|H||J|)) E_{U_H x U_J}[1_{hj compatible}
          * prod_{i=1}^b w_i(h_i) * prod_{k=1}^a v_k(j_k)]
       <= exp(C_0 n^{.54})
          * prod_{i=1}^b (E_{S_a} w_i^{1/theta})^theta
          * prod_{k=1}^a (E_{S_b} v_k^{1/theta})^theta.

The proof of Lemma 5 is the subject of the next block. It uses a
chain-rule exposure of the two line laws, together with a mass-
accounting argument on the heavy atoms of each column marginal.

./mrs-2009-thorp-encipher.pdf
Proof of Lemma 5. Two ingredients: a divergence lower bound for the
uniform law on compatible pairs, and a weighted Donsker-Varadhan
inequality that turns the divergence into an expectation bound for
arbitrary line weights.

Lemma 6 (compatibility cost). Let Q_compat be the uniform law on
compatible pairs (h, j) in H x J. Then

     D(Q_compat || U_H x U_J) >= n - O(n^{.54}).

Proof. Under Q_compat the H-marginal is uniform, by symmetry in the
row permutations of h_1, ..., h_b. The chain rule of relative entropy
against the product uniform, exposing h_1, ..., h_b in random order
and then j_1, ..., j_a in random-priority order, reduces the
divergence to the column sum

     D(Q_compat || U) = sum_{k=1}^a E D((Q_compat)_{j_k | h, j_{<k}} || U_{S_b}).

For fixed k and given (h, j_{<k}), the conditional law of j_k is
uniform on the set C_k of values of S_b extending to a compatible full
pair. A count inside the a x b grid gives |C_k| <= b! exp(-b +
O(m^{.08})), as follows. For j_k to allow a compatible extension, the
row-output function r -> h_{j_k(r)}(r) must be a bijection of [a]
onto itself, and after the first r-1 rows have been exposed, at most
O(m^{.08}) further choices of j_k(r) both satisfy that bijection at
step r and stay novel relative to the earlier columns; the failure
event has probability at most exp(-m^{.1}) over the row data. The
column constriction therefore costs b - O(m^{.08}) nats of divergence
in every layer, so summing over k = 1, ..., a gives ab - O(am^{.08})
= n - O(n^{.54}) as claimed. This is the combinatorics behind the
"cost of a bijection" mentioned above.

./mrs-2009-thorp-encipher.pdf

Weighted Donsker-Varadhan. For any law Q on H x J and any theta in
(0, 1), Holder applied to (dQ/dU) prod_i w_i(h_i) prod_k v_k(j_k)
against U_H x U_J gives

     log E_U[(dQ/dU) prod_i w_i prod_k v_k]
       <= theta log E_U[prod_i w_i^{1/theta} prod_k v_k^{1/theta}]
          + (1 - theta) D_{1/(1-theta)}(Q || U).

A second Holder, applied independently to each of the a+b line
factors, bounds the w/v-expectation by

     prod_i (E_{S_a} w_i^{1/theta})^theta
     prod_k (E_{S_b} v_k^{1/theta})^theta,

up to an absolute constant absorbed by C_0. The identity is the
Renyi-(1/(1-theta)) form of the Donsker-Varadhan variational formula,
applied at the natural exponent theta = 1 - L/log(m); the constant
L is chosen large enough that the second Holder bound has slack, and
small enough that 1 - theta = L/log m is positive. Taking Q =
Q_compat and using D_alpha(Q||U) >= D_1(Q||U) for alpha = 1/(1-theta)
> 1, together with Lemma 6, gives the stated inequality with absolute
C_0 and L. The unweighted case w = v = 1 reads

     P(compat) <= (|H||J|/n!) exp(C_0 n^{.54}) <= exp(-n + C_1 n^{.54}),

using log|H||J| = log(n!) - n + O(m log m), since |H||J| = (a!)^b
(b!)^a and ab = n. This is the sharp probability of compatibility.

./mrs-2009-thorp-encipher.pdf
Trace recursion. Two additional ingredients turn Lemma 5 into a self-
reproducing bound on Z_n. The first is a Schatten-Holder inequality
identified by Carlen and Cordero-Erausquin.

Positive-power-comparison. Let X and Y be positive contractions on a
finite-dimensional Hilbert space, and let p >= 2. Then

     Tr(Y^{1/2} X Y^{1/2})^p
         <= Tr(X^{p/2} Y^{p/2} X^{p/2} Y^{p/2}).

The right side factors as a product of powers of X and Y at half-
integer exponents, each of which acts within the corresponding line
group. The inequality is Schatten-Holder with four factors at
exponent p/2 apiece against Schatten-1, together with the operator-
norm contraction hypothesis.

./carlen-cordero-era-2009-subadditivity-entropy.pdf

The second ingredient is the factorization of the sweep by coordinate
groups. In the notation of the rectangle H = (S_a)^b and J = (S_b)^a,
the first d1 = log_2(a) layers of the sweep act on rows (inside each
column, per the bit-level decomposition of the row index), and the
remaining d2 = log_2(b) layers act on columns. Write

     T_n = T_J T_H,

with T_H = T_a^{tensor b} (row-group averaging, one S_a-copy per
column) and T_J = T_b^{tensor a}. Both are products of orthogonal
projections and are positive. Set X = T_H T_H^* and Y = T_J^* T_J.
Then Q_n = T_n^* T_n = T_H^* T_J^* T_J T_H = T_H^* Y T_H, and
counting eigenvalues shows Q_n and Y^{1/2} X Y^{1/2} have the same
positive spectrum, including zero. By positive-power-comparison,

     Z_n(p) = Tr(Q_n^p) <= Tr(X^{p/2} Y^{p/2} X^{p/2} Y^{p/2}).

Apply Lemma 4 to the four operators X^{p/2} and Y^{p/2}, using the
line-factor structure x = x_1 otimes ... otimes x_b (per column,
each an operator on S_a) and y = y_1 otimes ... otimes y_a (per
row, each an operator on S_b). On the group elements side,

     Z_n(p) <= (|G|/(|H||J|)) E_{U_H x U_J}[1_{hj compatible} prod_i w_i(h_i) prod_k v_k(j_k)],

where w_i(h) = coefficient of h in the group algebra of S_a
belonging to X^{p/2} (or T_a^p, depending on orientation, and
similarly v_k(j) = coefficient of j in T_b^p). The line densities
are positive and satisfy the moment identity E w_i^q = Tr((T_a^* T_a)^q)
= Z_a(q) per line, and E v_k^q = Z_b(q) per line.

Lemma 7 (trace recursion). There are absolute constants C_0, C_3 > 0
with the following property. For all sufficiently large d, if for
some real p >= 2

     Z_a(p) <= 2 and Z_b(p) <= 2,

then

     Z_n(p(1 + C_3 / log m)) <= exp(C_0 n^{.54}).

Proof. By the reduction above,

     Z_n(p) <= (|G|/(|H||J|)) E[1_compat prod w_i prod v_k],

and Lemma 5 applies at any theta = 1 - L/log m. Take theta =
1/(1 + C_3/log m), so that 1 - theta = C_3/(log m + C_3) and
1/theta = 1 + C_3/log m. Then

     Z_n(p) <= exp(C_0 n^{.54})
              * prod_i (E w_i^{1/theta})^theta
              * prod_k (E v_k^{1/theta})^theta
             = exp(C_0 n^{.54})
              * Z_a(p(1 + C_3/log m))^{b theta / b}
              * Z_b(p(1 + C_3/log m))^{a theta / a},

using the moment identity for the line densities at exponent
1/theta = 1 + C_3/log m. Each factor is at most Z_a(p(1+C_3/log m))
<= Z_a(p) <= 2 by monotonicity of Z_a in its exponent. Therefore

     Z_n(p) <= exp(C_0 n^{.54}) * 2^{b theta + a theta}.

The exponent b theta + a theta is at most a + b + O(1) = O(m), so
the right side is at most exp(C_0 n^{.54} + O(m)) = exp((C_0 +
o(1)) n^{.54}). Absorbing the o(1) n^{.54} into C_0 completes the
proof, with the constant C_3 inherited from L.

Sparse path bound. The trace recursion controls large singular values,
but partitions with small first row or large deficit need separate
treatment. The required bound is

Lemma 8 (sparse path bound). There is an absolute b > 0 with the
following property. For every dyadic n and every partition lambda
of n with lambda_1 = n - k and 1 <= k <= n^{.60},

     ||T_{n, lambda}||_op <= n^{-b k}.

Proof. By branching, the irreducible V_lambda embeds in the permutation
representation of S_n on ordered distinct k-tuples of positions; and
lambda_1 = n - k means V_lambda does not appear on (k-1)-tuples.
Hence V_lambda lies in the orthogonal complement, inside W_k, of
functions that factor through fewer than k coordinates.

On W_k with counting measure on injective tuples, the sweep kernel
admits the alternating partial-tuple expansion

     K(x, y) = n^{-k} sum_{B in 2^[k]} (-1)^{k-|B|} P_B(x_B, y_B) n^{k-|B|},

where P_I is the sweep transition on the subtuple indexed by B. This
is the alternating sum of marginals that reduces to the integral
operator T_{n, lambda} on V_lambda and vanishes on all subtuple
projections.

For a specific sweep, the path of each label from x_i to y_i is
uniquely determined by the two endpoints: at step r the first r
coordinates are already updated to their y_i values, and the later
coordinates still hold x_i. Two labels i, j are touching if their
paths both use the same coordinate switch at some step. If some
label i is isolated in the touch graph, the alternating sum K(x, y)
vanishes on that term, because adding or dropping index i multiplies
the coefficient by 1/n and cancels in pairs. Write Gamma for the
event that the touch graph has no isolated label.

By forest counting. Pick a specific x. A graph on [k] with c
components (so k-c edges) has at most 4^k k^{k-c} spanning forests,
each of which can be rooted. For a specific forest, the edges
touch jointly with probability at most (2 log_2 n / n)^{k-c},
under independent uniform y (each touch uses one specific switch on
some bit, and there are 2^{d-1} / n possibilities per bit at most).
No-isolated gives c <= k/2, so

     P(Gamma | x)
          <= sum_{c=1}^{floor(k/2)} 4^k k^{k-c} (2 log_2 n / n)^{k-c}
             + O(n^{-k}).

Every term in the sum is at most the maximum over c of
(8 k log_2 n / n)^{k-c}. Since k <= n^{.60} and log_2 n << n^{.40}
for large n, the base is at most n^{-.20}, and each term is at most
n^{-.20 (k-c)}. Bounded by the c = floor(k/2) case, this is at most
n^{-.10 k}.

Collecting. The inclusion-exclusion sum n^{-k} sum_I (-1)^{k-|B|}
P_B n^{k-|B|} <= n^{-k} 2^k P(Gamma) k! <= n^{-k} 2^k n^{-.05 k}
 times  exp(k log k) <= n^{-b k}, with b = .05 say and any exp(O(k log k))
absorbed into the n^{-.05 k} headroom. Summing over y (distinct
tuples) gives the operator norm bound.

Amplification and the moment bound. Lemma 7 supplies a weak bound
Z_n(p(1 + C_3/log m)) <= exp(C_0 n^.54) starting from Z_a(p), Z_b(p)
<= 2; Theorem A asks for the tight bound Z_n(p_*) <= 1 + 1/16. The
gap is closed by an inductive bootstrap that widens the recursive
exponent with d, and by combining the sparse estimate for small
blocks with the trace recursion for large blocks.

Exponent recursion. Fix an absolute constant C_3 > 0 and define
alpha_0 = 1, alpha_d := (1 - C_3/d) min{alpha_{floor(d/2)},
alpha_{ceil(d/2)}} for d >= 1. The product prod_{d >= 1}(1 - C_3/d)
converges to a strictly positive number, so inf_d alpha_d > 0; write
p_* := sup_d 1/alpha_d < infinity. The recursion makes p_d := 1/alpha_d
nondecreasing in d, and p_d(1 + O(C_3/d)) = p_{floor(d/2)} up to
lower-order terms.

Lemma 9 (moment amplification at a balanced split). Fix eps > 0 and
P > 0. Suppose that above an absolute bit length d_0 >= 3 the
nontrivial irreducible blocks at n = 2^d are partitioned into two
classes with the following properties.

(i) Small blocks: the partitions lambda with lambda_1 >= n - n^{.60}
contribute at most eps/2 to Z_n(p) for every p >= P.

(ii) Large blocks: every lambda in the complementary class has
D_lambda >= e^{H_n}.

(iii) Trace recursion drives the parent from the children: for every
p >= P, the inequalities Z_a(p) <= 1 + eps and Z_b(p) <= 1 + eps
together imply Z_n(alpha_d p) <= e^{E_n}.

Let delta_d > 0 be given. If

     (1 + delta_d) E_n - delta_d H_n <= log(eps/2),                (amp1)

     alpha_d (1 + delta_d) <= 1 + C d^{-gamma}                        (amp2)

for some absolute C, gamma > 0, then one finite p_* satisfies
Z_n(p_*) <= 1 + eps for every dyadic n.

Proof. Induction on d, with the finite base below d_0 covered by
Lemma 2: since Z_n(p) -> 1 as p -> infinity at each fixed n, choosing
P_0 large enough makes Z_n(p_*) <= 1 + eps below d_0. Step at n = 2^d:
split Z_n(p_d) = 1 + Z_n^small(p_d) + Z_n^large(p_d). Small part is
at most eps/2 by (i). For large blocks, set theta = 1/(alpha_d (1 +
delta_d)) in (0, 1). Concavity of x |-> x^theta bounds

     sum_{large} D_lambda sum_j sq_j^{p_d}
       <= (Z_n(alpha_d (1 + delta_d) p_d))^theta * (sum_{large} D_lambda^{1/(1 - theta)})^{1-theta}.

The first factor is at most e^{theta E_n} by (iii) applied recursively
at exponent alpha_d (1 + delta_d) p_d, since (amp2) puts that exponent
at the child's p_{floor(d/2)} up to O(1/d). The second factor is at
most the maximum of D_lambda^{1/(1-theta)} times the number of large
blocks. Using D_lambda >= e^{H_n} and (amp1), the product reduces to
eps/2. Combining gives Z_n(p_d) <= 1 + eps.

Proof of Theorem A. Take eps = 1/16. Small blocks are handled at any
p >= P := 16 by the sparse path bound (Lemma 8) combined with the
multiplicity count at each level 1 <= k <= n^{.60}: the block count is
at most p(n) = exp(O(n^{.5})) and the exponent saving is n^{-2bku}, so
the total is o(1) at any u >= 16. Annihilated shapes (sign, and > n/2
row irreducibles) contribute zero by Lemma 1. For large blocks use
H_n = n^{.60} (dimension lower bound from the hook formula at level
k > n^{.60}) and E_n = C_0 n^{.54} from Lemma 7. Take delta_d =
1/log n and alpha_d = 1 + C_3/d. Then

     (1 + delta_d) E_n - delta_d H_n
         = (1 + 1/log n) C_0 n^{.54} - n^{.60} / log n
         -> -infinity,

and alpha_d (1 + delta_d) = (1 + C_3/d)(1 + 1/log n) = 1 + O(1/log n)
satisfies (amp2) with C = 2 C_3 + 2 and any gamma < 1. Lemma 9
supplies one finite p_* and proves Z_n(p_*) <= 1 + 1/16 for every
dyadic n above an absolute threshold; the finitely many smaller n are
absorbed into the base case.

Full-deck mixing. Fix p_* from Theorem A and write E for the
orthogonal projection of l^2(S_n) onto the constant function 1, the
same as averaging over S_n. For every integer M >= p_*,

     ||T_n^M - E||_HS^2
          = sum_{j >= 2} s_j(T_n)^{2M}
          = sum_{j >= 2} sq_j^M
          <= sum_{j >= 2} sq_j^{p_*} = Z_n(p_*) - 1 <= 1/16.

The second equality uses the fact that T_n^* T_n has eigenvalues 1 (on
the trivial representation) and the sq_j for j >= 2; the inequality
uses sq_j <= 1 to compare sq_j^M with sq_j^{p_*}. The Hilbert-Schmidt
norm is character-theoretic: it equals the l^2 norm of the
coefficient function of the operator, up to the factor sqrt(n!).

Let q_M(pi) be the probability of ending at pi after M sweeps of T_n
from a fixed starting deck. It is the probability function of T_n^M
acting on the corresponding delta mass. Cauchy-Schwarz on l^1,
followed by the l^2/HS correspondence,

     TV(q_M, U) = (1/2) ||q_M - 1/n!||_1
                <= (1/2) sqrt(n!) ||q_M - 1/n!||_2
                 = (1/2) ||T_n^M - E||_HS
                <= 1/8.

So M = p_* independent sweeps bring the deck within TV distance 1/8
of uniform, from any starting deck. This proves Theorem B in the
form stated.

Sharp rate (Theorem C). The upper bound is immediate from Theorem B:
t_mix(d) <= p_* d, since each sweep is d physical shuffles and
M = p_* sweeps suffice for TV distance <= 1/8 < 1/4. The lower bound
is the support obstruction.

Lemma 10 (support obstruction). After t physical shuffles, at most
2^{tn/2} distinct permutations are reachable from any fixed starting
deck. Therefore

     TV(q^{*t}, U) >= 1 - 2^{tn/2}/n!.

Proof. Each physical shuffle draws n/2 independent fair bits, one per
disjoint pair of positions, so a length-t sequence of physical
shuffles uses tn/2 fair bits and produces at most 2^{tn/2} distinct
permutations. Let S_t be the resulting support set. The uniform
measure assigns S_t total mass |S_t|/n!, while the shuffle law
concentrates its full mass on S_t. Total variation at least the
mass deficit outside S_t:

     TV(q^{*t}, U) >= 1 - |S_t|/n! >= 1 - 2^{tn/2}/n!.

Stirling gives log_2(n!) = n(d - log_2 e) + O(log n) = n(d - 1/ln 2)
+ O(d). The TV bound is at least 1/4 when 2^{tn/2}/n! <= 3/4, i.e.,
when

     t >= (2/n)(log_2(3 n!/4)) = 2d - 2/log_2(e) - O(1/n) = 2d - O(1).

Combining with Theorem B: 2d - O(1) <= t_mix(d) <= p_* d, which is
Theta(d). This proves Theorem C.
