A stack of $n=2^d$ cards is indexed by the bit strings of length $d$ in $V=mathbb F_2^d$,bbF_2^d$, ordered lexicographically. One physical shuffle pairs every card $x$ with $x+e_1$, the string obtained by flipping the first coordinate, and independently for each pair chooses one of the two orders with a fair coin. Writing $R(x_1,...,x_d)=(x_2,...,x_d,x_1)$ for the cyclic rotation, a physical shuffle is $RS$ where $S$ applies the chosen swaps in the pairs ${x,x+e_1}$. Repeating $RS$ alone would only randomize the first few coordinates, so the shuffle alternates between the swap and the rotation. In the rotating frame $X_t=R^{-t}Y_t$, where $Y_t$ is the physical permutation after $t$ shuffles, the dynamics become
$$ X_{t+1} = R^{-t}S_{t+1}R^t X_t . $$
A layer is one step of $X$, acting by fair independent switches along a single coordinate matching of the cube. A sweep consists of $d$ consecutive layers, one in each coordinate, so that after $d$ physical shuffles the rotating and physical frames coincide.

Thorp's shuffle is used as the basis of deterministic enciphering on small domains by Morris, Rogaway and Stegers, and in the swap-or-not scheme of Hoang, Morris and Rogaway. Its mixing properties were first bounded by Morris, who obtained $O(d^{44})$ physical shuffles for power-of-two decks; the intermediate $O(d^{29})$ estimate of Montenegro and Tetali; and the subsequent $O(d^3)$ bound of Morris for $n=2^d$. For every even deck size, Morris proved $O((log n)^4)$. Czumaj and Vocking later showed that endpoint positions of any prescribed $lfloor cnrfloor$ cards mix in $O(d^2)$ physical shuffles, for each fixed $0<c<1$. In a different direction, Morris's chameleon analysis studies a tagged card conditional on an occupancy history in an auxiliary zigzag shuffle. Fourier analysis of random walks on symmetric groups goes back to Diaconis and Shahshahani. Estimates to date do not settle the dependence on the constant, and the focus is less on the leading constant than on a comparison between two far more refined laws: the law of the entire permutation, conditioned on the observed trajectories of a fixed subset of the cards.

Fix $m=n/2$ labels of the deck as outside labels, leaving $m$ free labels. Reveal the full trajectories of the outside labels through an interval ending at a sweep boundary. Two facts then hold. First, at every boundary the outside labels occupy $m$ slots, and the free labels occupy the complementary $m$ slots. Second, every switch coin on an edge incident to an outside card is determined by the trajectory, because the trajectory lists both the incoming and outgoing endpoints of that edge; the only unexposed coins are the ones on free-free edges. The unexposed coins compose to a bijection $T$ from the initial free slots to the final free slots, and indexing both sets in increasing order turns $T$ into a permutation $g$ in $S_m$. The permutation's conditional law $nu_{mathcal E}calE$, given the outside trajectory $mathcal E$,calE$, is the object of study.

The main assertion is that this conditional law is nearly uniform.

Theorem. Let $n=2^d$ and start the shuffle from any deterministic deck. Fix $m$ outside labels and $m$ free labels. Take $s_0=400$ and $J=40000$ and run $s_0+J(s_0+1)=16040400$ sweeps, an initial $s_0$-sweep preparation followed by $J$ chunks of $s_0+1$ sweeps. $$ sup_{deterministic starts} mathbb E_bbE_{mathcal E}calE|nu_{mathcal E}calE-U_m|_{TV} → 0 qquad(dto∞). $$
Consequently, the full-deck law from every deterministic start is within $1/4$ in total variation of uniform after $(s_0+J(s_0+1))d = 16040400 d$ physical shuffles.

The statement is equivalent to the pair consisting of $mathcal E$calE$ and $g$ having law close to the product of the environment law and $U_m$:
$$ |mathcal L(calL(mathcal E,g)-calE,g)-mathcal L(calL(mathcal E)calE) ⊗  U_m|_{TV} = mathbb E_bbE_{mathcal E}calE|nu_{mathcal E}calE-U_m|_{TV} . $$
The equivalence says that the free-slot permutation is nearly independent of the observed environment. The averaged statement is not the same as a statement for each environment; rare environments can leave too few useful free switches. The proof deletes a small amount of probability under the actual outside-path law, restoring it at the end.

The upper bound is sharp in the exponent of $d$. In $t$ physical shuffles there are at most $2^{tn/2}$ switch strings (half the coins are redundant to the choices of switched pairs), so the support of the shuffle law in $S_n$ has uniform mass at most $2^{tn/2}/n!$ and the law is at total-variation distance at least $1-2^{tn/2}/n!$ from uniform. In particular, distance at most $1/4$ requires
$$ tgeleftlceil(2)/(n)log_2(3n!)/(4)rightrceil = 2d - O(1), $$
and distance at least $1-2^{-n/2}$ holds whenever $tle d-2$, so the mixing time is of order exactly $d$.

The proof has three stages. First, any ordered half-deck, from any deterministic starting layout, is brought within $n^{-3}$ of uniform ordered endpoints after $400$ sweeps. The route is a two-copy calculation for one sweep, split into averages within large subcubes and fluctuations inside them, followed by a two-sweep contraction. Second, after the initial $400$-sweep preparation, alternating a $400$-sweep preparation interval with a single active sweep and repeating this chunk $40000$ times, truncation of the preparation law gives domination by a uniform free set together with an independent uniform slot permutation. During the active sweep, counting two-free edges met by each free carrier, and deleting outcomes with too many poor carriers, gives enough fresh coin constraints that agreement at many endpoints becomes unlikely. A symmetric-group character bound converts endpoint agreement into a trace contraction, and preparation domination promotes the trace to a positive-operator contraction that iterates across chunks. Third, coupling two decks, one from any fixed start and one from uniform, uses the preparation lemma on the outside labels and the conditional bound on the free labels.

Three alternative constructions give weaker but independent upper bounds, each with its own mechanism and its own mass and independence arguments. A reset construction extracts an exact uniform component from a long block, then computes a return probability for a central relative-permutation increment between two coupled copies, using the splitting technique of Nummelin. A trajectory-overlay construction adds predictable independent fair switches to a base trajectory without changing the underlying shuffle law, mixes one group of indices with a character estimate, and then uses three rounds on two transverse partitions, completed by a contingency-table local-limit theorem.

The conditional slot-bijection lemma fixes the basic bookkeeping. Conditional on the outside trajectory, the outside-attached switch coins are fixed and the remaining coins are independent and fair. Composing the free-free coins through an interval gives a bijection $T_e$ from the initial to the final free-slot set, hence a permutation $g_e=iota_T^{-1}T_eiota_0$ in $S_m$ where $iota_0,iota_T$ are the increasing identifications of slot sets with $[m]$. The conditional law of $g_e$ does not depend on which free labels entered the interval, and the laws on disjoint intervals compose by convolution with the later increment on the left. Uniqueness of the single-card path through one sweep is used repeatedly: initial and final positions determine every intermediate position, because just before coordinate $i$ is updated, its earlier hits are terminal and later hits are still initial.

For one tracked free card, the same lemma gives a linear evolution. Conditional masses average across a free-free edge and transport across a mixed edge; the uniform vector follows identically. A signed difference from uniform therefore has sum zero and Euclidean norm nonincreasing in time, and this linear rule is adapted to progressively revealed paths even though it is a formula for the conditional law given the full trajectory. This linear evolution supplies the one-sweep and two-sweep estimates of $Q$ used in the half-deck preparation.

The analysis is done on row vectors of probabilities or of their differences, in the ordinary Euclidean norm. If $u$ is a free-slot row vector, then $|uQ|le|u|$ by convexity of the square applied inside each column followed by row sums. Put $A_E=mathbb E[QQ^bbE[QQ^top]$, with the expectation over the paths from the fixed blocked start $E$. For an observed hole path, the conditional position of a single hole is determined by the mass evolution rule: at a two-hole pair the two outgoing masses are both the average of the incoming ones, and at a mixed pair the hole mass is transported to the outgoing hole. Total mass is preserved and uniform mass $1/h$ per hole follows the same rule. A signed difference from uniform (supported on the holes, total mass zero) has squared Euclidean norm non-increasing at every update. The forward mass calculation through a layer uses only the observed paths up to that time, since unobserved switch coins remain independent fair even after conditioning on the future part.

Lemma (two-sweep mass contraction). Fix $0<rhole1$. For all sufficiently large $d$ depending only on $rho$, any deterministic placement of $hgerho N$ holes and any signed mass vector supported on the holes with sum zero has expected squared norm after two sweeps at most $N^{-rho/16}L$, where $L$ is the initial squared norm. The estimate also holds conditionally on the past when the two sweeps begin.

Proof. Consider a single sweep, split into its first $r=lfloor d/2rfloor$ layers and its last $d-r$ layers. Conditional on the switch coins of the first $r$ layers, the evolutions inside different level-$r$ blocks (fixed by bits above $r$) proceed independently during layers above $r$, because a switch above coordinate $r$ never enters a lower block. Within each level-$r$ block $B$, the number $H_B'$ of holes at the end of the sweep is a sum of independent Bernoulli variables with mean $h_B'=h/2^{d-r}$, and Chernoff gives
$$ mathbb PbbPbig(|H_B'-h_B'|ge(rho)/(4)2^rbig)le 2e^{-rho^2 2^r/64}. $$
On the complementary event $D$ of the intersection over the $2^{d-r}$ level-$r$ blocks, holes remain roughly proportional across blocks in the second sweep, and the layer updates act by pair averaging.

Within any level-$i$ block $B$ in the second sweep, write $u=H_B/|B|$ and $m=M_B/|B|$ for the first moments of indicator and signed mass, $q$ for the second moment of mass, and $a=m/u$, $v=q-ua^2$ when $u>0$. Cauchy-Schwarz with support on the holes gives $vge0$, and rearrangement gives
$$ u=(u_1+u_2)/(2),quad m=(m_1+m_2)/(2),quad q=(q_1+q_2)/(2)-(q_1u_2+q_2u_1-2m_1m_2)/(4), $$
and
$$ v=tfrac12[(1-u_2/2)v_1+(1-u_1/2)v_2]+(1-u)J_B,qquad J_B=tfrac12(u_1a_1^2+u_2a_2^2)-ua^2ge0. $$

Weighting by block sizes, the sum of $|B|v_B$ at level $i>r$ is at most $1-rho/4$ times that at level $i-1$, plus the sum of $|B|J_B$ at level $i$. Each $|B|J_B$ equals the sum over the two children minus the parent of the quantity $|B|ua^2=M_B^2/H_B$. Thus the sum of $|B|J_B$ above level $r$ telescopes and is bounded by $sum_{B level r}M_B^2/H_B$. Cauchy-Schwarz gives $sum_BM_B^2/H_Ble L$, and at the root $v=q$ because total mass is zero. Restricting to the first sweep's output, the expected squared norm on $D$ is at most
$$ (1-rho/4)^{d-r}L+sum_{B level r}((M_B')^2)/(H_B'), $$
where $M_B'$ is the signed mass in $B$ after the first $r$ layers and $H_B'$ is the hole count there. Conditionally on the level-$r$ output, the mean of $|M_B'|^2$ is at most $L/2^{d-r}$, and on $D$ the bound $H_B'ge(rho/2)2^r$ holds, so the residual is at most $2L/(rho 2^r)$. Combining with the $D^c$ probability $le 2^{d-r}e^{-rho^2 2^r/64}$,
$$ mathbb E[bbE[|output|^2]leBig[(1-rho/4)^{d-r}+(2)/(rho 2^r)+2^{d-r}e^{-rho^2 2^r/64}Big]Lle N^{-rho/16}L $$
for $d$ sufficiently large. The estimate holds conditionally on the past.

The remaining comparison concerns only uniform shuffles within groups. Lemma (three rounds on transverse partitions). Let $R_1,...,R_K$ and $C_1,...,C_K$ partition $V$ with $|R_icap C_j|=n/K^2$. Independently shuffle uniformly within every $R$-group, then within every $C$-group, then within every $R$-group again. For the fixed $K=8$ and $n=2^d$, the resulting permutation law has total-variation distance $o(1)$ from uniform on $mathfrak S(V)$frakS(V)$ as $dto∞$.

Proof. The law is invariant on the right and on the left by the product subgroup of permutations within the $R$-groups, by the first and last shuffles. As for a uniform permutation, its probabilities depend only on the count matrix $X=(X_{ij})$, $X_{ij}$ being the number of moves from $R_i$ to $R_j$: two permutations with equal counts are identified by rearranging inputs within the groups to match their output groups and then rearranging outputs within the groups. So it suffices to compare the count laws.

Write $p_M$ for the analogous law on $M$ items for $K$ groups of equal size $m=M/K$. Its probability is zero off nonnegative integer tables with all row and column sums $m$, and on such tables is
$$ p_M(x)=frac{(m!)^{2K}}{M!prod_{i,j}x_{ij}!}. $$
This follows by partitioning each input group into its outputs' groups and then ordering the inputs assigned to each output group. In the ideal shuffle, each $C_ell$ at the middle stage contains $n/K^2$ labels from each initial $R_i$ and $n/K^2$ slots belonging to each final $R_j$, so within $C_ell$ the labels are uniformly permuted subject to those slot counts. Conditionally on the first shuffles the resulting tables $T^{(ell)}$ within the $C_ell$ are independent with law $p_{n/K}$, independent in law of that conditioning; each $T^{(ell)}$ has row and column sums $a_0=n/K^2$ and $X_{ij}=sum_{ell=1}^KT^{(ell)}_{ij}$. The last $R$-shuffles do not change total counts.

The comparison of $sum_ell T^{(ell)}$ with $p_n$ uses the free coordinates of the upper-left $(K-1)times(K-1)$ subtable, a vector of size $b=(K-1)^2$. For $M$ divisible by $K^2$ and $Xsim p_M$, center the vector at its coordinate mean $M/K^2$ and scale by $sqrt M$ to obtain $Z_M$. Let $J$ be the linear map completing a free-coordinate vector to a full matrix with zero row and column sums. Uniformly for $|z|$ bounded on the centered scaled lattice,
$$ Pr(Z_M=z)=M^{-b/2}(G(z)+o(1)),qquad G(z)=c_Kexp(-K^2|Jz|^2/2), $$
with $c_K>0$ depending only on $K$. Substitute $x_{ij}=a+sqrt M(Jz)_{ij}$ with $a=M/K^2$ in $p_M(x)$ (nonnegativity holds for bounded $z$ and large $M$). Stirling's formula and the expansion of $xlog x-x$ about $a$ have canceling linear terms in the sum; the constant terms cancel with the numerator and $M!$; the quadratic terms give $K^2|Jz|^2/2$ with remainders $o(1)$ for bounded $z$; and the square-root factors produce $M^{K-1/2-K^2/2}=M^{-b/2}$ times $(2pi)^{-b/2}K^{K^2-K}(1+o(1))$.

Adding independent uniform noise $W/sqrt M$ with $W$ uniform on $[0,1)^b$, the smoothed variable $Z_M+W/sqrt M$ has density converging in $L^1$ to $G$. On compacts this follows from the pointwise estimate and continuity, and tail probabilities are uniformly small because each count coordinate is hypergeometric with variance at most $M$; the limit density is integrable since $J$ is injective. This proves the $L^1$ convergence and in particular normalization, so $G$ is a centered nondegenerate Gaussian density.

For the sum of $K$ tables from $p_{n/K}$, the centered vector at scale $1/sqrt n$ is a sum of $K$ independent $Z_{n/K}/sqrt K$. With independent noise $W_i/sqrt n$ on each summand, independence and total-variation contraction under addition, together with the density convergence, give convergence in total variation to the law with density $G$: the sum of $K$ centered Gaussians, each divided by $sqrt K$, has the same law. The convergence remains with noise only on the first summand, since that smoothed summand by itself converges in total variation to a Gaussian (scaled by $1/sqrt K$) and its distance to its translate by any vector of norm at most $(K-1)sqrt b/sqrt n$ tends to zero uniformly. Conditioning on the other summands and their noises bounds by this $o(1)$ the distance induced by removing their noises. Thus, with a single noise, both the centered scaled sum and the $p_n$ vector are close to the same law. Total variation between these two noisy laws equals the discrete total variation without noise, since both lattices have the same cells spread out uniformly by the noise. This proves the desired count-law comparison, hence mixing of the ideal product.
