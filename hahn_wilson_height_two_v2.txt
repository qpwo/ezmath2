

Which infinite spectra can be built from truncated Brown--Peterson spectra using
finitely many cofiber sequences and retracts? The Hahn--Wilson conjecture proposes a
cohomological answer: being fp (finitely presented over the Steenrod algebra),
together with a chromatic height bound, should be exactly the class of such finite
constructions. Counterexamples at height two are built. Their chromatic localizations
keep every finiteness property one could ask for locally; the obstruction lies purely
in how the local pieces are joined.

Fix a prime p and write K(j) for Morava K-theory, with K(0)=HQ. A nonzero
finite p-local spectrum has type j if j is the least index for which its
K(j)-homology is nonzero; the thick subcategory theorem classifies thick
subcategories of finite p-local spectra by type [Hopkins-Smith 1998]; the K(n)-local analogue is [Hovey-Strickland 1999].

A bounded-below p-complete spectrum X is fp if H^*(X;F_p) is finitely
presented over the mod-p Steenrod algebra. Mahowald and Rezk prove that this is
equivalent to asking that pi_*(Vwedge X) be finite in total for some (hence
every) finite type-one p-local spectrum V, meaning finitely many nonzero homotopy
groups, each finite [Mahowald-Rezk 1999, Proposition 3.2]. For an fp-spectrum, fp-type at most
n means this test holds for a finite spectrum of type n+1. Exact fp-type two means
fp-type at most two but not at most one.

./pdf/mahowald-rezk-1999-brown-comenetz-duality.pdf

Mahowald--Rezk's duality theory makes fp-spectra a natural home for infinite
spectra with controlled chromatic behavior [Mahowald-Rezk 1999, Theorem 8.11], and
Hahn--Wilson's redshift theorem supplies further examples: for the multiplicative
forms in their theorem, K(BP< n>_p^wedge)_p^wedge has fp-type

Mahowald and Rezk asked whether L_n^fXto L_nX is an equivalence for every

The algebraic argument comes first. The discussion of law charts defines the reduced
setting and proves its chart geometry. The discussion of complete operations computes
complete operations using compatible maps on finite reductions and realizes perfect
modules as actual complexes, so that the calculation controls maps and cones, not
only endomorphism groups. The construction of the operator and the gluing construction construct
the Laurent operator and the gluing. The obstruction argument proves the
finite-tower criterion and verifies its hypothesis for the glued object together
with its growth estimate. These arguments explain how complete operations control
finite constructions and how the gluing obstructs them globally. The large-prime comparison and the transfer argument establish the comparison
with spectra and prove the transfer proposition. The latter takes the connective
cover of the resulting E(2)-local spectrum, following Mahowald--Rezk
[Mahowald-Rezk 1999, Proposition 3.5 and Section 7.2(2)]; in particular it proves the
total-finiteness test behind fp-ness. The large-prime comparison additionally gives
the Brown--Peterson quotient calculation and a thick-transport principle for
equivalences of underlying categories. The consequences and scope establishes
exact fp-type two and both localization comparisons, then compares the example with
the positive local theory and proves the separate obstruction for the connective
cover of Morava E-theory.

Lemma.
Let A be a k-algebra with a locally finite continuous action of Z, and let
Z_m be the subgroup of index p^m. Suppose A=∪_{mgeq0}A^{Z_m} and the
action on Spec A is geometrically free. Then Spec AtoSpec(A^Z) is an fpqc
Z-torsor.

Proof.

First descend the free action to finite quotients, then prove the finite torsor
assertion directly and pass to the limit. Set A_m=A^{Z_m}. Every element has
finite orbit, so A is integral over each A_m. Two geometric points with the
same restriction to A_m differ by Z_m: restrict first to a subalgebra generated
by finitely many finite orbits, use the Chinese remainder theorem and an invariant
product of separating functions, and use flatness of the field extension (finite
group invariants commute with field extension). The finite-level assertions give
nonempty closed sets of possible elements of Z_m, and compactness supplies a
compatible element for the whole algebra.

Integrality lifts every geometric point of A_m to one of A, and the induced
Z/Z_m-action on Spec A_m is free: if a coset fixed such a point, lifting to a
point of A shows the translate and the original differ by Z_m, so geometric
freeness upstairs forces the coset to be trivial.

A finite constant group K acting geometrically freely on an affine scheme gives a
torsor over the invariant ring even when |K| is not invertible. At a lift of a
geometric point of the invariant spectrum choose b_1,...,b_{|K|} whose

field extension); then D_b=det(g(b_j))_{gin K,j} satisfies: D_b^2 is
invariant, and after inverting it the coefficients of any element in the basis

integrality.

Lemma (Periodic splitting).

and to eS_0 after canceling that power, hence vanishes; continue inductively.

two-sided Ore division ring of fractions Q_0. Clearing common denominators
