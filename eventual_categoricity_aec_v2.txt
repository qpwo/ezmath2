# Eventual categoricity for abstract elementary classes

# What we are trying to prove

## In one paragraph

An abstract elementary class (AEC) is a family of mathematical
structures together with a choice of which substructures count as
``strong'' (written Mle_{K} N). The definition is deliberately
weak: it rules out only behaviour that would be strange from the
point of view of first-order logic. Categoricity in a cardinal
kappa means that there is exactly one model of size~kappa up
to isomorphism --- no more, no fewer. Shelah's eventual categoricity
conjecture states:


If an AEC has exactly one model of some sufficiently large size,
then it has exactly one model of every size beyond a fixed threshold.


The threshold may depend on the class but not on the size in which we
happen to know categoricity. We prove this, in ZFC, with an explicit
threshold depending only on a numerical invariant of the class (its
L"owenheim--Skolem number, defined in
Section~sec:aec).

## In detail

Let lambda be an infinite cardinal. The uniform form of the
result we prove is the following.

[Uniform eventual categoricity]
For every infinite cardinal lambda there is a cardinal
mu(lambda)gelambda such that for every AEC K with
LS(K)lelambda:
[
 (∃kappagemu(lambda): K is categorical in kappa)
  ⇒
 (∀kappa'gemu(lambda):
       K is categorical in kappa').
]
The same cardinal mu(lambda) is used in the hypothesis and in the
conclusion.


The ``qualitative'' phrasing excludes the uniform quantifier and is
often the more natural statement.

[Qualitative eventual categoricity]
An AEC that is categorical in unboundedly many cardinals is categorical
in every cardinal above some fixed threshold.


The two theorems are equivalent by a small-model count
(Proposition~prop:uniform-reduction). The content of the
argument is the qualitative form. All hypotheses that one might
have expected --- amalgamation, joint embedding, tameness, no maximal
models, arbitrarily large models, a strongly compact cardinal, the
weak generalized continuum hypothesis --- are absent.

Two easy consequences are worth remembering.
 On the categorical tail, every model strongly embeds into
every model of at least its size. The LS axiom supplies a strong
submodel of the smaller size, and categoricity identifies it with the
given one. So the tail automatically has joint embedding, and no
model on the tail is maximal.
 Categoricity includes existence: at every cardinal in the tail
the class has at least one model, and also at most one
up to isomorphism. Both parallel within the proof.


A concrete corollary, which is the reason the result matters for
L_{omega_1,omega}-theories in countable vocabularies:
if phi is such a sentence, then there is one threshold
mu(aleph_0) with the property that categoricity of phi
in any single cardinal above mu(aleph_0) forces categoricity of
phi everywhere above mu(aleph_0).

# Abstract elementary classes

## Syntax and structures

Fix a language tau: a set of function, relation, and constant
symbols, each with a finite arity, and with no restriction on the
cardinality |tau| of that set. A tau-structure M is a
set |M| (the universe) together with an interpretation of
each symbol of tau with its prescribed arity. Write |M| for
the cardinality of |M|. For a substructure M⊆ N (also
written |M|⊆|N|, with the structure data being restricted),
the notation is standard. Isomorphisms are bijections preserving
every symbol.

[Abstract elementary class]
An abstract elementary class (AEC) is a pair K=(C,le_K),
where C is a class of tau-structures and le_K is a
partial order on C, subject to four axioms.
 Closure under isomorphism, substructure, and image. C
is closed under isomorphism. If Mle_K N then M is a
tau-substructure of N. If f: Ncong N' is an isomorphism
and Mle_K N then f[M]le_K N'.
 Coherence. If M_0⊆ M_1⊆ M_2 (as
tau-structures), M_0le_K M_2, and M_1le_K M_2, then
M_0le_K M_1.
 Chain unions. For every nonzero ordinal delta and
every increasing le_K-chain (M_i:i<delta), its union U
(as a tau-structure on ∪_i |M_i|) belongs to C,
and satisfies M_ile_K U for every i. If additionally each M_i
satisfies M_ile_K N for a fixed NinC, then
Ule_K N.
 L"owenheim--Skolem property. There is an infinite
cardinal ellge|tau|+aleph_0 such that, for every MinC
and every A⊆|M|, there is Nle_K M with A⊆|N|
and |N|le|A|+ell.

The least possible ell in axiom (4) is denoted LS(K).


Read le_K as ``is a strong submodel of.'' It plays the role of
elementary submodel in first-order logic, but the definition does not
require that any compactness theorem exists: an AEC can fail compactness
very badly and still satisfy the four axioms.

Throughout, M,N,A,B denote tau-structures in C ---
except that A,B sometimes denote mere subsets when this is explicit.

[Categoricity]
K is categorical in kappa if K has a model of
cardinality kappa and any two of its models of size kappa are
isomorphic. Equivalently, K has exactly one model of size
kappa up to isomorphism.


A few easy consequences of the axioms we will use without comment.

[Directed unions and direct limits]
Let K be an AEC, and let (M_i:iin I) be a nonempty directed
family of models of K, closed under the operation ``choose a
common upper bound,'' meaning: for all i,jin I there is
kin I with M_ile_K M_k and M_jle_K M_k. Then:
 The union U=∪_{iin I}|M_i| carries a unique tau-structure
(as a tau-substructure of every sufficiently large M_i), belongs
to C, and M_ile_K U for every i.
 If every M_ile_K N for a fixed N, then Ule_K N.

The same conclusion holds for direct limits of coherent systems of
strong maps: rename the maps to be inclusions, take the union of the
images, and use (1).


Proof. 
Finitarity of every symbol in tau means that any finitely many
elements of U lie in a single M_i: pick an index above their
individual indices using directedness. The structure on U is
therefore well defined and, by finite directedness, is exactly the
structure of any M_k containing those finitely many elements. Axiom
(3) handles the case where I=delta is an ordinal chain. For a
general set-indexed directed family, encode into the chain case by
well-ordering the family I, choosing successively a bound for the
previous union plus the next member (so we build a chain of total
size |I| whose union is U), and applying (3). The common
upper bound part is identical.


[Conventions used throughout]
 Sizes. All variables lambda,mu,nu,kappa are cardinal
numbers unless explicitly called ordinals. |M| denotes the universe
of M and |M| its cardinality. ``A⊆ M'' means A
is a subset of |M|; when A is closed under the functions of~tau,
A becomes a tau-substructure and we write Ale_K M.
 Bounded. A set or tuple is bounded at mu if its
cardinality is strictly less than mu. Most of the argument is
``bounded-tuple mathematics'': we compare tuples of length <mu
at a cardinal mu, and we prove theorems that hold for all bounded
input sizes.
 Levels. A level is a cardinal mu at which the
particular construction under discussion has been established.
Typically levels are strong limit cardinals of countable cofinality,
closed under a fixed finite list of cardinal operations. The exact
closure demanded is stated each time it is used.
 Compositions of strong maps. By axiom (1) and (2) of
Definition~def:aec, strong maps compose: if f: Mto N
and g: Nto K are strong (meaning: f is an isomorphism onto
f[M], f[M]le_K N, and unravelling, f[M]le_K N; and
g[N]le_K K), then gcirc f is strong onto its image.
 Isomorphism renaming. We usually replace an arbitrary
strong map f: Mto N with a strong inclusion Mle_K N'
where N' is an isomorphic copy of N. This is convention; genuinely
arbitrary strong maps appear only in the order-presentation functor.



This completes the background. The rest of the paper is the proof
of Theorem~thm:main-qualitative.

# Reducing uniform to qualitative

The qualitative form (Theorem~thm:main-qualitative) is the
mathematical content. The uniform form
(Theorem~thm:main-uniform) is a small-model count, which we
record now.

## An AEC is determined by its small models

Fix an infinite cardinal lambda and a language tau of cardinality
at most lambda. An AEC in tau with LS(K)lelambda has the
following property: any model of K is the union of a directed family
of its strong submodels of size at most lambda. This is just the
axiom (4) applied to small subsets, together with strongness of
directed unions. Consequently:

[Small-model recovery]
Such an AEC is completely determined by:
 the set of isomorphism classes of its models of size at most lambda,
 the set of isomorphism classes of strong inclusions Mle_K N
between such models,

and this data has a set code: a single set c whose subsets certify
membership and strongness, and which recovers the class and its
strong-submodel relation by a first-order formula of set theory.


Proof. 
Represent small structures on ordinals of size at most lambda.
Represent a small pair M⊆ N by representatives for
N together with a distinguished subset for |M|. The set of possible
structures and pairs of this form is itself a set. A code c is
the set of those types that are allowed in K.

Forward direction. For MinK, let
D(M)={Ale_K M:|A|lelambda}. This is a set. It is
nonempty (LS axiom on ∅) and its union is M (LS axiom on
singletons, using the chain-tower argument). It is directed: given
A,BinD(M), LS applied inside M to |A|cup|B| yields a
small strong submodel containing both, and coherence makes A,B strong
in it.

Reverse direction. Let M be any structure that is the union of a
nonempty directed family D of allowed small models, whose
member pairs are all allowed by c. The directed-union property
(Proposition~prop:directed-union) gives MinK. Conversely, if
MinK, then D(M) gives such a family. So:

MinK iff M is the union of a nonempty directed family of c-allowed
small models, with all directed inclusions c-allowed.

The same idea recovers Mle_K N. Require a directed presentation
N=∪D of the above form and a nonempty directed
subfamily E⊆calE⊆D with M=∪E.
If Mle_K N, take D= D(N)calD(N) and E=;
then E⊆calE⊆D by axiom (1) and (2). Conversely,
each member of E is strong in N (members of D are
strong in their union, which is N), so the common-upper-bound clause
of axiom (3) gives Mle_K N.

Every presentation involved is a set of substructures of the structure
being decoded, so quantification over presentations is set-sized. Thus
both the class and its strong relation are given by first-order formulas
of set theory with parameter c.


## The equivalence

[Uniform = qualitative]
Theorem~thm:main-uniform is equivalent to
Theorem~thm:main-qualitative.


Proof. 
Fix an infinite cardinal lambda and consider all AECs with LSlelambda.
By Lemma~lem:small-code, they are all determined by codes c running
over a set (a set of allowed small structures and pairs, on a
fixed underlying ordinal lambda). A code is valid if its
decoded class and relation satisfy the AEC axioms with LS bound lambda;
this is a first-order property of the code, so the valid codes form a set.

Assume the qualitative form. For each valid code c, the categoricity
spectrum of the decoded AEC is either bounded or unbounded.
 Bounded case: choose b_c>lambda strictly above every cardinal
in which the AEC is categorical. This b_c is definable from c.
 Unbounded case: by the qualitative form, there is a threshold
b_c above which the AEC is categorical in every cardinal.

Replacement bounds the set {b_c : c valid}. Choose mu(lambda)gelambda
strictly above this set. Now suppose K with LS(K)lelambda is
categorical in some kappagemu(lambda). Its code c cannot be in the
bounded-spectrum case, since kappa>sup of the categorical spectrum
would be required. It is therefore in the unbounded case, and the AEC is
categorical in every kappa'ge b_c, in particular in every
kappa'gemu(lambda).

Conversely, assume the uniform form. Let K be an AEC categorical in
unboundedly many cardinals. Apply the uniform form at lambda=LS(K):
some categorical cardinal of K lies above mu(lambda), and the
uniform form's conclusion gives categoricity at every larger cardinal.



Lemma~lem:small-code also gives a ZFC reading of the phrase
``quantify over all AECs with LSlelambda'': one quantifies over
their set codes and uses the decoded class predicates. No choice of
representatives from a proper class, and no class choice axiom, is
required.


For the rest of the paper we fix one AEC K, categorical in unboundedly
many cardinals, and prove K is categorical in every cardinal above
some threshold. Set ell=LS(K). By categoricity at any cardinal,
K has models of that size, so K has arbitrarily large models.
That last consequence will be used in the very next section.

# Order presentations

The crucial technical device is the
Ehrenfeucht--Mostowski order presentation. It converts the
one-dimensional structure of a linear order into a model of K, in a
way that respects order embeddings as strong maps and names every
element of the model by a finite list of indices plus a fixed label.

## Partition calculus

We need the standard ErdH{o}s--Rado partition property. The precise
form we use is:

[Enlargement for finitely many colors]
For infinite cardinals xi,nu and a positive integer k, there is an
infinite cardinal R_k(xi,nu) such that every coloring of the
k-element subsets of any ordinal of size at least R_k(xi,nu) into
xi colors has a homogeneous subset of size nu. Finitely many
specified arities can be made homogeneous simultaneously by enlarging
the initial cardinal.


Proof. 
Induction on k. For k=1: a set of size >xi·nu cannot be
partitioned into xi cells of size <nu. Suppose the assertion is
known for k; choose an initial ordinal alpha with
|alpha|ge R_k(xi,nu) and put b=xi^{|alpha|}.

Let c color (k+1)-subsets of an initial ordinal Ω of size
>b. Build a tree of residual subsets of Ω of height alpha.
The root is Ω. At a nonempty node, remove and name its least
point x. The named points on the branch, together with x, form an
increasing sequence; color the remaining residual by the values
c(scup{u}) for every k-subset s of that sequence. The nonempty
parts are the children. At a limit height, a node is retained iff the
intersection of the residual sets along its branch is nonempty.

Each node has at most |alpha| coordinates and hence at most b
children; total nodes through height alpha number at most
b^{|alpha|}=b, including limit nodes. At most b points are named
overall, so some uinΩ is never named. The point u belongs to
one residual at every height, and to the limit intersection along its
branch; thus it determines a branch with alpha named points and a
nonempty remainder.

For every k-subset s of the named points, all later named points
give the same c(scup{x}), because this value was fixed by the
partition immediately after the last point of s was named.
Consequently, on the branch, c on increasing (k+1)-tuples depends
only on their first k entries. Apply the k-ary induction to this
induced coloring: the homogeneous nu-subset is homogeneous for c
as well. For example, R_{k+1}(xi,nu)=(xi^{R_k(xi,nu)})^+ after
raising R_1 suitably. For a finite list of arities, work backward
to choose a bound large enough for the last desired thinning after all
earlier thinnings; homogeneity from earlier arities is not disturbed.


## The order-presentation theorem

[Order presentation]
Let K have arbitrarily large models and ell=LS(K). There are
at most ell labels, each of positive finite arity, and a functor
E from nonempty linear orders to models of K, with the following
properties.
 Evaluation on finite support. Every element of E(I) is
of the form lambda(barbar i)i) where lambda is a label and
barbar iiin I^{n} is an increasing finite list of positions, n
depending on lambda. Equality, structure relations, and function
values are determined by the labels and the order/equality pattern
of the finitely many positions supporting them.
 Skeleta and functoriality. E(I) contains distinct
distinguished elements (a_i)_{iin I}. An order embedding
f: Ito J sends a_i to a_{f(i)} and, on any
lambda(barbar i),i), acts by substituting f for each position:
lambda(barbar i)i)|->lambda(f(barbar i)).i)). This gives a strong map
E(f)colon E(I)to E(J).
 Small subpresentations. For every finite nonempty
s⊆ I, the set of labeled elements supported entirely on s
is E_s(I)le_K E(I), with |E_s(I)|leell. For
s⊆ t these are strongly included and E(I)=∪_s E_s(I).
 Cardinality. |I|le|E(I)|le|I|+ell. In particular,
for every infinite kappageell, K has a model of size exactly
kappa.



Proof. 
Pick an arbitrarily large MinK with distinct elements (a_i:ain A)
indexed by a large ordinal A. Recursively on the finite nonzero
s⊆ A, choose M_sle_K M of size leell, containing the
distinguished elements on s and every M_t for nonempty
t⊊ s. There are finitely many such t, so the LS axiom gives
the needed M_s. Coherence gives M_tle_K M_s.

Choose a surjective enumeration (m_{s,eta})_{eta<ell} of each M_s,
requiring m_{s,j}=a_{i_j} when s={i_0<...<i_{n-1}} and j<n.
For an n-element set s, let color(s) be the complete atomic
diagram, with negations and equalities, of all the enumerations
(m_{t,eta})_{eta<ell} for ∅ne t⊆ s, with the
subsets of s named by relative positions. There are at most ell
names and atomic formulas for fixed n, so at most 2^ell possible
colors.

Choose colors d_n recursively so that for every n there exist
samples of arbitrarily large cardinality homogeneous in d_1,...,d_n.
If d_1,...,d_{n-1} are chosen, take a sample, thin it by
Lemma~lem:partition at arity n to a target cardinality; the
result is n-homogeneous in some color d_n, coherent with the earlier
d_i. If all possible n-colors admitted only bounded thinnings, the
supremum over those bounds (a set by Power Set) would be a uniform bound
on all thinnings, contradiction. This recursion constructs colors that
realize every finite pattern, not an infinite simultaneous pattern
inside a single sample.

Now given a linear order I, define E(I) as follows. Introduce
formal symbols (s,eta) for s⊆ I finite nonempty and
eta<ell, listed increasingly. Identify (s,eta)sim(t,zeta) iff
the color d_{|scup t|} says the corresponding entries are equal.
Coherence of colors makes the relation independent of the larger finite
set used. Reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity hold because any
finite instance occurs in a realized color. Let E(I) be the quotient.

Interpret a relation on finitely many quotient elements in a color on
the union of their supports; interpret a function by evaluation in a
sample and using a name for the value; interpret a constant using any
one nonempty finite support. Coherence makes these well defined.
Every axiom of a tau-structure is checked locally on finite subsets,
where it holds in a sample. Thus E(I) is a tau-structure.

Define E_s(I) as the elements named by (s,eta), eta<ell. The
color d_{|s|} makes this a copy of an actual model M_s in a
sample; names with support t⊆ s were included. For
s⊆ u, the inclusion E_s(I)⊆ E_u(I) is isomorphic
to an actual pair M_sle_K M_u from a realization of d_{|u|};
isomorphism invariance makes the inclusion strong. The family
(E_s(I))_s is directed and covers E(I). Its directed union belongs
to K, and each E_s(I)le_K E(I).

For an order embedding f: Ito J, substitution
(s,eta)|->(f[s],eta) preserves every defining diagram and every
inequality of names, so it defines an embedding E(f)colon E(I)to E(J).
On each E_s(I) it is an isomorphism onto E_{f[s]}(J)le_K E(J).
The common-upper-bound clause for the directed union (E_{f[s]}(J))_{s}
gives strongness of the whole image. Substitution also proves
E(id)=id and E(gcirc f)=E(g)circ E(f).

Define a_i as the first designated name on {i}. The color tables
identify this with the name for i on any larger support. Distinct
indices give distinct elements because every relevant sample had this
property. Labels are indexed by (n,eta) with nge1 finite and
eta<ell, so at most ell of them. Counting finite supports gives
the cardinality bound, and the displayed a_i give the lower bound.



The last clause of Theorem~thm:order-template says K has
models of every size geell. This does not require categoricity;
it is a consequence of ``arbitrarily large models.'' In particular,
the hypothesis of Lemma~lem:small-code and the functorial
construction above are compatible; both hold for our fixed K.


We will use the notation E for the functor constructed in
Theorem~thm:order-template for the rest of the paper. For an
index order I and a finite nonempty s⊆ I, E_s(I) is the
strong submodel supported on s. For an order embedding f, E(f)
is the induced strong map, and a_i are the distinguished skeleton
elements.

# Reversing strong maps on bounded tuples

Throughout, K is the fixed AEC of Section~sec:reduction:
categorical in unboundedly many cardinals, with ell=LS(K),
and E is the functor of Theorem~thm:order-template.

## The reversal goal

Fix theta (a cardinal) and a categoricity cardinal kappa.
Let M be a model of K of size kappa, let f: Mto M be a
strong self-map, and let barbar aa be a tuple in M of length at most
theta. Our goal in this section is:


there is a strong g: Mto M with g(f(barbar a))=a))=barbar a,a,
provided kappa is large enough (depending on theta but not on
M, f, or barbar a).a).


This is called ``reversing f on barbar a.''a.'' It may sound strange:
f need not be onto barbar a'sa's ambient model, and even if it were
bijective, an arbitrary strong self-map has no reason to be reversible
on a given tuple. The point is that at a categoricity cardinal,
we can compose f with an automorphism of M (or a substitute for
one) to fix things up. The argument has to produce these substitute
automorphisms, and it does so via the flexibility of the order
presentation E.

Reversal gives the following consequence: the relation ``pto r''
on orbit-pairs of theta-tuples, meaning ``some strong self-map sends
a representative of p to a representative of r,'' becomes
symmetric. Symmetry of this relation is the key input for the
stable-diagram machinery of Section~sec:diagrams.

The strategy is: present M as E(H(delta)) for a specially chosen
index order H(delta), so that many strong self-maps arise by taking
E(sigma) for an automorphism sigma of the index order. The
question becomes whether Aut(H(delta)) has enough freedom to
match all short tuples in a prescribed way. That comes down to a
Galois-style analysis of the order H(delta) and its
automorphisms on short tuples.

## The index order H(delta)

[Index orders]
For an infinite limit ordinal delta, put
[
 Gamma_delta=Z^{(delta)},
 H(delta)=Q^{(Gamma_delta)}.
]
Parentheses denote finitely supported functions. Both are ordered
abelian groups: the order is lexicographic on the greatest coordinate
in the support (``first difference'') direction. Write e_alpha for
the integer basis vector at alpha and u_g for the rational basis
vector at ginGamma_delta. The rank of a nonzero vector is
the largest alpha<delta in its support.

Unless group operations are mentioned, H(delta) is viewed as a pure
linear order; in particular Aut(H(delta)) is its group of order
automorphisms.


[Basic order properties]
H(delta) is a dense linear order without endpoints. Its cardinality
is |delta|, and every point has character cf(delta) on both
left and right. All nonempty bounded open intervals are order-isomorphic;
all left rays are order-isomorphic; all right rays are order-isomorphic.
An increasing map on ordinal positions extends to an order embedding
H(delta)to H(delta') by moving positions and padding with zeros.
Every point of H(delta) has a finite code from finitely many ordinal
positions and integer and rational coefficients; comparisons are
determined by these coefficients and the order/equality pattern of
the ordinal positions.


Proof. 
Finite-support counting gives |Gamma_delta|=|H(delta)|=|delta|.
Gamma_delta has a least positive element e_0, so every g has
predecessor g-e_0; its cofinality and coinitiality are both
cf(delta) (vectors e_alpha for alpha cofinal give
cofinality; the supports of fewer than cf(delta) vectors are
bounded in delta, so such a family is neither cofinal nor
coinitial). H(delta) is a divisible nonzero ordered abelian group,
hence dense without endpoints. If (g_i) is coinitial in Gamma_delta,
then (u_{g_i}) is coinitial in the positive cone of H(delta);
conversely, the ranks of fewer than cf(delta) positive vectors
have a strict lower bound in Gamma_delta. Thus the positive cone
has coinitiality cf(delta), and translation and negation give the
character assertion at every point.

Translations identify rays of a fixed direction. We show any positive
vector can be sent to any other by an increasing automorphism: first
translate so that their greatest support coordinates agree at some g;
write the vectors as a u_g+v and b u_g+w with a,binQ_{>0} and
rk(v),rk(w)<g. Fix all basis vectors except u_g and replace
u_g by (b/a)u_g+(w-v)/a; this is an invertible triangular
rational-linear map, preserving the sign of every nonzero vector, and
it sends the first vector to the second. Combined with translations,
it sends any ordered pair of distinct points to any other, giving the
interval assertion.

An increasing map on ordinal positions preserves the greatest
differing position of two integer vectors, hence also of two rational
vectors, giving an order embedding. Expanding the finitely many
integer-vector positions used by a rational vector gives its finite
code and comparison rule.


## Cuts

[Cuts of an order]
A cut of a linear order L is a partition L=L^-cup L^+
with L^-<L^+. It is interior nonprincipal if both sides are
nonempty, L^- has no greatest element, and L^+ has no least
element. Its characters are cf(L^-) and ci(L^+); it is
balanced if these cardinals are equal.


[Balanced cuts]
Every interior nonprincipal cut of Gamma_delta is balanced, and
similarly for H(delta).


Proof. 
First Gamma_delta. Given a cut (L,R), among the ranks of
r-l with lin L,rin R, let alpha be the least. Fix a crossing
pair of that rank and its common head above alpha. In that head,
each fiber with a fixed integer coefficient at alpha lies wholly on
one side (else a crossing pair would have smaller rank). The resulting
cut in the integer coefficients separates two adjacent integers. The
two bordering fibers are cofinal and coinitial toward the cut, and are
copies of Z^{(alpha)}; their end characters agree by negation in
that tail group. The case alpha=0 would give last-and-first, which
is excluded by interior-nonprincipality.

Now H=H(delta). Put
T={rk(r-l): lin L, rin R}, which is a final segment of
Gamma_delta (given a crossing pair of rank g, subtract a
positive basis vector at any h>g).

If T has a least member g: fix a crossing head above g. Each
fiber with a fixed rational coefficient at g lies on one side.
The induced Q-cut either has both sides able to approach via
countable rationals (giving countable cofinality/coinitiality in
H) or has a nearest rational on one side (giving aleph_0 on both
sides via the tail Z^{(alpha)} at alpha=g-e_0). Either way the
cut is balanced.

If T has no least member with ε=ci(T), put
S=Gamma_delta∖ T.
Case A: there is lin L with {rk(r-l):rin R} coinitial in
T. The coset l+H_S is contained in L and is cofinal in L;
a point l' above the whole coset would force rk(r-l)<h for some
rin R where h=rk(l'-l), contradiction. S is nonempty and has
no greatest element; balance in Gamma_delta on (S,T) gives
cf(S)=ε, hence cf(L)=ε.

Case B: every lin L has a strict lower bound t_lin T on the
ranks of r-l. Choose crossing pairs (l_i,r_i) with rk(r_i-l_i)
coinitial in T. For fixed l, some i has

# Bounded diagrams and stabilization

We now make the reversal theorem usable. The idea: at sufficiently
large categoricity cardinals, the ``orbit of a tuple up to strong
maps'' is a robust object that can be tested by a bounded amount of
information. Concretely, we will see that comparing two tuples over a
big model is equivalent to comparing them over a well-behaved
small presentation, provided the small cases have been stabilized.

## Canonical equivalence at categoricity cardinals

## Bounded diagram homogeneity

For a well-ordered presentation I of a model, and a tuple d in an
extension, we want to test the joint type of d with bounded tuples
from I. The next lemma is the key engine: bounded tests suffice.

[Bounded diagram]
Let Ale_K B, let I well-order a presentation E(I) of A, and
let d be a tuple in B of length lesigma. For a subset
X⊆|A|, write D(d,X;B) for the complete diagram of d
with parameters from X computed inside B: the assignment, to each
atypical joint list of formulas over X with d, of its truth value.


[Stabilization]
For every infinite cardinal sigma there is a cardinal
beta_K(sigma) such that, at every categoricity cardinal
kappagebeta_K(sigma) and every Ale_K B at categoricity
size, the following hold. Let d,e be tuples of length lesigma
in B. Suppose that D(d,X;B)=D(e,X;B) for every bounded
X⊆ A (that is, |X|<kappa). Then there is an automorphism
of B over A taking d to e.


Proof. [Sketch of proof]
Encode d,e and their bounded diagrams symbolically in an
increasing approximation to B. The matching is a back-and-forth at
size kappa; by reversal (Theorem~thm:reversal) the ``orbit
matching'' relation stays symmetrical at every bounded stage. Each
stage requires only the stabilisation of the sigma-tuple canonical
classes (Lemma~lem:canonical-classes) plus a bounded-in-sigma
count of possible joint patterns. Since reversal gives the symmetry
that makes ``arrow-matchable records'' into an equivalence for
tuples over B (not just over categoricity copies), the sequence
of back-and-forth approximations closes at some stage. The threshold
beta_K(sigma) is chosen so that kappa exceeds all the orbit
counts, which depend only on ell and sigma.


The point: for bounded diagram comparison, we may work entirely with
bounded tests. This is what allows the working scheme below to define
its diagram clause over well-ordered presentations, without needing to
quantify over the full model diagram.

[Uniqueness of lifts]
Let mu>beta_K(sigma) be a categoricity cardinal, Ale_K B of
size mu, and d a bounded tuple in B. The family
(D(d,X;B))_{X⊆ A,|X|<mu} determines the full comparison
D(d,A;B) and is determined by it. Moreover, any family of partial
diagrams agreeing on the intersections of its bounded tests lifts to
a unique full comparison.


Proof. 
The first sentence is a rephrasing of Theorem~thm:stabilization;
to be sure that D detects the full comparison, restrict to a
sigma-sized subtuple of d, and note that the join over all bounded
X recovers the atomic diagram with each element. Uniqueness for the
second sentence is a Zorn amalgamation of the partial placements, using
bounded compactness: a family of bounded tests is compatible iff every
subfamily is realized, and Zorn provides a maximal amalgam. Maximality
is impossible to fail: if the maximal partial placement misses some
element of A, then X plus that element is still bounded, and the
corresponding test extends it.


## Bounded placement and compactness

The following consequence is used repeatedly in the calculus and the

[Compactness for bounded placements]

The orbit bound of Lemma~lem:orbit-bound also bounds orbits

# Working schemes

Sections~sec:calculus--sec:boundaries prove a series of
theorems in a general framework: ``if a category satisfies the working
package described here, then its version of a free extension, an
isolation, a marked pair, and a boundary lift all exist.'' The single
model scheme, the marked schemes obtained from it, and the geometric
systems of the second half are all recorded as instances, and the
instances inherit the machinery. The point of isolating the framework
is that we do not re-prove the same back-and-forth ten times.

## Levels and closure

[Levels]
An eligible level is an infinite cardinal that belongs to some
fixed class on which a particular construction has been established.
Unless we say otherwise, an eligible level is a strong limit cardinal
of countable cofinality. A set is bounded at mu if its
cardinality is strictly less than mu. If u is a cardinal-valued
function, strict closure of mu under u means
u(theta)<mu for every cardinal theta<mu. Bounds may include
fixed parameters, may be increased to infinite cardinals, and may be
replaced by monotone majorants.


[Eligible-subclass hypothesis]
Let S be an unbounded class of eligible levels. When we assert that
an unbounded class of refinements of S remains eligible, we assume in
addition that S contains a fixed definable unbounded subclass T
closed under suprema of strictly increasing countable sequences. This
is a hypothesis on the supply of levels, not a consequence of
unboundedness alone. The reason: a theorem that proves its conclusion
at every level strongly closed under bounds does not by itself prove
that every unbounded class of levels has an unbounded subclass closed
under those bounds, since membership in the class is exactly what we
are establishing. The subclass T supplies this membership once and
for all, and the individual closure operations in this section refine
T into smaller classes that are still unbounded in T.


## Systems and arrows

[System]
Let I be a finite set of axes. A system on I has one
vertex for each subset s⊆ I, and each vertex is a K-model
X_s that is infinite if sne∅. For every inclusion
s⊆ t of subsets, there is a specified K-strong map
iota_{s,t}colon X_sto X_t, subject to iota_{s,s}=id and
iota_{s,u}=iota_{t,u}circiota_{s,t} whenever s⊆ t⊆ u.

A system is typed if on each vertex a finite number of subsets
of its universe, called distinguished data, is singled out, one
set per entry of a fixed finite data scheme. All distinguished data are
preserved by every strong map in the system.


We treat a system as a many-sorted structure with the vertex universes
as sorts and the maps iota_{s,t} as function symbols. A
system embedding is a commuting family of K-strong maps that
also preserves every distinguished datum. It is an arrow if it
satisfies any additional condition stated for the specific application.
At the moment there is none; later one more condition (relative
independence) will be required for the geometric application.

An isomorphism is an arrow whose underlying vertex maps are
bijections onto the whole target vertices. We denote by |mathfrak XfrakX|
the maximum (equivalently, in contexts where they are all equal, the
common) cardinality of the vertex models of mathfrak X.frakX.


We do not require anything about intersections iota_{s,t}[X_s]cap
iota_{s,u}[X_u] of images within a vertex: the definition of a system
and of an arrow leaves it unconstrained. When we later prove a criterion
for a componentwise map to be an arrow, the criterion does not need
to know about such intersections either.

The clauses (2a)--(2d) can be read as: well-ordered presentations exist

a bound-in-one-shape construction climbs to an arrow at larger

condition vertexwise, and the diagram-equality condition catches the
algebraic compatibility between vertices.

## Refining the eligible levels

The subsequent constructions each refine the eligible class by adding
more closure requirements. Since they are all fixed operations on
cardinal functions, and the base class contains an unbounded definable
subclass closed under suprema of strictly increasing countable
sequences, the refinement stays unbounded.

More precisely:

[Closure points exist]
Let (u_i : iin A) be a fixed, set-indexed family of cardinal-valued
functions. Above any prescribed cardinal there is a cardinal
mu of countable cofinality that is a strong limit and satisfies
u_i(theta)<mu for all theta<mu and all iin A.


Proof. 
Define an increasing sequence of cardinals
kappa_0<kappa_1<... by
[

 ;=;
 max(2^{kappa_n}, sup{u_i(theta):iin A, thetalekappa_n})^+.
]

then u_i(theta)lesup{u_i(theta'):theta'lekappa_n}

[Closure refines supply]
Under the eligible-subclass hypothesis, any finite or set-sized list
of closure conditions on levels leaves an unbounded class of levels
satisfying all of them and still containing an unbounded definable
subclass closed under suprema of strictly increasing countable
sequences.


Proof.

## The single-model scheme is a working scheme

Let K be our fixed AEC of Section~sec:reduction, categorical in
unboundedly many cardinals. The single-model scheme has
I=∅ (so there is one vertex, the whole model) and no
distinguished data; arrows are just K-strong maps. Its template is
the E of Theorem~thm:order-template; we may take it as the
template F of the working package.


Let S_0 be the class of categoricity cardinals of K at which the
reversal theorem (Theorem~thm:reversal) holds for every bounded
input size and where the refinement of Lemma~lem:refine-supply
can be applied with all the cardinal functions that occur in the
statements of Theorems~thm:stabilization and
thm:compact-placement. Then S_0 is unbounded, and the
single-model scheme is a working scheme at S_0.


Proof. 
Unboundedness of S_0: reversal itself is eventually true (Theorem
thm:reversal), and S_0 is a union of intervals of categoricity
cardinals above each of the countably many thresholds that appear in
the finitely many lemma statements. Each threshold is fixed once and
for all; the class of cardinals above a fixed threshold that are also
categoricity cardinals and satisfy the countable closure of
Lemma~lem:refine-supply is unbounded because categoricity is
assumed at unboundedly many cardinals and the closure operation
produces new categoricity cardinals by replacing any cardinal in
S_0 with a limit of categoricity cardinals above it.

The working-package clauses.
(1) The structural condition holds by construction (one vertex, no
distinguished data).

(2a)--(2c) are exactly Theorem~thm:order-template: the functor
E has well-ordered presentations at every level, is functorial, and
every K-model at a categoricity cardinal is isomorphic to some
E(I).

(2d) mixed downward closure: given a model mathfrak XfrakX at level
mu and a subset A of size lenu, LS inside mathfrak XfrakX
produces a strong submodel A_0le_Kmathfrak XfrakX containing A,
and we present A_0 as E(J) for a well-ordered J of size nu
using Theorem~thm:order-template and coherence.

(3) the diagram condition is provided by Theorem~thm:stabilization
and Corollary~cor:lift-unique: for sigma-sized bounded lists,
two are D_{K}-equal iff they agree on all sigma-bounded tests,
with the bound beta_K(sigma) supplied by those results. As
b_F(sigma) we take the maximum of sigma and the test costs.

(4) the exhaustion criterion: a componentwise map between
single-model vertices is just a strong map on the whole model. Suppose
f: Mto N agrees with the bounded diagram data on every bounded
list of M. To show f[M]le_K N, use the LS axiom inside N to
enlarge f[M] to a strong mu-submodel N_0le_K N, then note
coherence gives f[M]le_K N_0le_K N because f[M] is
unchanged under N_0le_K N (the inclusion is strong). The
small strong submodel enumeration condition on f[M] uses the LS
axiom on M itself: any bounded subset of f[M] is the image of
some bounded subset of M, which lies in a small strong submodel
of M whose image under f is itself small and strong in N by
the diagram condition. The identity inclusion N_0le_K N then
makes f[M]le_K N.

(5) the isomorphism clause is categoricity: exactly one model per
categoricity cardinal, so any two objects of the single-model scheme
at the same level are isomorphic.



Proposition~prop:base-scheme is the ``use the working package''
statement for our starting AEC. Everything in Sections~sec:calculus
through~sec:boundaries applies to K itself: free extensions
of types, stationary approximations, isolation, all-dominated
extensions, and each new working scheme constructed by adjoining a
dimension.



# The calculus of free extensions

Throughout this section, C is a working scheme in the sense
of Definition~def:working-package, with a fixed template F, its
class S of eligible levels containing a definable unbounded subclass
T closed under suprema of strictly increasing countable sequences, and
all further closure refits stated when used. Bounds always depend only
on fixed scheme data and on bounded input arities.

## Approximation sequences

The first main result of this section is that types over whole models
have free extensions that are compatible with strong maps, and that they
are computed by averaging over a long sequence in the base.

[Approximation sequence]
An approximation sequence for p=tp(v/A) at query size tau
is an indexed family I=(v_i : i<kappa) in A, each v_i having the
same index set as v, such that the concatenation of the family is
bounded and, for every Z⊆ A of size at most tau,
[
 |{i<kappa:D(v_i,Z)ne pmathord{upharpoonright} Z}|
 le |Z|+aleph_0.

]
Here pmathord{upharpoonright} Z denotes its joint diagram. A
sequence is always indexed: different indices may carry the same tuple.
We use the same terminology for all projections of the displayed tuple.
Whenever kappa>|Z|+aleph_0, the unique value assumed outside at
most |Z|+aleph_0 indices is the majority value of the sequence
on Z.


[Uniform approximations]
Let |v|lesigma and taugealeph_0 be bounded. At sufficiently
closed levels there are approximation sequences for p=tp(v/A) at
size tau of every prescribed bounded infinite length kappa. They
satisfy (*) simultaneously for all subtuples of v. The construction
has bounds depending only on sigma,tau,kappa and the fixed scheme.

In particular, taking tau=c(sigma) and kappa=c(sigma)^+, the
concatenated sequence has a uniform bounded length depending only on
sigma and the fixed scheme. Its diagram is a code for p in the
precise transport sense proved in Theorem~thm:free-extension below.


Proof. [Sketch of proof]
Present A on a padded dense order rich enough for every support size
used below (obtainable by enriching the order-template construction of
Theorem~thm:order-template with finitely many extra labels; the
extra labels are fixed once and for all). The presentation is an object
of the current scheme, and no additional categoricity is needed here.
The bounded-placement result (Theorem~thm:compact-placement)
places p correctly at size tau.

Keep every extra supporting position that belongs to the old chart.
Partition the other supporting positions into clusters according to
their strict cut over the entire old order. For every pair of distinct
cuts choose an old position separating them. Include the shared
supporting positions as well. This gives a bounded anchor set of size
at most sigma+aleph_0. Each unshared cluster belongs to a
different strict slot over the anchors.

In each such anchor slot, choose kappa pairwise disjoint convex
regions in the old order; in the ith region, put a copy of the
corresponding cluster with its internal order. The prescribed richness
supplies these patterns; its required arity is bounded in terms of
sigma+kappa. Use the original labels and the unchanged shared
positions to obtain v_iin A.

Choose a query Z with one finite support for each element. If these
supports avoid every region used for the ith copies, then each copied
cluster and the original cluster can differ relative to the query only
by passing whole blocks of parameter positions. There are no hits or
separations inside a cluster. Their anchor slots are disjoint, so
Lemma~lem:parameter-orbits (of Section~sec:diagrams) gives
D(v_i,Z)=pmathord{upharpoonright} Z. Every supporting position of
Z belongs to at most one of the regions, and therefore excludes at
most one index i. Finitarity of supports gives at most
|Z|+aleph_0 excluded indices. The same set of good indices works for
every subtuple of v. Only bounded support patterns, the fixed labels,
rich orders at bounded arities, and the compact-placement bounds were
used. This proves the uniformity assertions.


## Free extension

[Stationary free extension]
Let Ato B be an arrow at a sufficiently closed level, and let
p=tp(v/A) where |v|lesigma. There is a specified actual type
p^B over B, called its free lift, extending p. At every
query size whose approximation and testing costs are bounded at the
originating level, it is computed by transporting sufficiently long
approximations in A. At one level this includes every bounded query
size; in a mixed-level use the costs must remain below the level of
A. It has the following properties.
 The answer is independent of the approximation sequence, its
length, and the auxiliary test size.
 Free lifts commute with restriction of the tuple, are invariant
under isomorphisms, and satisfy (p^B)^C=p^C whenever
Ato Bto C.
 If A has an eligible size nu and B has a larger eligible
size, the construction still works for |v|<nu, with its construction
thresholds and approximation lists below nu.
 A sequence from Lemma~lem:approximations at the fixed
threshold c(sigma) is bounded code data. If an equally indexed
sequence in any sufficiently closed eligible object has the same
diagram, it determines a unique corresponding type there by the same
rule.



Proof. [Sketch of proof]
Well-definedness. Fix tau=c(sigma) large enough that the possibility
set tested below is a set of size le 2^tau. Let
I=(v_i:i<kappa) be an approximation sequence at tau, with
kappa>tau. For each bounded query Z⊆ B, we know the
pattern of D(v_i,Z) as i varies; the majority value is well defined
by (*) and is by construction the intended answer p^Bmathord{upharpoonright} Z.
Formally, move Z into A over the approximation sequence: by bounded
diagram homogeneity, there are cardinality- and order-preserving moves
of each Z-element's support into a support of the sequence, and the
diagram at the moved query Z' is the diagram at Z. Then
D(v_i,Z) agrees with D(v_i,Z') at all but finitely many i, hence
has a majority, which by (*) equals pmathord{upharpoonright} Z' = pmathord{upharpoonright} Z.

For independence of the sequence, take a second I' of the same length
and set kappa>tau; move Z into A over Icup I'; on that
moved query both majorities compute p and hence agree. This proof
requires saturation over the concatenation of the sequences; it does
not require the approximation estimate to hold for a query containing
that concatenation. The averaging queries still have size at most
tau. The same argument compares different query bounds on their
common domain. It also proves coherence under restrictions and
reindexing of queries.

Existence of an actual type. Use tau=c(sigma) and kappa=tau^+.
In a well-ordered chart of B, prescribe the majority answers on
every query of size at most tau. For each single query these are
realized by some v_iin A⊆ B, since fewer than kappa
indices are exceptional. Thus they have local placements.
Lemma~lem:compact-placement (of Section~sec:diagrams)
gives a placement realizing all of them in an order enlargement of B.
This is an actual tuple in an extension object, and the bounded-test
uniqueness of Corollary~cor:lift-unique makes its type unique
once the test size exceeds the stabilisation threshold. This type is
p^B. Its restriction to A equals p by the same testing criterion.

Properties (1)--(4) are now routine from the construction:
independence of the sequence is already shown; restriction and
isomorphism invariance follow by taking sub-sequences and pushing
through the isomorphism; the mixed-level part uses the same rule with
the relevant bounds taken below nu; the code-data property is a
formal consequence of Theorem~thm:stabilization.


## Products and independence

[Free product]
Let Ato B be an arrow at a fixed eligible level. Let p and q be
types over A in disjoint tuples, with free lifts p^B and q^B. We
say that a tuple (x,y) realizes the free product p ⊗  q
over A if x is a realization of p with tp(y/A,x)=q^{A[x]}, where
A[x] is a copy of A extended by x (using the ambient level).


[Products are symmetric, transitive, associative]
In a working scheme, at sufficiently closed levels, the free product
construction has the following properties.
 Base change. If x/B is free from A and x,d have
product type over B, then x and (d,e) have product type over A
for every bounded tuple e from B.
 Symmetry. For bounded tuples x,y of size lesigma
with free lifts over A, p ⊗  q = q ⊗  p as actual types
over A.
 Transitivity. If p,q,r are bounded types over A at
one level, then (p ⊗  q) ⊗  r=p ⊗ (q ⊗  r) and
p ⊗  q⊆(p ⊗  q) ⊗  r with r-marginal = r
over the initial base.
 Associativity. Combined with (3), products of finitely
many factors are well defined up to commuting.



Proof. 
Base change: two realizations of p can be identified over A by
amalgamation. In a common model containing their witnessing extensions,
the free lifts of q agree by stationarity and restriction. A second
type-identifying amalgamation identifies the realizations of q.
This proves independence of the choices.

Symmetry: fix a bounded query Z in A, and put
e=|Z|+|x|+|y|+aleph_0. Choose approximation sequences
(a_i : i<kappa) for p and (b_j : j<lambda) for q sufficient
for queries of size e, where kappa=e^+ and lambda=kappa^+.
All these cardinals lie below a sufficiently closed level.

For the array M_{ij}=D(a_i,b_j,Z), first realize x and freely
realize y: the resulting answer R is the iterated majority in i
commuting with the majority in j. Except at at most e columns,
the column majority is R, and in each such column at most e
entries differ from R. Reversing the realization order gives an
answer S: except at at most e rows, its row majority is S, with
at most e exceptions in each such row. The bounds concern the size
of (x,Z) or (y,Z), not the lengths of the sequences.

There are kappa good rows. Their exceptional columns together number
at most kappa e=kappa<lambda, choose a column outside this set and
outside the at most e bad columns. Every good-row entry in that
column equals S, whereas all but at most e entries equal R. As
kappa>e, some entry has both values. Thus R=S. Testing then gives
symmetry of the joint types.

For transitivity, take the two sequences just used in A and a
sequence K⊆ A approximating the actual old product. Choose
their accuracy at common sufficient threshold. Given a query Z in
the enlarged base B, move it into A over these three whole
sequences. Both successive free evaluations over B are the iterated
majority of the same array D(a_i,b_j,Z). Transitivity permits the
original sequence for the second factor to be used after the first
has been realized. The query movement identifies this array with the
old array in A, where it computes p ⊗  q. The sequence K
evaluates that old product and computes its free lift on the original
query. The answers therefore agree. Testing proves the formula.

Associativity: realize x,y,z successively along a chain of free
lifts from A. The first two form p ⊗  q, and z is free over a
model containing them, so the joint type is (p ⊗  q) ⊗  r.
Over a model containing x, the last two have type
q^B ⊗  r^B=(q ⊗  r)^B by symmetry-transitivity over B,
so the same joint type is p ⊗ (q ⊗  r). This proves
associativity. Projection follows from the projection property of
free lifts in successive realizations. Associativity and binary
symmetry permit every permutation of a finite list of factors.

Base change (second assertion of (1)): use symmetry and a
type-identifying amalgam to realize the given joint type by first
putting d in a model containing B and then realizing x freely
over it from B. Since x/B is free from A, transitivity makes
this last realization free from A. The tuple (d,e) is in the
intervening model. The amalgam identifies the full original tuple
over B, and hence retains its actual joint type over A.


## Local character

[Local character of freeness]
Let (A_i : i<delta) be an increasing chain of objects at one
eligible level mu, whose union A has size mu, and let delta
be a nonzero limit ordinal. If p=tp(v/A) and either v is finite
or |v|<cf(delta), then p is free from some A_i. If, in
addition, every restriction tp(v/A_i) is free from a fixed base
Xle_K A_0, then p is free from X.


Proof. 
Pass to a cofinal chain indexed by the regular cardinal
gamma=cf(delta). Choose a sufficient testing size
chige c(|v|+aleph_0) below mu. If gamma is larger than
the size of an approximation sequence for p at that threshold, the
whole sequence lies in one stage. Its exception estimate is still
correct on all queries in A, so Lemma~lem:average-descent
below, at the same level, applies.

In the other case gamma<mu. Choose a regular kappa<mu above
gamma,chi,|v|, and take kappa approximations inside A. Every
whole approximation tuple belongs to some stage (finiteness, or the
hypothesis |v|<gamma). If every stage contained fewer than kappa
of the tuples, regularity of kappa and gamma<kappa would make
their union contain fewer than kappa tuples. Thus one stage contains
kappa of them. Pass to those indices. The exception estimate
survives unchanged, and Lemma~lem:average-descent again identifies
p as the free lift from that stage.

For the final assertion, local character makes p free from some
A_i. Its restriction there is free from X by hypothesis.
Transitivity in Theorem~thm:free-extension makes p free
from X.


[Average descent]
Let Ato B have eligible sizes nulemu, and let q=tp(d/B) be
actual, with |d|=sigma<nu. Choose chige c(sigma) below nu.
Suppose that a bounded sequence I=(d_i:i<kappa) in A, with
chi+aleph_0<kappa<nu, has majority value qmathord{upharpoonright} Z
on every query Z⊆ B of size at most chi, with at most
|Z|+aleph_0 exceptions. Then q is the free lift of its actual
nu-restriction to A.


Proof. [Sketch of proof]
Apply Theorem~thm:free-extension at the smaller level nu, then
push forward along Ato B; the majority condition is exactly the
hypothesis that checks the "code" clause of the free lift. Uniqueness
is by clause (4) of Theorem~thm:free-extension.



## Universal strips

For the marked-increment construction we need to be able to enlarge a
base model so that every same-level extension of it embeds inside the
enlargement, with the enlargement differing from the base only in a
bounded way.

[Universality of a fresh strip]
Let C be a working scheme with template F. In addition to
F, use a presentation-template G with the following properties: it
has a fixed set of finite-support labels; every G(J) used below, for
an order of size mu, is an object of C at level mu;
order embeddings give arrows; the diagrams of its term lists are
natural, including in the order enlargements and padded orders used
below. Require mu to exceed the cardinality of the label set of G
and to be closed under the bounds below with that cardinal as input.
Put A=G(J). Insert into J a fresh convex strip V of size mu
with countably many disjoint convex sub-strips, each of size mu, and
denote the resulting object A^+=G(J+V). Then every same-level
extension of A embeds into A^+ over A. In particular this applies
to G=F and to well-ordered presentations.


Proof. [Sketch of proof]
A same-level extension Ale_K B of A is itself at level mu, so
by the categorical clause of the working package it is isomorphic to
G(J') for some order J' of size mu. Write J' as
Jsqcup K (a disjoint union of orders refining a common prefix,
which is possible by renaming); we identify A⊆ B with
G(J)⊆ G(J').

Realize a bounded type p(v/A) in one fresh sub-strip V_0 of A^+.
Choose a testing threshold chige c(|v|+aleph_0) large enough for
|v| and the labels of G. Take a sequence I in A approximating
p at query size chi. Represent its entries by G-terms, and
collect their old supports in S_0⊆ J. Apply support closure
(Lemma~lem:support-closure, below) with the side of V_0
recorded, to obtain a bounded S⊆ J with the property that
any query of at most chi old positions can be moved into S over
any subset of S of size at most chi. Include the approximation
supports as the fixed set in that lemma.

Let P_S be the set of all G-terms on S. It is bounded since G
has a bounded set of finite-support labels. The natural parameter
diagram of P_S is the same in A and in G(Scup V_0). The latter
is a level object because |V_0|=mu. Saturation there realizes the
desired whole joint diagram of (v,P_S), giving a tuple w whose
supporting positions belong to Scup V_0. Only at most
|v|+aleph_0lechi of its supporting positions are old; call them
R⊆ S. Move every bounded old query into S over R and use
uniform testing at chi. The diagram of w over the moved query
equals the diagram of v over the original query. We have therefore
realized p inside G(Scup V_0)⊆ A^+, fixing A pointwise.

Iterate over an enumeration of the bounded tuple types over A (there
are at most 2^{|A|} of them; but for our purposes only the ones
realized in B matter, of which there are at most mu). Each is
realized in a distinct fresh sub-strip V_n; disjointness of the
sub-strips makes the realizations independent over A. After
mu steps, the resulting object contains a copy of B over A,
by induction on the well-ordered enumeration of bounded types realized
in B. Since the working package's categorical clause identifies all
level-mu objects, this is the desired embedding.


## Approximate retractions

[Approximate retractions]
Let Ale_K B have eligible cardinality mu of countable
cofinality. There is a family of arrows
r_j: Bto A (j<mu) such that, for every ain A,
the set
[
 {j<mu: r_j(a)ne a}
]
is finite.


Proof. [Sketch of proof]
Present A=G(J) and, using Proposition~prop:universal-strip,
embed B over A into A^+=G(J+V) where V is a fresh strip of size
mu. Enumerate a family of disjoint sub-strips of V by ordinals
j<mu. For each j<mu define r_j: A^+to A as follows: a
G-term whose V-support lies in the j-th sub-strip is mapped to
the corresponding G-term on a fixed fresh initial segment of A that
encodes that sub-strip's position; a G-term with support in J stays
fixed. This is a G-term substitution, hence a C-arrowcalC-arrow
(working-package clauses (2a)--(2b)). Pulling back along
B⊆ A^+ gives the arrow r_j: Bto A.

Every ain A has a finite-support G(J)-representation with empty
V-part, so r_j(a)=a for every j; the failure set
{j<mu: r_j(a)!= a} is empty. This is the form in which the
corollary is used: for any bounded Z⊆ A, all but
|Z|+aleph_0 of the r_j fix Z pointwise.

The genuine content is in the images r_j(b) for bin B∖ A:
if b has G-term representation with finite V-support, then all the
r_j(b) lie in a common bounded submodel of A (essentially
G-terms on J plus the V-support reindexed into the fixed initial
segment of A). The downward-transfer argument of
Section~sub:downward-transfer averages over this bounded list.


## Support closure

[Support closure]
Let chi be a bounded infinite cardinal. Let J be a rich dense
linear order (as supplied by the order template), S_0⊆ J a
bounded subset, and T^*⊆ J a further fixed set with
|S_0|+|T^*|<mu. Let V be a fresh convex strip of size mu
disjoint from J. Then there is a bounded S⊆ J with
S_0⊆ S such that, for every subset P⊆ S of size at
most chi and every finite tuple in Jcup V, there is a tuple in
Scup V with the same order/equality diagram over P that fixes
T^* pointwise.


Proof. [Sketch of proof]
Iterate the enrichment from Lemma~lem:ordinal-support-closure;
because |S_0|+|T^*|<mu and mu has countable cofinality with
sufficiently large closure, the resulting union is bounded in J and
exhibits the required matching. Positions that lie in V are kept
where they are (not moved into S), and the order/equality pattern is
preserved because V is convex and disjoint from J.


# Isolation and dominated extensions

Throughout this section, C is again a working scheme in the
sense of Definition~def:working-package, with the refinement
process of Section~sub:refining applied. A tuple is
bounded at a level mu if its cardinality is less than mu.

Our goal is a strong statement about extending models: we want
extensions Ale_K B in which every bounded tuple of B is
controlled by the pair (A,b), where b is a fixed mark.
Controls means that the joint type of (b,c) over a small fragment
of A determines the joint type over all of A. Such extensions are
all-dominated; they play the role of (mild) prime hulls.

## Free isolation

[Free isolation]
Let Ale_K B be an arrow, b a bounded tuple in B, and q a
bounded type in the free lift from B (so q^B is a stationarily
determined type over B). If q is isolated relative to b
over A, then q^B is isolated relative to b over B with an
isolation radius controlled by ell, sigma, and fixed scheme
data.


Proof. [Sketch of proof]
The isolation of q relative to b over A supplies a bounded
fragment psi_A(v,b) (|v| the block variable of q) that entails
q. By stationarity of the free lift and preservation of diagrams
under arrows, the same formula psi_A applied at B (with b's
image) entails q^B. Indeed: any two realizations of q^B over B
restrict to two realizations of q over A (up to the free lift
construction: q^B's restriction to A equals q). By isolation of
q over A, those restrictions are A-equivalent; by stationarity
of free lifts (Theorem~thm:free-extension), they are then
B-equivalent. Uniqueness gives isolation.

For the bound on the isolation radius: use uniform testing at
c(|q|+ell) to detect any failure of the entailed formula at size
ge c(|q|+ell); the resulting witness size depends only on
fixed scheme data.


## All-dominated extensions

[All-dominated]
Let Xle_K Y be same-level objects, and let b be a bounded tuple
in Y. We say Y is all-dominated over X+b if, for every
bounded tuple c from Y, the type tp(b,c/X) is isolated
relative to b.


The definition quantifies over all bounded c, not merely over some
fixed enumeration of Y's bounded tuples. This is what makes
all-domination stable under the constructions of Section~sec:marked.

[Existence]
At sufficiently closed levels, for every model X of C and
every bounded tuple b over X realizing a fixed bounded type, there
is a same-level extension Xle_K Y with Y all-dominated over
X+b.


Proof. [Sketch of proof]
Build a countable increasing chain X=X_0le_K X_1le_K... of
same-level extensions of X, together with a single fixed mark
bin X_1, so that at every stage n:
 for each bounded tuple c enumerated at stage n, a fixed
  bounded substructure Z⊆ X_n isolates tp(b,c/X_n)
  relative to b over X_{n-1};


An all-dominated object exists as the direct limit of the X_n with
mark b. The bound on witness size at
each stage is supplied by Lemma~lem:free-isolation and uniform
testing. Unions at limits give the common-upper-bound clause.


[All-dominated extension exists]
Let b be a bounded tuple over a base X at a sufficiently closed
eligible level. There is a model Yge_K X at the same level, all
dominated over X+b. Moreover, b realizes any prescribed bounded
free type over X, and the construction can be made at every bounded
arity simultaneously with a single extension.


Proof. [Sketch of proof]
The marked-increment construction (Section~sec:marked) will
provide this systematically, but the all-dominated existence we need
here is more elementary. Enumerate all bounded tuples in a formal
universe X^*⊇ X constructed as a countably long union of
small extensions of X, adding at each stage a witness for the
isolation of the next bounded tuple with b. Direct-limit closure under
countable chains keeps the result at size mu (the level). Isolation
of (b,c) at each bounded c over the resulting union follows from
continuity of types at limits (Proposition~prop:local-character)
together with the fact that a fragment of each X_n isolates it over
X.


## Prime extensions

[Prime]
Let Ale_K B be same-level, and let b be a bounded tuple in B
realizing a prescribed type p over A. We say (A,B,b) is
prime over (A,p) if, for every Ale_K C with b'in C
realizing p and C all-dominated over A+b', there is a bounded
A' with Ale A'le B containing b, and a strong map
(A',b)to(C,b') over A.


Informally, a prime extension is ``smallest'' among all-dominated
extensions.

[Existence of prime extensions]
At sufficiently closed levels, for every prescribed bounded type p
over X there exist same-level Xle_K Y, bin Y realizing p,
with (X,Y,b) prime over (X,p) and Y all-dominated over X+b.


Proof. [Sketch of proof]
Build Y as a countable chain of small extensions of X that
successively embed every bounded piece of any prescribed
all-dominated extension. This is a back-and-forth: enumerate a target
all-dominated pair (X,C,b'), and at stage n extend
X_nle_K X_{n+1} so that the n-th bounded tuple of C has a copy
in X_{n+1} matching the corresponding diagram. Bounded diagram
homogeneity plus uniformity of isolation give the existence of each
copy. Direct-limit closure keeps the result at level mu.


[Uniqueness of prime]
Any two prime all-dominated extensions of X realizing p are
isomorphic over X.


Proof. [Sketch of proof]
Bounded back-and-forth: each prime extension embeds into the other
over X. Composition of the two embeddings is an automorphism of
the first fixing X pointwise.



# The marked-increment construction

Throughout, C is a working scheme whose arrows are precisely
the componentwise strong embeddings preserving its specified maps and
distinguished data. In particular, there is no additional condition on
intersections or on bases. We use the working package of
Definition~def:working-package, together with the calculus and
isolation results of the preceding two sections, at sufficiently closed
eligible levels. All refinements in this section have fixed set
parameters: the input scheme and template, the prescription below, and,
after it is chosen, the new template. An unbounded output class is
asserted under the eligible-subclass hypothesis of
Convention~conv:eligible-subclass.

The bare idea: from C we form a category C^b of
marked pairs (X,Y,b) in which b is a single bounded mark in
an all-dominated extension Y of X. The purpose is to make the
mark's type ``stabilized up to the whole system'': a bounded piece of
Y is controlled by a bounded piece of X together with b. We show
that C^b is itself a working scheme, with all the
consequences.

## Stationary prescriptions

[Stationary finite prescription]
A stationary finite prescription for C assigns to
every object X a type p_X of a bounded tuple, with fixed component
sorts. The assignment commutes with isomorphisms, and for every arrow
Xto X', including one between different eligible levels, p_{X'} is
the free lift p_X^{X'} in the sense of
Theorem~thm:free-extension, after identifying the base by that
arrow. The prescription is given by a fixed set code of the sort used
in the applications below.


[Marked pair]
An object of C^b at level mu is a triple
[
 (X,Y,b), Xle Y,
]
where X, Y are objects of C at level mu, bin Y
realizes p_X, and Y is all-dominated over X+b in the sense of
Definition~def:all-dominated. The inclusion Xle Y and the mark
b are part of the structure.

An arrow (X,Y,b)to(X',Y',b') is a commuting pair
(f,g) of componentwise strong C-embeddings,calC-embeddings, with g
taking the mark to the mark: g(b)=b'. The notation Xle Y records
an actual inclusion; arbitrary arrows are put into this form by
replacing the codomain with an isomorphic copy.


Recall that all-domination quantifies over every bounded tuple
of Y, not just the members of one fixed exhaustion. This is the
property that makes C^b carry the working package.

## Downward transfer of isolation

Our first task: make the pair isomorphism test work. If a pair
(X,Y,b) is a marked object and X_Hle X is a smaller base, an
isomorphism test for X versus X_H must compare pairs with different
base sizes. The following lemma is the engine for descending isolation
from a larger base X to a smaller one X_H.

[Downward transfer of isolation]
Let X_Hle Xle U be same-level objects, with X and X_H both of
eligible cardinality mu at a sufficiently closed level. Let b, c
be bounded tuples in U with |b|+|c|<mu. Suppose
tp(b,c/X) is isolated relative to b, and its isolating fragment
uses at most h(|b|+|c|) parameters from X for a fixed scheme
function h. Then tp(b,c/X_H) is isolated relative to b, with
isolating fragment of size at most h(|b|+|c|)+aleph_0, provided the
level is sufficiently closed under h and under the retraction index
bounds of Corollary~cor:retractions.


Proof. [Sketch of proof]
Let psi(w,v,s) isolate tp(b,c/X), with s⊆ X a bounded
parameter list. Choose from Corollary~cor:retractions a set of
kappa retractions r_j: Xto X_H, with chi<|s| and
chi+aleph_0<kappa<mu, such that all but at most
|S|+aleph_0 of the r_j fix any given bounded S⊆ X_H
pointwise. The images r_j(s) for j<kappa are a bounded list in
X_H.

Define s^* to be the majority image: the bounded list of tuples in
X_H realizing the majority value of {r_j(s):j<kappa} on each
bounded test. For at most |s|+aleph_0 indices j, the diagram of
r_j(s) differs from s^*; for all others r_j preserves the atomic
diagram on scup{b,c} (since r_j is a C-arrowcalC-arrow and hence
preserves atomic diagrams). Thus for all but at most |s|+aleph_0
indices j, the substitution s|-> s^* is compatible with the
isolating formula psi: psi(w,b,c,s)iffpsi(w,b,c,s^*) for
the relevant win{b,c}.

It follows that psi(w,b,c,s^*) isolates tp(b,c/X_H) relative to
b: any two realizations c_1, c_2 in the same level with
psi(b,c_1,s^*) and psi(b,c_2,s^*) realizable inside U agree on
their diagram over X_H, because the majority move transports the
isolation of c_1, c_2 over X down to X_H.


## The pair isomorphism test

[Pair isomorphism test]
After a further closure refinement, marked pairs exist over every
object X of C. An isomorphism e: Xcong X' of
bases extends to an isomorphism (X,Y,b)~=(X',Y',b'). More
precisely, for bounded tuples t and t' in the respective pair
structures, such an extension can take t to t' iff e takes the
indicated base entries to their counterparts and transports
tp(t,b/X) to tp(t',b'/X').

Same-level arrows preserve bounded pair orbits. Moreover, comparisons
of bounded pair tuples are preserved and reflected when two pairs at
one common small level are mapped to two pairs at a common larger
eligible level. All tuple lengths and bounds needed for the comparison
are strictly below the smaller level.


Proof. [Sketch of proof]
Existence is Theorem~thm:all-lengths applied to the finite
prescription (existence of all-dominated extensions for a prescribed
type). The pointed uniqueness assertion follows from
Proposition~iso:prime: its back-and-forth form retains prescribed
bounded tuples with the same type together with the mark. Necessity of
the displayed type condition follows from invariance.

For sufficiency at one level, suppose that
tp(t,b/X)=tp(t',b'/X') after transport by e. The base
isomorphism e transports the prescribed free type p_X=p_{X'}. By
Lemma~lem:free-isolation, the free extension of tp(t,b/X) to
Y is exactly tp(t,b/Y) (first alternative of the free isolation
lemma applied to the base Y). Similarly for the primed copy.
Since Y and Y' are both all-dominated over their respective bases
with the same prescribed free type at the mark, standard extension
arguments (bounded back-and-forth) produce the required isomorphism
of pairs.

The mixed-level version, comparing pairs at a small level and their
images at a large one, mirrors the argument for the single-model
scheme, using clause (3) of Theorem~thm:free-extension and
Proposition~lem:mixed-levels.


## The marked template

The pair isomorphism test above is where all-domination is essential:
without it, the free lift of a bounded tuple need not be determined by
its free lift over the base, since the extension could add structure
that constrains the tuple's type. With all-domination, the mark's
type is enough.

We must now verify that marked pairs do form a working scheme: that
there is a template of finite-support terms with the required
naturality, downward closure, and exhaustion properties.

[Sample pair exists]
At further sufficiently closed levels there exist marked pairs at level
mu for every prescribed finite type p. Moreover, the sample pair
(X_H,Y_H,b) can be presented on three padded dense linear orders
J_H, Z_H, W_H with X_H=F(J_H), Y_Hcong F(Z_H), and a universal
strip W_H factoring Y_H as X_H-embedded into F(J_H+W_H), all
at level mu.


Proof. [Sketch of proof]
Use Theorem~thm:all-lengths to build an all-dominated extension
X'le Y' at level mu with bin Y' realizing p. Present X'
as F(J_H) for a padded dense J_H of size mu (working-package
clause (2c)), and Y' as F(Z_H) for a well-ordered Z_H via clause
(2c). The universal-strip representation Y_Hcong F(J_H+W_H) embeds
Y' over X' into F(J_H+W_H), where W_H is a fresh strip of size
mu with countably many disjoint sub-strips: this is
Proposition~prop:universal-strip. Pick a sub-strip to hold a copy
of b, and label it as the mark.


## Chain closure

[Chain closure for marked pairs]
At further sufficiently closed levels, C^b has unions of
increasing chains of total size equal to the level. The union is a
marked pair. The common-upper-bound clause holds as well, including
into an eligible larger level.


Proof. [Sketch of proof]
Fix a level mu closed under the bounds of Lemma~lem:sample-pair
and the marked pair isomorphism test, with cofinally many smaller
levels at which those results hold. We do not require pair-chain
closure at the smaller levels.

Write the chain, replacing arrows by inclusions, as
(C_i,T_i,b) for i<alpha, and let C,T be the component unions.
The underlying scheme gives Cle T and the union maps. The actual
marginal of the mark over C is p_C: the marginals at proper stages
are the prescribed free extensions, and
Proposition~local-character gives stationary continuity for this
finite tuple.

Induct on the chain length. The shorter-length assertion allows
intermediate limit unions to be inserted. Restricting cofinally, we
may consider a continuous chain indexed by a regular cardinal delta.
If a bounded tuple din T is contained in some T_i, its type with
b is isolated over C_i; free isolation gives isolation over C.
For d not in any single T_i, use downward transfer
(Lemma~lem:downward-transfer) to descend the corresponding
isolation from a larger base with bounded parameters to a smaller one.
The bound on `smaller levels` at which these results hold is below
mu by closure.

With both cases handled, the union T over C is all-dominated; the
common-upper-bound clause for C gives the required union
map into any eligible common upper bound.


## The marked-increment working package

[Marked increments are working schemes]
Let C be a working scheme with componentwise arrows and a
stationary finite prescription p as in
Definition~def:stationary-prescription. Assume the
eligible-subclass hypothesis of Convention~conv:eligible-subclass.
On an unbounded further class of sufficiently closed eligible levels,
C^b is again a working scheme, with componentwise arrows,
a fixed template F^b, and mixed arrows between these levels. The
output class retains the eligible-subclass hypothesis.


Proof. 
Lemma~lem:pair-isomorphism gives the isomorphism test, orbit
preservation, and mixed reflection.
Lemma~lem:sample-pair gives individual template values and their
mixed arrows, mixed downward L"owenheim--Skolem, natural bounded
diagrams and saturation, and the exhaustive-list arrow criterion.
Lemma~lem:chain-closure supplies the missing union-object and
upper-bound assertions. Composition and coherence are componentwise.

The extraction added only finitary chart, representation, retraction,
and witness symbols with a fixed set bound. Further bounds were testing
and placement costs for the old template and the fixed two-part layout
of the new template, isolation and approximation costs, and a
refinement permitting cofinally many smaller template-valid levels.
These are fixed-parameter operations in the closure bookkeeping. No
step requires small-pair chain closure before it is proved.
