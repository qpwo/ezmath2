# Midpoint lenses in segment spaces


For a real segment-forest dual with unbounded finite component heights
and the real infinite-height coordinate predual, we bound the tail of
an arbitrary displacement in a symmetric lens by 2√(R^2-|x|^2).
Here R is the lens radius and x is the center, which we take finitely
supported. Both given norms are asymptotically midpoint uniformly convex,
and neither space admits an equivalent asymptotically uniformly convex
norm. The finite-height forest dual is reflexive.


# What a midpoint lens measures

## Norms and Banach spaces
A real normed vector space is a set Y whose elements can be
added and multiplied by real scalars, together with a norm
x|->norm{x} obeying
[
 norm{x}=0 ⇔ x=0,
 norm{lambda x}=|lambda|norm{x},
 norm{x+y}lenorm{x}+norm{y}.
]
Every scalar in this paper is real. The model example is R^n with
the Euclidean norm
norm{(x_1,...,x_n)}=(x_1^2+...+x_n^2)^{1/2}.
A Banach space is a real normed vector space in which every
Cauchy sequence has a limit inside Y: no holes, nothing missing.
All spaces in this paper are real Banach spaces.

For a scalar R>0, the closed ball of radius R is
RB={yin Y:norm{y}le R}, and the unit sphere is
S={y:norm{y}=1}. Balls are convex: if x,yin RB and
0le tle1, then tx+(1-t)yin RB.

## The lens
A midpoint lens is what remains of the ball when we impose two
symmetric constraints at once. Pick a center xin Y and a radius
R>0. The lens at (x,R) is

 L(x,R)L(x,R) = bigl{yin Y :
                    norm{x+y}le R,
                     norm{x-y}le Rbigr}.

The vector x+y is ``x pushed up by y'', and x-y is ``x
pushed down by y''. The lens consists of displacements that keep
both pushed vectors inside the ball of radius R. In the plane, with
Y=R^2, the lens is the intersection of two closed disks of radius
R, one centered at x and one centered at -x.

How thick can a lens be near the boundary? If x already sits inside
RB but near the sphere, and R is only slightly larger, the lens is
a thin shell around 0. Every question in this paper has the shape:
how small is y as a function of the squared-radius deficit
R^2-norm{x^2?}

## The trivial case: Hilbert space
A Hilbert space is a Banach space whose norm comes from an
inner product, as in R^n with the Euclidean norm. The
parallelogram identity says

 norm{x+y}^2+norm{x-y}^2 = 2norm{x}^2 + 2norm{y}^2
 (x,yin Y).

Suppose yinL(x,R).L(x,R). Adding the two ineualities
norm{x+y}^2le R^2 and norm{x-y}^2le R^2, substituting in
(eq:parallelogram), and dividing by two gives
[
 norm{x}^2+norm{y}^2le R^2,
 norm{y}^2le R^2-norm{x}^2.
]
In Hilbert space the lens~(eq:lens) is a ball of radius
sqrt{R^2-norm{x}^2}, and every displacement is that small. The
quantity R^2-norm{x}^2 is the squared-radius deficit of the
center: how far the square of the center length sits below the square
of the lens radius.

In our spaces the situation changes because the norm is not Euclidean:
the parallelogram identity fails, and the pair of lens ineualities no
longer pins down norm{y} exactly. A bound of the same shape survives
in projection, however, once we project away a finite coordinate head.

## What this paper proves
Two closely related spaces appear throughout, and the same main
ineuality holds for both.

The first is a real Banach space X=J^*, the dual of a specific
tree-number space J whose norm is built from sums over disjoint
segments. It is reflexive (X^{**}=X: the double dual
recovers the original space) and separable (a countable dense
subset exists). The model to keep in mind is a Hilbert space ell_2
renormed so that long chains are more expensive than spread-out mass.

The second is
B_∞ ⊊ J_∞^*, the closed coordinate span inside
the dual of the same construction on a single infinite rooted tree
T_∞=N^{<omega} of finite words. Here the closure of the
coordinate span is strictly smaller than the full dual; this is the
point of the distinction.

For each space, H denotes a finite ancestral set: a finite
set of vertices that contains every ancestor of each of its members.
P_H projects to the coordinates indexed by H, and Q_H=I-P_H is
the complementary projection.

[Main lens bound]
Let Y be X or B_∞. Let H be a finite ancestral set, and let
x,yin Y satisfy P_Hx=x. For every Rge0 such that
norm{x+y}le R and norm{x-y}le R,

 norm{Q_Hy}le 2sqrt{R^2-norm{x}^2}.



Heads and radii are chosen for each x and each R once; a single H
then controls all displacements y of the lens at (x,R)
simultaneously. That uniformity is the central feature. It makes the
estimate compatible with the cofinite infimum that appears in the
midpoint modulus, defined shortly.

## Midpoint moduli
For t>0, an infinite dimensional Banach space Y, and the family of
closed linear subspaces of finite codimension, the
averaged asymptotic midpoint modulus is

 deltahat_Y(t)
 = inf_{substack{xin Y norm{x}=1}}
   sup_{F⊆ Y  closed,  dim Y/F<∞}
   inf_{substack{yin Fnorm{y}=1}}
   (
    frac{norm{x+ty}+norm{x-ty}}{2}-1
   ).

The inner expression is the excess of the average pair-length over 1.
The space Y is asymptotically midpoint uniformly convex
(or AMUC) when deltahat_Y(t)>0 for every t>0. Intuition:
no matter which unit directions you probe, a fixed proportional step
t cannot leave both x+ty and x-ty on the spherical cap. Finite-
codimensional head coordinates can be killed, but the remaining tail
dilutes the average. So the excess is uniformly positive.

Similarly define the maximum-based modulus

 overlinetheta_Y(t)
 = inf_{norm{x}=1}
   sup_F
   inf_{norm{y}=1 , yin F}
   max{norm{x+ty},norm{x-ty}}-1.

The two moduli are qualitatively equivalent:
frac12overlinetheta_Y(t)ledeltahat_Y(t)leoverlinetheta_Y(t).
The right ineuality is pointwise (average le maximum). For the left,
fix a unit center x and a norming functional fin Y^* with
norm{f}=1 and f(x)=1. Intersect any cofinite F with ker f.
Both endpoints then satisfy f(xpm y)ge1-norm{y}, so norm{xpm y}ge1,
and an excess a in the maximum becomes at least a/2 in the average.

The two conventions agree qualitatively. They differ numerically: a
bound derived for the average typically has a cleaner constant than the
same bound converted to the maximum.

# Vocabulary from analysis

## Duals and weak nullity
Let Y be a real Banach space. A bounded linear functional on
Y is a linear map f:YtoR whose operator norm
[
 norm{f}=sup_{norm{y}le1}|f(y)|
]
is finite. The dual space Y^* is the Banach space of all such
f. A functional fin Y^* is norming for a nonzero xin Y
when norm{f}=1 and f(x)=norm{x}. The Hahn--Banach theorem
guarantees a norming functional for every xne0; uniqueness
generally fails and is not needed.

Each yin Y defines a functional on Y^* by evaluation
f|-> f(y). This is a canonical linear isometric injection
Y↪ Y^{**}, and Y is reflexive when this map
is onto. Reflexivity allows us to recover the norm of any y by
testing against functionals fin Y^* with norm{f}le1.

A cofinite subspace of Y is a closed linear subspace of finite
codimension. Equivalently, F is the set of common solutions of
finitely many bounded linear equations f_1(y)=...=f_k(y)=0. This
is the only kind of restriction we ever impose: forbid a finite set of
linear observations, and no more.

A sequence (y_j)subset Y is weakly null if f(y_j)to0 for
every fin Y^*. A weak null sequence can still avoid the origin in
norm; it merely leaves the stage through every individual observation.
For example, if (e_j) is an orthonormal basis of a Hilbert space,
then (e_j) is weakly null even though norm{e_j}equiv1.

## ell_2 direct sums
For real Banach spaces A and B, the ell_2 direct sum is
[
 A ⊕ _2 B = {(a,b):ain A, bin B},
 norm{(a,b)} = (norm{a}^2+norm{b}^2)^{1/2}.
]
For a countable family (A_i)_{iin I},
[
 (⊕_{iin I}A_i)_{ell_2}
 = Bigl{(a_i)_{iin I} : textstylesum_inorm{a_i}^2<∞Bigr},

 norm{(a_i)} = (sum_inorm{a_i}^2)^{1/2}.
]
Duality passes through:
(⊕_i A_i)^*=(⊕_i A_i^*)_{ell_2}, and reflexivity of
the summands passes up to reflexivity of the sum.

## The one-sided AUC modulus
For a norm N on a Banach space Z, define the one-sided
asymptotic modulus

 overlinedelta_N(t)
 = inf_{substack{zin Z N(z)=1}}
   sup_{substack{F⊆ Z cofinite}}
   inf_{substack{win F N(w)=1}}
   (N(z+tw)-1), t>0.

The infimum over the unit center z and cofinite escape F asks for
the worst-case growth of a single pushed-up vector z+tw. A norm N
is asymptotically uniformly convex (AUC) when
overlinedelta_N(t)>0 for every t>0. This is strictly stronger
than AMUC: only z+tw is tested, never z-tw. Section~sec:obstruction
exhibits a family of unit root-path vectors and unit child increments,
showing that no equivalent renorming of X or B_∞ can be AUC.

# Tree spaces and their segment norms

## Rooted trees of words
For an integer hge1, let
[
 T_h=N^{le h}
 ={finite words in 1,2,3,... of length le h}
]
be the rooted tree whose root is the empty word o and whose edges
join each word s to s^frown j=sj. A vertex is a word;
its length is the number of letters in it. The ancestors
of a word v are the prefixes of v, including v itself; every
vertex has countably many children s^frown1,s^frown2,...,
and the deepest vertices of T_h have length h. The tree T_h has
height h: no branch is longer than h edges. Let
T_∞=N^{<omega} be the same tree without a height bound.

A finite forest is a disjoint union of components, one for each
height:
[
 F={hge1}({h}times T_h).
]
Vertex (h,s) of F lives in component h and sits at word
s; components of higher h are taller. So F has
countably many components and the component heights are unbounded.

Our first target space is attached to F; our second is
attached to T_∞ alone.

## Segments and disjointness
A segment of a rooted tree is a set
[a,b]={v : apreceq vpreceq b} for an ancestor a and a
descendant b of a, both inclusive. Equivalently, a segment is a
vertical chain in the tree with a top and a bottom. In T_1, every
segment is a singleton or a two-vertex path; in a very tall component,
a segment can be a long chain. Segments are confined to one component
of F; they never cross from {h}times T_h to
{h'}times T_{h'} when hne h'.

Two segments are disjoint if the sets of vertices are disjoint.
Because each segment is a chain, disjoint segments typically live in
incomparable branches of the tree, or in different components.

## The segment norm
Let u:F be a function of finite support (zero for all
but finitely many vertices). For a segment S, the segment sum
is
[
 u(S)=sum_{vin S}u(v).
]
The segment norm of u is

 rho(u) =
 sup_{substack{S_1,...,S_m disjoint segmentsmge1}}
 (sum_{i=1}^m u(S_i)^2)^{1/2}.

The supremum is over all finite families of pairwise disjoint segments.
Using single-vertex segments S={v}, we get
max_v|u(v)|lerho(u); on the other hand, decomposing a family of
disjoint segments into its individual vertices shows
rho(u)lesum_v|u(v)|. So rho is squeezed between the
ell_∞ and ell_1 norms: it is a genuine norm.

Example: a solid chain. Suppose u is supported on one long
segment S and is identically one on it. Any family of disjoint
subsegments of S has total length at most |S|, and the sum of
their squares is largest when the family is S alone, so
rho(u)=|S|, the length of S. A long unbroken chain is therefore
very costly under rho.

Example: an antichain. Suppose u is supported on an antichain
A (a set of vertices no two of which are comparable). Every segment
meets A in at most one vertex, so any disjoint family picks out at
most one u(v) from A per segment. Taking all singletons gives
rho(u)=(sum_{vin A}u(v)^2)^{1/2}. On an antichain,
the segment norm collapses to the ordinary Euclidean norm. This is why
the space below is Hilbert-like when it is spread out over many
independent branches.

## The primal space J and its dual X
Take the completion of the finitely supported functions under rho;
call the result J. Its dual
[
 X=J^* = {bounded linear functionals on J}
]
is our first target space. On T_∞, the same construction defines
the primal space J_∞. The two cases use the same notation when
there is no ambiguity; we simply say ``J'' for either.

For future use, note the coefficient ineuality

 Bigl|sum_i a_i d_{S_i}Bigr|_{J^*}
 le (sum_i a_i^2)^{1/2}
 (S_i pairwise disjoint),

where d_S=sum_{vin S}e_v is the segment functional
associated with S. This is the immediate consequence of
(eq:segment-norm): for u with rho(u)le1,
[
 |sum_i a_i u(S_i)|
 le (sum_i a_i^2)^{1/2}(sum_i u(S_i)^2)^{1/2}
 le (sum_i a_i^2)^{1/2}.
]

## The infinite-height coordinate predual B_∞
On T_∞, define
[
 B_∞
 = spanbar{e_v^* : vin T_∞}
 ⊆ J_∞^*,
]
where the closure is in J_∞^* and e_v^*:u|-> u(v) is the
evaluation functional at vertex v. Each e_v^* has norm one
(testing against e_v). This closed coordinate span is strictly
smaller than J_∞^*. Indeed fix an infinite branch
beta⊆ T_∞; the finitely supported sum
f_beta(u)=sum_{vinbeta}u(v) extends by continuity to a norm-one
element of J_∞^*, and f_beta has distance 1 from
B_∞: for any yin B_∞ and any vertex vinbeta beyond
y's support, norm{f_beta-y}ge|f_beta(e_v)-y(e_v)|=1.

So B_∞ differs from X in two ways: its primal is the single
infinite tree, and its target is squeezed to a coordinate span inside
the full dual.

## Basic bounds and standing assumptions
For every xin X or xin B_∞ and every finitely supported
u,

 norm{x}_∞lenorm{x}lenorm{x}_2,
 rho(u)_∞lerho(u)lerho(u)_1.

A single coordinate is never larger than the norm, and the Euclidean
coefficient norm dominates it. On the primal side rho sits between
the ell_∞ and ell_1 norms. On an antichain all four
comparisons are equalities.

Standing assumptions. From now on:
 ``Either space'' means X=J^* or B_∞; both are real.
 ``J'' means J or J_∞.
 ``A vertex v'' is an element of F or of T_∞.
 ``A finite ancestral set H'' is a finite set of vertices that
      contains every ancestor of each of its members.

Every subsequent theorem explicitly names which of the two spaces it
concerns, because some of the proofs use reflexivity of X whereas
B_∞ requires the coordinate-span formulation.

# Coordinate structure of the forest

## Ancestral heads and projections
A finite set H of vertices is ancestral if whenever vin H
and upreceq v, one also has uin H. Intuition: to include a
vertex, one must include all of its ancestors up to the root. On the
forest, roots are the bottom vertices of components; on
T_∞, the root is the empty word o.

For a finite ancestral set H, define
[
 P_H:Yto Y, P_Hx=sum_{vin H}x(e_v)e_v^*,
 Q_H=I-P_H.
]
The projection P_H records the coordinates at H; Q_H discards
them. We call Q_Hy the tail of y outside H.

[Contraction]
On either space Y=X or Y=B_∞, and for any finite ancestral
H, both P_H and Q_H are bounded linear operators of norm at
most one.


Proof.
A segment of the tree meets H in a (possibly empty) prefix and
F∖F∖ H in a (possibly empty) suffix. If
S_1,...,S_m are disjoint segments, then their intersections
S_icap H remain disjoint, and u(S_icap H) is the segment sum of
P_Hu on that intersection. Applying the defining
supremum~(eq:segment-norm) to P_Hu therefore gives
rho(P_Hu)lerho(u). Extending by density to J and passing to
adjoints on the duals X and B_∞ proves the bound for P_H.
For Q_H, the same argument applies to the complements
S_i∖ H, which are also disjoint segments (possibly empty).


[Coordinate density]
Finitely supported vectors are norm-dense in X and in B_∞.
Along any increasing sequence of finite ancestral sets
H_1⊆ H_2⊆... whose union is F or
T_∞, one has P_{H_n}xto x in norm for every x.


Proof.
For B_∞ the space is by definition the norm closure of the
finitely supported coordinate span, so finite vectors are dense by
construction.

For X=J^*, let Z⊆ X be the norm closure of the finitely
supported coordinate functionals. If Zne X, Hahn--Banach gives a
nonzero uin X^* that vanishes on Z. By reflexivity of X
(Lemma~lem:reflexive), X^*=J, so uin J; in particular
u(e_v^*)=u(v)=0 for every vertex v. Finitely supported functions
are dense in J, so u=0, a contradiction. Hence Z=X.

For the projection statement fix x and eps>0. By the density just
proved, choose a finitely supported y with norm{x-y}<eps. Once
H_n contains the support of y one has P_{H_n}y=y, so
contractivity of P_{H_n} gives
[
 norm{P_{H_n}x-x}lenorm{P_{H_n}(x-y)}+norm{y-x}le2eps.
]


## Gates and the cone decomposition
The minimal vertices of F∖F∖ H are gates of
H; write G_H for the set. Because H is ancestral, distinct gates
are pairwise incomparable. The descendant subtrees
[
 U_g={vinF : gpreceq v}(gin G_H)
]
partition F∖F∖ H, and each U_g is itself a
countably branching tree rooted at g. Any segment that crosses the
boundary between H and its complement enters the complement at a
unique gate.

[Cone decomposition]
For a finite ancestral H and xin Y,

 norm{Q_Hx}^2=sum_{gin G_H}norm{x1_bf1_{U_g}}^2.

Also, the gate-coordinate map
x|->(x(e_g))_{gin G_H} is a contraction from Y to
ell_2(G_H).


Proof.
For the equality, note first that on the primal side every segment
of the complement lies wholly in a single U_g. Any disjoint family
S_1,...,S_msubsetF∖F∖ H can be partitioned into
groups indexed by g, and the contributions to rho^2 split:
rho(u)^2=sum_{g}rho(u1_bf1_{U_g})^2 for finitely supported
u in F∖F∖ H. Extend by density to J and dualise
to get the sum identity on X and on B_∞.

For the contraction, let A⊆ G_H be finite and let
a=(a_g)_{gin A} be real coefficients. The vector
v_A=sum_{gin A}a_g e_gin J is finitely supported on an antichain
A; since every segment meets A in at most one vertex,
rho(v_A)=(sum_{gin A}a_g^2)^{1/2}. Then
[
 |sum_{gin A}a_g x(e_g)|
 =|x(v_A)|lenorm{x},rho(v_A)
 =norm{x}(sum_{gin A}a_g^2)^{1/2}.
]
Taking the supremum over a proves the contraction.


## Reflexivity and separability of X
[Reflexivity and separability]
The space X=J^* is reflexive and separable.


Proof.
Fix hge1 and consider the component
J_h=overlineoverline{c_c_{00}(T_h)}^{rho}. Every segment in T_h has at
most h+1 vertices, so the elementary estimate
[
 norm{u}_2lerho_h(u)le√(h+1),norm{u}_2
]
holds. Hence J_h is Hilbert-isomorphic as a topological vector
space, and consequently separable and reflexive. Combining the
components in ell_2:
J=(⊕_{hge1}J_h)_{ell_2}. The ell_2 sum of separable
reflexive spaces is separable and reflexive, so J is both. Its dual
X=J^* is reflexive by the same argument applied to the reverse side,
and separable because the dual of a separable Banach space is
separable in the weak-* topology; here reflexivity upgrades that
to norm separability. (A direct proof also works: coordinate
functionals with rational coefficient and finite support form a
countable dense set.)


## Norming tests attached to finite heads
[Norming test in a finite head]
Let H be finite ancestral, and let 0ne x=P_Hxin Y. There exists
p=P_Hpin J with rho(p)=1 and x(p)=norm{x}.


Proof.
The space P_HY is finite dimensional, so the closed unit ball of
P_HJ is compact. The functional v|-> x(v), defined for
vin P_HJ with rho(v)le1, attains a maximum. Because x
vanishes on the J-annihilator of P_HJ (the functionals supported
on F∖F∖ H), any maximizer may be taken supported on
H. Rescale the maximizer so that rho(p)=1.


# Atoms: convex models of a finite head

## Segment functionals
For a segment S of the tree, define the segment functional
[
 d_S = sum_{vin S}e_vin J,
 d_S(u) = u(S) = sum_{vin S}u(v).
]
The norm of d_S in J^* is exactly one. Indeed, by the definition
of rho, the single-element family {S} gives
rho(u)ge|u(S)|; hence |d_S(u)|lerho(u) for all u. Taking
u=1_Sbf1_S (the indicator of S), we have u(S)=|S| and
rho(u)=|S| (see the ``solid chain'' example above), so
|d_S(u)|/rho(u)=1. Therefore norm{d_S}=1.

## Atoms and their coefficient bound
An atom is a functional of the form

 A = sum_{i=1}^m a_i d_{S_i},
  S_1,...,S_m pairwise disjoint,
  sum_{i=1}^m a_i^2le1.

The segment family (S_i) and coefficients (a_i) are part of the
data; the same point of J^* can have several atomic representations.
By the Cauchy--Schwarz argument sketched under~(eq:coefficient),
an atom has norm at most one:

 |A(u)|
 =|sum_i a_i u(S_i)|
 le(sum_i a_i^2)^{1/2}
     (sum_i u(S_i)^2)^{1/2}
 le 1·rho(u).

The first ineuality is Cauchy--Schwarz in i; the second uses the
definition of rho on the disjoint segment family. Atoms are the
building blocks from which the finite-stage unit balls are recovered.

## The finite atomic ball
[Finite atomic ball]
Let H be a finite ancestral set. The closed unit ball of P_HY
equals the convex hull of atoms supported in H. Every element of
that convex hull is a convex combination of at most |H|+1 atoms
with segments in H.


Proof.
There are finitely many families of disjoint segments in the finite set
H. For each such family (S_1,...,S_m), the corresponding atoms
form the image under a linear map of the Euclidean ball
{ainR^m : sum a_i^2le1}, which is compact. The union over all
families is compact; its convex hull C is therefore a compact convex
subset of the finite-dimensional space P_HJ^* = P_HY.

For a test uin P_HJ with rho(u)le1, the support function
of C at u computes
[
 sup_{Ain C}A(u)
 =sup_{disjoint families}(sum_i u(S_i)^2)^{1/2}
 =rho(u).
]
This is exactly the support function of the closed unit ball of
P_HY. In finite dimension, two closed convex sets with the same
support function are equal. Therefore C is the unit ball of
P_HY.

The Carathéodory bound: a convex body in a real vector space of
dimension d is the convex hull of any set whose every member is
expressible as a convex combination of at most d+1 of the set's
points. Here P_HY has dimension |H| (its coordinates are indexed
by H, and R has dimension one), so every point of the unit ball
is a convex combination of at most |H|+1 atoms.


[Completed atomic ball]
The unit ball of Y is the norm closure of the union over finite
ancestral H of the finite-stage atomic balls of
Lemma~lem:finite-hull. Equivalently, B_Y is the norm-closed
convex hull of all atoms in Y.


Proof.
Every atom has norm at most one, so the norm-closed convex hull of
all atoms lies in B_Y. Conversely, take any xin B_Y. Fix an
ancestral exhaustion H_1⊆ H_2⊆... of F
(or of T_∞); by density~(lem:density),
P_{H_n}xto x. Contractivity of P_H gives
norm{P_{H_n}x}lenorm{x}le1. By the finite atomic ball,
P_{H_n}x is a convex combination of atoms supported in H_n. So
x is a limit of points in the convex hull of all atoms, and
therefore belongs to its norm closure.


## Uniqueness of a crossing per atom
A segment may encounter many vertices of a finite ancestral head H,
but only one gate of H (the first vertex that lies outside H).
Because an atom's segments are pairwise disjoint, at most one atom
segment crosses the boundary at any fixed gate.

[At most one crossing per gate]
Let H be finite ancestral, let gin G_H be a gate, and let
A=sum_i a_i d_{S_i} be an atom. There is at most one index i
such that S_i meets H and its first exterior vertex is g.


Proof.
Two such segments would both contain the vertex g, contradicting
disjointness. Distinct gates are incomparable, so a single segment
S_i can meet at most one gate: if g,g'in G_H with gprec g'
and both g,g'in S_i, then g' would be exterior but g interior,
a contradiction with the definition of gate. So each atom segment
crosses at no more than one gate.


[Finite head test]
For x=P_Hxin Y with xne0, the norming test pin J of
Lemma~lem:norming can be chosen with the following additional
property: the segment sums p(S_i) computed at an atom's segment
family are well-defined for every S_i, so an atom can be
evaluated on the head test.


Proof.
The maximizer p in Lemma~lem:norming is finitely supported
inside H; segment sums p(S_i)=sum_{vin S_icap H}p(v) are then
finite for any atom's segment family, even if S_i extends beyond H.


## The main use of atoms
The finite atomic ball will be used as follows. In the proof of
Theorem~thm:main, the two endpoint vectors xpm y (scaled to
lie in the unit ball of Y) are approximated by finite convex
combinations of atoms. The atom structure lets us track (i) the
total coefficient energy sum a_i^2le1, (ii)
the segment sums p(S_i) of a norming head test p, and
(iii) the gate values A(e_g) crossing from H to its
complement. Section~sec:crossing controls the interaction of
gate values of two atoms that leave the head at the same gate with
opposite signs.

# Cancellation across a gate

## Crossing sums
Fix a finite ancestral set H and a segment test pin J supported in
H, with rho(p)le1. For a disjoint family S of segments
define, at each gate gin G_H:
 the crossing sum b_g(S)=p(S) if there is a
unique SinS whose head Scap H has bottom vertex
parent(g) and whose first exterior vertex is g, and
b_g(S)=0 if no such S exists;
 the unused squared-test energy

 L_{S}-sum_{SinS}


The nonnegativity holds because S is a disjoint segment
family and rho(p)le1, so sum_{SinS} For an
atom A with segment family (S_i), write b_g(A)=b_g((S_i)) and
L_A=L_{(S_i)}; by Lemma~lem:one-crossing at most one segment
of A crosses any given gate, so b_g(A) is unambiguous.

Since p is supported in H, we also have p(S)=p(Scap H) for any
segment S: the tail portion contributes nothing. Consequently
b_g(S) depends only on the head portions Scap H of the
segments in S.

## The opposite crossing lemma
[Opposite crossings]
For any two disjoint segment families S,S'S' and any
test pin J supported on H with rho(p)le1,

 2sum_{substack{gin G_H b_g(S)b_g( S')<0}S')<0}
    |b_g(S)b_g( S')|S')|
 le L_{S}+L_{S'}S'.



Proof.
Fix a gate gin G_H where b=b_g(S) and b'=b_g(S')S')
have opposite signs. Both crossing segments end their H-portion at
the same vertex v=parent(g). Writing the two heads as
[a,v] and [a',v], we have a,a'preceq v; both a and a' lie on
the same ancestral chain up to v, so the heads are nested. They cannot
be equal: if [a,v]=[a',v], then p([a,v])=p([a',v]), so b=b', and
bb'=(b)^2ge0, contradicting bb'<0. Therefore one head is strictly
larger; without loss aprec a', so [a,v]⊋[a',v].

Let the crossing segment in S be S_i=[a,w]. Split it at
prev(a'):
[
 S_i=[a,w] rightsquigarrow [a,prev(a')]
  quadandquad
  [a',w].
]
Both pieces are nonempty (because aprec a', and wnotin H by
crossing), are subsets of S_i, and remain disjoint from every other
segment of S. The p-sums of the two pieces are
[
 p([a,prev(a')])=p([a,v])-p([a',v])=b-b',

 p([a',w])=p([a',v])=b',
]
using p supported in H. So the squared p-sum changes from b^2
to
[
 (b-b')^2+(b')^2
 =b^2-2bb'+2(b')^2,
]
an increase of 2(b')^2-2bb'ge2|bb'|: the last ineuality uses
bb'<0, which forces -2bb'ge2|bb'|.

At every opposite-sign gate, split the longer head in whichever family
contains it. Different gates require different segments (Lemma~lem:one-crossing),
so each segment of S is cut at most once, and each segment
of S'S' at most once. The new family Stilde
of the modified S is still disjoint. Because
rho(p)le1 and Stilde is a disjoint segment family,
[
 sum_{SinStilde}
]
Hence the total increase in the S squared sum satisfies
[
 2sum_{substack{g:,b_g(S)b_g( S')<0calS')<0 head in S longer}}
      |b_g(S)b_g( S')|S')|
 le L_{S}
]
The same argument for S'S' gives
[
 2sum_{substack{g:,b_gb_g'<0 head in S'S' longer}}
      |b_gb_g'|
 le L_{S'}S'.
]
Adding the two ineualities proves~(eq:opposite).

## The truncated variant
There is a second proof that keeps only the earlier piece of the
longer head. Given the same situation with aprec a' and opposite
signs b,b', replace S_i=[a,w] by its prefix
[a,prev(a')] alone. This prefix is a segment, the
family remains disjoint, and its squared p-sum becomes
[
 (b-b')^2=b^2-2bb'+(b')^2,
]
an increase of (b')^2-2bb'ge|bb'| since bb'<0. The gain is at
least half that of the two-piece split (which gave 2(b')^2-2bb'),
and it costs one segment instead of two. The truncated version is
preferred when the split cannot be performed because the shorter
segment is an initial or terminal part of the longer one in a way that
is awkward to track. For the lens theorem, the two-piece split is more
convenient because it preserves the total vertex set covered by the
family.

# The main lens bound

## Reductions
We prove Theorem~thm:main. Let Y be X or B_∞, and let
H be a finite ancestral set. Suppose x,yin Y satisfy P_Hx=x and
|xpm y|le R.

The trivial cases close the argument. If R=0, then x+y=x-y=0, so
x=y=0. If x=0, contractivity of Q_H gives
|Q_Hy|le|y|le Rle 2√(R^2)=2√(R^2-|0|^2).
Assume xne0 and R>0.

Both sides of the claimed bound~(eq:main-bound) are linear in
R: dividing (x,y) by R produces a configuration with
|x/Rpm y/R|le1, and multiplying the resulting bound by R
recovers~(eq:main-bound). So it suffices to prove the case
R=1. Write r=|x|in(0,1] and Delta=1-r^2.

## Auxiliary: rooted mixtures
[Rooted mixtures]
Let G be a finite antichain of vertices (for example G=G_H). For
each gin G, let f_g=sum_{j}c_{g,j}d_{[g,w_{g,j}]}in J^*
be a finite real linear combination of segments all starting at g,
and put M_g=sum_j|c_{g,j}|. Then

 Bigl|sum_{gin G}f_gBigr|_{J^*}
 le(sum_{gin G}M_g^2)^{1/2}.



Proof.
Fix uin J with rho(u)le1. For each g with at least one
w_{g,j}, choose j_u(g) maximizing |u([g,w_{g,j}])|; let
m_g=|u([g,w_{g,j_u(g)}])| and m_g=0 if there is no such j.
Then
[
 |f_g(u)| le sum_j|c_{g,j}|·|u([g,w_{g,j}])| le M_g m_g.
]
The chosen segments [g,w_{g,j_u(g)}] lie in the distinct cones
U_g of the antichain G, hence are pairwise disjoint. So
sum_g m_g^2lerho(u)^2le1. Therefore
[
 |sum_{gin G}f_g(u)|
 lesum_{gin G}M_g m_g
 le(sum_gM_g^2)^{1/2}(sum_gm_g^2)^{1/2}
 le(sum_gM_g^2)^{1/2}.
]
Taking the supremum over rho(u)le1 gives~(eq:rooted-mix).


Proof.
A norming test and atom laws.:
By Lemma~lem:norming, choose pin J supported in H with
rho(p)=1 and x(p)=r.

First suppose y has finite support; then xpm y lie in P_KY for
every finite ancestral K⊇ Hcupsuppy. Fix such
a K. By Lemma~lem:finite-hull, each of x+y and x-y is a
convex combination of atoms in K with sum_ia_i^2le1.

Let sigmain{+1,-1} be a uniformly random sign. Conditionally on
sigma=+1, choose an atom A from a finite law whose mean is x+y;
conditionally on sigma=-1, choose A from a finite law whose mean
is x-y. Then

 EA=x, E(sigma A)=y.


Joint budget.:
For each atom A=sum_ia_id_{S_i} in the mixture, define
[
 b_i=rp(S_i),
 A^circ=sum_ib_id_{S_i},
 V(A)=sum_i(a_i-b_i)^2,
 L(A)=r^2-sum_ib_i^2.
]
Since rho(p)le1 and the S_i are disjoint segments, we have
sum_ip(S_i)^2le1, hence sum_ib_i^2le r^2, so L(A)ge0. Also
V(A)ge0. Expanding:
[
 V(A)+L(A)
 =sum_ia_i^2-2sum_ia_ib_i+sum_ib_i^2+r^2-sum_ib_i^2
 =sum_ia_i^2-2A(rp)+r^2.
]
Take expectations. Since EA(p)=x(p)=r:
[
 E[V(A)+L(A)]=Esum_ia_i^2-2r· r+r^2=Esum_ia_i^2-r^2.
]
Each atom has sum_ia_i^2le1, so

 eta+theta:=E V(A)+E L(A)le 1-r^2=Delta,

with both eta and theta nonnegative.

Replacement.:
Since the segments S_i are disjoint and the coefficients of A-A^circ
on them are a_i-b_i, the coefficient ineuality~(eq:coefficient)
gives
[
 |A-A^circ|le(sum_i(a_i-b_i)^2)^{1/2}=√(V(A)).
]
Applying the contractive Q_H,
|Q_H(A-A^circ)|le√(V(A)); by Jensen's ineuality,

 E|Q_H(A-A^circ)|
 le E√(V(A))le√(EV(A))=sqrteta.


Crossing coefficients and the tail.:
For gin G_H, let
[
 b_g(A)=
  b_i,&if S_i crosses at g,
  0,&otherwise.

]
By Lemma~lem:one-crossing, at most one segment of an atom crosses
at any gate, so b_g(A) is a well-defined real number.

Now Q_HA^circ=sum_ib_id_{S_i∖ H}. Each S_i∖ H is
a segment [g,w] inside one exterior cone U_g. So
[
 Q_HE(sigma A^circ)=sum_{gin G_H}f_g,
 f_g=E[sigma b_g(A),d_{S_g(A)∖ H}],
]
where S_g(A) is the crossing segment at g (or 0) inside A. This
is a rooted mixture with mass M_g=E|b_g(A)| at g, since
|sigma|=1. Lemma~lem:rooted-mixtures therefore gives

 |Q_HE(sigma A^circ)|le|m|_2, m_g=E|b_g(A)|.


Bounding |m|_2.:
Take two independent copies of the atom mixture, with atoms
A_1,A_2, and write b_g=b_g(A_1), b_g'=b_g(A_2), L=L(A_1),
L'=L(A_2). Apply the opposite-crossing Lemma~lem:opposite
with the test rp (which has rho(rp)=rle R=1); because rle1,
the lemma is applicable in the version stated:

 2sum_{g:,b_gb_g'<0}|b_gb_g'|le L+L'.

Taking expectations:

 2,Esum_{g:,b_gb_g'<0}|b_gb_g'|le EL+EL'=2theta.


For each g, expand using independence of the two copies:
[
 (E|b_g|)^2-(Eb_g)^2=(2Eb_g^+)(2Eb_g^-)
 =2,E[|b_gb_g'|,1_bf1_{b_gb_g'<0}].
]
Here b_g^+=max(b_g,0) and b_g^-=max(-b_g,0) denote positive and
negative parts. Summing over g and using~(eq:crossing-avg):

 |m|_2^2-|Eb|_2^2
 =2,Esum_{g:,b_gb_g'<0}|b_gb_g'|le 2theta.


Bounding |Eb|_2.:
Let W=Q_H(A-A^circ)=sum_i(a_i-b_i)d_{S_i∖ H}, and let
a_gin{0}cup{a_i} be the atom coefficient for the crossing
segment at g. Then W(e_g)=a_g-b_g. Since EA=x and P_Hx=x,
we have EA(e_g)=0 for every gate g, so Ea_g=0. Thus
EW(e_g)=-Eb_g. The gate-coordinate contraction
(Lemma~lem:cones) and the coefficient bound give
[
 |(EW(e_g))_g|_2^2le E|W|^2le EV(A)=eta.
]
Therefore

 |m|_2^2leeta+2theta.


Assembly.:
Using~(eq:mixture-means), E(sigma A)=y, so
[
 Q_Hy=Q_HE(sigma A)=Q_HE(sigma A^circ)+Q_HE(sigma(A-A^circ)).
]
Triangle ineuality with (eq:reconstruction), contraction, and
(eq:replacement) gives
[
 |Q_Hy|le|m|_2+E|Q_H(A-A^circ)|
 le√(eta+2theta)+sqrteta.
]
The elementary ineuality sqrteta+√(eta+2theta)
le2√(eta+theta) (both sides nonnegative, indistinguishable
after squaring) and~(eq:budget) then give
[
 |Q_Hy|le 2√(eta+theta)le 2sqrtDelta=2√(1-r^2).
]
This closes the finite-support case with R=1.

Unscaling.:
For general R>0, dividing x,y by R produces a configuration with
unit lens radius; the finite-support ineuality applied to that
configuration, multiplied by R, yields
[
 |Q_Hy|le 2R√(1-(r/R)^2)=2√(R^2-r^2)=2√(R^2-|x|^2).
]
This proves Theorem~thm:main for finitely supported y.

Limiting argument.:
For general yin Y, choose an increasing sequence of finite ancestral
sets K_1⊆ K_2⊆... in F (or T_∞)
satisfying H⊆ K_n and ∪_n K_n=F (or

|P_{K_n}(xpm y)|le|xpm y|le R. The finite-support estimate
applies to P_{K_n}y with the same head H:
[
 |Q_HP_{K_n}y|le 2√(R^2-|x|^2).
]
Since P_{K_n}yto y in norm (Lemma~lem:density) and Q_H is
continuous, Q_HP_{K_n}yto Q_Hy. Hence the estimate holds for y.


# The averaged midpoint modulus

## Statement
We convert Theorem~thm:main into a numerical AMUC modulus.

[Midpoint growth]
Let Y be X or B_∞. For every t>0,

 deltahat_Y(t)ge√(1+t^2/4)-1/2
 quadandquad
 overlinetheta_Y(t)ge√(1+t^2/4)-1.

In particular deltahat_Y(t)>0 for every t>0, so Y is
asymptotically midpoint uniformly convex.


## Finite-head case
Fix t>0 and a finitely supported unit vector x_0=P_Hx_0 in Y.
Let yinker P_H be a unit vector, so P_Hy=0 and |y|=1.

Apply Theorem~thm:main to the pair (x_0,ty) with
R=max{|x_0+ty|,|x_0-ty|}. The hypotheses are satisfied since
P_Hx_0=x_0.
[
 t|y|=tle 2√(R^2-|x_0|^2)=2√(R^2-1).
]
Squaring and dividing by four gives R^2-1ge t^2/4, so

 Rge√(1+t^2/4).

On the other hand, P_H(x_0pm ty)=x_0pm tP_Hy=x_0, so by P_H-contractivity

 |x_0+ty|ge|x_0|=1, |x_0-ty|ge 1.

Therefore
[
 max{|x_0+ty|,|x_0-ty|}ge√(1+t^2/4),
]
and, using~(eq:endpoint-floors),
[
 (|x_0+ty|+|x_0-ty|)/(2)ge1+√(1+t^2/4)/2.
]
Subtracting 1 gives the excesses
[
 √(1+t^2/4)-1
 quadandquad
 √(1+t^2/4)-1/2.
]
Both estimates hold for every unit yinker P_H.

## Passage to the modulus
For a fixed unit vector x_0 supported in a finite ancestral H:
 Take the infimum over unit yinker P_H. The excess in
either average or maximum is at least
(√(1+t^2/4)-1) or (√(1+t^2/4)-1)/2 respectively.
 The supremum over all cofinite subspaces F is at least the
value at the particular cofinite subspace F=ker P_H.
 The infimum over all finitely supported unit centers x_0 is
therefore at least the corresponding excesses from the previous step.

This proves Corollary~cor:midpoint for finitely supported unit
centers.

## Approximation for the general unit center
Let xin Y be any unit vector. By Lemma~lem:density, choose a
finitely supported unit approximation x_0 with |x-x_0|<eps, and
let H contain its support. For any yinker P_H,
[
 |xpm ty|ge|x_0pm ty|-eps.
]
The right side converges to |x_0pm ty| as epsto0, so the
excesses of maximum and average evaluated at (x,ty) are at least
those evaluated at (x_0,ty). Taking infimum over unit yinker P_H,
supremum over cofinite F, and letting eps↓0 gives the
same bound at every unit xin Y. Combined with the finite-head
bound above, this proves~(eq:midpoint-bound).

## A quadratic consequence at small radii
From the ineuality √(1+u)-1ge u/3 valid for 0<ule1, the
averaged modulus satisfies
[
 deltahat_Y(t)ge t^2/24  (0<tle 2).
]
This is the qualitative statement that the modulus, though possibly
very small for tiny t, is uniformly positive on every compact
subinterval of (0,∞). Section~sec:variants catalogues
alternative proofs of the same conclusion.

# No equivalent AUC norm

## Unit root paths
For a vertex v, let
[
 h_v = d_{[o,v]} = sum_{opreceq wpreceq v} e_w^*
]
be the root-path functional at v. The family {[o,v]}
consists of one segment, so the coefficient bound~(eq:coefficient)
gives norm{h_v}le1. Evaluating on the single vertex e_v of the
primal gives h_v(e_v)=1, so norm{h_v}=1 exactly.

If v is not a deepest vertex, its children v^frown j exist for
every jge1, and

 h_{v^frown j}-h_v
 = e_{v^frown j}^*,
  norm{e_{v^frown j}^*}=1.

Sibling coordinate functionals therefore have unit norm and satisfy
the same coefficient bound as any finite family of distinct singletons.
In the primal these functionals are the segment functionals of
one-vertex segments, so this is again a case of~(eq:coefficient).

## Coordinates are weakly null
Every vertex of T_∞ has countably many children, so the
siblings v^frown 1, v^frown 2,... of a fixed vertex v form a
countably infinite set of distinct vertices with a common parent. The
following holds in either Y=X or Y=B_∞.

[Weak nullity of coordinates]
For every Lin Y^*,

 sum_{v}|L(e_v^*)|^2lenorm{L}_{Y^*}^2.

Consequently L(e_v^*)to0 along any sequence of distinct vertices,
and in particular the sibling sequence
(e_{v^frown j}^*)_j is weakly null.


Proof.
For a finitely supported array c=(c_v), apply the coefficient
ineuality~(eq:coefficient) to the disjoint one-vertex segments
{v}:
[
 Bigl|sum_v c_ve_v^*Bigr|_Y
 le(sum_v c_v^2)^{1/2}.
]
So the map T:ell_2to Y, c|->sum_v c_ve_v^*, is a well-defined
bounded linear map of norm at most one. Its adjoint
T^*:Y^*toell_2 is then also of norm at most one, and
T^*L=(L(e_v^*))_v. This is~(eq:coordinate-square-sum).
Square summability forces the terms to tend to zero, and a sequence
tending to zero in every functional is weakly null.



The argument only uses that the one-vertex segments {v} are
pairwise disjoint for distinct vertices, and that rho is defined
by a supremum over disjoint segment families. Nothing in it needs a
distinction between X and B_∞.


## A positive one-sided modulus forces growth
Recall the one-sided asymptotic modulus~(eq:one-sided-modulus).
A norm N on Y is asymptotically uniformly convex (AUC) when
overlinedelta_N(t)>0 for every t>0. We now show the following.

[No equivalent AUC norm]
Let Y be X or B_∞, let N be a norm on Y equivalent to
the given norm with
[
 anorm{y}le N(y)le bnorm{y}(yin Y),
]
and let 0<ale b<∞. Then

 overlinedelta_N!((a)/(2b))=0,

 overlinedelta_N!((a)/(b))=0.

In particular Y admits no equivalent AUC norm.


Proof.
Suppose, towards a contradiction, that
overlinedelta_N(a/(2b))>0. Put t_0=a/(2b) and choose
0<gamma<overlinedelta_N(t_0). By the definition of the
one-sided modulus with center h_v and cofinite subspaces, there is a
cofinite subspace F⊆ Y such that

 N(h_v/N(h_v)+t_0w)ge1+gamma
  for every N-unit win F.

The radius t_0 was fixed; the ineuality holds simultaneously for
all N-unit win F, in particular for those obtained from an
approximation in F of a sibling direction.

Fix such a vertex v, put x=h_v/N(h_v), and let
e_j=e_{v^frown j}^* be a sibling coordinate. Set
y_j=e_j/N(h_v), so that h_{v^frown j}=h_v+y_j. The equivalent
norms give t_0le N(y_j)le b/a: the lower bound is
anorm{e_j}/N(h_v)ge a/b=2t_0, and the upper bound
bnorm{e_j}/N(h_v)le b/a.

The sibling sequence (e_j)_j is weakly null
(Lemma~lem:weak-null-coord), so is
(u_j)=(y_j/N(y_j)), and their images in the finite-dimensional
quotient Y/F converge to zero in norm. Choose w_jin F with
N(w_j-u_j)to0, and for large j replace w_j by
w_j/N(w_j) if necessary so as to normalize, at most doubling the
error. Call the resulting N-unit vector w_jin F; it still
satisfies N(w_j-u_j)to0.

Select j large enough that N(w_j-u_j)<gamma/(2t_0). Then
[
 N(x+t_0u_j)
 ge N(x+t_0w_j)-t_0N(w_j-u_j)
 ge1+gamma-(gamma)/(2)=1+(gamma)/(2).
]
Since N(y_j)ge t_0, ray convexity of r|-> N(x+ru_j) (valid
for rge t_0 by convexity along the ray from the origin) gives
[
 N(x+y_j)
 ge1+(N(y_j))/(t_0)·(gamma)/(2)
 ge1+gamma.
]
Equivalently,
[
 N(h_{v^frown j})ge(1+gamma)N(h_v).
]

The choice of j depends on v, but the growth factor is uniform.
For the infinite tree, start at the root o and pick successive
children o=v_0prec v_1prec v_2prec... selected as above at each
step. Then
[
 N(h_{v_k})ge(1+gamma)^kN(h_o),
]
and N(h_{v_k})le b, N(h_o)ge a, so no chain of length
k>log(b/a)/log(1+gamma) can be built. This contradiction proves
overlinedelta_N(t_0)=0.

For the finite forest, the same iteration is available within a single
component, provided the height is large enough. Since each
T_h appears as a component of F with h arbitrary, choose
h>log(b/a)/log(1+gamma) in advance; contractivity of the
coordinate projection onto that component (which we have not used,
but which holds by the same argument as Lemma~lem:projections)
shows no contradiction is introduced by restricting to it. The same
iteration inside T_h reaches a deepest vertex within kle h steps.

At radius t_0=a/b the identical iteration applies: the ray-convexity
step only needs N(y_j)ge t_0, and N(y_j)ge a/bge t_0 always
holds. So the two vanishing statements in~(eq:modulus-vanishes)
come from one argument, run once at each of the two radii.


## What goes wrong in the AUC definition
The AUC definition tests a single endpoint z+tw and requires the
norm of that single endpoint to grow. The AMUC definition of
Section~sec:moduli tests the average of norm{x+ty} and
norm{x-ty}, and the sign average kills precisely the path-norm
growth that AUC exploits. Concretely: pushing x toward a child
direction of v raises x+ty but lowers x-ty by the same amount,
so the average sees no excess at all. This is why the lens bound of
Theorem~thm:main and the AUC obstruction of
Theorem~thm:no-auc coexist in the same space.

# Alternative proofs and their constants

The main theorem admits several independent proofs, each retaining a
different piece of the representation data. All of them use the same
cancellation step. This section records what is common and then lists
the constants so that a reader can compare mechanisms without reading
every proof.

## What all routes share
Every argument starts with a finite ancestral head H, a norming test
p supported in H for the center, and finite laws of atoms
representing the scaled endpoints. For a represented atom
A=sum_ic_id_{S_i} the coefficient c_i is replaced by the head sum
s_i=p(S_i), and three quantities are tracked:
[
 E(A)=sum_i(c_i-s_i)^2,
 D(A)=1-sum_is_i^2,
 L(A)=1-A(p).
]
Both E(A) and D(A) are nonnegative, and
2L(A)=1-sum_ic_i^2+D(A)+E(A). When two atoms leave the head at the
same gate with opposite head sums b,b', the heads are nested; cutting
the longer one at the start of the shorter raises its squared
p-sum by 2b^2-2bb'ge2|bb'|. Cutting simultaneously at every
opposite gate keeps the family disjoint, so the total rise is at most
D(A). That single estimate is Lemma~lem:opposite, and it is the
only place where the nesting of segments is used. Every route below
differs only in which of E, D and L receives which budget.

## The main route
Charging E(A) and D(A) to one joint budget gives
Theorem~thm:main,
[
 norm{Q_Hy}le2sqrt{R^2-norm{x}^2},
]
and the averaged modulus deltahat_Y(t)ge
(√(1+t^2/4)-1)/2. No other route improves the leading
constant 2 on the tail.

## Routes that keep one endpoint law
Charging the two endpoint laws separately rather than their signed
mixture costs a factor of two in the crossing mass, and sometimes a
further factor in the pairing.
 Weighted endpoint laws: with r=norm{x} and
      L=(norm{x+z}+norm{x-z})/2,
      norm{z}le(sqrt2+sqrt6)√(L(L-r)), valid at every radius.
      The associated modulus is omega(t)/2 for
      omega(t)=(√(1+4(t/C)^2)-1)/2 and
      C=sqrt2+sqrt6.
 Radial deficit: norm{Q_Hy}le
      (sqrt2+sqrt6)sqrt{R(R-norm{x})}, with the endpoint laws
      mixed by a random sign.
 Separate endpoint laws: norm{Q_Hy}le
      (2+2sqrt6)sqrt d with d=1-norm{x} at unit radius.
 Conditional joint budget: norm{y}le
      (1+2sqrt2)√(Delta) with Delta=R^2-norm{x}^2. Bounding
      the coefficient error and the slack separately instead of summing
      them gives (1+2sqrt3)sqrtDelta.


## Routes that keep the original coefficients
Keeping the original crossing coefficients rather than the head sums
leaves a wholly exterior term visible as a separate error.
 Signed square-root budget: norm{z}le
      (√(12)+2sqrt2)sqrt d for d=1-norm{x}.
 Product perturbation: the fourth-root function
      [
       omega_{coef}(d)
       =2(√(2d)+sqrt{6d+4√(2d)}).
      ]
 Signed recovery in the forest, which avoids the factor two of an
      indicator reconstruction:
      g(s)=√(2s)+sqrt{6s+4√(2s)}.
 Approximate laws: norm{y}le(1+d)(7sqrt2+4)sqrt dle
      32sqrt d for 0<dle1.
 Unit head with an enlarged lens: norm{y}le
      (√(12)+2sqrt2)√(R(R-1)).


## Sign choices at the boundary
Choosing a preferred sign at each exit point removes the minority mass
outright.
 Preferred parent: 3(√(L^2-1)
      +2sqrt{(1-L^{-2})/2}) at lens radius 1<L; in the forest,
      3(sqrt2+2)sqrt{1-norm{h}}.
 Majority sign at the first exit:
      norm{y}/(1+delta)le3C_{ex}√(eta) where
      eta=delta/(1+delta) and C_{ex}=2+3sqrt2. This
      yields deltahat_{B_∞}(t)ge
      t^2/(576C_{ex}^2).
 Trimming by maximal splitting gain: a purely qualitative
      statement. There are no sequences of finite ancestral heads
      H_n, unit head-supported centers x_n and tails
      z_ninker P_{H_n} with max{norm{x_npm z_n}}to1 and
      liminfnorm{z_n}>0.
 Small center tail: norm{u}le25sqrt e whenever
      norm{x}=1, norm{Q_Hx}le e, x(p)ge1-e and
      max{norm{xpm u}}le1+e. This gives
      deltahat_Z(t)ge t^2/1024, and also
      deltahat_Z(t)ge e/2 whenever 25sqrt e<t.
 Signs read off the target tail:
      norm{y}le(2sqrt2+2sqrt6)sqrt{1-norm{x}}
      le8sqrt{1-norm{x}}, and an eps-separated family in the
      lens forces norm{p}le1-eps^2/256.
 Coordinate-null displacements: if xpm z_j lie in the unit
      ball and z_j(e_v)to0 for every vertex v, then
      [
       limsup_jnorm{z_j}le(sqrt2+2sqrt6)sqrt{1-norm{x}}<7.
      ]


## Primal clipping routes
A different family of arguments perturbs a primal test rather than
choosing signs among coefficients. The relevant quantity is the
cumulative sum of the tail test along each exterior path.
 Paired clipping: deltahat_Z(t)ge
      √(1+t^2/12)-1.
 Selected frontier shift, which spends a factor two on choosing
      the endpoint: deltahat_Z(t)ge
      (√(1+t^2/48)-1)/4.
 Root shift with a suffix threshold chosen independently of the
      shift size: the rational gain d_0(t)=5t^2/(512+3t^2).
 Prefix weights at the actual center: gain gamma=b/(2L_c) with
      L_c=√(1+12c^2) and b=1+ct-L_c for 0<c<t/6.
 Ordered sign comparison at a single orientation:
      norm{y}le(sqrt2+√(10))sqrteps from a head deficit
      eps.
 Weaker trimming schedule keeping only norm{Q_D}le2 and one
      orientation: norm{z}le(1+h)(2√(10h)+2√(2h)).


## Qualitative sequential routes
The forest admits a sequential argument that uses only vanishing
deficits: if H_n are finite ancestral, x_n=P_{H_n}x_n,
P_{H_n}y_n=0, norm{x_npm y_n}le1 and norm{x_n}to1, then
norm{y_n}to0. No rate is extracted, but the conclusion is uniform
in the head and in its component heights, which suffices for AMUC.

## Comparison
The cleanest constant on the tail is the joint-budget constant 2 of
Theorem~thm:main. The other constants record what each mechanism
pays: 2 for the joint budget, sqrt2+sqrt6 for a signed mixture
with per-law weights, 2+2sqrt6 for two separate laws,
sqrt2+2sqrt6<7 for coordinate-null displacements, 8 for
target-tail signs, and 32 for approximate atomic laws. The fourth-root
functions omega_{coef} and g come from perturbing pairwise
products of crossing coefficients rather than their absolute masses.
None of these bounds is claimed optimal. None of them is needed for the
statement of the theorem.
