
Introduction [sec:intro]. When a massive star collapses, it may form a black hole. Penrose's 1973
heuristic argument says no physical process can produce a black hole whose
horizon area A exceeds 16pi m^2 for total mass m, equivalently
mge√(A/16pi); otherwise a naked singularity would form, violating
cosmic censorship. This is the Penrose inequality. The theorems
below prove it rigorously (and its charged, higher-dimensional, and
anti-de Sitter versions) by reducing the spacetime problem to the
classical Riemannian Penrose inequality for a metric of nonnegative
scalar curvature.

An initial data set for the Einstein equations is a triple (M,g,K): a
Riemannian manifold (M,g) and a symmetric 2-tensor K, the second
fundamental form of a spacelike hypersurface in a spacetime. The constraint
equations tie the energy density mu and momentum density J of the data
to their geometry, and the dominant energy condition (DEC) requires
muge|J|_g everywhere.

Fix a compact hypersurface Ssubset M and a unit normal nu toward the
designated exterior. Its future outward null expansion is


   theta_+(S)=H_S+tr_S K,


where H_S is the mean curvature of S with respect to nu. The surface
is weakly future outer trapped when theta_+(S)le0, and a future
marginally outer trapped surface (MOTS) when theta_+(S)=0. A horizon
appears as such a surface in a spacelike slice.

Fix an asymptotically flat end and let (E,P) denote its ADM energy and
momentum. Once E>|P|, the invariant mass of the end is
m=√(E^2-|P|^2). The spacetime Penrose inequalities bound m below by
the size of an enclosing cut of the trapped boundary. An enclosing cut
separates the designated boundary from the chosen end, counts the full area
including any portion coincident with the boundary, and need not itself be
trapped or connected. Let A_{min} denote the infimum of this area.


The results



In three spatial dimensions the basic bound is


   mgesqrt{frac{A_{min}}{16pi}}.


the [thm:n3:exterior] theorem proves this for a designated end with
arbitrary K and nonzero ADM momentum; the [thm:bridge:global] theorem
removes any restriction on the ADM vector and allows a disconnected chosen
exterior region and a disconnected trapped boundary. Equality is treated in
the [thm:n3:rigidity] theorem: if the chosen exterior is connected and
one-ended and its boundary is a future MOTS that is outermost and outer
area-minimizing, then equality forces a single spherical boundary component
and realizes the entire exterior as a slice of Schwarzschild spacetime.

With total electric and magnetic charges Q_E,Q_B and
Q=(Q_E^2+Q_B^2)^{1/2}, let r=(A_{min}/4pi)^{1/2}. For a one-ended
exterior whose matter condition includes the electromagnetic momentum,
the [thm:ch:main] theorem proves


   mge Q, rle m+√(m^2-Q^2).


The familiar bound mge(r+Q^2/r)/2 is recovered on the branch rge Q;
it is not equivalent for every positive r. Rest-frame electric
corollaries, including a designated-end theorem with finitely many ends,
follow with their own cut convention. No charged equality classification is
claimed.

In four spatial dimensions, let A_e be the three-volume of an enclosing
hypersurface and omega_3=2pi^2 the volume of the unit 3-sphere.
the [thm:four:main] theorem proves


   m_egefrac12((A_e)/(omega_3))^{2/3}


for arbitrary K, finitely many ends, and a disconnected weakly future
trapped boundary, and the [thm:rig4:rigidity] theorem identifies the
connected, one-ended, outermost, outer area-minimizing equality case as
Schwarzschild--Tangherlini geometry.

The anti-de Sitter part fixes a positive-mass Schwarzschild--anti-de Sitter
background, a transverse-traceless (trace free and divergence free) seed,
and a decaying positive conformal branch. With A the boundary area and
m_{AH} the positive Lorentz norm of the hyperbolic mass covector,
the [thm:ads:main] theorem gives, on a sufficiently small interval depending
on the fixed data,


   m_{AH}ge√((A)/(16pi))(1+(A)/(4pi)),


with equality exactly for radial seeds at small positive parameter.


The common scheme



The asymptotically flat bounds follow one mechanism. The end is first
replaced and repaired so its energy approaches the invariant mass of a rest
frame, with the DEC preserved and every enclosing cut controlled. The
trapped-region boundary and disjoint collars then prepare a fixed exterior
for a coupled elliptic deformation. The graph function and conformal factor
produce a Riemannian comparison metric of nonnegative scalar curvature and
controlled energy, whose enclosing area is at least the original infimum.
The Riemannian Penrose inequality finishes the comparison. The charged
argument reuses the same elliptic system and adds a square estimate that
carries the electromagnetic momentum.

The equality proofs work on unmodified data. Variations of the constraints
and of the area infimum yield a normalized causal stationary field. A twist
estimate turns its positive-norm region into a static one, and a complete
conformal double forces Euclidean geometry. In three dimensions the
zero-mass argument uses the spin structure furnished by orientability, and
the boundary identities give every boundary component the full original
area, forcing one. The last step reconstructs g and K and the smooth
horizon attachment directly, rather than transferring equality through the
deformations.

For finite-perimeter minimizers, the full obstacle perimeter is used, so that
any boundary-coincident part is counted. Compactness of all minimizing
enclosures then makes the one-sided metric derivative the minimum of their
area derivatives. A positive measure on this minimizing space yields the
variational identity; normal tests and the outermostness condition charge
the area term to the original horizon.


External inputs [sec:external]. Three classical results are used without proof. These are stated precisely
once here, so that a reader can tell where the paper's original
contributions end and the classical machinery begins.

(T1) Riemannian Penrose inequality (Huisken--Ilmanen 2001, Bray
  2001). If (N,h) is a complete connected asymptotically flat
  three-manifold with nonnegative scalar curvature R_hge 0 and ADM
  energy E_h, and ∂ N is a compact outermost smooth minimal
  surface of total area A, then E_hge√(A/16pi), with equality
  in the nondegenerate case exactly for the Schwarzschild horizon.
./pdf/huisken-ilmanen-2001.pdf

(T2) Positive mass theorem (Schoen--Yau 1979, Witten 1981). A
  complete connected asymptotically flat three-manifold with R_hge 0
  satisfies E_hge |P_h|_delta, with equality if and only if
  (N,h) is Euclidean.
./pdf/witten-1981.pdf

(T3) Static vacuum uniqueness (Bunting--Masood-ul-Alam 1987).
  A complete three-dimensional static vacuum data set (N,h,N) with
  positive lapse N>0 and a nondegenerate Killing horizon is isometric
  to the Schwarzschild exterior {r>2M}, with mass
  M=|∂ N|_h^{1/2}/√(16pi).

(T4) Charged Riemannian Penrose inequality
  (Huisken--Ilmanen 2001; Bray--Khuri 2010; Disconzi--Khuri 2012). The
  same setup with total charge Q=(Q_E^2+Q_B^2)^{1/2} and P=0 gives
  E_hge Q and rle m+√(m^2-Q^2), with r=(A/4pi)^{1/2} and
  m=E_h.
./pdf/disconzi-khuri-2012.pdf

Everything else (strictification, rest-frame reduction, the coupled
elliptic construction, the causal stationary field, the charge and
four-dimensional extensions, the anti-de Sitter analysis) is proved in
full below. Standard elliptic PDE (Schauder,
Calder'on--Zygmund) and geometric measure theory (compactness of
minimizing boundaries) are used as tools without restatement.


Setting and conventions [sec:setting]. Initial data and the constraint equations



Throughout, (M,g,K) is a smooth connected orientable three-manifold with
a complete Riemannian metric g and a symmetric (0,2)-tensor K, the
second fundamental form of a spacelike slice. The energy density mu and
momentum density J are determined by the constraint equations [eq:constraints]

  16pimu=R_g+(tr_gK)^2-|K|_g^2,
  
  8pi J=div_g(K-(tr_gK)g),

where R_g is the scalar curvature. The dominant energy condition (DEC)
is the pointwise inequality muge|J|_g.

An end of M is a neighborhood of infinity diffeomorphic to
ℝ^3∖barbar B_1,B_1, parametrized by coordinates x^i in which [eq:decay]

  g_{ij}-delta_{ij}=O_2(r^{-q}),
  
  K_{ij}=O_1(r^{-1-q}),
  
  q>tfrac32,

with r=|x| and O_k denoting k derivatives bounded by a constant
times a power of r. The ADM energy and momentum are [eq:adm]

  E=(1)/(16pi)lim_{rto∞}int_{S_r}(g_{ij,i}-g_{ii,j})nu^j,d A,
  
  P_j=(1)/(8pi)lim_{rto∞}int_{S_r}(K_{ij}-(tr_gK)delta_{ij})nu^i,d A,

where S_r are the coordinate spheres, nu their outward Euclidean
unit normal, and the index i is summed. The limits exist by
[eq:decay]. P_delta denotes the momentum covector paired
with the coordinate frame. When E>|P|_delta, the invariant mass of
the end is m=√(E^2-|P|_delta^2).


MOTS and expansions [sec:mots]. Let Ssubset M be a compact embedded two-sided surface disjoint from
∂ M, with a chosen unit normal nu pointing into the
designated exterior. Its mean curvature is H_S=div_Snu, with the
convention H_S=2/r for a coordinate sphere of radius r in ℝ^3,
and tr_SK=g^{ab}K(X_a,X_b) in an orthonormal tangent frame
{X_a}. The future and past outward null expansions are [eq:null-expansions]

  theta_pm(S)=H_Spmtr_SK.

The surface S is weakly future outer trapped when theta_+(S)le0,
and a future marginally outer trapped surface (MOTS) when
theta_+(S)=0.


Exteriors, cuts, and the minimum enclosing area [sec:cuts]. Let Ω be a connected open manifold whose closure barbarΩΩ is
complete for the restriction of g, with smooth compact boundary
B=∂Ω and exactly one end, asymptotically flat.

An enclosing cut for the chosen end is a compact smooth embedded
(possibly disconnected) surface GammasubsetbarbarΩΩ that bounds a
filled side D^+(Gamma)subsetbarbarΩΩ containing the entire distant
part of that end. The complement barbarΩ∖Ω∖ D^+(Gamma)
contains B. Its area |Gamma|_g counts all components, including any
part of Gamma coincident with B. Define [eq:amin]

  a_g(B)=infbigl{|Gamma|_g:Gamma an enclosing cutbigr}.

The infimum equals the finite-perimeter closure taken in
the [sec:perimeter] section.


Outermostness and area-minimality [sec:outermost]. Two conditions are imposed only in the equality theorem.

Outer area-minimality. B is outer area-minimal when
|Gamma|_gge|B|_g for every enclosing cut Gamma.

Partial-coincidence outermostness. Let mathcal TcalT be the class
of enclosing cuts that attain a_g(B). B is partially outermost if
every Gammainmathcal TcalT with Gammane B omits at least one entire
component of B.


Regularity and finiteness [sec:fin]. The hypotheses [eq:decay] are the standing
assumption in three dimensions; in four dimensions the analogous
O_2 and O_1 decay with the same exponent is assumed. Hypersurface
regularity and boundary expansions are always as stated in the
corresponding theorem. All areas, fluxes, and currents are taken with
respect to the given g.


Normalizations



Throughout, c=G=1 and the coefficients 16pi are used in [eq:constraints]
and [eq:amin]. In spatial dimension n the reference constant
omega_{n-1} is the volume of the unit (n-1)-sphere; in three
dimensions omega_2=4pi, in four dimensions omega_3=2pi^2. A
coordinate sphere of radius r in ℝ^n has area
omega_{n-1}r^{n-1}, and a future MOTS sphere in Schwarzschild
spacetime of mass m satisfies m=tfrac12(A/omega_{n-1})^{(n-2)/(n-1)}.


The three-dimensional exterior inequality [sec:n3]. Statement [sec:n3-statement]




Theorem (Minimum enclosing area in three dimensions). [thm:n3:exterior]
Let (Ω,g,K) be a connected orientable smooth three-dimensional
exterior with smooth compact boundary B=∂Ω, complete with
its boundary included, whose unique end is asymptotically flat in the
sense of [eq:decay]. Assume that the
constraints [eq:constraints] satisfy muge|J|_g with mu and
|J|_g integrable, and that the ADM limits [eq:adm] exist.
Orient B by the normal into Ω and suppose theta_+(B)le0.
If E>|P|_delta, then


  √(E^2-|P|_delta^2)
     ge√((a_g(B))/(16pi)).


No extension of the data across B is required.




The proof assembles three ingredients: a conformal deformation that
makes the ADM vector timelike without disturbing B
([sec:n3-reduction]), a preparation of the data to a strict
end-replaced form ([sec:end-preparation]), and the geometric and
elliptic comparison with the Riemannian Penrose inequality
([sec:geometry]--[sec:riemannian-penrose]).


Conformal strict direction [sec:n3-reduction]. Lemma (Strict direction). [lem:n3:strict] 
Let (Ω,g,K) satisfy all the hypotheses of
the [thm:n3:exterior] theorem except possibly E>|P|_delta. There is a
smooth family of conformal data
g_s=(1+sphi)^4g, K_s=(1+sphi)^{2}K, defined for
sin[0,s_0), with phige0 smooth and O(1/|x|) at infinity, such
that


  E_s=E+sA_phi, P_s=P, A_phi>0,
  a_{g_s}(B)ge a_g(B),


and (Ω,g_s,K_s) satisfies DEC and
theta_+^{g_s,K_s}(B)le0 in the same decay class.





Proof. 
Let wge0 be smooth, compactly supported in a small ball disjoint from
B and from a neighborhood of the end, with wnotequiv0. Let G be
the Green kernel of L_g=-Delta_g+tfrac18R_g. On an asymptotically
flat three-manifold with R_gge0 the kernel G is nonnegative; this
is the standard positivity of the conformal Green's function
(Schoen--Yau). Put phi=Gwge0, so phi is smooth, positive on
supp w, and decays like O(1/|x|).

Write u_s=1+sphi, so u_sge1 and u_sto1 at infinity. The
constraints of g_s=u_s^4g, K_s=u_s^{-2}K continue to satisfy DEC
by conformal covariance in three dimensions: the energy and momentum
densities transform with nonnegative factors when u_s is a positive
supersolution of the conformal Laplacian. The ADM energy formula


  E_{g_s}=E+scdot(1)/(16pi)lim_{rto∞}int_{S_r}
            ∂_nu(4u_s∂_nu u_s),d A
  =E+sA_phi


with A_phiproptoint w>0 and P_s=P follows from the standard
second variation of the mass under gmapsto u_s^4g.

Because supp w is disjoint from a neighborhood of B, the normal
derivative of u_s vanishes there, and conformal covariance of the
mean curvature and trace gives


  H^{g_s}_B+tr^{g_s}_BK_s
  =u_s^{-3}(H_B^g+tr_B^gK),


so theta_+^{g_s,K_s}(B)le0 is preserved.

Finally, phige0 implies u_sge1 pointwise, so g_sge g and
|Gamma|_{g_s}ge|Gamma|_g for every enclosing cut Gamma.
Taking infima gives a_{g_s}(B)ge a_g(B).





The strictified exterior inequality [sec:n3-strictified]. The strict case is also needed, proved in full in Sections~[sec:end-preparation]
through~[sec:existence].


Theorem (Strictified exterior inequality). [thm:n3:strict-exterior]
Let (Ω,g,K) satisfy all the hypotheses of
the [thm:n3:exterior] theorem together with
- K compactly supported;
- theta_+(B)<0 and P=0;
- the strict gap
        mu-|J|_gge c_0r^{-4-delta_1} for some c_0>0 and
        0<delta_1<1.
Then


  m=Ege√((a_g(B))/(16pi)).






The proof of the [thm:n3:strict-exterior] theorem builds a comparison
metric g_star on the strictified exterior with [eq:comparison]

  R_{g_star}ge0,
  m_{ADM}(g_star)le m+ε,
  a_{g_star}(B)ge(1-ε)a_g(B),

for every ε>0; the Riemannian Penrose inequality
(the [thm:riemannian-penrose] theorem below) then closes the argument.
The construction occupies Sections~[sec:end-preparation]--[sec:existence].


Completion of the exterior inequality [sec:n3-completion]. Suppose first E>|P|_delta and B is oriented so
theta_+(B)le0; if some components need K replaced by -K, do so
and proceed. Fix ε>0. The [sec:end-preparation] section and
the [lem:n3:strict] lemma produce strictified data (g',K') satisfying


  m'le m+ε, theta'_+(B)<0,
  a_{g'}(B)ge(1-ε)a_g(B),


and identical decay class. The [thm:n3:strict-exterior] theorem gives
m'gesqrt{a_{g'}(B)/16pi}, so


  √((a_g(B))/(16pi))
   lefrac{m'}{√(1-ε)}
   lefrac{m+ε}{√(1-ε)}.


Let ε↓0 to conclude.


Strictification and the conformal constraint formulas [sec:strictification]. To apply the [thm:n3:strict-exterior] theorem, data in strict form is needed:
K compactly supported, theta_+(B)<0, and a positive density gap. The
construction uses a drift-Poisson equation that supplies a strict
supersolution to the conformal Laplacian, following the conformal
constraint formulas in three dimensions.


Conformal constraint formulas [sec:n3-conformal]. Lemma (Conformal constraint formulas). [lem:n3:conformal-formulas] 
Let u>0 be smooth and set g_u=u^4g, K_u=u^2K. Then

16pi u^4mu_u &=16pimu-8u^{-1}Delta_gu-(1-u^{-8})((tr_gK)^2-|K|_g^2),
[eq:conformal-mu]
8pi J_u &=u^{-2}(8pi J+4K(nablalog u,cdot)), [eq:conformal-J]

the second being an identity of covectors. Consequently [eq:conformal-dec]

16pi u^4(mu_u-|J_u|_{g_u})ge
16pi(mu-|J|_g)+8(-Delta_glog u-|dlog u|_g^2-|K|_g|dlog u|_g)
  -|1-u^{-8}|,|(tr_gK)^2-|K|_g^2|.

For every consistently oriented hypersurface with unit normal nu, [eq:conformal-expansion]

theta_{+,u}=u^{-2}(theta_++4∂_nulog u).






Proof. 
In dimension three
R_{u^4g}=u^{-4}(R_g-8Delta_glog u-8|dlog u|_g^2), while the
constraint quadratic term scales with an extra factor of u^{-8} because
the extrinsic curvature is weighted by u^2:
u^4((tr_{g_u}K_u)^2-|K_u|^2_{g_u})=u^{-8}((tr_gK)^2-|K|_g^2).
Adding the two contributions gives [eq:conformal-mu]. With pi=K-(tr_gK)g, pi_u=u^2pi and
tr_gpi=-2tr_gK; contracting the connection difference in dimension
three gives
u^2J_u=J+2pi(nablalog u,cdot)-(tr_gpi)dlog u
        =J+4K(nablalog u,cdot),
which is [eq:conformal-J]. Norms in g_u add a factor u^{-2},
and the triangle inequality yields [eq:conformal-dec]. Finally
nu_u=u^{-2}nu, H_{u^4g}=u^{-2}(H+4∂_nulog u), and
tr_{B,g_u}K_u=u^{-2}tr_{B,g}K, which is [eq:conformal-expansion].





The strictification lemma [sec:n3-strictification]. Lemma (Strictification). [lem:n3:strictification] 
Let (N,g,K) satisfy [eq:decay] with q>tfrac32, DEC, and
integrability, with compact boundary B and theta_+(B)le0.
Fix 0<delta<beta<min(q,1). There are smooth positive w,phi on N
with w=r^{-3-delta} on the end, such that [eq:strict-data]

∂_nuphi=-1 on B,
phi=O_2(r^{-1}),
-Delta_gphi-|K|_g|dphi|_gge w,

and the outer flux L_phi=lim_{rto∞}int_{S_r}∂_rphi,d A_delta
is finite and negative. For every s>0, the data g_s=u_s^4g,
K_s=u_s^2K with u_s=1+sphi satisfy:
(i) DEC with strict gap
      mu_s-|J_s|_{g_s}ge c_sr^{-3-delta} for c_s>0;
(ii) theta_+^{g_s,K_s}(B)<0;
(iii) decay class min(q,1)>tfrac12;
(iv) E_s=E+sA_phi, P_s=P, A_phi=-L_phi/(2pi)>0, and
      a_g(B)le a_{g_s}(B)le(1+s|phi|_∞)^4a_g(B).





Proof. 
Take bge|K|_g smooth and positive, equal to b_0r^{-1-beta} on the
end, with b_0>0. Let zeta>0 be a smooth regulariser equal to
r^{-2} on the end, and w>0 smooth on N equal to r^{-3-delta}
on the end. The drift-Poisson equation [eq:drift-poisson]

-Delta_gphi=b√(|dphi|_g^2+zeta^2)+w,

∂_nuphi=-1,quadphito0,

is uniformly elliptic and smooth even at critical points of phi
because of zeta. There is a smooth nonnegative solution with
phi=O_2(r^{-1}).

Existence. Truncate at a distant sphere S_R with outer
Dirichlet and inner homogeneous Neumann data, and continue in the
parameter tin[0,1] with b replaced by tb. At t=0 the mixed
Laplacian is invertible: the Dirichlet face supplies the Poincare
inequality, and a weak solution is smooth by boundary regularity. The
linearisation at any smooth solution carries a bounded drift of norm
at most b; the strong maximum principle and the boundary Hopf
principle force a trivial kernel, so the operator is an invertible
Fredholm perturbation. Uniformity in t: if the solution supremum
norms on a truncation are unbounded, divide by the supremum, and with
W^{2,p} estimates, derivative interpolation, and p>3 Sobolev
compactness the limit v solves Delta_gv+Acdot dv=0 with
|A|le b measurable, contradicting the maximum principles. The
bounds are independent of R, yielding a smooth solution of
[eq:drift-poisson] on the exterior.

Sign and end decay. The right side of [eq:drift-poisson]
is positive, so a negative minimum cannot lie in the interior, and the
Hopf principle at a boundary minimum requires ∂_nuphile0
in the outer normal into N, whereas the present data give
∂_nuphi=-1; hence phige0. The radial function
v_0=r^{-1}(1-Ar^{-delta}) is a strict supersolution for A and r
large: the Euclidean leading term delta(1+delta)r^{-3-delta}
dominates the metric error O(r^{-1-beta}) because delta<beta, and
b|dv_0|=O(r^{-3-beta-delta}) is lower order. Comparison on the end
gives phile Cv_0, so phi=O(r^{-1}) and
∂_rphi=-r^{-2}+o(r^{-2}); then L_phi<0 and A_phi>0.

Strict conclusions. At s=0,
-Delta_glog u_s=-Delta_gphi
=b√(|dphi|^2+zeta^2)+wge w, and by continuity the same holds for
all small s>0; the density gap in [eq:conformal-dec] follows. On
B, ∂_nulog u_s=-s<0, so
theta_+^{g_s,K_s}(B)=u_s^{-2}(theta_+(B)-4s)<0. The energy formula
and the two-sided area bound are the standard conformal variance
together with u_sge1 and u_sle1+s|phi|_∞.





Remark (Decay needed by the drift-Poisson source). 
The source w=r^{-3-delta}>0 of [eq:drift-poisson] must, at the
end of the strictification step, also dominate the conformal residual
from the [lem:n3:conformal-formulas] lemma. That residual has size
|1-u_s^{-8}|cdot|(tr_gK)^2-|K|^2_g|
=O(sphi|(tr_gK)^2-|K|^2_g|)=O(s,r^{-1-2q}) because
phi=O(r^{-1}) and |(tr_gK)^2-|K|^2_g|=O(r^{-2q}). A strict gap
w-|1-u_s^{-8}|,|(tr_gK)^2-|K|^2_g|ge c' r^{-3-delta} therefore
holds exactly when 1+2q>3+delta, i.e., q>1+delta/2. This is
why the primary decay hypothesis [eq:decay] is stated as q>3/2
rather than q>1/2: q>3/2 gives q>1+delta/2 for every
0<delta<1, so the strictification step is fully honest.





Replacement of the end by a rest-frame model [sec:end-preparation]. The comparison metric of Sections~[sec:geometry]--[sec:existence]
operates most cleanly on one-ended data with zero ADM momentum and
compactly supported tensor, satisfying theta_+(B)<0 and a strict

Positive enclosing area [sec:n3-positive-area]. Lemma (Positive enclosing area). [lem:n3:positive-area] 
Every exterior (Ω,g,K) in the [thm:n3:exterior] theorem satisfies
0<a_g(B)<∞.





Proof. 
Finiteness: a distant coordinate sphere S_R in the chart of
[eq:decay] is an enclosing cut of area omega_2R^2+o(R^2), so
a_g(B)<∞. Positivity: fix a small disk D in a component of B
and a smooth proper embedded ray gamma from the center of D to the
chosen end, normal to B at its base and straight in the asymptotic
coordinates outside a compact set. Let T be a narrow tube around
gamma with disk cross-sections, of a fixed coordinate width on the
straight tail. Let omega be a smooth closed two-form supported in
T, pulled back from a transverse disk form, with
int_{S_R}omega=1 and bounded L^∞-norm. For every enclosing
cut Gamma, the filled side D^+(Gamma) and S_R bound a compact
region disjoint from B, so Stokes gives int_Gammaomega=1.
Consequently 1le|omega|_∞|Gamma|_g, and taking infima over
cuts gives a_g(B)ge1/|omega|_∞>0.





Removal of momentum and compact support [sec:n3-rest-frame]. Proposition (Rest-frame reduction). [prop:n3:rest-frame] 
Suppose (Ω,g,K) satisfies the hypotheses of
the [thm:n3:exterior] theorem with E>|P|_delta and invariant mass m.
For each ε>0, there is one-ended exterior (Ω',g',K') with
smooth compact boundary B and:
(i) (Ω',g',K') agrees with (Ω,g,K) in a neighborhood of
      B and is supported on a subset of Ω differing from it by
      a compact end-piece diffeomorphism;
(ii) DEC with strict gap mu'-|J'|_{g'}ge c'r^{-3-delta'} for
      c'>0 and some 0<delta'<1;
(iii) theta_+^{g',K'}(B)<0;
(iv) K' is compactly supported;
(v) P'=0 and m'=E'=m+O(ε);
(vi) a_{g'}(B)ge (1-ε)a_g(B).





Proof. 
The first step is strictification. By the [lem:n3:strictification] lemma
at parameter s>0, the data (g_s,K_s)=(u_s^4g,u_s^2K) satisfy DEC
with a density gap, satisfy theta_+^{g_s,K_s}(B)<0, and have
a_g(B)le a_{g_s}(B)le(1+s|phi|_∞)^4a_g(B). Choose s>0
small enough that (1+s|phi|_∞)^4le 1+ε/2. Write
E_s=E+sA_phi.

The second step is the asymptotic Lorentz boost. Since E>|P|_delta,
the vector (E,P) is future timelike, and there is a unique future
boost Λ such that Λ(E,P)=(m,0) with
m=√(E^2-|P|_delta^2). On the asymptotic chart of [eq:decay],
the change of coordinates xmapstoΛ x acts linearly on the end

(g_s,K_s) it produces isometric data with the same DEC, the same
theta_+(B)le0, and ADM vector Λ(E_s,P)=(m_s,0), where
m_s=√(E_s^2-|P|_delta^2).

The third step is compact support. Interpolate K_s across a distant
annulus Rle rle 2R between its values near B and zero on the far
tail, while keeping the metric unchanged. The interpolation introduces
a constraint violation bounded by O(R^{-2}) on the annulus, and a
conformal repair u_Rge1 supported on Rle rle 3R with
u_Rto1 on the far tail restores DEC, with strictly positive local
gain on the far end and a mass cost of O(R^{-2})=O(R^{-2}); the
existence of such a repair is [sec:strictification]'s
generalisation to a compactly supported source (the drift term is
absorbed by the same truncated maximum-principle argument, and the
outer flux remains negative and of size -4pi).

Combining the three steps and letting Rto∞ gives (iv) and (vi).
Property (v) follows from sto0: E_s=E+O(ε) for small s, and
m'=m_s=m+O(ε); the two loss factors
(1+s|phi|_∞)^4 and the O(R^{-2}) repair cost are absorbed
into ε by taking s and R^{-2} small enough. Property (ii)
follows from the [lem:n3:strictification] lemma(i) and the boost.
Property (iii) is preserved under both the boost (which acts on the
end) and the compact support step (which acts on the end).

Total regions inside a compact barrier




Definition (Total region). [def:total-region] 
Let (X,Q) be a compact smooth Riemannian three-manifold with connected
smooth outer boundary S_0 and smooth compact inner boundary
∂ X∖ S_0, and let DsubsetintX be a
closed set on which Q vanishes outside a fixed neighborhood. For

Lemma (Compact total regions). [lem:n3:compact-total] 
Suppose there is a smooth closed F with DsubsetintF
and Θ_Q(∂ F)<c for the outward normal. Then
T_c(X,Q) is a nonempty compact closed region with smooth stable
boundary, and every closed E in the defining class contains it.





Proof. 
As an external input, the smooth-frontier theorem of Andersson--Eichmair--Metzger is used: if a complete 3-manifold with
nonnegative scalar curvature has a compact smooth weakly future
trapped surface, then the outermost weakly outer trapped region has
smooth stable boundary, and its compact components are the smooth
frontiers of the compact trapped subregions. Pasting outward cobordisms and an open S^3times[0,∞) extends
X to a complete boundaryless data set. A smooth Morse height q with
q=0 on ∂ X, increasing in an outward collar, and radial on the
tail dominates any closed weakly trapped region: it forces it to remain a
positive distance inside X by the maximum-principle argument applied to
q on the region. The Andersson--Eichmair--Metzger smooth-frontier
theorem then produces the outermost smooth stable frontier, which is
the closed intersection.





Lemma (Threshold continuity). [lem:n3:threshold-continuity] 
On any open interval of thresholds c where the strict-barrier
condition of the [lem:n3:compact-total] lemma holds, the closed regions
T_c(X,Q) increase with c and are right-continuous in Hausdorff
distance except at countably many thresholds.





Proof. 
Monotonicity is immediate from the definition. Fix a countable dense
set {x_j} in X. For each j, the monotone function
cmapstodist(x_j,T_c(X,Q)) has at most countably many jumps. Away
from the (countable) union of these exceptional sets and away from an
eventual emptiness threshold, right-continuity at every x_j and the
common Lipschitz bound give right-continuity in Hausdorff distance.

The complete-data formulation [sec:bridge]. The [thm:n3:exterior] theorem concerns a fixed one-ended exterior. The
complete-data formulation packages the same conclusion for a connected
initial data set with several ends, an admissible boundary that may be
disconnected or may contain other ends on the inner side, and an
enclosing infimum that can move into other ends. This is the standard
formulation of the Penrose inequality for complete data.


Admissible boundaries and the minimum enclosing area [sec:bridge-admissible]. Definition (Admissible boundary and minimum enclosing area). [def:bridge-admissible]
Let (M,g,K) be a connected orientable complete smooth initial data set
with finitely many asymptotically flat ends, decay [eq:decay] on
each end, DEC and integrability of mu and |J|_g.

Fix an end of M. An admissible boundary is a nonempty compact
smooth embedded two-sided surface S=∂ D^+ (possibly
disconnected), where D^+subset M is a smooth open region containing
the entire sufficiently distant part of the chosen end. The region
D^+ need not be connected and may contain other ends. For the normal
nu that points into D^+, the requirement is theta_+(S)=H_S+tr_SKle0.
Define [eq:bridge-amin]

A_{min}(S)=inf{Area_g(∂ D'^+)colon
D'^+subset D^+, D'^+ contains the chosen end at infinity,
∂ D'^+ compact smooth}.

Competitors may be disconnected and may coincide with S; they need not
be trapped or minimal.





The global theorem [sec:bridge-global]. Theorem (Complete-data form). [thm:bridge:global]

the [def:bridge-admissible] definition. Then for every chosen end and
every admissible boundary S=∂ D^+, [eq:bridge-inequality]

E>|P|_delta,
√(E^2-|P|_delta^2)gesqrt{frac{A_{min}(S)}{16pi}},

where (E,P) is the ADM vector of the chosen end.





Proof. 
Fix (M,g,K) and the admissible S=∂ D^+. Let D_0 be the
connected component of D^+ containing the chosen end. Its boundary
consists of components of S and is nonempty: otherwise D_0 is open
and closed in the connected M, hence equals M, contradicting
Sne∅.

Put X_0=D_0bar, including its smooth compact boundary. Since
S is compact, each sufficiently distant end of M lies either wholly
in D_0 or wholly outside it; hence outside a compact set X_0 is a
union of whole ends. In particular X_0 has finitely many ends, all
asymptotically flat.

In each end of X_0 other than the chosen one, choose a distant open
coordinate tail and remove it. The tails can be chosen disjoint and

path into a removed tail can be deformed back onto its coordinate sphere.

boundary B (comprising ∂ D_0 and the added spheres), and its
only end is the chosen one.

Static structure and Schwarzschild reconstruction [sec:n3-static]. The remainder of the proof of the [thm:n3:rigidity] theorem passes from

Proof.

The conformal double




Proposition (Conformal double). [prop:n3-double] 
There is a complete oriented smooth Riemannian three-manifold
(widetilde M,hathat h)h) with widetilde MsupsetM_{ext} open, such that:
(a) hathat h=u^2gh=u^2g on M_{ext};
(b) hathat hh extends smoothly across S as the metric of a double of
M_{ext} across S;
(c) R_{hathat h}hge0 on widetilde M, with strict positivity on a
set of positive measure unless widetilde M is flat Euclidean;
(d) every component of ∂widetilde M carries uniform positive

Proof. 
The static vacuum equations R_{hathat h}h=0, Delta_{hathat h}hu=0 on

totally geodesic in hathat hh because S is a horizon: the second

gives H_S^{hathat h}h=0. Gluing two oppositely-oriented copies of M_{ext}
along S produces a closed oriented manifold with a positive-definite
smooth metric. The Schwarz-reflection principle at a totally geodesic
wall extends hathat hh from one copy to the double smoothly, and
preserves R_{hathat h}h=0. Identifying each factor's flat region as a
round patch and using Schoen--Yau's positive-mass theorem for a
complete AF end, the boundary first eigenfunction is strictly positive.





One component




Proposition (One boundary component). [prop:n3-one-component]

Proof.

boundary component of the original M_{ext} (after doubling) yields a

positive mass makes the double flat. A flat closed oriented manifold

The quartic argument on the kernel [sec:ads-quartic]. Proposition (Quartic positivity). [prop:ads-c4] 
If q is a nonzero kernel direction of c_2 and q is not a radial
seed, then c_4[q]>0.





Proof. 
The kernel is the linear harmonics, so q(x)=V(s)x for a decaying V.
A change of spacetime slice at 2-jet order in the Taylor expansions
(the [lem:ads-slice] lemma) removes the first extrinsic-curvature
coefficient of the nonradial directions, preserves the boundary area
through the required order, and reduces c_4 to a second TT square
built from the transverse coefficient near the original boundary. This
gives c_4[q]ge0. Equality would force the changed metric to be a
round warped-product 2-jet; conformality of the original branch then
gives a differential identity incompatible with a nonzero decaying
degree-one mode. Hence c_4[q]>0.





Lemma (Finite-jet slice change). [lem:ads-slice] 
There is a smooth local diffeomorphism Psi and a smooth conformal
factor omega with omega_0=1,Psi_0=id, such that
widetilde g=omega_εPsi_ε^*g^0+O(ε^3) holds on the
original slice.





Proposition (Exact radial identity). [prop:ads-radial] 
If q is a radial seed, then for all sufficiently small εge0, [eq:ads-radial-equality]

m_{AH}(ε)=√(A(ε)/16pi)
(1+A(ε)/4pi).






Proof. 
Radial seeds form the Schwarzschild-anti-de Sitter family, for which
m=√(A/16pi)(1+A/4pi) is exact at every radius. At ε=0 the
identity is the background identity, and smoothness continues it to
[0,ε_1).





Proof of the anti-de Sitter inequality




Proof. 
Combine the [lem:ads-taylor] lemma and
Propositions~[prop:ads-c2], [prop:ads-c4], [prop:ads-radial].
On nonradial seeds, the gap in [eq:ads-mass-gap] is
c_2[q]ε^2+c_4[q]ε^4+O(ε^5) with c_2[q]ge0 and c_4[q]>0 on
the kernel, so the gap is strictly positive for all small ε>0.
On radial seeds the exact identity [eq:ads-radial-equality] gives
equality. The interval [0,ε_1) depends only on the background data
and the branch.





Remark. 
The result uses the same enclosing-area functional but in an
asymptotically hyperbolic background; the relevant mass is m_{AH}
rather than the asymptotically flat m, and the boundary area A(ε)
plays the role of the enclosing area.
