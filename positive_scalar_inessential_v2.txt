Nonnegative scalar curvature reduces to positive curvature or Ricci flatness.  Fix g with Scal_gge 0.  Short-time Ricci flow exists uniquely on closed M, and scalar curvature evolves by
∂_tScal = DeltaScal + 2|Ric|^2.

The original equation now has uniformly C^{0,alpha'} nondivergence coefficients and right-hand side;  interior Schauder estimates give C^{2,alpha'}, and the difference-quotient equation iterates to uniform higher bounds, in particular uniform C^{2,alpha} at the originally chosen alpha<1. Symmetries and parameter dependence.  An isometry preserving the data preserves the solution;  for a gluing, a fixed reference deck transformation carrying one data set to another carries the solution to the solution.  Covariant Holder norms are equivalent to reference-chart norms and are unchanged under an isometry.  Parameter dependence uses mixed bounds:  for any coefficient h(x,a), bounds on its mixed derivatives imply smoothness of a|-> h(·,a) in every C_b^{j,alpha} norm, since Taylor's formula in a applied also to x-derivatives through order j+1 gives |h(·,a+u)-h(·,a)-D_ah(·,a)u|_{C_b^{j,alpha}}le C|u|^2, and the same for every parameter derivative.  The equation is then a smooth Banach map in a as well as f, and the invertible linearization at each parameter gives a locally smooth solution;  higher-order part of the uniform-linear lemma extends this to all norms, and uniqueness identifies local branches.  Compactness gives uniform mixed bounds and a common band margin from finitely many neighborhoods.

Finally df is bounded, N is positive and bounded, v is bounded below, so g^+ is uniformly equivalent to the reference product metric and has bounded coefficient derivatives of every order;  completeness follows.  QED.

After a rotation, the tube and its projection bounds depend only on d.

Write the target strip as T^{d-1}times[s_-,s_+].  Double W, opposite orientation on the second copy.  Retain the T^{d-1} component of the target map, and send the interval component monotonically along complementary semicircles of S^1. On the first copy, take the circle angle alpha(t)=pi/2+pi t/2 for |t|le 1/2, extended monotonically to 0,pi at the walls, flat near the endpoints;  on the second copy use the complementary semicircle with the same endpoint values. Proof of the aspherical corollary.  Work on one connected component of a closed smooth aspherical manifold.  Scalar curvature vanishes identically in dimensions zero and one, so suppose nge 2.  If M is oriented, its classifying map is a homotopy equivalence because M is aspherical, hence sends the nonzero rational fundamental class to a nonzero class;  the main theorem excludes positive scalar curvature.  If M is not orientable, its connected orientation double cover is a closed connected oriented smooth manifold with the same contractible universal cover;  a positive-scalar-curvature metric on M lifts, contradicting the oriented case.  QED.

Proof of the aspherical rigidity corollary.  Work on one connected component of M; dimensions zero and one are automatic.  Let MMtohat M be the orientation double cover if M is nonorientable, or Mhat=MM=M itself if oriented;  in either case MMhat is closed, oriented, aspherical, and gghat has nonnegative scalar curvature.  The scalar-flat alternative either produces a strictly positive scalar-curvature metric on Mhat,M, excluded by the aspherical corollary, or makes gghat Ricci-flat.  In the Ricci-flat case, the compact-manifold consequence of the Cheeger-Gromoll splitting theorem (Cheeger and Gromoll, 1971) gives an isometric splitting
(widetilde M,widetilde g)~=(R^k,g_{Eucl})times(N,h)
of the universal Riemannian cover, with N compact connected and boundaryless.  Asphericity makes widetilde M contractible;  N is a deformation retract of R^ktimes N, hence also contractible, so dim N=0 by the mod-two fundamental class.  Thus widetilde g is Euclidean and g is flat because the covering is a local isometry.  QED.
