Instead of freezing, cyclically permute the ordered positions of a

LEMMA 3 (Two splitting directions). Let lambda > 0 be an eigenvalue of
h^0(w) and let u, v be L^2(dmu_w)-orthonormal in its real
eigenspace. The traceless compression to span{u,v} of the
first variation of the Laplacian on h^0(w) has rank two as the
conformal direction varies over smooth real functions on S^2. If w
is antipodally even and u, v have the same antipodal parity, the same
statement holds with conformal directions restricted to even functions.
The splitting matrices are unchanged by the fixed-area coordinate change
h^0(w) |-> h(w). Round-block version: Colin de Verdiere (Comment.
Math. Helv. 63, 1988, 184-193) and Greilhuber-Kepplinger (2026,
Proposition 4.6 for the symmetric-square algebra and Section 3 for the
rank-two statement). Local source for the general two-dimensional form:
./pdf/greilhuber-kepplinger-2312.16939.pdf

Proof. In two dimensions the normalized Dirichlet form of h^0(w) is
 q(f,g) = int_{S^2} < nabla_0 f, nabla_0 g>_{g_0}, dmu , 
independent of w, and the mass form is
m_w(f,g) = int f g, dmu_w. Differentiating the mass form,
 dot m_w(f,g) = 2 int_{S^2} fg (dot w - int_{S^2} dot w, dmu_w) dmu_w . 
For an m_w-orthonormal basis u_1,...,u_r of a repeated positive
eigenspace, differentiation of q = lambda m_w gives the symmetric
cluster matrix
 dot M_{ij} = -2lambda int_{S^2} u_i u_j
   (dot w - int_{S^2} dot w, dmu_w) dmu_w . 
The mean term is scalar. The two traceless entries on u,v are, up to
fixed nonzero factors,
 U_1 = u^2 - v^2 ,  U_2 = 2uv . 
Independence: a nontrivial a U_1 + b U_2 = 0 is a nonzero traceless
quadratic form in (u,v) vanishing everywhere on S^2. Its zero set in
R^2 is two lines; at a point (u,v)!=(0,0) on this set,
some fixed nonzero linear combination cu+dv vanishes on a connected
open arc, hence (unique continuation for smooth Laplace eigenfunctions
on the connected sphere: Aronszajn, Krzywicki, Szarski, Ark. Mat. 4,
1962, Section 5, Remark 3) everywhere on S^2, contradicting
orthonormality. Independence gives positive definite Gram matrix for
dmu_w, so tests in directions U_1, U_2 show rank two in the variable
dot w. Parity: if u, v share antipodal parity, U_1, U_2 are even,
and the same tests are available. Fixed-area change: conjugating the
Laplacian by a parameter-dependent pullback adds a commutator with the
Laplacian, whose compression to a lambda id eigenspace
vanishes. Zero eigenvalue needs no splitting.

LEMMA 4 (Finite-cutoff avoidance). Call h simple through position q
if lambda_1 < ... < lambda_q < lambda_{q+1}. Parameters whose
metrics are simple through any fixed q are dense in U; any
two such parameters can be joined inside U by a smooth path
simple through q and constant near its endpoints. In the even
parameter family the same statements hold for prescribed finite cutoffs
within each parity, cross-parity coincidences allowed.

Proof. Finite-dimensional reduction over clusters. In the fixed-area
gauge the operators share domain H^2(S^2) subset L^2(mu) and depend
smoothly (H^2 to L^2) on finite parameters. On a contour in the
resolvent set, elliptic estimates give bounded inverses L^2 to H^2;
the inverse-difference identity and a Neumann series give smooth
parameter dependence of the contour resolvents, hence a smooth, finite
rank Riesz projection. This is standard perturbation theory: Kato,
Perturbation Theory for Linear Operators, Springer 1995, Chapter III
Section 6.4 Theorem 6.17 and Chapter IV Section 3.4 Theorem 3.16. The
avoidance step itself is a finite-dimensional general-position argument
in the spirit of Uhlenbeck (Amer. J. Math. 98, 1976, 1059-1078).

Start with a smooth preliminary path w_0(t), 0 le t le 1, in the
convex U, constant near endpoints. The endpoints have positive
gaps through q, so an initial and final time interval remains simple
under small perturbations. A smooth cutoff chi(t) vanishing near
0,1 and equal to 1 outside the constant zones isolates the region
where perturbation acts.

Fix cutoff q. Uniform comparison with the round mass form bounds
lambda_{q+1}(h^0(w)) above near the path; pick Λ larger. Every
cluster that can affect simplicity through q has eigenvalue below
Λ, including all members of any cluster that straddles q.
Reverse comparison bounds the number below Λ uniformly.
Consider
 X_0 = {(t,u,v) : -Delta_{h^0(w_0(t))} u = lambda u, ;
   -Delta_{h^0(w_0(t))} v = lambda v, ; 0 < lambda le Λ,
   ; < u,v>_{m_w} = 0, ; |u|_{m_w} = |v|_{m_w} = 1 } . 
X_0 is compact in the smooth topology: writing -Delta_0 u = lambda
rho_{w_0(t)} u gives uniform smooth coefficient bounds, elliptic
estimates give derivative bounds, diagonal subsequence passes the
equation and orthonormality to the limit, and the first positive
eigenvalue stays away from zero. Parity version restricts to pairs of
fixed parity (closed condition).

At every point of X_0 the splitting lemma provides two smooth
directions with invertible derivative of the two traceless entries; the
property is open in (t,u,v). A finite cover supplies a single list
xi_1,...,xi_D with rank two at every point of X_0 (all even in
the parity version). The same rank assertion survives in a small
E = span(xi_j)-neighborhood of the path: a nearby
counterexample sequence would converge to a limiting pair on the path
with a nonzero two-by-two determinant.

Consider the perturbation family
 w(t, z) = w_0(t) + chi(t) sum_{j=1}^D z_j xi_j ,
    z = (z_1,...,z_D) . 
For |z| small the family stays in U; endpoints remain simple
since chi = 0 there. Outside the constant endpoints, chi = 1, and
the two traceless entries have rank two in the z variables.

Sets to avoid. At a bad point (t_*, z_*) with the exact consecutive
block r,...,s entirely repeated (this is the whole cluster, possibly
s > q+1), the strict spectral gaps outside the block are unchanged on
a neighborhood. A contour enclosing just this eigenvalue gives a smooth
rank-m Riesz projection, m = s-r+1; projecting a basis at the center
and orthonormalizing gives a smooth real frame, smooth in angle by
elliptic regularity. In this frame the operator is a smooth symmetric
matrix M(t,z). Set
 F(t,z) = (M_{11}(t,z) - M_{22}(t,z),, 2 M_{12}(t,z)) . 
At the center, ∂_z F has rank two by xi_j choice and the
conformal-variation formula; shrinking the neighborhood keeps the rank.
The implicit function theorem makes F^{-1}(0) a smooth
codimension-two submanifold.

If a point in this neighborhood still has the entire block r,...,s
equal, then M is scalar and F = 0. That is the only containment
used. When a larger cluster partly splits leaving a smaller repeated
block, the smaller has its own chart centered there. The two equations
handle every m ge 2.

For fixed (r,s), the stratum whose entire repeated block is exactly
r,...,s with strict gaps outside is second-countable inside
[0,1]times R^D. A countable subcover by neighborhoods of this
stratum covers it, and every point of the stratum in such a neighborhood
lies in the chart's zero set. Union over countably many (r,s) covers
all bad points by countably many codimension-two zero sets. Each zero set
projects to a null set in R^D:bbR^D: locally two z-coordinates are
smooth functions of t and the remaining D-2 coordinates, so the
projection is a locally Lipschitz image of a (D-1)-dimensional set.
Countable union of null sets is null, so arbitrarily small z avoid all
bad sets. Formula above fixes endpoints.

Endpoint density is the same argument without t. Take one parameter,
choose the finite pool xi_j for its normalized low pairs, keep rank
in a neighborhood; each exact-block stratum is codimension two in the
finite parameter space, bad set null, complement dense. Parity: repeat
separately for the two parity lists with the common pool of even
directions.

LEMMA 5 (Retained isolated double). Let h^0(w_*) have an isolated
eigenvalue of exact multiplicity two at positions i, i+1. For any
q ge i+1 and any neighborhood of w_* in U, there is a
parameter in that neighborhood with positions i,i+1 still equal and
all other inequalities through q (including after q) strict. The
perturbations may be unrestricted conformal perturbations, even when
w_* was even.

Proof. Let u, v be orthonormal for the chosen double, U_1 = u^2 - v^2,
U_2 = 2uv. The kernel of the linearized constraint that keeps its
cluster scalar is
 K = {xi in C^∞(S^2;R) :
   int U_1 xi, dmu_w = int U_2 xi, dmu_w = 0} , 
codimension two by Lemma 3.

Claim: for any different repeated positive eigenspace, some
xi in K has nonscalar compression there. Suppose the
contrary. Choose orthonormal a, b in that eigenspace. Then the
functionals represented by A_1 = a^2 - b^2 and A_2 = 2ab both vanish
on K. A linear functional vanishing on the kernel of the
surjection xi |-> (int U_1 xi, int U_2 xi) is a linear
combination of the two component functionals. Independence of A_1, A_2
forces span(A_1,A_2) = span(U_1,U_2). Equality on
every xi with rho_w > 0 gives a pointwise identity
 Q(a,b) = T Q(u,v),  Q(x,y) = (x^2 - y^2,, 2xy) , 
for a constant invertible real T. No additive constant: each of the
four product functions has dmu_w-mean zero. This argument is in the
full smooth tangent space before any finite-dimensional restriction.

Common nodal sets. a = 0 iff Q(a,b) in {(-t,0) : t ge 0}.
T^{-1} of this closed ray is another closed ray through the origin.
Identifying R^2 ~= C, Q is the squaring map, and
the inverse image of a closed ray is one unoriented line. Hence
 {a = 0} = {cu + dv = 0} , 
with (c,d) != (0,0). Points where both entries vanish are included
because the ray contains the origin.

Contradiction. Set f = cu + dv, eigenvalue lambda_*. Take a
connected component Ω of the common nonzero set; sign-normalize
so a>0 and f>0 on Ω. Both a|_Ω in H^1_0(Ω) and
f|_Ω in H^1_0(Ω) hold without any regularity of the nodal
boundary: the positive truncations
 f_ε = (f-ε)_+ 
have compact support inside Ω (support stays away from the
boundary), converge to f in H^1(Ω) by dominated convergence for
functions and gradients, and each approximates a compactly supported
smooth test. Same for a. Weak eigenvalue equations tested against each
other on Ω: symmetry of the Dirichlet form gives
 (mu_*-lambda_*) int_Ω af, dmu_w = 0 . 
The integral is positive; contradiction with mu_* != lambda_*.

Realize xi at fixed double. Pick zeta_1,zeta_2 with invertible
derivative of the two traceless entries. In the three-parameter family
 w(t,x_1,x_2) = w + t xi + x_1 zeta_1 + x_2 zeta_2 , 
let M_d be the smooth 2times 2 Riesz cluster matrix of the chosen
double and set F_d = (M_{d,11}-M_{d,22},, 2M_{d,12}). Then F_d = 0

tangent xi. On any other repeated cluster its matrix is

Finitely many repetitions to remove. Extend the list past q+1 to the
last member q' of the cluster containing lambda_{q+1}. Strict gap

LEMMA 6 (Pole-regular sector modes). Let zeta(theta) > 0 be smooth
and axisymmetric on S^2, with zeta(pi-theta) = zeta(theta). In
the metric zeta^2 g_0, fix a nonnegative integer azimuthal number m
and one azimuthal factor (constant if m=0). Every eigenvalue of the
meridional profile problem is simple. Its lowest profile is positive on
(0,pi) and even under equatorial reflection. Along a
reflection-symmetric smooth deformation, every fixed position in the
m=0 list retains its reflection parity; the position occupied at round
by degree l keeps parity (-1)^l.

Proof. The separated equation for a profile v is
 -(sintheta, v')' + (m^2/sintheta) v = lambda zeta^2 sintheta, v . 
The form domain is the closure of smooth sphere sector profiles in the
energy-plus-mass norm; compact spectral theory on the smooth compact
sphere, restricted to this closed sector, supplies eigenvalues and the
variational minimum. Profiles are smooth at the poles: for m ge 1
they are t^m a(t^2) in polar distance t with a smooth; for m = 0
they are smooth even functions of t.

Wronskian. For two profiles u, v at the same eigenvalue,
W = sintheta(uv' - u'v) is constant. Pole behavior makes W to 0:
for m ge 1, u,v = O(t^m) and derivatives O(t^{m-1}); for m = 0
the functions are bounded with O(t) derivatives. Hence W = 0. At an
interior point where u ne 0, v and a multiple of u have equal
value and derivative; interior ODE uniqueness forces v proportional
to u.

Ground profile positive. Replace a minimizing real profile by its
absolute value; energy and mass are unchanged. The resulting nonnegative
minimizer is smooth on (0,pi) and satisfies the weak equation. An
interior zero would be a minimum with zero derivative, and ODE
uniqueness would force the profile to vanish. So the ground profile is
positive. Reflection preserves the equation and pole conditions;
simplicity and positivity force the mass-normalized ground profile to be
reflection-even. With its azimuthal factor, its antipodal parity is
(-1)^m.

Axisymmetric list m=0. Simplicity at every position by the same
Wronskian. Min-max gives continuous ordered eigenvalues along a smooth
deformation; a local simple spectral projection gives a continuous unit
eigenfunction. Reflection acts on that line by pm 1; the sign is
continuous, hence constant. At round the sign is that of the Legendre
polynomial P_l, (-1)^l. No nodal count for a singular interval
problem is needed.

Nonisometric cones. The reference path has a phase with simple
eigenvalues through the chosen finite band and another with an isolated
double eigenvalue in that band. Its ordered spectra at these phases
differ, so at least one continuous ordered eigenvalue
lambda_j(H(s)) is nonconstant, with image containing a nontrivial
interval. Phases with distinct values of this eigenvalue give distinct
spectra of the scaled links a^2 H(s) and hence nonisometric links. A
pointed cone isometry preserves distance to the tip and restricts on
the unit sphere to an isometry of its intrinsic link metric. So the
corresponding pointed tangent cones are nonisometric. Every control
sequence therefore has uncountably many pairwise nonisometric pointed
tangent cones at infinity.

Continuity in product topology. For a fixed j, A_j is continuous in

in every coordinate to x in X. For any fixed coordinate, its
fixed-point equation holds for all sufficiently large members of the
subsequence; continuity of that output coordinate passes the equation
to the limit. So Phi(x) = x.
