The minimal model program reduces an algebraic variety to a birational model whose canonical divisor is as positive as the geometry permits, by contracting along canonical-negative rays and flipping small contractions. For curves the answer is classical, for surfaces it is the Enriques-Kodaira classification, and for threefolds it rests on Mori's flip theorem. The result is that every smooth connected complex projective variety of dimension at least three whose canonical divisor is pseudo-effective of numerical dimension one admits a projective Q-factorial terminal minimal model with nef canonical divisor, with no hypothesis on the Euler characteristic.

https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511662560

Throughout, a variety is a separated scheme of finite type over the complex numbers; all varieties and morphisms are over C, and all birational models are projective. A divisor D on a normal variety is effective when D is a nonnegative integral combination of prime divisors, Cartier when it is locally cut out by a single equation, and Q-Cartier when some positive integer multiple is Cartier; Q-factorial means every Weil divisor is Q-Cartier. Intersection numbers of rational Cartier divisors are defined after clearing denominators, and on an integral surface that need not be normal, intersections are taken with the fundamental cycle. The canonical divisor K_X of a smooth variety is the divisor of a global top-degree differential form; for a smooth variety over C its first Chern class is -c_1(T^*X). A divisor is nef when it has nonnegative degree on every integral curve, and pseudo-effective when its numerical class lies in the closure of the cone generated by effective divisors. A divisor is big when sections of its large multiples grow like m^n on an n-fold quotient, the maximal possible rate.

For a normal variety U and a boundary Delta (a Q-divisor with K_U+Delta Q-Cartier), and for a prime divisor F over U, presented on a resolution r: U~ -> U carrying F, the log discrepancy is
    a(F; U, Delta) = 1 + coeff_F ( K_{U~} - r^*(K_U+Delta) ).
An exceptional prime has log discrepancy 1. The pair (U,Delta) is terminal when a(F;U,Delta) > 1 for every exceptional prime F, and Kawamata log terminal (klt) when a(F;U,Delta) > 0 for every exceptional prime and the coefficients of Delta are strictly below 1. A terminal variety is smooth in codimension two. These classes and the discrepancy calculus are developed systematically by Kollar and Mori.

For a Cartier divisor D on a smooth projective n-fold X, measure its positivity by the section-growth invariant
    kappa_sigma(X,D) = max { k in {0,1,...,n} : there is an ample Cartier divisor A
        with limsup_{m->oo} h^0(X, O_X(mD+A)) / m^k > 0 };
the maximum of the empty set is taken to be -infinity. The ample twist A is fixed before the limit is taken, so the definition allows the quadratic growth to appear only along the multiples of one fixed positive integer. The invariant kappa_sigma(X,D) = 1 forbids, for every fixed ample Cartier twist, a positive quadratic limsup; it does not by itself impose the upper bound h^0(X,mD+A) = O(m). The invariants and their interaction with divisorial Zariski decompositions are Nakayama's.

The two positivity mechanisms on which the proof rests are classical. The proof has three parts: a discrepancy estimate for a single step, a stabilization lemma showing that after finitely many steps the maps are isomorphisms outside codimension three, and the construction of the perturbations together with the canonical comparison. Start with a single step.

LEMMA (strict discrepancy increase). Suppose U --> U+ is a K-negative divisorial contraction or a K-flip between normal projective Q-Gorenstein varieties. Write h: U -> R for the contraction and h+: U+ -> R for the other morphism, with h+ the identity in the divisorial case. For every prime divisor F over these varieties,
    a(F,U) <= a(F,U+),
and the ineuality is strict whenever the center of F on R lies in the locus where either h or h+ has a positive-dimensional fiber.

Proof. Take a common smooth projective resolution W carrying F, with projections p: W -> U and q: W -> U+, and set g = h o p = h+ o q for the common map to R. Consider the divisor D = p^*K_U - q^*K_{U+}. It is q-exceptional, because the step extracts no divisors. On every curve contracted by g the divisors -p^*K_U and q^*K_{U+} have nonnegative degree, and in the divisorial case q^*K_{U+} has degree zero; hence -D is g-nef and therefore q-nef. The negativity lemma gives D >= 0.

For the support assertion, let r be a closed point of R with a positive-dimensional fiber on either side. There is a curve in g^{-1}(r) mapping onto a curve in that fiber: take a component dominating that curve and cut it by sufficiently general ample divisors. The relative ampleness signs give D. C < 0 for this lifted curve, so g^{-1}(r) meets Supp D.

Use the fiber-support form of the negativity lemma: the support of an effective divisor that is antinef over a proper birational morphism to a normal variety either contains or is disjoint from each fiber. The same conclusion can be seen directly on the reduced irreducible components of the fiber. If such a component T is not contained in Supp D but meets it, a Cartier multiple of D restricts to a nonzero effective Cartier divisor on the integral projective variety T; its degree against sufficiently many ample hyperplanes is positive, producing a curve contracted by g with positive D-degree, contradicting antinefness. The argument needs T neither normal nor the fiber reduced, only the support being at issue; zero-dimensional components already lie in the support when they meet it. The fibers of g are connected because R is normal and g is proper and birational, and containment therefore propagates through their reduced irreducible components.

It follows that every such fiber lies in Supp D. If the center of F on R lies in this locus, then F is contained in Supp D on the chosen resolution. Finally a(F,U+) - a(F,U) = coeff_F D, which proves both claims. QED

LEMMA (stabilization in codimension two). Let
    X = U_0 --> U_1 --> U_2 --> ...
be a finite or infinite sequence of K-negative divisorial contractions and K-flips of projective Q-factorial varieties, starting from a smooth n-fold with n >= 3. After finitely many steps every remaining map is an isomorphism outside closed subsets of codimension at least three on both sides.

Proof. The standard minimal model properties give preservation of Q-factoriality, invariance of the Picard number under a flip, and a drop of one under an elementary divisorial contraction. There are therefore only finitely many divisorial contractions. Since the steps extract no divisors, only finitely many prime divisors of X can disappear; denote this finite set by D.

Consider a flip whose target U_{i+1} carries a codimension-two component T of its flipped locus. A terminal variety is smooth in codimension two, so blowing up T at its generic smooth point defines a prime valuation F with a(F, U_{i+1}) = 2. The center of F on the base R lies in the locus of positive-dimensional fibers of the flipped contraction, so the strict-discrepancy-increase lemma gives a(F,X) <= a(F,U_i) < 2 = a(F,U_{i+1}). Thus F is not exceptional over X: it is an original prime divisor, hence a member of D. After this occurrence its discrepancy is at least two forever, so it can never again satisfy the strict ineuality. Each such flip therefore consumes a distinct member of the finite set D, and after a finite prefix all steps are flips and every flipped locus has codimension at least three.

For the source side put d = n - 2 and let A_d(V) be the real span of the classes [T] of closed integral d-folds T in V, inside the homology H_{2d}(V,R). For a remaining flip U --> U+ over R, remove from R the images of both exceptional loci and take inverse images; this gives a common open subset U0. Each exceptional locus consists of positive-dimensional fibers, so its image has dimension at most one less than its own; the source contraction is small and the target exceptional locus has dimension at most n - 3, so the closed complements Z in U and Z+ in U+ satisfy dim Z <= d and dim Z+ <= d - 1, the extra removed points lying in an isomorphism locus of dimension at most n - 3.

Restriction gives maps
    A_d(U) --> H^{BM}_{2d}(U0,R) <-- A_d(U+)
with the same image: intersect an integral d-fold with U0 and take its closure on the other model, while a d-fold contained in the complement restricts to zero. The right-hand map is injective by the localization sequence and H_{2d}(Z+,R) = 0, so dim A_d(U+) <= dim A_d(U). If the flipping locus has a d-dimensional component T, then [T] lies in the kernel of the left restriction and is nonzero, since for an ample Cartier divisor A one has <c_1(A)^d,[T]> = A^d . T > 0. The ineuality is then strict. A nonnegative integer can drop only finitely many times, so eventually the flipping loci also have codimension at least three, and the same construction of the common open gives the codimension bound on both complements. QED

LEMMA (uniform ambient multiplicity). Let V be a smooth projective complex variety, let Gamma be an integral curve in V, and let iota: N -> V be the map from its normalization. For a section s write ord_z(s) for its order in the regular local ring of V at z. There is a positive constant g, depending only on this map, such that for every line bundle D on V, every nonzero section s in H^0(V,D), and every closed point z in Gamma,
    ord_z(s) >= - (deg_N iota^*D) / g.

Proof. Choose a line bundle G of positive degree on the fixed smooth curve N such that iota^*(Omega^1_V) embeds as a subbundle of a direct sum G^{⊕ a} for some a. Such a choice follows by global generation of (iota^*(Omega^1_V))^* (x) G for a sufficiently ample G, followed by dualization; set g = deg G > 0.

This holds even where Gamma is singular: for b >= 1 the bundles of principal parts on the smooth variety V carry the locally split exact sequence
    0 -> Sym^b(Omega^1_V) (x) D -> P^b(D) -> P^{b-1}(D) -> 0,
and at a point where the order equals b the b-th jet is nonzero, so the graded piece contributes a nonzero section along Gamma. Taking symmetric powers of the subbundle inclusion embeds this leading jet into a direct sum of copies of G^{⊗ b} (x) iota^*D; a nonzero component is a nonzero section of this line bundle on N, so 0 <= b g + deg_N iota^*D. If a fixed curve e on a resolution of a surface T always receives a definite fraction of the fixed part of a section system, then the surface square of the divisor cannot shrink to zero: the fixed part is negative by the Hodge index theorem, the moving part is nonnegative, and their sum is the surface square.

LEMMA (surface fixed-part estimate). Let T be an integral projective surface, let f: T_1 -> T be a projective birational morphism from a smooth surface, and fix a curve e in T_1 contracted by f. For each j let D_j be a rational Cartier divisor on T and k_j > 0 an integer with k_j D_j Cartier. Suppose a nonzero linear system of k_j f^*D_j is generated outside the inverse image of a finite subset of T, with fixed divisor F_j. If for one fixed c > 0,
    coeff_e F_j >= c k_j    for all j,
then there is epsilon > 0, independent of j, such that
    D_j^2 . [T] >= epsilon    for all j.
No normality assumption on T is needed.

Proof. Every curve in Supp F_j maps to a point of T, by the generation assumption. Fix a very ample Cartier divisor H_T on T and an ample Cartier divisor J on T_1, and put Q = f^*H_T. The projection formula gives Q^2 = H_T^2 . [T] > 0 and Q . F_j = f^*D_j . F_j = 0. The Hodge index theorem on the fixed smooth surface T_1 makes the negative intersection form positive definite on Q^perp inside N^1(T_1)_R, so there is one constant K > 0 with (J . F)^2 <= K (-F^2) for every F in Q^perp. Effectiveness of F_j gives J . F_j >= c k_j (J . e), and therefore, with the fixed positive number epsilon = c^2 (J . e)^2 / K, F_j^2 <= -epsilon k_j^2.

After removal of F_j the residual linear system has no fixed curve, and its square is nonnegative: choose two members with no common curve and take their effective intersection, or use a nowhere vanishing member if the system is trivial. By the orthogonality and the projection formula,
    0 <= (k_j f^*D_j - F_j)^2 = k_j^2 (D_j^2 . [T]) + F_j^2.
Now F_j^2 <= -epsilon k_j^2 proves D_j^2 . [T] >= epsilon. QED

Now the two lemmas combine. Suppose the total square vanishes. Then no curve can have negative degree for L, and L is nef. That is the criterion on which the whole proof rests.

PROPOSITION (nefness criterion). Assume the approximation hypothesis and suppose L^2 . H^{n-2} = 0. Then L is nef, including on curves lying in the singular locus of Y.

Proof. Suppose some curve C_0 satisfies L . C_0 < 0; it may lie inside Sing Y, which has codimension at least three. Take a very general surface through one of its points. Choose n - 2 very general members H_1,...,H_{n-2} of |H|, all passing through a point x of C_0. They meet properly, and their intersection meets each Z_j and Sing Y in at most finitely many points, since the hyperplanes through x have no other base point and the countably many conditions can be imposed simultaneously over C. Write the intersection cycle as
    H_1 ... H_{n-2} . [Y] = sum_i a_i [T^{(i)}],   a_i > 0,
where each T^{(i)} is an integral surface; the dimension theorem guarantees a component through x, and one such component is fixed and called T.

On every T^{(i)} the system induced by |d_j M_j| has at most a finite base locus, so its square is nonnegative, including when T^{(i)} is nonnormal: choose a section nonzero at the generic point, then a second section not vanishing identically on any curve of the first zero divisor, and the resulting Cartier intersections with [T^{(i)}] form an effective zero-cycle. Hence M_j^2 . [T^{(i)}] >= 0 for every i, and
    sum_i a_i (M_j^2 . [T^{(i)}]) = M_j^2 . H^{n-2} -> 0,
using L^2 . H^{n-2} = 0 and M_j -> L. In particular M_j^2 . [T] -> 0.

Let Gamma be a component of the inverse image of C_0 in a resolution of Y, normalized by iota: N -> Yhat, with a closed point z above x. Resolve the surface T by a smooth projective surface T_0 -> T, choose a point w in T_0 above z, and blow it up; let T_1 be the resulting surface, e in T_1 the exceptional curve of the blow-up, and f: T_1 -> T the composite. The surface T_1, the curve e, and f are fixed for the rest of the argument.

The projection formula and M_j -> L give, for one fixed delta > 0,
    deg_N iota^* pi^* M_j <= -delta
for all sufficiently large j; this degree equals the positive degree of N -> C_0 times M_j . C_0. For each such j choose any positive multiple k_j of d_j. The uniform ambient multiplicity lemma, applied on the fixed Yhat and Gamma, shows that every nonzero pullback section s' in H^0(Yhat, k_j pi^* M_j) of a section on Y satisfies
    ord_z(s') >= c k_j,   c = delta / g > 0,
with c depending only on the fixed normalized curve and the margin delta.

The sections on Y induce a nonzero linear system of k_j f^*(M_j|_T), generated outside f^{-1}(T intersect Z_j); nonzeroness follows from generation at points of T minus Z_j. Every nonzero section in this induced system has order at least c k_j at w before the last blow-up, because the local ring map O_{Yhat,z} -> O_{T_0,w} sends m_z^b into m_w^b, and the uniform bound applies to a section inducing it; sections whose restriction is identically zero do not contribute. Thus the fixed divisor F_j of the induced system satisfies coeff_e F_j >= c k_j. All the hypotheses of the surface fixed-part estimate hold with D_j = M_j|_T, so that estimate gives a fixed epsilon > 0 with
    M_j^2 . [T] >= epsilon
for all sufficiently large j, contradicting M_j^2 . [T] -> 0. Therefore no curve of negative L-degree exists, and L is nef. QED

The main result now follows by assembling the pieces. Apply the positive-perturbations proposition to obtain a finite canonical sequence from X to a terminal model Y with L = K_Y, B_Y, and M_j = L + t_j B_Y, whose multiples are generated outside closed subsets Z_j of codimension at least three. The variety Y is terminal, hence smooth in codimension two, which is exactly the standing hypothesis of the numerical results.

Fix a very ample Cartier divisor H on Y and a projective resolution pi: Yhat -> Y that is an isomorphism over the smooth locus. The surface-growth lemma gives a fixed Cartier divisor P on Yhat. Choose a positive integer r_0 with r_0 L Cartier. Then
    h^0(Yhat, m pi^*L + P) >= (m^2/2) L^2 . H^{n-2} + O(m)     for r_0 | m,
and also L^2 . H^{n-2} >= 0.

Suppose this intersection number is positive. Choose a smooth projective common resolution W dominating Yhat and every model in the finite prefix, with maps rho: W -> Yhat and u: W -> X. The positive-perturbations proposition gives u^*K_X - rho^* pi^* L >= 0. For every positive multiple m of r_0 this effective difference gives an injection
    H^0(Yhat, m pi^*L + P) --> H^0(W, m u^*K_X + rho^*P).

Set P_X = u_* rho^* P. This is a fixed integral Weil divisor, hence Cartier on the smooth X. A rational section f in the right-hand space satisfies div_W(f) + m u^*K_X + rho^*P >= 0, and pushing this ineuality forward gives div_X(f) + m K_X + P_X >= 0. So there is an injective linear map
    H^0(W, m u^*K_X + rho^*P) --> H^0(X, m K_X + P_X).
Choose a single ample Cartier divisor A_X for which A_X - P_X has a nonzero section. Multiplication by that fixed section gives
    h^0(X, m K_X + A_X) >= h^0(Yhat, m pi^*L + P).

By the quadratic growth, the left side is bounded below by c m^2 for a fixed c > 0 along all sufficiently large multiples of r_0. Hence
    limsup_{m->oo} h^0(X, m K_X + A_X) / m^2 > 0,
contradicting kappa_sigma(X,K_X) = 1 with the fixed ample twist A_X. It follows that L^2 . H^{n-2} = 0. The nefness criterion then implies that L = K_Y is nef, including on curves in the singular locus. The remaining conclusions, including the effective exceptional comparison u^*K_X = rho^*K_Y + E and its support condition, are supplied by the positive-perturbations proposition. This completes the proof of the theorem.

Two features keep the argument within reach. First, the perturbations stay on one model: the discrepancy count and the cycle-class decrease show that after finitely many steps the minimal model program stops moving codimension two, so one very general surface passes through every later stage, and every section estimate is made on a single fixed resolution with a single fixed twist. Second, the contradiction is numerical: positive surface square forces quadratic growth of sections, which numerical dimension one forbids, while a negative curve forces a positive surface square, which the vanishing square forbids. The same pair of ideas reaches smooth threefolds through Mori's classification, fourfolds through the work of Alexeev, Hacon, and Kawamata, and the numerical-dimension-one stratum in every dimension. The result covers every smooth fivefold with pseudo-effective canonical divisor of numerical dimension one, with no condition on the Euler characteristic, and the comparison of canonical divisors is effective.
