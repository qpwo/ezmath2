Consider the zero-field Sherrington-Kirkpatrick spin glass, with energy
functional
H_{n,J}(sigma)=-n^{-1/2}sum_{1le i<jle n}J_{ij}sigma_isigma_j
on spin configurations sigma=(sigma_1,...,sigma_n)in{-1,1}^n. The
couplings J_{ij}, 1le i<jle n, are independent real random variables
of mean zero and variance one, and the extension J_{ji}=J_{ij}, J_{ii}=0
makes J a symmetric matrix. Two coupling laws are considered: the
standard Gaussian, and the uniform law on the two-point set {-1,1}. At
inverse temperature beta the Gibbs measure on the cube is
pi_{n,J,beta}(sigma)=Z_{n,J,beta}^{-1}exp(-beta H_{n,J}(sigma)),
with Z_{n,J,beta} the normalizer. The value beta=1 is fixed. The
shorthand pi_{n,J} denotes pi_{n,J,1}.

The interaction is disordered and of infinite range: each spin interacts
with every other spin with a coupling of typical size n^{-1/2}, of random
sign, so that ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic bonds coexist on the
same footing. Spin configurations of comparable energy then differ at a
macroscopic number of sites, and a single global magnetization is not a
sufficient summary of the low-temperature phases. Parisi introduced an
infinite family of overlap parameters, organised hierarchically, to
describe the resulting state space.

./pdf/parisi-1979.pdf

The static theory of this hierarchical structure is by now a theorem
rather than a heuristic. The cavity-method interpolation of Aizenman,
Lebowitz, and Ruelle and the subsequent reorganization of Aizenman, Sims,
and Starr give the Parisi variational formula as an identity for the
infinite-volume free energy of the SK model. Guerra proved the matching
lower bound, uniformly in the size of the system, by a two-parameter
interpolation between the SK Hamiltonian and a mixture of independent
spin systems. Talagrand completed the identification by proving the
corresponding upper bound, confirming the Parisi prediction in full
generality. In mixed p-spin models the analogous statements are due to
Panchenko (Annals of Probability 42 (2014), 946-958) and to Guerra and
Toninelli, who proved existence of the thermodynamic limit and the
associated variational characterization. Modern offshoots, concerned
with replica symmetry breaking in more general external fields and with
lower-temperature regimes, include the constructions of Lopatto and of
Aronow and Lopatto, and the existence results of Zhou, all at full
replica symmetry breaking depth.

Consider the heat-bath process on {-1,1}^n governed by the Gibbs
measure pi_{n,J}. Each site carries an independent Poisson clock of
rate one. When the clock at site i rings, the spin sigma_i is
replaced by a fresh sample from its conditional law given the other
n-1 spins. The rate of the jump at site i is one and the jump
distribution depends only on the current configuration through the local
field
h_i(sigma)=n^{-1/2}sum_{jne i}J_{ij}sigma_j,
in the sense that the accepted new value ain{-1,1} has probability
(1+atanh h_i(sigma))/2 and is independent of sigma_i conditional
on sigma_{-i}. The resulting Markov process on the cube is reversible
and stationary for pi_{n,J}. Write P_t^J=e^{-tH} for its semigroup
acting on L^2(pi_{n,J}), with Hge0 the heat-bath generator. One
unit of time corresponds to n site updates on average.

The equilibrium spin autocorrelation measures how much the configuration
remembers its initial state after time t under P_t^J, averaged both
over the stationary Gibbs randomness of the spins and over the sites:
C_{n,J}(t)=n^{-1}sum_{i=1}^n<sigma_i,P_t^Jsigma_i>_{pi_{n,J}}, tge0.
Since P_0^J is the identity and P_t^Jsigma_i is the conditional
expectation of sigma_i given t steps of the process, C_{n,J}(0)=1
and C_{n,J} is nonincreasing and takes values in [0,1]. At
beta=1 the correlation decays on a time scale that grows with n,
and the appropriate rescaling defines
A_{n,J}(s)=n^{1/3}C_{n,J}(sn^{2/3}), s>0.
The only randomness in A_{n,J} is the coupling matrix J; thermal
fluctuations and the noise of the clock process are already averaged in
C_{n,J}.

A point of terminology will be used repeatedly. A bound holds with tight
constants if it holds with some nonnegative random constant on an event of
probability tending to one. In applications that select a subsequence,
include the tight bounds, the eigenvalues, and the countably many relevant
error quantities in the joint law before passing to a Skorokhod
representation. On the represented space, finite bounds and coordinate
convergence are deterministic statements. The conditional laws of the
remaining finite-dimensional variables lift the representation to the
original finite problems, and a further diagonal extraction handles
quantities that vanish in iterated limits. The representation says
nothing about convergence of the original sequence; each subsequential
limit must be identified. Once this is done, the subsequence criterion
gives the asserted distributional convergence.

When an estimate is applied to a disorder-dependent test f, the quenched
ineuality is understood to hold on one event for every test in the stated
class, with the displayed bounds on the norm, energy, and polynomial
growth of that test. One way to obtain such an event, used repeatedly, is
to bound the error by the stated norms times a common nonnegative random
quantity R_n. Convergence R_nto0 then permits arbitrary measurable
choices of the test. Convergence on a countable core alone is not used to
infer this uniformity.
