# Uniform Permanence inWeakly Reversible Mass-Action Systems




maketitle

Abstract: 
A chemical reaction network records which mixtures of molecules turn
into which. Under mass-action kinetics the rate of a reaction is
proportional to the concentrations of its reactants, so the concentrations
obey a polynomial differential equation. A network is weakly
reversible when every reaction can be undone by a chain of reactions.
We prove that every finite weakly reversible network with fixed positive
rate constants is permanent on each positive stoichiometric class:
every solution is global and positive, and after a transient that may
depend on the initial point the whole trajectory is trapped in one compact
convex set that depends only on the network, the rates, and the class.
It follows that there are constants 0<eps<eps^{-1} bounding every
concentration from below and above for all large time, uniformly over all
initial points in the class, even when that class is unbounded. The
central tool, proved in full, is a lemma saying that a minimum of finitely
many affine functions can be arranged to reveal the exponent vector of any
point at which it is active, to any prescribed tolerance.

# Introduction

## Reaction networks and mass-action kinetics

Fix d chemical species. A complex is a vector
yinZ_{ge0}^d whose i-th entry counts molecules of species i in a
mixture. A reaction network consists of a finite set
CsubsetZ_{ge0}^d of complexes and a finite set Rsubset Ctimes C
of reactions; we write yto y' for (y,y')in R. For example,
A+Bto C is the reaction from e_A+e_B to e_C.

Assign to each reaction a positive rate constant k_{yto y'}.
Mass-action kinetics postulates that yto y' runs at speed
k_{yto y'}x^y, where x_ige0 is the concentration of species i and
x^y=prod_i x_i^{y_i}. Since that reaction changes the state by
y'-y, the concentrations satisfy

  dot x=f(x):=sum_{yto y'in R} k_{yto y'},x^y,(y'-y).

This is the mass-action ordinary differential equation; we always use
fixed positive rates. Its right-hand side is a combination of the
difference vectors y'-y, so f(x) always lies in the
stoichiometric subspace
[
  S:=span_{R}{y'-y:yto y'in R}.
]
Hence every solution starting at c stays in c+S. Because concentrations
are positive, the relevant state space is the positive stoichiometric
class
[
  P:=(c+S)capR_{>0}^d,
]
the part of the conservation surface on which every species is present.
The class P may be bounded or unbounded; the unbounded case is where the
statement below is delicate.

## An example

Take d=2, C={e_1,e_2}, and R={e_1to e_2, e_2to e_1} with rates
k_1,k_2>0. Then
[
  dot x_1=-k_1x_1+k_2x_2, dot x_2=k_1x_1-k_2x_2,
]
and S=span{e_2-e_1}, so each class is a segment
x_1+x_2=c_0 with c_0>0. Every solution on the class converges to the
interior equilibrium
(k_2c_0/(k_1+k_2), k_1c_0/(k_1+k_2)), so the class is permanent
and K_P can be that single point. This is the easy, bounded,
single-equilibrium case. The theorem also covers unbounded classes and
networks whose long-time behavior is far richer than convergence to one
point.

## Persistence and permanence

Two failures are possible: a concentration can approach 0, or it can grow
without bound. A global positive solution is persistent if
liminf_{tto∞}x_i(t)>0 for every i; these lower bounds may depend
on the trajectory. We call the system permanent on the class P if
every solution starting in P is global and positive, and if there is a
single compact set Ksubset P that eventually contains every such
solution. The entry time into K may depend on the initial point; K may
not. Permanence is thus stronger than persistence: it demands uniform
lower and upper bounds and a single absorbing set for the whole class.

## Weak reversibility and the main result

A network is weakly reversible if for every reaction yto y'
there is a directed chain of reactions leading from y' back to y.
Informally, no reaction is a one-way dead end. This purely combinatorial
condition turns out to control the long-time behavior of (eq:ma).


Let CsubsetZ_{ge0}^d be finite and Rsubset Ctimes C weakly
reversible, and fix positive rates in (eq:ma). For every positive
stoichiometric class P there is a nonempty compact convex set
K_Psubset P with the following property. Every x^0in P gives a unique
global positive solution of (eq:ma), and there is a finite
T(x^0)ge0 with x(t)in K_P for all tge T(x^0). The set K_P is
forward invariant. Hence there is eps_Pin(0,1), depending only on the
network, the rates, and P, such that every such solution satisfies

  eps_Ple x_i(t)leeps_P^{-1}
  (1le ile d, tge T(x^0)).



The uniformity in (eq:bounds) holds within a fixed class and fixed
rates. The transient time may depend on x^0, but the bounds do not: they
are decided before x^0 is chosen.

## Background

The structural theory starts with Horn and Jackson and with Feinberg
[cite]. A system is complex balanced if
at some positive state the total incoming and outgoing flux agree at every
complex; then each positive class contains a unique positive equilibrium
and carries an entropy Lyapunov function. Complex balance constrains the
rates, while weak reversibility is purely combinatorial, and the gap
between them is what makes weak reversibility hard. Feinberg
[cite] raised the question of excluding boundary limits.
Partial answers followed: Anderson [cite] proved convergence
for complex-balanced systems with a single linkage class; Craciun, Nazarov,
and Pantea [cite] proved permanence for two-species
weakly reversible (more generally endotactic) systems; Pantea
[cite] proved persistence of bounded trajectories when
dim S=2; Gopalkrishnan, Miller, and Shiu
[cite] proved permanence under strong
endotacticity, which covers every weakly reversible network with one
linkage class; and Boros and Hofbauer [cite] gave another
proof for one linkage class, producing a compact forward-invariant
absorbing set. Craciun's toric differential inclusions [cite]
prove persistence for bounded positive trajectories with rates bounded
above and below. The immediate input here is the affine lemma and trapping
construction of [cite], which produce a compact convex invariant
set depending on the chosen initial point; the present argument removes
that dependence.

## Proof strategy

Fix a representative c of a class. For hin(0,1) and an exponent vector
pinR^d, write X_h(p)=(h^{p_1},...,h^{p_d}). Every point of the box
B_h=[h,h^{-1}]^d is X_h(p) for p=log x/log hin[-1,1]^d. As
hto0, monomials are ordered by exponents, because h^{p· v}to0
whenever p· v>0. This turns monomial algebra into the geometry of
exponent vectors.

The proof builds a concave piecewise-linear function
F_h(x)=min_lambda (r_lambda· x+b_lambda(h)), a minimum of finitely
many affine functions whose slopes r_lambdain[-1,1]^d are fixed as
hto0. The affine lemma (Section~sec:affine) guarantees that at any
point where the label with slope r is active, the true exponent p is
within a chosen tolerance of r. Choosing the tolerance small relative to
the directions v that matter (coordinate vectors and differences of
complexes), an active slope reproduces every sign p· v we need.

At c the active labels have zero slope, so F_h(c)=M_h is the global
maximum and the plateau K_h={x:F_h(x)=M_h} is compact, convex, contains
c, and lies inside interior B_{sqrt h}.

Along a trajectory in P, the function F_h does not decrease, and outside
K_h it strictly increases at a uniform rate delta_h>0. The increase
comes from a flux estimate: order the complexes by the level r· y, cut
between consecutive levels, and use weak reversibility to show that the net
upward flux across each cut is nonnegative, and positive when a reaction
crosses it. Hence r· f(x)ge0, strictly positive when
rnotin S^perp. At points of P∖ K_h every active slope lies
outside S^perp, so the increase is strict. A trajectory that stays in
B_h must therefore reach K_h in finite time and remain there.

Finally, K_hsubsetinterior B_{sqrt h} lets us change scale. Repeated
square roots, capped at a scale h_P fixed by the class, produce the single
absorbing set K_P=K_{h_P}cap(c+S).

Section~sec:affine proves the affine lemma,
Section~sec:trapping builds the plateaus and the strict increase, and
Section~sec:perm assembles the common absorbing set.

# Affine minima with arbitrary tolerance

We use a finite minimum of affine functions on the positive orthant. Its key
feature is that an active slope approximates the exponent vector of the
point where it is active, to a tolerance chosen in advance. The tolerance
may be a wild, even discontinuous, function of the slope.

Fix mge1, uinR^m, and 0<h<1, and put
X_h(u)=(h^{u_1},...,h^{u_m}). A label
lambda=(r_lambda,b_lambda) consists of a slope r_lambdainR^m,
independent of h, and an offset b_lambda(h) defined for all
sufficiently small h>0. For a finite nonempty family Λ of labels,
set
[
  F_h(x)=min_{lambdainΛ}(r_lambda· x+b_lambda(h)).
]
A label is active at x if its affine value equals F_h(x); every
minimizing label is active in case of a tie. Offsets in a finite family are
restricted to a common interval of small h.

[Affine approximation]
Let mge1, let a<b be real, and let E:[a,b]^mto(0,∞) be any
function. There is a finite nonempty family Λ of labels with
r_lambdain[a,b]^m and b_lambda(h)=o(h^a) (hto0+) such that the
following holds. If h_nuto0+, if p^nuinR^m converges to some
pin[a,b]^m, and if a fixed lambdainΛ is active at
X_{h_nu}(p^nu) for every nu, then
[
  norm{p-r_lambda}_∞<E(r_lambda).
]
The slopes and the family do not depend on h. No regularity of E is
assumed, and the points p^nu need not lie in [a,b]^m.


Proof. 
We induct on m. Write mu(u)=min_i u_i. The largest coordinate of
X_h(u) is h^{mu(u)}. The proof first builds a family that works near a
prescribed value t of the minimum exponent, then patches finitely many
such families together using decreasing offsets -h^{q_j} that force the
minimum exponent of any active label into the correct range.

Throughout, asymptotic comparisons use fixed gaps between limiting
exponents: if p_i>q then eventually p_i^nuge q+delta for some
delta>0, so h_nu^{p_i^nu}=o(h_nu^q); if p_i<q then
h_nu^{p_i^nu}/h_nu^qto∞. We only compare across strict gaps.

medskipnoindentStep 1: a family for a fixed minimum.
Fix tin[a,b] and build a finite family Λ_t containing (tone,0)
with mu(r)=t for every slope, working whenever mu(p)=t. If m=1 or
t=b, take Λ_t={(tone,0)}: a point pin[a,b]^m with
mu(p)=t then equals tone, giving zero error. Otherwise m>1 and
t<b. Call a label of type k if exactly k entries of its slope
exceed t. Build the types k=1,...,m-1 in order, after choosing
beta_k with
[
  t<beta_k<b,
  beta_k<beta_{k-1} (k>1),
  beta_k-t<E(r) for every slope r of type k-1.
]
These finitely many positive conditions are satisfiable, and
beta_{k-1}>beta_k>t.

For each k-element set Usubset{1,...,m} and sin[beta_k,b]^U,
let r(s) agree with s on U and equal t off U. Apply the induction
hypothesis in dimension k to the cube [beta_k,b]^U with tolerance
s|-> E(r(s)). For every resulting label (s,d), add to Λ_t
the type-k label

  r=r(s), c(h)=-h^{beta_k}+d(h), d(h)=o(h^{beta_k}).

All slopes of Λ_t have minimum exponent t, and all offsets are
o(h^t). The negative offset favors raising slope entries on the
coordinates where X_h(p) is small, and the decreasing beta_k make that
preference stronger with each type.

To verify the guarantee, let p^nuto pin[a,b]^m with mu(p)=t and
suppose a type-k label (r,c) stays active within Λ_t along
h_nuto0+. Let U be its raised set. For k>0, comparing with
(tone,0) gives

  sum_{iin U}(r_i-t)h^{p_i^nu}le h^{beta_k}-d(h)=(1+o(1))h^{beta_k}.

Each coefficient r_i-tgebeta_k-t>0. If some p_i<beta_k, the left
side divided by h^{beta_k} tends to +∞, contradicting the bounded
right side. Hence

  p_igebeta_k (iin U).

On the same U, all labels share their behaviour off U and the term
-h^{beta_k}, so (s,d) is active in the family furnished by induction
for the sub-vector p_U^nu. Its limit p_U lies in [beta_k,b]^U by
(eq:raised) and p_ile b; the induction hypothesis, which allows
sub-vectors approaching from outside the cube, gives

  max_{iin U}abs{p_i-r_i}<E(r).


Coordinates off U remain (this includes type 0, where U=∅).
We claim that if k<m-1 then

  p_ilebeta_{k+1}(inotin U).

Otherwise pick jnotin U with p_j>beta_{k+1} and a type-(k+1) label
raised on W=Ucup{j}. Every exponent on W exceeds beta_{k+1}: for
iin U, p_igebeta_k>beta_{k+1}, and p_j>beta_{k+1}. So the
competitor's value minus that of (tone,0) equals
-h^{beta_{k+1}}+o(h^{beta_{k+1}}), because each monomial on W is
o(h^{beta_{k+1}}) and the offset is o(h^{beta_{k+1}}). For the active
label the same difference is 0 if k=0, and is at least
-h^{beta_k}+o(h^{beta_k}) (each term being ge0) if k>0. Since
h^{beta_k}=o(h^{beta_{k+1}}), the competitor is strictly smaller for
small h, contradicting activity. This proves (eq:offU). Hence off
U,
[
  abs{p_i-r_i}lebeta_{k+1}-t<E(r),
]
because the choice of beta_{k+1} gives beta_{k+1}-t<E(r) for every
slope r of type k. If k=m-1, exactly one coordinate lies off U; by
mu(p)=t and p_igebeta_k>t on U, that coordinate equals t, where
r also equals t, so its error is 0<E(r). This proves the required
strict bound for every active label at the fixed minimum.

medskipnoindentStep 2: extension to nearby minima.
For a fixed label lambda=(r,c)inΛ_t, let A_lambdasubset[a,b]^m
be the set of limits pin[a,b]^m of sequences p^nu with h_nuto0+
such that lambda is active within Λ_t at X_{h_nu}(p^nu).
A_lambda is closed: if z_nin A_lambda and z_nto z, choose
0<h_n<1/n and u_n with norm{u_n-z_n}_∞<1/n and lambda active
at X_{h_n}(u_n); then u_nto z, so zin A_lambda. This argument uses
actual active points and needs no continuity of the offsets in h.

The set of bad limits
[
  B_t=∪_{lambda=(r,c)inΛ_t}
  {pin A_lambda:norm{p-r}_∞ge E(r)}
]
is compact, being a finite union of closed subsets of the compact [a,b]^m.
Step 1 shows B_t contains no point with mu(p)=t. Since mu is
continuous and B_t is compact, there is rho_t>0 with B_t disjoint
from {muin I_t}, I_t:=(t-rho_t,t+rho_t): if B_t is nonempty take
rho_t<min_{pin B_t}abs{mu(p)-t}, and if B_t is empty any
rho_t>0 works. Thus Λ_t has the limiting activity property
whenever mu(p)in I_t. Shrink rho_t if necessary so that every local
offset obeys

  c(h)=o(h^{t+rho_t}).

For a nonsingleton family choose t+rho_t<min_kbeta_k and use
(eq:local-labels); for a singleton the offset is zero.

medskipnoindentStep 3: combining the families.
Take a finite subcover of [a,b] by the intervals I_t, discard contained
intervals (keeping one of any coinciding pair), and order the surviving
centers as t_1<...<t_N. Write I_{t_j}=(L_j,R_j). Noncontainment
forces
[
  L_1<...<L_N, R_1<...<R_N.
]
Consecutive intervals overlap, since if R_jle L_{j+1} a point of
[R_j,L_{j+1}]subset(t_j,t_{j+1})subset[a,b] would be uncovered. The
first interval contains a and the last contains b. For 1le j<N
choose a cut
[
  q_jin(max{t_j,L_{j+1}}, min{t_{j+1},R_j}),
]
which is nonempty because consecutive intervals overlap and each contains
its own center. With q_0=a and q_N=b,

  t_j<q_j<t_{j+1} (1le j<N),
  [q_{j-1},q_j]subset I_{t_j} (1le jle N).

The sequential formulation yields the uniform statement used below. The
order of choices matters: the family, hence its finite slope set, is fixed
before the upper bound on h is chosen.

[Uniform activity bound]
Let mge1, a<b, and E:[a,b]^mto(0,∞) be as in
Lemma~lem:affine, and fix a family Λ supplied by that lemma.
There is h_0in(0,1) such that for every 0<h<h_0 and every
pin[a,b]^m, each label lambdainΛ active at X_h(p) satisfies
norm{p-r_lambda}_∞<E(r_lambda).


Proof. 
Otherwise there are h_nuto0+, points p^nuin[a,b]^m, and active
labels lambda_nu with
norm{p^nu-r_{lambda_nu}}_∞ge E(r_{lambda_nu}). By finiteness
and compactness, pass to a subsequence on which lambda_nu=lambda is
fixed and p^nuto pin[a,b]^m. The inequality passes to the limit and
contradicts Lemma~lem:affine. No continuity of E is needed, because
its argument r_lambda is fixed on the subsequence.
