Abstract: 
Let (W,D) be a projective QQ-factorial klt pair with effective rational boundary, and put
P=-(K_W+D). We show that a positive Cartier multiple of P has a nonzero section provided that
P is nef, that its pullback to a resolution carries a semipositive metric with locally bounded
weights, and that the resolution has nonzero structure-sheaf Euler characteristic. Consequently a
smooth rationally connected projective variety with smoothly semipositive anticanonical bundle has a
nonzero anticanonical plurisection that is naturally invariant under every algebraic torus action.

# Introduction

## The question
Fix a pair (W,D) and write P=-(K_W+D) for the anti-log-canonical divisor. Nefness of P means
that Pcdot Cge 0 for every curve C. Nefness alone produces no divisor on W: it is a numerical
shadow of positivity, not a section. The nonvanishing problem asks the sharper question: is
H^0(W,mP)neq 0 for some positive integer m? A ``yes'' means P is not only nef but carries
genuine holomorphic functions. This paper answers ``yes'' under an explicit analytic hypothesis, and
then applies the answer to quotients by torus actions.

## The criterion
All varieties are integral and defined over CC.

[Bounded-metric nonvanishing]
Let (W,D) be a projective QQ-factorial klt pair with effective rational boundary, and let
P=-(K_W+D). Assume:
[label=(roman*)]
 P is nef;
 on a smooth projective resolution rho:widetilde Wto W, the pullback rho^*P carries a
semipositive metric whose weights are locally bounded;
 chi(widetilde W,OO_{widetilde W})neq 0.

Then H^0(W,OO_W(mP))neq 0 for some integer m>0 with mP Cartier.


The hypotheses play distinct roles. Nefness lets us pair with curves and push classes; the bounded
semipositive metric is exactly the positivity that hard Lefschetz and adjoint direct-image theory
consume; the Euler characteristic is the spark that produces twisted differential forms. The
QQ-factoriality and the effective boundary enter the birational numerical arguments and the
cotangent-subsheaf step respectively; both are used and neither is hidden. All three hypotheses are
stable under passing to a common higher resolution, because bounded psh weights pull back and
chi(OO) is a birational invariant of smooth projective varieties.

## The application
The point of controlling the boundary is that the theorem survives quotienting by a torus.

[Invariant sections]
Let Z be a smooth rationally connected projective variety whose anticanonical bundle has a smooth
semipositive Hermitian metric. Then for every algebraic torus T acting on Z there is an integer
m>0 with
[
H^0(Z,-mK_Z)^Tneq 0,
]
the invariants taken with respect to the natural anticanonical linearization induced by the
differential of the action on T_Z.


The word ``natural'' is essential. To build the quotient we perturb the anticanonical class by a small
ample class and a small character; this auxiliary linearization only selects an open set. The
linearization in the conclusion is the differential one and is never shifted. A character twist
changes the answer; Remark~rem:character records the elementary example on mathbb P^1.bbP^1.

Together with the finite-cover structure theorem of the companion article [cite],
Theorem~thm:equivariant yields Corollary~cor:smooth: for every smooth connected projective
variety X with smoothly semipositive anticanonical bundle, H^0(X,-mK_X)neq 0 for some m>0.
The companion proves Corollary~cor:smooth directly by a finite-volume argument; here we route it
through the bounded-metric criterion and a stable quotient, borrowing only the companion's
finite-cover structure and finite-'etale norm lemma. The positive multiple cannot be reduced to the
first power in general: an Enriques surface has H^0(S,-K_S)=0, and taking the product with
mathbb P^1bbP^1 gives a smoothly semipositive example with no first-power section (Ssec:descent).

## How the proofs run

Theorem~thm:klt. Eight steps.
[label=(arabic*)]
 Twisted forms. Riemann--Roch and the nonzero Euler characteristic produce, for
infinitely many twists tto∞, a nonzero section of Ω^p_{widetilde W} ⊗ OO(trho^*P)
(hard Lefschetz with multiplier ideals).
 Determinant. Determinants of these forms give effective divisors
N_isim_{QQ}M+m_iP, m_ito∞, with -M pseudoeffective.
 Maximal fibration. Choose a fibration pi:Sto W, f:Sto Y of maximal base dimension
whose polarization is pseudoeffectively dominated by pi^*P; maximality forbids any extra pencil
controlling the general fiber.
 Decomposition. The N_i split as rpi^*P=f^*V+H with V a line bundle on Y and H
effective, so that over every prime of Y some component of f^*G is missing from H.
 Rounding. Discrepancy rounding of the klt pair gives E=lceil Rrceil,
Xi=lceil Rrceil-R, and K_S+pi^*P+Xi=E; maximality gives rank-one adjoint direct images.
 Fiber-integral metric. Normalized L^a-integrals of a section of H converge to a
fiberwise essential supremum, giving a bounded semipositive metric on V; the missing components bound
it at the boundary.
 Vanishing. A small mixture with a strictly positive metric (Skoda) keeps the multiplier
ideal trivial, so Fujino's Koll'ar--Nadel vanishing applies to every nonnegative twist.
 Euler polynomial. Vanishing for all twists makes chi(Y,mathcal GcalG ⊗  aV) a
polynomial positive at a=0; some positive value is nonzero, whence a section of mP on W.


Theorem~thm:equivariant. An ample perturbation with a small character makes the stable
locus equal the semistable one, and W=U/T is projective. Luna slices exhibit a klt boundary
D=sum(1-1/e_B)B and identify -K_Z|_U=q^*P with the natural linearization. Convexity along
algebraic arcs shows translated lifts have no poles; compactifying the action graph upgrades this to a
locally uniform bound, which makes the orbit-supremum metric bounded and psh (Kiselman). Rational
connectedness gives chi=1 on a resolution of W. Theorem~thm:klt then yields a section, the
bounded quotient metric bounds its pullback, and removable singularities extend it over the complement
of U as an invariant global section.

## Context
Two notions must be separated. A divisor is num-effective if it is numerically equivalent to an
effective real divisor; a section of a positive multiple asks for linear equivalence. The two
differ exactly where our analytic hypothesis enters. Lazi'c--Matsumura--Peternell--Tsakanikas--Xie
[cite] prove num-effectivity of nef anti-log-canonical divisors on projective log-canonical
threefolds that are QQ-factorial or have rational singularities; their Theorem~E, under a nonzero
chi(OO) hypothesis, is the closest predecessor to Theorem~thm:klt. We upgrade num-effectivity
to an actual section in arbitrary dimension, at the price of the bounded metric. M"uller
[cite] proves ordinary nonvanishing for klt pairs with semiample anticanonical divisor on
general rationally connected fibers (all dimensions) and unconditionally in dimension three, plus an
equivariant statement for commutative groups. Our equivariant theorem reaches the same invariant
conclusion with a different positivity hypothesis (smooth semipositivity rather than semiampleness),
restricted to smooth rationally connected varieties and torus actions. That smooth semipositivity is
genuinely weaker already appears in the surface classification of Chen--Filip--Sun--Tosatti--Zhang
[cite]: rational surfaces with smoothly semipositive anticanonical bundle and a smooth
elliptic anticanonical curve of nontorsion degree-zero normal bundle, whose anticanonical bundle is not
semiample.

The technical ancestry: hard Lefschetz with multiplier ideals is the mechanism of
Demailly--Peternell--Schneider [cite]; the determinant step is Lazi'c--Peternell [cite],
adapted to anticanonical geometry in [cite], whose Theorem~4.1 controls the negative first
Chern class of the saturated cotangent-tensor subsheaf. Positivity transfers to the base of a fibration
through adjoint direct images of Berndtsson [cite], Berndtsson--Pu{a}un
[cite], and Pu{a}un--Takayama [cite]; we use the singular version
because rounding leaves fractional divisorial poles even when the metric is bounded. Skoda's
integrability theorem [cite] and Fujino's Koll'ar--Nadel vanishing [cite] let one
metric construction govern all twists at once. On the quotient side we use projective GIT
[cite], torus linearization [cite], attracting cells of
Bial{}ynicki-Birula [cite], Luna's slice theorem [cite], and Kiselman's minimum
principle [cite]. The smooth application draws on Yau's prescribed-Ricci theorem
[cite], the structure theorem of Demailly--Peternell--Schneider [cite], and
Campana--Demailly--Peternell [cite].

# A bounded-metric criterion for klt pairs

Throughout, (W,D) satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem~thm:klt and P=-(K_W+D). Equalities of
line bundles mean linear equivalence; equalities of rational divisors mean QQ-linear equivalence. A
statement about an effective divisor, its support, or a coefficient is a statement about the actual
divisor; we keep these uses apart.

## Analytic and numerical tools

We record the imported results in the exact forms used.

Hard Lefschetz with multiplier ideals. Let B be a line bundle on a compact K"ahler n-fold
S with a semipositive singular metric h of weight varphi. Then cite[Theorem~2.1.1]{DPS2001}
gives a surjection

H^0(S,Ω_S^{n-q} ⊗  B ⊗ II(h))→ H^q(S,K_S ⊗  B ⊗ II(h)),

where II(h) tests local integrability of |g|^2e^{-varphi} in a frame. In particular every positive
power of a bounded metric has trivial multiplier ideal.

Singular adjoint direct images. For a projective surjective morphism f:Sto Y of smooth
varieties with connected fibers and a semipositive singular metric on B, the theorem of
Pu{a}un--Takayama cite[S3.2.1 and Theorems~3.3.4--3.3.5]{PaunTakayama2018} applies: if the multiplier
ideal is trivial on the total space and on general fibers, the fiberwise L^2 metric on
f_*(K_{S/Y}+B) has semipositive curvature in the singular sense. On a base-change open where the
direct image has rank one and its generator has finite integral, the negative logarithm of that integral
is a psh weight. The integral agrees with the metric almost everywhere; no continuity or global base
change is needed. We apply this for each integer twist separately, so the base-change open may depend on
the twist.

Koll'ar--Nadel vanishing. The theorem of Fujino cite[Theorem~1.3]{Fujino2018}: if f:Sto Y is
a surjective morphism from a compact K"ahler manifold to a projective variety and a singular metric on
B has curvature at least ε f^*omega_A for some ε>0 and a positive curvature form of
an ample line bundle A on Y, then

H^i(Y,R^j f_*(K_S ⊗  B ⊗ II(h)))=0qquad(i>0, jge0).

Only j=0 is used.

A numerical fact. Because W is QQ-factorial, pushforward along a birational morphism
pi:Sto W (with S smooth) preserves pseudoeffectivity; compare cite[Lemma~3.4]{LMPTX2023}. Indeed,
if F is numerically trivial on S, then pi^*pi_*F-F is exceptional and numerically trivial over
W; the negativity lemma applied to it and its negative forces it to vanish. Intersecting
pi^*pi_*F=F with curves dominating curves of W makes pi_*F numerically trivial. Hence
pi_* is a well-defined continuous linear map on finite-dimensional numerical class groups, sending
effective classes to effective classes and preserving their closed cone. Pullback of a pseudoeffective
QQ-Cartier class by the birational maps used below also stays pseudoeffective.

## Twisted forms and the determinant

The first goal is an unbounded sequence of effective divisors differing from one fixed class by
multiples of P. We adapt the determinant argument of Lazi'c--Peternell cite[Lemma~4.1]{LP2018}, as
used in cite[Lemma~5.1]{LMPTX2023}. Here the twisting exponents are already positive and unbounded, so a
basis containing a high-exponent section has an unbounded sum of exponents; this lets us avoid importing
the numerical-nontriviality hypothesis of the cited lemma.


Either H^0(W,mP)ne0 for some positive Cartier multiple, or there are a Weil divisor M, strictly
increasing positive integers m_i, and effective Weil divisors N_i with

N_isim_{QQ}M+m_iP, -MinPE(W),

all m_iP Cartier.


Proof. 
The case W a point is immediate, so take dim W=n>0. Choose a smooth projective resolution
rho:widetilde Wto W on which the metric is bounded, and r_0>0 with r_0P Cartier.

Step 1 (Riemann--Roch). The function tmapstochi(widetilde W,K_{widetilde W}+trho^*(r_0P)) is
a polynomial in t. At t=0 it equals (-1)^nchi(widetilde W,OO_{widetilde W})ne0 by hypothesis,
so it is nonzero for all but finitely many positive integers t. For such t at least one
cohomology group of K_{widetilde W}+trho^*(r_0P) is nonzero.

Step 2 (hard Lefschetz). Apply (eq:HL) with B=trho^*(r_0P) and its bounded semipositive
metric, whose multiplier ideal is trivial. If H^qne0, we get a nonzero section
[
sigmain H^0(widetilde W,Ω^{n-q}_{widetilde W} ⊗ OO(trho^*P)).
]
There are finitely many cohomological degrees q, so a single fixed q, and hence a fixed p=n-q,
occurs for an infinite sequence t=n_ito∞, giving nonzero sections
[
sigma_iin H^0(widetilde W,Ω^p_{widetilde W} ⊗ OO(n_irho^*P)),
 n_ito∞.
]

Step 3 (the p=0 case). If p=0, projecting to W and using rho_*OO_{widetilde W}=OO_W
gives H^0(W,OO(n_i r_0P))ne0, the first alternative.

Step 4 (the determinant line). Now p>0. Work over the function field K=CC(W). Trivialize
r_0P rationally, so untwisting the sigma_i produces vectors in ∧^pΩ^1_{K/CC}. Let
F_K be their K-span, of dimension b>0. Its determinant is a line
[
det F_K subset ∧^b(∧^pΩ^1_{K/CC})
 subset (Ω^1_{K/CC})^{ ⊗  pb},
]
the last inclusion via alternating tensors in characteristic zero. Intersect this line with the
reflexive tensor power (Ω_W^1)^{[ ⊗  pb]} and take the saturated reflexive rank-one
extension; it is OO_W(M) for a Weil divisor M.

Step 5 (a section). The determinant of any b of the untwisted vectors is a nonzero section of
OO_W(M+sum n_iP) on the smooth locus of W, giving the identity. It is regular at every
codimension-one point, since rho is an isomorphism there and the sigma_i are holomorphic;
reflexivity extends it across codimension two. Its zero divisor N is an effective Weil divisor, so
Nsim_{QQ}M+sum n_iP for the chosen subset of exponents.

Step 6 (unbounded exponents). The exponent sums are unbounded: fix any nonzero vector at an
arbitrarily high twist n_i and extend it to a basis; all exponents are positive, so the sum is at
least n_i. Selecting a strictly increasing subsequence of these sums gives the m_i, effective
divisors N_i, and (eq:Ni). Finally, (W,D) is projective klt with effective boundary and
- (K_W+D)=P nef, so cite[Theorem~4.1]{LMPTX2023} applies to the saturated rank-one subsheaf
OO_W(M) of the reflexive cotangent tensor power: its negative first Chern class is pseudoeffective.
Since W is QQ-factorial, -M is pseudoeffective, giving -MinPE(W).


## A maximal dominated fibration

Assume now the second alternative of Lemma~lem:determinant. The next goal is to write a multiple
of a birational pullback of P as a pullback from a base plus an effective divisor, with a component
missing over each base prime. That missing component will keep the induced base metric free of a
divisorial pole. To get it, take a base of maximal dimension whose polarization is pseudoeffectively
dominated by P; maximality will forbid pencils that are nonconstant on a general fiber.

Consider diagrams

[column sep=large]
S arrow[r,"f"] arrow[d,"pi"'] & Y
W &


with S,Y smooth projective, pi birational, f surjective, and

d,pi^*P-f^*AinPE(S)

for some integer d>0 and ample line bundle A on Y; the map to a point is allowed, with the
polarization omitted. Choose a diagram with dim Y maximal.

We may require connected general fibers. Passing to the relative algebraic closure of CC(Y) in
CC(W) is a generically finite change of base; the pullback of A there is big and, on a smooth
projective model, dominates a small ample class, so (eq:domination) persists after scaling, and
smooth birational changes of the base are harmless. With CC(Y) algebraically closed in CC(W), the
finite map in the Stein factorization of f is birational, hence an isomorphism as Y is normal. Thus
f has connected fibers and f_*OO_S=OO_Y, as the direct-image theorem needs.

We also use a normal equidimensional intermediate model

[row sep=large,column sep=large]
& S arrow[d] arrow[ddl,bend right=25,"f"'] arrow[ddr,bend left=25,"pi"] &
& S_0 arrow[dl,"f_0"'] arrow[dr,"pi_0"'] &
Y && W .


Here pi_0 is birational and S resolves S_0 and the pair on W. Construction: start from a
projective graph of the rational map, flatten the main component by a projective modification of the
base [cite], replace the base by a smooth projective modification, and normalize the
main flat transform; finite normalization preserves equidimensionality over the smooth base. Finally
resolve above this model, also dominating the resolution on which the metric is bounded. The smooth
space S need not be equidimensional over Y; equidimensionality is used only on S_0, where it
forces every vertical prime to dominate a prime of Y.

[Excluding a new pencil]
In a maximal diagram (eq:diagram), let B be a line bundle on a birational model over S. If its
class is pseudoeffectively dominated by c,pi^*P+E' for some c>0 and a pi-exceptional divisor
E', then no pencil in |B| is nonconstant on the general fiber of f. The conclusion persists after
adding to B the pullback of any fixed divisor on Y.


Proof. 
Resolve the pencil, remove its fixed part, and combine its map to mathbb P^1bbP^1 with f. If the pencil
is nonconstant on the general fiber, the combined image has dimension >dim Y. A product polarization
gives a basepoint-free line bundle F on the resolved source, pseudoeffectively dominated by a multiple
of pi^*P plus an exceptional divisor, using (eq:domination).

Choose a general member F_0 of that system; it has no pi-exceptional component. Push the class
comparison to W using the numerical fact of Ssubsec:inputs; the exceptional term vanishes and
c'P-pi_*F_0 is pseudoeffective. Pulling back, c'pi^*P-pi^*pi_*F_0 is pseudoeffective. As
pi_*F_0 is effective QQ-Cartier, its pullback contains the strict transform F_0 plus an
effective exceptional remainder, so c'pi^*P-F_0 is pseudoeffective. This dominates a polarization of
the larger projective image; on a smooth model the pulled-back polarization is big, hence dominates a
small ample class, and after scaling and pulling back gives a diagram of the allowed form. That
contradicts maximality.

For the final assertion, choose b with bA minus the fixed base divisor ample; the extra pullback is
then dominated by bf^*A, hence by bd,pi^*P via (eq:domination). Increase c.


## Decomposition with a missing component

Pull the N_i to S_0 (effective QQ-Cartier). The next lemma produces the decomposition controlling
the base metric.


For some r>0 with rP Cartier there are a line bundle V on Y and an effective Cartier divisor H
on S with

r,pi^*P=f^*V+H,

and for each prime G of Y some component of f^*G dominating G has coefficient zero in H.


Proof. 
For m_i<m_j<m_k put
[
alpha=(m_k-m_j)/(m_k-m_i), beta=(m_j-m_i)/(m_k-m_i),
]
so alpha,beta>0, alpha+beta=1, and N_jsim_{QQ}alpha N_i+beta N_k. Clearing denominators,
the two effective divisors give sections of one line bundle; that bundle is pseudoeffectively dominated
by a multiple of P because -M is pseudoeffective in (eq:Ni). By Lemma~lem:pencil their
ratio is constant on the general fiber. The ratio lies in CC(Y) (relative algebraic closure), so its
principal divisor has no horizontal component on S_0.

Hence the horizontal parts of pi_0^*N_i depend affinely on m_i as actual divisors. Fixing the
first two indices writes them as A_0+m_iB_0 with Supp A_0,Supp B_0 in one finite union of primes.
Effectivity for unbounded m_i forces B_0ge0. So the difference of two members divided by the
difference of exponents is QQ-linearly equivalent to pi_0^*P with effective horizontal part.
Clearing denominators gives a rational section of a positive Cartier multiple of pi_0^*P with no
horizontal poles; write its divisor as T_0.

For each base prime G set
[
v_G=min_{Fmapsto G}(coeff_F(T_0))/(coeff_F(f_0^*G)),
]
a minimum over the finitely many primes F above G (denominators positive); only finitely many v_G
are nonzero. Every vertical prime of S_0 sits above a base prime by equidimensionality, so
[
T_0-f_0^*(sum_G v_G,G)ge0.
]
The difference is QQ-Cartier (T_0 is Cartier up to the chosen multiple, Y smooth). Scaling makes
the base divisor integral and the remainder Cartier, giving V and an effective Cartier remainder on
S_0; pull to S to get (eq:decomposition). The component attaining the minimum has remainder
coefficient zero, and its strict transform on S still dominates G with coefficient zero in H.


If Y is a point, (eq:decomposition) immediately gives an effective divisor linearly equivalent to
rpi^*P, hence a section downstairs. Henceforth dim Y>0. We build a semipositive metric on V from
fiber integrals, and use the missing components to bound it. The integrals require adjoint line bundles
whose fiberwise section spaces have dimension one; discrepancy rounding supplies these, and maximality
supplies the rank-one property.

## Rounding and rank one for every twist

With compatible canonical divisors put

R=K_S-pi^*(K_W+D), E=lceil Rrceil, Xi=lceil Rrceil-R.

Choose the resolution so Supp R is simple normal crossings. As the pair is klt, every coefficient of
R exceeds -1. Along a nonexceptional prime the coefficient of R is minus its coefficient in D,
which lies in (-1,0], so its ceiling is zero; thus E is integral, effective and pi-exceptional.
The divisor Xi is effective with simple normal crossings support and coefficients in [0,1). The key
identity is

K_S+pi^*P+Xi=E.



For every integer age0, rank f_*OO_S(E+arpi^*P)=1.

Proof. 
Over the generic fiber the section of E+aH (from (eq:decomposition)) is nonzero, including
a=0. Were the generic-fiber section space at least two-dimensional, twisting by a sufficiently ample
pullback from Y would extend two independent generic sections to global sections whose ratio is
nonconstant on the general fiber. Their line bundle is dominated by a multiple of pi^*P plus the
exceptional E, together with the base twist as in Lemma~lem:pencil, contradicting that lemma.


For age0 define the integral line bundle

B_a=(ar+1)pi^*P+Xi=E+arpi^*P-K_S.

The second form proves integrality although each summand of the first can be rational. Equip B_a
initially with weights (ar+1)varphi_P+varphi_Xi, where varphi_P are the bounded weights on
pi^*P and varphi_Xi is the divisorial weight. Its curvature is semipositive and its multiplier
ideal is trivial, because the weights of P are bounded and the coefficients of the snc divisor Xi
are below one. The same holds on general fibers after shrinking to where the boundary is relatively
normal crossings.

## A bounded metric from normalized fiber integrals


V carries a singular Hermitian metric whose local weights are psh and locally bounded on Y.


Proof. 
Fix a dense Zariski open Y^circ over which f is smooth with connected fibers, Xi is relatively
snc, and no fiber lies in Ecup H. In a coordinate neighborhood in Y^circ choose frames v of V
and eta of -K_Y. The section s=f^*v,s_H of rpi^*P has divisor H there, and s_E s^a f^*eta
belongs to K_{S/Y}+B_a. By Lemma~lem:rankone it generates the direct image at a general point;
on its base-change open this generator identifies the adjoint direct image with aV-K_Y. Since the
direct image has rank one, its semipositive L^2 metric has psh weight equal to minus the logarithm of
the squared L^2 norm of this generator. Dividing by a gives

u_a(y)=-frac1alogint_{S_y}t^a,dmu_y, t=|s|^2_{h_P^r}, age1.

The measure mu_y is the squared adjoint norm density of s_Ef^*eta for the metric on pi^*P+Xi;
locally it is a bounded positive factor (a nonzero holomorphic section squared) times prod_j|z_j|^{-2c_j}
with c_j<1. It is finite on a general compact fiber and has the same null sets as smooth volume; zeros
of s_E and divisorial poles are measure zero. Only boundedness of varphi_P is used.

Upper bound on Y^circ. For y_0in Y^circ pick a point of S_{y_0} off
Ecup HcupSuppXi. On a small product neighborhood for the smooth map the holomorphic factors are
bounded away from zero and the metric weights are bounded, giving a neighborhood of y_0, constants
c,b>0, and open pieces in its fibers with tge c and mu_y-mass ge b. Thus
int_{S_y}t^a,dmu_yge b c^a and u_a(y)le-log c-(log b)/(a). These estimates continue the
u_a across each exponent-dependent base-change exceptional set and bound them locally uniformly above.

The limit. Let Y^* be the countable intersection of the full-measure sets where the integral
formula agrees with the psh representatives for every a, a common full-measure subset of Y^circ
(not a common Zariski open). For yin Y^*, the elementary L^a-norm limit on (S_y,mu_y) gives

lim_{ato∞}u_a(y)=-logesssup_{S_y}|s|^2_{h_P^r}=:u(y).

Indeed (int t^a)^{1/a}le M_y,mu_y(S_y)^{1/a} with M_y=esssup t, and for any c<M_y the set
{t>c} has positive measure, giving liminf(int t^a)^{1/a}ge c; let c↑ M_y.

A psh representative. The u_a are locally uniformly bounded above on Y^circ (properness,
holomorphicity of s, bounded varphi_P). They are locally bounded below: mu_y(S_y) is bounded
above, since snc charts give density le Cprod_j|z_j|^{-2c_j} with c_j<1 and properness gives a
finite cover above a compact base neighborhood. By local compactness of psh functions a subsequence
converges in L^1_{loc}, and (eq:esssup) identifies every subsequential limit a.e., so
u has a psh representative. Under v'=gv, t scales by |g|^2 and u by -log|g|^2; changing
eta contributes a term divided by a, vanishing in the limit. Thus u defines a metric on V over
Y^circ.

Lower bound at the boundary. Positivity is not enough; we need local boundedness on all of Y.
On the inverse image of a relatively compact base neighborhood, properness and bounded h_P bound
|s|^2 above, so (eq:esssup) bounds u below even near a missing base fiber. For an omitted prime
Gsubset Y, choose the component F above G on which H has coefficient zero. At a general point
of F, sne0, Fto G is submersive, and transverse coordinates read
z_1circ f=w_1^e,a(w) with a(w)ne0. Together with coordinates along G, nearby general fibers meet
a neighborhood of that point in a nontrivial open set. On a smaller neighborhood |s|^2_{h_P^r}ge c>0
(the section is nonzero, h_P bounded), and those openings have positive mu_y-measure (same null
sets as volume). A uniform lower bound on the measures is not needed for an essential supremum. Thus
the component may lie in E or Xi without harm, because on nearby general fibers those supports are
measure zero; hence esssupge c and ule-log c near a dense open subset of G.

Extension. The psh removable-singularity theorem extends u across these codimension-one sets
using the above upper bound; the remainder is an analytic set of codimension at least two in the smooth
base, and psh Hartogs extends u there too. The lower bound survives by upper semicontinuity and the
extension has a local upper bound. The local extensions are unique and respect frame changes, giving a
bounded metric on V over all of Y. This is local boundedness; no continuity of the original metric
or of its essential supremum is asserted.


## Mixing in small strict positivity

The bounded metric on V lets us write every nonnegative integral twist arpi^*P=af^*V+aH with a
semipositive metric whose only unbounded weights come from the integral divisor aH, giving exactly the
ideal OO_S(-aH) that cancels aH. Vanishing still needs strict positivity in base directions, which

# Stable torus quotients and invariant sections

Let Z be smooth projective, let L=-K_Z carry a smooth semipositive Hermitian metric, and let the
algebraic torus T act on Z. We build a projective quotient with klt stabilizer boundary and a
bounded semipositive anti-log-canonical metric. Rational connectedness is used only at the very end,
where it gives Euler characteristic one on a resolution of the quotient.

Replace T by its image in Aut(Z): the kernel acts trivially on T_Z and hence on the natural
linearization of L, so invariants are unchanged. For the quotient construction assume t=dim T>0;
the trivial action is handled at the end. Average the metric over the compact form Ksimeq(S^1)^t of
T to obtain a smooth K-invariant semipositive metric h on the same naturally linearized bundle.

## Weights and an ample perturbation

For a T-linearized line bundle C, a point xin Z, and an integral one-parameter subgroup
lambda:CC^*to T, set

w_C(x,lambda)=the weight on C_{x_0}, x_0=lim_{tauto0}lambda(tau)x,

with the limit existing by projectivity and the weight convention that a fiber vector of weight w
transforms by tau^w. For very ample C this is the maximum over eigensections nonvanishing at x of
the pairing with lambda. Sign check: if s is an eigensection of weight alpha and l a
nonzero lift at x, the scalar of s relative to lambda(tau)l is tau^{-alpha} times its scalar
at x; the smallest order over a generating set is -maxalpha, and a basepoint-free set has minimal
order zero, so the transformed lift has order maxalpha, matching (eq:weight). In this
convention semistability is w_C(x,lambda)ge0 for all nonzero one-parameter subgroups, and stability
is strict positivity; see [cite] and cite[S1.1.5]{DolgachevHu1998}.

Torus linearization [cite], in the forms cite[Proposition~2.4 and Corollary~2.6]{KKLV1989},
gives an equivariantly very ample J; pass to a high power so J+L is also very ample with the natural
linearization on L. For each bundle the nonvanishing eigensections at a point form a subset of a fixed
finite list, so the weight functions, and the difference
[
w_L=w_{J+L}-w_J,
]
take only finitely many patterns; each is homogeneous and piecewise linear on a finite rational
polyhedral fan in Lie(T)_{RR} (the real cocharacter space).


On a dense Zariski open subset of Z, the generic weight pattern of L is strictly positive on every
nonzero real cocharacter.

Proof. 
Fix a nonzero integral cocharacter lambda. The Bial{}ynicki-Birula decomposition [cite] has an
open attracting stratum (smooth attracting pieces with the tangent-weight description follow from
cite[Theorem~1.5 and Corollary~7.3]{JS2018}). At the limiting fixed component all tangent weights are
ge0, and some are positive: otherwise the attracting stratum has no positive-dimensional transverse
directions and openness would make lambda act trivially, contradicting effectiveness. The weight of
L=det T_Z is the sum of those tangent weights, hence strictly positive on this open stratum. The
generic patterns of J and J+L hold on one dense open where every chosen eigensection is nonvanishing;
it meets the attracting stratum for each lambda, so its weight function is positive for every nonzero
integral cocharacter. Since it is a finite union of rational linear pieces, positivity on every nonzero
integral cocharacter upgrades to every nonzero real cocharacter (a nonpositive locus on a rational cone
would contain a nonzero rational point), and on the unit sphere this continuous homogeneous function has
a positive minimum.



There are a positive rational delta and a rational character shift of arbitrarily small size for the
ample rational linearized bundle L+delta J such that its stable locus U is nonempty and equals its
semistable locus; moreover

w_L(x,lambda)ge0quadfor all integral one-parameter subgroups lambda, all xin U.

The shift only defines U; the linearization on L remains natural.

Proof. 
L is nef, so L+delta J is ample for every delta>0. Subdivide the finitely many weight fans into one
finite pointed rational fan on which every pattern of J and L is linear. For each pattern violating
(eq:weakweights), choose an integral ray of negative weight; there are finitely many. For small
delta>0 and a small rational character shift all these chosen weights stay negative, so such a
pattern cannot occur at a semistable point of the perturbed linearization. Meanwhile Lemma~lem:genericweight
and its uniform positive lower bound on the unit sphere keep the generic pattern strictly positive, so
the stable locus is nonempty.

Having fixed such a rational delta, choose the character off the finitely many hyperplanes where some
pattern vanishes on a ray of the common fan. Then every semistable point has strictly positive weights on
those rays, and linearity on pointed cones makes the weights strictly positive on every nonzero
cocharacter, so the point is stable. Clearing denominators gives an ordinary ample linearized bundle with
the same loci.


By projective GIT the quotient

q:U→ W=U/T

is a geometric quotient, W is projective, and the action has finite stabilizers on U. Stable equals
semistable, so this is the full projective GIT quotient, not an open piece of one.

## Stabilizer boundary and the anticanonical identity


W is normal and QQ-factorial, and there is an effective rational divisor
[
D=sum_B(1-frac1{e_B})B
]
on W, with e_B the generic ramification index of a finite stabilizer slice over the prime B, such
that (W,D) is klt. With P=-(K_W+D),

L|_U=q^*P

as equivariant QQ-line bundles, using the natural linearization on L and the trivial action on the
base.

Proof. 
Use Luna's slice theorem [cite] in an invariant affine chart from the ample GIT linearization.
Near an orbit with finite stabilizer G there is a smooth slice S_{sl} and, after an 'etale
base localization, a local model Ttimes^G S_{sl}to S_{sl}/G. The slice is smooth as U
is smooth with constant orbit dimension. Normality follows from taking invariants of a normal ring.
Locally QQ-factoriality: a Weil divisor pulls back to a Cartier divisor on the smooth finite cover,
and the product of its translates gives a local equation for a positive multiple downstairs; this
descends through the 'etale localizations. Hence W is normal and QQ-factorial.

Discarding an ineffective kernel, at a general point over a prime B the finite map has ramification
order e_B and ramification divisor coefficient e_B-1, so in codimension one
K_{S_{sl}}=p^*(K_W+D). The e_B are intrinsic (multiplicities of the quotient pullback of B),
so the charts glue to one global effective rational divisor D, with only finitely many ramified primes.

Klt: extend any divisorial valuation over W to the function field of the slice. If it has ramification
index e, its log discrepancy upstairs (for a smooth variety with zero boundary) is e times its log
discrepancy for (W,D) downstairs; the upstairs discrepancies are positive, so the downstairs ones are
too. This is the finite-morphism criterion cite[Proposition~5.20]{KM1998}; the finite-group computation
with its ramification boundary is in cite[Proposition~3.5 and equation~(3.4)]{BGLM2024}.

Linearization in (eq:canonicalquotient): on U the infinitesimal action is an injective subbundle
Lie(T) ⊗ OO_Uto T_U of rank dim T (finite stabilizers). Over a dense smooth W^circsubset W
with U^circ=q^{-1}(W^circ) where q is a submersion, the tangent sequence
[
0toLie(T) ⊗ OO_{U^circ}to T_{U^circ}to q^*T_{W^circ}to0
]
together with the triviality of the adjoint action (a torus) makes a nonzero element of detLie(T) an
equivariant trivialization of the orbit determinant; taking determinants identifies L|_{U^circ} with
q^*(-K_{W^circ}) equivariantly. The orders of this rational identification are exactly the
ramification orders above, introducing D and no character twist. A prime in U cannot map into
codimension ge2 in W (dimension count), so these slice computations account for every prime upstairs.
Finitely many slice charts have stabilizer groups of bounded exponent; a common multiple clears denominators
and Cartier indices, giving an actual line-bundle isomorphism with trivial fiberwise action. This is an
equivariant rational-line-bundle identity, not just a numerical one.


## A uniform bound on translated lifts

[Orbit bound]
Let x_0in U and l(x) a nonvanishing algebraic local frame of L near x_0. Then
sup_{tin T}|tcdot l(x)|_h is bounded above on a neighborhood of x_0. The same holds for holomorphic
frames and positive tensor powers of L.


Proof. 
Step 1 (no poles along arcs). Let x(s)to x_0in U, let l(s) be a nonzero holomorphic lift with
nonzero value at 0, and let t(s)in T be meromorphic at 0. Writing t(s)=lambda(s)u(s) with an
integral cocharacter lambda and a holomorphic u invertible at 0, replace (x,l) by
(u x,u l); the new initial point u(0)x_0 still lies in U, so (eq:weakweights) remains available,
and it suffices to treat t(s)=lambda(s).

For rational rin[0,1] let b(r) be the order of lambda(s^r)l(s) against a nonvanishing local frame
at the limiting base point (positive means a zero, negative a pole), defined via a common ramified
parameter and divided by its degree. By projectivity the limiting base point exists and algebraicity makes
the order finite and rational; smoothness and positivity of the metric give

b(r)=lim_{s↓0}(-log|lambda(s^r)l(s)|_h^2)/(-log|s|^2).

For fixed real s>0 the numerator is convex in r: the pullback weight along the holomorphic orbit map
CC^*to Z is psh and, being invariant under the compact circle, convex in the real log coordinate;
composing with rmapsto rlog s and dividing by the positive denominator preserves convexity. Passing to
limits makes b convex on rational points, with b(0)=0.

Step 2 (initial slope). We claim b(r)=r,w_L(x_0,lambda) for small positive rational r. Use the
very ample J and J+L of Ssubsec:weights: pick a nonvanishing lift to J along the arc and tensor
with l(s) to get a lift to J+L. For a very ample C with eigensection weights alpha_j and orders
nu_jge0 of the C-lift (nu_j=∞ if a section vanishes identically), the transformed lift has
orders nu_j-ralpha_j and, since the sections generate C, order -min_j(nu_j-ralpha_j). For small
enough r>0 every minimizer has nu_j=0, so the order is rmax_{nu_j=0}alpha_j=r,w_C(x_0,lambda).
Subtracting the J-formula from the J+L-formula gives the claim.

Step 3 (no pole). Convexity with b(0)=0 gives b(r)le r,b(1), hence
b(1)ge b(r)/r=w_L(x_0,lambda)ge0 by (eq:weakweights). So a transformed lift has no pole along any
such arc.

Step 4 (uniformity). Suppose sup_{tin T}|tcdot l(x)|_h were unbounded as xto x_0. Compactify
T projectively and compactify the total space of L as mathbb P(bbP(OO_Z ⊕  L) with its infinity
divisor. Take the closure of the graph of (t,x)mapsto tcdot l(x), keeping x a parameter, in the
resulting proper family over the parameter neighborhood. Since Z is projective and h smooth, an
unbounded sequence of norms gives a point at fiber infinity above x_0; algebraic curve selection through
it, with generic point in the graph, yields after normalization an arc x(s)to x_0 and meromorphic
t(s) with t(s)l(x(s)) having a pole, contradicting Step 3. Hence the bound is locally uniform.

A holomorphic frame differs from an algebraic one by a nowhere-vanishing holomorphic function, bounded
above and below on a smaller neighborhood, and positive powers preserve the estimate.


## The orbit-supremum metric

Fix a divisible positive integer N for which (eq:canonicalquotient) is an isomorphism of line
bundles. For yin W and vin(NP)_y, pull v back to the orbit and set

|v|_{h_{NP}}=sup_{xin q^{-1}(y)}|q^*v(x)|_{h^N}.

The definition is independent of the orbit representative (the isomorphism is equivariant) and scales by
|c| in v; the choice of N kills finite-stabilizer characters.


(eq:supnorm) defines a metric whose Nth root on P has locally bounded psh weights on W, and
whose pullback to every smooth resolution also has locally bounded psh weights. In particular P is nef.

Proof. 
Boundedness. In a finite slice chart, nearby quotient points have representatives on the slice near a
fixed one; Lemma~lem:orbitbound bounds the pulled-back frame of NP above, and the value at the
representative itself is bounded below in a small chart (smooth metric, nonvanishing frame). So the
supremum is finite and bounded above and below in frames, and its negative logarithm is locally bounded.

Plurisubharmonicity. Over a small smooth slice ball B and the universal cover CC^tto T, let e
be the pulled-back frame of NP and
[
Phi(z,zeta)=-log|exp(zeta)cdot e(z)|_{h^N}^2, zin B, zetainCC^t.
]
The action map is holomorphic and the translated frame is holomorphic and nonvanishing, so Phi is
jointly psh. It is independent of Imzeta by compact-form invariance. The domain
BtimesCC^t is pseudoconvex with connected tube fibers, so Kiselman's minimum principle
[cite], in the form cite[Theorem~1.1]{DWZZ2018}, makes inf_{zeta}Phi(z,zeta) psh or
identically -∞; the bounds exclude the latter. This equals the weight of (eq:supnorm).
Kiselman gives positivity after boundedness; it does not supply the bound.

The slice weights are invariant under the finite slice group with the right pullback transformation, so
they descend to locally bounded upper-semicontinuous functions. For positivity, take a holomorphic disc in
the quotient, normalize a component of its finite pullback dominating the disc, lift it after a finite
ramified disc cover, and use that the pulled-back weight is subharmonic there and hence subharmonic
downstairs off the branch points; boundedness extends it across them. The same argument after composing
with a disc in a smooth resolution gives subharmonic restrictions, so the pulled-back weights on the
resolution are psh (equivalently extend from the regular locus with local upper bounds,
cite[Chapter~I, Theorem~5.24]{Demailly2012}, on the resolution). Taking the Nth root gives a metric on P.

Nefness. Let C be an irreducible curve on a resolution. A locally bounded psh weight restricts to a
psh function on the normalization of C (not identically -∞), and the resulting positive curvature
measure has total mass equal to deg(P|_C)ge0. So P has nonnegative intersection with every curve on
the resolution, and the projection formula gives nefness on W.


## The Euler premise and extension

Proof. [Proof of Theorem~thm:equivariant]
Assume Z is rationally connected. If the image of T is trivial, every section is naturally invariant and
Theorem~thm:klt applied to (Z,0) gives a nonzero one, since L is nef with a smooth semipositive
metric and chi(Z,OO_Z)=1.

Otherwise take the quotient (W,D) of Proposition~prop:boundary, QQ-factorial and klt with
effective rational boundary; Proposition~prop:quotientmetric gives nefness and the bounded metric
for P=-(K_W+D).

Let widetilde Wto W be a smooth projective resolution. The rational map Zdashrightarrowwidetilde W
is dominant; resolve its indeterminacy to a morphism widetilde Ztowidetilde W. Rational connectedness
is preserved by smooth projective birational modification and by dominant rational maps to smooth
projective targets [cite]; equivalently, pullback of forms under the dominant map is injective
with H^0(widetilde Z,Ω^p)=0 for p>0, so H^0(widetilde W,Ω^p)=0 for p>0 and Hodge
symmetry yields chi(widetilde W,OO_{widetilde W})=1 (if W is a point, directly).

Theorem~thm:klt gives a nonzero section sigma of a positive Cartier multiple mP; take a power so
m is also divisible by the integer of (eq:canonicalquotient). Its pullback widetildesigma is a
nonzero invariant section of mL on U. The bounded quotient metric makes |sigma| bounded on the
compact W; (eq:supnorm) then gives

|widetildesigma(x)|_{h^m}le |sigma(q(x))|_{h_{mP}}le Cqquad(xin U).


For xin Z∖ U, pick a holomorphic nonvanishing frame of mL on a small coordinate neighborhood;
smoothness of h bounds the frame's norm above and below, so the coefficient of widetildesigma is a
bounded holomorphic function off the algebraic set Z∖ U. The removable-singularity theorem
extends it across Z∖ U cite[Chapter~I, Corollary~5.25]{Demailly2012}, in particular at the
generic point of every omitted divisor; normality handles the codimension-two remainder. Unique local
extensions glue to a global section of mL.

For tin T, the translated global section agrees with the original on the dense open U, hence
everywhere, and it is nonzero because the original is nonzero on U. This is the invariant nonvanishing.



Natural linearization cannot be dropped even on mathbb P^1.bbP^1. For tcdot[z_0:z_1]=[tz_0:z_1] the weights
on H^0(mathbb P^1,-mK_bbP^1,-mK_{mathbb P^1}bbP^1) run from -m to m under the natural linearization. Tensoring the
linearization on -K_{mathbb P^1}bbP^1 by the character tmapsto t^2 shifts the weights in the mth power to
run from m to 3m, so every positive power has zero invariant subspace. This is why the auxiliary
character used to choose the stable open is not transferred to L.


# The smooth projective application

A section on a compact factor of the universal cover descends only if it respects the deck action. The
invariant statement of Theorem~thm:equivariant supplies exactly that compatibility with the residual
torus monodromy, giving another proof of the smooth nonvanishing result of the companion article
cite[Theorem ``Anticanonical nonvanishing'']{SmoothCompanion}, whose finite-volume proof is replaced here by
the bounded-metric criterion and stable quotient. From the companion we take only the finite-cover structure
and the finite-'etale norm.


Let X be a smooth connected projective complex variety with smoothly semipositive anticanonical bundle.
Then H^0(X,-mK_X)neq 0 for some integer m>0.


Proof. 
Use the companion's cite[Theorem ``Finite cover with compact torus monodromy'']{SmoothCompanion} in its form
retaining the deck action: there is a finite 'etale cover X'to X whose universal cover is
[
widetilde X=CC^qtimes Btimes Z,
]
with compact projective factors, K_B trivial, and Z rationally connected with smoothly semipositive
anticanonical bundle (either factor may be a point). The deck group acts by translations on CC^q,
trivially on B, and on Z through a compact torus inside an algebraic torus T. The Euclidean and B
factors carry invariant nowhere-zero canonical frames.

By Theorem~thm:equivariant choose a nonzero T-invariant section s_Z of -mK_Z (a constant section
if Z is a point). Tensor with the mth powers of the dual canonical frames on the other two factors to get
a nonzero section of -mK_{widetilde X} invariant under the deck group of widetilde Xto X'; the natural
linearization is essential, since the action on the anticanonical product is the differential one used in
Theorem~thm:equivariant. The section descends to X' and is algebraic by projectivity. If d is the
degree of X'to X, the companion's finite 'etale norm cite[Lemma ``Finite 'etale norm'']{SmoothCompanion}
gives a nonzero section of -mdK_X.


The geometric input rests on Yau's prescribed-Ricci theorem [cite] and the structure results of
Demailly--Peternell--Schneider [cite] and Campana--Demailly--Peternell cite[Theorem~1.4]{CDP2015}. A
finite cover need not be a product: the residual torus monodromy is why an invariant section on Z, not an
arbitrary one, is needed.

The positive multiple in Corollary~cor:smooth cannot generally be reduced to the first power. For an
Enriques surface S, the bundle K_S is nontrivial of order two and H^0(S,-K_S)=0 [cite]. A
flat metric on -K_S and the Fubini--Study metric on OO_{mathbb P^1}bbP^1(2) make the anticanonical bundle of
Stimesmathbb P^1bbP^1 smoothly semipositive; the product formula for sections shows it has no first-power
section while its second power does. Its restriction to a mathbb P^1bbP^1 fiber has degree two, so this example
is not explained by torsion of the anticanonical bundle itself.
