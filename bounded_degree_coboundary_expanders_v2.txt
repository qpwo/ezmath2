For every integer d ge 3 there is a constant D < ∞ and a constant
ε > 0 with the following property. For every integer m ge 2
there is a finite, connected, pure d-dimensional simplicial complex X_m
with |X_m(0)| to ∞ such that each vertex lies in at most D faces of
dimension d and such that, for every integer i with 0 le i < d and
every cochain f in C^i(X_m; 𝔽_2),
|delta_i f|_{X_m,,i+1} ge ε,dist_{X_m,,i}(f, B^i(X_m)).
Neither D nor ε depends on m, i, or f.

An expander graph is a graph for which the edge boundary of any set of
vertices of size at most half the total is a fixed fraction of that set;
equivalently, the second eigenvalue of the graph Laplacian is bounded below
by a constant independent of the number of vertices. Coboundary expansion
lifts the condition to higher-dimensional complexes. Let Y be a finite
pure s-dimensional simplicial complex, let Y(i) denote its set of
i-faces, write C^i(Y; 𝔽_2) for the set of functions
Y(i) to 𝔽_2, and put
w_i^Y(sigma) = frac{|{tau in Y(s) : sigma ⊆ tau}|}{binom{s+1}{i+1},|Y(s)|}, 
|f|_{Y,i} = sum_{sigma : f(sigma) ne 0} w_i^Y(sigma).
The interpretation is probabilistic: a random s-face is sampled uniformly
and then one of its (i+1)-vertex subsets is sampled uniformly, and
|f|_{Y,i} is the probability that f is nonzero on the sampled
i-face.

./pdf/lubotzky-1994-discrete-groups-expanding-graphs.pdf

For f in C^i(Y; 𝔽_2) the coboundary is
(delta_i f)(tau) = sum_{sigma subset tau,, |sigma| = i+1} f(sigma),
summed in 𝔽_2. The cocycles are Z^i(Y) = ker delta_i; the
coboundaries are B^i(Y) = imdelta_{i-1}, with B^0(Y)
taken to consist of the constant functions; the cohomology is
H^i(Y; 𝔽_2) = Z^i(Y)/B^i(Y). For a subspace
A ⊆ C^i(Y; 𝔽_2) define
dist_{Y,i}(f,A) = min_{a in A} |f - a|_{Y,i}.

The distinction between cocycles and coboundaries drives the whole
argument. A cocycle f in Z^i(Y) has |delta_i f| = 0, so any
inequality of the form |delta_i f| ge ε dist_{Y,i}(f, Z^i(Y))
is satisfied trivially by f; the inequality against B^i(Y) fails
for f precisely because f is not a coboundary. An inequality with
Z^i(Y) on the right is called a cosystolic inequality, and with
B^i(Y) on the right a coboundary expansion inequality. Passing from
one to the other requires the vanishing of H^i(Y; 𝔽_2) in
every degree in which expansion is required.

Prior work splits into two lines, one random-combinatorial and one
algebraic. Linial and Meshulam in 2006, and Meshulam and Wallach in
2009, studied the random k-dimensional complex in which a complete
(k-1)-skeleton is fixed and k-faces are inserted independently at
a given rate, and located the threshold rate at which cohomology
vanishes. Those models possess complete lower skeleta and therefore
have unbounded vertex degree.

The second is coboundary expansion, which is stronger than cosystolic
expansion. Lubotzky and Meshulam in 2015 constructed random Latin-square
coboundary expanders in dimension two, and Lubotzky, Luria, and
Rosenthal in 2019 constructed Steiner-system coboundary expanders in
every dimension. Both families bound the number of top-dimensional
faces through a codimension-one face only. Latin-square complexes have
a complete tripartite 1-skeleton, and Steiner-system complexes have a
complete (d-1)-skeleton, so the number of top-dimensional faces
through a single vertex grows with the size. Those are not bounded
degree conditions at the vertex level.

non-Abelian coefficients. Neither family gives the vanishing in every

The construction belongs to the Kac-Moody-Steinberg
cosystolic programme of Oppenheim and Valentiner-Branth, whose cosystolic
expansion theorem supplies the quantitative input. The specific local
groups used in the construction already appear in the affine-type-A polynomial-matrix
example of that paper, where finite quotients by irreducible polynomials
are considered. The new ingredient is the passage to quotients
modulo powers t^m of t and the algebraic verification that the
resulting finite complexes have vanishing cohomology in every degree up
to d - 1, so that the cosystolic expansion inequality becomes ordinary

Fix an integer d ge 3 and put n = 2d and N = n + 1 = 2d + 1.
Write S = {0, 1, ..., N - 1}. Choose once and for all a prime q
satisfying the requirements collected later; each is a finite lower
bound depending only on n. Set k = 𝔽_q and R = k[t].
For a ge 1 put
Gamma_a(m) = ker(SL_a(R) to SL_a(R/(t^m))).
Let U le SL_N(R) be the inverse image, under evaluation
at t = 0, of the upper unitriangular subgroup of SL_N(k):
the set of matrices in SL_N(k[t]) whose reduction at
t = 0 is upper triangular with diagonal entries 1.

For 0 le i < N define the simple generator
x_i = e_{i,i+1}(1) when 1 le i < N, and x_0 = e_{N,1}(t),
where e_{a,b}(f) = I + f E_{a,b} and rows and columns are indexed
1, ..., N. The generator x_0 carries a factor of t because its
associated edge is the wrap-around edge of the cycle
1 to 2 to ... to N to 1, and only with that factor does the
matrix belong to SL_N(k[t]) while reducing to a
unitriangular matrix at t = 0. The generators x_i satisfy the
relations of an affine root system of type widetilde A_n with node
set S; the corresponding Coxeter graph is the cycle with edges
i - (i+1) bmod N.

For J ⊆ S put U_J = < x_j : j in J > and
U_∅ = {1}, and put
H_i = U_{S ∖ {i}} = < x_j : j in S, j ne i >.

For a group G and subgroups (K_i)_{i in S} of G, the coset
complex CC(G; (K_i)_{i in S}) has as vertices the
tagged left cosets g K_i with i in S; a finite set of such
vertices spans a face if the corresponding cosets have nonempty common
intersection. The chambers, that is the maximal faces, are the sets
{g K_i : i in S} with g in G, so the complex is pure of
dimension N - 1 and its chambers are indexed by G / ∩_i K_i.

For m ge 2 put
G_m = U / Gamma_N(m),  T_m = CC(G_m; (overlineoverline H_i)_H_i)_{i in S}), 
X_m = skel_d T_m,
where the bar denotes reduction modulo t^m. The group G_m is the
finite image of U in SL_N(R/(t^m)), and T_m is the
finite coset complex that will be analyzed.

The local structure of U and its finite quotients is described by
the next lemma, which is the only place where the specific cycle
arrangement of the x_j enters.

Lemma. For every proper J ⊊ S, the group U_J is the set of
matrices g in SL_N(R) with g_{aa} = 1 for every
a in S, and with
g_{ab} = 0  unless  g_{ab} in V_{ab}(J),
where V_{ab}(J) = k when the unique simple directed path in the cycle
1 to 2 to ... to N to 1 from a to b lies in J and avoids
edge 0, V_{ab}(J) = kt when that path lies in J and uses edge 0,
and V_{ab}(J) = 0 otherwise. Moreover, the U_J are finite, and
U_J cap U_K = U_{J cap K},  ∩_{i in A} H_i = U_{S ∖ A}  (∅ ne A ⊆ S),
and for every m ge 2 the reduction R to R/(t^m) is injective on
each U_J and preserves all these intersections. Finally
|H_i| = M := q^{binom{N}{2}} for every i in S, and |U_{{i}}| = q.

Proof. Choose an edge j_0 notin J and delete it, turning the cycle
into a linear order (j_0 + 1, j_0 + 2, ..., j_0 + N - 1) on S.
In this linear order, the set of matrices g with g_{aa} = 1 and
g_{ab} in V_{ab}(J) for a < b is a product of unitriangular groups,
one for each path component of J; unitriangular groups over a
commutative ring are groups, so this set is a subgroup of
SL_N(R). Every entry has degree at most one, so no
power t^2 or higher appears; the subgroup is therefore determined by
its image in SL_N(k  ⊕  kt) = SL_N(k[t]/(t^2)),
which is finite. This gives the inclusion
{g : g_{aa} = 1, g_{ab} in V_{ab}(J)} ⊆ SL_N(R)
as a finite subgroup.

Because q is prime, raising x_j to powers e_{j, j+1}(a) with
a ranging over k produces every coefficient in the simple root
subgroup of x_j. The commutator identity
[e_{a,b}(f), e_{b,c}(g)] = e_{a,c}(fg) for distinct a,b,c
composes path entries: at each step an additional edge of the cycle
is appended. Unitriangular elimination in the linear order that was
obtained by deleting j_0 then produces every matrix in the displayed
set, proving U_J = {g : g_{aa} = 1, g_{ab} in V_{ab}(J)}.

For the intersection identities, note that the entry pattern of U_J
is described entirely by J: a matrix lies in U_J cap U_K exactly
when each g_{ab} lies in V_{ab}(J) cap V_{ab}(K) = V_{ab}(J cap K)
together with g_{aa} = 1, which is the entry pattern defining
U_{J cap K}. For the second identity, H_i = U_{S ∖ {i}},
so ∩_{i in A} H_i = ∩_{i in A} U_{S ∖ {i}} = U_{S ∖ A}
by the first identity applied to the intersected subsets.

For the reduction step, every element of every U_J has entries in
k cup kt and therefore degree at most one. Because m ge 2, the
reduction R to R/(t^m) is injective on the degree-one piece, so it
is injective on each U_J and preserves the entry-pattern description.

For the order computation, fix i in S and take J = S ∖ {i},
that is H_i = U_J. Deleting edge i turns the cycle into a linear
order on the remaining N indices. In that order, the number of pairs
(a, b) with V_{ab}(J) ne 0 is exactly the number of pairs with a
strictly before b, which is binom{N}{2}. Each such pair contributes
an entry in k cup kt with q choices, giving |U_J| = q^{binom{N}{2}}.
The generator x_j has order q because q is prime and the entries
e_{j,j+1}(a) with a in k and e_{N,1}(bt) with b in k are
distinct as elements of U; in particular |U_{{i}}| = q.

The proof splits into two large pieces. The first is geometric: an
infinite complex L is identified with the subcomplex of an affine
twin building opposite a chamber, and shown to be acyclic in a range.
The second is homological and finite: the local-group computations
just made let the cohomology of the finite quotient complexes T_m
be related to the group homology of U and its congruence subgroups.

For the geometric piece, set G = SL_N(k[t, t^{-1}]),
with T the group of constant diagonal matrices of determinant one.
Define parabolic subgroups
B_+ = {g in G : g(t) in SL_N(k[t]), g(0)  upper triangular},
B_- = {g in G : g(t^{-1}) in SL_N(k[t^{-1}]), g(∞)  lower triangular}.
Both are Borel subgroups of G; B_+ = U rtimes T, and
B_+ cap B_- = T.

Let Delta_+ be the simplicial complex whose chambers are the left
B_+-cosets in G; the faces of a chamber are the parabolic cosets
it contains. Tits's theorem identifies Delta_+ as a building of
type widetilde A_n; similarly Delta_- is a building from the
B_--cosets. The pair (Delta_+, Delta_-) is an affine twin building
of type widetilde A_n, which comes with a codistance map
delta^* : { chambers of  Delta_- } times { chambers of  Delta_+ } to W cup {∞},
where W is the affine Coxeter group of type widetilde A_n. The
codistance is symmetric, satisfies delta^*(c, c_+) = 1 exactly when
c lies in B_+ c_- for fixed positive base chamber c_+ and negative
base chamber c_-, and satisfies a panel axiom: if c' is a chamber
adjacent to c along an s-panel, then delta^*(c', c_+) lies in
{ delta^*(c, c_+), delta^*(c, c_+) s }, and when the second option
ws has smaller Coxeter length than w = delta^*(c, c_+), every
chamber of that panel other than c has codistance ws.

Let L' be the union of all chambers of Delta_- of codistance 1
from c_+, together with all their faces. Then L' is a subcomplex
of Delta_-.

The next lemma says that U acts simply transitively on the chambers
of L', and that the type-i vertex stabilizer is exactly H_i, so
L' is naturally isomorphic to L = CC(U; (H_i)_{i in S}).

Lemma. The group U acts simply transitively on the chambers of L',
and the map u H_i |-> the type-i vertex of u c_- identifies
L with L'.

Proof. By the codistance axioms, the chambers of Delta_- at codistance
1 from c_+ form a single B_+-orbit B_+ c_-. Because B_+ = U T
and T stabilizes c_-, U is transitive on these chambers. If
u c_- = c_- with u in U, then u fixes the negative base chamber,
so u in B_+ cap B_-^{stab(c_+)} = T, and U cap T = {1}.
Hence U is simply transitive on the chambers of L'.

For the vertex stabilizers, in the lattice model of Delta_-, vertices
are homothety classes of k[[t^{-1}]]-lattices Λ ⊆ k[[t^{-1}]]^N
of rank N, and an element g in G preserves a vertex of exponent
vector b = (b_1, ..., b_N) precisely when deg(g_{ab}) le b_a - b_b
for every a, b. The base negative chamber has exponent vectors
(0, ..., 0) at type 0, and (0, ..., 0, 1, ..., 1) with
i zeros and N - i ones at type i.

At type 0, the bound gives deg(g_{ab}) le 0, so g_{ab} in k.
Combined with U being upper unitriangular at t = 0, the stabilizer
is the set of constant upper unitriangular matrices: this is H_0.

At type i ne 0, split rows and columns after position i. The bound
deg(g_{ab}) le b_a - b_b then gives deg(g_{ab}) le 0 in the two
diagonal blocks, deg(g_{ab}) le -1 in the upper-right block (so
g_{ab} = 0 there since g_{ab} in k[t]), and deg(g_{ab}) le 1
in the lower-left block. Combined with U, the stabilizer is a pair
of constant upper unitriangular diagonal blocks, a zero upper-right
block, and arbitrary entries in kt in the lower-left block. That is
exactly the entry pattern of H_i from the local-group lemma with
J = S ∖ {i}, so the stabilizer is H_i.

The map u H_i |-> the type-i vertex of u c_- is then well
defined by the second paragraph, is a bijection on vertices by the
first paragraph and the local-group computation, and preserves faces
because faces are intersections of vertex cosets. Consequently
L ~= L' as simplicial complexes.

The filtration of Delta_- by codistance feeds the homology of L.
For a fixed element w in W of positive Coxeter length, the codistance
w chambers are grouped into blocks by J-residues, where
J = S ∖ D with D = { s in S : ell(ws) < ell(w) }. The
descent set D is nonempty because w ne 1, so J is proper.
The parabolic W_J is finite spherical: deleting any node from the
affine cycle widetilde A_n leaves a finite path. Let w_J be the
longest element of W_J.

Within a block R of type J, write F for the common D-face
shared by all chambers of R, and write c_{max} for the unique
chamber of R at codistance w · w_J from c_+. Then the
codistance-w chambers of R are exactly the chambers of R opposite
c_{max}: such chambers form a spherical opposition complex O
inside R, and the entire block, together with its faces, is the
simplicial join F * O.

The reasoning is a walk along J-residues. Fix a codistance-w
chamber c in R. Every s in J is an ascent of w, meaning
ell(ws) > ell(w), and the panel axiom then forces c to be the
shortest chamber of its s-panel. Iterating along a reduced expression
of w_J reaches c_{max} at codistance w · w_J. Any minimal
gallery in R from c_{max} to a chamber d has type equal to a
reduced word for the Weyl distance v in W_J from c_{max} to d;
applying the descending panel axiom at each step gives delta^*(d, c_+) = w · w_J · v.
The equality delta^*(d, c_+) = w is equivalent to v = w_J because
w_J^2 = 1. So the codistance-w chambers of R are precisely the
chambers at Weyl distance w_J from c_{max}, which is the definition
of the spherical opposition complex of c_{max} in R.

The intersection of F * O with the previously added subcomplex is
exactly ∂ F * O. A face of F * O that
omits at least one vertex of F lies in a D-descent panel of R,
hence in a codistance-ws chamber for some s in D of strictly
smaller length, and therefore in a previously added block. A face
containing all of F cannot lie in a chamber of a different J-residue,
because its D-face F already determines the residue.

The relative chains of one block therefore collapse to the chains
of the opposition complex, shifted by |D|: a face of F * O that
survives in C_*(F * O, ∂ F * O) is a face F cup sigma
with F a complete face of F and sigma ⊆ O, and the
boundary map induced by deleting vertices of F is zero while the
boundary map induced by deleting vertices of sigma is the augmented
boundary of sigma inside O. This gives
H_k(F * O, ∂ F * O; 𝔽_2) ~= widetilde H_{k - |D|}(O; 𝔽_2).

The opposition complex O inside a spherical building of type J is
acyclic in a certain range, provided q is large enough. For a
spherical building of type A_r over 𝔽_q, and for a
chamber c of that building, let O be the complex of chambers
opposite c, together with all their faces. Then
widetilde H_j(O; 𝔽_2) = 0  for  0 le j < r - 1,
provided q ge 2^{r-1}. This is a consequence of the integral cone
function of Oppenheim and Valentiner-Branth (Corollary 6.10 of the KMS
cosystolic paper): the cone function gives a chain contraction over
ℤ that descends to any coefficients, and the constant
2^{r-1} accommodates the reduction.

For a product of spherical buildings of type A_{r_1} times ... times A_{r_k},
the opposition complex is the join O_1 * ... * O_k of the factor
opposition complexes, and the augmented chain formula for joins carries
the vanishing to degree less than (r_1 + ... + r_k) - 1.

Within the codistance filtration, each J-residue is a product of
spherical buildings of type A with total rank |J|, so its
opposition complex O has dimension |J| - 1 and satisfies the
vanishing above for q ge 2^{|J|-1}. Since |J| le |S| - 1 = n - 1
and |J| - 1 le n - 2, the requirement q ge 2^{n-1} covers every
proper residue.

Now pass to the pair (T_{<w}, T_{le w}) where T_{<w} is the union
of all blocks of codistance strictly less than w, and T_{le w} is
the union with the codistance-w blocks. The relative homology of the
pair is the direct sum of the block contributions, and by the block
homology computation each block contributes vanishing in degrees below
|D| + dim O = |D| + |J| - 1 = |S| - 1 = n, so
H_k(T_{le w}, T_{<w}; 𝔽_2) = 0  for  k < n.

The long exact sequence in homology of the pair (T_{le w}, T_{<w}) then
gives, for 0 le j le n - 2, that H_j(T_{<w}) to H_j(T_{le w})
is an isomorphism, because in that range both H_j and H_{j+1} of the
pair vanish.

The full negative building is the increasing union of the T_{le w}
over all codistances w, and reduced homology of a simplicial complex
over a field commutes with such increasing unions. The affine building
Delta_- is a Euclidean building, hence a CAT(0) space,
hence contractible.

A corollary of the connectivity of L is that the simple generators
x_i generate U. In a connected pure complex any two chambers can
be joined by a chain of chambers in which consecutive chambers share
at least one vertex. Label the chambers of L by elements of U via
the simply transitive action, and walk such a chain from the base
chamber to reach u. Consecutive chambers sharing a vertex of type i
have labels u, v in U with u H_i = v H_i, hence u^{-1} v in H_i.
So u in < H_i : i in S >, and each H_i is generated by
all x_j with j ne i. Hence U = < x_j : j in S >.

For a group K and an abelian group A with trivial K-action,
group homology H_j(K; A) is the j-th left derived functor of the
coinvariant functor M |-> M_K = M  ⊗ _{ℤ[K]} ℤ.
Concretely, choose a projective resolution P_* to ℤ of
the trivial module ℤ over the group ring ℤ[K],
and take the homology of P_*  ⊗ _{ℤ[K]} A. Equivalently,
take a contractible free K-complex and quotient.

The complex L is only (n - 2)-acyclic, not contractible, but the
odd-order stabilizers compensate: modulo bounded finite stabilizer
corrections, the simplicial chains of L behave like a partial
projective resolution of the trivial 𝔽_2[U]-module.

Lemma. Let K be a group acting on a nonempty simplicial complex Z
without inversions, and suppose every stabilizer of a nonempty face
of Z is a finite group of odd order. If widetilde H_j(Z; 𝔽_2) = 0
for 0 le j le h, then
H_j(K; 𝔽_2) ~= H_j(C_*(Z; 𝔽_2)_K)  (0 le j le h),
where C_*(Z; 𝔽_2)_K is the chain complex of K-orbits of
faces with the induced boundary.

Proof. For each r ge 0, the permutation module C_r(Z; 𝔽_2)
is a direct sum of transitive permutation modules 𝔽_2[K/H]
indexed by face stabilizers H. Because |H| is odd, averaging over
H splits the augmentation 𝔽_2[H] to 𝔽_2, so
𝔽_2 is a projective 𝔽_2[H]-module; by induction
the induced module 𝔽_2[K/H] = 𝔽_2  ⊗ _{𝔽_2[H]} 𝔽_2[K]
is a projective 𝔽_2[K]-module. Hence every chain module
C_r(Z; 𝔽_2) is projective.

The augmented chain complex C_*(Z; 𝔽_2) to 𝔽_2 is
exact through degree h by hypothesis. Retain C_0, ..., C_{h+1}
together with their differentials, and complete to a full projective
resolution of 𝔽_2 by choosing a projective module mapping
onto ker ∂_{h+1} and continuing upward. The resulting
P_* to 𝔽_2 agrees with C_*(Z; 𝔽_2) in degrees
0, ..., h+1 with identical differentials. Consequently
H_j(K; 𝔽_2) = H_j(P_*  ⊗ _K 𝔽_2) = H_j(C_*(Z; 𝔽_2)_K)
for 0 le j le h.

The two applications of this lemma are the main homology comparisons.
For the first, take K = U, Z = L, h = n - 2. Each nonempty face

H_j(U; 𝔽_2) ~= H_j(C_*(L; 𝔽_2)_U) for 0 le j le n - 2.

Because U acts transitively on faces of each fixed type A ⊆ S,
the orbit complex U backslash L has exactly one face of each type.
In particular U backslash L is a single n-simplex. The orbit
boundary is the ordinary boundary with coefficient one at every face,
so the orbit chain complex is acyclic in positive degrees. Combining
with the previous paragraph,
H_j(U; 𝔽_2) = 0  (1 le j le n - 2).

For the second homology comparison, relate L and T_m. Reduction

of face sets widetilde pi : L to T_m by
widetilde pi(u H_i) = pi(u) overlineoverline H_i.H_i. This map is well-defined

The subgroup Gamma_N(m) acts freely on L: an element of Gamma_N(m)

The following definitions make the statement precise. For a commutative
nonunital ring A, its integer unitization is ℤ ltimes A = ℤ  ⊕  A
with multiplication (u, a)(v, b) = (uv, ub + va + ab) and identity
(1, 0). The projection to ℤ has kernel A, and
GL_a(A) = ker (GL_a(ℤ ltimes A) to GL_a(ℤ)).
A row b in (ℤ ltimes A)^ell is A-unimodular if
b equiv e_1 pmod{A} and there exists c equiv e_1 pmod{A} with
b · c = 1. The stable range satisfies sr(A) le s
if every A-unimodular row b of length ell ge s + 1 can be
shortened: there exist v_1, ..., v_{ell-1} in A with
(b_1 + v_1 b_ell, ..., b_{ell-1} + v_{ell-1} b_ell)
also A-unimodular. These are the stable-range conventions of
Vaserstein (1969), Section 2, pages 384--385 of Matematicheskii
Sbornik 8.

An inverse system (A_m) of abelian groups is pro-zero if for every
index m there is an m' ge m whose transition map A_{m'} to A_m
is zero. A level map of inverse systems is a pro-isomorphism exactly
when its kernel and cokernel systems are pro-zero. The system (A_m)
of commutative nonunital rings is Tor-unital if for every i ge 1
the inverse system (Tor_i^{ℤ ltimes A_m}(ℤ, ℤ))_m
is pro-zero.

Morrow's theorem states that the powers of any ideal in a commutative
Noetherian ring form a Tor-unital inverse system. For the powers
A_m = t^m k[t] of the ideal (t) ⊆ k[t], this Tor-unitality
holds. Iwasa's theorem then states: if (A_m) is a Tor-unital inverse
system of commutative nonunital rings, and if
r = max{2, sup_m sr(A_m)} < ∞, then
(H_j(GL_a(A_m); ℤ))_m to (H_j(GL_{a+1}(A_m); ℤ))_m
is a pro-isomorphism for a ge 2j + r - 1.

The stable range is small for the rings A_m. For an A_m-unimodular
row b = (b_1, ..., b_{r'}) in (ℤ ltimes A_m)^{r'} with
r' ge 3 and b equiv e_1 pmod{t^m}, the polynomial b_1 is coprime
to t. For each irreducible divisor p of b_1 with b_{r'} notequiv 0 pmod p,
choose v_p in k avoiding the value -b_2/b_{r'} modulo p (only
one residue in k is forbidden, and q > 1). By the Chinese remainder
theorem in k[t] (a principal ideal domain) lift (v_p)_p to a single
v in t^m k[t] = A_m. Then
b' = (b_1, b_2 + v b_{r'}, b_3, ..., b_{r'-1}) is A_m-unimodular
and has the same reduction mod t^m: at a divisor where b_{r'} equiv 0,
original unimodularity already makes some component of (b_2, ..., b_{r'-1})
nonzero, and at a divisor where b_{r'} notequiv 0 the choice of v_p
forces the modified second component nonzero.

For the unitization lift, if a row b satisfies b · c = 1 over
R = k[t] with b equiv e_1 pmod{t^m}, then
c' = e_1 + (1 - b_1)c also satisfies b · c' = 1 and
c' equiv e_1 pmod{t^m}. The difference between b · c' and 1
lies in A_m, so the equality holds in ℤ ltimes A_m. Hence
sr(A_m) le 2 for every m.

Combining the stable range bound with Iwasa's theorem (with r = 2),
the map (H_j(GL_a(A_m); ℤ))_m to (H_j(GL_{a+1}(A_m); ℤ))_m
is a pro-isomorphism for a ge 2j + 1.

The group GL_a(A_m) equals Gamma_a(m). A matrix over
ℤ ltimes A_m that reduces to one congruent to I modulo t^m
lifts back to a matrix in GL_a(ℤ ltimes A_m); its
determinant lies in R^* = k^*, and the only element of k^* congruent
to 1 modulo t^m is 1. Hence the determinant is 1, and the
matrix lies in SL_a(k[t]).

Lemma (stabilization). Let h ge 0 and suppose a ge 2h + 1. Then
the inclusion on the first a coordinates induces surjections
H_j(Gamma_a(m); 𝔽_2) to H_j(Gamma_{a+1}(m); 𝔽_2)
for every 0 le j le h and every m ge 1.

Proof. The integral pro-isomorphism just obtained gives
pro-isomorphisms
(H_j(Gamma_a(m); ℤ))_m to (H_j(Gamma_{a+1}(m); ℤ))_m
for a ge 2j + 1. The universal coefficient theorem
0 to H_j(G; ℤ)  ⊗  𝔽_2 to H_j(G; 𝔽_2) to Tor_1^{ℤ}(H_{j-1}(G; ℤ), 𝔽_2) to 0
is natural in G, and its tensor and Tor functors extend to inverse
systems and preserve pro-isomorphisms. For j ge 1, both integral
degrees j and j-1 are in the stable range at a ge 2j + 1, so
the levelwise universal coefficient sequences assemble into a
pro-isomorphism of 𝔽_2-homology systems. Consequently the
cokernels of the levelwise maps H_j(Gamma_a(m); 𝔽_2) to H_j(Gamma_{a+1}(m); 𝔽_2)
form a pro-zero inverse system.

For m' ge m, the index [Gamma_a(m) : Gamma_a(m')] is finite and
odd. Filtering matrices congruent to I modulo t^m by successive
powers of t identifies each successive quotient with an additive
subgroup of matrices over k, whose order is a power of q and
therefore odd. The transfer map
tr : H_j(Gamma_a(m); 𝔽_2) to H_j(Gamma_a(m'); 𝔽_2),
summing over representatives of Gamma_a(m)/Gamma_a(m'), satisfies
inc_* circ tr = [Gamma_a(m) : Gamma_a(m')] = 1
over 𝔽_2. Hence inc_* : H_j(Gamma_a(m'); 𝔽_2) to H_j(Gamma_a(m); 𝔽_2)
is surjective, and every transition map between the cokernels is also
surjective.

A pro-zero inverse system with surjective transition maps is zero at
every level. For each m choose m' ge m with zero transition
A_{m'} to A_m; the same transition is surjective by the previous
paragraph; so its image is simultaneously {0} and all of A_m.
Applying this to the cokernel system gives levelwise surjectivity of
H_j(Gamma_a(m); 𝔽_2) to H_j(Gamma_{a+1}(m); 𝔽_2)
for 0 le j le h.

The next step uses the stabilization lemma to represent every homology
class on a chosen set of N-2 coordinates, so that the two remaining

Lemma (trivial conjugation). For 1 le j le d - 1 and every m ge 1,
the conjugation action of SL_N(R) on
H_j(Gamma_N(m); 𝔽_2) is trivial.

Proof. The choice N = 2d + 1 gives N - 2 = 2d - 1 = 2(d - 1) + 1.
Apply the stabilization lemma twice, at h = d - 1: first with
a = N - 2, then with a = N - 1. In both cases the hypothesis
a ge 2h + 1 is met because N - 2 = 2(d-1) + 1. Composing, the
natural map H_j(Gamma_{N-2}(m); 𝔽_2) to H_j(Gamma_N(m); 𝔽_2)
is surjective for every 1 le j le d - 1.

Signed permutation matrices of determinant one lie in SL_N(R),
normalize Gamma_N(m), and act transitively on the (N-2)-subsets of coordinates
within SL_N(R). Consequently the surjectivity extends
to every (N-2)-coordinate subgroup Gamma_T(m) ⊆ Gamma_N(m).

Fix distinct a, b in S and take T = S ∖ {a, b}. The
elementary matrix e_{ab}(f) = I + f E_{ab} acts on the source
H_j(Gamma_T(m); 𝔽_2) trivially because it commutes with
every element of Gamma_T(m). Combined with the surjection from the
previous paragraph, the same matrix acts trivially on
H_j(Gamma_N(m); 𝔽_2). Elementary matrices generate
SL_N(R) because R is a Euclidean domain, so the
whole group SL_N(R) acts trivially.

The finite quotient G_m also acts by conjugation, and the next
proposition assembles the triviality of that action with the homology
of U and the homology of T_m to show that the latter vanishes.

Proposition (vanishing cohomology). For every m ge 2,
H_j(Gamma_N(m); 𝔽_2) = H_j(T_m; 𝔽_2) = H^j(T_m; 𝔽_2) = 0
for 1 le j le d - 1.

Proof. The order of G_m is odd. The quotient U / Gamma_N(1)
is the upper unitriangular group over k, of odd order; and each deeper
quotient Gamma_N(i) / Gamma_N(i+1) is an additive group of N times N
matrices over k, again of odd order.

Choose a projective 𝔽_2[U]-resolution P_* to 𝔽_2 of
the trivial module. Restriction to Gamma_N(m) leaves P_* projective
because 𝔽_2[U] is free over 𝔽_2[Gamma_N(m)], so
(P_*)_{Gamma_N(m)} = P_*  ⊗ _{𝔽_2[Gamma_N(m)]} 𝔽_2
computes H_*(Gamma_N(m); 𝔽_2).

The group G_m acts by left multiplication on 𝔽_2[U], hence
on the chain complex and on its homology. Averaging over the odd-order
group G_m shows that the coinvariant functor M |-> M_{G_m}
is exact on 𝔽_2[G_m]-modules; combined with the tensor
identity M_U = (M_{Gamma_N(m)})_{G_m} for any 𝔽_2[U]-module M,
taking homology gives
H_j(U; 𝔽_2) ~= H_j(Gamma_N(m); 𝔽_2)_{G_m}.
By the trivial-conjugation lemma the right side equals
H_j(Gamma_N(m); 𝔽_2) for 1 le j le d - 1.

Combining with the vanishing H_j(U; 𝔽_2) = 0 for 1 le j le n - 2
(which contains 1 le j le d - 1 because d - 1 le n - 2 when n ge d + 1),
H_j(Gamma_N(m); 𝔽_2) = 0  (1 le j le d - 1).
By H_j(T_m; 𝔽_2) ~= H_j(Gamma_N(m); 𝔽_2) for 0 < j < n - 1,
the homology of T_m vanishes in the same range.

The last ingredient is the quantitative expansion theorem, due to
Oppenheim and Valentiner-Branth, that supplies the cosystolic inequality
on the (n-1)-skeleton of T_m with a uniform constant.

A Kac-Moody-Steinberg group of type widetilde A_n is built from the
proper spherical subdiagrams of the affine cycle. For each proper
J ⊊ S, the associated spherical local group is U_J. The
universal completion is the group obtained from the free product of
the U_J by identifying U_J with its image in U_K whenever
J ⊆ K. A finite quotient phi : widehatwidehat UU to Q defines
a KMS complex when phi is injective on every U_J and preserves
all pairwise intersections U_J cap U_K = U_{J cap K}; the resulting
complex is CC(Q; (phi(U_{S ∖ {i}}))_{i in S}).

./pdf/oppenheim-valentinerbranth-2026-mathann-cosystolic-kms.pdf

The Oppenheim-Valentiner-Branth cosystolic expansion theorem (Theorem
1.2 of the cosystolic-KMS paper) states that for every integer n ge 3 there are
ε_0 > 0, mu_0 > 0, and q_0 in ℕ such that if
q ge q_0 and Y_i are the (n-1)-skeleta of a family of n-dimensional
n-classical KMS complexes over 𝔽_q, then every Y_i is an
(ε_0, mu_0)-cosystolic expander with respect to any finitely
generated abelian group of coefficients. In 𝔽_2 coefficients
this reads
|delta_i f|_{Y, i+1} ge ε_0 dist_{Y,i}(f, Z^i(Y))  (0 le i < n - 1)
for every f in C^i(Y; 𝔽_2), where the norm is the one
defined above with the weight functions w_i^Y.

The verification that T_m satisfies the KMS hypotheses proceeds in
three steps. Dimension and classicalness: T_m has dimension N - 1 = n,
and every proper subdiagram of the affine cycle widetilde A_n is a
disjoint union of type-A paths, so every proper subdiagram has
irreducible components of type A_r, and T_m is n-classical.

Realization of the universal completion: for each proper J ⊆ S,
the map U_J to U extends to a homomorphism widehatwidehat UU to U, which
is surjective by the generation corollary. On each U_J this map is
given by the entry-pattern description of the local-group lemma.

Local injectivity and intersections: by the local-group lemma, the
composite widehatwidehat UU to U to G_m = U / Gamma_N(m) is injective on
each U_J for m ge 2 and preserves every pairwise intersection
U_J cap U_K = U_{J cap K}. Hence T_m = CC(G_m; (overlineoverline H_i)_H_i)_{i in S})
is a finite n-dimensional n-classical KMS complex.

Well-definedness of q: the requirements are that
q is an odd prime, that q ge 2^{n-1} (needed for the spherical
opposition vanishing), and that q ge q_0 (needed for the cosystolic
expansion theorem). All are finite lower bounds depending only on n,
and arbitrarily large odd primes exist. Fix such a prime q and keep
it fixed from now on.

The chosen q gives a cosystolic inequality with a uniform constant
ε_0 > 0 on the (n-1)-skeleton Y_m = skel_{n-1}(T_m):
|delta_i f|_{Y_m, i+1} ge ε_0 dist_{Y_m,i}(f, Z^i(Y_m))  (0 le i < n - 1).
This is the main input for the final assembly.

It remains to pass from the (n-1)-skeleton Y_m to the d-skeleton
X_m = skel_d(T_m), and to replace distances to cocycles
Z^i by distances to coboundaries B^i.

First, degree bounds. Every chamber of T_m contains exactly one vertex
of each type i in S. The common stabilizer of the base chamber's
vertices is U_∅ = {1}, so the chambers of T_m are in
bijection with G_m. A vertex of type i is a coset g overlineoverline H_i;H_i;
the number of chambers through it equals |overlineoverline H_i|H_i| = |H_i| = M,
independent of i and m. Each chamber has N vertices, of types
0, ..., N-1 one each.

A d-face containing a fixed vertex has d+1 vertices, so
binom{N-1}{d} choices of its other vertices. Each choice lies inside
at most M chambers through the fixed vertex. Hence every vertex lies
in at most M binom{N-1}{d} faces of dimension d. Therefore
D = M binom{N-1}{d} works.

Second, growth. The subgroup {e_{12}(f) : f in k[t]} of U is
isomorphic to the additive group of k[t], and its image in
G_m = U / Gamma_N(m) has order q^m: the polynomials of degree
less than m give distinct residue classes modulo t^m. Because
|G_m| ge q^m, and the number of vertices of T_m is N |G_m| / M,
|X_m(0)| = N |G_m| / M ge N q^m / M to ∞
as m to ∞. The divergence rate depends only on n and M.

Third, weight comparison. Let T be a finite pure n-complex and let
X = skel_s(T) for some 0 le s le n. Write
mu_i = w_i^T and nu_i = w_i^X for the intrinsic weights of T
and of X on i-faces. For every s-face tau in T(s), let
c(tau) be the number of n-faces of T containing tau. Suppose
0 < a le c(tau) le b for every tau in T(s), and put
Λ = frac{binom{n+1}{s+1}, |T(n)|}{|T(s)|}.
Then for every i-face sigma in T(i) with 0 le i le s,
(Λ)/(b), mu_i(sigma) le nu_i(sigma) le (Λ)/(a), mu_i(sigma).

Proof. The case i = s follows from the definitions: on one hand
mu_s(sigma) = c(sigma) / (binom{n+1}{s+1} |T(n)|), on
the other nu_s(sigma) = 1 / |T(s)|, so the ratio is Λ / c(sigma).

For i < s, use the two averaging identities
mu_i(sigma) = binom{s+1}{i+1}^{-1} sum_{tau in T(s),, sigma ⊆ tau} mu_s(tau), 
nu_i(sigma) = binom{s+1}{i+1}^{-1} sum_{tau in T(s),, sigma ⊆ tau} nu_s(tau).
The first is a double count of chamber--face incidences in T; the
second is the definition of the skeleton weights on X. Summing the
i = s bounds over all tau in T(s) containing sigma and dividing
by binom{s+1}{i+1} gives the asserted inequalities. The same
inequalities hold for norms and for distances to any subspace.

For the first application, X = Y_m and s = n - 1. Each (n-1)-face
